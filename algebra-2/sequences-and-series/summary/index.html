Sequences and Series: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Sequence, term ana_nan​, index nnn
A function on the positive integers (or on the integers upward from a stated start such as 000), so ana_nan​ is a(n)a(n)a(n). The index says WHICH term; the term is its value. The graph is isolated dots, never a curve.
Explicit rule and recursive rule
Explicit gives ana_nan​ as an expression in nnn, reaching any term in one substitution. Recursive gives seeds plus a step rule and must be climbed rung by rung: a rule looking back kkk steps needs kkk seeds, and without them defines nothing.
Sigma notation ∑k=mpak\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{p} a_kk=m∑p​ak​
Add the summand aka_kak​ at every integer from mmm up to ppp, BOTH limits included. The index is a dummy: renaming it changes nothing and it never appears in the answer. It fixes how many terms there are even when the summand hides it, so ∑k=165=30\sum_{k=1}^{6} 5 = 30∑k=16​5=30.
Series, partial sum SnS_nSn​, converge and diverge
A series adds the terms of a sequence, and SnS_nSn​ adds the first nnn. An infinite series converges when its partial sums close in on a single number SSS, and its sum is DEFINED to be that SSS; otherwise it diverges, with no sum.
Arithmetic sequence, common difference ddd
an+1−an=da_{n+1} - a_n = dan+1​−an​=d for EVERY neighbouring pair, so two matching differences prove nothing. Equivalently ana_nan​ is linear in nnn with slope ddd, so ak=4k+1a_k = 4k + 1ak​=4k+1 has d=4d = 4d=4 on sight.
Geometric sequence, common ratio rrr
an+1/an=ra_{n+1}/a_n = ran+1​/an​=r for every pair, with a1≠0a_1 \neq 0a1​=0 and r≠0r \neq 0r=0. Sizes grow when ∣r∣>1|r| > 1∣r∣>1 and shrink when ∣r∣<1|r| < 1∣r∣<1; a negative rrr alternates the signs.
Binomial coefficient (nk)\binom{n}{k}(kn​), Pascal's triangle
(nk)\binom{n}{k}(kn​) counts which kkk of the nnn factors of (x+y)n(x+y)^n(x+y)n donate their yyy. Row nnn lists (n0)\binom{n}{0}(0n​) through (nn)\binom{n}{n}(nn​): n+1n + 1n+1 entries, second entry nnn.
Factorial m!m!m!
The product m(m−1)⋯2⋅1m(m-1)\cdots 2 \cdot 1m(m−1)⋯2⋅1, so 4!=244! = 244!=24, with 0!=10! = 10!=1.

Formulas and theorems

  • Arithmetic: the nnnth term

    an=a1+(n−1)dam=ak+(m−k)d\begin{gathered} a_n = a_1 + (n-1)d \\ a_m = a_k + (m-k)d \end{gathered}an​=a1​+(n−1)dam​=ak​+(m−k)d​

    Use when Any arithmetic sequence, any ddd including 000 and negatives. The multiplier counts STEPS, never terms: a1=5a_1 = 5a1​=5 and d=3d = 3d=3 give a12=5+11(3)=38a_{12} = 5 + 11(3) = 38a12​=5+11(3)=38. Rearranged, d=am−akm−kd = \dfrac{a_m - a_k}{m - k}d=m−kam​−ak​​ with m≠km \neq km=k.

  • Counting terms

    n=L−a1d+1m,m+1,…,phas p−m+1 terms\begin{gathered} n = \frac{L - a_1}{d} + 1 \\ m, m+1, \ldots, p \\ \text{has } p - m + 1 \text{ terms} \end{gathered}n=dL−a1​​+1m,m+1,…,phas p−m+1 terms​

    Use when The first needs an arithmetic sequence with last term LLL and d≠0d \neq 0d=0; at d=0d = 0d=0 the values cannot reveal the count. The second holds for any integers m≤pm \le pm≤p, and sigma limits include both ends.

    e.g. 6,13,…,976, 13, \ldots, 976,13,…,97 has 917+1=14\tfrac{91}{7} + 1 = 14791​+1=14 terms.

  • Arithmetic series

    Sn=n2(a1+an)Sn=n2(2a1+(n−1)d)\begin{gathered} S_n = \frac{n}{2}\left(a_1 + a_n\right) \\ S_n = \frac{n}{2}\bigl(2a_1 + (n-1)d\bigr) \end{gathered}Sn​=2n​(a1​+an​)Sn​=2n​(2a1​+(n−1)d)​

    Use when Constant difference required, and nnn is the NUMBER of terms, not the last term. Use the first form when the last term is given, the second otherwise.

  • The running total of an arithmetic series

    Sn=d2n2+(a1−d2)nan=Sn−Sn−1\begin{gathered} S_n = \frac{d}{2}n^2 + \left(a_1 - \frac{d}{2}\right)n \\ a_n = S_n - S_{n-1} \end{gathered}Sn​=2d​n2+(a1​−2d​)nan​=Sn​−Sn−1​​

    Use when Runs BOTH ways: an arithmetic total is quadratic in nnn with no constant term, and conversely Sn=An2+BnS_n = An^2 + BnSn​=An2+Bn forces an=2An+(B−A)a_n = 2An + (B - A)an​=2An+(B−A), so d=2Ad = 2Ad=2A. A nonzero constant term means not arithmetic.

    e.g. Sn=3n2−nS_n = 3n^2 - nSn​=3n2−n gives an=6n−4a_n = 6n - 4an​=6n−4 and d=6d = 6d=6.

  • Geometric: the nnnth term

    an=a1r n−1,amak=r m−ka_n = a_1 r^{\,n-1}, \qquad \frac{a_m}{a_k} = r^{\,m-k}an​=a1​rn−1,ak​am​​=rm−k

    Use when a1≠0a_1 \neq 0a1​=0 and r≠0r \neq 0r=0. The exponent is n−1n-1n−1, one per multiplication between term 111 and term nnn. Keep a negative ratio bracketed: a1=3a_1 = 3a1​=3 and r=−2r = -2r=−2 give a6=3(−2)5=−96a_6 = 3(-2)^5 = -96a6​=3(−2)5=−96.

  • Finite geometric series

    Sn=a(1−r n)1−r=a(r n−1)r−1(r≠1)Sn=na(r=1)\begin{gathered} S_n = \frac{a\left(1 - r^{\,n}\right)}{1 - r} = \frac{a\left(r^{\,n} - 1\right)}{r - 1} \quad (r \neq 1) \\ S_n = na \quad (r = 1) \end{gathered}Sn​=1−ra(1−rn)​=r−1a(rn−1)​(r=1)Sn​=na(r=1)​

    Use when The fraction needs r≠1r \neq 1r=1: the denominator 1−r1 - r1−r vanishes there; at r=1r = 1r=1 every term equals aaa, so the sum is nanana. Only r=1r = 1r=1 is excluded: r=−1r = -1r=−1 gives a denominator of 222.

    e.g. 4−8+16−32+64=4(1−(−2)5)1−(−2)=4(33)3=444 - 8 + 16 - 32 + 64 = \dfrac{4\left(1 - (-2)^5\right)}{1 - (-2)} = \dfrac{4(33)}{3} = 444−8+16−32+64=1−(−2)4(1−(−2)5)​=34(33)​=44.

  • Infinite geometric series

    ∑n=1∞ar n−1=a1−rS−Sn=S r n\begin{gathered} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a r^{\,n-1} = \frac{a}{1 - r} \\ S - S_n = S\,r^{\,n} \end{gathered}n=1∑∞​arn−1=1−ra​S−Sn​=Srn​

    Use when Needs ∣r∣<1|r| < 1∣r∣<1, a condition on SIZE, so it covers negative ratios. With a≠0a \neq 0a=0 and ∣r∣≥1|r| \ge 1∣r∣≥1 the partial sums never settle and there is no sum. S−SnS - S_nS−Sn​ is the exact error left after nnn terms.

    e.g. 6−2+23−⋯=61−(−13)=643=926 - 2 + \tfrac23 - \cdots = \dfrac{6}{1 - \left(-\tfrac13\right)} = \dfrac{6}{\tfrac43} = \dfrac926−2+32​−⋯=1−(−31​)6​=34​6​=29​.

  • The Binomial Theorem

    (x+y)n=∑k=0n(nk)x n−ky k(x−y)n=∑k=0n(−1)k(nk)x n−ky k\begin{gathered} (x+y)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} x^{\,n-k} y^{\,k} \\ (x-y)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^k \binom{n}{k} x^{\,n-k} y^{\,k} \end{gathered}(x+y)n=k=0∑n​(kn​)xn−kyk(x−y)n=k=0∑n​(−1)k(kn​)xn−kyk​

    Use when nnn a whole number. Exactly n+1n + 1n+1 terms, and every term's two exponents sum to nnn. For a difference the coefficient is negative exactly when kkk is odd.

  • General term of an expansion

    Tk=(nk)A n−kB kk=0,1,…,n\begin{gathered} T_k = \binom{n}{k} A^{\,n-k} B^{\,k} \\ k = 0, 1, \ldots, n \end{gathered}Tk​=(kn​)An−kBkk=0,1,…,n​

    Use when AAA and BBB are the WHOLE terms of (A+B)n(A+B)^n(A+B)n, signs included. The list starts at k=0k = 0k=0, so TkT_kTk​ is the (k+1)(k+1)(k+1)st term and the rrrth term is (nr−1)A n−r+1B r−1\binom{n}{r-1}A^{\,n-r+1}B^{\,r-1}(r−1n​)An−r+1Br−1.

  • Binomial coefficients

    (nk)=n!k! (n−k)!(nk)=(n−1k−1)+(n−1k)(nk)=(nn−k)\begin{gathered} \binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!\,(n-k)!} \\ \binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k-1} + \binom{n-1}{k} \\ \binom{n}{k} = \binom{n}{n-k} \end{gathered}(kn​)=k!(n−k)!n!​(kn​)=(k−1n−1​)+(kn−1​)(kn​)=(n−kn​)​
    Text description

    Rows 0 to 5 of Pascal's triangle, with the neighbouring entries 4 and 6 joined by short lines down to the 10 they sum to in the row below.

    Use when Integers with 0≤k≤n0 \le k \le n0≤k≤n, and 0!=10! = 10!=1. Pascal's rule needs an interior 1≤k≤n−11 \le k \le n-11≤k≤n−1; the ends are held by (n0)=(nn)=1\binom{n}{0} = \binom{n}{n} = 1(0n​)=(nn​)=1. Row nnn totals 2n2^n2n. Cancel (n−k)!(n-k)!(n−k)! before multiplying.

    e.g. (103)=10×9×83!=120\binom{10}{3} = \dfrac{10 \times 9 \times 8}{3!} = 120(310​)=3!10×9×8​=120.

Problem types, step by step

Find a term of an arithmetic sequence from two given terms

  1. Count the steps between the known indices and divide the rise: d=am−akm−kd = \dfrac{a_m - a_k}{m - k}d=m−kam​−ak​​.
  2. Walk back to the first term with a1=ak−(k−1)da_1 = a_k - (k-1)da1​=ak​−(k−1)d.
  3. Substitute into an=a1+(n−1)da_n = a_1 + (n-1)dan​=a1​+(n−1)d, simplify to a linear rule in nnn, and check it reproduces both given terms.

e.g. a4=11a_4 = 11a4​=11, a9=26a_9 = 26a9​=26: d=155=3d = \tfrac{15}{5} = 3d=515​=3, a1=2a_1 = 2a1​=2, an=3n−1a_n = 3n - 1an​=3n−1, so a20=59a_{20} = 59a20​=59.

Add an arithmetic series

  1. Confirm one constant ddd.
  2. Count the terms: n=L−a1d+1n = \dfrac{L - a_1}{d} + 1n=dL−a1​​+1 from a last term, or p−m+1p - m + 1p−m+1 from sigma limits.
  3. Get the first and last term, evaluating the summand at each limit for a sigma sum.
  4. Apply Sn=n2(a1+an)S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}(a_1 + a_n)Sn​=2n​(a1​+an​), or n2(2a1+(n−1)d)\dfrac{n}{2}\bigl(2a_1 + (n-1)d\bigr)2n​(2a1​+(n−1)d) when the last term is unknown.

e.g. ∑k=530(2k−1)\sum_{k=5}^{30}(2k-1)∑k=530​(2k−1): 262626 terms running 999 to 595959, so S=13(68)=884S = 13(68) = 884S=13(68)=884.

Find a term or the ratio of a geometric sequence

  1. Divide consecutive terms to confirm one constant rrr, or from two given terms solve amak=r m−k\dfrac{a_m}{a_k} = r^{\,m-k}ak​am​​=rm−k.
  2. An even m−km - km−k leaves two candidate ratios, so use a stated sign or another term to choose.
  3. Back out a1=akr k−1a_1 = \dfrac{a_k}{r^{\,k-1}}a1​=rk−1ak​​, then substitute into an=a1r n−1a_n = a_1 r^{\,n-1}an​=a1​rn−1.

e.g. a2=6a_2 = 6a2​=6, a5=48a_5 = 48a5​=48: r3=8r^3 = 8r3=8, so r=2r = 2r=2, a1=3a_1 = 3a1​=3, and a7=3⋅26=192a_7 = 3 \cdot 2^6 = 192a7​=3⋅26=192.

Sum a finite geometric series given its last term

  1. Find rrr by division and confirm it.
  2. Solve a1r n−1=La_1 r^{\,n-1} = La1​rn−1=L for nnn by matching powers, or with logarithms.
  3. Substitute into Sn=a(r n−1)r−1S_n = \dfrac{a\left(r^{\,n} - 1\right)}{r - 1}Sn​=r−1a(rn−1)​, switching to Sn=naS_n = naSn​=na if r=1r = 1r=1.

e.g. 5+15+⋯+12155 + 15 + \cdots + 12155+15+⋯+1215: 3 n−1=2433^{\,n-1} = 2433n−1=243 so n=6n = 6n=6, and S=5(729−1)2=1820S = \dfrac{5(729 - 1)}{2} = 1820S=25(729−1)​=1820.

Find how many terms a threshold requires

  1. Write the term, or the partial sum, as a formula in nnn, then isolate the power of rrr.
  2. Take logarithms and divide by log⁡r\log rlogr, reversing the inequality when log⁡r\log rlogr is negative.
  3. Round up to a whole number and test both sides of the boundary.

e.g. 3+6+12+⋯3 + 6 + 12 + \cdots3+6+12+⋯: 3(2n−1)>30003(2^n - 1) > 30003(2n−1)>3000 gives 2n>10012^n > 10012n>1001, so n=10n = 10n=10 (S9=1533S_9 = 1533S9​=1533, S10=3069S_{10} = 3069S10​=3069).

Sum an infinite geometric series, including one written in sigma form

  1. Identify rrr, and get the first term actually summed by substituting the lower limit into the summand.
  2. Compare ∣r∣|r|∣r∣ with 111; if ∣r∣≥1|r| \ge 1∣r∣≥1 the series diverges and there is no sum to report.
  3. Apply S=a1−rS = \dfrac{a}{1-r}S=1−ra​, watching the denominator when rrr is negative.

e.g. ∑n=2∞3(25)n\sum_{n=2}^{\infty} 3\left(\tfrac25\right)^n∑n=2∞​3(52​)n: a=1225a = \tfrac{12}{25}a=2512​, r=25r = \tfrac25r=52​, so S=12/253/5=45S = \tfrac{12/25}{3/5} = \tfrac45S=3/512/25​=54​.

Write a repeating decimal as a fraction

  1. Split off any non-repeating head and read the repeating part as a series of blocks.
  2. For a block of bbb digits, the first block over 10b10^b10b is aaa, and r=10−br = 10^{-b}r=10−b.
  3. Sum with a1−r\dfrac{a}{1-r}1−ra​, add the head back, and reduce.

e.g. 0.18‾=18/1001−1/100=1899=2110.\overline{18} = \dfrac{18/100}{1 - 1/100} = \dfrac{18}{99} = \dfrac{2}{11}0.18=1−1/10018/100​=9918​=112​.

Pull one term or coefficient out of a binomial expansion

  1. Write the general term (nk)A n−kB k\binom{n}{k}A^{\,n-k}B^{\,k}(kn​)An−kBk with AAA and BBB complete, brackets and signs included.
  2. Collect every power of the variable into a single exponent by adding exponents.
  3. Set it equal to the target exponent (zero for a constant term) and solve for kkk; a non-integer kkk means no such term exists.
  4. Evaluate (nk)\binom{n}{k}(kn​), the numerical powers, and the sign.

e.g. Constant term of (x2+2x)6\left(x^2 + \tfrac{2}{x}\right)^6(x2+x2​)6: the power is x12−3kx^{12-3k}x12−3k, so k=4k = 4k=4 and (64)24=240\binom{6}{4}2^4 = 240(46​)24=240.

Expand a power of a binomial

  1. Write row nnn of Pascal's triangle, or use n!k! (n−k)!\dfrac{n!}{k!\,(n-k)!}k!(n−k)!n!​.
  2. March kkk from 000 to nnn, giving AAA the exponent n−kn-kn−k and BBB the exponent kkk, each raised whole.
  3. Evaluate every power, letting a negative BBB produce the alternating signs.
  4. Check by setting each variable to 111: the coefficients must total the unexpanded value.

e.g. (x−2)3=x3+3x2(−2)+3x(4)+(−8)=x3−6x2+12x−8(x-2)^3 = x^3 + 3x^2(-2) + 3x(4) + (-8) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 12x - 8(x−2)3=x3+3x2(−2)+3x(4)+(−8)=x3−6x2+12x−8.

Exam traps

  • Trap Multiplying the step by the term number: for 4,11,18,…4, 11, 18, \ldots4,11,18,…, writing a30=4+30(7)=214a_{30} = 4 + 30(7) = 214a30​=4+30(7)=214.

    Fix 214214214 is the 31st term: reaching term nnn takes n−1n-1n−1 steps, so a30=4+29(7)=207a_{30} = 4 + 29(7) = 207a30​=4+29(7)=207. The exponent in a1r n−1a_1r^{\,n-1}a1​rn−1 counts the same way, so the 5th term of 2,6,18,…2, 6, 18, \ldots2,6,18,… is 2⋅34=1622 \cdot 3^4 = 1622⋅34=162. Recovering ddd or rrr from two terms divides by steps too.

  • Trap Losing the +1+1+1 that turns steps back into terms.

    Fix (L−a1)÷d(L - a_1) \div d(L−a1​)÷d counts steps: from 333 to 434343 by fours is 101010 steps but 111111 terms. Sigma limits are inclusive too, so ∑k=36\sum_{k=3}^{6}∑k=36​ holds 444.

  • Trap Reading 'diverges' as 'blows up', then trusting a1−r\dfrac{a}{1-r}1−ra​ at r=−1r = -1r=−1.

    Fix 7−7+7−7+⋯7 - 7 + 7 - 7 + \cdots7−7+7−7+⋯ has bounded partial sums 7,0,7,0,…7, 0, 7, 0, \ldots7,0,7,0,… that never settle, so it has no sum, though the formula would report 72\tfrac7227​.

  • Trap Sign slips with a negative ratio.

    Fix 1−(−13)=431 - \left(-\tfrac13\right) = \tfrac431−(−31​)=34​, never 23\tfrac2332​: subtracting a negative adds. An even exponent kills the sign too, so (−3)6=+729(-3)^6 = +729(−3)6=+729.

  • Trap Calling TkT_kTk​ the kkkth term of an expansion.

    Fix The list starts at k=0k = 0k=0, so TkT_kTk​ is the (k+1)(k+1)(k+1)st term. The 3rd term of (x+y)7(x+y)^7(x+y)7 has k=2k = 2k=2, giving 21x5y221x^5y^221x5y2, not the 35x4y335x^4y^335x4y3 that k=3k = 3k=3 returns.

  • Trap Leaving a coefficient outside the power: reading the k=1k = 1k=1 term of (2a+3)4(2a+3)^4(2a+3)4 as 4a3(3)=12a34a^3(3) = 12a^34a3(3)=12a3.

    Fix The whole bracketed quantity is raised, so that term is 4(2a)3(3)=96a34(2a)^3(3) = 96a^34(2a)3(3)=96a3.

  • Trap Marking every coefficient of (x−y)n(x - y)^n(x−y)n negative because the binomial has a minus sign.

    Fix The sign is (−1)k(-1)^k(−1)k, so it is negative exactly when kkk is odd. In (x−1)6(x-1)^6(x−1)6 the coefficient of x4x^4x4 is +15+15+15.

  • Trap Reading a percentage change as the common ratio.

    Fix A rise of 666 percent gives r=1.06r = 1.06r=1.06, not 0.060.060.06; a drop of 202020 percent gives r=0.80r = 0.80r=0.80.

  • Trap Averaging to find a term sitting between two others in a geometric sequence.

    Fix The ratio must stay constant, so the term between 444 and 252525 satisfies x2=100x^2 = 100x2=100, giving x=10x = 10x=10 or x=−10x = -10x=−10, not 14.514.514.5.

  • Trap Assuming shrinking terms force convergence, so any series whose terms go to 000 has a sum.

    Fix That is a geometric fact, not a general one: 1+12+13+⋯1 + \tfrac12 + \tfrac13 + \cdots1+21​+31​+⋯ has terms shrinking to 000 and partial sums past every bound. For a geometric series the test is ∣r∣<1\lvert r \rvert < 1∣r∣<1.
