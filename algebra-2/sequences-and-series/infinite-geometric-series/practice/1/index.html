  1. 1

    Exactly one of these infinite geometric series converges. Which one?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    A geometric series with a nonzero first term converges precisely when the size of its ratio is less than 111, so find rrr in each case.

    The first has r=2r = 2r=2, the second has r=1r = 1r=1, and the fourth has r=−1r = -1r=−1, so all three fail the test. The fourth is the interesting failure: its partial sums are 1,0,1,0,…1, 0, 1, 0, \ldots1,0,1,0,…, which stay bounded and still never settle.

    Third series: r=36=12,∣12∣<1\text{Third series: } r = \frac{3}{6} = \frac12, \qquad \left|\tfrac12\right| < 1Third series: r=63​=21​,​21​​<1

    Only the third has a ratio smaller than 111 in size, so only it converges.

    What is the sum of the infinite geometric series 8+4+2+1+⋯8 + 4 + 2 + 1 + \cdots8+4+2+1+⋯?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The first term is a=8a = 8a=8 and the ratio is r=48=12r = \tfrac48 = \tfrac12r=84​=21​. Since ∣12∣<1\left|\tfrac12\right| < 1​21​​<1, the series converges and the sum formula applies.

    S=a1−r=81−12=812=16S = \frac{a}{1-r} = \frac{8}{1 - \tfrac12} = \frac{8}{\tfrac12} = 16S=1−ra​=1−21​8​=21​8​=16

    The partial sums 8,12,14,15,15.5,…8, 12, 14, 15, 15.5, \ldots8,12,14,15,15.5,… are climbing toward 161616, which is a quick sanity check.

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

  4. 4

    An infinite geometric series has a nonzero first term. For which common ratio does it diverge?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    With a nonzero first term, the series converges if and only if ∣r∣<1|r| < 1∣r∣<1, so compare the size of each ratio with 111.

    The sizes are 0.90.90.9, 0.50.50.5, 1.21.21.2, and 0.250.250.25. Three of them are below 111, and 0.90.90.9 is below 111 even though it is close to it, so those three series converge.

    ∣1.2∣=1.2>1|1.2| = 1.2 > 1∣1.2∣=1.2>1

    Only r=1.2r = 1.2r=1.2 fails the test. Its powers (1.2)n(1.2)^n(1.2)n grow past every bound, so the partial sums run away and the series diverges.

    What is the sum of 12+14+18+116+⋯\tfrac12 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac18 + \tfrac{1}{16} + \cdots21​+41​+81​+161​+⋯?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    This is the cake from the lesson: a=12a = \tfrac12a=21​ and r=12r = \tfrac12r=21​.

    S=121−12=1212=1S = \frac{\tfrac12}{1 - \tfrac12} = \frac{\tfrac12}{\tfrac12} = 1S=1−21​21​​=21​21​​=1

    Every partial sum (12\tfrac1221​, 34\tfrac3443​, 78\tfrac7887​, …\ldots…) is less than 111, and the sum is exactly 111, because the sum is the number the partial sums close in on, not one of them.

    An infinite geometric series has first term 101010 and common ratio 15\tfrac1551​. What is its sum?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Since ∣15∣<1\left|\tfrac15\right| < 1​51​​<1, the series converges. Apply the formula with a=10a = 10a=10 and r=15r = \tfrac15r=51​.

    S=101−15=1045=10⋅54=252S = \frac{10}{1 - \tfrac15} = \frac{10}{\tfrac45} = 10 \cdot \frac54 = \frac{25}{2}S=1−51​10​=54​10​=10⋅45​=225​

    So the sum is 12.512.512.5. Dividing by rrr rather than 1−r1 - r1−r would give 505050, which is the trap here.

  8. 8

    The partial sums of a series are 0.9,  0.99,  0.999,  0.9999,  …0.9,\; 0.99,\; 0.999,\; 0.9999,\; \ldots0.9,0.99,0.999,0.9999,… . What is the sum of the series?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The sum of a series is defined to be the number its partial sums close in on, if there is one. Each partial sum here adds one more 999, so the gap up to 111 is 0.10.10.1, then 0.010.010.01, then 0.0010.0010.001, shrinking past any tolerance you name.

    1−Sn=(110)ncloses in on01 - S_n = \left(\tfrac{1}{10}\right)^{n} \quad\text{closes in on}\quad 01−Sn​=(101​)ncloses in on0

    So the partial sums close in on 111, and the sum is exactly 111. No partial sum equals 111, and that is fine: the sum is not one of the partial sums.

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    r=4/254/5=425⋅54=15r = \frac{4/25}{4/5} = \frac{4}{25} \cdot \frac{5}{4} = \frac15r=4/54/25​=254​⋅45​=51​

  10. 10

    An infinite geometric series has a nonzero first term. The formula S=a1−rS = \dfrac{a}{1-r}S=1−ra​ is valid only when which condition holds?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The partial sums split as Sn=a1−r−a1−r r nS_n = \dfrac{a}{1-r} - \dfrac{a}{1-r}\,r^{\,n}Sn​=1−ra​−1−ra​rn, so the formula is the number they close in on only when the powers r nr^{\,n}rn die away.

    r n closes in on 0exactly for∣r∣<1r^{\,n} \text{ closes in on } 0 \quad\text{exactly for}\quad |r| < 1rn closes in on 0exactly for∣r∣<1

    The sign of aaa and the sign of rrr are irrelevant; the series 1−12+14−⋯1 - \tfrac12 + \tfrac14 - \cdots1−21​+41​−⋯ has a negative ratio and converges perfectly well, so r>0r > 0r>0 and a>0a > 0a>0 are not required. What matters is that the ratio is smaller than 111 in size. Applied outside that range the formula returns nonsense, such as 1+2+4+⋯=11−2=−11 + 2 + 4 + \cdots = \tfrac{1}{1-2} = -11+2+4+⋯=1−21​=−1.

    The stem rules out a first term of 000 for a reason. The all-zeros series converges to 000 for every ratio, and the formula returns 01−r=0\tfrac{0}{1-r} = 01−r0​=0 there too, so without the nonzero hypothesis the words "only when" would be false.
