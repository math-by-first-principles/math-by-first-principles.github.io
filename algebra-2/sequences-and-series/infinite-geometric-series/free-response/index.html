  1. Problem 1 A fixed opening term

    Find the exact value of 2+∑k=1∞32k2+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac3{2^k}2+∑k=1∞​2k3​.

    1. Hint 1

      Separate the fixed opening term from the geometric tail.

    2. Hint 2

      The tail starts with 32\frac3223​ and has ratio 12\frac1221​.

    Answer

    555.

    Full solution

    The tail converges because ∣r∣=12|r|=\frac12∣r∣=21​, which is less than 111.

    Its sum is

    3/21−1/2=3\frac{3/2}{1-1/2}=31−1/23/2​=3

    Adding the fixed opening 222 gives 555.

    The opening term is not the first term used in the tail's geometric formula.

    Answer

    555.

    Key idea

    A finite opening part can be added to a convergent geometric tail after the tail is identified correctly.

  2. Problem 2 A squared ratio

    For which real xxx does 1+x24+(x24)2+⋯1+\frac{x^2}{4}+(\frac{x^2}{4})^2+\cdots1+4x2​+(4x2​)2+⋯ converge?

    1. Hint 1

      The first term is nonzero, so convergence requires the ratio's absolute value to be less than one.

    2. Hint 2

      The ratio x2/4x^2/4x2/4 is already zero or positive.

    Answer

    −2<x<2-2<x<2−2<x<2.

    Full solution

    The criterion becomes x2/4<1x^2/4<1x2/4<1, or

    x2<4x^2<4x2<4

    Thus −2<x<2-2<x<2−2<x<2.

    At either endpoint the ratio is one, and beyond the endpoints it exceeds one.

    At x=0x=0x=0 the series is 1+0+0+⋯1+0+0+\cdots1+0+0+⋯, which also converges.

    Answer

    −2<x<2-2<x<2−2<x<2.

    Key idea

    A convergence condition can become a quadratic inequality when the ratio depends on a squared parameter.

  4. Problem 4 A sequence of corrections

    Answer

    Full solution

    The first readings 13,12,121313,12,12\frac1313,12,1231​ lie on alternating sides of 12.2512.2512.25, with shrinking corrections.

    Answer

    494\frac{49}{4}449​ units, or 12.2512.2512.25 units.

  5. Problem 5 Keeping alternate terms

    A geometric series has terms an=14(−25)n−1a_n=14(-\frac25)^{n-1}an​=14(−52​)n−1 for positive integers nnn. Form a new series using just the terms with even indices, a2+a4+a6+⋯a_2+a_4+a_6+\cdotsa2​+a4​+a6​+⋯. Find its first term, ratio, and sum.

    1. Hint 1

      Successive retained terms are two original ratio steps apart.

    2. Hint 2

      The retained series starts at the second original term, not the first.

    Answer

    First term −285-\frac{28}5−528​; ratio 425\frac4{25}254​; sum −203-\frac{20}3−320​.

    Full solution

    The first retained term is a2=14(−25)=−285a_2=14(-\frac25)=-\frac{28}5a2​=14(−52​)=−528​, and each retained term is two ratio steps after the last, so the ratio is

    (−25)2=425.(-\frac25)^2=\frac4{25}.(−52​)2=254​.

    The ratio has size below one, so

    S=−28/51−4/25S=\frac{-28/5}{1-4/25}S=1−4/25−28/5​

    giving

    S=−203.S=-\frac{20}3.S=−320​.

    The odd-indexed terms sum to 1421/25=503\frac{14}{21/25}=\frac{50}321/2514​=350​, and 503−203=10\frac{50}3-\frac{20}3=10350​−320​=10 checks against the original total

    141+2/5=10.\frac{14}{1+2/5}=10.1+2/514​=10.

    Answer

    First term −285-\frac{28}5−528​; ratio 425\frac4{25}254​; sum −203-\frac{20}3−320​.

    Key idea

    Keeping the even-indexed terms of a geometric series starts the new series at the second term and squares the common ratio.

  6. Problem 6 A tail and its opening

    A convergent geometric series has first term a≠0a\ne0a=0 and ratio r=34r=\frac34r=43​. Its sum after the first two terms are removed is 272727. Find aaa and the sum of the full series.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

      Express its sum in terms of aaa, then restore the original starting point.

    Answer

    a=12a=12a=12; the full sum is 484848.

    Full solution

    The tail begins with a(34)2a(\frac34)^2a(43​)2, so its sum is

    27=a(9/16)1/427=\frac{a(9/16)}{1/4}27=1/4a(9/16)​

    This simplifies to 27=9a/427=9a/427=9a/4, giving a=12a=12a=12.

    The full sum is

    12/(1−34)=48.12/(1-\frac34)=48.12/(1−43​)=48.

    Its first two terms are 121212 and 999, whose sum 212121 leaves the given tail sum 272727.

    Answer

    a=12a=12a=12; the full sum is 484848.

    Key idea

    Removing initial terms shifts the first term of a geometric tail without changing its ratio.

  7. Problem 7 Two estimates to compare

    For the series 6+1.5+0.375+⋯6+1.5+0.375+\cdots6+1.5+0.375+⋯, one estimate of its infinite sum uses the first three terms. Another estimate is 7.97.97.9. Which estimate is closer to the sum, and by exactly how much is its absolute error smaller?

    1. Hint 1

      Identify the common ratio and check that an infinite sum exists.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the exact sum and compare its distances from the two estimates using fractions.

    Answer

    The estimate 7.97.97.9 is closer; its absolute error is smaller by 140\frac1{40}401​.

    Full solution

    Each term is one quarter of the preceding term, so the series converges.

    Its exact sum is

    S=61−1/4S=\frac{6}{1-1/4}S=1−1/46​

    Thus S=8S=8S=8.

    The first three terms total 6+32+38=6386+\frac32+\frac38=\frac{63}{8}6+23​+83​=863​, whose error is

    8−638=18.8-\frac{63}{8}=\frac18.8−863​=81​.

    The other estimate is 7910\frac{79}{10}1079​, whose error is 110\frac1{10}101​.

    The second error is smaller, and the difference is

    18−110=140\frac18-\frac1{10}=\frac1{40}81​−101​=401​

    Both estimates lie below the true sum, so these differences are their absolute errors.

    Answer

    The estimate 7.97.97.9 is closer; its absolute error is smaller by 140\frac1{40}401​.

    Key idea

    Comparing approximations requires their distances from the exact sum, regardless of how each approximation was produced.

  8. Problem 8 Two families of partial sums

    Two series have partial sums Pn=cnP_n=cnPn​=cn and Qn=c+(−1)nQ_n=c+(-1)^nQn​=c+(−1)n for positive integers nnn, where ccc is a real constant. Find every value of ccc for which each series converges. For each divergent case, state whether its partial sums are bounded.

    1. Hint 1

      A series converges exactly when its partial sums settle on one number.

    2. Hint 2

      Consider c = 0 separately for the first series; for the second, compare odd and even n.

    Answer

    PPP: converges (to 000) only for c=0c=0c=0, and is unbounded for c≠0c\ne0c=0. QQQ: diverges for every ccc, with bounded partial sums.

    Full solution

    If c=0c=0c=0, every PnP_nPn​ is 000, so the first series converges to 000.

    If c≠0c\ne0c=0, then ∣Pn∣=∣c∣n|P_n|=|c|n∣Pn​∣=∣c∣n passes every bound, so it diverges with unbounded partial sums.

    For every ccc, QnQ_nQn​ is c−1c-1c−1 at odd nnn and c+1c+1c+1 at even nnn.

    These two values differ by 222, so the partial sums never settle and the series diverges, yet they stay between c−1c-1c−1 and c+1c+1c+1, so they are bounded.

    Answer

    PPP: converges (to 000) only for c=0c=0c=0, and is unbounded for c≠0c\ne0c=0. QQQ: diverges for every ccc, with bounded partial sums.

    Key idea

    Divergence means the partial sums fail to settle on one value, which can happen with bounded or with unbounded partial sums.

  10. Problem 10 A sign-changing claim

    For real a≠0a\ne0a=0 and −1<r<0-1<r<0−1<r<0, a student says a+ar+ar2+⋯a+ar+ar^2+\cdotsa+ar+ar2+⋯ converges even though its terms change sign. Is that true? Explain why the changing signs do not prevent the partial sums from settling.
