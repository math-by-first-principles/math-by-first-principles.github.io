    1. Hint 1

      Cancel the dividend's highest power with a multiple of the divisor.

    Subtracting from the dividend leaves

    R(x)=−x+2R(x)=-x+2R(x)=−x+2

    Its degree is 1<31<31<3, so division stops with Q=xQ=xQ=x.

    Expanding (x3+x+1)x+(−x+2)(x^3+x+1)x+(-x+2)(x3+x+1)x+(−x+2) restores x4+x2+2x^4+x^2+2x4+x2+2.

  2. Problem 2 Filling in missing columns

    Write 3x5−4x2+73x^5-4x^2+73x5−4x2+7 with every missing power's column shown, ready for long division.

    1. Hint 1

      List every power from the polynomial's degree down to the constant term, whether or not it appears in the given expression.

    2. Hint 2

      A power that is absent from the polynomial still needs a column; give it coefficient 000 so the column stays open.

    Answer

    3x5+0x4+0x3−4x2+0x+73x^5+0x^4+0x^3-4x^2+0x+73x5+0x4+0x3−4x2+0x+7.

    Full solution

    The polynomial has degree 555, so its columns run from x5x^5x5 down to x0x^0x0, six powers in all.

    Only three of them, x5x^5x5, x2x^2x2, and the constant term, appear in the given expression, so the powers x4x^4x4, x3x^3x3, and x1x^1x1 are missing.

    3x5−4x2+7=3x5+0x4+0x3−4x2+0x+7\begin{aligned} 3x^5-4x^2+7 &= 3x^5+0x^4+0x^3 \\ &\quad -4x^2+0x+7 \end{aligned}3x5−4x2+7​=3x5+0x4+0x3−4x2+0x+7​

    Each inserted 0xk0x^k0xk adds nothing to the value, but it opens a column so the tableau's subtractions line up matching powers.

    Answer

    3x5+0x4+0x3−4x2+0x+73x^5+0x^4+0x^3-4x^2+0x+73x5+0x4+0x3−4x2+0x+7.

    Key idea

    Preparing a division starts with detecting which powers are absent, then giving each a coefficient of 000 so every column stays aligned.

  3. Problem 3 Finding a three-term quotient

    Divide x3+x2−10x+13x^3+x^2-10x+13x3+x2−10x+13 by x+4x+4x+4. Give the quotient and remainder.

    1. Hint 1

      Divide the leading terms to find the first quotient term, then multiply back and subtract the whole product before repeating.

    2. Hint 2

      After each subtraction, compare the new remaining degree with the divisor's degree 111 to decide whether another pass is needed.

    Answer

    Full solution

    First pass: x3÷x=x2x^3\div x=x^2x3÷x=x2, and

    x2(x+4)=x3+4x2x^2(x+4)=x^3+4x^2x2(x+4)=x3+4x2

    Subtracting from the dividend leaves −3x2−10x+13-3x^2-10x+13−3x2−10x+13.

    Second pass: −3x2÷x=−3x-3x^2\div x=-3x−3x2÷x=−3x, and −3x(x+4)=−3x2−12x-3x(x+4)=-3x^2-12x−3x(x+4)=−3x2−12x; subtracting leaves 2x+132x+132x+13.

    A quotient of several terms still comes from the same forced move, repeated one leading-term cancellation at a time.

  4. Problem 4 Recovering a rectangular panel's length

    A rectangular panel has area 2x3+7x2+7x+22x^3+7x^2+7x+22x3+7x2+7x+2 square meters and width 2x+12x+12x+1 meters, where x>0x>0x>0. Find its length in polynomial standard form and verify the area by multiplication.

    1. Hint 1

      Area equals width times length, so divide the area polynomial by the width.

    2. Hint 2

      The successive quotient terms come from canceling each leading term.

    Answer

    Length x2+3x+2x^2+3x+2x2+3x+2 meters; verification (2x+1)(x2+3x+2)=2x3+7x2+7x+2(2x+1)(x^2+3x+2)=2x^3+7x^2+7x+2(2x+1)(x2+3x+2)=2x3+7x2+7x+2.

    Full solution

    Dividing leading terms first gives x2x^2x2.

    Subtracting (2x+1)x2(2x+1)x^2(2x+1)x2 leaves 6x2+7x+26x^2+7x+26x2+7x+2.

    The next term is 3x3x3x, leaving 4x+24x+24x+2.

    The final term is 222, leaving zero.

    The length is x2+3x+2x^2+3x+2x2+3x+2.

    One contribution to the product is

    2x(x2+3x+2)=2x3+6x2+4x.2x(x^2+3x+2)=2x^3+6x^2+4x.2x(x2+3x+2)=2x3+6x2+4x.

    The other contribution is x2+3x+2x^2+3x+2x2+3x+2.

    Adding restores the stated area.

    The length and width are both positive for x>0x>0x>0, matching the physical panel.

    Answer

    Length x2+3x+2x^2+3x+2x2+3x+2 meters; verification (2x+1)(x2+3x+2)=2x3+7x2+7x+2(2x+1)(x^2+3x+2)=2x^3+7x^2+7x+2(2x+1)(x2+3x+2)=2x3+7x2+7x+2.

    Key idea

    An exact polynomial division can recover a missing dimension from an area model.

  5. Problem 5 A division after a change

    Dividing P(x)P(x)P(x) by x2+2x^2+2x2+2 gives quotient x+1x+1x+1 and remainder 3x−13x-13x−1. Find the quotient and remainder when P(x)+2x2+5P(x)+2x^2+5P(x)+2x2+5 is divided by the same divisor.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the given identity and separate the added expression into divisor multiples and a smaller remainder.

    2. Hint 2

      Two copies of x2+2x^2+2x2+2 account for 2x2+42x^2+42x2+4.

    Answer

    New quotient x+3x+3x+3; new remainder 3x3x3x.

    Full solution

    The added term satisfies

    2x2+5=2(x2+2)+12x^2+5=2(x^2+2)+12x2+5=2(x2+2)+1

    Thus two more copies of the divisor join the quotient, and 111 joins the remainder.

    The quotient becomes x+3x+3x+3 and the remainder becomes 3x3x3x.

    Its degree 111 is below 222, so this is finished.

    Subtracting the original identity from the new one returns 2x2+52x^2+52x2+5, checking the change.

    Answer

    New quotient x+3x+3x+3; new remainder 3x3x3x.

    Key idea

    Adding a divisor multiple changes the quotient, while an additional smaller term changes the remainder.

    1. Hint 1

  7. Problem 7 A missing division result

    A division of P(x)=2x4−x3+3x2−x+5P(x)=2x^4-x^3+3x^2-x+5P(x)=2x4−x3+3x2−x+5 by D(x)=x2+1D(x)=x^2+1D(x)=x2+1 has a quotient beginning 2x2−x2x^2-x2x2−x. Finish the division, and state why the final remainder is allowed.

    1. Hint 1

      Subtract the product of the given partial quotient and the divisor.

    2. Hint 2

      Continue if the working remainder still has degree at least 222.

    Answer

    Q(x)=2x2−x+1Q(x)=2x^2-x+1Q(x)=2x2−x+1; R(x)=4R(x)=4R(x)=4, of degree 0<20<20<2.

    Full solution

    The partial product is

    D(x)(2x2−x)=2x4−x3+2x2−x\begin{aligned} D(x)(2x^2-x) &= 2x^4-x^3 \\ &\quad +2x^2-x \end{aligned}D(x)(2x2−x)​=2x4−x3+2x2−x​

    Subtracting it from PPP leaves x2+5x^2+5x2+5.

    One more copy of DDD leaves 444, so Q=2x2−x+1Q=2x^2-x+1Q=2x2−x+1 and R=4R=4R=4.

    The remainder degree is below the divisor degree.

    Adding that product and 444 reconstructs all five coefficients of PPP.

    Answer

  9. Problem 9 Multiplying a dividend by x

    Let D(x)=x2+3D(x)=x^2+3D(x)=x2+3. Polynomials PPP and QQQ satisfy P=DQ+3x+4P=DQ+3x+4P=DQ+3x+4. A student says division of xPxPxP by DDD has remainder 4x−94x-94x−9, whatever QQQ is. Is the claim correct? Find the new quotient in terms of QQQ.

    1. Hint 1

      Multiplying the dividend also multiplies the old remainder, which may need more division.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiply the given identity by xxx, then remove one more multiple of DDD from the expression outside the divisor product.

    Answer

    Yes; the new quotient is xQ+3xQ+3xQ+3 and the remainder is 4x−94x-94x−9.

    Full solution

    Multiplying the given identity by xxx gives

    xP=D(xQ)+3x2+4x.xP=D(xQ)+3x^2+4x.xP=D(xQ)+3x2+4x.

    The expression outside the divisor product now has degree 222, so it is not a finished remainder.

    Since D=x2+3D=x^2+3D=x2+3, write

    3x2+4x=3D+(4x−9).3x^2+4x=3D+(4x-9).3x2+4x=3D+(4x−9).

    Combining the divisor multiples gives

    xP=D(xQ+3)+(4x−9).xP=D(xQ+3)+(4x-9).xP=D(xQ+3)+(4x−9).

    The remainder has degree 111, below the degree 222 of DDD, so this is the completed division.

    Expanding the right side restores the multiplied original identity, independently of the choice of QQQ.

    Answer

    Yes; the new quotient is xQ+3xQ+3xQ+3 and the remainder is 4x−94x-94x−9.

    Key idea

    Multiplying a dividend can make its old remainder require another division step.

  10. Problem 10 Scaling both sides of a division

    Suppose division of a nonzero polynomial PPP by a nonzero polynomial DDD gives quotient QQQ and remainder RRR. A learner says division of 3P3P3P by 3D3D3D has quotient QQQ and remainder RRR. Decide when that statement is correct, and give the general correct remainder.

    1. Hint 1

      Scale the entire division identity by 333.

    2. Hint 2

      Check which part is absorbed into the divisor and which part remains outside the product.

    Answer

    Correct exactly when R=0R=0R=0; in general the quotient is QQQ and the remainder is 3R3R3R.

    Multiply P=DQ+RP=DQ+RP=DQ+R by 333.

    3P=(3D)Q+3R3P=(3D)Q+3R3P=(3D)Q+3R

    Scaling by a nonzero constant preserves degree, so 3R3R3R is still zero or has degree below 3D3D3D.

    Uniqueness makes this the correct result.

    It equals the claimed remainder RRR exactly when 3R=R3R=R3R=R, which forces R=0R=0R=0.

    Answer

    Correct exactly when R=0R=0R=0; in general the quotient is QQQ and the remainder is 3R3R3R.

    Key idea

    Scaling both dividend and divisor by the same nonzero constant preserves the quotient and scales the remainder by that constant.
