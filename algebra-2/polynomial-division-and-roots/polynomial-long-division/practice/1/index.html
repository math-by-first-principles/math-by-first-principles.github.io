  1. 1

    The identity x2+3x+5=(x+1)(x+2)+3x^2 + 3x + 5 = (x + 1)(x + 2) + 3x2+3x+5=(x+1)(x+2)+3 holds. When x2+3x+5x^2 + 3x + 5x2+3x+5 is divided by x+1x + 1x+1, what is the quotient?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Match the identity to the division algorithm's template, dividend equals divisor times quotient plus remainder.

    P=D⋅Q+RwithD=x+1P = D \cdot Q + R \quad\text{with}\quad D = x + 1P=D⋅Q+RwithD=x+1

    The factor multiplying the divisor is x+2x + 2x+2, so that is the quotient, and the leftover 333 is the remainder. The pair is valid because deg⁡3=0\deg 3 = 0deg3=0 is below deg⁡(x+1)=1\deg(x + 1) = 1deg(x+1)=1, and by uniqueness it is the only valid pair.

  2. 2

    When a polynomial is divided by a divisor of degree 111, what can the remainder be?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The division algorithm requires the remainder to be the zero polynomial or to have degree strictly below the divisor's degree.

    R=0  or  deg⁡R<1R = 0 \ \text{ or } \ \deg R < 1R=0  or  degR<1

    A polynomial of degree less than 111 has degree 000, which is a nonzero constant. So the remainder is a constant, and 000 is allowed; it is not required to be 000.

  3. 3

    While dividing a polynomial by x−5x - 5x−5, your working remainder is now the constant 121212. Why does the algorithm stop here?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The stopping rule is about degree: the loop runs while the working remainder has degree at least the divisor's degree, and halts as soon as it drops below.

    deg⁡12=0<1=deg⁡(x−5)\deg 12 = 0 < 1 = \deg(x - 5)deg12=0<1=deg(x−5)

    No multiple of x−5x - 5x−5 can cancel a constant without introducing a new xxx term, so no further pass can make progress. The sign of the remainder and the number of passes taken are irrelevant.

  4. 4

    A division by x+1x + 1x+1 produced quotient x+3x + 3x+3 and remainder 222. What was the dividend?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Rebuild the dividend from the division identity, dividend equals divisor times quotient plus remainder.

    (x+1)(x+3)+2=x2+4x+3+2=x2+4x+5(x + 1)(x + 3) + 2 = x^2 + 4x + 3 + 2 = x^2 + 4x + 5(x+1)(x+3)+2=x2+4x+3+2=x2+4x+5

    This multiply-back computation is exactly the check you should run after every division.

  5. 5

    What is the quotient when x2−9x^2 - 9x2−9 is divided by x−3x - 3x−3?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Rewrite the dividend with a placeholder, x2+0x−9x^2 + 0x - 9x2+0x−9, then divide. First pass: x2÷x=xx^2 \div x = xx2÷x=x, and x(x−3)=x2−3xx(x - 3) = x^2 - 3xx(x−3)=x2−3x; subtracting leaves 3x−93x - 93x−9.

    Second pass: 3x÷x=33x \div x = 33x÷x=3, and 3(x−3)=3x−93(x - 3) = 3x - 93(x−3)=3x−9; subtracting leaves 000.

    x2−9=(x−3)(x+3)x^2 - 9 = (x - 3)(x + 3)x2−9=(x−3)(x+3)

    The quotient is x+3x + 3x+3 with remainder 000: the division certifies the familiar difference-of-squares factorization.

  6. 6

    Integer division of aaa by bbb demands 0≤r<b0 \leq r < b0≤r<b. What replaces that condition in polynomial division by D(x)D(x)D(x)?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    A polynomial's value moves with xxx, so numerical size cannot measure whether the remainder is smaller than the divisor. The measure that does not move with xxx is the degree.

    R=0  or  deg⁡R<deg⁡DR = 0 \ \text{ or } \ \deg R < \deg DR=0  or  degR<degD

    The zero polynomial is named separately because it is not assigned a degree. This degree condition is what makes the quotient and remainder unique, just as 0≤r<b0 \leq r < b0≤r<b does for integers.

  7. 7

    A degree-333 polynomial is divided by a degree-111 divisor. What is the degree of the quotient?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    In P=DQ+RP = DQ + RP=DQ+R the remainder is too low in degree to affect the leading term, so the leading term of PPP equals the leading term of DQDQDQ, and degrees add under multiplication.

    deg⁡Q=deg⁡P−deg⁡D=3−1=2\deg Q = \deg P - \deg D = 3 - 1 = 2degQ=degP−degD=3−1=2

    A cubic divided by a linear divisor always gives a quadratic quotient; use this as a sanity check on every answer.

  8. 8

    What is the quotient when x2+8x+15x^2 + 8x + 15x2+8x+15 is divided by x+5x + 5x+5?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    First pass: x2÷x=xx^2 \div x = xx2÷x=x, and x(x+5)=x2+5xx(x + 5) = x^2 + 5xx(x+5)=x2+5x; subtracting leaves 3x+153x + 153x+15.

    Second pass: 3x÷x=33x \div x = 33x÷x=3, and 3(x+5)=3x+153(x + 5) = 3x + 153(x+5)=3x+15; subtracting leaves 000.

    x2+8x+15=(x+5)(x+3)x^2 + 8x + 15 = (x + 5)(x + 3)x2+8x+15=(x+5)(x+3)

    The quotient is x+3x + 3x+3 with remainder 000.

  9. 9

    Divide x2+4x+9x^2 + 4x + 9x2+4x+9 by x+1x + 1x+1. What are the quotient and remainder?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    First pass: x2÷x=xx^2 \div x = xx2÷x=x, and x(x+1)=x2+xx(x + 1) = x^2 + xx(x+1)=x2+x; subtracting leaves 3x+93x + 93x+9.

    Second pass: 3x÷x=33x \div x = 33x÷x=3, and 3(x+1)=3x+33(x + 1) = 3x + 33(x+1)=3x+3; subtracting leaves 666, of degree 0<10 < 10<1: stop.

    x2+4x+9=(x+1)(x+3)+6x^2 + 4x + 9 = (x + 1)(x + 3) + 6x2+4x+9=(x+1)(x+3)+6

    Check: (x+1)(x+3)=x2+4x+3(x + 1)(x + 3) = x^2 + 4x + 3(x+1)(x+3)=x2+4x+3, and adding 666 restores x2+4x+9x^2 + 4x + 9x2+4x+9. The quotient is x+3x + 3x+3 and the remainder is 666.

  10. 10

    In a correctly executed pass of the division loop, what happens to the working remainder's leading term after the subtraction?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The quotient term qqq is chosen precisely so that q⋅Dq \cdot Dq⋅D has the same leading term as the working remainder.

    W−qD  has no term of  deg⁡WW - qD \ \text{ has no term of } \ \deg WW−qD  has no term of  degW

    The two copies of the leading term cancel exactly, so the new working remainder has strictly smaller degree. That guaranteed drop is why the loop must eventually stop.

  11. 11

    A division of P(x)P(x)P(x) by D(x)D(x)D(x) ends with remainder 000. What does that tell you?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    With R=0R = 0R=0 the division identity loses its correction term.

    P(x)=D(x) Q(x)P(x) = D(x)\,Q(x)P(x)=D(x)Q(x)

    That equation displays PPP as a product of two polynomials, so DDD (and QQQ) are factors of PPP. Division with zero remainder is how a factorization is certified.

  12. 12

    What happens when you divide x+1x + 1x+1 by x2+1x^2 + 1x2+1?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The dividend's degree 111 is already below the divisor's degree 222, so the pair Q=0Q = 0Q=0, R=x+1R = x + 1R=x+1 satisfies both promises of the division algorithm.

    x+1=(x2+1)⋅0+(x+1),deg⁡(x+1)=1<2x + 1 = (x^2 + 1) \cdot 0 + (x + 1), \qquad \deg(x + 1) = 1 < 2x+1=(x2+1)⋅0+(x+1),deg(x+1)=1<2

    By uniqueness this is the only valid answer, just as 3÷53 \div 53÷5 gives quotient 000 and remainder 333. The division is perfectly well defined.
