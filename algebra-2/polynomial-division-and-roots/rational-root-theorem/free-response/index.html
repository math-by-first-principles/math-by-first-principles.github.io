  1. Problem 1 A list to prepare

    List all distinct rational-root candidates for P(x)=3x4−2x2+6P(x)=3x^4-2x^2+6P(x)=3x4−2x2+6, reduced to lowest terms and with both signs.

    1. Hint 1

      Possible numerators divide the constant term and denominators divide the leading coefficient.

    2. Hint 2

      Use positive numerators 1,2,3,61,2,3,61,2,3,6 and denominators 1,31,31,3, then remove duplicates.

    Answer

    ±1,±2,±3,±6,±13,±23\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm6,\pm\frac13,\pm\frac23±1,±2,±3,±6,±31​,±32​.

    Full solution

    The numerator divisors are 1,2,3,61,2,3,61,2,3,6 and the denominator divisors are 1,31,31,3.

    Dividing by 111 gives 1,2,3,61,2,3,61,2,3,6; dividing by 333 adds 13,23\frac13,\frac2331​,32​ while 33\frac3333​ and 63\frac6336​ duplicate 111 and 222.

    Attach both signs to these six positive values.

    This is a candidate list, not a list of verified roots.

    Answer

    ±1,±2,±3,±6,±13,±23\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm6,\pm\frac13,\pm\frac23±1,±2,±3,±6,±31​,±32​.

    Key idea

    Reduction and removal of duplicates make the rational-root candidate list complete without repeated entries.

  2. Problem 2 Two unknown coefficients

    An integer-coefficient polynomial has 76\frac7667​ as a root. Its leading coefficient AAA and nonzero constant term BBB are both positive. Find the smallest positive values of AAA and BBB permitted by the Rational Root Theorem.

    1. Hint 1

      The given fraction is already in lowest terms.

    2. Hint 2

      The denominator must divide AAA and the numerator must divide BBB.

    Answer

    A=6A=6A=6, B=7B=7B=7.

    Full solution

    Since 76\frac7667​ is reduced, the theorem requires

    6∣A6\mid A6∣A

    and

    7∣B7\mid B7∣B

    The least positive permitted values are 666 and 777.

    These are necessary coefficient restrictions; they do not by themselves determine the intervening coefficients or guarantee a root.

    Answer

    A=6A=6A=6, B=7B=7B=7.

    Key idea

    A known reduced rational root restricts both endpoint coefficients by divisibility.

  3. Problem 3 A fractional coefficient rule

    1. Hint 1

      The theorem first needs integer coefficients.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiply the equation by 444, a nonzero constant that changes no roots.

    Answer

    ±1,±12\pm1,\pm\frac12±1,±21​.

    Full solution

    Clearing the numerical denominators gives

    2x3−3x+1=02x^3-3x+1=02x3−3x+1=0

    The constant term permits numerator magnitude 111, and the leading coefficient permits denominators 111 and 222.

    Thus the full list is ±1,±12\pm1,\pm\frac12±1,±21​.

    Answer

    ±1,±12\pm1,\pm\frac12±1,±21​.

    Key idea

    Clearing constant denominators gives an equivalent integer-coefficient equation suitable for the theorem.

  4. Problem 4 A zero constant term

    1. Hint 1

      First remove the largest common power of xxx.

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    Only x=0x=0x=0, occurring twice.

    Full solution

    The polynomial is

    P(x)=x2(2x2+3x−4)P(x)=x^2(2x^2+3x-4)P(x)=x2(2x2+3x−4)

    This gives the root 000 twice.

    For the cofactor, candidates are ±1,±2,±4,±12\pm1,\pm2,\pm4,\pm\frac12±1,±2,±4,±21​.

    Its values at 1,−1,2,−21,-1,2,-21,−1,2,−2 are respectively 1,−5,10,−21,-5,10,-21,−5,10,−2; at 4,−4,12,−124,-4,\frac12,-\frac124,−4,21​,−21​ they are 40,16,−2,−540,16,-2,-540,16,−2,−5.

    None is zero.

    Thus the cofactor has no rational root, so 000 is the complete rational-root list.

    Answer

    Only x=0x=0x=0, occurring twice.

    Key idea

    A zero constant term calls for removing powers of xxx before making a finite candidate list.

  5. Problem 5 An interval and a list

    For F(x)=x4−3x2−1F(x)=x^4-3x^2-1F(x)=x4−3x2−1, prove that it has an irrational real zero between 111 and 222. You do not need its exact value.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the endpoints to establish a real zero, then address whether it could be rational.

    2. Hint 2

      The polynomial is monic with constant term −1-1−1, so the full rational candidate list is short.

    Answer

    At least one irrational real zero lies in (1,2)(1,2)(1,2).

    Full solution

    The endpoint values are

    F(1)=−3F(1)=-3F(1)=−3

    and

    F(2)=3F(2)=3F(2)=3

    A polynomial graph is unbroken, so the sign change gives a real zero between them.

    The only possible rational roots are ±1\pm1±1, and both give

    F(±1)=−3.F(\pm1)=-3.F(±1)=−3.

    The full list fails, proving that any real zero in the interval is irrational.

    Answer

    At least one irrational real zero lies in (1,2)(1,2)(1,2).

    Key idea

    A sign change establishes a real zero, and a failed full rational list establishes its irrationality.

  6. Problem 6 An unsupplied root

    Find all complex roots of P(x)=3x3+x2+4x−4P(x)=3x^3+x^2+4x-4P(x)=3x3+x2+4x−4, showing a complete rational candidate list and a factorization that accounts for all roots.

    1. Hint 1

      List candidates from the leading and constant coefficients, then test manageable entries.

    2. Hint 2

      A successful linear division leaves a quadratic you can solve over the complex numbers.

    Answer

    x=23, −1+i72, −1−i72x=\frac23,\ \frac{-1+i\sqrt7}{2},\ \frac{-1-i\sqrt7}{2}x=32​, 2−1+i7​​, 2−1−i7​​.

    Full solution

    The reduced candidate list is ±1,±2,±4,±13,±23,±43\pm1,\pm2,\pm4,\pm\frac13,\pm\frac23,\pm\frac43±1,±2,±4,±31​,±32​,±34​.

    At 23\frac2332​ the polynomial is zero.

    Dividing by 3x−23x-23x−2 gives x2+x+2x^2+x+2x2+x+2, so

    P(x)=(3x−2)(x2+x+2)P(x)=(3x-2)(x^2+x+2)P(x)=(3x−2)(x2+x+2)

    The quadratic formula gives

    x=−1±i72.x=\frac{-1\pm i\sqrt7}{2}.x=2−1±i7​​.

    These three roots account for degree 333, and expanding the factorization restores all coefficients.

    Answer

    x=23, −1+i72, −1−i72x=\frac23,\ \frac{-1+i\sqrt7}{2},\ \frac{-1-i\sqrt7}{2}x=32​, 2−1+i7​​, 2−1−i7​​.

    A rational-root search can reveal one linear factor while a quadratic supplies the remaining nonreal roots.

  7. Problem 7 A cubic to fully factor

    Find all rational roots of P(x)=3x3+5x2+x−1P(x)=3x^3+5x^2+x-1P(x)=3x3+5x2+x−1, including how many times each occurs as a linear factor.

    1. Hint 1

      The complete candidate list is determined by constant term −1-1−1 and leading coefficient 333.

    2. Hint 2

      After a root is found, test it again in the quotient.

    Answer

    x=−1x=-1x=−1 twice and x=13x=\frac13x=31​ once.

    Full solution

    The candidates are ±1,±13\pm1,\pm\frac13±1,±31​.

    The value P(−1)=0P(-1)=0P(−1)=0 leads to quotient 3x2+2x−13x^2+2x-13x2+2x−1 after division by x+1x+1x+1.

    That quotient also vanishes at −1-1−1, leaving 3x−13x-13x−1.

    Thus

    P(x)=(x+1)2(3x−1)P(x)=(x+1)^2(3x-1)P(x)=(x+1)2(3x−1)

    It gives −1-1−1 twice and 13\frac1331​ once.

    Expansion gives 3x3+5x2+x−13x^3+5x^2+x-13x3+5x2+x−1, checking both the roots and their repetitions.

    Answer

    x=−1x=-1x=−1 twice and x=13x=\frac13x=31​ once.

    Key idea

    A rational root can repeat, so the quotient should be tested after each successful division.

  8. Problem 8 A list and its opposite

    Consider this argument: 5x2+7x−65x^2+7x-65x2+7x−6 has candidate list ±1,±2,±3,±6,±15,±25,±35,±65\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\pm6,\pm\frac15,\pm\frac25,\pm\frac35,\pm\frac65±1,±2,±3,±6,±51​,±52​,±53​,±56​, and 35\frac3553​ turns out to be a root, so its negative −35-\frac35−53​ must be a root too, because the list is symmetric.     Answer

    The argument fails: P(35)=0P(\frac35)=0P(53​)=0 but P(−35)=−425P(-\frac35)=-\frac{42}{5}P(−53​)=−542​.

    P(35)=95+215−6P\left(\frac35\right)=\frac95+\frac{21}{5}-6P(53​)=59​+521​−6

    This is zero.

    P(−35)=95−215−6P\left(-\frac35\right)=\frac95-\frac{21}{5}-6P(−53​)=59​−521​−6

    This equals −425-\frac{42}{5}−542​.

    Answer

  9. Problem 9 A positive-root claim

    Let P(x)=x4+2x2+3x+1P(x)=x^4+2x^2+3x+1P(x)=x4+2x2+3x+1. A student says that since every term of PPP is positive when x>0x>0x>0, the candidate +1+1+1 never needs to be evaluated directly, though it still belongs on the theorem's candidate list. Confirm the full candidate list, explain the student's shortcut, and decide whether PPP has a rational root by evaluating what remains.

    1. Hint 1

      The full list comes from the leading coefficient and constant term.

    2. Hint 2

      For a positive input, every term of this polynomial is positive.

    Answer

    Candidate list ±1\pm1±1; PPP has no rational root.

    Full solution

    The theorem gives only 111 and −1-1−1 as candidates, from the divisors of the constant term 111 and leading coefficient 111.

    For x>0x>0x>0, every term x4x^4x4, 2x22x^22x2, 3x3x3x, and 111 is positive, so P(x)>0P(x)>0P(x)>0 for every positive xxx; this rules out +1+1+1 as a root without evaluating it, though it still counts as one of the two candidates the theorem lists.

    Testing the remaining candidate gives

    P(−1)=1+2−3+1P(-1)=1+2-3+1P(−1)=1+2−3+1

    This is 111, so the full rational search has failed.

    The conclusion concerns rational roots only.

    Answer

    Candidate list ±1\pm1±1; PPP has no rational root.

    Key idea

    A sign argument can rule out a candidate before evaluation, but the theorem's candidate list itself is unchanged; only which candidates need testing shrinks.

  10. Problem 10 A reduced fraction condition

    A worked solution rejects the proposed root 46\frac4664​ of 3x2+x−23x^2+x-23x2+x−2, reasoning that 666 does not divide the leading coefficient 333. Identify whether that reasoning is valid, and determine if the proposed value is actually a root.

    1. Hint 1

      The theorem restricts a fraction in lowest terms.

    2. Hint 2

      Reduce the proposed fraction before testing its denominator or evaluating it.

    Answer

    Invalid; 46=23\frac46=\frac2364​=32​ is a root.

    Full solution

    The fraction reduces to 23\frac2332​, whose denominator 333 divides the leading coefficient.

    Evaluate the reduced value:

    3(23)2+23−2=03\left(\frac23\right)^2+\frac23-2=03(32​)2+32​−2=0

    Thus it really is a root.

    A nonreduced denominator is not the denominator constrained by the theorem, so testing 666 against 333 was never the right comparison.

    Answer

    Invalid; 46=23\frac46=\frac2364​=32​ is a root.

    Key idea

    Lowest terms is essential when applying the denominator restriction in the Rational Root Theorem.
