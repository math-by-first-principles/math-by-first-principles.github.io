  2. 2

    Find the remainder when x75−2x40+5x^{75} - 2x^{40} + 5x75−2x40+5 is divided by x+1x + 1x+1.

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Synthetic division on 767676 columns would be absurd, but the Remainder Theorem needs only one evaluation, at x=−1x = -1x=−1. Odd powers of −1-1−1 give −1-1−1 and even powers give +1+1+1.

    P(−1)=(−1)75−2(−1)40+5=−1−2+5=2P(-1) = (-1)^{75} - 2(-1)^{40} + 5 = -1 - 2 + 5 = 2P(−1)=(−1)75−2(−1)40+5=−1−2+5=2

    The remainder is 222. This is the theorem's real power: the remainder costs one evaluation no matter how large the degree.

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The identity holds for every xxx, not just at x=3x = 3x=3, so substitute x=1x = 1x=1 directly into the divided form.

    P(1)=(1−3)(1+2+5)+4=(−2)(8)+4=−16+4=−12P(1) = (1 - 3)(1 + 2 + 5) + 4 = (-2)(8) + 4 = -16 + 4 = -12P(1)=(1−3)(1+2+5)+4=(−2)(8)+4=−16+4=−12

    No expansion is needed. The value 444 answers a different question: it is P(3)P(3)P(3), the remainder on dividing by x−3x - 3x−3.

  4. 4

    For which values of ccc does dividing x2−5x+6x^2 - 5x + 6x2−5x+6 by x−cx - cx−c leave remainder 000?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    By the Remainder Theorem the remainder is P(c)=c2−5c+6P(c) = c^2 - 5c + 6P(c)=c2−5c+6, so the division is exact exactly when this vanishes.

    c2−5c+6=0⟹(c−2)(c−3)=0⟹c=2 or c=3c^2 - 5c + 6 = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad (c - 2)(c - 3) = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad c = 2 \text{ or } c = 3c2−5c+6=0⟹(c−2)(c−3)=0⟹c=2 or c=3

    Both values work and no others do: the divisions by x−2x - 2x−2 and x−3x - 3x−3 are exact, matching x2−5x+6=(x−2)(x−3)x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3)x2−5x+6=(x−2)(x−3).

  9. 9

    Long division by x−cx - cx−c subtracts at every stage, yet synthetic division only ever adds. Why does adding give the same answer?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Each long-division stage subtracts a multiple of the whole divisor x−cx - cx−c. The xxx part of that multiple cancels the leading term by design, and the remaining part subtracts −c-c−c times the newest quotient coefficient.

    ak−(−c) bk=ak+c bka_k - (-c)\,b_k = a_k + c\,b_kak​−(−c)bk​=ak​+cbk​

    Subtracting a negative multiple is adding a positive one, and the corner holds ccc (the divisor's constant already negated) so that the tableau can add. That is also why dividing by x+5x + 5x+5 needs corner −5-5−5: the sign flip happens once, in the corner, and never again.

  10. 10

    Why can synthetic division, as built in this lesson, not be used directly to divide by x2−4x^2 - 4x2−4?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The tableau's recurrence bk−1=ak+c bkb_{k-1} = a_k + c\,b_kbk−1​=ak​+cbk​ is the coefficient bookkeeping of dividing by x−cx - cx−c specifically: one number multiplied by one corner value, column by column.

    deg⁡(remainder)<deg⁡(x2−4)=2\deg(\text{remainder}) < \deg(x^2 - 4) = 2deg(remainder)<deg(x2−4)=2

    Dividing by a quadratic leaves a remainder that can be linear, not just constant, and each stage of that long division updates two coefficients at once. The single-corner, single-carry tableau has no place to store that. (Dividing by x2−4=(x−2)(x+2)x^2 - 4 = (x-2)(x+2)x2−4=(x−2)(x+2) can still be done by two successive synthetic divisions, tracking both remainders carefully.)
