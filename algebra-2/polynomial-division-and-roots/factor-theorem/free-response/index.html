  1. Problem 1 A missing coefficient

    For which value of kkk is x+2x+2x+2 a factor of P(x)=3x3+kx2−7x−10P(x)=3x^3+kx^2-7x-10P(x)=3x3+kx2−7x−10?

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    k=5k=5k=5.

    Full solution

    The factor x+2x+2x+2 vanishes at −2-2−2.

    Substituting x=−2x=-2x=−2 into each term gives 3(−2)3=−243(-2)^3=-243(−2)3=−24, k(−2)2=4kk(-2)^2=4kk(−2)2=4k, and −7(−2)=14-7(-2)=14−7(−2)=14.

    P(−2)=−24+4k+14−10=4k−20\begin{aligned}P(-2)&=-24+4k+14-10\\&=4k-20\end{aligned}P(−2)​=−24+4k+14−10=4k−20​

    Setting 4k−20=04k-20=04k−20=0 gives k=5k=5k=5.

    Answer

    k=5k=5k=5.

    Key idea

    When the unknown sits inside the polynomial itself, the factor condition becomes a linear equation to solve for it.

  3. Problem 3 A recorded identity

    Suppose P(8)=−11P(8)=-11P(8)=−11, and dividing P(x)P(x)P(x) by x−8x-8x−8 gives quotient Q(x)=2x2−5x+1Q(x)=2x^2-5x+1Q(x)=2x2−5x+1. What remainder does this division leave?

    1. Hint 1

      The remainder of dividing by x−cx-cx−c is always a single number, and the master identity names exactly which one.

    2. Hint 2

      The formula for Q(x)Q(x)Q(x) does not change the value you already know for P(8)P(8)P(8).

    Answer

    Remainder −11-11−11.

    Full solution

    The master identity for division by x−8x-8x−8 is

    P(x)=(x−8) Q(x)+P(8)P(x)=(x-8)\,Q(x)+P(8)P(x)=(x−8)Q(x)+P(8)

    so whatever Q(x)Q(x)Q(x) turns out to be, the remainder is always the single number P(8)P(8)P(8).

    Since P(8)=−11P(8)=-11P(8)=−11, the remainder is −11-11−11, and substituting the given quotient gives

    P(x)=(x−8)(2x2−5x+1)−11P(x)=(x-8)\left(2x^2-5x+1\right)-11P(x)=(x−8)(2x2−5x+1)−11

    The quotient's own coefficients play no role in fixing the remainder; only the value P(8)P(8)P(8) does.

    Answer

    Remainder −11-11−11.

    Key idea

    The master identity writes a linear-division remainder as an evaluation.

  4. Problem 4 A supplied pair

    Full solution

    A second division by x−2x-2x−2 gives x2+2x+2x^2+2x+2x2+2x+2.

    x2+2x+2=(x+1)2+1.x^2+2x+2=(x+1)^2+1.x2+2x+2=(x+1)2+1.

    P(x)=(x−2)2(x2+2x+2)P(x)=(x-2)^2(x^2+2x+2)P(x)=(x−2)2(x2+2x+2)

  5. Problem 5 A real-coefficient construction

    Find the monic polynomial of least degree with real coefficients whose roots include −3-3−3, 111, and 2+3i2+3i2+3i. Give it in standard form.

    1. Hint 1

    Full solution

    The root 2−3i2-3i2−3i is also required, so the least degree is 444.

    P(x)=x4−2x3+2x2+38x−39P(x)=x^4-2x^3+2x^2+38x-39P(x)=x4−2x3+2x2+38x−39

    The three factors together supply all four required roots: −3-3−3, 111, and the conjugate pair 2±3i2\pm3i2±3i.

    Answer

  6. Problem 6 A coefficient constraint

  8. Problem 8 Agreement at three inputs

    Polynomials AAA and BBB each have degree at most 222, and they agree at the three distinct inputs −1-1−1, 222, and 444. Must they agree for every input? Explain using a root bound.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    Yes; A(x)=B(x)A(x)=B(x)A(x)=B(x) for every input.

    Full solution

    Define H=A−BH=A-BH=A−B.

    The three agreements mean

    H(−1)=0H(-1)=0H(−1)=0

    together with H(2)=0H(2)=0H(2)=0 and H(4)=0H(4)=0H(4)=0.

    A nonzero polynomial of degree at most 222 has at most two distinct roots.

    Hence HHH is zero, so A=BA=BA=B everywhere.

    Answer

    Yes; A(x)=B(x)A(x)=B(x)A(x)=B(x) for every input.

    Key idea

    Enough matching values force two bounded-degree polynomials to be identical.

  10. Problem 10 A repeated test

    If x−cx-cx−c is a factor of P(x)P(x)P(x), a student claims it must also be a factor of P(x)+(x−c)2P(x)+(x-c)^2P(x)+(x−c)2. Is the claim true? Explain without assuming any particular degree of PPP.

    Evaluating at ccc also gives zero.

    Answer

    True.

    Key idea

    Adding another multiple of a linear factor preserves divisibility by that factor.
