    Worked solution

    Part A

    Nothing can be read off the polynomial as written, because two of its six terms are like terms of degree 666 and two more are like terms of degree 333. Collect them first.

    The two sixth-degree terms are 2x62x^62x6 and −2x6-2x^6−2x6, and they wipe each other out. The two cubic terms are −8x3-8x^3−8x3 and 4x34x^34x3, which combine to −4x3-4x^3−4x3.     q(x)=−4x3−x+5.q(x) = -4x^3 - x + 5.q(x)=−4x3−x+5.

    Now every name is safe to read. The highest surviving power is x3x^3x3, so the degree is 333 and the leading term is −4x3-4x^3−4x3, making the leading coefficient −4-4−4, sign included. The constant term is 555, again sign included, and three terms survive, so this is a cubic trinomial.

    The trap that the housekeeping avoids is worth naming. Read before tidying, the expression looks like a polynomial of degree 666 with leading coefficient 222, and both of those readings are wrong: once the sum is collected, the sixth-degree terms are not there at all. A degree is a property of the polynomial, not of the way somebody happened to write it down.

    Part B

    deg⁡P=2+3+3=8.\deg P = 2 + 3 + 3 = 8.degP=2+3+3=8.

    Part C

    Rewrite each expression so that what is being done to xxx is visible, then apply one test.

    Dividing by the number 333 is the same as multiplying every term by 13\tfrac{1}{3}31​:

    The second expression divides by the variable itself. Written as a term it is 3x−43x^{-4}3x−4, and −4-4−4 is not a whole number, so the expression is not a polynomial. Notice what is at fault: not the 333 on top, but the variable underneath.

    The third looks worse than it is. Its coefficients are 7\sqrt{7}7​, −1-1−1 and 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​, and its exponents on xxx are 222, 111 and 000. The square root is applied to the number 777, never to xxx, so nothing is wrong at all: this is a quadratic polynomial that happens to carry an irrational leading coefficient.

    The fourth reverses the two roles the definition cares about. In 4x4^x4x the constant is the base and the variable is the exponent, which no term of the form c xkc\,x^kcxk can imitate. Its second term −x4-x^4−x4 is perfectly legal, but one illegal term is enough to disqualify the whole sum.

    So one rule decided all four: a polynomial is a finite sum of terms c xkc\,x^kcxk in which every kkk is a whole number. The rule polices the exponents on the variable and nothing else, which is why a coefficient may be a fraction, a negative number or an irrational number without any difficulty whatever.
