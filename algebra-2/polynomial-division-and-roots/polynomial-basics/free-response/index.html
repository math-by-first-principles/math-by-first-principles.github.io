  1. Problem 1 One expression, one test

    Determine whether f(x)=πx4−8x+19f(x)=\pi x^4-8x+\frac19f(x)=πx4−8x+91​ is a polynomial. If it is, give its degree and leading coefficient; if it is not, name the specific requirement it fails.

    1. Hint 1

      A polynomial restricts what happens to the variable xxx in each term; a coefficient may be any real number.

    2. Hint 2

      Check every term for xxx divided into, rooted, or placed in an exponent, and note each exponent on xxx.

    Answer

    Yes, it is a polynomial; degree 444, leading coefficient π\piπ.

    Full solution

    Each term of fff has a whole-number exponent on xxx: 444 in πx4\pi x^4πx4, 111 in −8x-8x−8x, and 000 in the constant 19\frac1991​, and xxx is never divided into, rooted, or placed in an exponent.

    f(x)=πx4−8x+19f(x)=\pi x^4-8x+\frac19f(x)=πx4−8x+91​

    So fff is a polynomial even though one coefficient, π\piπ, is irrational, since the restriction falls only on the variable's own exponent.

    The terms are already in descending order, so the leading term is πx4\pi x^4πx4, giving degree 444 and leading coefficient π\piπ.

    Answer

    Yes, it is a polynomial; degree 444, leading coefficient π\piπ.

    Key idea

    A coefficient may be any real number, including an irrational one; only the variable's own exponent decides whether an expression is a polynomial.

  2. Problem 2 A product label

    A nonzero polynomial A(x)A(x)A(x) has leading term −3x4-3x^4−3x4. The product A(x)B(x)A(x)B(x)A(x)B(x) has leading term 12x912x^912x9. Find the leading term of B(x)B(x)B(x).

    1. Hint 1

      The highest powers in a product come from its factors' leading terms.

    2. Hint 2

      The degrees add, while the leading coefficients multiply.

    Answer

    −4x5-4x^5−4x5.

    Full solution

    Write the leading term of BBB as bxnbx^nbxn, with b≠0b\ne0b=0.

    Then the leading term of the product is −3bxn+4-3b x^{n+4}−3bxn+4.

    Matching the given power and coefficient gives

    n+4=9−3b=12.\begin{aligned} n+4&=9 \\ -3b&=12. \end{aligned}n+4−3b​=9=12.​

    Thus n=5n=5n=5 and b=−4b=-4b=−4, so the required term is −4x5-4x^5−4x5.

    Multiplying it by −3x4-3x^4−3x4 checks the given product term.

    Answer

    −4x5-4x^5−4x5.

    Key idea

    A product's leading term determines one factor's leading term when the other is known.

  3. Problem 3 A difference of readings

    For f(x)=3x4−2x3+6x−5f(x)=3x^4-2x^3+6x-5f(x)=3x4−2x3+6x−5, find f(1)−f(0)f(1)-f(0)f(1)−f(0).

    1. Hint 1

      At zero only the constant remains; at one every power equals one.

    2. Hint 2

      The constant cancels in the requested difference.

    Answer

    777.

    Full solution

    The evaluations are f(1)=2f(1)=2f(1)=2 and f(0)=−5f(0)=-5f(0)=−5.

    f(1)−f(0)=2−(−5)f(1)-f(0)=2-(-5)f(1)−f(0)=2−(−5)

    This equals 777, also the sum 3−2+63-2+63−2+6 of the nonconstant coefficients.

    Answer

    777.

    Key idea

    The difference between the values at one and zero equals the sum of the nonconstant coefficients.

  4. Problem 4 A family of rules

    Let Fk(x)=(k−1)x4+(k+2)x3−5F_k(x)=(k-1)x^4+(k+2)x^3-5Fk​(x)=(k−1)x4+(k+2)x3−5. Find the real kkk for which FkF_kFk​ has degree 333, then state its leading coefficient, constant term, and far-end behavior.

    1. Hint 1

      The fourth-degree term must vanish while the cubic term remains.

    2. Hint 2

      After choosing kkk, read the sign and parity of the surviving leading term.

    Answer

    k=1k=1k=1; leading coefficient 333, constant −5-5−5; far left falls and far right rises.

    Full solution

    Degree 333 requires k−1=0k-1=0k−1=0, so k=1k=1k=1.

    At this value k+2=3≠0k+2=3\ne0k+2=3=0, and

    F1(x)=3x3−5F_1(x)=3x^3-5F1​(x)=3x3−5

    The positive cubic leading term makes the far left fall and the far right rise.

    The constant is −5-5−5.

    Answer

    Key idea

    A parameter can change the degree when it cancels the highest-power coefficient.

  5. Problem 5 A box model

    A box has side lengths xxx, x+1x+1x+1, and 2x+32x+32x+3 meters for real x>0x>0x>0. Write its volume polynomial in standard form. For that polynomial extended to all real inputs, give the degree, leading term, and far-end behavior.

    1. Hint 1

      Volume multiplies the three lengths.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand or combine the leading terms, then distinguish the physical domain from the polynomial domain.

    Answer

    V(x)=2x3+5x2+3xV(x)=2x^3+5x^2+3xV(x)=2x3+5x2+3x cubic meters; degree 333, leading term 2x32x^32x3; far left falls, far right rises.

    Full solution

    First

    (x+1)(2x+3)=2x2+5x+3.(x+1)(2x+3)=2x^2+5x+3.(x+1)(2x+3)=2x2+5x+3.

    Multiplying by xxx gives

    V(x)=2x3+5x2+3xV(x)=2x^3+5x^2+3xV(x)=2x3+5x2+3x

    Its degree is 333 and its leading term is 2x32x^32x3.

    As a polynomial on all real inputs its far left falls and far right rises, although negative inputs do not describe the stated physical box.

    Answer

    V(x)=2x3+5x2+3xV(x)=2x^3+5x^2+3xV(x)=2x3+5x2+3x cubic meters; degree 333, leading term 2x32x^32x3; far left falls, far right rises.

    Key idea

    A product model has degree equal to the sum of factor degrees, while its physical domain may be smaller.

  6. Problem 6 A visible curve

    The figure shows the complete zero and turning-point pattern of a degree-444 polynomial. Read its distinct real zeros and count its turning points. Explain whether either count exceeds the degree bounds.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the x-axis from -1.5 to 1.5, ticked every 0.5, and the y-axis from -0.5 to 2, ticked every 0.25. A single smooth curve meets the horizontal axis at exactly three points, x equals -1, x equals 0, and x equals 1. On each side of x equals 0 the curve dips below the axis into a valley, and at x equals 0 it rises only as far back as the axis, at a peak between the two valleys, before turning back down into the second valley. Near the top edge of the frame, both ends of the curve are marked with an arrow showing that they continue rising beyond what is drawn. No equation, coordinates, or turning points are labeled.

    1. Hint 1

      A real zero is an input where the curve meets the horizontal axis.

    2. Hint 2

      Degree 444 permits at most four distinct real zeros and three turning points.

    Answer

    Zeros −1,0,1-1,0,1−1,0,1; three turning points; neither count exceeds its bound.

    Full solution

    The curve meets the axis at −1-1−1, 000, and 111, so there are three distinct real zeros.

    It turns in the two valleys and at the central peak, giving three turning points.

    The bounds are

    3≤43\le43≤4

    for real zeros and

    3≤4−13\le4-13≤4−1

    for turning points.

    Both counts are allowed.

    Answer

    Zeros −1,0,1-1,0,1−1,0,1; three turning points; neither count exceeds its bound.

    Key idea

    Degree gives ceilings on distinct zeros and turning points, not a requirement that both ceilings be reached.

  7. Problem 7 Three coefficient clues

    The values f(0)=5f(0)=5f(0)=5 and f(1)=−1f(1)=-1f(1)=−1 check the coefficient clues.

  8. Problem 8 A statement about two rules

    Two monic polynomials each have degree 555. A student says their difference is either zero or has degree at most 444. Is that correct? Justify your answer.

    1. Hint 1

      Monic means each leading coefficient is one.

    2. Hint 2

      Inspect what happens to the two x5x^5x5 terms when subtracting.

    Answer

    Correct; the difference is zero or has degree at most 444.

    Full solution

    Write the polynomials as x5+A(x)x^5+A(x)x5+A(x) and x5+B(x)x^5+B(x)x5+B(x), where AAA and BBB have degree at most 444 or are zero.

    Their difference is

    (x5+A)−(x5+B)=A−B(x^5+A)-(x^5+B)=A-B(x5+A)−(x5+B)=A−B

    Thus no fifth-degree term survives.

    The result is zero or has degree at most 444.

    Answer

    Correct; the difference is zero or has degree at most 444.

    Key idea

    Equal leading terms cancel in subtraction, lowering the possible degree.

  9. Problem 9 A constant graph

    A student applies the rule of at most n−1n-1n−1 turning points to f(x)=−7f(x)=-7f(x)=−7, obtaining at most −1-1−1 turning points. Explain what went wrong and give the actual numbers of zeros and turning points.

    1. Hint 1

      The turning-point formula is stated for nonconstant polynomials.

    2. Hint 2

      Picture the horizontal line at height −7-7−7.

    Answer

    Zero real zeros and zero turning points; the n−1n-1n−1 bound requires a nonconstant polynomial.

    Full solution

    The function has degree zero, but the turning-point bound n−1n-1n−1 applies only when n≥1n\ge1n≥1.

    Its graph is the horizontal line y=−7y=-7y=−7.

    It never meets y=0y=0y=0 and never switches from rising to falling, so it has no real zeros and no turning points.

    Answer

    Zero real zeros and zero turning points; the n−1n-1n−1 bound requires a nonconstant polynomial.

    Key idea

    Constant polynomials need their own turning-point statement.

  10. Problem 10 A proposed conclusion

    A polynomial ppp has even positive degree and p(0)<0p(0)<0p(0)<0. A student concludes that it must have a positive real zero. Is the conclusion justified? Give a counterexample if it is false.

    1. Hint 1

      Even degree makes the ends agree but does not specify whether they rise or fall.

    2. Hint 2

      Consider a downward-opening quadratic whose values are all negative.

    Answer

    False; p(x)=−x2−1p(x)=-x^2-1p(x)=−x2−1 is a counterexample.

    Full solution

    Take

    p(x)=−x2−1p(x)=-x^2-1p(x)=−x2−1

    It has even degree 222 and p(0)=−1<0p(0)=-1<0p(0)=−1<0.

    Since x2≥0x^2\ge0x2≥0 for real xxx, p(x)≤−1p(x)\le-1p(x)≤−1, so it has no real zero at all.

    The missing information is whether the leading coefficient is positive.

    Answer

    False; p(x)=−x2−1p(x)=-x^2-1p(x)=−x2−1 is a counterexample.

    Key idea

    Parity determines whether the ends agree, while the leading coefficient determines their direction.
