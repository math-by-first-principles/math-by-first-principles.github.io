    Worked solution

    Part A

    Standard form gives the coefficients, factored form gives the zeros, so change the form. Two numbers with product −28-28−28 and sum −3-3−3 are −7-7−7 and 444.

    x2−3x−28=(x−7)(x+4)x^2 - 3x - 28 = (x - 7)(x + 4)x2−3x−28=(x−7)(x+4)

    The zero product property makes the roots x=7x = 7x=7 and x=−4x = -4x=−4, and those two numbers cut the line into the pieces x<−4x < -4x<−4, −4<x<7-4 < x < 7−4<x<7 and x>7x > 7x>7.

    Now take the factors one at a time, which is the whole reason for changing form. The factor x+4x + 4x+4 is zero at −4-4−4, negative for every xxx below −4-4−4 and positive for every xxx above it. The factor x−7x - 7x−7 is zero at 777, negative below 777 and positive above it.

    Part B

    A product of two numbers is negative exactly when the two disagree in sign, and positive exactly when they agree. Part A found them disagreeing only on the middle piece.

    Part C

    The argument is about the factors, not about the product.

    A linear factor x−rx - rx−r is zero at rrr, negative for every x<rx < rx<r and positive for every x>rx > rx>r, so it changes sign at rrr and nowhere else. Both factors of fff have that shape, so between two consecutive roots neither one can turn over.

    f(x)=(x−7)(x+4)f(x) = (x - 7)(x + 4)f(x)=(x−7)(x+4)

    Take any piece the roots cut out. It contains no root, so across that whole piece x−7x - 7x−7 holds one sign and x+4x + 4x+4 holds one sign. A product of two numbers whose signs are fixed has a fixed sign, because (+)(+)(+)(+)(+)(+) and (−)(−)(-)(-)(−)(−) are positive while (+)(−)(+)(-)(+)(−) is negative. So fff has one constant sign there, and any single interior value reveals it.

    A root is a different matter. There the product is exactly 000, which is neither positive nor negative, so it reports on neither of the pieces beside it. That is why a test value has to be taken strictly inside a piece: an endpoint describes the boundary, not the interior.

    In one line
