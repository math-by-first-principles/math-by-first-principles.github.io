    1. The map backwards, and the hypothesis it carries . Foundational, 9 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 9 pts

    Every earlier lesson in this chapter pushed from standard form toward factored form, the one of the three forms that puts a root on open display.     r12+r22=(r1+r2)2−2r1r2=36−2k3.r_1^2 + r_2^2 = (r_1 + r_2)^2 - 2 r_1 r_2 = 36 - \frac{2k}{3}.r12​+r22​=(r1​+r2​)2−2r1​r2​=36−32k​.

    Part C

    The roots are real exactly when the discriminant is not negative.

    Δ=(−18)2−4(3)k=324−12k≥0  ⟺  k≤27.\Delta = (-18)^2 - 4(3)k = 324 - 12k \ge 0 \iff k \le 27.Δ=(−18)2−4(3)k=324−12k≥0⟺k≤27.

    36−2(27)3=36−18=18.36 - \frac{2(27)}{3} = 36 - 18 = 18.36−32(27)​=36−18=18.

    So r12+r22≥18r_1^2 + r_2^2 \ge 18r12​+r22​≥18 for every kkk that leaves the roots real, and 181818 is actually reached: at k=27k = 27k=27 we have 3x2−18x+27=3(x−3)23x^2 - 18x + 27 = 3(x - 3)^23x2−18x+27=3(x−3)2, the two roots coincide at 333, and 9+9=189 + 9 = 189+9=18.

The bound comes from the discriminant, not from the expression, which is the same point the lesson makes when it insists that not every sum and product come from a real pair.

    Another way: Split the sum of squares into a midpoint part and a spread part

    For any two numbers, r12+r22=12[(r1+r2)2+(r1−r2)2]r_1^2 + r_2^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\left[(r_1 + r_2)^2 + (r_1 - r_2)^2\right]r12​+r22​=21​[(r1​+r2​)2+(r1​−r2​)2].
