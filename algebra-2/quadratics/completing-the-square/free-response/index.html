  1. Problem 1 An unfinished identity

    Find ppp and qqq so that x2−3x=(x+p)2+qx^2-3x=(x+p)^2+qx2−3x=(x+p)2+q for every real xxx.

    1. Hint 1

      Expand the square and compare the coefficient of xxx.

    2. Hint 2

      After matching the linear term, make the constant terms match as well.

    Answer

    p=−32p=-\frac32p=−23​, q=−94q=-\frac94q=−49​.

    Full solution

    Expansion gives x2+2px+p2+qx^2+2px+p^2+qx2+2px+p2+q.

    Matching terms yields

    2p=−3p=−32p2+q=0q=−94\begin{aligned} 2p&=-3 \\ p&=-\frac32 \\ p^2+q&=0 \\ q&=-\frac94 \end{aligned}2ppp2+qq​=−3=−23​=0=−49​​

    Expanding with these values returns x2−3xx^2-3xx2−3x.

    Answer

    p=−32p=-\frac32p=−23​, q=−94q=-\frac94q=−49​.

    Key idea

    Matching the linear and constant coefficients determines the completed square.

  2. Problem 2 A negative multiplier

    Write −4x2+8x+1-4x^2+8x+1−4x2+8x+1 in vertex form.

    1. Hint 1

      Take the leading coefficient out of the variable terms before completing the square.

    2. Hint 2

      Any constant added inside that factor also receives its multiplier.

    Answer

    −4(x−1)2+5-4(x-1)^2+5−4(x−1)2+5.

    Full solution

    Factor −4-4−4 from the variable terms, then complete the square: half of −2-2−2 is −1-1−1, and (−1)2=1(-1)^2=1(−1)2=1.

    f(x)=−4(x2−2x)+1f(x)=−4((x−1)2−1)+1f(x)=−4(x−1)2+5\begin{aligned} f(x)&=-4(x^2-2x)+1 \\ f(x)&=-4((x-1)^2-1)+1 \\ f(x)&=-4(x-1)^2+5 \end{aligned}f(x)f(x)f(x)​=−4(x2−2x)+1=−4((x−1)2−1)+1=−4(x−1)2+5​

    The −1-1−1 inside the bracket becomes +4+4+4 outside.

    Expanding the final form gives −4x2+8x+1-4x^2+8x+1−4x2+8x+1.

    Answer

    −4(x−1)2+5-4(x-1)^2+5−4(x−1)2+5.

    Key idea

    Distribute a negative leading coefficient to the square and the compensating constant.

  3. Problem 3 One isolated square

    Solve 5−(x+12)2=25-(x+\frac12)^2=25−(x+21​)2=2 over the real numbers.

    1. Hint 1

      First leave the squared expression alone on one side.

    2. Hint 2

      A positive square value permits two opposite square roots.

    Answer

    x=−12±3x=-\frac12\pm\sqrt3x=−21​±3​.

    Full solution

    Isolate the square by subtracting 555 from both sides, then multiplying by −1-1−1.

    (x+12)2=3x+12=±3x=−12±3\begin{aligned} (x+\tfrac12)^2&=3 \\ x+\tfrac12&=\pm\sqrt3 \\ x&=-\tfrac12\pm\sqrt3 \end{aligned}(x+21​)2x+21​x​=3=±3​=−21​±3​​

    For either value, the square is 333, so the original left side is 5−3=25-3=25−3=2.

    Answer

    x=−12±3x=-\frac12\pm\sqrt3x=−21​±3​.

    Key idea

    Isolating a positive square requires keeping both signs when recovering its base.

  4. Problem 4 Matching two outputs

    Find all real ttt for which 3t2+6t+23t^2+6t+23t2+6t+2 equals 555, by completing the square.

    1. Hint 1

      Move the target output before rewriting the variable terms.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide by the leading coefficient so the square is easy to complete.

    Answer

    t=−1±2t=-1\pm\sqrt2t=−1±2​.

    Full solution

    The equation becomes

    3t2+6t−3=0.3t^2+6t-3=0.3t2+6t−3=0.

    Divide by 333 to get t2+2t=1t^2+2t=1t2+2t=1, then complete the square: half of 222 is 111, and 12=11^2=112=1, so add 111 to both sides.

    t2+2t=1(t+1)2=2t=−1±2\begin{aligned} t^2+2t&=1 \\ (t+1)^2&=2 \\ t&=-1\pm\sqrt2 \end{aligned}t2+2t(t+1)2t​=1=2=−1±2​​

    The original expression is 3(t+1)2−13(t+1)^2-13(t+1)2−1, which equals 555 for either candidate.

    Answer

    t=−1±2t=-1\pm\sqrt2t=−1±2​.

    Key idea

    An output-matching problem becomes a completed square after subtracting the target.

  5. Problem 5 A model’s lowest cost

    A model assigns cost C(q)=2q2−6q+11C(q)=2q^2-6q+11C(q)=2q2−6q+11 dollars to a real setting q≥0q\ge0q≥0. Find the smallest cost and the setting that attains it.

    1. Hint 1

      Rewrite the rule so its varying part is a positive multiple of a square.

    2. Hint 2

      Check that the setting making the square zero belongs to the domain.

    Answer

    Minimum cost 132\frac{13}{2}213​ dollars at q=32q=\frac32q=23​.

    Full solution

    Factor 222 from the variable terms, then add 94\tfrac9449​ inside to complete the square, compensating by subtracting 2⋅94=922\cdot\tfrac94=\tfrac922⋅49​=29​ outside.

    C(q)=2(q2−3q)+11=2(q2−3q+94)−92+11=2(q−32)2+132\begin{aligned} C(q)&=2(q^2-3q)+11 \\ &=2\left(q^2-3q+\tfrac94\right)-\tfrac92+11 \\ &=2\left(q-\tfrac32\right)^2+\tfrac{13}{2} \end{aligned}C(q)​=2(q2−3q)+11=2(q2−3q+49​)−29​+11=2(q−23​)2+213​​

    The square is at least zero and is zero when q=3/2q=3/2q=3/2, an allowed setting.

    Thus the minimum is 13/213/213/2 dollars.

    Direct substitution gives

    9/2−9+11=13/2.9/2-9+11=13/2.9/2−9+11=13/2.

    Answer

    Minimum cost 132\frac{13}{2}213​ dollars at q=32q=\frac32q=23​.

    Key idea

    A positive square identifies an unconstrained minimum that must still satisfy the model’s domain.

  6. Problem 6 Restoring an old rule

    A notebook shows f(x)=3[(x+5)2−25]+2f(x)=3[(x+5)^2-25]+2f(x)=3[(x+5)2−25]+2. Write it in vertex form, then give the vertex.

    1. Hint 1

      The outside multiplier acts on both entries inside the bracket.

    2. Hint 2

      Combine the resulting constants outside the square.

    Answer

    f(x)=3(x+5)2−73f(x)=3(x+5)^2-73f(x)=3(x+5)2−73; vertex (−5,−73)(-5,-73)(−5,−73).

    Full solution

    Distribute the 333 across both terms inside the bracket, then combine the constants outside the square.

    f(x)=3[(x+5)2−25]+2=3(x+5)2−75+2=3(x+5)2−73\begin{aligned} f(x)&=3[(x+5)^2-25]+2 \\ &=3(x+5)^2-75+2 \\ &=3(x+5)^2-73 \end{aligned}f(x)​=3[(x+5)2−25]+2=3(x+5)2−75+2=3(x+5)2−73​

    This is vertex form, with vertex (−5,−73)(-5,-73)(−5,−73).

    Answer

    f(x)=3(x+5)2−73f(x)=3(x+5)^2-73f(x)=3(x+5)2−73; vertex (−5,−73)(-5,-73)(−5,−73).

    Key idea

    Distributing a leading coefficient across a completed square must reach every term inside it, including the subtracted constant.

  7. Problem 7 A family with a parameter

    For real bbb, write f(x)=2x2+bx+1f(x)=2x^2+bx+1f(x)=2x2+bx+1 in vertex form and express the vertex coordinates in terms of bbb.

    1. Hint 1

      Factor 2 out of the variable terms.

    2. Hint 2

      Half of the inside coefficient is b/4b/4b/4; add and subtract its square, b2/16b^2/16b2/16.

    Answer

    f(x)=2(x+b4)2+1−b28f(x)=2(x+\frac b4)^2+1-\frac{b^2}{8}f(x)=2(x+4b​)2+1−8b2​; vertex (−b4,1−b28)(-\frac b4,1-\frac{b^2}{8})(−4b​,1−8b2​).

    Full solution

    Inside the leading factor, the linear coefficient is b/2b/2b/2, so half of it is b/4b/4b/4, and its square is b2/16b^2/16b2/16.

    Add and subtract b2/16b^2/16b2/16 inside the parentheses.

    f(x)=2(x2+b2x+b216−b216)+1f(x)=2((x+b4)2−b216)+1\begin{aligned} f(x)&=2\left(x^2+\tfrac b2x+\tfrac{b^2}{16}-\tfrac{b^2}{16}\right)+1 \\ f(x)&=2\left(\left(x+\tfrac b4\right)^2-\tfrac{b^2}{16}\right)+1 \end{aligned}f(x)f(x)​=2(x2+2b​x+16b2​−16b2​)+1=2((x+4b​)2−16b2​)+1​

    Distribute the 222 across both terms inside the outer parentheses; the subtracted term becomes b2/8b^2/8b2/8.

    f(x)=2(x+b4)2+1−b28f(x)=2\left(x+\tfrac b4\right)^2+1-\tfrac{b^2}{8}f(x)=2(x+4b​)2+1−8b2​

    This is vertex form, with horizontal coordinate −b/4-b/4−b/4 and height 1−b2/81-b^2/81−b2/8.

    Answer

    f(x)=2(x+b4)2+1−b28f(x)=2(x+\frac b4)^2+1-\frac{b^2}{8}f(x)=2(x+4b​)2+1−8b2​; vertex (−b4,1−b28)(-\frac b4,1-\frac{b^2}{8})(−4b​,1−8b2​).

    Key idea

    Completing the square works with parameter coefficients as well as numbers.

  8. Problem 8 A proposed square form

    A student rewrites 3x2−6x+83x^2-6x+83x2−6x+8 as 3(x−1)2+73(x-1)^2+73(x−1)2+7, saying the added 111 was subtracted from 888. Find the error and correct the vertex form.

    1. Hint 1

      The added completing constant sits inside a factor of 3.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand the proposed form to see how much its constant differs from the original.

    Answer

    Correct form: 3(x−1)2+53(x-1)^2+53(x−1)2+5; the added inside 111 contributes 333 outside.

    Full solution

    The variable terms are 3(x2−2x)3(x^2-2x)3(x2−2x).

    Adding 111 inside increases the whole expression by 333, so subtract 333 outside.

    f(x)=3(x2−2x+1)+8−3f(x)=3(x−1)2+5\begin{aligned} f(x)&=3(x^2-2x+1)+8-3 \\ f(x)&=3(x-1)^2+5 \end{aligned}f(x)f(x)​=3(x2−2x+1)+8−3=3(x−1)2+5​

    The proposed expression expands to 3x2−6x+103x^2-6x+103x2−6x+10, so it was too large by 222.

    Answer

    Correct form: 3(x−1)2+53(x-1)^2+53(x−1)2+5; the added inside 111 contributes 333 outside.

    Key idea

    The amount compensated outside equals the inside completing constant times the leading coefficient.

  9. Problem 9 A parameterized minimum

    For real bbb, the expression x2+bx+b2/4+2x^2+bx+b^2/4+2x2+bx+b2/4+2 is claimed to have minimum 222. Is this true for every bbb? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      The first three terms may make a complete square already.

    2. Hint 2

      Check whether its base can equal zero for each real parameter.

    Answer

    Yes; its minimum is 222 at x=−b/2x=-b/2x=−b/2.

    Full solution

    The expression rewrites exactly as

    (x+b2)2+2(x+\tfrac b2)^2+2(x+2b​)2+2

    The square is at least zero for every real input.

    It reaches zero at x=−b/2x=-b/2x=−b/2, which is real for every real bbb.

    The claimed minimum is therefore attained in every case.

    Answer

    Yes; its minimum is 222 at x=−b/2x=-b/2x=−b/2.

    Key idea

    A real shift of a square changes the minimizing input but not its added constant minimum.

  10. Problem 10 Counting solutions in a family

    For which real ccc does 2x2−10x+c=02x^2-10x+c=02x2−10x+c=0 have two real solutions, exactly one, or none? Give the answer for each case.

    1. Hint 1

      Divide by the leading coefficient before completing the square.

    2. Hint 2

      The number of solutions depends on the sign of the constant once the square is isolated.

    Answer

    Two solutions when c<252c<\frac{25}{2}c<225​; exactly one when c=252c=\frac{25}{2}c=225​; none when c>252c>\frac{25}{2}c>225​.

    Full solution

    Divide by the leading coefficient, then complete the square: half of −5-5−5 is −52-\tfrac52−25​, and (−52)2=254\left(-\tfrac52\right)^2=\tfrac{25}{4}(−25​)2=425​.

    x2−5x+c2=0(x−52)2=254−c2\begin{aligned} x^2-5x+\tfrac c2&=0 \\ \left(x-\tfrac52\right)^2&=\tfrac{25}{4}-\tfrac c2 \end{aligned}x2−5x+2c​(x−25​)2​=0=425​−2c​​

    If 254−c2>0\tfrac{25}{4}-\tfrac c2>0425​−2c​>0, that is c<252c<\tfrac{25}{2}c<225​, there are two real solutions.

    If 254−c2=0\tfrac{25}{4}-\tfrac c2=0425​−2c​=0, that is c=252c=\tfrac{25}{2}c=225​, there is exactly one.

    If 254−c2<0\tfrac{25}{4}-\tfrac c2<0425​−2c​<0, that is c>252c>\tfrac{25}{2}c>225​, no real number squares to a negative value, so there is no real solution.

    Answer

    Two solutions when c<252c<\frac{25}{2}c<225​; exactly one when c=252c=\frac{25}{2}c=225​; none when c>252c>\frac{25}{2}c>225​.

    Key idea

    The number of real solutions of a quadratic with a nonzero leading coefficient turns on the sign of the constant left over after completing the square.
