  1. Problem 1 Two shifted expressions

    Solve (x+2)(x−3)=2(x+2)(x+2)(x-3)=2(x+2)(x+2)(x−3)=2(x+2) over the real numbers.

    1. Hint 1

      Keep possible zero factors instead of canceling them.

    2. Hint 2

      Move both sides together and take out their shared factor.

    Answer

    x=−2x=-2x=−2 or x=5x=5x=5.

    Full solution

    Subtract the right side and take out x+2x+2x+2.

    (x+2)(x−5)=0\begin{aligned} (x+2)(x-5)&=0 \end{aligned}(x+2)(x−5)​=0​

    The Zero Product Property gives x=−2x=-2x=−2 or x=5x=5x=5.

    At −2-2−2 both original sides are zero; at 555 both are 141414.

    Answer

    x=−2x=-2x=−2 or x=5x=5x=5.

    Key idea

    Moving a shared factor to a zero product preserves the solution where that factor vanishes.

  2. Problem 2 A balance of squares

    Solve (x+1)2=(2x−1)2(x+1)^2=(2x-1)^2(x+1)2=(2x−1)2 for real xxx.

    1. Hint 1

      Move the squares to one side to create a zero expression.

    2. Hint 2

      A difference of two squares splits into their difference times their sum.

    Answer

    x=0x=0x=0 or x=2x=2x=2.

    Full solution

    The difference factors as

    (2−x)(3x)=0(2-x)(3x)=0(2−x)(3x)=0

    , so x=2x=2x=2 or x=0x=0x=0.

    At 000, both original squares are 111; at 222, both are 999.

    Answer

    x=0x=0x=0 or x=2x=2x=2.

    Key idea

    Equal squares can be compared by factoring their difference.

  3. Problem 3 An unfinished product

    The polynomial f(x)=3x2−7x−6f(x)=3x^2-7x-6f(x)=3x2−7x−6 has zero 333. Write it as (x−3)L(x)(x-3)L(x)(x−3)L(x) and find the linear expression L(x)L(x)L(x).

    1. Hint 1

      The leading term of the missing factor must produce the squared coefficient.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand (x−3)(3x+d)(x-3)(3x+d)(x−3)(3x+d) and match a remaining coefficient.

    Answer

    L(x)=3x+2L(x)=3x+2L(x)=3x+2.

    Full solution

    The leading coefficient forces L(x)=3x+dL(x)=3x+dL(x)=3x+d.

    Expansion gives 3x2+(d−9)x−3d3x^2+(d-9)x-3d3x2+(d−9)x−3d.

    Matching the linear coefficient gives

    d−9=−7d-9=-7d−9=−7

    Hence d=2d=2d=2.

    The constant also checks, since −3d=−6-3d=-6−3d=−6.

    Answer

    L(x)=3x+2L(x)=3x+2L(x)=3x+2.

    Key idea

    A known zero gives one factor, and coefficient matching recovers the other.

  4. Problem 4 A panel border

    A square panel has side length xxx cm, where x>0x>0x>0. A border adds 1 cm along each edge, making the outer square’s side x+2x+2x+2 cm. The outer area is four times the panel area. Find the panel’s side length.

    1. Hint 1

      Translate the two areas before moving all terms to one side.

    2. Hint 2

      Factor the difference of the two squared expressions, then apply the positive-length condition.

    Answer

    x=2x=2x=2 cm.

    Full solution

    The area condition is

    (x+2)2=4x2.(x+2)^2=4x^2.(x+2)2=4x2.

    Subtract the right side and factor the difference of squares.

    (2−x)(3x+2)=0(2-x)(3x+2)=0(2−x)(3x+2)=0

    The factors give x=2x=2x=2 or x=−2/3x=-2/3x=−2/3.

    Only the positive value is a side length.

    At x=2x=2x=2 cm, the panel area is 444 square cm and the outer area is 161616 square cm, exactly four times as large.

    Answer

    x=2x=2x=2 cm.

    Key idea

    A factored area equation can give an algebraic root that the positive-length condition excludes.

  5. Problem 5 A horizontal crossing

    Find every intersection of y=x2+3x−4y=x^2+3x-4y=x2+3x−4 with the horizontal line y=6y=6y=6. Give each point as an ordered pair.

    1. Hint 1

      At an intersection, the two formulas for the height agree.

    2. Hint 2

      Move the target height across before factoring.

    Answer

    (−5,6)(-5,6)(−5,6) and (2,6)(2,6)(2,6).

    Full solution

    Set the quadratic output equal to 666.

    x2+3x−10=0(x+5)(x−2)=0\begin{aligned} x^2+3x-10&=0 \\ (x+5)(x-2)&=0 \end{aligned}x2+3x−10(x+5)(x−2)​=0=0​

    The roots are −5-5−5 and 222, each at height 666.

    Checking gives 25−15−4=625-15-4=625−15−4=6 and 4+6−4=64+6-4=64+6−4=6.

    These are zeros of the shifted function f(x)−6f(x)-6f(x)−6, not zeros of fff itself.

    Answer

    (−5,6)(-5,6)(−5,6) and (2,6)(2,6)(2,6).

    Key idea

    A horizontal intersection becomes a zero after the target height is subtracted.

  6. Problem 6 A restricted input range

    Find all xxx in 0≤x≤40\le x\le40≤x≤4 satisfying 3x(x−2)=x(x+2)3x(x-2)=x(x+2)3x(x−2)=x(x+2).

    1. Hint 1

      Find all algebraic candidates before applying the input interval.

    2. Hint 2

      Bring the products together without dividing by xxx.

    Answer

    x=0x=0x=0 or x=4x=4x=4.

    Full solution

    Move the right product to the left.

    2x2−8x=02x(x−4)=0\begin{aligned} 2x^2-8x&=0 \\ 2x(x-4)&=0 \end{aligned}2x2−8x2x(x−4)​=0=0​

    The roots are 000 and 444, both in the allowed closed interval.

  7. Problem 7 Recovering a rule

    A quadratic has leading coefficient 222, constant term −8-8−8, and zero 222. Find its other zero and its standard form.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the known zero to start a factorization.

    2. Hint 2

      The leading and constant terms determine the entries of the remaining linear factor.

    Answer

    Other zero −2-2−2; f(x)=2x2−8f(x)=2x^2-8f(x)=2x2−8.

    Full solution

    The known zero gives

    f(x)=(x−2)(2x+d).f(x)=(x-2)(2x+d).f(x)=(x−2)(2x+d).

    The constant condition is −2d=−8-2d=-8−2d=−8, so d=4d=4d=4.

    Thus

    f(x)=2(x−2)(x+2)f(x)=2(x-2)(x+2)f(x)=2(x−2)(x+2)

    The other zero is −2-2−2, and expansion gives 2x2−82x^2-82x2−8.

    Both zeros make the rule vanish.

    Answer

    Other zero −2-2−2; f(x)=2x2−8f(x)=2x^2-8f(x)=2x2−8.

    Key idea

    The leading coefficient and constant can finish a factorization started from a known zero.

  8. Problem 8 Building from one zero and an intercept

    A quadratic function has x=1x=1x=1 as a zero and yyy-intercept −6-6−6. Give two different quadratics, each in factored form with integer coefficients, that satisfy both conditions.

    1. Hint 1

      A zero at 111 gives one linear factor directly.

    2. Hint 2

      The yyy-intercept fixes the product of the leading coefficient and rrr, the number subtracted in the second factor.

    Answer

    For example, (x−1)(x+6)(x-1)(x+6)(x−1)(x+6) and 2(x−1)(x+3)2(x-1)(x+3)2(x−1)(x+3) (other correct pairs exist).

    Full solution

    A zero at x=1x=1x=1 gives the factor (x−1)(x-1)(x−1), so f(x)=a(x−1)(x−r)f(x)=a(x-1)(x-r)f(x)=a(x−1)(x−r) for a nonzero constant aaa and a constant rrr.

    The yyy-intercept means f(0)=−6f(0)=-6f(0)=−6.

    Since f(0)=a(−1)(−r)f(0)=a(-1)(-r)f(0)=a(−1)(−r), this gives ar=−6ar=-6ar=−6.

    Any integer factor pair of −6-6−6 works: a=1,r=−6a=1,r=-6a=1,r=−6 gives (x−1)(x+6)(x-1)(x+6)(x−1)(x+6).

    Substituting x=0x=0x=0 gives f(0)=(−1)(6)f(0)=(-1)(6)f(0)=(−1)(6), which is −6-6−6.

    Likewise a=2,r=−3a=2,r=-3a=2,r=−3 gives 2(x−1)(x+3)2(x-1)(x+3)2(x−1)(x+3); substituting x=0x=0x=0 gives f(0)=2(−1)(3)f(0)=2(-1)(3)f(0)=2(−1)(3), which is −6-6−6.

    Both are valid, and other pairs exist.

    Answer

    For example, (x−1)(x+6)(x-1)(x+6)(x−1)(x+6) and 2(x−1)(x+3)2(x-1)(x+3)2(x−1)(x+3) (other correct pairs exist).

    Key idea

    One zero and the yyy-intercept fix a family of quadratics, not a single one, since the leading coefficient and the other zero can trade off.

  9. Problem 9 A limited search

    A student tests every integer from −10-10−10 through 101010 in x2=14x^2=\frac14x2=41​ and finds no root. They conclude there are no real roots. Is this justified? Explain and give the real roots.

    1. Hint 1

      The list searched contains only integers.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the equation as a difference of squares using the square root of the constant.

    Answer

    No; x=−12x=-\frac12x=−21​ or x=12x=\frac12x=21​.

    Full solution

    An integer search says nothing about noninteger candidates.

    Here

    (x−12)(x+12)=0(x-\tfrac12)(x+\tfrac12)=0(x−21​)(x+21​)=0

    so the roots are 1/21/21/2 and −1/2-1/2−1/2, both real but absent from the searched list.

    Each has square 1/41/41/4.

    Answer

    No; x=−12x=-\frac12x=−21​ or x=12x=\frac12x=21​.

    Key idea

    A failed search excludes its tested candidates, not the rest of the stated domain.

  10. Problem 10 A marked point on a parabola

    The figure shows a parabola with an unspecified leading coefficient. Using only what the figure shows, determine whether the factor (x+2)(x+2)(x+2) appears once or twice in a factored form for fff, and find the sign of the leading coefficient. Explain your reasoning.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the horizontal axis running from negative 5 to 1 and the vertical axis running from negative 5 to 1, gridlines and number labels at every whole number. A single smooth curve rises from the lower left, touches the horizontal axis at exactly one marked point, negative 2 comma 0, and then descends back down to the lower right, staying at or below the horizontal axis everywhere. No equation and no other point is shown.

    1. Hint 1

      Notice whether the curve crosses to the other side of the axis at the marked point, or stays on the same side.

    2. Hint 2

      A repeated factor keeps the same sign on the two sides of its zero.

    Answer

    (x+2)(x+2)(x+2) appears twice; f(x)=a(x+2)2f(x)=a(x+2)^2f(x)=a(x+2)2 with a<0a<0a<0.

    Full solution

    The curve touches the axis at exactly one point, (−2,0)(-2,0)(−2,0), and does not cross there: it lies on the same side (below the axis) on both sides of the contact point.

    A quadratic that touches the axis at its vertex without crossing has a repeated zero there, so

    f(x)=a(x+2)2f(x)=a(x+2)^2f(x)=a(x+2)2

    and the factor x+2x+2x+2 occurs twice.

    The square is nonnegative everywhere, and the graph stays at or below the axis, so the leading coefficient must be negative: a<0a<0a<0.

    Answer

    (x+2)(x+2)(x+2) appears twice; f(x)=a(x+2)2f(x)=a(x+2)^2f(x)=a(x+2)2 with a<0a<0a<0.

    Key idea

    A repeated zero makes a quadratic touch the axis without changing sign.
