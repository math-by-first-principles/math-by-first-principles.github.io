  1. Problem 1 A rewritten rule

    Classify f(x)=(x+2)2−x(x+4)+7f(x)=(x+2)^2-x(x+4)+7f(x)=(x+2)2−x(x+4)+7, once simplified, as quadratic, linear, or constant.

    1. Hint 1

      The visible square does not decide the degree before like terms are combined.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand both products and compare their variable terms.

    Answer

    Constant; f(x)=11f(x)=11f(x)=11.

    Full solution

    f(x)=x2+4x+4−x2−4x+7f(x)=11\begin{aligned} f(x)&=x^2+4x+4-x^2-4x+7 \\ f(x)&=11 \end{aligned}f(x)f(x)​=x2+4x+4−x2−4x+7=11​

    The squared and linear terms cancel, so the leading quadratic coefficient is zero.

    Its graph is the horizontal line y=11y=11y=11, not a parabola.

    Answer

    Constant; f(x)=11f(x)=11f(x)=11.

    Key idea

    A quadratic needs a nonzero squared term after simplification.

  2. Problem 2 Where a rule begins

    Find the yyy-intercept of g(x)=4−x(3x+2)g(x)=4-x(3x+2)g(x)=4−x(3x+2).

    1. Hint 1

      An intercept on the vertical axis has input zero.

    2. Hint 2

      Evaluate the rule before expanding if that is shorter.

    Answer

    (0,4)(0,4)(0,4).

    Full solution

    The yyy-axis has x=0x=0x=0.

    g(0)=4−0(3(0)+2)g(0)=4-0(3(0)+2)g(0)=4−0(3(0)+2)

    This gives g(0)=4g(0)=4g(0)=4, so the intercept is (0,4)(0,4)(0,4).

    Answer

    (0,4)(0,4)(0,4).

    Key idea

    The vertical intercept is the output at input zero.

  3. Problem 3 A parent point moves

    The parent parabola y=x2y=x^2y=x2 is reflected across the xxx-axis, stretched vertically by 3, then shifted 2 units right and 1 unit down. Where does its point (−1,1)(-1,1)(−1,1) move?

    1. Hint 1

      Track the horizontal coordinate separately from the output changes.

    2. Hint 2

      Apply the listed output operations in order, then the horizontal shift.

    Answer

    (1,−4)(1,-4)(1,−4).

    Full solution

    The input moves from −1-1−1 to −1+2=1-1+2=1−1+2=1.

    The output is reflected and scaled, then lowered.

    y=−3(1)−1y=-3(1)-1y=−3(1)−1

    Hence the image is (1,−4)(1,-4)(1,−4).

    It checks in the transformed rule

    y=−3(x−2)2−1.y=-3(x-2)^2-1.y=−3(x−2)2−1.

    Answer

    (1,−4)(1,-4)(1,−4).

    Key idea

    Horizontal and output transformations act on different coordinates of a graph point.

  4. Problem 4 A curve on the grid

    The figure shows a quadratic function fff. Write fff in vertex and standard form, and state its range.

    Since a>0a>0a>0, the minimum is −9-9−9, so the range is [−9,∞)[-9,\infty)[−9,∞).

    Answer

    Vertex form (x+1)2−9(x+1)^2-9(x+1)2−9; standard form x2+2x−8x^2+2x-8x2+2x−8; range [−9,∞)[-9,\infty)[−9,∞).

    A vertex and one other point determine the coefficient needed to recover a quadratic rule.

  5. Problem 5 Moving a minimum

    The function f(x)=2(x−1)2+3f(x)=2(x-1)^2+3f(x)=2(x−1)2+3 is changed to g(x)=f(x+4)−5g(x)=f(x+4)-5g(x)=f(x+4)−5. Find the vertex, opening direction, and range of ggg.

    1. Hint 1

      Substitute the whole new input into the original rule.

    2. Hint 2

      Combine the horizontal and vertical shifts before reading the vertex.

    Answer

    Vertex (−3,−2)(-3,-2)(−3,−2); opens upward; range [−2,∞)[-2,\infty)[−2,∞).

    Full solution

    Substitute x+4x+4x+4 for the original input.

    g(x)=2(x+4−1)2+3−5g(x)=2(x+3)2−2\begin{aligned} g(x)&=2(x+4-1)^2+3-5 \\ g(x)&=2(x+3)^2-2 \end{aligned}g(x)g(x)​=2(x+4−1)2+3−5=2(x+3)2−2​

    The vertex is (−3,−2)(-3,-2)(−3,−2).

    The positive multiplier makes it the minimum; all outputs are at least −2-2−2.

    Answer

    Vertex (−3,−2)(-3,-2)(−3,−2); opens upward; range [−2,∞)[-2,\infty)[−2,∞).

    Key idea

    Substituting a shifted input into vertex form reveals the combined movement.

  6. Problem 6 Two labels for one rule

    A quadratic is given in factored form as (x+4)(x−10)(x+4)(x-10)(x+4)(x−10). Find its standard form ax2+bx+cax^2+bx+cax2+bx+c and its vertex form (x−h)2+k(x-h)^2+k(x−h)2+k.

    1. Hint 1

      Expand the factored form to reach standard form.

    2. Hint 2

      The axis sits halfway between the zeros; evaluate there for the vertex height.

    Answer

    x2−6x−40x^2-6x-40x2−6x−40; vertex form (x−3)2−49(x-3)^2-49(x−3)2−49.

    Full solution

    Expand the factored form.

    (x+4)(x−10)=x2−6x−40(x+4)(x-10)=x^2-6x-40(x+4)(x−10)=x2−6x−40

    The zeros are −4-4−4 and 101010, so the axis is their midpoint, x=3x=3x=3.

    Evaluate the factored form there.

    f(3)=(3+4)(3−10)f(3)=7(−7)f(3)=−49\begin{aligned} f(3)&=(3+4)(3-10) \\ f(3)&=7(-7) \\ f(3)&=-49 \end{aligned}f(3)f(3)f(3)​=(3+4)(3−10)=7(−7)=−49​

    So the vertex form is (x−3)2−49(x-3)^2-49(x−3)2−49.

    Answer

    x2−6x−40x^2-6x-40x2−6x−40; vertex form (x−3)2−49(x-3)^2-49(x−3)2−49.

    Key idea

    The axis is the midpoint of the zeros, and evaluating there gives the vertex height directly from factored form.

  7. Problem 7 Reading the intercept and the ends

    For f(x)=2x2−3x−6f(x)=2x^2-3x-6f(x)=2x2−3x−6, give the yyy-intercept and explain the end behavior as xxx grows large positively or negatively.

    1. Hint 1

      The constant term sets the output at zero.

    2. Hint 2

      For large nonzero inputs, factor out the highest power.

    Answer

    yyy-intercept (0,−6)(0,-6)(0,−6); both ends rise without bound.

    Full solution

    The intercept is f(0)=−6f(0)=-6f(0)=−6.

    For x≠0x\ne0x=0,

    f(x)=x2(2−3x−6x2)f(x)=x^2\left(2-\tfrac{3}{x}-\tfrac{6}{x^2}\right)f(x)=x2(2−x3​−x26​)

    The bracket approaches positive 222 as the magnitude of xxx grows.

    The squared factor grows positively in both directions, so the outputs rise without bound at both ends.

    Answer

    Key idea

    The leading coefficient controls distant behavior even when the vertical intercept is negative.

  8. Problem 8 Two equal readings

    A quadratic satisfies f(−2)=f(6)=9f(-2)=f(6)=9f(−2)=f(6)=9. A student locates its axis at x=4x=4x=4 by taking half the distance between the inputs. Is that correct? Explain and locate the axis.

    1. Hint 1

      A distance and a coordinate are different quantities.

    2. Hint 2

      Locate the midpoint of the two equal-height inputs.

    Answer

    No; the axis is x=2x=2x=2.

    Full solution

    The distance between the inputs is 888, and half that distance is 444.

    But starting at −2-2−2 and moving 444 reaches 222.

    Equivalently,

    h=−2+62h=\frac{-2+6}{2}h=2−2+6​

    Therefore h=2h=2h=2.

    Equal-height points on a parabola are reflected across this midpoint, not across half their distance measured from zero.

    Answer

    No; the axis is x=2x=2x=2.

    Key idea

    A symmetry axis is a midpoint coordinate, not half a separation.

  9. Problem 9 Opposite inputs

    For f(x)=ax2+bx+cf(x)=ax^2+bx+cf(x)=ax2+bx+c with a≠0a\ne0a=0, a student expands f(h+1)−f(h−1)f(h+1)-f(h-1)f(h+1)−f(h−1) and gets 4ah+2b4ah+2b4ah+2b. They say a vertical symmetry axis x=hx=hx=h must therefore satisfy h=−b/(2a)h=-b/(2a)h=−b/(2a). Is this reasoning valid? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Points one unit to either side of a mirror must have equal heights.

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    Yes; any vertical symmetry axis must have h=−b2ah=-\frac{b}{2a}h=−2ab​.

    Full solution

    The squared terms differ by 4h4h4h and the linear inputs differ by 222, so the stated expansion is correct.

    Symmetry requires

    4ah+2b=02ah+b=0h=−b2a\begin{aligned} 4ah+2b&=0 \\ 2ah+b&=0 \\ h&=-\frac{b}{2a} \end{aligned}4ah+2b2ah+bh​=0=0=−2ab​​

    Division is legal because a≠0a\ne0a=0.

    This proves where a vertical symmetry axis must lie, consistently with the known parabola symmetry.

    Answer

    Yes; any vertical symmetry axis must have h=−b2ah=-\frac{b}{2a}h=−2ab​.

    Key idea

    Equal heights on opposite sides of an axis force a relation among the quadratic’s coefficients.

  10. Problem 10 A shared pair of zeros

    Two quadratics have zeros −3-3−3 and 555. One has leading coefficient 111 and the other has leading coefficient −2-2−2. Are their vertices identical? Explain and give both vertices.

    1. Hint 1

      The midpoint of the zeros fixes the horizontal coordinate for both.

    2. Hint 2

      Evaluate each factored rule at that midpoint to compare heights.

    Answer

    No; the vertices are (1,−16)(1,-16)(1,−16) and (1,32)(1,32)(1,32), respectively.

    Full solution

    The common axis is the midpoint of the zeros.

    x=−3+52x=1\begin{aligned} x&=\frac{-3+5}{2} \\ x&=1 \end{aligned}xx​=2−3+5​=1​

    The first rule is (x+3)(x−5)(x+3)(x-5)(x+3)(x−5) and the second is −2(x+3)(x−5)-2(x+3)(x-5)−2(x+3)(x−5).

    At the axis,

    f(1)=4(−4)f(1)=−16g(1)=−2(4)(−4)g(1)=32\begin{aligned} f(1)&=4(-4) \\ f(1)&=-16 \\ g(1)&=-2(4)(-4) \\ g(1)&=32 \end{aligned}f(1)f(1)g(1)g(1)​=4(−4)=−16=−2(4)(−4)=32​

    The leading coefficient changes the vertex height and opening while the axis stays fixed.

    Answer

    No; the vertices are (1,−16)(1,-16)(1,−16) and (1,32)(1,32)(1,32), respectively.

    Key idea

    Two zeros determine a common axis but leave the parabola’s vertical scale free.
