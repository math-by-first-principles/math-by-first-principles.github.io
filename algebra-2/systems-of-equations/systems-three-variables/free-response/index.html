  1. Problem 1 A full elimination sweep

    Solve the system x+y+2z=9x+y+2z=9x+y+2z=9, 2x−y+z=32x-y+z=32x−y+z=3, and x+3y−z=4x+3y-z=4x+3y−z=4 for real x,y,zx,y,zx,y,z.

    1. Hint 1

      Eliminate the same variable from the other two equations using the first equation.

    2. Hint 2

      Combine the two resulting equations to eliminate a second variable, then back-substitute.

    Answer

    (x,y,z)=(1,2,3)(x,y,z)=(1,2,3)(x,y,z)=(1,2,3).

    Full solution

    Eliminate xxx using the first equation, x=9−y−2zx=9-y-2zx=9−y−2z.

    Substituting into the other two:

    2(9−y−2z)−y+z=3−3y−3z=−15\begin{aligned} 2(9-y-2z)-y+z&=3 \\ -3y-3z&=-15 \end{aligned}2(9−y−2z)−y+z−3y−3z​=3=−15​ (9−y−2z)+3y−z=42y−3z=−5\begin{aligned} (9-y-2z)+3y-z&=4 \\ 2y-3z&=-5 \end{aligned}(9−y−2z)+3y−z2y−3z​=4=−5​

    Dividing the first reduced equation by −3-3−3 gives y+z=5y+z=5y+z=5, so y=5−zy=5-zy=5−z.

    Substituting into the second:

    2(5−z)−3z=−5z=3\begin{aligned} 2(5-z)-3z&=-5 \\ z&=3 \end{aligned}2(5−z)−3zz​=−5=3​

    Then

    y=5−3=2\begin{aligned} y&=5-3 \\ &=2 \end{aligned}y​=5−3=2​

    and back-substituting into x=9−y−2zx=9-y-2zx=9−y−2z gives

    x=9−2−6=1\begin{aligned} x&=9-2-6 \\ &=1 \end{aligned}x​=9−2−6=1​

    Answer

    (x,y,z)=(1,2,3)(x,y,z)=(1,2,3)(x,y,z)=(1,2,3).

    Key idea

    A forward elimination sweep to triangular form, then back-substitution, solves a system with no equation already isolated to one variable.

  2. Problem 2 Three recorded totals

    Determine the solution set of x+y=4x+y=4x+y=4, z=2z=2z=2, and x+y+z=9x+y+z=9x+y+z=9 over the real numbers.

    1. Hint 1

      Combine the first two facts before trying individual values.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the implied total with the last condition.

    Answer

    No solution.

    Full solution

    Adding the first two equations forces x+y+z=6x+y+z=6x+y+z=6.

    Subtract that from the third equation.

    0=30=30=3

    The conditions disagree, so no triple works.

    Answer

    No solution.

    Key idea

    A single impossible consequence makes the entire system inconsistent.

  3. Problem 3 A family of triples

    Parameterize the real solutions of z=2x−3y+1z=2x-3y+1z=2x−3y+1 using x=sx=sx=s and y=ty=ty=t.

    1. Hint 1

      The equation determines the third coordinate after the first two are chosen.

    2. Hint 2

      Keep both freely chosen letters in the third coordinate.

    Answer

    (s,t,2s−3t+1)(s,t,2s-3t+1)(s,t,2s−3t+1) for real s,ts,ts,t.

    Full solution

    The choices x=sx=sx=s and y=ty=ty=t force

    z=2s−3t+1z=2s-3t+1z=2s−3t+1

    Every real pair s,ts,ts,t supplies a solution, and every solution can be obtained by choosing its first two coordinates.

    One genuine linear equation in three variables describes a plane.

    Answer

    (s,t,2s−3t+1)(s,t,2s-3t+1)(s,t,2s−3t+1) for real s,ts,ts,t.

    Key idea

    Two freely chosen coordinates parameterize a plane in three-dimensional space.

  4. Problem 4 Three route counters

    Three counters measure real flows a,b,ca,b,ca,b,c in liters per minute. Their readings are a+b=9a+b=9a+b=9, b+c=7b+c=7b+c=7, and a+c=10a+c=10a+c=10. Find the three flows.

    1. Hint 1

      Each reading combines two flows; combine readings to isolate one flow.

    2. Hint 2

      Add the first and third readings, then subtract the second.

    Answer

    a=6a=6a=6, b=3b=3b=3, c=4c=4c=4 liters per minute.

    Full solution

    Adding the first and third readings gives (a+b)+(a+c)=2a+b+c(a+b)+(a+c)=2a+b+c(a+b)+(a+c)=2a+b+c; subtracting the second reading b+c=7b+c=7b+c=7 removes bbb and ccc, leaving 2a2a2a alone.

    2a=9+10−7a=6b=9−6c=10−6\begin{aligned} 2a&=9+10-7 \\ a&=6 \\ b&=9-6 \\ c&=10-6 \end{aligned}2aabc​=9+10−7=6=9−6=10−6​

    Thus b=3b=3b=3 and c=4c=4c=4.

    All readings check: 6+3=96+3=96+3=9, 3+4=73+4=73+4=7, and 6+4=106+4=106+4=10.

    Answer

    a=6a=6a=6, b=3b=3b=3, c=4c=4c=4 liters per minute.

    Key idea

    Pairwise totals can determine three individual quantities.

  5. Problem 5 Three intersecting conditions

    Solve x+2y=1x+2y=1x+2y=1, y+z=4y+z=4y+z=4, and 2x+3y−z=−22x+3y-z=-22x+3y−z=−2 for real x,y,zx,y,zx,y,z. Describe every solution and its geometric shape.

    1. Hint 1

      Test whether the last condition adds a restriction beyond the first two.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract twice the first equation from the third, and compare with the second.

    Answer

    (x,y,z)=(1−2t,t,4−t)(x,y,z)=(1-2t,t,4-t)(x,y,z)=(1−2t,t,4−t) for real ttt; a line.

    Full solution

    Subtracting twice the first equation from the third gives −y−z=−4-y-z=-4−y−z=−4, the negative of the second.

    The last equation is redundant.

    Set y=ty=ty=t.

    x=1−2tz=4−t\begin{aligned} x&=1-2t \\ z&=4-t \end{aligned}xz​=1−2t=4−t​

    The third left side becomes 2(1−2t)+3t−(4−t)=−22(1-2t)+3t-(4-t)=-22(1−2t)+3t−(4−t)=−2 for every ttt.

    One free parameter gives a line.

    Answer

    (x,y,z)=(1−2t,t,4−t)(x,y,z)=(1-2t,t,4-t)(x,y,z)=(1−2t,t,4−t) for real ttt; a line.

    Key idea

    A redundant equation leaves the solutions constrained by the independent equations that remain.

  6. Problem 6 A missing right side

    The system is x−z=2x-z=2x−z=2, 2y+z=52y+z=52y+z=5, and x+2y=kx+2y=kx+2y=k. Find the real kkk for which solutions exist, then describe all of them.

    1. Hint 1

      Look for a total implied by the first two conditions.

    2. Hint 2

      Add their left sides and compare with the third.

    Answer

    k=7k=7k=7; (x,y,z)=(2+t,(5−t)/2,t)(x,y,z)=(2+t,(5-t)/2,t)(x,y,z)=(2+t,(5−t)/2,t) for real ttt.

    Full solution

    Adding the first two equations gives x+2y=7x+2y=7x+2y=7, so consistency requires k=7k=7k=7.

    If k≠7k\ne7k=7, subtraction gives a false row.

    At k=7k=7k=7, let z=tz=tz=t.

    x=2+ty=5−t2\begin{aligned} x&=2+t \\ y&=\frac{5-t}{2} \end{aligned}xy​=2+t=25−t​​

    Then

    x+2y=(2+t)+(5−t)=7\begin{aligned} x+2y&=(2+t)+(5-t) \\ &=7 \end{aligned}x+2y​=(2+t)+(5−t)=7​

    confirming the final equation for all ttt.

    Answer

    k=7k=7k=7; (x,y,z)=(2+t,(5−t)/2,t)(x,y,z)=(2+t,(5-t)/2,t)(x,y,z)=(2+t,(5−t)/2,t) for real ttt.

    Key idea

    A dependent third condition must repeat the total already forced by the others.

  7. Problem 7 Plane descriptions

    Find every real solution triple (x,y,z)(x,y,z)(x,y,z) of y−2z=3y-2z=3y−2z=3, 4y−8z=124y-8z=124y−8z=12, and −y+2z=−3-y+2z=-3−y+2z=−3, where xxx is otherwise unconstrained. Explain why one free letter is insufficient to describe all solutions.

    1. Hint 1

      Find how many distinct restrictions the equations impose.

    2. Hint 2

      Check which coordinate never appears, as well as the freedom within the surviving equation.

    Answer

    (x,y,z)=(s,3+2t,t)(x,y,z)=(s,3+2t,t)(x,y,z)=(s,3+2t,t) for real s,ts,ts,t; a plane with two free variables.

    Full solution

    The second equation is four times the first and the third is its negative.

    Only y−2z=3y-2z=3y−2z=3 survives.

    Set x=sx=sx=s and z=tz=tz=t.

    y=3+2ty=3+2ty=3+2t

    Both sss and ttt vary independently, so this is a plane and a one-parameter linear family would leave out solutions.

    Answer

    (x,y,z)=(s,3+2t,t)(x,y,z)=(s,3+2t,t)(x,y,z)=(s,3+2t,t) for real s,ts,ts,t; a plane with two free variables.

    Key idea

    Count independent restrictions before deciding how many free parameters are needed.

  8. Problem 8 A proposed point

    A student says three planes with a common line can still have exactly one common point after their equations are solved more carefully. Is this possible? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      The phrase common line describes points already lying on every plane.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the size of a line with a single point.

    Answer

    No; their common solution set includes the whole line.

    Full solution

    Every point of the common line satisfies all three equations by the meaning of common.

    A line contains infinitely many points.

    Reversible elimination preserves those solutions, so it cannot reduce that set to one point.

    Answer

    No; their common solution set includes the whole line.

    Key idea

    Solving equivalent equations cannot remove points known to satisfy the entire system.

  9. Problem 9 Two endings together

    A system reduces to x−y+z=8x-y+z=8x−y+z=8, 0=00=00=0, and 0=−20=-20=−2. A student ignores the second row and concludes there is no solution. Is this conclusion justified? Explain what each numerical row means.

    1. Hint 1

      A true numerical row and a false numerical row carry different information.

    2. Hint 2

      Ask whether any triple can satisfy the false numerical row.

    Answer

    Yes; no solution. The zero row is redundant, and the other numerical row is false.

    Full solution

    The row 0=00=00=0 imposes no condition.

    But

    0=−20=-20=−2

    is false independently of x,y,zx,y,zx,y,z.

    No triple satisfies every row, so the empty-set conclusion is justified even though another row vanished.

    Answer

    Yes; no solution. The zero row is redundant, and the other numerical row is false.

    Key idea

    A false row overrides any redundant rows in a system.

  10. Problem 10 Two descriptions of a line

    Are (2+t,1−t,3t)(2+t,1-t,3t)(2+t,1−t,3t) for real ttt and (2+u/3,1−u/3,u)(2+u/3,1-u/3,u)(2+u/3,1−u/3,u) for real uuu the same solution set? Justify your answer in both directions.

    1. Hint 1

      The free letter is a label for a point, not an extra constraint.

    2. Hint 2

      Relate the parameters by equating the third coordinates.

    Answer

    Yes; they describe the same line.

    Full solution

    For any real ttt, choose u=3tu=3tu=3t.

    Substitution in the second description produces (2+t,1−t,3t)(2+t,1-t,3t)(2+t,1−t,3t).

    Conversely, given any real uuu, choose

    t=u3t=\frac{u}{3}t=3u​

    Substitution in the first description produces the second.

    Each family therefore covers all points of the other.

    Answer

    Yes; they describe the same line.

    Key idea

    Different parameter choices can describe exactly the same solution set.
