Where you stand

  • Classify a two-variable linear system as having one solution, none, or infinitely many by comparing coefficients and then constants, and choose the value of an unknown constant that forces a named case. Review: Systems in Two Variables
  • Report an infinite solution set as a family that covers every solution and nothing else, rather than as a label or a sample point. Review: Systems in Two Variables , Systems in Three Variables , Matrices and Systems of Equations
  • Sweep a three-variable system forward to triangular form, climb back up, and count the rows that still constrain to fix the shape of the solution set. Review: Systems in Three Variables
  • Read a degenerate ending: a false row empties the set whatever else vanished, a vanishing row frees a variable only when no row is false, and a zero row is not by itself a missing pivot. Review: Systems in Three Variables , Matrices and Systems of Equations
  • Build a feasible region from linear inequalities: the boundary style from the symbol, the shaded side from a test point, and the corners, discarding any boundary crossing that fails another constraint. Review: Systems of Inequalities
  • Optimize a linear objective over a feasible region, and decide from boundedness or emptiness whether the optimum being sought exists at all. Review: Systems of Inequalities
  • Encode a system as an augmented matrix, placeholder zeros included, and reduce it with the three row operations, changing every entry of a row including its constant. Review: Matrices and Systems of Equations
  • Evaluate a two by two determinant as main diagonal minus anti-diagonal, and a three by three one by cofactor expansion with alternating signs. Review: Determinants and Cramer's Rule
  • Use the coefficient determinant as the test for a unique solution, and stop at not unique when it is zero, since none and infinitely many both remain open. Review: Determinants and Cramer's Rule
