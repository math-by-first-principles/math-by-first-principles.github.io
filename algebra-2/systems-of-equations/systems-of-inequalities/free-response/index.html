  1. Problem 1 A shaded half-plane

    The figure shows a shaded half-plane. Write its defining inequality.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the horizontal axis labeled x and the vertical axis labeled y, both from -3 to 3, gridlines and number labels at every whole number, and the origin labeled 0. A dashed line runs through the labeled points (0, 1) and (1, -1), extending to the edges of the grid. The side of the line containing the origin is shaded; the origin itself is not marked. No inequality or equation is written on the figure.

    1. Hint 1

      Use two points on its boundary to find the line.

    2. Hint 2

      Use an interior point and the boundary style to choose the symbol.

    Answer

    y<−2x+1y<-2x+1y<−2x+1, or 2x+y<12x+y<12x+y<1.

    Full solution

    The boundary passes through (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) and (1,−1)(1,-1)(1,−1), giving slope −2-2−2 and line y=−2x+1y=-2x+1y=−2x+1.

    The shaded point (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) lies below this line.

    The line is dashed, so its points are excluded.

    y<−2x+1y<-2x+1y<−2x+1

    Answer

    y<−2x+1y<-2x+1y<−2x+1, or 2x+y<12x+y<12x+y<1.

    Key idea

    A boundary equation, a shaded side, and inclusion information determine a half-plane.

  2. Problem 2 A boundary choice

    On the blank coordinate grid in the figure, graph x≥−1x\ge-1x≥−1 and y<2y<2y<2 together.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the horizontal axis labeled x running from -4 to 4 and the vertical axis labeled y running from -4 to 4, gridlines and number labels at every whole number, and the origin labeled 0. No point, boundary, or shading is drawn.

    1. Hint 1

      Each condition keeps one side of a horizontal or vertical line.

    2. Hint 2

      The equality signs decide which of the two boundaries is included.

    Answer

    The overlap is right of the solid line x=−1x=-1x=−1 and below the dashed line y=2y=2y=2.

    Full solution

    The condition x≥−1x\ge-1x≥−1 includes its vertical boundary and all points to its right.

    The condition y<2y<2y<2 excludes its horizontal boundary and keeps the lower side.

    A point such as (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) passes both tests.

    Shade their overlap.

    Answer

    The overlap is right of the solid line x=−1x=-1x=−1 and below the dashed line y=2y=2y=2.

    Key idea

    A system keeps only points accepted by every inequality.

  3. Problem 3 A fixed horizontal slice

    For the system y≥x−2y\ge x-2y≥x−2 and y≤5−xy\le5-xy≤5−x, determine all real xxx allowed on the horizontal line y=1y=1y=1.

    1. Hint 1

      The chosen height turns both conditions into one-variable inequalities.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the overlap of the resulting upper bounds on xxx.

    Answer

    x≤3x\le3x≤3, or (−∞,3](-\infty,3](−∞,3].

    Full solution

    Substitute y=1y=1y=1 in both conditions.

    1≥x−21≤5−x\begin{aligned} 1&\ge x-2 \\ 1&\le5-x \end{aligned}11​≥x−2≤5−x​

    These require x≤3x\le3x≤3 and x≤4x\le4x≤4.

    Their overlap is x≤3x\le3x≤3.

    At x=3x=3x=3, both original inequalities hold.

    Answer

    x≤3x\le3x≤3, or (−∞,3](-\infty,3](−∞,3].

    Key idea

    A horizontal slice of a feasible region is found by fixing its height in every condition.

  4. Problem 4 A bounded patch

    On the blank grid in the figure, graph x≥1x\ge1x≥1, y≥0y\ge0y≥0, y≤xy\le xy≤x, and x+y≤6x+y\le6x+y≤6. Give every corner of the feasible region.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the horizontal axis labeled x running from -1 to 7 and the vertical axis labeled y running from -1 to 7, gridlines and number labels at every whole number, and the origin labeled 0. No point, boundary, or shading is drawn.

    1. Hint 1

      Intersect all four allowed sides before keeping a boundary crossing.

    2. Hint 2

      Check each crossing against the inequalities it did not come from.

    Answer

    Corners: (1,0)(1,0)(1,0), (1,1)(1,1)(1,1), (3,3)(3,3)(3,3), (6,0)(6,0)(6,0); all edges solid.

    Full solution

    The lines x=1x=1x=1 and y=0y=0y=0 give (1,0)(1,0)(1,0).

    Intersecting x=1x=1x=1 and y=xy=xy=x gives (1,1)(1,1)(1,1).

    For the slanted pair,

    x+y=6y=x2x=6\begin{aligned} x+y&=6 \\ y&=x \\ 2x&=6 \end{aligned}x+yy2x​=6=x=6​

    This gives (3,3)(3,3)(3,3).

    Finally y=0y=0y=0 and x+y=6x+y=6x+y=6 give (6,0)(6,0)(6,0).

    All pass all four conditions.

    The crossing (1,5)(1,5)(1,5) fails y≤xy\le xy≤x and is discarded, and the crossing (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) from y=0y=0y=0 and y=xy=xy=x fails x≥1x\ge1x≥1 and is also discarded.

    Every boundary includes equality, so all edges are solid.

    Answer

    Corners: (1,0)(1,0)(1,0), (1,1)(1,1)(1,1), (3,3)(3,3)(3,3), (6,0)(6,0)(6,0); all edges solid.

    Key idea

    A boundary crossing is a corner only if it satisfies the whole system.

  5. Problem 5 A cooling schedule

    A cooling system runs two modes for real durations xxx and yyy hours. It requires x≥1x\ge1x≥1, y≥1y\ge1y≥1, x+y≤5x+y\le5x+y≤5, and x≤3x\le3x≤3. Cooling output is C=2x+5yC=2x+5yC=2x+5y units. Find the greatest output and the durations achieving it.

    1. Hint 1

      The linear output can be evaluated at the corners of the allowed region.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the intersections of the four boundaries and keep the feasible ones.

    Answer

    Maximum 22 units at x=1x=1x=1 hour and y=4y=4y=4 hours.

    Full solution

    Intersect the boundaries in pairs. x=1x=1x=1 with y=1y=1y=1 gives (1,1)(1,1)(1,1); x=1x=1x=1 with x+y=5x+y=5x+y=5 gives (1,4)(1,4)(1,4); x=3x=3x=3 with y=1y=1y=1 gives (3,1)(3,1)(3,1); x=3x=3x=3 with x+y=5x+y=5x+y=5 gives (3,2)(3,2)(3,2).

    The remaining pair, y=1y=1y=1 and x+y=5x+y=5x+y=5, gives (4,1)(4,1)(4,1), which fails x≤3x\le3x≤3 and is discarded.

    The region is bounded with solid boundaries.

    Evaluate each of the four kept corners.

    C(1,1)=7C(3,1)=11C(3,2)=16C(1,4)=22\begin{aligned} C(1,1)&=7 \\ C(3,1)&=11 \\ C(3,2)&=16 \\ C(1,4)&=22 \end{aligned}C(1,1)C(3,1)C(3,2)C(1,4)​=7=11=16=22​

    The largest output is 22 units.

    The schedule (1,4)(1,4)(1,4) satisfies all four conditions.

    Answer

    Maximum 22 units at x=1x=1x=1 hour and y=4y=4y=4 hours.

    Key idea

    A linear objective on a bounded region with solid boundaries reaches its best value at a corner.

  6. Problem 6 A slanted strip

    Find the minimum and maximum of P=x−2yP=x-2yP=x−2y subject to 0≤x≤40\le x\le40≤x≤4 and x≤y≤x+2x\le y\le x+2x≤y≤x+2. State where each is attained.

    1. Hint 1

      An extremum of a linear expression over a bounded region with solid boundaries occurs at one of its corners.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the region's four corners, then evaluate PPP at each.

    Answer

    Minimum −8-8−8 at (4,6)(4,6)(4,6); maximum 000 at (0,0)(0,0)(0,0).

    Full solution

    The region is a parallelogram cut by x=0x=0x=0 and x=4x=4x=4: at x=0x=0x=0 the strip runs from y=0y=0y=0 to y=2y=2y=2, and at x=4x=4x=4 from y=4y=4y=4 to y=6y=6y=6, giving the four corners (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), (0,2)(0,2)(0,2), (4,4)(4,4)(4,4), (4,6)(4,6)(4,6).

    P(0,0)=0P(0,2)=−4P(4,4)=−4P(4,6)=−8\begin{aligned} P(0,0)&=0 \\ P(0,2)&=-4 \\ P(4,4)&=-4 \\ P(4,6)&=-8 \end{aligned}P(0,0)P(0,2)P(4,4)P(4,6)​=0=−4=−4=−8​

    The smallest value is −8-8−8 and the largest is 000.

    Both corner points satisfy all the constraints.

    Answer

    Minimum −8-8−8 at (4,6)(4,6)(4,6); maximum 000 at (0,0)(0,0)(0,0).

    Key idea

    A strip cut off at two inputs can form a bounded region with both extrema.

  7. Problem 7 Resource requirements

    A process requires real amounts x,y≥0x,y\ge0x,y≥0, together with x+2y≥8x+2y\ge8x+2y≥8, x≤2x\le2x≤2, and y≤2y\le2y≤2. Determine whether any allowed amounts exist and whether x+yx+yx+y has an optimum there.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the upper bounds to estimate the largest possible left side of the requirement.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare that bound with the required minimum.

    Answer

    The region is empty; no minimum or maximum exists.

    Full solution

    The upper bounds imply

    x+2y≤6x+2y\le6x+2y≤6

    This contradicts x+2y≥8x+2y\ge8x+2y≥8.

    Thus no point is allowed, and there is nowhere to evaluate an objective.

    Answer

    The region is empty; no minimum or maximum exists.

    Key idea

    Separate upper bounds can make a combined minimum requirement impossible.

  8. Problem 8 Points between two choices

    Two points AAA and BBB satisfy 2x+y≤92x+y\le92x+y≤9. A student claims their midpoint also satisfies it. Is this correct? Justify without assuming where the points lie.

    1. Hint 1

      The midpoint averages the two coordinates.

    2. Hint 2

      Evaluate the linear expression at the averaged coordinates.

    Answer

    Yes; the midpoint satisfies the inequality.

    Full solution

    Write A=(a,b)A=(a,b)A=(a,b) and B=(c,d)B=(c,d)B=(c,d); the hypotheses are 2a+b≤92a+b\le92a+b≤9 and 2c+d≤92c+d\le92c+d≤9.

    Their midpoint is (a+c2,b+d2)\left(\frac{a+c}2,\frac{b+d}2\right)(2a+c​,2b+d​), so twice its first coordinate plus its second coordinate is

    S=2⋅a+c2+b+d2=(a+c)+b+d2=(2a+b)+(2c+d)2\begin{aligned} S&=2\cdot\frac{a+c}2+\frac{b+d}2 \\ &=(a+c)+\frac{b+d}2 \\ &=\frac{(2a+b)+(2c+d)}2 \end{aligned}S​=2⋅2a+c​+2b+d​=(a+c)+2b+d​=2(2a+b)+(2c+d)​​

    The numerator is at most 181818, so the value is at most 999.

    Answer

    Yes; the midpoint satisfies the inequality.

    Key idea

    Averaging two accepted points preserves a linear inequality.

  9. Problem 9 A claimed maximum

    For 0≤x≤20\le x\le20≤x≤2 and 0≤y<30\le y<30≤y<3, a student says P=x+yP=x+yP=x+y has a maximum of 555 at (2,3)(2,3)(2,3). Is this correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Check whether the proposed maximizing point belongs to the region.

    2. Hint 2

      For any allowed height, consider a slightly larger height still below the boundary.

    Answer

    No; no maximum exists, although PPP approaches 555.

    Full solution

    The point (2,3)(2,3)(2,3) is excluded because y<3y<3y<3.

    Every allowed point has x+y<5x+y<5x+y<5.

    At x=2x=2x=2, values of yyy can approach 333 from below, so PPP approaches 555.

    For any allowed yyy, the new height (y+3)/2(y+3)/2(y+3)/2 is larger and still below 333, increasing PPP.

    Answer

    No; no maximum exists, although PPP approaches 555.

    Key idea

    A missing boundary can prevent a bounded objective from attaining its upper bound.

  10. Problem 10 An unbounded direction

    The region is x≥0x\ge0x≥0 and −1≤y≤2-1\le y\le2−1≤y≤2. Does P=yP=yP=y have both a maximum and a minimum even though the region is unbounded? Explain and describe where they occur.

    1. Hint 1

      Consider which coordinate direction is unbounded, and whether the objective depends on that direction.

    2. Hint 2

      Check which coordinate the objective actually measures.

    Answer

    Yes; minimum −1-1−1 at (x,−1)(x,-1)(x,−1) and maximum 222 at (x,2)(x,2)(x,2), for all x≥0x\ge0x≥0.

    Full solution

    The region extends indefinitely as xxx increases.

    However,

    P≥−1P≤2\begin{aligned} P&\ge-1 \\ P&\le2 \end{aligned}PP​≥−1≤2​

    Both bounding heights are included.

    Every point (x,−1)(x,-1)(x,−1) with x≥0x\ge0x≥0 attains the minimum, and every point (x,2)(x,2)(x,2) attains the maximum.

    Answer

    Yes; minimum −1-1−1 at (x,−1)(x,-1)(x,−1) and maximum 222 at (x,2)(x,2)(x,2), for all x≥0x\ge0x≥0.

    Key idea

    An unbounded region can still bound an objective that ignores its unbounded direction.
