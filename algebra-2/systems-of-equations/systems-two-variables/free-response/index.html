  1. Problem 1 Two shifted quantities

    Find the real pair (u,v)(u,v)(u,v) satisfying 2(u−1)=v+32(u-1)=v+32(u−1)=v+3 and u+v=7u+v=7u+v=7.

    1. Hint 1

      Rewrite the first condition so that its variable terms are together.

    2. Hint 2

      Add the two conditions to eliminate one variable.

    Answer

    (u,v)=(4,3)(u,v)=(4,3)(u,v)=(4,3).

    Full solution

    The first condition gives 2u−v=52u-v=52u−v=5.

    Add it to the sum condition.

    3u=12u=4v=7−4\begin{aligned} 3u&=12 \\ u&=4 \\ v&=7-4 \end{aligned}3uuv​=12=4=7−4​

    Thus v=3v=3v=3.

    Check: 2(4−1)=62(4-1)=62(4−1)=6 and 3+3=63+3=63+3=6, and 4+3=74+3=74+3=7.

    Answer

    (u,v)=(4,3)(u,v)=(4,3)(u,v)=(4,3).

    Key idea

    A pair solves a system when it meets every condition at once.

  2. Problem 2 Two recorded totals

    Two instruments report 4a+6b=184a+6b=184a+6b=18 and 2a+3b=102a+3b=102a+3b=10. Determine whether any real settings (a,b)(a,b)(a,b) fit both reports.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the measured expressions before looking for individual settings.

    2. Hint 2

      Double the report with the smaller coefficients.

    Answer

    No settings fit; the system is inconsistent.

    Full solution

    Doubling the second report gives 4a+6b=204a+6b=204a+6b=20.

    Subtract the first report.

    0=20=20=2

    No real settings can make this true.

    Answer

    No settings fit; the system is inconsistent.

    Key idea

    Conflicting totals for the same expression make a system inconsistent.

  3. Problem 3 A family of pairs

    Write all solutions of y=5−x2y=5-\frac{x}{2}y=5−2x​ and x=10−2yx=10-2yx=10−2y as ordered pairs using a real parameter ttt.

    1. Hint 1

      An equivalent rearrangement may reveal that there is just one restriction.

    2. Hint 2

      Let y=ty=ty=t and find the matching first coordinate.

    Answer

    (x,y)=(10−2t,t)(x,y)=(10-2t,t)(x,y)=(10−2t,t) for every real ttt (equivalently (t,5−t2)(t,5-\tfrac{t}{2})(t,5−2t​), any parametrization of the same line x+2y=10x+2y=10x+2y=10).

    Full solution

    Multiplying the first equation by 222 and rearranging gives the second.

    Set y=ty=ty=t.

    x=10−2tx=10-2tx=10−2t

    Substitution returns 5−(10−2t)/2=t5-(10-2t)/2=t5−(10−2t)/2=t, so every real ttt works and every solution is included.

    Answer

    (x,y)=(10−2t,t)(x,y)=(10-2t,t)(x,y)=(10−2t,t) for every real ttt (equivalently (t,5−t2)(t,5-\tfrac{t}{2})(t,5−2t​), any parametrization of the same line x+2y=10x+2y=10x+2y=10).

    Key idea

    One surviving linear restriction leaves one freely chosen coordinate.

  4. Problem 4 Coefficients on a card

    A card lists matching coefficient ratios a1/a2=3/5a_1/a_2=3/5a1​/a2​=3/5 and b1/b2=3/5b_1/b_2=3/5b1​/b2​=3/5, with all six coefficients and constants nonzero. The constant ratio is c1/c2=2/5c_1/c_2=2/5c1​/c2​=2/5. Classify the system aix+biy=cia_ix+b_iy=c_iai​x+bi​y=ci​ and explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the scaling of the whole equations, not just the variable terms.

    2. Hint 2

    Inconsistent; no solution.

    Full solution

    The scaled variable terms agree because 5a1=3a25a_1=3a_25a1​=3a2​ and 5b1=3b25b_1=3b_25b1​=3b2​.

    But c1=2c2/5c_1=2c_2/5c1​=2c2​/5, so subtracting the scaled equations gives

    0=−c20=-c_20=−c2​

    This is false since c2≠0c_2\ne0c2​=0.

    The lines are parallel and distinct.

    Answer

    Inconsistent; no solution.

    Key idea

    Equal coefficient ratios require the same constant ratio for consistency.

  5. Problem 5 Calibration readings

    A scale displays d=kw+bd=kw+bd=kw+b, where www is the actual mass in grams and ddd is the displayed number. A 2 gram mass displays 7, and a 5 gram mass displays 13. Find kkk and bbb, and classify the resulting system.

    1. Hint 1

      Each measurement gives one condition on the same two settings.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the conditions to remove the offset.

    Answer

    k=2k=2k=2, b=3b=3b=3; consistent and independent.

    Full solution

    2k+b=75k+b=13\begin{aligned} 2k+b&=7 \\ 5k+b&=13 \end{aligned}2k+b5k+b​=7=13​

    Subtraction gives 3k=63k=63k=6, so k=2k=2k=2.

    Then b=7−4=3b=7-4=3b=7−4=3.

    Both readings check: 2(2)+3=72(2)+3=72(2)+3=7 and 2(5)+3=132(5)+3=132(5)+3=13.

    There is one pair of settings, so the system is consistent and independent.

    Answer

    k=2k=2k=2, b=3b=3b=3; consistent and independent.

    Key idea

    Two distinct inputs can determine the slope and offset of a linear rule.

  6. Problem 6 A movable relation

    For real kkk, consider y=kx+2y=kx+2y=kx+2 and y=(k+1)x+2y=(k+1)x+2y=(k+1)x+2. Determine the solution and classification for every value of kkk.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the equations without dividing by kkk.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract their right sides at a common point.

    Answer

    (x,y)=(0,2)(x,y)=(0,2)(x,y)=(0,2) for every real kkk; consistent and independent.

    Full solution

    At a common point, the two right sides agree.

    kx+2=(k+1)x+2kx+2=kx+x+20=x\begin{aligned} kx+2&=(k+1)x+2 \\ kx+2&=kx+x+2 \\ 0&=x \end{aligned}kx+2kx+20​=(k+1)x+2=kx+x+2=x​

    Expanding (k+1)x(k+1)x(k+1)x as kx+xkx+xkx+x and canceling kx+2kx+2kx+2 from both sides leaves 0=x0=x0=x.

    Substitution gives y=2y=2y=2.

    The slopes differ by 111 for every kkk, so this is a single intersection, including k=0k=0k=0.

    Answer

    (x,y)=(0,2)(x,y)=(0,2)(x,y)=(0,2) for every real kkk; consistent and independent.

    Key idea

    A parameter may change both lines while leaving their unique intersection fixed.

  7. Problem 7 Shared marks

    Two genuine linear equations in x,yx,yx,y are both satisfied by (1,4)(1,4)(1,4) and (3,0)(3,0)(3,0). Find the entire solution set and classify the system.

    1. Hint 1

      Two distinct points determine a line.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the change in height over the change in horizontal position.

    Answer

    (x,y)=(t,6−2t)(x,y)=(t,6-2t)(x,y)=(t,6−2t) for all real ttt; consistent and dependent.

    Full solution

    The slope through the given points is

    (0−4)/(3−1)=−2.(0-4)/(3-1)=-2.(0−4)/(3−1)=−2.

    The point (1,4)(1,4)(1,4) fixes the intercept.

    y=6−2xy=6-2xy=6−2x

    Each equation describes the unique line through those points, so both describe this line.

    Setting x=tx=tx=t lists every shared point.

    Answer

    (x,y)=(t,6−2t)(x,y)=(t,6-2t)(x,y)=(t,6−2t) for all real ttt; consistent and dependent.

    Key idea

    Two shared points force two genuine lines to coincide.

  8. Problem 8 A calculation record

    A student keeps x+2y=9x+2y=9x+2y=9 and replaces 3x+7y=303x+7y=303x+7y=30 by its difference from three times the kept equation. They get y=3y=3y=3 and announce infinitely many solutions because one variable disappeared. Is the conclusion correct? Explain and give the actual solution.

    1. Hint 1

      Removing a variable from one equation is different from losing a constraint.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the remaining one-variable equation in the kept equation.

    Answer

    No; the unique solution is (3,3)(3,3)(3,3).

    Full solution

    The replacement is correct:

    y=30−3(9)y=30-3(9)y=30−3(9)

    Hence y=3y=3y=3.

    Substituting in the kept equation gives x+6=9x+6=9x+6=9, so x=3x=3x=3.

    Both original equations hold: 3+6=93+6=93+6=9 and 9+21=309+21=309+21=30.

    A genuine equation for yyy still constrains it.

    Answer

    No; the unique solution is (3,3)(3,3)(3,3).

    Key idea

    Elimination that leaves a nonzero variable coefficient determines a value rather than a free variable.

  9. Problem 9 A segment of solutions

    Two genuine linear equations have every point of a nontrivial line segment as solutions. A student says the solution set must include the entire line containing that segment. Is this correct? Justify.

    1. Hint 1

      A nontrivial segment supplies two distinct shared points.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the line determined by those two points with each equation.

    Answer

    Yes; the full containing line is the solution set.

    Full solution

    Choose distinct points PPP and QQQ of the segment.

    Each equation describes a line through both, and there is just one line through two distinct points.

    Thus both equations describe that same entire line.

    Every point on it satisfies both equations, and points off it satisfy neither.

    Answer

    Yes; the full containing line is the solution set.

    Key idea

    Two linear equations that share a segment share its entire containing line.

  10. Problem 10 A vertical family

    For the system 3x=123x=123x=12 and 9x=369x=369x=36 in the variables xxx and yyy, someone lists (4,t)(4,t)(4,t) only for integer ttt. Does that give every real solution? Explain and correct the family if needed.

    1. Hint 1

      Neither equation contains yyy.

    2. Hint 2

      Check a noninteger value of the second coordinate.

    Answer

    No; all solutions are (4,t)(4,t)(4,t) for every real ttt.

    Full solution

    Both equations reduce to

    x=4x=4x=4

    They put no restriction on yyy.

    For example, (4,1/2)(4,1/2)(4,1/2) solves both but is absent from the proposed integer list.

    Allowing every real ttt supplies precisely the whole vertical line.

    Answer

    No; all solutions are (4,t)(4,t)(4,t) for every real ttt.

    Key idea

    A free coordinate ranges over the stated domain, not just convenient sample values.
