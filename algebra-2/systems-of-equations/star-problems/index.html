  1. Problem 1 Two row updates at once

    ★ Stretch Difficulty: 1 of 3 stars, Stretch

    A student starts with

    x+2y=8,3x−y=3.x+2y=8,\qquad3x-y=3.x+2y=8,3x−y=3.

    Calling these equations E1,E2E_1,E_2E1​,E2​, the student simultaneously replaces each equation by E1+E2E_1+E_2E1​+E2​, obtaining two copies of 4x+y=114x+y=114x+y=11. The student claims that adding equations is always reversible.

    (b) Instead replace the equations simultaneously by E1+E2E_1+E_2E1​+E2​ and E1−E2E_1-E_2E1​−E2​. Prove that this transformation preserves the solution set of any pair of linear equations, by explaining how to recover both original equations.

    1. Hint 1

      A permitted row addition keeps the source row available. Did the student retain enough independent information?

    2. Hint 2

      For part (b), call the new equations SSS and DDD. Recover E1E_1E1​ and E2E_2E2​ using S+DS+DS+D and S−DS-DS−D.

    Answer

    (a) The original solution is (x,y)=(2,3)(x,y)=(2,3)(x,y)=(2,3); (0,11)(0,11)(0,11) is an added, invalid solution. (b) The second transformation is reversible because E1=(S+D)/2E_1=(S+D)/2E1​=(S+D)/2 and E2=(S−D)/2E_2=(S-D)/2E2​=(S−D)/2.

    Full solution

    Adding the original equations gives 4x+y=114x+y=114x+y=11, but that is only one consequence of the original system.

    Replacing both equations by this same consequence discards their independent difference.

    For example, (0,11)(0,11)(0,11) satisfies both new rows but violates x+2y=8x+2y=8x+2y=8.

    To solve the original system, keep x+2y=8x+2y=8x+2y=8 alongside 4x+y=114x+y=114x+y=11.

    The second gives y=11−4xy=11-4xy=11−4x; substitution into the first gives x+22−8x=8x+22-8x=8x+22−8x=8, so x=2x=2x=2 and y=3y=3y=3.

    For part (b), regard each equation as an equality whose entire left and right sides are transformed.

    Every solution of E1,E2E_1,E_2E1​,E2​ satisfies their sum SSS and difference DDD.

    Conversely, adding SSS and DDD and dividing by 222 recovers E1E_1E1​; subtracting DDD from SSS and dividing by 222 recovers E2E_2E2​.

    The issue is not whether an individual row was formed by addition; it is whether the complete collection of equations retains enough information to reverse the operation.

    Answer

    (a) The original solution is (x,y)=(2,3)(x,y)=(2,3)(x,y)=(2,3); (0,11)(0,11)(0,11) is an added, invalid solution. (b) The second transformation is reversible because E1=(S+D)/2E_1=(S+D)/2E1​=(S+D)/2 and E2=(S−D)/2E_2=(S-D)/2E2​=(S−D)/2.

    Key idea

    Judge a row transformation by reversibility of the whole system, especially when several rows are changed simultaneously.

  4. Problem 4 How many partial checks are enough?

    ★★ Challenge Difficulty: 2 of 3 stars, Challenge

    (a) Four linear equations in two real unknowns each represent a line. Every choice of three of these equations has at least one common solution. Prove that all four equations have a common solution.

    (b) Show that replacing “three” by “two” would make the statement false, by giving explicit equations.

    (c) Show that the conclusion of part (a) can fail for four equations in three real unknowns: construct four equations such that every three have a common solution but all four do not.

    1. Hint 1

      In part (a), first consider whether any two of the four lines are nonparallel.

    2. Hint 2

      If two lines meet at a unique point, any third equation consistent with both must contain that point. In part (c), start with the three coordinate planes.

    Answer

    (a) All four are consistent. (b) For example, x=0x=0x=0, y=0y=0y=0, x+y=1x+y=1x+y=1 are pairwise consistent but not jointly consistent; a fourth equation x+2y=3x+2y=3x+2y=3 keeps every pair consistent. (c) Use x=0x=0x=0, y=0y=0y=0, z=0z=0z=0, x+y+z=1x+y+z=1x+y+z=1.

    Full solution

    For (a), suppose some two lines are nonparallel.

    They meet at a unique point PPP.

    Each remaining line belongs to a triple containing those two, so by the hypothesis it must pass through their only possible common point PPP.

    Thus all four share PPP.

    If no two are nonparallel, all four lines are parallel, allowing coincidence.

    The triple hypothesis implies that every pair is consistent: include any third equation to obtain a solution.

    Two parallel consistent lines must coincide.

    Therefore all four lines are the same line, and again they have common solutions.

    For (b), the four lines x=0x=0x=0, y=0y=0y=0, x+y=1x+y=1x+y=1, and x+2y=3x+2y=3x+2y=3 have pairwise distinct directions, so every pair meets.

    The first three already have no common point, because x=y=0x=y=0x=y=0 contradicts x+y=1x+y=1x+y=1.

    Hence the four together are inconsistent.

    For (c), take the four equations in the answer.

    The first three meet at (0,0,0)(0,0,0)(0,0,0).

    A triple containing x+y+z=1x+y+z=1x+y+z=1 and two coordinate equations is solved by setting the remaining coordinate to 111.

    But all four would require both x+y+z=0x+y+z=0x+y+z=0 and x+y+z=1x+y+z=1x+y+z=1.

    The number of unknowns changes how much partial consistency can guarantee.

    Answer

    (a) All four are consistent. (b) For example, x=0x=0x=0, y=0y=0y=0, x+y=1x+y=1x+y=1 are pairwise consistent but not jointly consistent; a fourth equation x+2y=3x+2y=3x+2y=3 keeps every pair consistent. (c) Use x=0x=0x=0, y=0y=0y=0, z=0z=0z=0, x+y+z=1x+y+z=1x+y+z=1.

    Key idea

    Partial consistency is powerful only when enough equations already pin down all remaining freedom.
