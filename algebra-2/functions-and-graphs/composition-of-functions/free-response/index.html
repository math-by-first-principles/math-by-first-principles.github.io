  1. Problem 1 Two lookup records

    Let fff be the pairs {(0,2),(2,1),(3,0)}\{(0,2),(2,1),(3,0)\}{(0,2),(2,1),(3,0)} and let ggg be the pairs {(0,4),(1,5)}\{(0,4),(1,5)\}{(0,4),(1,5)}. Evaluate (g∘f)(2)(g\circ f)(2)(g∘f)(2).

    1. Hint 1

      The rightmost function acts on the original input.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the output paired with 222 in fff, then use it as the input to ggg.

    Answer

    (g∘f)(2)=5(g\circ f)(2)=5(g∘f)(2)=5.

    Full solution

    The record for fff gives f(2)=1f(2)=1f(2)=1.

    The record for ggg then gives g(1)=5g(1)=5g(1)=5.

    g(f(2))=5g(f(2))=5g(f(2))=5

    The intermediate value 111 is in the domain of ggg, so the composition is defined.

    Answer

    (g∘f)(2)=5(g\circ f)(2)=5(g∘f)(2)=5.

    Key idea

    Composition follows an output from one rule into the input of the next.

  3. Problem 3 A three-position cycle

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    f2(5)=2f^2(5)=2f2(5)=2.

    Full solution

    The first application gives f(5)=0f(5)=0f(5)=0.

    The second gives f(0)=2f(0)=2f(0)=2.

    f2(5)=f(0)=2\begin{aligned}f^2(5)&=f(0)\\&=2\end{aligned}f2(5)​=f(0)=2​

    Every intermediate value is in the stated domain.

    Answer

    f2(5)=2f^2(5)=2f2(5)=2.

    Key idea

    An iterate feeds the first output back into the same function.

  6. Problem 6 Two possible splits

  7. Problem 7 A simplified chain

    Let f(x)=x−1x+1f(x)=\frac{x-1}{x+1}f(x)=x+1x−1​ and g(u)=u+1u−1g(u)=\frac{u+1}{u-1}g(u)=u−1u+1​ on their natural real domains. Find a simplified formula and the exact domain of g∘fg\circ fg∘f.

    1. Hint 1

      Record the inner exclusion before simplifying the nested fractions.

    2. Hint 2

      Then decide whether an output of fff can equal the forbidden outer input 111.

    Answer

    (g∘f)(x)=−x(g\circ f)(x)=-x(g∘f)(x)=−x for x≠−1x\ne-1x=−1.

    Full solution

    The inner function requires x≠−1x\ne-1x=−1.

    Its output cannot equal 111, since that would require x−1=x+1x-1=x+1x−1=x+1.

    Thus the outer function creates no extra exclusion.

    For an allowed input, combine the outer numerator and denominator over the common denominator x+1x+1x+1:

    x−1x+1+1=(x−1)+(x+1)x+1=2xx+1\begin{aligned}\frac{x-1}{x+1}+1&=\frac{(x-1)+(x+1)}{x+1}\\&=\frac{2x}{x+1}\end{aligned}x+1x−1​+1​=x+1(x−1)+(x+1)​=x+12x​​ x−1x+1−1=(x−1)−(x+1)x+1=−2x+1\begin{aligned}\frac{x-1}{x+1}-1&=\frac{(x-1)-(x+1)}{x+1}\\&=-\frac2{x+1}\end{aligned}x+1x−1​−1​=x+1(x−1)−(x+1)​=−x+12​​

    The second expression is nonzero.

    Their quotient is

    g(f(x))=−x.g(f(x))=-x.g(f(x))=−x.

    The original exclusion x≠−1x\ne-1x=−1 remains even though the formula simplifies.

    Answer

    (g∘f)(x)=−x(g\circ f)(x)=-x(g∘f)(x)=−x for x≠−1x\ne-1x=−1.

    Simplification of a composite does not erase the domain restrictions of its intermediate steps.

  8. Problem 8 Two composition orders

    Let f(x)=∣x∣f(x)=\lvert x\rvertf(x)=∣x∣ and g(x)=x2g(x)=x^2g(x)=x2, both on R\mathbb RR with codomain [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞). A student says these functions commute. Is that correct? Check formulas and domains.

    1. Hint 1

      Commuting means both orders produce the same function.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare taking a magnitude before squaring with taking a magnitude after squaring.

    Answer

    Yes; both composites equal x2x^2x2 on R\mathbb RR, with codomain [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞).

    Full solution

    One order gives

    g(f(x))=∣x∣2=x2\begin{aligned}g(f(x))&=\lvert x\rvert^2\\&=x^2\end{aligned}g(f(x))​=∣x∣2=x2​

    The other gives

    f(g(x))=∣x2∣=x2\begin{aligned}f(g(x))&=\lvert x^2\rvert\\&=x^2\end{aligned}f(g(x))​=∣x2∣=x2​

    Both inner outputs are accepted by the other function, so each composite has domain R\mathbb RR.

    Their codomains also match.

    Therefore the composites are equal.

    Answer

    Yes; both composites equal x2x^2x2 on R\mathbb RR, with codomain [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞).

    Key idea

    Some different functions commute, but the formulas and domains must be checked for the particular pair.
