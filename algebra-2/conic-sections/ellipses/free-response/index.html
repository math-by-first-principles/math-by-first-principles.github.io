  1. Problem 1 The oval on the grid

    Find the distance between the foci of the ellipse in the figure.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with both axes from -7 to 7 on equal scales, a grid line and a label at every integer. A single ellipse, taller than it is wide, is centered where the axes cross: it meets the x-axis at x equals -5 and x equals 5, and the y-axis at y equals -6 and y equals 6. No equation, vertex, focus or center is marked.

    1. Hint 1

      Read the two semiaxis lengths from the graph.

    2. Hint 2

      The square of the center-to-focus distance is the larger squared semiaxis minus the smaller one.

    Answer

    2112\sqrt{11}211​ units.

    Full solution

    The ellipse reaches 666 units above and below its center and 555 units to either side.

    Thus a=6a=6a=6 and b=5b=5b=5, so

    c2=36−25c^2=36-25c2=36−25 c2=11.c^2=11.c2=11.

    The center-to-focus distance is c=11c=\sqrt{11}c=11​, and the foci are on opposite sides of the center, giving

    2c=211.2c=2\sqrt{11}.2c=211​.

    Answer

    2112\sqrt{11}211​ units.

    Key idea

    The two foci are separated by twice the distance found from the difference of the squared semiaxes.

  3. Problem 3 Two distance measurements

    The major axis of an ellipse has length 20 units. At a point on the ellipse, the distances to the foci are in the ratio 2:32:32:3. Find those two distances.

    1. Hint 1

      The sum of the focal distances equals the full major-axis length.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the two distances as 2u2u2u and 3u3u3u, with u>0u>0u>0.

    Answer

    8 units and 12 units.

    Full solution

    The constant sum is 202020.

    Writing the distances as 2u2u2u and 3u3u3u gives

    5u=20.5u=20.5u=20.

    Thus u=4u=4u=4, so the distances are 888 and 121212 units.

    They sum to 202020 and have the required ratio.

    Answer

    8 units and 12 units.

    Key idea

    A focal-distance ratio and the constant focal sum determine the two distances.

  4. Problem 4 An equation from distances

  5. Problem 5 A triangle inside the oval

    In the figure, C is an ellipse's center, F is one focus, and B is a co-vertex. Find the major-axis length and explain how the triangle CFB determines it.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the x-axis from -8 to 1 and the y-axis from -7 to 0 on equal scales, a grid line and a label at every integer. Three labeled points form a right triangle: C at x equals -6 and y equals -5, F at x equals -1 and y equals -5 on the same horizontal line, and B at x equals -6 and y equals -3, directly above C. The segments CF, CB and FB are drawn, and a small square marks the right angle at C. The ellipse itself is not drawn, and no length or coordinate is written.

    1. Hint 1

      A co-vertex is equally far from both foci, so each focal distance there is a.

    2. Hint 2

      Read the two perpendicular legs from the grid and find the hypotenuse.

    Answer

    2292\sqrt{29}229​ units.

    Full solution

    The center is (−6,−5)(-6,-5)(−6,−5), F is (−1,−5)(-1,-5)(−1,−5), and B is (−6,−3)(-6,-3)(−6,−3).

    Thus CF is 555 units and CB is 222 units.

    They are perpendicular, so

    BF=52+22BF=\sqrt{5^2+2^2}BF=52+22​ BF=29.BF=\sqrt{29}.BF=29​.

    At a co-vertex each focal distance equals aaa, so a=29a=\sqrt{29}a=29​ and the full major axis has length 2a=2292a=2\sqrt{29}2a=229​ units.

    Answer

    2292\sqrt{29}229​ units.

    Key idea

    For an ellipse with distinct foci, the center, a focus and a co-vertex form a right triangle whose hypotenuse is the semimajor axis.

  6. Problem 6 A changed tracing length

    Two pins stay at (−6,1)(-6,1)(−6,1) and (−6,9)(-6,9)(−6,9). A taut string attached to the pins initially has length 18 units and is replaced by a string of length 22 units. Find the standard equation of the new ellipse and the increase in its minor-axis length.

    Initially a=9a=9a=9, so b2=81−16b^2=81-16b2=81−16, which is 656565.

    With the new string, a=11a=11a=11, so

    b2=121−16b^2=121-16b2=121−16 b2=105.b^2=105.b2=105.

    (x+6)2105+(y−5)2121=1.\frac{(x+6)^2}{105}+\frac{(y-5)^2}{121}=1.105(x+6)2​+121(y−5)2​=1.

  8. Problem 8 A negative right side

    A student says −3(x+5)2−7(y+6)2=−84-3(x+5)^2-7(y+6)^2=-84−3(x+5)2−7(y+6)2=−84 has no real points because the right side is negative. Is that correct? Give the major-axis direction and length if an ellipse exists.

    1. Hint 1

    Answer

    Full solution

    The larger denominator, 282828, is under the x term, so the major axis is horizontal, a=28=27a=\sqrt{28}=2\sqrt7a=28​=27​, and its length is 2a=472a=4\sqrt72a=47​ units.

    Answer

  9. Problem 9 Sliding the foci

    An ellipse and both of its foci are translated by the same displacement. A student claims that its eccentricity stays unchanged even though its center moves. Is the claim correct? Justify it from the lengths in the eccentricity ratio.

    1. Hint 1

      A translation preserves distances between points.

    2. Hint 2

      Consider the center-to-focus distance and half the major-axis length separately.

    Answer

    Yes; the eccentricity stays unchanged.

    Full solution

    A translation preserves the center-to-focus distance c and the semimajor-axis length a.

    The new lengths therefore satisfy

    cnewanew=ca.\frac{c_{\mathrm{new}}}{a_{\mathrm{new}}}=\frac ca.anew​cnew​​=ac​.

    This is the eccentricity before and after the move, so the claim is correct.

    Changing the center alone changes position, not this shape ratio.

    Answer

    Yes; the eccentricity stays unchanged.

    Key idea

    A translation preserves eccentricity because it preserves both lengths in c divided by a.

  10. Problem 10 Pins brought together

    The focal separation is zero, so c=0c=0c=0 while a=9a=9a=9, giving e=0e=0e=0.

    Answer

    Circle centered at (−2,4)(-2,4)(−2,4) with radius 9 units; (x+2)2+(y−4)2=81(x+2)^2+(y-4)^2=81(x+2)2+(y−4)2=81; e=0e=0e=0.
