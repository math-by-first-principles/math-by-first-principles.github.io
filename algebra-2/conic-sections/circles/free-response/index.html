  1. Problem 1 The constant entry

    The circle (x+7)2+(y−4)2=9(x+7)^2+(y-4)^2=9(x+7)2+(y−4)2=9 is written as x2+y2+Dx+Ey+F=0x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0x2+y2+Dx+Ey+F=0. Find FFF.

    1. Hint 1

      Expansion collects the center coordinates and radius into the constant term.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand the two squares, then move the right side to the left.

    Answer

    F=56F=56F=56.

    Full solution

    The constants from the squares are 494949 and 161616.

    Moving 999 to the left gives

    F=49+16−9F=49+16-9F=49+16−9 F=56.F=56.F=56.

    The expanded equation is

    x2+y2+14x−8y+56=0.x^2+y^2+14x-8y+56=0.x2+y2+14x−8y+56=0.

    Answer

    F=56F=56F=56.

    Key idea

    In general circle form, the constant combines the squared center coordinates with the negative squared radius.

  2. Problem 2 A coefficient record

    A circle is recorded as x2+y2+Dx+Ey+F=0x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0x2+y2+Dx+Ey+F=0, with D=14D=14D=14, E=−6E=-6E=−6, and F=33F=33F=33. Find its center.

    1. Hint 1

      The linear terms come from expanding squares centered at the circle's coordinates.

    2. Hint 2

      The coefficient of x is −2h-2h−2h, and the coefficient of y is −2k-2k−2k.

    Answer

    Center (−7,3)(-7,3)(−7,3).

    Full solution

    The center coordinates satisfy −2h=14-2h=14−2h=14 and −2k=−6-2k=-6−2k=−6, so

    h=−7.h=-7.h=−7.

    Also

    k=3.k=3.k=3.

    Answer

    Center (−7,3)(-7,3)(−7,3).

    Key idea

    The center coordinates are negative one-half of the linear coefficients in normalized general form.

  3. Problem 3 The plotted boundary

    The figure shows a circle. Write its equation in center-radius form.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the x-axis from -5 to 2 and the y-axis from -3 to 5 on equal scales, a grid line and a label at every integer.     1. Hint 1

      Opposite extremes of a circle are equally far from its center.

    2. Hint 2

      Read the left and right extremes to find the center's horizontal coordinate and the radius; do the same vertically.

    Answer

    (x+2)2+(y−1)2=4(x+2)^2+(y-1)^2=4(x+2)2+(y−1)2=4.

    Full solution

    The horizontal extremes are −4-4−4 and 000, so their midpoint is −2-2−2 and the radius is 222.

    The vertical extremes are −1-1−1 and 333, with midpoint 111.

    Squaring the distance to (−2,1)(-2,1)(−2,1) gives

    (x+2)2+(y−1)2=4.(x+2)^2+(y-1)^2=4.(x+2)2+(y−1)2=4.

    Each extreme is 222 units from the center.

    Answer

    (x+2)2+(y−1)2=4(x+2)^2+(y-1)^2=4(x+2)2+(y−1)2=4.

    Key idea

    A circle's opposite extremes determine its center and radius.

  4. Problem 4 Two possible centers

    A circle has radius 5\sqrt55​ and passes through (−8,2)(-8,2)(−8,2). Its center lies on the xxx-axis. Find all possible centers and their circle equations in center-radius form.

    1. Hint 1

      Write a center on the x-axis as (h,0)(h,0)(h,0), and use the given radius as the distance to the known point.

    2. Hint 2

      After squaring, isolate (−8−h)2(-8-h)^2(−8−h)2 and retain both signs.

    Answer

    Centers (−9,0)(-9,0)(−9,0) and (−7,0)(-7,0)(−7,0); equations (x+9)2+y2=5(x+9)^2+y^2=5(x+9)2+y2=5 and (x+7)2+y2=5(x+7)^2+y^2=5(x+7)2+y2=5.

    Full solution

    The squared distance to (−8,2)(-8,2)(−8,2) must be 555, so

    (−8−h)2+4=5.(-8-h)^2+4=5.(−8−h)2+4=5.

    Hence

    (h+8)2=1.(h+8)^2=1.(h+8)2=1.

    The two possibilities are h=−9h=-9h=−9 and h=−7h=-7h=−7, giving the two equations in the answer.

    Substituting (−8,2)(-8,2)(−8,2) gives 555 in each.

    Answer

    Centers (−9,0)(-9,0)(−9,0) and (−7,0)(-7,0)(−7,0); equations (x+9)2+y2=5(x+9)^2+y^2=5(x+9)2+y2=5 and (x+7)2+y2=5(x+7)^2+y^2=5(x+7)2+y2=5.

    Key idea

    Locating a circle center on a given line may leave two valid centers.

  5. Problem 5 The two rings

    The figure shows two circles. Classify their contact as no shared points, internal tangency, external tangency, or two intersections. Justify your choice from their center distance and radii.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the x-axis from -6 to 5 and the y-axis from -5 to 5 on equal scales, a grid line and a label at every integer. A large solid circle has its center marked A on the x-axis at x equals -1; it crosses the x-axis at x equals -5 and x equals 3 and reaches up to y equals 4 and down to y equals -4. A small dashed circle has its center marked B on the x-axis at x equals 2; it crosses the x-axis at x equals 1 and x equals 3. No point where they meet and no radius is marked.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the center distance with both the sum and the positive difference of the radii.

    2. Hint 2

      Read each radius from the grid; equality with the difference indicates contact from inside.

    Answer

    Internal tangency; one shared point.

    Full solution

    The centers are (−1,0)(-1,0)(−1,0) and (2,0)(2,0)(2,0), so their distance is d=3d=3d=3.

    The radii are 444 and 111, giving

    ∣4−1∣=3.|4-1|=3.∣4−1∣=3.

    The center distance equals the radius difference, so the small circle is internally tangent to the large one.

    They share exactly one point.

    Answer

    Internal tangency; one shared point.

    Key idea

    Distinct circles touch internally when their center distance equals the positive difference of their radii.

  6. Problem 6 A changing coefficient

    For real ttt, consider x2+y2+2tx+16y+68=0x^2+y^2+2tx+16y+68=0x2+y2+2tx+16y+68=0. Determine which values of ttt give a circle, a single point, or no real points. Give the point coordinates in the single-point cases.

    1. Hint 1

      Complete the squares while treating t as a fixed real coefficient.

    2. Hint 2

      Classify the sign of the constant on the right after completing both squares.

    Answer

    Circle: t<−2t<-2t<−2 or t>2t>2t>2. Single point: t=2t=2t=2 gives (−2,−8)(-2,-8)(−2,−8); t=−2t=-2t=−2 gives (2,−8)(2,-8)(2,−8). No real points: −2<t<2-2<t<2−2<t<2.

    Full solution

    Completing both squares gives

    (x+t)2+(y+8)2=t2−4.(x+t)^2+(y+8)^2=t^2-4.(x+t)2+(y+8)2=t2−4.

    The right side is positive for ∣t∣>2|t|>2∣t∣>2, zero for t=2t=2t=2 or t=−2t=-2t=−2, and negative for ∣t∣<2|t|<2∣t∣<2.

    A zero sum of two squares forces (x,y)=(−t,−8)(x,y)=(-t,-8)(x,y)=(−t,−8), giving the stated two points.

    Answer

    Circle: t<−2t<-2t<−2 or t>2t>2t>2. Single point: t=2t=2t=2 gives (−2,−8)(-2,-8)(−2,−8); t=−2t=-2t=−2 gives (2,−8)(2,-8)(2,−8). No real points: −2<t<2-2<t<2−2<t<2.

  7. Problem 7 A moving location

    A point has coordinates P=(t,−5)P=(t,-5)P=(t,−5), with real ttt. For which values of ttt is P strictly inside the circle x2+y2+8x+12y+50=0x^2+y^2+8x+12y+50=0x2+y2+8x+12y+50=0?

    1. Hint 1

      The normalized general-form expression is negative inside the circle.

    2. Hint 2

      Substitute the moving point and solve the resulting quadratic inequality.

    Answer

    −5<t<−3-5<t<-3−5<t<−3.

    Full solution

    At P the left side becomes

    t2+8t+15.t^2+8t+15.t2+8t+15.

    Its factorization is

    (t+5)(t+3).(t+5)(t+3).(t+5)(t+3).

    The product is negative between its roots, so P is inside for −5<t<−3-5<t<-3−5<t<−3.

    Completing the circle gives center (−4,−6)(-4,-6)(−4,−6) and radius 2\sqrt22​; the line y=−5y=-5y=−5 is 111 unit from the center, and its points inside satisfy (t+4)2+1<2(t+4)^2+1<2(t+4)2+1<2, the same interval.

    Answer

    −5<t<−3-5<t<-3−5<t<−3.

    Key idea

    A point is inside a circle when its squared distance from the center is less than the squared radius.

  8. Problem 8 A negative distance

    Answer

    No; the original graph is empty. After squaring, the graph is the circle (x−6)2+(y+4)2=9(x-6)^2+(y+4)^2=9(x−6)2+(y+4)2=9, centered at (6,−4)(6,-4)(6,−4) with radius 333.

  9. Problem 9 Two records of a boundary

    Do x2+y2+12x+8y+45=0x^2+y^2+12x+8y+45=0x2+y2+12x+8y+45=0 and −3x2−3y2−36x−24y−135=0-3x^2-3y^2-36x-24y-135=0−3x2−3y2−36x−24y−135=0 describe the same circle? Justify your answer and state how many points they share.

    1. Hint 1

      Multiplying an equation by a nonzero constant preserves its solutions.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare each coefficient, including the constant term.

    Answer

    Yes; they share infinitely many points.

    Full solution

    Every coefficient in the second equation is −3-3−3 times its counterpart in the first.

    Dividing the second equation by the nonzero number −3-3−3 gives the first exactly.

    Completing the first equation gives

    (x+6)2+(y+4)2=7.(x+6)^2+(y+4)^2=7.(x+6)2+(y+4)2=7.

    Both therefore describe the same circle, with every point shared.

    Answer

    Yes; they share infinitely many points.

    Key idea

    Nonzero scalar multiples of a circle equation describe the same boundary.

  10. Problem 10 Three locations on a line

    A designer requests a circle through (−2,3)(-2,3)(−2,3), (1,3)(1,3)(1,3), and (4,3)(4,3)(4,3). Is such a circle possible? Justify your answer by explaining what each possible discriminant sign would imply for its intersections with the horizontal line through these points.

    1. Hint 1

      The points share one y-coordinate, which can be substituted into a general center-radius equation.

    2. Hint 2
