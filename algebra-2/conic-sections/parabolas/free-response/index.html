    1. From two distances to one equation . Foundational, 11 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 11 pts

    Every fact this lesson proves about a parabola starts from the same single sentence: a point belongs to the curve exactly when its distance to the focus equals its distance to the directrix. This question puts that sentence to work on a parabola whose focus and directrix are not centered at the origin, then asks why the algebra that turns the sentence into an equation can be trusted.

    Part A

    Part C

    Squaring an equation can normally introduce extraneous solutions, because A=BA=BA=B does not in general have the same solutions as A2=B2A^2=B^2A2=B2: for instance A=3A=3A=3 and A=−3A=-3A=−3 give the same square but are different equations. What rescues this particular squaring step is the nature of the two quantities being squared.

    A square root, by its definition, is never negative, and an absolute value is never negative either. So both (x−2)2+(y+4)2\sqrt{(x-2)^2+(y+4)^2}(x−2)2+(y+4)2​ and ∣y−2∣|y-2|∣y−2∣ are nonnegative numbers for every xxx and yyy. For two nonnegative real numbers AAA and BBB, it is a genuine biconditional that

    A=B  ⟺  A2=B2,A = B \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A^2 = B^2,A=B⟺A2=B2,

    because the function t↦t2t \mapsto t^2t↦t2 is one-to-one on the nonnegative numbers (it never sends two different nonnegative values to the same square). So no solution of the original equation is lost, and no new solution is gained: the squared equation has exactly the same solution set.

    In one line

    (x−2)2=−12(y+1)(x-2)^2 = -12(y+1)(x−2)2=−12(y+1), with vertex (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1), p=−3p=-3p=−3, opening downward, and focus (2,−4)(2,-4)(2,−4) and directrix y=2y=2y=2 exactly as given. The squaring step loses nothing because both sides of the original equation are nonnegative distances, and for nonnegative quantities equality survives squaring in both directions.

    Another way: Find the vertex first, then write the equation directly

    Since the vertex is always the midpoint of the segment from the focus to the point of the directrix directly across from it, average the focus (2,−4)(2,-4)(2,−4) with the point (2,2)(2,2)(2,2) directly above it on the directrix: the vertex is (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1).
