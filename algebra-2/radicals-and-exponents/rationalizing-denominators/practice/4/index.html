    When rationalizing a denominator of the form x+yx + yx+y where xxx and yyy are cube roots, the multiplier used is x2−xy+y2x^2 - xy + y^2x2−xy+y2. Why that one?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Expand the product and watch every intermediate term cancel in pairs.

    (x+y)(x2−xy+y2)=x3−x2y+xy2+x2y−xy2+y3=x3+y3(x + y)(x^2 - xy + y^2) = x^3 - x^2y + xy^2 + x^2y - xy^2 + y^3 = x^3 + y^3(x+y)(x2−xy+y2)=x3−x2y+xy2+x2y−xy2+y3=x3+y3

    Only the cubes are left, and a cube is exactly what turns a cube root rational. The multiplier is not a conjugate and not a square; it is engineered so that the surviving powers are the ones that land back in the rational numbers.
