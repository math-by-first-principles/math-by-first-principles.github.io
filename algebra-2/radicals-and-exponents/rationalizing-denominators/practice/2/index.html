    Rationalize the denominator of 133\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{3}}33​1​.

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A cube root needs the factor that completes the cube, not a sign flip. Since 33=31/3\sqrt[3]{3} = 3^{1/3}33​=31/3, the missing exponent is 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​, and 32/3=933^{2/3} = \sqrt[3]{9}32/3=39​.

    Multiplying by 33\sqrt[3]{3}33​ instead would leave 93\sqrt[3]{9}39​ downstairs, which is still irrational.

    Solution

    That rewrite replaces a delicate subtraction of two nearly equal numbers with an easy division. Numerically both sides are about 0.07110.07110.0711.

    For positive rationals aaa and bbb, the product (a+b)(a−b)(\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})(a​+b​)(a​−b​) equals:

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The cross terms −ab-\sqrt{ab}−ab​ and +ab+\sqrt{ab}+ab​ cancel, so only the squared terms survive, and each of those is rational.

    (a+b)(a−b)=(a)2−(b)2=a−b(\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}) = (\sqrt{a})^2 - (\sqrt{b})^2 = a - b(a​+b​)(a​−b​)=(a​)2−(b​)2=a−b

    The last option, a−2ab+ba - 2\sqrt{ab} + ba−2ab​+b, is the square (a−b)2(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})^2(a​−b​)2, which keeps its radical. Squaring and conjugating are different operations.
