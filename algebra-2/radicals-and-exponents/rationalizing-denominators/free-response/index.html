    The answer

    Part A

    766\dfrac{7\sqrt6}{6}676​​.

    Part B

    3232\dfrac{3\sqrt[3]{2}}{2}2332​​.

    Writing both factors as powers of aaa, the exponents 1n\tfrac1nn1​ and n−1n\tfrac{n-1}{n}nn−1​ add to exactly 111, so the product is a1=aa^1=aa1=a; since aaa is a positive integer, that result is itself whole, not merely another radical.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Multiply top and bottom by the missing copy of 6\sqrt66​, which is exactly 111 in disguise.

    76⋅66=766.\frac{7}{\sqrt6}\cdot\frac{\sqrt6}{\sqrt6}=\frac{7\sqrt6}{6}.6​7​⋅6​6​​=676​​.

    The denominator is (6)2=6(\sqrt6)^2=6(6​)2=6, a whole number, so the radical is gone from downstairs.

    Part B

    Write the radicand as a power of its prime base: 4=224=2^24=22, so 43=22/3\sqrt[3]{4}=2^{2/3}34​=22/3. The exponent still needed to reach a whole power of 222 is 13\tfrac1331​, which is 23\sqrt[3]{2}32​, not 163\sqrt[3]{16}316​.

    The denominator became the whole number 222, and the multiplier was the smallest one that would do it.

    Part C

    Rewrite each radical as a rational power of aaa: an=a1/n\sqrt[n]{a}=a^{1/n}na​=a1/n and a n−1n=a(n−1)/n\sqrt[n]{a^{\,n-1}}=a^{(n-1)/n}nan−1​=a(n−1)/n. The exponent law for multiplying like bases says the exponents add:
