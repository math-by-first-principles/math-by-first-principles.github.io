    Routing (−8)2/6(-8)^{2/6}(−8)2/6 through (am)1/n\left(a^{m}\right)^{1/n}(am)1/n gives ((−8)2)1/6=641/6=2\left((-8)^{2}\right)^{1/6} = 64^{1/6} = 2((−8)2)1/6=641/6=2, while reducing the exponent to lowest terms gives (−8)1/3=−2(-8)^{1/3} = -2(−8)1/3=−2. What does this show?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A single expression cannot name two different numbers, so something in the chain is not licensed. Check the endpoints: (−8)1/3=−2(-8)^{1/3} = -2(−8)1/3=−2 is a correct use of the odd-root convention, and 641/6=264^{1/6} = 2641/6=2 is correct, and 26\tfrac2662​ and 13\tfrac1331​ are the same rational number.

    ((−8)2)1/6=2but(−8)1/3=−2\left((-8)^{2}\right)^{1/6} = 2 \quad \text{but} \quad (-8)^{1/3} = -2((−8)2)1/6=2but(−8)1/3=−2

    What fails is the step that treats both as (−8)2/6(-8)^{2/6}(−8)2/6. Well-definedness was proved from the fact that a positive number has exactly one positive nnn-th root, and −8-8−8 has none. The hypothesis a>0a > 0a>0 is exactly what the proof consumed.

    A cube of volume VVV has edge length V1/3V^{1/3}V1/3. If the volume grows from 646464 to 128128128 cubic units, by what factor does the edge length grow?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The factor is the ratio of the two edge lengths, and both volumes are positive, so the exponent distributes over the quotient.

    1281/3641/3=(12864)1/3=21/3\frac{128^{1/3}}{64^{1/3}} = \left(\frac{128}{64}\right)^{1/3} = 2^{1/3}641/31281/3​=(64128​)1/3=21/3

    Doubling the volume multiplies the edge by only the cube root of 222, roughly 1.261.261.26. The edge grows far more slowly than the volume, which is why the answer is not 222.

  5. 5

    For a>0a > 0a>0, simplify (a2/5)5/2\left(a^{2/5}\right)^{5/2}(a2/5)5/2.

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    A power of a power multiplies the exponents, and these two exponents are reciprocals.

    (a2/5)5/2=a25⋅52=a1=a\left(a^{2/5}\right)^{5/2} = a^{\frac25 \cdot \frac52} = a^{1} = a(a2/5)5/2=a52​⋅25​=a1=a

    Raising to nm\tfrac{n}{m}mn​ undoes raising to mn\tfrac{m}{n}nm​, which is exactly how you solve an equation like x2/5=cx^{2/5} = cx2/5=c for a positive xxx.

  8. 8

    Which statement about (−16)1/4(-16)^{1/4}(−16)1/4 is correct?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A fourth power is an even power, and an even power of any real number is never negative.

    24=16and(−2)4=16,so no real r has r4=−162^{4} = 16 \quad \text{and} \quad (-2)^{4} = 16, \quad \text{so no real } r \text{ has } r^{4} = -1624=16and(−2)4=16,so no real r has r4=−16

    So the expression names no real number. Odd roots of negatives, such as (−8)1/3=−2(-8)^{1/3} = -2(−8)1/3=−2, are a different matter; only odd indices survive a negative radicand in the real numbers.

    The lesson proves that (an)m=amn\left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^{m} = \sqrt[n]{a^{m}}(na​)m=nam​ for a>0a > 0a>0. Which fact does that proof depend on?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The argument shows that (an)m\left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^{m}(na​)m is a positive number whose nnn-th power is ama^{m}am, and then it needs to conclude that this pins the number down uniquely.

    (rm)n=rmn=(rn)m=amwithr=an>0\left(r^{m}\right)^{n} = r^{mn} = \left(r^{n}\right)^{m} = a^{m} \quad \text{with} \quad r = \sqrt[n]{a} > 0(rm)n=rmn=(rn)m=amwithr=na​>0

    Only the uniqueness of the positive nnn-th root turns that into an equality of names. The claim that every real number has exactly one nnn-th root is false, since 444 has two real square roots, and it is exactly that failure of uniqueness that the positivity requirement repairs.

    Correct answer: D
