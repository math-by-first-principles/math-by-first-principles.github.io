Complex Numbers: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Imaginary unit iii
Defined by the single equation i2=−1i^2 = -1i2=−1, with every ordinary law of algebra kept. No real number has a negative square, so iii lies nowhere on the real line.
Complex number, standard form a+bia + bia+bi
Real part aaa and imaginary part bbb, both real numbers. The imaginary part is the coefficient bbb with its sign, never bibibi: for 7−4i7 - 4i7−4i it is −4-4−4.
Pure imaginary number
A number bibibi with b≠0b \neq 0b=0, so its real part is 000. Taking b=0b = 0b=0 instead gives a real number: the reals sit inside the complex numbers unchanged.
Conjugate z‾\overline{z}z
For z=a+biz = a + biz=a+bi, the number a−bia - bia−bi: only the sign of the imaginary part flips. The conjugate of −3−5i-3 - 5i−3−5i is −3+5i-3 + 5i−3+5i, not 3+5i3 + 5i3+5i.
Conjugate pair
p+qip + qip+qi and p−qip - qip−qi with q≠0q \neq 0q=0: equal real parts, opposite imaginary parts, mirror images across the real axis.
Complex plane
The plane in which a+bia + bia+bi is the point (a,b)(a, b)(a,b). The horizontal real axis holds the numbers with b=0b = 0b=0; the vertical imaginary axis holds 000 and the pure imaginaries.
Discriminant Δ=b2−4ac\Delta = b^2 - 4acΔ=b2−4ac
For REAL a≠0a \neq 0a=0, bbb, ccc it no longer counts roots (there are always two with multiplicity); it locates them: Δ>0\Delta > 0Δ>0 two real, Δ=0\Delta = 0Δ=0 one repeated real, Δ<0\Delta < 0Δ<0 a non-real conjugate pair. Real coefficients matter for the LOCATION claims specifically: with one coefficient non-real, x2−2ix−1x^2 - 2ix - 1x2−2ix−1 still has Δ=0\Delta = 0Δ=0 and the repeated root iii, but the sign-based location trichotomy and the conjugate-pair conclusion no longer apply.

Formulas and theorems

  • The definition of iii, and roots of negatives

    i2=−1,−k=iki^2 = -1, \qquad \sqrt{-k} = i\sqrt{k}i2=−1,−k​=ik​

    Use when k>0k > 0k>0. The symbol −k\sqrt{-k}−k​ names ONE number, iki\sqrt{k}ik​, while x2=−kx^2 = -kx2=−k has two solutions, ±ik\pm i\sqrt{k}±ik​. Keep the iii outside the radical.

  • Powers of iii

    i1=i,i2=−1,i3=−i,i4=1in=ir  for  n=4q+r\begin{gathered} i^1 = i, \quad i^2 = -1, \quad i^3 = -i, \quad i^4 = 1 \\ i^n = i^r \ \text{ for } \ n = 4q + r \end{gathered}i1=i,i2=−1,i3=−i,i4=1in=ir  for  n=4q+r​

    Use when nnn a positive integer, rrr its remainder on division by 444; remainder 000 gives 111. Parity decides nothing: i6=−1i^6 = -1i6=−1 but i8=1i^8 = 1i8=1.

  • Equality of complex numbers

    a+bi=c+di  ⟺  a=c  and  b=da + bi = c + di \iff a = c \ \text{ and } \ b = da+bi=c+di⟺a=c  and  b=d

    Use when aaa, bbb, ccc, ddd ALL real. One complex equation therefore carries two real ones. The complex numbers have no order, so <<< and >>> between them are meaningless.

  • Addition and subtraction

    (a+bi)±(c+di)=(a±c)+(b±d) i(a + bi) \pm (c + di) = (a \pm c) + (b \pm d)\,i(a+bi)±(c+di)=(a±c)+(b±d)i

    Use when Any two complex numbers. Distribute a minus sign through BOTH terms of the second. Geometrically, adding c+dic + dic+di slides every point ccc across and ddd up; z−wz - wz−w is the arrow from www to zzz.

  • Multiplication

    (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc) i\begin{gathered} (a + bi)(c + di) \\ = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)\,i \end{gathered}(a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i​

    Use when Any two complex numbers. Rebuild it by expanding rather than memorizing: each i2i^2i2 becomes −1-1−1 and joins the real part with its sign flipped.

  • Conjugate product

    z z‾=(a+bi)(a−bi)=a2+b2=∣z∣2z\,\overline{z} = (a + bi)(a - bi) = a^2 + b^2 = |z|^2zz=(a+bi)(a−bi)=a2+b2=∣z∣2

    Use when Any z=a+biz = a + biz=a+bi. The value is REAL, never negative, and 000 only for z=0z = 0z=0. The i2i^2i2 turns the difference of squares into a SUM, which is what lets a conjugate clear a denominator.

  • Conjugation passes through arithmetic

    z+w‾=z‾+w‾zw‾=z‾ w‾z‾‾=z\begin{gathered} \overline{z + w} = \overline{z} + \overline{w} \\ \overline{zw} = \overline{z}\,\overline{w} \\ \overline{\overline{z}} = z \end{gathered}z+w​=z+wzw=zwz=z​
    Text description

    The point z and the point for its conjugate sit directly above and below each other at equal distances from the real axis, so conjugation reflects the plane across that axis.

    Use when Any complex zzz and www; the product rule repeats into powers. Conjugation fixes exactly the real numbers and reflects the plane across the real axis, so ∣z‾∣=∣−z∣=∣z∣|\overline{z}| = |-z| = |z|∣z∣=∣−z∣=∣z∣.

  • Division and reciprocals

    a+bic+di=(a+bi)(c−di)c2+d21c+di=c−dic2+d2\begin{gathered} \frac{a + bi}{c + di} = \frac{(a + bi)(c - di)}{c^2 + d^2} \\ \frac{1}{c + di} = \frac{c - di}{c^2 + d^2} \end{gathered}c+dia+bi​=c2+d2(a+bi)(c−di)​c+di1​=c2+d2c−di​​

    Use when c+di≠0c + di \neq 0c+di=0, the only forbidden divisor, since c2+d2=0c^2 + d^2 = 0c2+d2=0 only for c=d=0c = d = 0c=d=0. Multiply NUMERATOR AND DENOMINATOR by the DENOMINATOR's conjugate, which is multiplying by 111.

    e.g. 1i=−i02+12=−i\dfrac{1}{i} = \dfrac{-i}{0^2 + 1^2} = -ii1​=02+12−i​=−i, and i(−i)=1i(-i) = 1i(−i)=1.

  • Modulus, and the modulus of a product

    ∣a+bi∣=a2+b2,∣zw∣=∣z∣ ∣w∣|a + bi| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}, \qquad |zw| = |z|\,|w|∣a+bi∣=a2+b2​,∣zw∣=∣z∣∣w∣
    Text description

    The point a plus b i closes a right triangle whose legs along the axes have lengths a and b, so its hypotenuse, the distance from zero, is the modulus.

    Use when Any complex numbers. The modulus is the distance from 000: always a NONNEGATIVE REAL number, never complex, and 000 only for z=0z = 0z=0. Multiplicativity extends to any number of factors; no such rule holds for a sum.

    e.g. ∣(6+8i)(4−3i)∣=10⋅5=50|(6 + 8i)(4 - 3i)| = 10 \cdot 5 = 50∣(6+8i)(4−3i)∣=10⋅5=50, with no expansion.

  • Distance, and circles

    ∣z−w∣=(a−c)2+(b−d)2∣z−z0∣=r\begin{gathered} |z - w| = \sqrt{(a - c)^2 + (b - d)^2} \\ |z - z_0| = r \end{gathered}∣z−w∣=(a−c)2+(b−d)2​∣z−z0​∣=r​

    Use when For z=a+biz = a + biz=a+bi and w=c+diw = c + diw=c+di; distance from 000 is the case w=0w = 0w=0. The circle needs r>0r > 0r>0 and has center z0z_0z0​, with ∣z−z0∣<r|z - z_0| < r∣z−z0​∣<r inside and >r> r>r outside.

    e.g. ∣(−1+2i)−(3−i)∣=∣−4+3i∣=5|(-1 + 2i) - (3 - i)| = |-4 + 3i| = 5∣(−1+2i)−(3−i)∣=∣−4+3i∣=5.

  • Triangle inequality

    ∣∣z∣−∣w∣∣  ≤  ∣z+w∣  ≤  ∣z∣+∣w∣\bigl| |z| - |w| \bigr| \;\le\; |z + w| \;\le\; |z| + |w|​∣z∣−∣w∣​≤∣z+w∣≤∣z∣+∣w∣

    Use when Any two complex numbers. The upper bound is an equality only when the arrows point the same way, or one is 000; never solve with it as an equation.

    e.g. ∣z∣=3|z| = 3∣z∣=3 and ∣w∣=4|w| = 4∣w∣=4 pin ∣z+w∣|z + w|∣z+w∣ into [1,7][1, 7][1,7].

  • Quadratic formula with a negative discriminant

    x=−b±i∣Δ∣2aΔ=b2−4ac<0\begin{gathered} x = \frac{-b \pm i\sqrt{|\Delta|}}{2a} \\ \Delta = b^2 - 4ac < 0 \end{gathered}x=2a−b±i∣Δ∣​​Δ=b2−4ac<0​

    Use when aaa, bbb, ccc real and a≠0a \neq 0a=0. Divide BOTH numerator terms by 2a2a2a. The roots are a conjugate pair p±qip \pm qip±qi with q≠0q \neq 0q=0, sharing the real part p=−b2ap = -\tfrac{b}{2a}p=−2ab​, the axis of symmetry.

  • Conjugate root theorem

    az2+bz+c=0  ⟹  a z‾ 2+b z‾+c=0\begin{gathered} az^2 + bz + c = 0 \\ \implies a\,\overline{z}^{\,2} + b\,\overline{z} + c = 0 \end{gathered}az2+bz+c=0⟹az2+bz+c=0​

    Use when EVERY coefficient must be real, with a≠0a \neq 0a=0. It says nothing new about a real root, where z‾=z\overline{z} = zz=z; its force is that non-real roots never appear alone, so the inventories are two real, one repeated real, or one conjugate pair.

  • Sum and product of the roots, and complete factorization

    r1+r2=−ba,r1r2=caax2+bx+c=a(x−r1)(x−r2)\begin{gathered} r_1 + r_2 = -\frac{b}{a}, \qquad r_1 r_2 = \frac{c}{a} \\ ax^2 + bx + c = a(x - r_1)(x - r_2) \end{gathered}r1​+r2​=−ab​,r1​r2​=ac​ax2+bx+c=a(x−r1​)(x−r2​)​

    Use when Any quadratic with a≠0a \neq 0a=0; both relations hold whether the roots are real or not. For a non-real pair p±qip \pm qip±qi the product is p2+q2=∣z∣2>0p^2 + q^2 = |z|^2 > 0p2+q2=∣z∣2>0. Over the complex numbers every quadratic splits into two linear factors.

    e.g. Roots 1±2i1 \pm 2i1±2i: x2−2x+5=(x−1−2i)(x−1+2i)x^2 - 2x + 5 = (x - 1 - 2i)(x - 1 + 2i)x2−2x+5=(x−1−2i)(x−1+2i).

Problem types, step by step

Simplify or combine square roots of negative numbers

  1. Rewrite every −k\sqrt{-k}−k​ as iki\sqrt{k}ik​ before doing anything else with it.
  2. Simplify each real radical by pulling out perfect-square factors.
  3. Only now multiply, divide, or add, replacing every i2i^2i2 with −1-1−1.

e.g. −8⋅−18=(2i2)(3i2)=6i2⋅2=−12\sqrt{-8} \cdot \sqrt{-18} = \left(2i\sqrt{2}\right)\left(3i\sqrt{2}\right) = 6i^2 \cdot 2 = -12−8​⋅−18​=(2i2​)(3i2​)=6i2⋅2=−12.

Evaluate a power of iii

  1. Divide the exponent by 444 and keep the remainder (only the last two digits matter, since 100100100 is a multiple of 444).
  2. Read the remainder off the cycle: 0→10 \to 10→1, 1→i1 \to i1→i, 2→−12 \to -12→−1, 3→−i3 \to -i3→−i.

e.g. i47i^{47}i47: 47=4⋅11+347 = 4 \cdot 11 + 347=4⋅11+3 leaves remainder 333, so i47=−ii^{47} = -ii47=−i.

Add, subtract, or multiply two complex numbers

  1. For a difference, distribute the minus sign through both terms of the second number first.
  2. Combine real with real and imaginary with imaginary; for a product, expand all four products as with any binomials, signs attached.
  3. Replace every i2i^2i2 by −1-1−1 and report as a+bia + bia+bi, with no power of iii above the first.

e.g. (4+3i)−(6−8i)=−2+11i(4 + 3i) - (6 - 8i) = -2 + 11i(4+3i)−(6−8i)=−2+11i, and (3−2i)2=9−12i+4i2=5−12i(3 - 2i)^2 = 9 - 12i + 4i^2 = 5 - 12i(3−2i)2=9−12i+4i2=5−12i.

Divide two complex numbers

  1. Confirm the divisor is not 000, then multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the DENOMINATOR.
  2. The denominator becomes the real c2+d2c^2 + d^2c2+d2; expand the numerator, spend every i2i^2i2, and divide each part by it.

e.g. 4+2i1+i=(4+2i)(1−i)2=6−2i2=3−i\dfrac{4 + 2i}{1 + i} = \dfrac{(4 + 2i)(1 - i)}{2} = \dfrac{6 - 2i}{2} = 3 - i1+i4+2i​=2(4+2i)(1−i)​=26−2i​=3−i.

Measure in the complex plane: modulus, distance, circles

  1. Plot a+bia + bia+bi at (a,b)(a, b)(a,b): real part across, imaginary part up, signs included.
  2. For ∣z∣|z|∣z∣, square both parts, add, then take the nonnegative square root, in that order; subtract first for a gap between two numbers; multiply the separate moduli for a product.
  3. Given ∣z−z0∣=r|z - z_0| = r∣z−z0​∣=r, read the center and radius off and draw rather than solving; compare a distance to rrr to place a point inside, on, or outside.
  4. To pair it with a condition on one part, fix that part in z=a+biz = a + biz=a+bi and solve the real equation that results.

e.g. Modulus 555 with imaginary part 333: a2+9=25a^2 + 9 = 25a2+9=25, so z=4+3iz = 4 + 3iz=4+3i or −4+3i-4 + 3i−4+3i.

Solve a quadratic whose discriminant is negative

  1. Put it as ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0 and compute Δ=b2−4ac\Delta = b^2 - 4acΔ=b2−4ac.
  2. With Δ<0\Delta < 0Δ<0, rewrite Δ\sqrt{\Delta}Δ​ as i∣Δ∣i\sqrt{|\Delta|}i∣Δ∣​, simplify that radical, and divide BOTH numerator terms by 2a2a2a.
  3. Report the conjugate pair p±qip \pm qip±qi; check ppp against the axis −b2a-\tfrac{b}{2a}−2ab​ and substitute one root back, which certifies both.

e.g. x2+2x+10=0x^2 + 2x + 10 = 0x2+2x+10=0: Δ=−36\Delta = -36Δ=−36, so x=−2±6i2=−1±3ix = \tfrac{-2 \pm 6i}{2} = -1 \pm 3ix=2−2±6i​=−1±3i.

Rebuild a real quadratic from one non-real root

  1. Confirm the coefficients are required to be real, then conjugate the given root to get the second one.
  2. Add the pair for the sum 2p2p2p, and multiply them for the product p2+q2p^2 + q^2p2+q2, the squared modulus.
  3. Write x2−(sum) x+(product)x^2 - (\text{sum})\,x + (\text{product})x2−(sum)x+(product), scaling by aaa if a leading coefficient is required, then verify by solving.

e.g. Root 3−4i3 - 4i3−4i: sum 666, product 252525, so x2−6x+25=0x^2 - 6x + 25 = 0x2−6x+25=0.

Split one complex equation into two real equations

  1. Simplify each side to standard form, with every part a real expression.
  2. Set the real parts equal, set the imaginary parts equal, solve the real system, and substitute back to check.

e.g. (3x−1)+(y+2)i=8−5i(3x - 1) + (y + 2)i = 8 - 5i(3x−1)+(y+2)i=8−5i gives x=3x = 3x=3 and y=−7y = -7y=−7.

Exam traps

  • Trap Applying a b=ab\sqrt{a}\,\sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}a​b​=ab​ to two negatives: −4⋅−9=36=6\sqrt{-4} \cdot \sqrt{-9} = \sqrt{36} = 6−4​⋅−9​=36​=6.

    Fix That rule was proved for a,b≥0a, b \ge 0a,b≥0 only. Convert first: (2i)(3i)=6i2=−6(2i)(3i) = 6i^2 = -6(2i)(3i)=6i2=−6, the opposite sign.

  • Trap Leaving an i2i^2i2 standing, or reading it as 111.

    Fix Every i2i^2i2 becomes −1-1−1 on sight, so 8−10i+12i−15i28 - 10i + 12i - 15i^28−10i+12i−15i2 finishes at 23+2i23 + 2i23+2i, not −7+2i-7 + 2i−7+2i.

  • Trap Answering "no solution" when the discriminant is negative.

    Fix No REAL solution, but exactly two complex ones, a conjugate pair. Name the number system with the count.

  • Trap Dividing only part of the numerator by 2a2a2a, as 6±4i2=6±2i\tfrac{6 \pm 4i}{2} = 6 \pm 2i26±4i​=6±2i or 3±4i3 \pm 4i3±4i.

    Fix The denominator sits under the whole numerator: 6±4i2=3±2i\tfrac{6 \pm 4i}{2} = 3 \pm 2i26±4i​=3±2i.

  • Trap Using z2z^2z2 where z z‾=∣z∣2z\,\overline{z} = |z|^2zz=∣z∣2 is meant.

    Fix (3+4i)2=−7+24i(3 + 4i)^2 = -7 + 24i(3+4i)2=−7+24i, while (3+4i)(3−4i)=25(3 + 4i)(3 - 4i) = 25(3+4i)(3−4i)=25. The two agree only for real zzz.

  • Trap Computing the gap between zzz and www as ∣z∣−∣w∣|z| - |w|∣z∣−∣w∣.

    Fix Subtract first: the distance is ∣z−w∣|z - w|∣z−w∣. For z=3z = 3z=3 and w=3iw = 3iw=3i it is 323\sqrt{2}32​, while ∣z∣−∣w∣=0|z| - |w| = 0∣z∣−∣w∣=0.

  • Trap Pairing roots as conjugates without checking that the coefficients are real.

    Fix x2−2ix−1=(x−i)2x^2 - 2ix - 1 = (x - i)^2x2−2ix−1=(x−i)2 has the double root iii, and −i-i−i is not a root at all.
