  1. Problem 1 A shifted square

    Solve (x−2)2+6=0(x-2)^2+6=0(x−2)2+6=0 over the complex numbers.

    1. Hint 1

      Isolate the squared expression before taking square roots.

    2. Hint 2

      Both square roots of a negative real number are needed.

    Answer

    x=2±i6x=2\pm i\sqrt6x=2±i6​.

    Full solution

    The equation gives (x−2)2=−6(x-2)^2=-6(x−2)2=−6, so

    x−2=±i6x-2=\pm i\sqrt6x−2=±i6​

    Add 222 to obtain

    x=2±i6.x=2\pm i\sqrt6.x=2±i6​.

    Each candidate makes the square −6-6−6 and the original left side zero.

    Answer

    x=2±i6x=2\pm i\sqrt6x=2±i6​.

    Key idea

    A shifted square gives two complex roots when the isolated square is negative.

  3. Problem 3 An incomplete coefficient record

    (−5)2+42=41(-5)^2+4^2=41(−5)2+42=41

    A monic quadratic has negative root sum as its linear coefficient and root product as its constant, giving x2+10x+41x^2+10x+41x2+10x+41.

    Answer

    x2+10x+41x^2+10x+41x2+10x+41.

    Key idea

    The sum and imaginary parts of conjugate roots determine their monic quadratic.

  10. Problem 10 A count in two systems

    A report says the equation (x−4)2+2=0(x-4)^2+2=0(x−4)2+2=0 has no roots, but a second report says it has two. Resolve the disagreement, giving the roots and explaining what a negative discriminant says once complex numbers are allowed.

    1. Hint 1

      Each report needs to state its number system.

    2. Hint 2

    No real roots; two complex roots 4±i24\pm i\sqrt24±i2​. A negative discriminant places the roots off the real line.

    Full solution

    The equation gives (x−4)2=−2(x-4)^2=-2(x−4)2=−2, which has no real solution.

    Over the complex numbers it gives

    x=4±i2x=4\pm i\sqrt2x=4±i2​

    For either root, x−4=±i2x-4=\pm i\sqrt2x−4=±i2​, so

    (x−4)2=−2.(x-4)^2=-2.(x−4)2=−2.

    The expanded quadratic has discriminant −8-8−8, which specifies a distinct nonreal conjugate pair; it no longer means that roots are missing from the complex system.

    Answer

    No real roots; two complex roots 4±i24\pm i\sqrt24±i2​. A negative discriminant places the roots off the real line.

    Key idea

    The number system determines whether a quadratic refusal is about all roots or just real roots.
