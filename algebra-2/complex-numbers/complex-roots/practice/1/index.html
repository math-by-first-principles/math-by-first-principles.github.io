    Counting with multiplicity, how many roots does every quadratic equation have once complex numbers are allowed?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Over the complex numbers every quadratic factors into two linear factors.

    ax2+bx+c=a(x−r1)(x−r2)ax^2 + bx + c = a(x - r_1)(x - r_2)ax2+bx+c=a(x−r1​)(x−r2​)

    If Δ>0\Delta > 0Δ>0 the two roots are distinct reals, if Δ=0\Delta = 0Δ=0 the one real root counts twice, and if Δ<0\Delta < 0Δ<0 the roots are a conjugate pair. In every case the count with multiplicity is exactly 222; the discriminant only decides where the roots live, not how many there are.
