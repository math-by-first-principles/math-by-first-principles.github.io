  1. Problem 1 A plotted marker

    The figure shows the point AAA in the complex plane. Write the complex number represented by AAA.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the Real axis running from -5 to 5 and the Imaginary axis running from -5 to 5, gridlines and number labels at every integer. A single solid point, labeled A, is plotted one unit to the right of the origin and four units below it. No coordinates or complex number are printed.

    1. Hint 1

      The horizontal coordinate is the real part.

    2. Hint 2

      The vertical coordinate is the real coefficient multiplying iii.

    Answer

    1−4i1-4i1−4i.

    Full solution

    The grid places AAA one unit right and four units down from the origin.

    A=1+(−4)iA=1+(-4)iA=1+(−4)i

    Thus the represented number is 1−4i1-4i1−4i.

    Answer

    1−4i1-4i1−4i.

    Key idea

    The two coordinates of a plotted complex number are its real and imaginary parts.

  2. Problem 2 A displacement length

    Find ∣(−1+6i)−(−3−i)∣|(-1+6i)-(-3-i)|∣(−1+6i)−(−3−i)∣ exactly.

    1. Hint 1

      The modulus of a difference measures the length of the displacement.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the parts first, then apply the Pythagorean theorem.

    Answer

    53\sqrt{53}53​.

    Full solution

    The difference is 2+7i2+7i2+7i.

    Its length is

    ∣2+7i∣=22+72|2+7i|=\sqrt{2^2+7^2}∣2+7i∣=22+72​

    This gives 53\sqrt{53}53​, consistent with a horizontal gap of 222 and a vertical gap of 777.

    Answer

    53\sqrt{53}53​.

    Key idea

    Distance between complex points is the modulus of their difference.

  3. Problem 3 A reflected marker

    The figure shows a complex number BBB. State the coordinates of the point for B‾\overline BB.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the Real axis running from -5 to 5 and the Imaginary axis running from -5 to 5, gridlines and number labels at every integer. A single solid point, labeled B, is plotted two units to the right of the origin and four units below it. Its conjugate is not shown.

    1. Hint 1

      Conjugation reflects a point across the horizontal real axis.

    2. Hint 2

      Keep the horizontal coordinate and reverse the vertical coordinate.

    Answer

    (2,4)(2,4)(2,4).

    Full solution

    The plotted point is (2,−4)(2,-4)(2,−4).

    Conjugation changes 2−4i2-4i2−4i into 2+4i2+4i2+4i, so the reflected coordinates are (2,4)(2,4)(2,4).

    The two points lie equally far below and above the real axis.

    Answer

    (2,4)(2,4)(2,4).

    Key idea

    Conjugation preserves the real coordinate and reverses the imaginary coordinate.

  4. Problem 4 A completed route

    The figure shows an arrow from 000 to zzz and, starting at zzz, a copy of the arrow for www. Read zzz and www, calculate z+wz+wz+w, and give the remaining corner www of the addition parallelogram.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the Real axis from -4 to 5 and the Imaginary axis from -1 to 5, gridlines and number labels at every integer. A solid arrow starts at the origin and ends at a point labeled z, two units left and one unit up from the origin. A second, dashed arrow labeled w starts at that same point and ends at a solid point labeled E, three units right and two units up from z. No coordinate pairs are printed.

    1. Hint 1

      The second arrow represents a displacement, not its endpoint coordinates.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract its starting coordinates from its ending coordinates to read www.

    Answer

    z=−2+iz=-2+iz=−2+i; w=3+2iw=3+2iw=3+2i; z+w=1+3iz+w=1+3iz+w=1+3i; remaining corner (3,2)(3,2)(3,2).

    Full solution

    The first arrow ends at (−2,1)(-2,1)(−2,1), so z=−2+iz=-2+iz=−2+i.

    The second runs from (−2,1)(-2,1)(−2,1) to (1,3)(1,3)(1,3), a displacement of (3,2)(3,2)(3,2), so w=3+2iw=3+2iw=3+2i.

    z+w=(−2+3)+(1+2)iz+w=(-2+3)+(1+2)iz+w=(−2+3)+(1+2)i

    Thus z+w=1+3iz+w=1+3iz+w=1+3i.

    Starting the same displacement at 000 reaches (3,2)(3,2)(3,2), the missing corner; either route to the final point gives the same sum.

    Answer

    z=−2+iz=-2+iz=−2+i; w=3+2iw=3+2iw=3+2i; z+w=1+3iz+w=1+3iz+w=1+3i; remaining corner (3,2)(3,2)(3,2).

    Key idea

    Addition preserves a translated arrow's coordinate change, wherever it starts.

  5. Problem 5 A circle record

    The figure shows a circle in the complex plane and its center CCC. Write its equation in the form ∣z−c∣=r|z-c|=r∣z−c∣=r. Then decide whether −1+i-1+i−1+i lies inside, on, or outside the circle.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the Real axis from -6 to 2 and the Imaginary axis from -5 to 3, gridlines and number labels at every integer. A circle of radius three grid units is centered at a marked point labeled C, two units left and one unit below the origin. No center coordinates, radius, or equation is printed.

    1. Hint 1

      Read the center and measure the radius using equal grid units.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the distance from −1+i-1+i−1+i to the center with the radius.

    Answer

    ∣z−(−2−i)∣=3|z-(-2-i)|=3∣z−(−2−i)∣=3; −1+i-1+i−1+i lies inside.

    Full solution

    The center is c=−2−ic=-2-ic=−2−i and the radius is 333, giving

    ∣z−(−2−i)∣=3|z-(-2-i)|=3∣z−(−2−i)∣=3

    For the tested point, the displacement is 1+2i1+2i1+2i, whose squared length is 555.

    Since 5<95<95<9, its distance is less than 333, so it lies inside.

    Answer

    ∣z−(−2−i)∣=3|z-(-2-i)|=3∣z−(−2−i)∣=3; −1+i-1+i−1+i lies inside.

    Key idea

    A modulus equation specifies all points at a fixed distance from its center.

  6. Problem 6 An unknown scale

    Complex numbers uuu and vvv satisfy ∣u∣=5|u|=\sqrt5∣u∣=5​ and ∣uv∣=15|uv|=15∣uv∣=15. Find ∣v∣|v|∣v∣ exactly, then find ∣uv‾∣|u\overline v|∣uv∣.

    Answer

    Full solution

    Divide ∣uv∣=∣u∣∣v∣|uv|=|u||v|∣uv∣=∣u∣∣v∣ by the positive number 5\sqrt55​.

    ∣v∣=155|v|=\frac{15}{\sqrt5}∣v∣=5​15​

    Rationalizing gives 353\sqrt535​.

    Since ∣v‾∣=∣v∣|\overline v|=|v|∣v∣=∣v∣,

    ∣uv‾∣=5(35)|u\overline v|=\sqrt5(3\sqrt5)∣uv∣=5​(35​)

    This equals 151515, checking the product relation.

    Answer

    ∣v∣=35|v|=3\sqrt5∣v∣=35​; ∣uv‾∣=15|u\overline v|=15∣uv∣=15.

    Key idea

    Conjugation preserves modulus, and modulus is multiplicative.

  7. Problem 7 A shifted circle

    Every point zzz on ∣z−(1−2i)∣=2|z-(1-2i)|=2∣z−(1−2i)∣=2 is moved by adding −3+i-3+i−3+i. Describe the resulting circle by its center, radius, and a modulus equation in the new point www.

    1. Hint 1

      Adding the same number to every point is a rigid slide.

    2. Hint 2

      The center moves by the same addition, and distances from it stay fixed.

    Answer

    Center −2−i-2-i−2−i, radius 222; ∣w−(−2−i)∣=2|w-(-2-i)|=2∣w−(−2−i)∣=2.

    Full solution

    The center moves to

    (1−2i)+(−3+i)=−2−i(1-2i)+(-3+i)=-2-i(1−2i)+(−3+i)=−2−i

    The slide preserves radius 222.

    Since w=z−3+iw=z-3+iw=z−3+i, the new displacement from the center equals the old one, so the resulting circle is

    ∣w−(−2−i)∣=2|w-(-2-i)|=2∣w−(−2−i)∣=2

    Answer

    Center −2−i-2-i−2−i, radius 222; ∣w−(−2−i)∣=2|w-(-2-i)|=2∣w−(−2−i)∣=2.

    Key idea

    A translation moves a circle and its center together without changing its radius.

  9. Problem 9 A comparison of markers

    A student says z=4+7iz=4+7iz=4+7i and w=7+4iw=7+4iw=7+4i have the same distance from zero but are different numbers. Is the statement correct? Explain, and say whether equal distances would justify writing z=wz=wz=w.

    1. Hint 1

      Equality compares parts, while modulus compares distances.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the sums of squares and then compare the coordinate pairs.

    Answer

    Correct; both moduli are 65\sqrt{65}65​, but z≠wz\ne wz=w. Equal distances do not justify equality.

    Full solution

    The two squared moduli are

    ∣z∣2=42+72=65∣w∣2=72+42=65\begin{aligned} |z|^2&=4^2+7^2 \\ &=65 \\ |w|^2&=7^2+4^2 \\ &=65 \end{aligned}∣z∣2∣w∣2​=42+72=65=72+42=65​

    The real parts 444 and 777 differ, so the numbers differ even though the moduli match.

    Equal modulus locates points on the same circle about zero; it does not identify one point.

    Answer

    Correct; both moduli are 65\sqrt{65}65​, but z≠wz\ne wz=w. Equal distances do not justify equality.

    Key idea

    Equal distance from zero does not imply equal complex coordinates.

  10. Problem 10 Two possible routes

    The pair z=2z=2z=2, w=−7w=-7w=−7 has the required moduli and sum −5-5−5, whose modulus is 555.

    Answer
