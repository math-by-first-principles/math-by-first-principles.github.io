  1. Problem 1 Two evaluations

    A polynomial ppp has real coefficients and p(5+i)=3−2ip(5+i)=3-2ip(5+i)=3−2i. Find p(5−i)p(5-i)p(5−i).

    1. Hint 1

      Conjugation passes through a polynomial whose coefficients are real.

    2. Hint 2

      Conjugate the known output along with the input.

    Answer

    3+2i3+2i3+2i.

    Full solution

    Real coefficients are unchanged by conjugation, so

    p(z‾)=p(z)‾p(\overline z)=\overline{p(z)}p(z)=p(z)​

    The input 5−i5-i5−i is the conjugate of 5+i5+i5+i.

    Its output is therefore the conjugate of 3−2i3-2i3−2i, namely 3+2i3+2i3+2i.

    Answer

    3+2i3+2i3+2i.

    Key idea

    A polynomial with real coefficients takes conjugate inputs to conjugate outputs.

  2. Problem 2 Testing a substitution rule

    This simplifies to 3−i3-i3−i.

    The conjugate of 1+i1+i1+i is 1−i1-i1−i, so q(i)‾=1−i\overline{q(i)}=1-iq(i)​=1−i, while q(−i)=3−iq(-i)=3-iq(−i)=3−i, and these are unequal.

    q(−1−i)=2−2i≠0.q(-1-i)=2-2i\neq0.q(−1−i)=2−2i=0.

  3. Problem 3 A rational quadratic

    A monic quadratic with rational coefficients has a root −3+2-3+\sqrt2−3+2​. Find its constant coefficient.

    1. Hint 1

      Rational coefficients determine the other root.

    2. Hint 2

      The constant coefficient of a monic quadratic is the product of its roots.

    Answer

    777.

    Full solution

    The surd conjugate −3−2-3-\sqrt2−3−2​ must be the other root.

    Their product is

    (−3+2)(−3−2)=7(-3+\sqrt2)(-3-\sqrt2)=7(−3+2​)(−3−2​)=7

    The middle terms cancel and the constant is 9−2=79-2=79−2=7.

    The corresponding quadratic is x2+6x+7x^2+6x+7x2+6x+7, which checks the required rational coefficients.

    Answer

    777.

    Key idea

    A surd conjugate pair has a rational product.

  6. Problem 6 Odd degree forces a real root

    A real polynomial has odd degree 111111 and splits over the reals into linear factors and quadratic factors with negative discriminant, counting repeated factors separately, with two more linear factors than quadratic factors. First explain in general why any real polynomial of odd degree must have at least one real root. Then apply that reasoning here: how many factors of each kind are present, and how many real roots are there with multiplicity?

    1. Hint 1

      Every real polynomial splits into linear factors and quadratic factors with negative discriminant, and a quadratic factor always contributes an even amount to the degree.

    2. Hint 2

      Write both the degree equation and the given difference between factor counts to find the specific factor counts.

    Answer

    Odd degree forces an odd, hence nonzero, count of linear factors, so a real root always exists; here, 555 linear factors, 333 quadratic factors, and 555 real roots with multiplicity.

    Full solution

    Every real polynomial factors into real linear factors and real quadratic factors with negative discriminant.

    If kkk counts the linear factors and mmm the quadratics, the degree is

    n=k+2mn=k+2mn=k+2m

    Since 2m2m2m is always even, kkk has the same parity as nnn.

    An odd degree nnn forces kkk to be odd, and an odd number is never 000, so at least one linear factor exists, and it supplies a real root.

    Apply this with n=11n=11n=11.

    Let kkk count linear factors and mmm count quadratics.

    Then

    k+2m=11k+2m=11k+2m=11 k=m+2k=m+2k=m+2

    Substitution gives 3m+2=113m+2=113m+2=11, so m=3m=3m=3 and k=5k=5k=5.

    Each linear factor supplies one real root, so this polynomial has 555 real roots counted with multiplicity, and 555 is indeed odd, matching the general argument.

    The count 5+2(3)=115+2(3)=115+2(3)=11 also checks the total degree.

    Answer

    Odd degree forces an odd, hence nonzero, count of linear factors, so a real root always exists; here, 555 linear factors, 333 quadratic factors, and 555 real roots with multiplicity.

    Key idea

    Odd degree forces an odd, hence nonzero, count of linear factors, so a real root always exists.

  8. Problem 8 Equal outputs

    A polynomial ppp has real coefficients. A student claims: "If p(4+3i)=p(4−3i)p(4+3i)=p(4-3i)p(4+3i)=p(4−3i), their common value must be real." Is the claim true? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      The outputs at conjugate inputs are themselves conjugates.

    2. Hint 2

      Ask which complex numbers equal their own conjugates.

    Answer

    True; the common value is real.

    Full solution

    Let w=p(4+3i)w=p(4+3i)w=p(4+3i).

    Since the coefficients are real,

    p(4−3i)=w‾p(4-3i)=\overline wp(4−3i)=w

    The claimed equality therefore says

    w=w‾.w=\overline w.w=w.

    If w=a+biw=a+biw=a+bi with real a,ba,ba,b, matching imaginary parts gives b=−bb=-bb=−b, so b=0b=0b=0.

    Thus the common output is real.

    Answer

    True; the common value is real.

    Key idea

    Equal conjugate outputs must have zero imaginary part.

  10. Problem 10 Scaling a root pair

    Conversely, real ccc makes c,6c,10cc,6c,10cc,6c,10c all real.
