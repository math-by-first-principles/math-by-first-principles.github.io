    What is the multiplicity of the root −3-3−3 in p(x)=x4+3x3−7x2−15x+18p(x) = x^4 + 3x^3 - 7x^2 - 15x + 18p(x)=x4+3x3−7x2−15x+18?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Divide by (x+3)(x + 3)(x+3) repeatedly and stop when a remainder is nonzero. Synthetic division of 1,3,−7,−15,181, 3, -7, -15, 181,3,−7,−15,18 by −3-3−3 gives the row 1,0,−7,61, 0, -7, 61,0,−7,6 with remainder 000, so the quotient is x3−7x+6x^3 - 7x + 6x3−7x+6.

    Divide again. Synthetic division of 1,0,−7,61, 0, -7, 61,0,−7,6 by −3-3−3 gives 1,−3,21, -3, 21,−3,2 with remainder 000, so the new quotient is x2−3x+2x^2 - 3x + 2x2−3x+2.

    Now test a third time.

    (−3)2−3(−3)+2=9+9+2=20≠0(-3)^2 - 3(-3) + 2 = 9 + 9 + 2 = 20 \ne 0(−3)2−3(−3)+2=9+9+2=20=0

    The third division fails, so (x+3)(x+3)(x+3) divides exactly twice and the multiplicity is 222. In full, p(x)=(x+3)2(x−1)(x−2)p(x) = (x + 3)^2(x - 1)(x - 2)p(x)=(x+3)2(x−1)(x−2).

    A polynomial of degree 333 with complex coefficients has leading coefficient 222 and the single root iii of multiplicity 333. What is p(x)p(x)p(x) expanded?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    A root iii of multiplicity 333 means the factor (x−i)(x - i)(x−i) appears three times, so p(x)=2(x−i)3p(x) = 2(x - i)^3p(x)=2(x−i)3.

    Expand using i2=−1i^2 = -1i2=−1 and i3=−ii^3 = -ii3=−i, so that (−i)2=−1(-i)^2 = -1(−i)2=−1 and (−i)3=i(-i)^3 = i(−i)3=i.

    (x−i)3=x3−3ix2+3x(−i)2+(−i)3=x3−3ix2−3x+i(x - i)^3 = x^3 - 3ix^2 + 3x(-i)^2 + (-i)^3 = x^3 - 3ix^2 - 3x + i(x−i)3=x3−3ix2+3x(−i)2+(−i)3=x3−3ix2−3x+i

    Multiplying by 222 gives 2x3−6ix2−6x+2i2x^3 - 6ix^2 - 6x + 2i2x3−6ix2−6x+2i. Checking at x=ix = ix=i: 2i3−6i(i2)−6i+2i=−2i+6i−6i+2i=02i^3 - 6i(i^2) - 6i + 2i = -2i + 6i - 6i + 2i = 02i3−6i(i2)−6i+2i=−2i+6i−6i+2i=0, as it must be.

  3. 3

    Let ppp have degree n≥1n \ge 1n≥1 with complex coefficients. Which statement is FALSE?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The complete factorization p(x)=a(x−r1)⋯(x−rn)p(x) = a(x - r_1)\cdots(x - r_n)p(x)=a(x−r1​)⋯(x−rn​) has exactly nnn linear factors, which makes the first, second, and fourth statements true.

    The third fails whenever a factor repeats.

    x2−2x+1=(x−1)2x^2 - 2x + 1 = (x - 1)^2x2−2x+1=(x−1)2

    This has degree 222 but only one distinct root. A polynomial has exactly nnn distinct roots precisely when every root is simple, which is a special case rather than the rule.

  4. 4

    A polynomial of degree 666 with complex coefficients has exactly three distinct roots: 111 with multiplicity 333, −2-2−2 with multiplicity 222, and iii with multiplicity mmm. Find mmm.

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The multiplicities of the distinct roots add up to the degree, since each of the six linear factors carries exactly one root.

    3+2+m=63 + 2 + m = 63+2+m=6

    So m=1m = 1m=1. The multiplicity cannot be 000, because iii was given as a root, and a multiplicity of 000 would mean the factor (x−i)(x - i)(x−i) never appears.

  5. 5

    Which polynomial has degree 555 but only 222 distinct roots?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Add the exponents to get the degree, then count the different numbers named by the factors.

    (x−1)4(x+6):degree 4+1=5,roots 1 and −6(x - 1)^4(x + 6): \quad \text{degree } 4 + 1 = 5, \quad \text{roots } 1 \text{ and } -6(x−1)4(x+6):degree 4+1=5,roots 1 and −6

    The second choice has five distinct roots, the third has only one, and the fourth has three. Only the first has degree 555 with exactly 222 distinct roots.

  6. 6

    Which polynomial has degree 444, leading coefficient 333, and roots exactly 000, 222 (multiplicity 222), and −1-1−1?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Write one factor per root, repeated as many times as its multiplicity, and put the leading coefficient in front. The root 000 contributes the factor xxx.

    p(x)=3⋅x⋅(x−2)2⋅(x+1)p(x) = 3 \cdot x \cdot (x - 2)^2 \cdot (x + 1)p(x)=3⋅x⋅(x−2)2⋅(x+1)

    The degree is 1+2+1=41 + 2 + 1 = 41+2+1=4, as required. The first choice has degree 333 and no root at 000, the third gives 000 multiplicity 222 and 222 multiplicity 111 (the multiplicities are swapped), and the last has leading coefficient 111.

  7. 7

    One root of p(x)=x2+(1−i)x−ip(x) = x^2 + (1 - i)x - ip(x)=x2+(1−i)x−i is iii. What is the other root?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Since iii is a root, (x−i)(x - i)(x−i) is a factor, and the other factor must be (x−r)(x - r)(x−r) for the second root rrr. Compare the constant terms: (−i)(−r)=−i(-i)(-r) = -i(−i)(−r)=−i forces r=−1r = -1r=−1.

    Check by expanding.

    (x−i)(x+1)=x2+x−ix−i=x2+(1−i)x−i(x - i)(x + 1) = x^2 + x - ix - i = x^2 + (1 - i)x - i(x−i)(x+1)=x2+x−ix−i=x2+(1−i)x−i

    That is exactly ppp, so the other root is −1-1−1. Notice this polynomial has non-real coefficients, and its two roots are not conjugates of each other.

  8. 8

    Which of these is NOT something the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra (together with the Factor Theorem) gives you?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The theorem is an existence statement. It says a root is there; it never says how to find it.

    The first choice is the theorem itself, the second follows by dividing out one root at a time, and the third is the closure statement those two together deliver.

    p(x)=a(x−r1)(x−r2)⋯(x−rn)p(x) = a(x - r_1)(x - r_2)\cdots(x - r_n)p(x)=a(x−r1​)(x−r2​)⋯(x−rn​)

    No formula is included, and for degree 555 and above Ruffini and Abel proved that no general formula in radicals exists at all.

    Solution

    The first factor gives the root 111, twice. Solve the quadratic factor with the quadratic formula, whose discriminant is 22−4(1)(5)=−162^2 - 4(1)(5) = -1622−4(1)(5)=−16.

    x=−2±−162=−2±4i2=−1±2ix = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{-16}}{2} = \frac{-2 \pm 4i}{2} = -1 \pm 2ix=2−2±−16​​=2−2±4i​=−1±2i

    So the distinct roots are 111, −1+2i-1 + 2i−1+2i, and −1−2i-1 - 2i−1−2i. The degree is 444, and the multiplicities 2+1+12 + 1 + 12+1+1 add to 444, as they must.

    How many distinct roots does p(x)=x5−x3p(x) = x^5 - x^3p(x)=x5−x3 have?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Factor out the largest common power of xxx, then use the difference of squares.

    x5−x3=x3(x2−1)=x3(x−1)(x+1)x^5 - x^3 = x^3(x^2 - 1) = x^3(x - 1)(x + 1)x5−x3=x3(x2−1)=x3(x−1)(x+1)

    The degree is 555, so there are five roots counted with multiplicity: 000 three times, plus 111 and −1-1−1. Only three different numbers appear, so there are 333 distinct roots.

    What are the roots of p(x)=x3−1p(x) = x^3 - 1p(x)=x3−1 over the complex numbers?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Since p(1)=0p(1) = 0p(1)=0, the Factor Theorem gives the factor (x−1)(x - 1)(x−1). Synthetic division of 1,0,0,−11, 0, 0, -11,0,0,−1 by 111 produces the row 1,1,11, 1, 11,1,1 with remainder 000, so the quotient is x2+x+1x^2 + x + 1x2+x+1.

    Apply the quadratic formula to the quotient, whose discriminant is 1−4=−31 - 4 = -31−4=−3.

    x=−1±−32=−1±i32x = \frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{-3}}{2} = \frac{-1 \pm i\sqrt{3}}{2}x=2−1±−3​​=2−1±i3​​

    A cubic has three roots, and here they are: 111 and the two non-real ones. Only one of them is real, which is why "the cube root of 111" is a misleading phrase.
