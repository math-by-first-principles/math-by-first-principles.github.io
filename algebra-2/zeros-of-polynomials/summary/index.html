Zeros of Polynomials: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Multiplicity, simple, repeated
The exponent mmm in p(x)=(x−r)mq(x)p(x) = (x - r)^m q(x)p(x)=(x−r)mq(x), q(r)≠0q(r) \neq 0q(r)=0: how many times (x−r)(x - r)(x−r) divides ppp. m=1m = 1m=1 is simple, m≥2m \ge 2m≥2 repeated.
Counted with multiplicity, versus distinct
The root list repeats a root as often as its multiplicity; the distinct roots are the values on it. (x−2)3(x+1)(x - 2)^3(x + 1)(x−2)3(x+1): four with multiplicity, two distinct.
Complex conjugate z‾\overline{z}z
a+bi‾=a−bi\overline{a + bi} = a - bia+bi​=a−bi. Respects +++ and ×\times×, and fixes exactly the reals: z‾=z\overline{z} = zz=z if and only if zzz is real.
Surd conjugate s∗s^{*}s∗
(a+bd)∗=a−bd(a + b\sqrt{d})^{*} = a - b\sqrt{d}(a+bd​)∗=a−bd​, from the swap d↦−d\sqrt{d} \mapsto -\sqrt{d}d​↦−d​. A DIFFERENT swap from the bar, fixing exactly the rationals: 2+52 + \sqrt{5}2+5​ is real, so 2+5‾\overline{2 + \sqrt{5}}2+5​​ is itself.
Elementary symmetric function eke_kek​
The sum of every product of kkk different ENTRIES of the root list: e1e_1e1​ the roots, e2e_2e2​ all pairwise products, ene_nen​ the product of all. Swapping two roots leaves it unchanged.
Depressed polynomial
The quotient left after dividing out a root, one degree lower. The remaining roots are hunted there.
Sign chart, test point
The real zeros cut the line into intervals; the chart records the sign PPP holds on each. A test point is one value strictly inside an interval, never a zero.
Turning point
Where the graph stops rising and starts falling, or the reverse. It need not lie on the axis, and a zero need not be one.

Formulas and theorems

  • Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, and complete factorization

    p(x)=a(x−r1)(x−r2)⋯(x−rn)p(x) = a(x - r_1)(x - r_2)\cdots(x - r_n)p(x)=a(x−r1​)(x−r2​)⋯(x−rn​)

    Use when Degree n≥1n \ge 1n≥1; coefficients may be complex, because dividing out a root can leave a quotient whose coefficients are no longer real. a≠0a \neq 0a=0 is the leading coefficient; the rir_iri​ are listed with multiplicity and are the ONLY roots. Existence only: it names no root. A constant such as p(x)=7p(x) = 7p(x)=7 has none.

  • Root Counting Theorem

    m1+m2+⋯+mk=nm_1 + m_2 + \cdots + m_k = nm1​+m2​+⋯+mk​=n

    Use when s1,…,sks_1, \ldots, s_ks1​,…,sk​ the DISTINCT roots with multiplicities mim_imi​, over C\mathbb{C}C, n≥1n \ge 1n≥1. So exactly nnn roots counted with multiplicity, at most nnn distinct, and exactly nnn distinct precisely when every root is simple. For a general complex-coefficient polynomial the real count is unconstrained, from 000 to nnn; real-coefficient polynomials carry the extra parity restriction below.

  • Conjugate root theorem, and the real quadratic it forces

    p(z)=0 ⟹ p(z‾)=0(x−z)(x−z‾)=x2−2ax+(a2+b2)\begin{gathered} p(z) = 0 \ \Longrightarrow \ p(\overline{z}) = 0 \\ (x - z)(x - \overline{z}) \\ = x^2 - 2ax + (a^2 + b^2) \end{gathered}p(z)=0 ⟹ p(z)=0(x−z)(x−z)=x2−2ax+(a2+b2)​

    Use when EVERY coefficient real, and z=a+biz = a + biz=a+bi with b≠0b \neq 0b=0 for the factor. Strip that and it dies: x−ix - ix−i has root iii and not −i-i−i. Not reversible: i(x2+1)i(x^2 + 1)i(x2+1) pairs i,−ii, -ii,−i with nonreal coefficients. Real roots are their own conjugates; nonreal ones pair with EQUAL multiplicity. The factor's discriminant −4b2<0-4b^2 < 0−4b2<0 keeps it unsplittable over R\mathbb{R}R.

  • Surd conjugate theorem, and its factor

    p(a+bd )=0⟹ p(a−bd )=0\begin{gathered} p\big(a + b\sqrt{d}\,\big) = 0 \\ \Longrightarrow \ p\big(a - b\sqrt{d}\,\big) = 0 \end{gathered}p(a+bd​)=0⟹ p(a−bd​)=0​

    Use when Coefficients RATIONAL, strictly more than real; aaa, bbb, ddd all rational, b≠0b \neq 0b=0, d\sqrt{d}d​ irrational. The pair forces the factor x2−2ax+(a2−b2d)x^2 - 2ax + (a^2 - b^2d)x2−2ax+(a2−b2d). That ddd is rational is load-bearing: drop it and the swap can fail to pair a root with a genuine partner.

    e.g. A root 2+52 + \sqrt{5}2+5​ forces the factor x2−4x−1x^2 - 4x - 1x2−4x−1.

  • Factoring a real polynomial over R\mathbb{R}R, and the parity it forces

    p(x)=a(x−r1)⋯(x−rk)⋅ d1(x)⋯dm(x)n=k+2m\begin{gathered} p(x) = a(x - r_1)\cdots(x - r_k) \\ \cdot\ d_1(x)\cdots d_m(x) \\ n = k + 2m \end{gathered}p(x)=a(x−r1​)⋯(x−rk​)⋅ d1​(x)⋯dm​(x)n=k+2m​

    Use when Real coefficients, n≥1n \ge 1n≥1, each rir_iri​ real and each djd_jdj​ a real quadratic of negative discriminant. The nonreal roots number 2m2m2m, always EVEN, so kkk and nnn share a parity. Odd nnn forces k≥1k \ge 1k≥1: at least one real root, possibly exactly one. A real cubic has 111 or 333 real roots WITH MULTIPLICITY, never 000.

  • Vieta's formulas

    ek=(−1)k an−kan,k=1,2,…,ne_k = (-1)^k\,\frac{a_{n-k}}{a_n}, \qquad k = 1, 2, \ldots, nek​=(−1)kan​an−k​​,k=1,2,…,n

    Use when an≠0a_n \neq 0an​=0, and r1,…,rnr_1, \ldots, r_nr1​,…,rn​ the COMPLETE list over C\mathbb{C}C, repeats repeated and nonreal roots kept.   • Multiplicity decides how the graph meets the axis

    P(x)=(x−r)mG(x), G(r)≠0m odd  ⟺  crossesm even  ⟺  touches and turns back\begin{gathered} P(x) = (x - r)^m G(x), \ G(r) \neq 0 \\ m \ \text{odd} \iff \text{crosses} \\ m \ \text{even} \iff \text{touches and turns back} \end{gathered}P(x)=(x−r)mG(x), G(r)=0m odd⟺crossesm even⟺touches and turns back​
    Text description

    A curve passing straight through the axis at a zero of odd multiplicity, and meeting the axis at a zero of even multiplicity without crossing, turning back the way it came.

    Use when PPP real, rrr a REAL zero. Both run both ways, and m=1m = 1m=1 is odd, so a simple zero crosses. Even mmm makes (r,0)(r, 0)(r,0) a turning point. A larger mmm only flattens the curve; it never changes which happens. Nonreal roots give no xxx-intercept: −(x−2)2(x2+1)-(x - 2)^2(x^2 + 1)−(x−2)2(x2+1) has degree 444 and one intercept.

    e.g. (x−3)2(x+1)(x - 3)^2(x + 1)(x−3)2(x+1): P(2)=3P(2) = 3P(2)=3, P(4)=5P(4) = 5P(4)=5, so it touches at 333; P(−2)=−25P(-2) = -25P(−2)=−25, P(0)=9P(0) = 9P(0)=9, so it crosses at −1-1−1.

  • End behavior, all four cases

    P(x) follows anxn for large ∣x∣P(x) \ \text{follows} \ a_nx^n \ \text{for large} \ |x|P(x) follows an​xn for large ∣x∣
    Text description

    Four miniature graphs in a grid: even degree sends both arms the same way, up for a positive leading coefficient and down for a negative one, while odd degree sends them opposite ways.

    Use when Real PPP of degree n≥1n \ge 1n≥1, leading coefficient an≠0a_n \neq 0an​=0. Even nnn: both arms up if an>0a_n > 0an​>0, both down if an<0a_n < 0an​<0. Odd nnn: left down and right up if an>0a_n > 0an​>0, left up and right down if an<0a_n < 0an​<0.

  • Sign changes, and which turning points are reachable

    P(u) P(v)<0⟹ P(c)=0 for some cbetween u and v\begin{gathered} P(u)\,P(v) < 0 \\ \Longrightarrow \ P(c) = 0 \ \text{for some} \ c \\ \text{between} \ u \ \text{and} \ v \end{gathered}P(u)P(v)<0⟹ P(c)=0 for some cbetween u and v​

    Use when Real PPP, unbroken graph. The converse fails, an even zero having no sign change: a change REQUIRES a real zero, a real zero does not force one. So PPP holds one sign on any interval free of real zeros, and one test point settles it. At most n−1n - 1n−1 turning points, at least one strictly between consecutive real zeros; their coordinates are GENERALLY beyond algebra apart from a parabola's vertex and the even zeros themselves.

Problem types, step by step

Factor completely and list every root

  1. Rational Root Theorem: candidates ±pq\pm\frac{p}{q}±qp​, ppp dividing the constant term, qqq the leading coefficient. Test by synthetic division.
  2. If a nonreal or surd root is given, check the coefficient system, pair it, and divide by the quadratic that pair forces.
  3. Divide out each confirmed root and continue on the depressed polynomial; at a quadratic use the quadratic formula, keeping nonreal roots.
  4. Expand the factorization, and substitute each root into the ORIGINAL polynomial.

e.g. x3−4x2+9x−10x^3 - 4x^2 + 9x - 10x3−4x2+9x−10 has root 222, quotient x2−2x+5x^2 - 2x + 5x2−2x+5, so the roots are 222, 1+2i1 + 2i1+2i, 1−2i1 - 2i1−2i.

Measure the multiplicity of a known root

  1. Divide ppp by (x−r)(x - r)(x−r) and confirm remainder 000.
  2. Divide the QUOTIENT by (x−r)(x - r)(x−r) again, not the original.
  3. Repeat until a remainder is nonzero; the multiplicity is the number of clean divisions.
  4. Check the multiplicities of all distinct roots add to the degree.

e.g. x4−5x3+6x2+4x−8=(x−2)3(x+1)x^4 - 5x^3 + 6x^2 + 4x - 8 = (x - 2)^3(x + 1)x4−5x3+6x2+4x−8=(x−2)3(x+1), so 222 has multiplicity 333 and 3+1=43 + 1 = 43+1=4.

Build the polynomial of least degree from a root list

  1. Read which coefficient system is demanded: real forces conjugate partners, rational forces surd conjugates too, complex forces nothing.
  2. Add every partner, matching multiplicities, and count factors for the least degree.
  3. Multiply each pair into its quadratic first, clearing the iii or the radical.
  4. Multiply the remaining linear factors in, then fix the leading coefficient from a given point or a monic requirement.

e.g. Real coefficients, roots 222 and 1−4i1 - 4i1−4i: (x−2)(x2−2x+17)=x3−4x2+21x−34(x - 2)(x^2 - 2x + 17) = x^3 - 4x^2 + 21x - 34(x−2)(x2−2x+17)=x3−4x2+21x−34.

Extract a symmetric quantity without finding the roots

  1. Standard form, listing every coefficient, with 000 entered for missing terms.
  2. Compute the eke_kek​ needed from ek=(−1)kan−kane_k = (-1)^k\frac{a_{n-k}}{a_n}ek​=(−1)kan​an−k​​, dividing by ana_nan​ and reading the parity of kkk each time.
  3. Rewrite the requested expression in the eke_kek​ BEFORE substituting numbers, then substitute.

e.g. 2x3−6x2+x−52x^3 - 6x^2 + x - 52x3−6x2+x−5: e1=3e_1 = 3e1​=3, e2=12e_2 = \frac{1}{2}e2​=21​, so r12+r22+r32=9−1=8r_1^2 + r_2^2 + r_3^2 = 9 - 1 = 8r12​+r22​+r32​=9−1=8.

Sketch a polynomial from its factored form

  1. Multiply the leading terms for the degree and leading coefficient, and fix the two arms.
  2. Mark each real zero with its multiplicity: odd crosses, even touches and turns back, large flattens.
  3. Test one point strictly inside each interval, unbounded ones included, and plot P(0)P(0)P(0) as an anchor.
  4. Reconcile: outer signs must match the arms, and the sign must flip at exactly the odd zeros.

e.g. (x+2)(x−1)2(x−3)(x + 2)(x - 1)^2(x - 3)(x+2)(x−1)2(x−3): both arms up, crosses at −2-2−2 and 333, touches at 111, P(0)=−6P(0) = -6P(0)=−6.

Recover a polynomial from a graph

  1. Each crossing gives a factor of odd multiplicity, smallest 111; each touch one of even multiplicity, smallest 222.
  2. Add the exponents for the LEAST possible degree; a stated degree can leave no room to spend, forcing uniqueness.
  3. Read the leading coefficient's sign and the degree parity from the arms, then substitute one extra point to pin that coefficient.
  4. Name what is unrecoverable: the true degree may exceed the least by any even amount, and nonreal roots are invisible.

e.g. A cubic crossing at −2-2−2, touching at 111, through (0,6)(0, 6)(0,6): P(x)=3(x+2)(x−1)2P(x) = 3(x + 2)(x - 1)^2P(x)=3(x+2)(x−1)2.

Exam traps

  • Trap "A degree-nnn polynomial has nnn roots", so x2−2x+1x^2 - 2x + 1x2−2x+1 is given 222 distinct roots and x2+1x^2 + 1x2+1 is given 222 real ones.

    Fix (x−1)2(x - 1)^2(x−1)2 has one distinct root, and x2+1x^2 + 1x2+1 has no real root. Exactly nnn roots, counted with multiplicity, over C\mathbb{C}C.

  • Trap Pairing a nonreal root without checking the coefficients: reading 2(x−3)2(x+i)2(x - 3)^2(x + i)2(x−3)2(x+i) as having roots 3,3,−i,i3, 3, -i, i3,3,−i,i.

    Fix That is degree 333, and iii is not a root. The pairing needs EVERY coefficient real; expanded, this one carries 2i−122i - 122i−12 and 18i18i18i.

  • Trap Using the surd conjugate theorem when the coefficients are merely real, or putting a bar on a surd.

    Fix x2−(2+2)x+22=(x−2)(x−2)x^2 - (2 + \sqrt{2})x + 2\sqrt{2} = (x - 2)(x - \sqrt{2})x2−(2+2​)x+22​=(x−2)(x−2​) is real with 2\sqrt{2}2​ a root and −2-\sqrt{2}−2​ not. Real buys the complex pairing; the surd conjugate theorem costs rational.

  • Trap Vieta from memory: the product of the roots of x4−3x3+7x−2x^4 - 3x^3 + 7x - 2x4−3x3+7x−2 read as 222, or the sum for 2x3−6x2+x−52x^3 - 6x^2 + x - 52x3−6x2+x−5 read as 666.

    Fix The product is −2-2−2: the sign is (−1)k(-1)^k(−1)k and k=4k = 4k=4 is even. The sum is −−62=3-\frac{-6}{2} = 3−2−6​=3: every symmetric sum divides by ana_nan​.

  • Trap Feeding Vieta an incomplete list: x3−5x2+8x−4=(x−1)(x−2)2x^3 - 5x^2 + 8x - 4 = (x - 1)(x - 2)^2x3−5x2+8x−4=(x−1)(x−2)2 summed as 1+2=31 + 2 = 31+2=3.

    Fix The list is 1,2,21, 2, 21,2,2, summing to 555. Nonreal roots count too: x3−5x2+8x−6x^3 - 5x^2 + 8x - 6x3−5x2+8x−6 has list 3,1+i,1−i3, 1 + i, 1 - i3,1+i,1−i, again 555.

  • Trap Treating every real zero as a crossing, so (x−3)2(x+1)(x - 3)^2(x + 1)(x−3)2(x+1) is drawn punching through the axis at 333.

    Fix That multiplicity is even, so it touches and turns back, and (3,0)(3, 0)(3,0) is a turning point. Test points on both sides give the same sign.

  • Trap Labelling a hump never evaluated, or placing it midway between two zeros.

    Fix For (x+2)(x−1)2(x−3)(x + 2)(x - 1)^2(x - 3)(x+2)(x−1)2(x−3) the midpoint P(−0.5)=−11.8125P(-0.5) = -11.8125P(−0.5)=−11.8125 sits above P(−1)=−16P(-1) = -16P(−1)=−16, so the low point is elsewhere. Claim only evaluated heights.
