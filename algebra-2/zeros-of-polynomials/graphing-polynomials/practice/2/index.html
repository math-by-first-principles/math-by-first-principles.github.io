  1. 1

    Which polynomial has the graph shown?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Read the three features the picture shows. The curve crosses at x=−2x = -2x=−2, so that zero has odd multiplicity. It touches and turns back at x=1x = 1x=1, so that zero has even multiplicity. The right arm rises, and for a cubic that means a positive leading coefficient.

    P(x)=(x+2)1(x−1)2P(x) = (x+2)^1(x-1)^2P(x)=(x+2)1(x−1)2

    The first choice has the roles reversed, touching at −2-2−2 and crossing at 111. The one with (x−2)(x-2)(x−2) and (x+1)2(x+1)^2(x+1)2 has its zeros at 222 and −1-1−1, in the wrong places. The negative version has the right zeros but sends the right arm downward. Only (x+2)(x−1)2(x+2)(x-1)^2(x+2)(x−1)2 matches all three features.

  2. 2

    What is the sign of P(x)=x(x−2)(x+3)P(x) = x(x-2)(x+3)P(x)=x(x−2)(x+3) on the interval 0<x<20 < x < 20<x<2?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The zeros are 000, 222, and −3-3−3, so the interval 0<x<20 < x < 20<x<2 is free of zeros and PPP keeps one sign on it. Test x=1x = 1x=1.

    P(1)=(1)(1−2)(1+3)=(1)(−1)(4)=−4<0P(1) = (1)(1-2)(1+3) = (1)(-1)(4) = -4 < 0P(1)=(1)(1−2)(1+3)=(1)(−1)(4)=−4<0

    Exactly one of the three factors is negative there, so the product is negative and the curve lies below the axis.

  3. 3

    The polynomial P(x)=x3−3x2+4P(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 4P(x)=x3−3x2+4 has x=2x = 2x=2 as a zero. What does its graph do at x=2x = 2x=2?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Divide by (x−2)(x-2)(x−2) synthetically, using the coefficients 1,−3,0,41, -3, 0, 41,−3,0,4. Bring down 111, multiply by 222 and add to get −1-1−1, multiply by 222 and add to get −2-2−2, multiply by 222 and add to get 000. The remainder is 000, so the depressed polynomial is x2−x−2x^2 - x - 2x2−x−2.

    Now divide by (x−2)(x-2)(x−2) again, since a second zero remainder would mean x=2x = 2x=2 repeats. Testing the depressed polynomial at 222 gives 4−2−2=04 - 2 - 2 = 04−2−2=0, so it does.

    P(x)=(x−2)2(x+1)P(x) = (x-2)^2(x+1)P(x)=(x−2)2(x+1)

    The multiplicity of x=2x = 2x=2 is 222, which is even, so the graph touches the axis there and turns back.

  4. 4

    A graph crosses the x-axis at x=−1x = -1x=−1, touches it at x=1x = 1x=1, and crosses it at x=4x = 4x=4, with no other x-intercepts. What is the least possible degree of the polynomial?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Each crossing needs an odd multiplicity, and the smallest odd value is 111. The touch needs an even multiplicity, and the smallest even value is 222.

    1+2+1=41 + 2 + 1 = 41+2+1=4

    So the degree is at least 444, and P(x)=(x+1)(x−1)2(x−4)P(x) = (x+1)(x-1)^2(x-4)P(x)=(x+1)(x−1)2(x−4) reaches it. The true degree could be 666 or 888, since extra multiplicity comes in steps of two and non-real roots come in pairs, but it cannot be smaller than 444 and it cannot be odd.

  5. 5

    What is the y-intercept of the graph of P(x)=3(x−1)(x+2)2P(x) = 3(x-1)(x+2)^2P(x)=3(x−1)(x+2)2?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Substitute x=0x = 0x=0 into every factor, and do not forget the constant 333 out front.

    P(0)=3(0−1)(0+2)2=3(−1)(4)=−12P(0) = 3(0-1)(0+2)^2 = 3(-1)(4) = -12P(0)=3(0−1)(0+2)2=3(−1)(4)=−12

    The graph passes through (0,−12)(0, -12)(0,−12).

  6. 6

    The graph of P(x)=a(x−1)(x+2)P(x) = a(x-1)(x+2)P(x)=a(x−1)(x+2) passes through (0,6)(0, 6)(0,6). What is aaa?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The zeros fix the factors, and the extra point fixes the scale factor aaa. Substitute x=0x = 0x=0 and set the result equal to 666.

    a(0−1)(0+2)=−2a=6⟹a=−3a(0-1)(0+2) = -2a = 6 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad a = -3a(0−1)(0+2)=−2a=6⟹a=−3

    So P(x)=−3(x−1)(x+2)P(x) = -3(x-1)(x+2)P(x)=−3(x−1)(x+2). Because aaa is negative, this parabola opens downward, which the two zeros alone could never have told you.

  7. 7

    A degree 555 polynomial with real coefficients has real zeros only at x=−2x = -2x=−2 (multiplicity 111) and x=1x = 1x=1 (multiplicity 222). How many non-real roots does it have, counted with multiplicity?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    By the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra there are exactly 555 roots counted with multiplicity. The real zeros use up 1+2=31 + 2 = 31+2=3 of them.

    5−3=25 - 3 = 25−3=2

    So 222 roots are non-real, which is exactly one conjugate pair. The answer had to be even, since non-real roots of a real polynomial always come in conjugate pairs. Those two roots contribute no x-intercept, so the graph meets the axis at just two points.

  8. 8

    Which statement about the graph of P(x)=(x2+1)(x−3)2P(x) = (x^2+1)(x-3)^2P(x)=(x2+1)(x−3)2 is true?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The factor x2+1x^2 + 1x2+1 has no real zero, since x2=−1x^2 = -1x2=−1 has no real solution, so it contributes no x-intercept. The factor (x−3)2(x-3)^2(x−3)2 gives the real zero 333 with multiplicity 222.

    P(x)=(x2+1)⏟> 0 always(x−3)2≥0P(x) = \underbrace{(x^2+1)}_{> \, 0 \ \text{always}} (x-3)^2 \geq 0P(x)=>0 always(x2+1)​​(x−3)2≥0

    The multiplicity is even, so the curve touches the axis at x=3x = 3x=3 and turns back rather than crossing. Since P(x)≥0P(x) \geq 0P(x)≥0 for every real xxx, with equality only at 333, the graph meets the axis exactly once.

  9. 9

    At which zero is the graph of P(x)=(x−1)(x+2)5P(x) = (x-1)(x+2)^5P(x)=(x−1)(x+2)5 most flattened against the x-axis?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Near a zero rrr of multiplicity mmm, the size of PPP is governed by the factor ∣x−r∣m|x-r|^m∣x−r∣m, and a bigger mmm makes that factor far smaller at the same distance from rrr.

    (0.2)1=0.2versus(0.2)5=0.000 32(0.2)^1 = 0.2 \qquad \text{versus} \qquad (0.2)^5 = 0.000\,32(0.2)1=0.2versus(0.2)5=0.00032

    The zero x=−2x = -2x=−2 has multiplicity 555 against multiplicity 111 at x=1x = 1x=1, so the curve hugs the axis far more closely at −2-2−2 before pulling away. It still crosses there, because 555 is odd.

  10. 10

    A monic cubic has simple x-intercepts at 111, 222, and 333, and no others. What is its y-intercept?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Monic means the leading coefficient is 111, and the three simple zeros give the three factors, so the polynomial is fully determined.

    P(x)=(x−1)(x−2)(x−3),P(0)=(−1)(−2)(−3)=−6P(x) = (x-1)(x-2)(x-3), \qquad P(0) = (-1)(-2)(-3) = -6P(x)=(x−1)(x−2)(x−3),P(0)=(−1)(−2)(−3)=−6

    Three negative factors multiply to a negative number, so the y-intercept is (0,−6)(0, -6)(0,−6).

  11. 11

    What is the greatest number of distinct x-intercepts the graph of a degree 444 polynomial can have?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Each distinct real zero contributes at least 111 to the total multiplicity, and the total multiplicity is exactly the degree.

    number of distinct real zeros≤deg⁡P=4\text{number of distinct real zeros} \leq \deg P = 4number of distinct real zeros≤degP=4

    So there can be at most 444 x-intercepts, and P(x)=(x−1)(x−2)(x−3)(x−4)P(x) = (x-1)(x-2)(x-3)(x-4)P(x)=(x−1)(x−2)(x−3)(x−4) achieves it. There can be fewer, if some zeros repeat or some roots are non-real.

  12. 12

    How many x-intercepts does the graph of P(x)=(x−2)(x2−4x+5)P(x) = (x-2)(x^2 - 4x + 5)P(x)=(x−2)(x2−4x+5) have?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The factor (x−2)(x-2)(x−2) gives the real zero 222. For the quadratic factor, compute the discriminant.

    b2−4ac=(−4)2−4(1)(5)=16−20=−4<0b^2 - 4ac = (-4)^2 - 4(1)(5) = 16 - 20 = -4 < 0b2−4ac=(−4)2−4(1)(5)=16−20=−4<0

    A negative discriminant means the quadratic has a non-real conjugate pair of roots and no real zero, so it contributes no x-intercept. The graph therefore meets the x-axis exactly once, at x=2x = 2x=2.
