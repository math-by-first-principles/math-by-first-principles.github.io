Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    Dividing P(x)P(x)P(x) by (x+3)(x+3)(x+3) leaves remainder 000. Dividing the resulting quotient by (x+3)(x+3)(x+3) leaves remainder 000 again. Dividing that second quotient by (x+3)(x+3)(x+3) leaves remainder 555. What is the multiplicity of the zero x=−3x = -3x=−3, and what does the graph do there?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Each division with remainder 000 pulls out one factor of (x+3)(x+3)(x+3), and the first nonzero remainder says the factor has run out. Two clean divisions and then a remainder of 555 means (x+3)(x+3)(x+3) divides PPP exactly twice.

    P(x)=(x+3)2G(x),G(−3)=5≠0P(x) = (x+3)^2 G(x), \qquad G(-3) = 5 \neq 0P(x)=(x+3)2G(x),G(−3)=5=0

    That last remainder is the value of the quotient at −3-3−3, by the Remainder Theorem, and it is nonzero, so the multiplicity is exactly 222, not 333.

    A multiplicity of 222 is even, so (x+3)2(x+3)^2(x+3)2 stays positive on both sides of −3-3−3 and PPP keeps the sign of G(−3)G(-3)G(−3) on both sides. The curve comes down to the axis at x=−3x = -3x=−3 and leaves on the side it arrived from, so it touches and turns back rather than crossing. Repeated synthetic division is exactly how you measure a multiplicity in practice.

  2. 2

    For P(x)=(x−1)2(x+2)3P(x) = (x-1)^2(x+2)^3P(x)=(x−1)2(x+2)3, how many times does the graph cross the x-axis?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    A crossing happens at a real zero of odd multiplicity, so check the parity of each exponent.

    x=1 (m=2, even),x=−2 (m=3, odd)x = 1 \ (m = 2, \text{ even}), \qquad x = -2 \ (m = 3, \text{ odd})x=1 (m=2, even),x=−2 (m=3, odd)

    Only x=−2x = -2x=−2 has odd multiplicity, so the graph crosses there and nowhere else. At x=1x = 1x=1 it touches the axis and turns back. The crossing at −2-2−2 is flattened, since the multiplicity is 333, but a flattened crossing is still a crossing.

  3. 3

    What is the sign of P(x)=(x+1)(x−2)(x−4)P(x) = (x+1)(x-2)(x-4)P(x)=(x+1)(x−2)(x−4) on the interval 2<x<42 < x < 42<x<4?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The interval 2<x<42 < x < 42<x<4 has no zero of PPP inside it, so a single test point decides the sign of the whole interval. Take x=3x = 3x=3.

    P(3)=(4)(1)(−1)=−4<0P(3) = (4)(1)(-1) = -4 < 0P(3)=(4)(1)(−1)=−4<0

    Exactly one factor is negative there, so the product is negative and the curve runs below the axis across the entire interval.

  4. 4

    What is the y-intercept of the graph of P(x)=−2(x−3)2(x+1)P(x) = -2(x-3)^2(x+1)P(x)=−2(x−3)2(x+1)?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Substitute x=0x = 0x=0 into every factor, keeping the leading constant.

    P(0)=−2(0−3)2(0+1)=−2(9)(1)=−18P(0) = -2(0-3)^2(0+1) = -2(9)(1) = -18P(0)=−2(0−3)2(0+1)=−2(9)(1)=−18

    The graph passes through (0,−18)(0, -18)(0,−18). Squaring −3-3−3 makes it positive, so the only minus sign that survives is the one out front.

  5. 5

    How many x-intercepts does the graph of P(x)=x3+2x2−5x−6P(x) = x^3 + 2x^2 - 5x - 6P(x)=x3+2x2−5x−6 have?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The Rational Root Theorem offers the divisors of −6-6−6, since the leading coefficient is 111. Test x=2x = 2x=2.

    P(2)=8+8−10−6=0P(2) = 8 + 8 - 10 - 6 = 0P(2)=8+8−10−6=0

    So (x−2)(x-2)(x−2) is a factor. Synthetic division by 222 on the coefficients 1,2,−5,−61, 2, -5, -61,2,−5,−6 gives the depressed polynomial x2+4x+3x^2 + 4x + 3x2+4x+3, which factors easily.

    P(x)=(x−2)(x+1)(x+3)P(x) = (x-2)(x+1)(x+3)P(x)=(x−2)(x+1)(x+3)

    The three real zeros 222, −1-1−1, and −3-3−3 are all simple, so the graph crosses the axis at three distinct points.

  6. 6

    A degree 777 polynomial with real coefficients has real zeros only at x=1x = 1x=1 (multiplicity 222) and x=−3x = -3x=−3 (multiplicity 111). How many non-real roots does it have, counted with multiplicity?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    There are exactly 777 roots counted with multiplicity, and the real zeros use up 2+1=32 + 1 = 32+1=3 of them.

    7−3=47 - 3 = 47−3=4

    So 444 roots are non-real, which is two conjugate pairs. The answer had to be even, since non-real roots of a real polynomial always pair off with their conjugates, and that check rules out 333 and 555 immediately.

  7. 7

    What is the sign of P(x)=(x+2)2(x−1)P(x) = (x+2)^2(x-1)P(x)=(x+2)2(x−1) just to the left of x=−2x = -2x=−2?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Write P(x)=(x+2)2G(x)P(x) = (x+2)^2 G(x)P(x)=(x+2)2G(x) with G(x)=x−1G(x) = x - 1G(x)=x−1, and evaluate GGG at the zero.

    G(−2)=−2−1=−3<0G(-2) = -2 - 1 = -3 < 0G(−2)=−2−1=−3<0

    Near x=−2x = -2x=−2 the factor GGG keeps that negative sign, and (x+2)2(x+2)^2(x+2)2 is positive on both sides of −2-2−2. A positive times a negative is negative, so PPP is negative just to the left of −2-2−2, and by the same reasoning just to the right of it as well. That is the even-multiplicity picture: the curve rises to touch the axis from below and drops back down.

  8. 8

    Which polynomial has the graph shown?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Read the picture. The curve touches the axis at x=−1x = -1x=−1 and at x=2x = 2x=2 without ever crossing, so both zeros have even multiplicity. Both arms point downward, so the degree is even and the leading coefficient is negative.

    P(x)=−(x+1)2(x−2)2P(x) = -(x+1)^2(x-2)^2P(x)=−(x+1)2(x−2)2

    The version without the minus sign has both arms rising, which is upside down. The choice with exponents 111 and 333 crosses at both zeros rather than touching. The last choice has degree 333, so its arms would point in opposite directions.

  9. 9

    What does the graph of P(x)=x4−4x3+4x2P(x) = x^4 - 4x^3 + 4x^2P(x)=x4−4x3+4x2 do at x=0x = 0x=0 and at x=2x = 2x=2?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Factor out the common x2x^2x2 first, then recognize the perfect square that is left.

    x4−4x3+4x2=x2(x2−4x+4)=x2(x−2)2x^4 - 4x^3 + 4x^2 = x^2(x^2 - 4x + 4) = x^2(x-2)^2x4−4x3+4x2=x2(x2−4x+4)=x2(x−2)2

    Both zeros have multiplicity 222, which is even, so the graph touches the axis at both and turns back at both. In fact P(x)=[x(x−2)]2≥0P(x) = \left[x(x-2)\right]^2 \geq 0P(x)=[x(x−2)]2≥0, so the curve never goes below the axis at all.

  10. 10

    For which values of xxx is P(x)=(x2+4)(x−3)3P(x) = (x^2+4)(x-3)^3P(x)=(x2+4)(x−3)3 positive?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The factor x2+4x^2 + 4x2+4 is positive for every real xxx, since a square is never negative, so it never affects the sign. The sign of PPP is the sign of (x−3)3(x-3)^3(x−3)3.

    (x−3)3>0  ⟺  x−3>0  ⟺  x>3(x-3)^3 > 0 \iff x - 3 > 0 \iff x > 3(x−3)3>0⟺x−3>0⟺x>3

    An odd power keeps the sign of its base, so PPP is positive exactly when x>3x > 3x>3 and negative when x<3x < 3x<3. The single real zero at 333 has multiplicity 333, odd, so the graph crosses there, which is precisely the sign change we just found.

  11. 11

    From the factored form P(x)=(x+2)(x−1)(x−4)P(x) = (x+2)(x-1)(x-4)P(x)=(x+2)(x−1)(x−4) alone, which of these can you NOT determine?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Three of the four come straight off the factored form. The real zeros −2-2−2, 111, and 444 are the x-intercepts. Substituting x=0x = 0x=0 gives the y-intercept.

    P(0)=(2)(−1)(−4)=8P(0) = (2)(-1)(-4) = 8P(0)=(2)(−1)(−4)=8

    And the leading term x⋅x⋅x=x3x \cdot x \cdot x = x^3x⋅x⋅x=x3 has odd degree with a positive coefficient, which fixes the end behavior: down on the left, up on the right.

    The dip is a different matter. Testing x=2x = 2x=2 gives P(2)=(4)(1)(−2)=−8P(2) = (4)(1)(-2) = -8P(2)=(4)(1)(−2)=−8, so the curve does sink below the axis between 111 and 444 and therefore has a lowest point in there, but nothing in this chapter locates it. Finding where the curve stops falling and starts rising means finding where its slope is zero, which is a calculus question.

    Two turning points would have been exceptions, and this is neither of them. A parabola's vertex sits halfway between its roots by symmetry, and a real zero of even multiplicity is a turning point at the exactly known coordinates (r,0)(r, 0)(r,0). This dip belongs to a cubic and sits off the axis, so it is out of reach. Beware the tempting guess that it bottoms out at the midpoint x=2.5x = 2.5x=2.5. Arithmetic alone refutes that.

    P(2.5)=(4.5)(1.5)(−1.5)=−10.125,P(2.75)=(4.75)(1.75)(−1.25)=−10.390625P(2.5) = (4.5)(1.5)(-1.5) = -10.125, \qquad P(2.75) = (4.75)(1.75)(-1.25) = -10.390625P(2.5)=(4.5)(1.5)(−1.5)=−10.125,P(2.75)=(4.75)(1.75)(−1.25)=−10.390625

    The curve is already lower at 2.752.752.75 than at the midpoint, so the midpoint is not the bottom. Evaluation can refute a wrong guess, but it can never hand you the exact one.

  12. 12

    A degree 444 polynomial with real coefficients has the roots 1+i1+i1+i, 1−i1-i1−i, and 333 with multiplicity 222. How many x-intercepts does its graph have?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Count the roots first as a check: the conjugate pair contributes 222 and the double root contributes 222, giving 2+2=42 + 2 = 42+2=4, which matches the degree, so the list is complete.

    real zeros: x=3 only\text{real zeros}: \ x = 3 \ \text{only}real zeros: x=3 only

    The roots 1±i1 \pm i1±i are not real, so they are not points on the x-axis and produce no intercept. The graph therefore meets the axis at exactly 111 point, and since the multiplicity of 333 is even, it touches there rather than crossing.
