  1. Problem 1 Reading a sign

    The figure shows a polynomial curve, and every x-intercept is visible. On which open intervals is its value negative?

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the x-axis from -5.5 to 3.5, ticked at every integer, and the y-axis from -18 to 18, ticked every 6. A smooth curve rises off the top of the frame on the left, crosses the x-axis at x equals -4, dips down, rises back up crossing at x equals -1, reaches a peak, then descends, crossing at x equals 2 and continuing off the bottom of the frame on the right. Arrows at both ends show the curve continuing beyond the frame. No equation or sign labels are shown.

    1. Hint 1

      Negative values appear below the horizontal axis.

    2. Hint 2

      Read the three crossing locations and separate the intervals they create.

    Answer

    −4<x<−1-4<x<-1−4<x<−1 and x>2x>2x>2.

    Full solution

    The curve crosses at −4-4−4, −1-1−1, and 222.

    It is below the axis between the first two and to the right of the last one.

    Those regions are

    −4<x<−1-4<x<-1−4<x<−1

    and x>2x>2x>2.

    The zeros themselves are excluded because the question asks for negative values.

    Answer

    −4<x<−1-4<x<-1−4<x<−1 and x>2x>2x>2.

    Key idea

    The intervals below the horizontal axis are exactly where a graph has negative values.

  2. Problem 2 An added factor

    A real polynomial ppp has exactly three distinct real zeros. How many x-intercepts does the graph of q(x)=(x2+2x+10)3p(x)q(x)=(x^2+2x+10)^3p(x)q(x)=(x2+2x+10)3p(x) have?

    1. Hint 1

      A new intercept requires the extra factor to vanish at a real input.

    2. Hint 2

      For real xxx, compare x2+2x+10x^2+2x+10x2+2x+10 with zero.

    Answer

    333 x-intercepts.

    Full solution

    For every real xxx, x2+2x+10=(x+1)2+9x^2+2x+10=(x+1)^2+9x2+2x+10=(x+1)2+9, which is at least 999, so it is positive.

    Its cube is also positive.

    Thus q(x)=0q(x)=0q(x)=0 exactly at the real zeros of ppp, so the three distinct intercepts are unchanged.

    The added factor has nonreal roots, which add no x-intercepts.

    Answer

    333 x-intercepts.

    Key idea

    A factor that is positive at every real input adds no x-intercepts.

  3. Problem 3 An interval report

    A polynomial ppp has no real zero in −1<x<5-1<x<5−1<x<5, and p(2)=−7p(2)=-7p(2)=−7. Determine the sign of p(4)p(4)p(4).

    1. Hint 1

      A polynomial is continuous and cannot change sign without reaching zero.

    2. Hint 2

      The known input and the requested input are inside the same interval without zeros.

    Answer

    p(4)<0p(4)<0p(4)<0.

    Full solution

    The value at 222 is negative.

    If p(4)p(4)p(4) were positive, the unbroken polynomial graph would have to pass through zero between 222 and 444.

    If p(4)=0p(4)=0p(4)=0, it would itself be a forbidden zero.

    Therefore

    p(4)<0p(4)<0p(4)<0

    No exact evaluation is needed.

    Answer

    p(4)<0p(4)<0p(4)<0.

    Key idea

    One sign test determines every value's sign within an interval containing no real zero.

  4. Problem 4 A quartic from its graph

  5. Problem 5 Comparing two curves

    Let p(x)=x(x−2)2p(x)=x(x-2)^2p(x)=x(x−2)2 and q(x)=(x2+3x+5)p(x)q(x)=(x^2+3x+5)p(x)q(x)=(x2+3x+5)p(x). Compare their degrees, x-intercepts, crossing or touching behavior, and signs for real inputs.

    1. Hint 1

      The added factor contributes degree but is positive for every real input.

    2. Hint 2

      Read multiplicities from ppp, then check whether the extra factor vanishes at either zero.

    Answer

    Degrees 333 and 555; both cross at 000 and touch at 222; both negative for x<0x<0x<0, positive for x>0x>0x>0 except at 222.

    Full solution

    The degrees are 333 for ppp and 555 for qqq.

    The quadratic x2+3x+5x^2+3x+5x2+3x+5 has discriminant 9−20=−119-20=-119−20=−11, which is negative, so it is positive for every real xxx.

    Thus qqq has the same real zeros and multiplicities as ppp: 000 is simple and 222 has multiplicity 222.

    Both cross at 000 and touch at 222.

    Away from x=2x=2x=2, (x−2)2(x-2)^2(x−2)2 is positive, so the sign of ppp is the sign of xxx.

    The positive extra factor preserves that sign for qqq.

    For example,

    p(−1)=−9p(-1)=-9p(−1)=−9 p(1)=1p(1)=1p(1)=1 p(3)=3p(3)=3p(3)=3

    These signs cover all three intervals.

    Answer

    Degrees 333 and 555; both cross at 000 and touch at 222; both negative for x<0x<0x<0, positive for x>0x>0x>0 except at 222.

    Key idea

    A positive polynomial factor can raise the degree without changing real zeros or signs.

  7. Problem 7 Labels on a plotted curve

    The graph shows p(x)=x2(x+1)(x−3)p(x)=x^2(x+1)(x-3)p(x)=x2(x+1)(x−3) and a marked turning point A at the origin. A student labels A as a local maximum and labels the minimum function value as −16-16−16 because that is the bottom of the plotting window. Which label is justified by the stated evidence? Explain without calculus.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the x-axis from -2 to 4, ticked at every integer, and the y-axis from -16 to 20, ticked every 4. A smooth curve rises off the top of the frame on the left, descends and crosses the x-axis at x equals -1, dips down and touches the axis from below at the origin, marked A, dips to a minimum, then rises and crosses the axis at x equals 3, continuing off the top of the frame on the right. No other point or turning-point coordinate is labeled.

    1. Hint 1

      The sign on either side of an even-multiplicity zero determines its local type.

    2. Hint 2

      A viewing-window boundary is a plotting choice, not a computed function extremum.

    Answer

    Only A as a local maximum is justified; the minimum-value label is not.

    Full solution

    Near 000, x2x^2x2 is positive except at 000, x+1x+1x+1 is positive, and x−3x-3x−3 is negative.

    Thus the curve is below the axis on both sides of 000, proving that A is a local maximum.

    The value −16-16−16 was chosen as the plotting-window boundary.

    No calculation establishes that ppp attains that value or that it is the least value of ppp.

    A window label is not evidence for an exact extremum.

    Answer

    Only A as a local maximum is justified; the minimum-value label is not.

    Key idea

    A plotting-window boundary does not determine a polynomial curve's exact maximum or minimum.

  8. Problem 8 Two positive readings

    A student knows only that a real polynomial satisfies p(−3)>0p(-3)>0p(−3)>0 and p(3)>0p(3)>0p(3)>0. The student concludes that it has no real zero between those inputs. Is the conclusion justified? Give a counterexample if needed.

    1. Hint 1

      Positive endpoint values do not control whether the curve dips to the axis in between.

    2. Hint 2

      A polynomial with a repeated root can be positive at both endpoints.

    Answer

    No; for example, p(x)=x2p(x)=x^2p(x)=x2 has a zero at 000 and both endpoint values equal 999.

    Full solution

    Take p(x)=x2p(x)=x^2p(x)=x2.

    Then

    p(−3)=9p(-3)=9p(−3)=9 p(3)=9p(3)=9p(3)=9

    but p(0)=0p(0)=0p(0)=0, so the claimed conclusion fails.

    A polynomial must have a zero to change sign, but a zero need not produce a sign change.

    Here the even-multiplicity zero merely touches the axis.

    Answer

    No; for example, p(x)=x2p(x)=x^2p(x)=x2 has a zero at 000 and both endpoint values equal 999.

    Key idea

    Equal signs at two endpoints do not rule out real zeros between them.

  9. Problem 9 A curve without crossings

    A nonconstant polynomial ppp with real coefficients has no x-intercepts, and p(0)<0p(0)<0p(0)<0. A student claims that p(x)<0p(x)<0p(x)<0 for every real xxx. Is the claim true? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      There is no real zero anywhere to allow a sign change.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare a hypothetical positive value with the known negative value at 000.

    Answer

    True; p(x)<0p(x)<0p(x)<0 for every real xxx.

    Full solution

    A zero value would itself be an x-intercept.

    If some input had a positive value, continuity between that input and 000 would force an x-intercept as well.

    Both contradict the premise.

    Hence

    p(x)<0p(x)<0p(x)<0

    for every real xxx.

    Answer

    True; p(x)<0p(x)<0p(x)<0 for every real xxx.

    Key idea

    A polynomial with no real zeros keeps one sign on the entire real line.

  10. Problem 10 What a sketch determines

    The figure shows all x-intercepts and both end directions of a nonzero real polynomial. Find its least possible degree and the sign of its leading coefficient. Does the sketch prove the exact multiplicities or the exact locations of its nonreal roots? Explain.

    Text description of this figure

    A grid with the x-axis from -5 to 1 and the y-axis from -6 to 2, both with unit ticks. A smooth curve rises from the lower left, touches the x-axis from below at x equals -3 without crossing it, dips down, rises back up to touch the axis from below at x equals 0, then falls off the bottom of the frame on the right. Arrows at both ends show the curve continuing downward beyond the frame. No equation, coordinates, or multiplicity is labeled.

    1. Hint 1

      A crossing needs at least one factor and a touch needs at least two.

    2. Hint 2

      Extra even multiplicities or nonreal conjugate pairs can preserve the displayed real-axis behavior.

    Answer

    Least degree 444; negative leading coefficient; exact multiplicities and nonreal root locations are not established.

    Full solution

    The two displayed intercepts are touches, each with even multiplicity at least 222.

    Thus

    n≥2+2n\ge2+2n≥2+2 n≥4n\ge4n≥4

    Both ends fall, so the degree is even and the leading coefficient is negative.

    The curve shows even multiplicity at each root, not an exact exponent.

    Higher even multiplicities or extra factors with no real roots can preserve the same real-axis features.

    No nonreal root is a point on this real graph.

    Answer

    Least degree 444; negative leading coefficient; exact multiplicities and nonreal root locations are not established.

    Key idea

    A schematic curve supports multiplicity parity and a minimum degree, but not a complete complex root list.
