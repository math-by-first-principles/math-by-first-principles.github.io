    A committee of 444 is chosen from 666 men and 555 women. How many committees contain at least one woman?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Count the opposite of what you want. The group has 111111 people, so the total number of committees is

    (114)=11×10×9×84×3×2×1=330.\binom{11}{4} = \frac{11 \times 10 \times 9 \times 8}{4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = 330.(411​)=4×3×2×111×10×9×8​=330.

    The committees with no woman are made entirely of the 666 men, and there are (64)=15\binom{6}{4} = 15(46​)=15 of those. Every committee either has at least one woman or has none, so subtract.

    330−15=315330 - 15 = 315330−15=315

    The value 600600600 comes from the tempting but wrong method of appointing one woman first (555 ways) and filling the other three seats freely ((103)=120\binom{10}{3} = 120(310​)=120 ways). That builds a committee holding two women twice, once for each woman it could have appointed first.

  2. 2

    How many distinguishable arrangements are there of the letters in SUCCESS?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The word has 777 letters: three S's, two C's, one U, and one E, and 3+2+1+1=73 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 73+2+1+1=7.

    Tagged letters would give 7!=5,0407! = 5{,}0407!=5,040 arrangements, and each actual word is produced 3! 2! 1! 1!=123!\,2!\,1!\,1! = 123!2!1!1!=12 times.

    7!3! 2!=5,04012=420\frac{7!}{3!\,2!} = \frac{5{,}040}{12} = 4203!2!7!​=125,040​=420

    Dividing by only the 3!3!3! leaves 840840840, which still counts every word twice, once for each order of the two C's.

  3. 3

    How many 555-card hands from a standard 525252-card deck contain exactly 222 aces? (There are 444 aces in the deck.)

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Build the hand in two stages. First choose which 222 of the 444 aces appear, and then choose the other 333 cards from the 484848 non-aces, since any further ace would break the word 'exactly'.

    (42)=6,(483)=48×47×466=17,296\binom{4}{2} = 6, \qquad \binom{48}{3} = \frac{48 \times 47 \times 46}{6} = 17{,}296(24​)=6,(348​)=648×47×46​=17,296

    The second stage offers the same number of choices whichever aces were picked, so the two stages multiply.

    6×17,296=103,7766 \times 17{,}296 = 103{,}7766×17,296=103,776

    Drawing the last three cards from all 505050 remaining cards would give 117,600117{,}600117,600 and would wrongly allow a third ace into a hand that is supposed to hold exactly two.

  4. 4

    Four different math books and three different science books are lined up on a shelf. In how many arrangements are all four math books together (in a single block, in any order)?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Glue the four math books into a single block. The shelf now holds 444 objects, the block and the three science books, and they can be ordered in 4!=244! = 244!=24 ways.

    Inside the block the four math books are all different, so they can be ordered in 4!=244! = 244!=24 ways as well, and this number does not depend on where the block sits.

    4!×4!=24×24=5764! \times 4! = 24 \times 24 = 5764!×4!=24×24=576

    The value 5,040=7!5{,}040 = 7!5,040=7! counts every arrangement of the seven books with no condition at all.

  5. 5

    How many distinguishable arrangements of the letters in ALGEBRA have the two A's next to each other?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Glue the two A's into a single block. The word now consists of 666 objects, the AA block plus L, G, E, B, and R, and all six are different from one another.

    6!=7206! = 7206!=720

    The block needs no internal factor. The two A's are identical, so swapping them inside the block produces the very same word, which is why multiplying by 2!2!2! to get 1,4401{,}4401,440 double counts every arrangement.

    For contrast, the total number of arrangements of ALGEBRA is 7!/2!=2,5207!/2! = 2{,}5207!/2!=2,520, so the arrangements whose A's are apart number 2,520−720=1,8002{,}520 - 720 = 1{,}8002,520−720=1,800.

  6. 6

    In how many ways can 101010 people be split into two teams of 555, if the teams have no names, so that swapping the two teams gives the same split?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    First pick the five people who form one team, which can be done in (105)=252\binom{10}{5} = 252(510​)=252 ways, since picking them decides the other team automatically.

    But this procedure builds every split exactly twice: once when it picks team A as the chosen five, and once when it picks team B. The overcount is the same for every split, so the division principle applies with d=2d = 2d=2.

    2522=126\frac{252}{2} = 1262252​=126

    The division by 222 is needed only because the teams are unnamed. Had one team been the home team and the other the away team, the answer would have stayed 252252252.

    How many strings of 444 letters, formed from the 262626-letter alphabet with repeats allowed, contain at least one A?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Count the opposite. All strings number 264=456,97626^4 = 456{,}976264=456,976, and the strings containing no A use only the other 252525 letters at each of the four positions.

    254=390,62525^4 = 390{,}625254=390,625

    Every string either contains an A or does not, so subtract.

    456,976−390,625=66,351456{,}976 - 390{,}625 = 66{,}351456,976−390,625=66,351

    The value 70,30470{,}30470,304 comes from choosing a position for a guaranteed A (444 ways) and filling the other three positions freely (26326^3263 ways). That method counts a string with two A's twice, so it overshoots.

    Eight people sit in a row of eight chairs. In how many arrangements do two particular people, Ana and Ben, refuse to sit next to each other?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Count the arrangements you do not want, which are the ones where Ana and Ben sit together, and subtract them from the total.

    All seatings number 8!=40,3208! = 40{,}3208!=40,320. For the seatings with Ana and Ben together, glue them into a block, giving 777 objects to arrange in 7!=5,0407! = 5{,}0407!=5,040 ways, and 2!2!2! ways to order Ana and Ben inside the block.

    7!×2!=5,040×2=10,0807! \times 2! = 5{,}040 \times 2 = 10{,}0807!×2!=5,040×2=10,080

    Subtract the forbidden seatings from all of them.

    40,320−10,080=30,24040{,}320 - 10{,}080 = 30{,}24040,320−10,080=30,240

    For which value of nnn does (n2)=66\binom{n}{2} = 66(2n​)=66?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Write the combination in closed form and set it equal to 666666.

    (n2)=n(n−1)2=66⟹n(n−1)=132\binom{n}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2} = 66 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad n(n-1) = 132(2n​)=2n(n−1)​=66⟹n(n−1)=132

    Expand and solve the quadratic completely.

    n2−n−132=0⟹(n−12)(n+11)=0n^2 - n - 132 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (n-12)(n+11) = 0n2−n−132=0⟹(n−12)(n+11)=0

    The roots are n=12n = 12n=12 and n=−11n = -11n=−11, and a count of objects cannot be negative, so n=12n = 12n=12. Check it: (122)=12×112=66\binom{12}{2} = \frac{12 \times 11}{2} = 66(212​)=212×11​=66, while (112)=55\binom{11}{2} = 55(211​)=55 falls short.

  10. 10

    How many distinguishable arrangements of the letters in MISSISSIPPI have all four S's together (in a single block)?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Glue the four S's into one block. What remains to be arranged is the block itself plus one M, four I's, and two P's, which is 1+1+4+2=81 + 1 + 4 + 2 = 81+1+4+2=8 objects.

    Those 888 objects are not all distinct: the four I's are identical and so are the two P's, so use the repeated-object formula.

    8!4! 2!=40,32024×2=40,32048=840\frac{8!}{4!\,2!} = \frac{40{,}320}{24 \times 2} = \frac{40{,}320}{48} = 8404!2!8!​=24×240,320​=4840,320​=840

    The block needs no internal factor, because the four S's are identical and rearranging them inside the block changes nothing.

  11. 11

    A coin is flipped 101010 times. In how many of the 2102^{10}210 possible sequences do at least 222 heads appear?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The opposite of 'at least 222 heads' is 'fewer than 222 heads', which means exactly 000 heads or exactly 111 head. Those two cases are much easier to count.

    There is 111 sequence with no head at all, and the sequences with exactly one head are decided by which flip it was, so there are (101)=10\binom{10}{1} = 10(110​)=10 of them.

    1+10=111 + 10 = 111+10=11

    Subtract those from the 210=1,0242^{10} = 1{,}024210=1,024 sequences in all.

    1,024−11=1,0131{,}024 - 11 = 1{,}0131,024−11=1,013

    Subtracting only the all-tails sequence would give 1,0231{,}0231,023, which is the count for 'at least 111 head'.

    Six people sit in a row of six chairs. In how many arrangements is Maria, one of the six, NOT sitting in either of the two end chairs?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Seat the restricted person first, since the condition is about her. Maria must take one of the 444 middle chairs, and the other five people then fill the remaining five chairs in 5!=1205! = 1205!=120 ways.

    4×5!=4×120=4804 \times 5! = 4 \times 120 = 4804×5!=4×120=480

    Complementary counting agrees. All seatings number 6!=7206! = 7206!=720, and the seatings with Maria at an end number 2×5!=2402 \times 5! = 2402×5!=240, so those without her at an end number 720−240=480720 - 240 = 480720−240=480.
