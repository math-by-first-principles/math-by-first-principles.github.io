    Using the digits 111 through 999, how many 333-digit numbers have three different digits and are even?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The condition lands on the units digit, so choose that first. To be even the number must end in 222, 444, 666, or 888, which is 444 choices.

    With the units digit used up, 888 digits remain for the hundreds place and then 777 for the tens place, and those counts do not depend on which even digit was chosen.

    4×8×7=2244 \times 8 \times 7 = 2244×8×7=224

    Halving the total P(9,3)=504P(9,3) = 504P(9,3)=504 to get 252252252 assumes evens and odds are equally common, but the digits 111 through 999 hold four even digits and five odd ones. The odd count is 5×8×7=2805 \times 8 \times 7 = 2805×8×7=280, and indeed 224+280=504224 + 280 = 504224+280=504.

  2. 2

    How many of the integers from 111 to 100010001000 inclusive contain at least one digit 777?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Write every integer from 000 to 999999999 as a three-digit string, padding with leading zeros, so that 555 becomes 005005005. That is 100010001000 strings in all.

    A string with no 777 has 999 choices at each of its three positions, so the strings avoiding 777 number

    93=729.9^3 = 729.93=729.

    Subtract to get the strings containing at least one 777.

    1000−729=2711000 - 729 = 2711000−729=271

    The two integers at the edges do not disturb this: the padded 000000000 stands for 000, which is outside the range but contains no 777, and 100010001000 itself contains no 777. So the answer for 111 through 100010001000 is 271271271.

    How many subsets of a 777-element set have an odd number of elements?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Add up the subset counts of each odd size, using (7k)\binom{7}{k}(k7​) for the subsets of size kkk. The sizes do not overlap, so the counts add.

    (71)+(73)+(75)+(77)=7+35+21+1=64\binom{7}{1} + \binom{7}{3} + \binom{7}{5} + \binom{7}{7} = 7 + 35 + 21 + 1 = 64(17​)+(37​)+(57​)+(77​)=7+35+21+1=64

    There are 646464 such subsets, which is exactly half of the 27=1282^7 = 12827=128 subsets in all. That is no accident: pairing each subset with the one obtained by adding or removing a fixed element matches every odd subset to exactly one even subset.

    A committee of 555 is chosen from 666 women and 666 men. How many committees contain exactly 333 women?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Exactly 333 women means the other 222 members are men. Choose each group separately, and neither group is ordered.

    (63)=20,(62)=15\binom{6}{3} = 20, \qquad \binom{6}{2} = 15(36​)=20,(26​)=15

    The number of ways to choose the men does not depend on which women were chosen, so multiply.

    20×15=30020 \times 15 = 30020×15=300

    The value 792=(125)792 = \binom{12}{5}792=(512​) counts all committees of five, with no condition on the mix.

  5. 5

    A committee of 444 is formed from 555 seniors and 666 juniors. How many committees contain at least 333 seniors?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Here the complement is no easier than the direct count, since 'at least 333 seniors' allows only two cases. Split into them and add, because a committee cannot have both exactly 333 and exactly 444 seniors.

    With exactly 333 seniors, choose the seniors and then the single junior.

    (53)×(61)=10×6=60\binom{5}{3} \times \binom{6}{1} = 10 \times 6 = 60(35​)×(16​)=10×6=60

    With exactly 444 seniors, all four seats go to seniors and no junior is chosen.

    (54)×(60)=5×1=5\binom{5}{4} \times \binom{6}{0} = 5 \times 1 = 5(45​)×(06​)=5×1=5

    The two cases do not overlap and cover everything asked for, so the addition principle gives 60+5=6560 + 5 = 6560+5=65.

  6. 6

    The digits 1,2,3,4,51, 2, 3, 4, 51,2,3,4,5 are each used exactly once to form a 555-digit number. How many of these numbers are odd?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A number is odd exactly when its units digit is odd, so decide the units digit first. It must be 111, 333, or 555, which is 333 choices.

    The remaining four digits then fill the other four places in any order, and they are all different.

    3×4!=3×24=723 \times 4! = 3 \times 24 = 723×4!=3×24=72

    Check the total: the even numbers end in 222 or 444, giving 2×4!=482 \times 4! = 482×4!=48, and 72+48=120=5!72 + 48 = 120 = 5!72+48=120=5!, which is every arrangement of the five digits.

    The remaining three positions may use 000, but not any digit already used. After the leading digit, 999 digits remain (the ten digits minus the one used), then 888, then 777.

    9×9×8×7=4,5369 \times 9 \times 8 \times 7 = 4{,}5369×9×8×7=4,536

  8. 8

    Ten teams play a season in which every pair of teams meets exactly twice, once at each team's home ground. How many games are played?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    A game is decided by an ordered pair of distinct teams, the host and the visitor, since the same two teams meeting at different grounds are different games.

    P(10,2)=10×9=90P(10,2) = 10 \times 9 = 90P(10,2)=10×9=90

    The same answer comes from counting unordered pairs and doubling: there are (102)=45\binom{10}{2} = 45(210​)=45 pairs of teams, and each pair meets twice, giving 2×45=902 \times 45 = 902×45=90.

    Three books are chosen from a shelf of 121212. Two of the books are volumes of the same novel, and they may not both be chosen. How many selections are possible?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Count everything, then remove what is forbidden. The unrestricted selections number

    (123)=12×11×103×2×1=220.\binom{12}{3} = \frac{12 \times 11 \times 10}{3 \times 2 \times 1} = 220.(312​)=3×2×112×11×10​=220.

    A forbidden selection contains both volumes, so two of its three books are already fixed and the remaining book may be any of the other 101010 on the shelf, giving (101)=10\binom{10}{1} = 10(110​)=10 forbidden selections.

    220−10=210220 - 10 = 210220−10=210

    The value (103)=120\binom{10}{3} = 120(310​)=120 counts the selections that avoid both volumes entirely, which is stricter than the rule requires, since taking exactly one volume is allowed.

  10. 10

    An exam has 101010 questions, and a student must answer exactly 888 of them. Questions 111 and 222 are compulsory. How many choices of questions does the student have?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Questions 111 and 222 are forced, so they are not choices. Two of the eight answers are settled, leaving 666 more to be picked from the 888 optional questions.

    The set of questions answered is unordered, so this is a combination.

    (86)=(82)=8×72×1=28\binom{8}{6} = \binom{8}{2} = \frac{8 \times 7}{2 \times 1} = 28(68​)=(28​)=2×18×7​=28

    The symmetry (86)=(82)\binom{8}{6} = \binom{8}{2}(68​)=(28​) is the observation that choosing 666 questions to answer is the same act as choosing the 222 optional questions to skip. Without the compulsory rule the answer would be (108)=45\binom{10}{8} = 45(810​)=45.

  12. 12

    A shelf holds 444 different math books, 333 different physics books, and 222 different chemistry books. In how many ways can 222 books be chosen if they must come from different subjects?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Split into cases by which pair of subjects is used. The three cases do not overlap and cover everything, so their counts add.

    A math book with a physics book gives 4×3=124 \times 3 = 124×3=12 pairs, a math book with a chemistry book gives 4×2=84 \times 2 = 84×2=8, and a physics book with a chemistry book gives 3×2=63 \times 2 = 63×2=6.

    12+8+6=2612 + 8 + 6 = 2612+8+6=26

    Complementary counting confirms it. All pairs number (92)=36\binom{9}{2} = 36(29​)=36, and the same-subject pairs number (42)+(32)+(22)=6+3+1=10\binom{4}{2} + \binom{3}{2} + \binom{2}{2} = 6 + 3 + 1 = 10(24​)+(23​)+(22​)=6+3+1=10, so the different-subject pairs number 36−10=2636 - 10 = 2636−10=26.
