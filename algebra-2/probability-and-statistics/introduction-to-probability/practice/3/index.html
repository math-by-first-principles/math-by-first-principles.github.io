    Five cards are dealt from a standard 525252-card deck. Which expression gives the probability that the hand contains exactly two aces?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    A hand is a set of five cards, so the sample space is counted with combinations.

    ∣S∣=(525)=2,598,960\lvert S \rvert = \binom{52}{5} = 2{,}598{,}960∣S∣=(552​)=2,598,960

    To build a hand with exactly two aces, choose which two of the four aces appear, then fill the remaining three slots from the 484848 cards that are not aces. Those two choices are made in sequence, so multiply them.

    ∣A∣=(42)(483)=6×17,296=103,776\lvert A \rvert = \binom{4}{2}\binom{48}{3} = 6 \times 17{,}296 = 103{,}776∣A∣=(24​)(348​)=6×17,296=103,776

    Dividing gives P(A)=103,7762,598,960≈0.0399P(A) = \frac{103{,}776}{2{,}598{,}960} \approx 0.0399P(A)=2,598,960103,776​≈0.0399. Drawing the last three cards from all 505050 remaining cards would allow a third ace to sneak in, so the hand would no longer have exactly two.

  2. 2

    Four people are chosen at random. Assuming 365365365 equally likely birthdays and ignoring leap years, which expression gives the probability that all four birthdays are different?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    An outcome assigns one day to each of the four people, so the multiplication principle sizes the sample space.

    ∣S∣=3654\lvert S \rvert = 365^{4}∣S∣=3654

    To make all four birthdays different, hand out the days one person at a time, each new person avoiding every day already taken.

    ∣D∣=365×364×363×362\lvert D \rvert = 365 \times 364 \times 363 \times 362∣D∣=365×364×363×362

    So P(D)=365×364×363×3623654≈0.9836P(D) = \dfrac{365 \times 364 \times 363 \times 362}{365^{4}} \approx 0.9836P(D)=3654365×364×363×362​≈0.9836. Subtracting that from 111 would answer the opposite question, the probability that at least two of the four birthdays match.

    A disease affects 2%2\%2% of a population. A test detects it in 90%90\%90% of the people who have it, and it also comes back positive for 10%10\%10% of the people who do not. Someone tests positive. What is the probability that this person has the disease?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Turn the percentages into counts. Imagine 100010001000 people, of whom 2%2\%2%, that is 202020, have the disease and 980980980 do not.

    Among the 202020 sick people the test finds 90%90\%90%, giving 0.9×20=180.9 \times 20 = 180.9×20=18 positives. Among the 980980980 healthy people it fires anyway 10%10\%10% of the time, giving 0.1×980=980.1 \times 980 = 980.1×980=98 positives.

    total positives=18+98=116\text{total positives} = 18 + 98 = 116total positives=18+98=116

    A positive result shrinks the sample space to those 116116116 people, and the sick ones among them number 181818.

    P(disease∣positive)=18116=958≈0.155P(\text{disease} \mid \text{positive}) = \frac{18}{116} = \frac{9}{58} \approx 0.155P(disease∣positive)=11618​=589​≈0.155

    The figure 0.900.900.90 is P(positive∣disease)P(\text{positive} \mid \text{disease})P(positive∣disease), the reversed conditional, and reading it as the answer is the classic base rate error.

  4. 4

    Events AAA and BBB are independent with P(A)=0.3P(A) = 0.3P(A)=0.3 and P(B)=0.6P(B) = 0.6P(B)=0.6. What is P(Ac∩B)P(A^{c} \cap B)P(Ac∩B), the probability that BBB happens and AAA does not?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Split BBB into the part inside AAA and the part outside it, since every outcome of BBB is in exactly one of the two.

    P(Ac∩B)=P(B)−P(A∩B)=0.6−(0.3)(0.6)=0.6−0.18=0.42P(A^{c} \cap B) = P(B) - P(A \cap B) = 0.6 - (0.3)(0.6) = 0.6 - 0.18 = 0.42P(Ac∩B)=P(B)−P(A∩B)=0.6−(0.3)(0.6)=0.6−0.18=0.42

    Factoring the same computation shows why the answer is so tidy.

    P(B)(1−P(A))=0.6×0.7=0.42=P(Ac) P(B)P(B)\bigl(1 - P(A)\bigr) = 0.6 \times 0.7 = 0.42 = P(A^{c})\,P(B)P(B)(1−P(A))=0.6×0.7=0.42=P(Ac)P(B)

    So AcA^{c}Ac and BBB are independent too, which is exactly what you would hope: if BBB says nothing about AAA, it says nothing about "not AAA" either.

  5. 5

    Events AAA and BBB are both mutually exclusive and independent. Which statement must be true?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Write down what each condition says and set them equal. Mutual exclusivity forces the intersection to be empty, and independence names its probability.

    P(A∩B)=0andP(A∩B)=P(A) P(B)P(A \cap B) = 0 \qquad \text{and} \qquad P(A \cap B) = P(A)\,P(B)P(A∩B)=0andP(A∩B)=P(A)P(B)

    Therefore P(A)P(B)=0P(A)P(B) = 0P(A)P(B)=0, and a product of real numbers is zero only when one of its factors is zero.

    P(A)=0orP(B)=0P(A) = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad P(B) = 0P(A)=0orP(B)=0

    This is the one degenerate case in which the two conditions can hold together: at least one of the events must be impossible. Nothing forces the two probabilities to be equal or to sum to 111, so those statements are not consequences.

    Three cards are drawn from a standard deck without replacement. What is the probability that all three are hearts?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Chain the three draws with the multiplication rule, shrinking the deck each time.

    P=1352⋅1251⋅1150=1716132600=11850≈0.0129P = \frac{13}{52} \cdot \frac{12}{51} \cdot \frac{11}{50} = \frac{1716}{132600} = \frac{11}{850} \approx 0.0129P=5213​⋅5112​⋅5011​=1326001716​=85011​≈0.0129

    Combinations give the same value, which is a good check on the arithmetic.

    (133)(523)=28622100=11850\frac{\binom{13}{3}}{\binom{52}{3}} = \frac{286}{22100} = \frac{11}{850}(352​)(313​)​=22100286​=85011​

    The value 164=(14)3\frac{1}{64} = \left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^3641​=(41​)3 would be right only with replacement, since it assumes the three draws are independent.

  7. 7

    Two fair dice are rolled. Let AAA be "the first die is even" and BBB be "the sum is 777". Are AAA and BBB independent?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Compute all three quantities and compare. There are 363636 equally likely rolls, 181818 of which have an even first die, and 666 of which sum to 777.

    P(A)=1836=12,P(B)=636=16P(A) = \frac{18}{36} = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad P(B) = \frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}P(A)=3618​=21​,P(B)=366​=61​

    The rolls in both events are (2,5)(2,5)(2,5), (4,3)(4,3)(4,3), and (6,1)(6,1)(6,1), so P(A∩B)=336=112P(A \cap B) = \frac{3}{36} = \frac{1}{12}P(A∩B)=363​=121​. Now test the product rule.

    P(A) P(B)=12⋅16=112=P(A∩B)P(A)\,P(B) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{12} = P(A \cap B)P(A)P(B)=21​⋅61​=121​=P(A∩B)

    The equation holds, so the events are independent. Whatever the first die shows, exactly one value of the second die completes a sum of 777, which is why the first die carries no information about the sum being 777.

    Two fair dice are rolled. Given that the sum is 888, what is the probability that the first die is even?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Conditioning shrinks the sample space to the rolls that sum to 888.

    B={(2,6),(3,5),(4,4),(5,3),(6,2)},∣B∣=5B = \{(2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2)\}, \qquad \lvert B \rvert = 5B={(2,6),(3,5),(4,4),(5,3),(6,2)},∣B∣=5

    Of those, the rolls with an even first die are (2,6)(2,6)(2,6), (4,4)(4,4)(4,4), and (6,2)(6,2)(6,2).

    P(first even∣sum=8)=35P(\text{first even} \mid \text{sum} = 8) = \frac{3}{5}P(first even∣sum=8)=53​

    Since P(first even)=12P(\text{first even}) = \frac{1}{2}P(first even)=21​ and 35≠12\frac{3}{5} \ne \frac{1}{2}53​=21​, these two events are dependent. Compare that with a sum of 777, where the same pair of events turns out to be independent. Independence is a numerical accident of the particular events, not a property you can see at a glance.

    A box holds 333 defective and 777 good items. Two are drawn at random without replacement. Given that the first is defective, what is the probability that the second is also defective?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Conditioning on the first draw changes the box. One defective item has been removed, so 222 defectives remain among 999 items.

    P(2nd defective∣1st defective)=29P(\text{2nd defective} \mid \text{1st defective}) = \frac{2}{9}P(2nd defective∣1st defective)=92​

    The unconditional probability that the second item is defective is 310\frac{3}{10}103​, so learning about the first draw lowered it. That is precisely what it means for the draws to be dependent.

    Two events satisfy P(A)=0.7P(A) = 0.7P(A)=0.7 and P(B)=0.6P(B) = 0.6P(B)=0.6. What is the smallest possible value of P(A∩B)P(A \cap B)P(A∩B)?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Rearrange the addition rule to isolate the intersection.

    P(A∩B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∪B)=1.3−P(A∪B)P(A \cap B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cup B) = 1.3 - P(A \cup B)P(A∩B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A∪B)=1.3−P(A∪B)

    The overlap is smallest when the union is as large as possible, and no probability can exceed 111, so P(A∪B)≤1P(A \cup B) \le 1P(A∪B)≤1.

    P(A∩B)≥1.3−1=0.3P(A \cap B) \ge 1.3 - 1 = 0.3P(A∩B)≥1.3−1=0.3

    The events cannot be disjoint: together they claim more than the whole sample space, so at least 0.30.30.3 of it must be shared. The value 0.420.420.42 is what independence would give, which is possible but is not the minimum.

    In a lottery you choose 666 different numbers from 111 to 494949, and six numbers are then drawn at random. What is the probability that your ticket matches all six drawn numbers?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The draw produces a set of six numbers, and the order they come out in does not affect whether your ticket matches, so the sample space is counted with combinations.

    ∣S∣=(496)=13,983,816\lvert S \rvert = \binom{49}{6} = 13{,}983{,}816∣S∣=(649​)=13,983,816

    Exactly one of those sets is the one on your ticket, so the event has a single favourable outcome.

    P(jackpot)=113,983,816≈7.2×10−8P(\text{jackpot}) = \frac{1}{13{,}983{,}816} \approx 7.2 \times 10^{-8}P(jackpot)=13,983,8161​≈7.2×10−8

    The expression 1496\frac{1}{49^6}4961​ would be right for six draws made with replacement in a fixed order, which is not how the draw works.
