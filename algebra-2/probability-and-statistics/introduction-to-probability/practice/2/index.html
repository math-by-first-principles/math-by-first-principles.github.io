  1. 1

    One card is drawn from a standard 525252-card deck. What is the probability that it is a heart or a face card (jack, queen, or king)?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    There are 131313 hearts and 121212 face cards, and three cards (the jack, queen, and king of hearts) belong to both groups. Adding 131313 and 121212 would count those three twice, so the addition rule subtracts them once.

    P(heart∪face)=1352+1252−352=2252=1126P(\text{heart} \cup \text{face}) = \frac{13}{52} + \frac{12}{52} - \frac{3}{52} = \frac{22}{52} = \frac{11}{26}P(heart∪face)=5213​+5212​−523​=5222​=2611​

    The value 2552\frac{25}{52}5225​ is exactly the answer you get by forgetting the correction term, and it claims 252525 favourable cards when only 222222 exist.

    One card is drawn from a standard deck. What is the probability that it is red or a king?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    There are 262626 red cards and 444 kings, and two cards are both red and kings (the king of hearts and the king of diamonds).

    P(red∪king)=2652+452−252=2852=713P(\text{red} \cup \text{king}) = \frac{26}{52} + \frac{4}{52} - \frac{2}{52} = \frac{28}{52} = \frac{7}{13}P(red∪king)=5226​+524​−522​=5228​=137​

    The value 1526=3052\frac{15}{26} = \frac{30}{52}2615​=5230​ is what you get by skipping the subtraction, which double counts the two red kings.

  3. 3

    Two fair dice are rolled. What is the probability that at least one die shows a 444?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Use complementary counting, since "at least one" is the opposite of "none". For neither die to show a 444, each die must land on one of the five other faces.

    P(no 4)=5×536=2536P(\text{no } 4) = \frac{5 \times 5}{36} = \frac{25}{36}P(no 4)=365×5​=3625​

    Subtract from 111.

    P(at least one 4)=1−2536=1136P(\text{at least one } 4) = 1 - \frac{25}{36} = \frac{11}{36}P(at least one 4)=1−3625​=3611​

    The answer 13=1236\frac{1}{3} = \frac{12}{36}31​=3612​ comes from adding 16+16\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{6}61​+61​, which double counts the roll (4,4)(4,4)(4,4).

  4. 4

    A fair coin is flipped three times. What is the probability of getting at least one head?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The sample space has 23=82^3 = 823=8 equally likely outcomes. The complement of "at least one head" is "no heads at all", which happens only for TTT, a single outcome.

    P(no heads)=18P(\text{no heads}) = \frac{1}{8}P(no heads)=81​

    Subtract from 111.

    P(at least one head)=1−18=78P(\text{at least one head}) = 1 - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{7}{8}P(at least one head)=1−81​=87​

    Events AAA and BBB satisfy P(A)=0.6P(A) = 0.6P(A)=0.6, P(B)=0.5P(B) = 0.5P(B)=0.5, and P(A∩B)=0.3P(A \cap B) = 0.3P(A∩B)=0.3. Which statement is true?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Test independence by comparing the intersection with the product of the two probabilities.

    P(A) P(B)=0.6×0.5=0.3=P(A∩B)P(A)\,P(B) = 0.6 \times 0.5 = 0.3 = P(A \cap B)P(A)P(B)=0.6×0.5=0.3=P(A∩B)

    The two sides agree, so the events are independent. They are not mutually exclusive, because P(A∩B)=0.3≠0P(A \cap B) = 0.3 \ne 0P(A∩B)=0.3=0. The conditional probability is P(A∣B)=0.30.5=0.6P(A \mid B) = \frac{0.3}{0.5} = 0.6P(A∣B)=0.50.3​=0.6, matching P(A)P(A)P(A) as independence requires, and the union is 0.6+0.5−0.3=0.80.6 + 0.5 - 0.3 = 0.80.6+0.5−0.3=0.8, not 1.11.11.1.

    A committee of 333 people is chosen at random from a group of 666 men and 444 women. What is the probability that all three chosen are men?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A committee is a set, so order does not matter and the counts are combinations. The sample space is every three-person committee from the 101010 people.

    ∣S∣=(103)=120,∣A∣=(63)=20\lvert S \rvert = \binom{10}{3} = 120, \qquad \lvert A \rvert = \binom{6}{3} = 20∣S∣=(310​)=120,∣A∣=(36​)=20

    Divide.

    P(all men)=20120=16P(\text{all men}) = \frac{20}{120} = \frac{1}{6}P(all men)=12020​=61​

    The value 27125=(35)3\frac{27}{125} = \left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^312527​=(53​)3 would be right only if each pick were made from the full group of 101010 with replacement, which would allow the same person to be chosen twice.

  7. 7

    A bag holds 555 red and 333 green marbles. Two are drawn without replacement. What is the probability that at least one is green?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    "At least one green" is the complement of "no green at all", and no green means both marbles are red, which was computed as 514\frac{5}{14}145​.

    P(at least one green)=1−P(both red)=1−514=914P(\text{at least one green}) = 1 - P(\text{both red}) = 1 - \frac{5}{14} = \frac{9}{14}P(at least one green)=1−P(both red)=1−145​=149​

    Counting the cases directly (exactly one green, or two green) would give the same answer with three times the work.

    Solution

    Events AAA and BBB are mutually exclusive with P(A)=0.3P(A) = 0.3P(A)=0.3 and P(B)=0.4P(B) = 0.4P(B)=0.4. What is P(A∣B)P(A \mid B)P(A∣B)?

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Mutually exclusive means A∩B=∅A \cap B = \emptysetA∩B=∅, so the intersection has probability 000. Since P(B)=0.4>0P(B) = 0.4 > 0P(B)=0.4>0, the conditional probability is defined.

    P(A∣B)=P(A∩B)P(B)=00.4=0P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} = \frac{0}{0.4} = 0P(A∣B)=P(B)P(A∩B)​=0.40​=0

    Learning that BBB happened rules AAA out completely, which is why the probability collapses from 0.30.30.3 all the way to 000.

    A fair spinner has 888 equal sectors numbered 111 through 888. Let AAA be "the result is even" and BBB be "the result is a multiple of 333". What is P(A∣B)P(A \mid B)P(A∣B)?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Conditioning on BBB shrinks the sample space to the multiples of 333 on the spinner.

    B={3,6},A∩B={6}B = \{3, 6\}, \qquad A \cap B = \{6\}B={3,6},A∩B={6}

    One of those two outcomes is even.

    P(A∣B)=∣A∩B∣∣B∣=12P(A \mid B) = \frac{\lvert A \cap B \rvert}{\lvert B \rvert} = \frac{1}{2}P(A∣B)=∣B∣∣A∩B∣​=21​

    Since P(A)=48=12P(A) = \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}P(A)=84​=21​ as well, the conditional probability equals the unconditional one, so AAA and BBB happen to be independent here.

  12. 12

    One card is drawn from a standard deck. Given that the card is a heart, what is the probability that it is a face card?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Knowing the card is a heart shrinks the sample space from 525252 cards to the 131313 hearts. Inside that world, the face cards are the jack, queen, and king of hearts.

    P(face∣heart)=∣face∩heart∣∣heart∣=313P(\text{face} \mid \text{heart}) = \frac{\lvert \text{face} \cap \text{heart} \rvert}{\lvert \text{heart} \rvert} = \frac{3}{13}P(face∣heart)=∣heart∣∣face∩heart∣​=133​

    The value 352\frac{3}{52}523​ is P(face and heart)P(\text{face and heart})P(face and heart), the numerator before the sample space was shrunk.
