  2. Problem 2 Two coordinate labels

    For the same normal distribution, the raw value 20 has z-score −1-1−1, and the raw value 35 has z-score 2. Find its standard deviation.

    1. Hint 1

      Within this distribution, differences in raw values are the corresponding differences in z-scores multiplied by the standard deviation.

    2. Hint 2

      The two z-scores are three standard deviations apart.

    Answer

    σ=5\sigma=5σ=5.

    Full solution

    The raw values differ by 35−20=1535-20=1535−20=15, and their z-scores differ by 2−(−1)=32-(-1)=32−(−1)=3.

    Thus

    3σ=15,3\sigma=15,3σ=15,

    so σ=5\sigma=5σ=5.

    The implied mean is 252525, which gives z-scores −1-1−1 and 222 for the stated values.

    Answer

    σ=5\sigma=5σ=5.

    Key idea

    Within one distribution with positive standard deviation, a raw-value difference equals the z-score difference times the standard deviation.

  6. Problem 6 A central measurement band

    A normal distribution has a central interval from 12 to 28 units extending two standard deviations on each side of its mean. Under the empirical rule, about what percentage of values fall below 8 units? State the mean and standard deviation used.

    1. Hint 1

      The midpoint of the central interval is the mean, and its half-width equals two standard deviations.

    2. Hint 2

      Locate 8 relative to that mean, then split the appropriate outer area equally between the two tails.

    Answer

    μ=20\mu=20μ=20 units, σ=4\sigma=4σ=4 units; approximately 0.15%0.15\%0.15% below 8 units.

    Full solution

    The mean is (12+28)/2=20(12+28)/2=20(12+28)/2=20, and the half-width is 888, so σ=4\sigma=4σ=4.

    The value 888 is three standard deviations below the mean because

    8−204=−3.\frac{8-20}{4}=-3.48−20​=−3.

    The rounded rule leaves about 0.3%0.3\%0.3% outside the central three-standard-deviation interval.

    Symmetry assigns half to the lower tail, about 0.15%0.15\%0.15%.

    Answer

    μ=20\mu=20μ=20 units, σ=4\sigma=4σ=4 units; approximately 0.15%0.15\%0.15% below 8 units.

    Key idea

    Recovering a normal scale from a central interval lets the empirical rule locate another tail.

  9. Problem 9 A mirrored comparison

    A normal quantity X has mean 50 and positive standard deviation. A student claims P(X<43)=P(X>57)P(X<43)=P(X>57)P(X<43)=P(X>57) without knowing the standard deviation. Is the claim correct? Explain why the tails have this relationship.

    1. Hint 1

      Both cutoffs are the same raw distance from the mean.

    2. Hint 2

      Compute each cutoff's signed distance from the mean, then use the symmetry of a normal density.

    Answer

    Yes; the tails are mirror images about 50, with both cutoffs 7 units from the mean.

    Full solution

    The deviations are 43−50=−743-50=-743−50=−7 and 57−50=757-50=757−50=7.

    Their z-scores are −7/σ-7/\sigma−7/σ and 7/σ7/\sigma7/σ.

    A normal density is symmetric about its mean, so reflection exchanges the lower and upper tails and gives

    P(X<43)=P(X>57).P(X<43)=P(X>57).P(X<43)=P(X>57).

    Their numerical size depends on the positive standard deviation, but their equality does not.

    Answer

    Yes; the tails are mirror images about 50, with both cutoffs 7 units from the mean.

    Key idea

    Normal tails at equal distances on opposite sides of the mean have equal areas.

  10. Problem 10 Two percentages
