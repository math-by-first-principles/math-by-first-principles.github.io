  4. 4

    Why does the sample variance divide by n−1n - 1n−1 instead of nnn?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The least-squares theorem says ∑(xi−c)2\sum (x_i - c)^2∑(xi​−c)2 is smallest at c=xˉc = \bar{x}c=xˉ. Feeding the population's true mean μ\muμ into that same sum can therefore only give something at least as big.

    ∑(xi−xˉ)2≤∑(xi−μ)2\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 \le \sum (x_i - \mu)^2∑(xi​−xˉ)2≤∑(xi​−μ)2

    Equality happens in exactly one case, the coincidence xˉ=μ\bar{x} = \muxˉ=μ, which a sample essentially never delivers.

    The spread you want is the one measured from μ\muμ, on the right. The spread you can actually compute is the one on the left, and it can never come out too big: it is too small in every sample but that lucky one, and not by bad luck but by construction, because the sample fitted its center to itself. An estimate that misses the same way nearly every time is biased, and this one is biased downward.

    Dividing by n−1n - 1n−1 rather than nnn inflates the result to compensate, so it makes the answer larger, not smaller. Proving that n−1n - 1n−1 is the exactly right correction needs expected values, which is a debt this lesson leaves open.

  5. 5

    Which measure of spread is resistant to outliers?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The interquartile range is Q3−Q1Q_3 - Q_1Q3​−Q1​, and both quartiles are medians of halves, so they depend only on the order of the values. In any data set big enough that the extremes sit outside the box, pushing the largest value further out leaves it as the largest value, so it changes no quartile and the IQR does not move. (With only three or four values the largest value is itself part of Q3Q_3Q3​, and then even the IQR shifts, which is why that caveat belongs on the claim.)

    The other three feel the size of the extreme values in every data set. The range is max−min\text{max} - \text{min}max−min, which is built from precisely the two most outlier-prone points. The variance and the standard deviation square the deviations, which makes a far-off value dominate the sum.

    The rule to carry away is that the median pairs with the IQR, and the mean pairs with the standard deviation. Never mix a resistant center with a sensitive spread.

    A class of 202020 students has a mean score of 707070. A second class of 303030 students has a mean score of 808080. What is the mean score of all 505050 students?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The two means cannot simply be averaged, because they summarize different numbers of students. Turn each mean back into a total.

    20×70=1400,30×80=240020 \times 70 = 1400, \qquad 30 \times 80 = 240020×70=1400,30×80=2400

    Now pool the scores and divide by the total number of students.

    xˉ=1400+240050=380050=76\bar{x} = \frac{1400 + 2400}{50} = \frac{3800}{50} = 76xˉ=501400+2400​=503800​=76

    The naive average of 707070 and 808080 is 757575, which is the trap. The true mean is pulled toward 808080 because more students sit in the higher-scoring class, and 767676 reflects that weighting.

  8. 8

    For a population of nnn values with mean μ\muμ and population variance σ2\sigma^2σ2, what does the minimum value of S(c)=∑(xi−c)2S(c) = \sum (x_i - c)^2S(c)=∑(xi​−c)2 equal?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The minimum of SSS sits at c=μc = \muc=μ, so the smallest possible total squared distance is

    S(μ)=∑(xi−μ)2.S(\mu) = \sum (x_i - \mu)^2 .S(μ)=∑(xi​−μ)2.

    Now read the definition of the population variance backwards. It is the average squared deviation, σ2=1n∑(xi−μ)2\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n}\sum (x_i - \mu)^2σ2=n1​∑(xi​−μ)2, so multiplying both sides by nnn recovers the sum.

    ∑(xi−μ)2=nσ2\sum (x_i - \mu)^2 = n\sigma^2∑(xi​−μ)2=nσ2

    So the variance is exactly the minimum total squared distance, shared out one part per value. That is not a coincidence of notation: the variance is defined to be the best score any center could achieve, divided by nnn. It is zero only when every value equals the mean.

    For the data 2,4,92, 4, 92,4,9 (mean 555, median 444), which center gives the smaller total absolute distance ∑∣xi−c∣\sum \lvert x_i - c \rvert∑∣xi​−c∣?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Compute the total absolute distance at each candidate. At the mean, c=5c = 5c=5,

    ∣2−5∣+∣4−5∣+∣9−5∣=3+1+4=8.\lvert 2 - 5\rvert + \lvert 4 - 5\rvert + \lvert 9 - 5\rvert = 3 + 1 + 4 = 8.∣2−5∣+∣4−5∣+∣9−5∣=3+1+4=8.

    At the median, c=4c = 4c=4,

    ∣2−4∣+∣4−4∣+∣9−4∣=2+0+5=7.\lvert 2 - 4\rvert + \lvert 4 - 4\rvert + \lvert 9 - 4\rvert = 2 + 0 + 5 = 7.∣2−4∣+∣4−4∣+∣9−4∣=2+0+5=7.

    The median wins, and by the pairing theorem it is the unique winner for this odd-sized data set. Run the same contest with squared distance and the result flips: the mean scores 9+1+16=269 + 1 + 16 = 269+1+16=26 while the median scores 4+0+25=294 + 0 + 25 = 294+0+25=29. Each center wins its own game and loses the other one.

    You measure the height of all 121212 players on a basketball team, and the team itself is the entire group you care about. Which divisor should the variance use?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The divisor is a statement about what you are claiming, not about how many numbers you happen to have.

    Here the twelve players are the whole group of interest. There is no larger population being estimated, so the mean you computed is the true mean μ\muμ, not a stand-in for one. Nothing is being fitted, so nothing needs correcting.

    σ2=112∑(xi−μ)2\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{12}\sum (x_i - \mu)^2σ2=121​∑(xi​−μ)2

    The n−1n - 1n−1 correction exists only to compensate for measuring deviations from a sample mean that was fitted to the sample itself. If you had chosen 121212 players out of a whole league in order to estimate the league, you would divide by 111111.

    The population 4,6,8,10,124, 6, 8, 10, 124,6,8,10,12 has mean 888 and population variance 888. Which change leaves the population standard deviation exactly unchanged?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Adding a constant to every value shifts the mean by that same constant, so every deviation survives untouched.

    (xi+7)−(xˉ+7)=xi−xˉ(x_i + 7) - (\bar{x} + 7) = x_i - \bar{x}(xi​+7)−(xˉ+7)=xi​−xˉ

    Each of the other three changes it. Multiplying by 777 multiplies every deviation by 777 and so multiplies the variance by 494949. Adding a value equal to the mean contributes a zero deviation while raising the count, dropping the variance to 406≈6.67\frac{40}{6} \approx 6.67640​≈6.67.
