    1. The denominator you build, and the one you reach for . Foundational, 13 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 13 pts

    Two rational expressions can always be combined over the product of their denominators, and they can also be combined over the least common denominator, which is usually smaller. This question runs one sum both ways and asks what the difference between the two routes really amounts to.

    2. B

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Everything asked here is settled by the factored forms, so factor both denominators before comparing anything. A trinomial and a perfect square look unrelated until they are products, and then whatever they have in common is visible at a glance.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Neither denominator is a product yet, and nothing about a common denominator can be settled while they are sums. The first is a trinomial whose two numbers multiply to 101010 and add to 777, and the second is a perfect square:

    x2+7x+10=(x+2)(x+5),x2+10x+25=(x+5)2x^2+7x+10 = (x+2)(x+5), \qquad x^2+10x+25 = (x+5)^2x2+7x+10=(x+2)(x+5),x2+10x+25=(x+5)2

    The distinct factors across the pair are x+2x+2x+2 and x+5x+5x+5. The highest power of x+2x+2x+2 reached inside a single denominator is the first, and the highest power of x+5x+5x+5 is the second, so

    LCD=(x+2)(x+5)2\text{LCD} = (x+2)(x+5)^2LCD=(x+2)(x+5)2

    The excluded values come from the denominators as they were given, and those vanish at x=−2x=-2x=−2 and at x=−5x=-5x=−5. You can also read them straight off the factored least common denominator, which is zero at exactly those two values and nowhere else.

    Part B

    3x2+30x+75+4x2+28x+403x^2+30x+75+4x^2+28x+403x2+30x+75+4x2+28x+40

    which collects to 7x2+58x+1157x^2+58x+1157x2+58x+115, so the route arrives at

    7x2+58x+115(x+2)(x+5)3\frac{7x^2+58x+115}{(x+2)(x+5)^3}(x+2)(x+5)37x2+58x+115​

    That is not in lowest terms, and finishing means factoring a quadratic you created yourself, 7x2+58x+115=(x+5)(7x+23)7x^2+58x+115 = (x+5)(7x+23)7x2+58x+115=(x+5)(7x+23), then cancelling the surplus x+5x+5x+5.     Now the condition. A factor common to both denominators appears twice in their product but only once in the least common denominator, so the product runs surplus exactly when a common factor exists. The product is therefore the least common denominator precisely when the two denominators share no factor other than 111.

    Whole numbers count as factors here, which is what the pair 2x2x2x and 444 is for. They share 222. Their product is 8x8x8x, while the least common denominator takes one xxx and lcm⁡(2,4)=4\operatorname{lcm}(2,4)=4lcm(2,4)=4:

    LCD(2x,4)=4x\text{LCD}(2x, 4) = 4xLCD(2x,4)=4x

    A criterion that inspected only the polynomial factors would have called 8x8x8x least, and it is not.

    Part B

    Write the two expressions as AP\dfrac{A}{P}PA​ and BQ\dfrac{B}{Q}QB​, each in lowest terms, with PPP and QQQ sharing no common factor. Because they share nothing, the recipe for the least common denominator takes every factor of each, so it is the product PQPQPQ, and combining gives

    AP±BQ=AQ±BPPQ\frac{A}{P} \pm \frac{B}{Q} = \frac{AQ \pm BP}{PQ}PA​±QB​=PQAQ±BP​

    Call that numerator NNN. Being in lowest terms means no irreducible factor divides both NNN and PQPQPQ, so suppose some irreducible fff divides PQPQPQ. Take the first case; the second is the same argument with the letters swapped.
