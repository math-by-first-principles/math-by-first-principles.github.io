      Compare the factors that look alike: are they the same, or opposites?

    2. Hint 2

      Ask what single number turns one factor of each look-alike pair into the other, and count how many such pairs there are.

  2. Problem 2 Factor, exclude, divide out, record

    Write 42−3x6x2+30x−1596\frac{42-3x}{6x^2+30x-1596}6x2+30x−159642−3x​ in lowest terms, together with every restriction it must carry.

    1. Hint 1

      A common factor can be hidden when a polynomial is written as a sum.

    2. Hint 2

      Take the common numerical factor out of each part, factor what is left, then compare the factors that look alike.

    3. Hint 3

      Read the excluded values off the denominator you started with, before you divide anything out.

    Answer

    −12(x+19)-\frac{1}{2(x+19)}−2(x+19)1​, equivalently −12x+38-\frac{1}{2x+38}−2x+381​, for x≠14x\ne14x=14 and x≠−19x\ne-19x=−19.

    Factor both parts.

    The numerator has a common factor of 333:

    42−3x=3(14−x).42-3x=3(14-x).42−3x=3(14−x).

    The denominator has a common factor of 666, leaving x2+5x−266x^2+5x-266x2+5x−266, and the numbers −14-14−14 and 191919 multiply to −266-266−266 and add to 555, so the denominator is 6(x−14)(x+19)6(x-14)(x+19)6(x−14)(x+19).

    Read the excluded values off that original denominator now.

    It is zero at 141414 and at −19-19−19.

    The factors 14−x14-x14−x and x−14x-14x−14 are the same two quantities subtracted in opposite order, so 14−x=−(x−14)14-x=-(x-14)14−x=−(x−14), and the numerator is −3(x−14)-3(x-14)−3(x−14).

    On the domain x−14x-14x−14 is nonzero, so it divides out, and the numerical factors reduce the way 36\frac{3}{6}63​ reduces to 12\frac1221​:

    −3(x−14)6(x−14)(x+19)=−12(x+19)\frac{-3(x-14)}{6(x-14)(x+19)}=-\frac{1}{2(x+19)}6(x−14)(x+19)−3(x−14)​=−2(x+19)1​

  3. Problem 3 Numerical factors

    Write 12x2+1218x2+108\frac{12x^2+12}{18x^2+108}18x2+10812x2+12​ in lowest terms for real xxx, with its domain.

    1. Hint 1

      Lowest terms asks for two kinds of common factor: numerical ones and polynomial ones.

    2. Hint 2

      A quadratic with no real zero has no linear factor with real coefficients. Ask what the two could still have in common.

    Answer

    2(x2+1)3(x2+6)\frac{2(x^2+1)}{3(x^2+6)}3(x2+6)2(x2+1)​, equivalently 2x2+23x2+18\frac{2x^2+2}{3x^2+18}3x2+182x2+2​; all real xxx.

    Full solution

    The numerator is 12(x2+1)12(x^2+1)12(x2+1) and the denominator is 18(x2+6)18(x^2+6)18(x2+6).

    Reducing 12/1812/1812/18 gives

    2(x2+1)3(x2+6)\frac{2(x^2+1)}{3(x^2+6)}3(x2+6)2(x2+1)​

    The denominator is positive for all real xxx, so nothing is excluded.

    Neither x2+1x^2+1x2+1 nor x2+6x^2+6x2+6 has a real zero, so neither has a linear factor with real coefficients, and a common non-constant factor with real coefficients would have to be a quadratic dividing both.

    A quadratic that divides another quadratic is a constant multiple of it, so such a factor would be a constant multiple of each, and since both are monic that would force x2+1x^2+1x2+1 and x2+6x^2+6x2+6 to be equal, which they are not.

    Nothing further cancels.

    Answer

    2(x2+1)3(x2+6)\frac{2(x^2+1)}{3(x^2+6)}3(x2+6)2(x2+1)​, equivalently 2x2+23x2+18\frac{2x^2+2}{3x^2+18}3x2+182x2+2​; all real xxx.

    Key idea

    Lowest terms requires reducing numerical factors as well as removing shared polynomial factors.

  4. Problem 4 Mass along a strip

    A strip has mass 4x2+11x−34x^2+11x-34x2+11x−3 grams and length x+3x+3x+3 cm, where x>1x>1x>1. Find its mass per cm as a simplified expression, and evaluate it when x=5x=5x=5.

    1. Hint 1

      Mass per cm is total mass divided by total length.

    2. Hint 2

      Factor both quantities before reducing the quotient.

    Answer

    4x−14x-14x−1 grams per cm for x>1x>1x>1; 191919 grams per cm when x=5x=5x=5.

    Full solution

    The quotient is (4x2+11x−3)/(x+3)(4x^2+11x-3)/(x+3)(4x2+11x−3)/(x+3).

    Its denominator is positive for every x>1x>1x>1, so nothing on the model's range is excluded.

    The numerator factors as (4x−1)(x+3)(4x-1)(x+3)(4x−1)(x+3), and dividing out the common factor leaves

    (4x−1)(x+3)x+3=4x−1\frac{(4x-1)(x+3)}{x+3}=4x-1x+3(4x−1)(x+3)​=4x−1

    for x>1x>1x>1.

    At x=5x=5x=5 the rate is 191919 grams per cm.

    The original quantities are 152152152 grams and 888 cm, whose quotient is also 191919.

    Answer

    4x−14x-14x−1 grams per cm for x>1x>1x>1; 191919 grams per cm when x=5x=5x=5.

    Key idea

    A rate formed from polynomial measurements can often be simplified before substitution.

  5. Problem 5 Designing a restricted formula

    Construct a rational expression with monic quadratic denominator that equals 2x+52x+52x+5 wherever it is defined and excludes exactly the real inputs −16-16−16 and 131313. Give the numerator and denominator in factored form.

    1. Hint 1

      The excluded inputs determine the monic denominator.

    2. Hint 2

      Ask what the numerator must contain if the denominator is to disappear entirely.

    Answer

    (2x+5)(x+16)(x−13)(x+16)(x−13)\frac{(2x+5)(x+16)(x-13)}{(x+16)(x-13)}(x+16)(x−13)(2x+5)(x+16)(x−13)​, with x≠−16x\ne-16x=−16 and x≠13x\ne13x=13.

    Full solution

    The only monic quadratic whose zeros are exactly −16-16−16 and 131313 is (x+16)(x−13)(x+16)(x-13)(x+16)(x−13).

    Multiplying it by the desired output gives the numerator, and on the permitted inputs the quotient is that output:

    (2x+5)(x+16)(x−13)(x+16)(x−13)=2x+5\frac{(2x+5)(x+16)(x-13)}{(x+16)(x-13)}=2x+5(x+16)(x−13)(2x+5)(x+16)(x−13)​=2x+5

    The original denominator is zero precisely at −16-16−16 and 131313, so these exclusions are exact, and no other input is excluded.

    Answer

    (2x+5)(x+16)(x−13)(x+16)(x−13)\frac{(2x+5)(x+16)(x-13)}{(x+16)(x-13)}(x+16)(x−13)(2x+5)(x+16)(x−13)​, with x≠−16x\ne-16x=−16 and x≠13x\ne13x=13.

    Key idea

    The original denominator of a single quotient decides its excluded inputs, so it is the part built from them first.

  6. Problem 6 Two cubic expressions

    Simplify (x+1)3−(x−1)36x2+2\frac{(x+1)^3-(x-1)^3}{6x^2+2}6x2+2(x+1)3−(x−1)3​ for real xxx and state its domain.

    1. Hint 1

      How can subtracting two polynomials change the degree of the result?

    2. Hint 2

      Expand both cubes and collect, then compare the degree of what survives with the denominator's.

    Answer

    111; all real xxx.

    Full solution

    Expand the two cubes.

    The cubic terms cancel, the linear terms cancel, and the remaining numerator is

    (x+1)3−(x−1)3=6x2+2(x+1)^3-(x-1)^3=6x^2+2(x+1)3−(x−1)3=6x2+2

    The denominator is positive for every real xxx.

    The expression is therefore 111 throughout the real line.

    At x=0x=0x=0, the numerator and denominator are both 222, providing a quick check.

    Answer

    111; all real xxx.

    Key idea

    Simplification may first require revealing a common factor hidden by subtraction.

  7. Problem 7 A quadratic denominator

    Let aaa be real and R(x)=x4+ax2+1x2+1R(x)=\frac{x^4+ax^2+1}{x^2+1}R(x)=x2+1x4+ax2+1​. Find the value of aaa for which the denominator cancels completely, then give the reduced expression and its real domain.

    1. Hint 1

      Polynomial division can reveal the remainder without finding any real denominator zero.

    2. Hint 2

      The first quotient term is x2x^2x2; continue until the remainder is constant.

    Answer

    a=2a=2a=2; R(x)=x2+1R(x)=x^2+1R(x)=x2+1 for all real xxx.

    Full solution

    The denominator is positive for every real xxx, so the domain is the full real line.

    Let NNN denote the numerator.

    Division gives quotient x2+a−1x^2+a-1x2+a−1 and remainder 2−a2-a2−a, since

    N=(x2+1)(x2+a−1)+2−aN=(x^2+1)(x^2+a-1)+2-aN=(x2+1)(x2+a−1)+2−a

    Complete cancellation requires the remainder to be zero, so a=2a=2a=2.

    The numerator then becomes (x2+1)2(x^2+1)^2(x2+1)2, and the reduced expression is x2+1x^2+1x2+1.

    There are no excluded real inputs.

    Answer

    a=2a=2a=2; R(x)=x2+1R(x)=x^2+1R(x)=x2+1 for all real xxx.

    Key idea

    Exact polynomial division can identify a cancellable quadratic factor even when it has no real zeros.

  8. Problem 8 A right answer from a wrong move

    A classmate simplifies 4x+68x+17\frac{4x+68}{x+17}x+174x+68​ by crossing out the constant terms 686868 and 171717, then the xxx left in each part, and reports the result as 444.

    Decide whether 444 is the correct simplified form, and state its restriction. Then decide whether the crossing-out is a valid method, supporting your decision by applying the same crossing-out to 4x+20x+17\frac{4x+20}{x+17}x+174x+20​.

    1. Hint 1

      A cancellation divides the whole numerator and the whole denominator by the same factor.

    2. Hint 2

      For the first decision, try writing the numerator as a product; for the second, evaluate both forms of the new expression at one input it allows.

    Answer

    Yes: 444 for x≠−17x\ne-17x=−17. The method is not valid: it also turns 4x+20x+17\frac{4x+20}{x+17}x+174x+20​ into 444, but at x=1x=1x=1 that expression is 43\frac4334​.

    Full solution

    The original denominator x+17x+17x+17 is zero only at −17-17−17, so that is the one excluded value.

    The numerator has a common factor of 444:

    4x+68=4(x+17).4x+68=4(x+17).4x+68=4(x+17).

    On the domain x+17x+17x+17 is nonzero, so it divides out of the whole numerator and the whole denominator:

    4(x+17)x+17=4\frac{4(x+17)}{x+17}=4x+174(x+17)​=4

    So the reported 444 is right, provided it carries x≠−17x\ne-17x=−17.

    The method is another matter.

    Applied to 4x+20x+17\frac{4x+20}{x+17}x+174x+20​, crossing out 202020 and 171717 and then the xxxs again reports 444.

    But x=1x=1x=1 is allowed there, and the value at 111 is 2418=43\frac{24}{18}=\frac431824​=34​, not 444.

    A method that gives a wrong result is not valid, so the right result above came from the factor 4(x+17)4(x+17)4(x+17), not from the crossing-out.

    Crossing out is not division.

    Dividing the whole numerator 4x+684x+684x+68 by 171717 gives 4x17+4\frac{4x}{17}+4174x​+4, not 4x4x4x: the 686868 does not disappear, it becomes 444.

    Deleting addends is not a valid rule, even when it lands on a right answer.

    Dividing the whole numerator and the whole denominator by the same common factor keeps the value on the original domain, and every input the original excluded stays excluded.

    Answer

    Yes: 444 for x≠−17x\ne-17x=−17. The method is not valid: it also turns 4x+20x+17\frac{4x+20}{x+17}x+174x+20​ into 444, but at x=1x=1x=1 that expression is 43\frac4334​.

    Key idea

    A right answer does not certify the method: crossing out addends is invalid even where a genuine common factor happens to give the same result.

  9. Problem 9 Same simplified form, different domains

    Let A=(x+18)(2x−5)(2x−5)(x−4)A=\frac{(x+18)(2x-5)}{(2x-5)(x-4)}A=(2x−5)(x−4)(x+18)(2x−5)​ and B=(x+18)(3x−7)(3x−7)(x−4)B=\frac{(x+18)(3x-7)}{(3x-7)(x-4)}B=(3x−7)(x−4)(x+18)(3x−7)​.

    Both simplify to x+18x−4\frac{x+18}{x-4}x−4x+18​ wherever each is defined. Find every input at which exactly one of AAA and BBB is defined, and say which one is defined there.

    1. Hint 1

      Two expressions can share a simplified form and still differ in where they are defined.

    2. Hint 2

      List the excluded values of each original expression, then compare the two lists.

    Answer

    At x=52x=\frac52x=25​ only BBB is defined, and at x=73x=\frac73x=37​ only AAA is defined.

    Full solution

    Read each domain off its original denominator.

    The denominator of AAA is (2x−5)(x−4)(2x-5)(x-4)(2x−5)(x−4), which is zero at 52\frac5225​ and at 444.

    The denominator of BBB is (3x−7)(x−4)(3x-7)(x-4)(3x−7)(x−4), which is zero at 73\frac7337​ and at 444.

    So both are undefined at 444.

    At 52\frac5225​ only AAA is undefined, so BBB is defined there; at 73\frac7337​ only BBB is undefined, so AAA is defined there.

    Those two inputs are the only ones at which exactly one of them is defined.

    A check at x=52x=\frac52x=25​: the denominator of BBB is 12⋅(−32)\frac12\cdot\left(-\frac32\right)21​⋅(−23​), which is not zero, so BBB is defined there, and its value is that of x+18x−4\frac{x+18}{x-4}x−4x+18​, which is −413-\frac{41}{3}−341​.

    At every input other than 444, 52\frac5225​ and 73\frac7337​, both are defined and both equal x+18x−4\frac{x+18}{x-4}x−4x+18​.

    Simplifying preserved their values, not their domains, and the domains are what tell them apart.

    Answer

    At x=52x=\frac52x=25​ only BBB is defined, and at x=73x=\frac73x=37​ only AAA is defined.

    Key idea

    Expressions with the same simplified form can still differ in their domains, because each keeps the restrictions of its own original denominator.

  10. Problem 10 A domain after reduction

    A real rational expression reduces to a nonzero constant after all common factors are canceled. A student concludes that the original expression has no excluded inputs. Is that conclusion forced? Give one example supporting your decision and state its exact domain.

    Then show that for every such expression, the numerator is also zero at each excluded input.

    1. Hint 1

      What decides the domain: the original expression or the reduced one?

    2. Hint 2

      Write a possible numerator and denominator in factored form, and investigate their real zeros before simplifying.

    3. Hint 3

      If canceling everything common leaves only constants, what one factor must the numerator and the denominator each be a constant multiple of?

    Answer

    No; 5(x−15)(x+21)(x−15)(x+21)\frac{5(x-15)(x+21)}{(x-15)(x+21)}(x−15)(x+21)5(x−15)(x+21)​ reduces to 555 but has domain x≠15x\ne15x=15, x≠−21x\ne-21x=−21. For every such expression, each excluded input is also a zero of the numerator.

    Full solution

    For the example, the denominator (x−15)(x+21)(x-15)(x+21)(x−15)(x+21) is zero at 151515 and at −21-21−21, and nowhere else.

    At every allowed input the factor (x−15)(x+21)(x-15)(x+21)(x−15)(x+21) is nonzero and divides out:

    5(x−15)(x+21)(x−15)(x+21)=5\frac{5(x-15)(x+21)}{(x-15)(x+21)}=5(x−15)(x+21)5(x−15)(x+21)​=5

    The reduced constant 555 is defined at 151515 and at −21-21−21, but the original is 00\frac{0}{0}00​ at both and is undefined there.

    So a constant reduced value does not determine the original domain, and the conclusion is not forced.

    For the general claim, let FFF be everything the numerator and denominator have in common.

    Canceling it leaves only constants, so the numerator is aFaFaF and the denominator is bFbFbF for nonzero constants aaa and bbb, and the reduced value is ab\frac{a}{b}ba​.

    At an excluded input the denominator bFbFbF is zero, and bbb is not, so FFF is zero there.

    Then the numerator aFaFaF is zero too.

    Answer

    No; 5(x−15)(x+21)(x−15)(x+21)\frac{5(x-15)(x+21)}{(x-15)(x+21)}(x−15)(x+21)5(x−15)(x+21)​ reduces to 555 but has domain x≠15x\ne15x=15, x≠−21x\ne-21x=−21. For every such expression, each excluded input is also a zero of the numerator.

    Key idea

    An expression that reduces to a constant is 00\frac0000​ at each of its excluded inputs, and the constant keeps no trace of them.
