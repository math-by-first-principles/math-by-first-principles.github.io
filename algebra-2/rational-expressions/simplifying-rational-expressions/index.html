Simplifying Rational Expressions

Intuition

You already know how to reduce 68\frac{6}{8}86​ to 34\frac{3}{4}43​. You find the factor 222 sitting inside both the top and the bottom, and you divide it out. A rational expression is that same object with polynomials in place of the integers, and reducing it is that same move. One thing is genuinely new. The denominator 888 can never be zero, but a denominator like x−1x - 1x−1 can be, for one value of xxx, and at that value the expression has no meaning at all. So every rational expression arrives with a list of excluded values, the inputs it forbids, and keeping that list honest is the habit this whole chapter runs on.

Learning goals

  • Find the domain from the original denominator, before cancelling
  • Cancel common factors only, never common terms
  • Attach the restriction to a simplified expression
  • Pull a −1-1−1 out of opposite factors
  • Recognize lowest terms, including reducing any common numerical factor

What a rational expression is

A rational expression is a quotient

p(x)q(x),\frac{p(x)}{q(x)},q(x)p(x)​,

where ppp and qqq are polynomials and qqq is not the zero polynomial. The name is the same word as in rational number, and for the same reason: both are ratios. A rational number is a ratio of two integers, and a rational expression is a ratio of two polynomials.

These all qualify:

x+2x2−9,5x,3x3−x7,x2+4x.\frac{x+2}{x^2-9}, \qquad \frac{5}{x}, \qquad \frac{3x^3-x}{7}, \qquad x^2+4x.x2−9x+2​,x5​,73x3−x​,x2+4x.

The last two are worth a second look. In 3x3−x7\frac{3x^3-x}{7}73x3−x​ the denominator is the constant polynomial 777, which is a perfectly good polynomial. And x2+4xx^2+4xx2+4x is a rational expression because it can be written as x2+4x1\frac{x^2+4x}{1}1x2+4x​. Every polynomial is a rational expression, exactly as every integer nnn is the fraction n1\frac{n}{1}1n​.

The condition on qqq needs care, because it is easy to misread. Saying ”qqq is not the zero polynomial” means not all of its coefficients are 000. It does not mean ”q(x)q(x)q(x) is never 000”. The polynomial x−1x-1x−1 is a perfectly good denominator, yet it evaluates to 000 at x=1x=1x=1. That gap, between a polynomial that is never the zero polynomial and a polynomial that can still equal zero at a particular input, is the entire subject of the next section.

The domain comes first

The domain of a rational expression is the set of real numbers you are allowed to substitute for xxx. Polynomials themselves cause no trouble: a polynomial can be evaluated at every real number. The only thing that can go wrong is division by zero. So:

the domain of p(x)q(x) is every real number x with q(x)≠0.\text{the domain of } \frac{p(x)}{q(x)} \text{ is every real number } x \text{ with } q(x) \neq 0.the domain of q(x)p(x)​ is every real number x with q(x)=0.

The excluded values are exactly the real zeros of the denominator, the solutions of q(x)=0q(x)=0q(x)=0. Nothing the numerator does can rescue them. If q(x0)=0q(x_0)=0q(x0​)=0 and p(x0)≠0p(x_0) \neq 0p(x0​)=0, no number times 000 can give something nonzero, so no quotient exists there. If p(x0)=0p(x_0)=0p(x0​)=0 as well, the quotient is no better off: every number times 000 gives 000, so nothing pins a single value down. Either way, x0x_0x0​ is excluded.

Take x+5x2−4x+3\frac{x+5}{x^2-4x+3}x2−4x+3x+5​. Factor the denominator:

x2−4x+3=(x−1)(x−3),x^2-4x+3 = (x-1)(x-3),x2−4x+3=(x−1)(x−3),

which is zero exactly when x=1x=1x=1 or x=3x=3x=3. The domain is every real number except 111 and 333. Notice that x=−5x=-5x=−5, the zero of the numerator, is not excluded at all; there the expression is a legitimate 048=0\frac{0}{48}=0480​=0.

Find the excluded values from the original denominator, before you cancel anything. This is the most important habit in the chapter. Cancelling can erase a factor from the page, but it cannot erase the restriction that factor caused. The value that made it zero is still excluded, even though nothing left in the simplified expression shows it.

Why cancelling works

Reducing 68\frac{6}{8}86​ to 34\frac{3}{4}43​ works because dividing the top and the bottom of a fraction by the same nonzero number never changes its value:

68=2⋅32⋅4=34.\frac{6}{8} = \frac{2\cdot3}{2\cdot4} = \frac{3}{4}.86​=2⋅42⋅3​=43​.

The two 222s divide out because they are a shared factor, not because they merely look alike. Written for any numbers, that is

acbc=abwhenever b≠0 and c≠0.\frac{ac}{bc} = \frac{a}{b} \qquad \text{whenever } b \neq 0 \text{ and } c \neq 0.bcac​=ba​whenever b=0 and c=0.

Both conditions matter. You need b≠0b \neq 0b=0 so that ab\frac{a}{b}ba​ names a number at all, and you need c≠0c \neq 0c=0 so that the factor you are cancelling was never secretly zero.

That rule follows directly from what a quotient means: ab\frac{a}{b}ba​ is the one number that, multiplied by bbb, gives back aaa. You can read the argument written out in full in the Go deeper section at the end of this lesson.

See the full proof

Move to polynomials and nothing about the idea changes, only the objects being multiplied. If ppp, qqq and ddd are polynomials, then at any real number xxx for which q(x)≠0q(x) \neq 0q(x)=0 and d(x)≠0d(x) \neq 0d(x)=0,

p(x) d(x)q(x) d(x)=p(x)q(x).\frac{p(x)\,d(x)}{q(x)\,d(x)} = \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}.q(x)d(x)p(x)d(x)​=q(x)p(x)​.

That equation holds at those values of xxx, and nowhere else. Keep the condition in view. You will see it bite within the page.

The rule cancels factors, and only factors. The ccc in acbc\frac{ac}{bc}bcac​ multiplies all of the numerator and all of the denominator. A term in a sum does not have that power, which is why x+2x\frac{x+2}{x}xx+2​ is the single most common cancellation mistake in algebra. The xxx upstairs is added to 222; it does not multiply the 222. Dividing the numerator by xxx divides every term of it, so what really happens is

x+2x=xx+2x=1+2x(x≠0).\frac{x+2}{x} = \frac{x}{x} + \frac{2}{x} = 1 + \frac{2}{x} \qquad (x \neq 0).xx+2​=xx​+x2​=1+x2​(x=0).

The 222 does not vanish; it turns into 2x\frac{2}{x}x2​. Crossing out the xxx and reporting 222 is a false statement, and one input settles it. At x=3x=3x=3,

3+23=53,but the crossing-out answer says 2.\frac{3+2}{3} = \frac{5}{3}, \qquad \text{but the crossing-out answer says } 2.33+2​=35​,but the crossing-out answer says 2.

So x+2x\frac{x+2}{x}xx+2​ is already in lowest terms; there is nothing there to cancel. Cancelling is never “erase a symbol you can see twice”. It is “divide the top and the bottom by the same nonzero quantity”, and dividing the top means dividing all of it.

This is why the first step of every simplification is to factor. Until the numerator and denominator are written as products, the theorem has nothing to grip. Written as they stand, x2−1x^2-1x2−1 and x−1x-1x−1 share no visible common factor; written as (x−1)(x+1)(x-1)(x+1)(x−1)(x+1) and (x−1)(x-1)(x−1), they obviously do.

The hole that cancelling leaves behind

Here is the example the rest of the chapter leans on. Take

x2−1x−1=(x−1)(x+1)x−1.\frac{x^2-1}{x-1} = \frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1}.x−1x2−1​=x−1(x−1)(x+1)​.

Read the domain off that denominator before touching anything: it is zero exactly at x=1x=1x=1, so x=1x=1x=1 is excluded. For every other xxx the factor x−1x-1x−1 is nonzero, so the cancellation theorem applies with c=x−1c = x-1c=x−1, and it gives

x2−1x−1=x+1(x≠1).\frac{x^2-1}{x-1} = x+1 \qquad (x \neq 1).x−1x2−1​=x+1(x=1).

Now look hard at what that line does and does not say. The expression x2−1x−1\frac{x^2-1}{x-1}x−1x2−1​ and the polynomial x+1x+1x+1 are not the same object. The polynomial x+1x+1x+1 is perfectly happy at x=1x=1x=1, where it takes the value 222. The original expression is undefined at x=1x=1x=1, because its denominator is 000 there. Every row agrees except one, and in that row the left column has nothing at all to report. So here is the principle, and it is the intellectual heart of the lesson:

Cancelling preserves the value wherever both sides are defined. It does not preserve the domain.

Not every restriction hides. If a factor of the denominator survives the cancelling, its restriction is still visible in the answer. The dangerous ones are the restrictions that belonged to factors you cancelled away: the simplified expression is defined there and gives no hint that the original was not.

Check your understanding

For which values of xxx is the statement x2−4x−2=x+2\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2} = x+2x−2x2−4​=x+2 true?

Answer choices

Factor the numerator and cancel, keeping track of where the cancelled factor is nonzero.

x2−4x−2=(x−2)(x+2)x−2=x+2(x≠2)\frac{x^2-4}{x-2} = \frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2} = x+2 \qquad (x \neq 2)x−2x2−4​=x−2(x−2)(x+2)​=x+2(x=2)

At every x≠2x \neq 2x=2 both sides are defined and equal. At x=2x=2x=2 the left side is undefined (its denominator is 000) while the right side is 444, so the statement fails there. The equality holds for all real xxx except x=2x=2x=2.

Simplifying, step by step

A rational expression is in lowest terms when there is nothing left to divide out: any common numerical factor between the coefficients has been reduced, the way 68\frac{6}{8}86​ reduces to 34\frac{3}{4}43​, and the numerator and denominator share no common non-constant factor, one that actually contains xxx. Bare constants are left out of that second half on purpose, because every nonzero constant divides every polynomial; if a shared constant alone disqualified an expression, nothing would ever be in lowest terms. To get there, follow four steps, and never skip the second.

  1. Factor the numerator and the denominator completely.
  2. Read the excluded values off the original denominator. Every zero of it is excluded, whether or not its factor survives step 3.
  3. Divide out every common factor, numerical and polynomial. Each cancellation is legal precisely because that factor is nonzero everywhere on the domain from step 2.
  4. Write the simplified expression together with its restrictions.

Worked example 1 Simplify 3x2+6xx2−4\dfrac{3x^2+6x}{x^2-4}x2−43x2+6x​ and state its domain

Factor the top and the bottom completely. The numerator has a common factor of 3x3x3x, and the denominator is a difference of squares:

3x2+6xx2−4=3x(x+2)(x−2)(x+2).\frac{3x^2+6x}{x^2-4} = \frac{3x(x+2)}{(x-2)(x+2)}.x2−43x2+6x​=(x−2)(x+2)3x(x+2)​.

Now read the excluded values from that denominator, before cancelling. It is zero when x=2x=2x=2 and when x=−2x=-2x=−2, so the domain is every real number except 222 and −2-2−2.

On that domain both x−2x-2x−2 and x+2x+2x+2 are nonzero, so the common factor x+2x+2x+2 may be divided out:

3x(x+2)(x−2)(x+2)=3xx−2(x≠2 and x≠−2).\frac{3x(x+2)}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \frac{3x}{x-2} \qquad (x \neq 2 \text{ and } x \neq -2).(x−2)(x+2)3x(x+2)​=x−23x​(x=2 and x=−2).

Watch what just happened to the two restrictions. The restriction x≠2x \neq 2x=2 is still plain to see in the answer, because x−2x-2x−2 is still in the denominator. The restriction x≠−2x \neq -2x=−2 has vanished from sight: 3xx−2\frac{3x}{x-2}x−23x​ is perfectly well defined at x=−2x=-2x=−2, where it equals −6−4=32\frac{-6}{-4} = \frac{3}{2}−4−6​=23​. The original is not defined there. That is why the restriction is written beside the answer, and it is why step 2 comes before step 3.

Worked example 2 Simplify 9−x2x2−2x−3\dfrac{9-x^2}{x^2-2x-3}x2−2x−39−x2​

Factor both parts. The numerator is a difference of squares, and the denominator is a trinomial whose factors must multiply to −3-3−3 and add to −2-2−2:

9−x2x2−2x−3=(3−x)(3+x)(x−3)(x+1).\frac{9-x^2}{x^2-2x-3} = \frac{(3-x)(3+x)}{(x-3)(x+1)}.x2−2x−39−x2​=(x−3)(x+1)(3−x)(3+x)​.

The denominator is zero at x=3x=3x=3 and x=−1x=-1x=−1, so those two values are excluded.

The top and bottom look as though they share nothing, but 3−x3-x3−x and x−3x-3x−3 are opposites, and one is −1-1−1 times the other:

3−x=−(x−3).3-x = -(x-3).3−x=−(x−3).

Rewrite the numerator using that, and the common factor appears:

−(x−3)(x+3)(x−3)(x+1)=−(x+3)x+1(x≠3 and x≠−1).\frac{-(x-3)(x+3)}{(x-3)(x+1)} = \frac{-(x+3)}{x+1} \qquad (x \neq 3 \text{ and } x \neq -1).(x−3)(x+1)−(x−3)(x+3)​=x+1−(x+3)​(x=3 and x=−1).

A quick check at x=0x=0x=0, which is in the domain: the original gives 9−00−0−3=9−3=−3\frac{9-0}{0-0-3} = \frac{9}{-3} = -30−0−39−0​=−39​=−3, and the simplified form gives −(0+3)0+1=−3\frac{-(0+3)}{0+1} = -30+1−(0+3)​=−3. They agree, as they must.

Worked example 3 Simplify x3−8x2−4\dfrac{x^3-8}{x^2-4}x2−4x3−8​

The denominator factors on sight as (x−2)(x+2)(x-2)(x+2)(x−2)(x+2), so the excluded values are x=2x=2x=2 and x=−2x=-2x=−2.

The numerator is a difference of cubes, x3−8=x3−23x^3-8=x^3-2^3x3−8=x3−23, and the difference-of-cubes identity factors it directly:

x3−8=(x−2)(x2+2x+4).x^3-8 = (x-2)(x^2+2x+4).x3−8=(x−2)(x2+2x+4).

Now assemble and cancel the common factor x−2x-2x−2, which is nonzero everywhere on the domain:

x3−8x2−4=(x−2)(x2+2x+4)(x−2)(x+2)=x2+2x+4x+2(x≠2 and x≠−2).\frac{x^3-8}{x^2-4} = \frac{(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \frac{x^2+2x+4}{x+2} \qquad (x \neq 2 \text{ and } x \neq -2).x2−4x3−8​=(x−2)(x+2)(x−2)(x2+2x+4)​=x+2x2+2x+4​(x=2 and x=−2).

Is that in lowest terms? Check whether x+2x+2x+2 could still be a factor of x2+2x+4x^2+2x+4x2+2x+4: if it were, then x=−2x=-2x=−2 would make x2+2x+4x^2+2x+4x2+2x+4 equal 000. Worked example 4 Simplify x2−9x2+9\dfrac{x^2-9}{x^2+9}x2+9x2−9​ and state its domain

Start, as always, with the denominator. The equation x2+9=0x^2+9=0x2+9=0 would need x2=−9x^2=-9x2=−9, and no real number has a negative square, so the denominator is never zero. This rational expression has no excluded values at all: its domain is every real number.

Factoring gives

x2−9x2+9=(x−3)(x+3)x2+9,\frac{x^2-9}{x^2+9} = \frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x^2+9},x2+9x2−9​=x2+9(x−3)(x+3)​,

and x2+9x^2+9x2+9 has no real zeros, so it cannot have a factor of the form x−3x-3x−3 or x+3x+3x+3: if it did, plugging in x=3x=3x=3 or x=−3x=-3x=−3 would have to give 000, and it does not (both give 181818). There is no common non-constant factor, and the expression is already in lowest terms.

The tempting moves here are all illegal. The 999s are terms, not factors, so they do not cancel, and neither do the x2x^2x2s. One input kills both ideas at once. At x=1x=1x=1 the expression is

1−91+9=−810=−45,\frac{1-9}{1+9} = \frac{-8}{10} = -\frac{4}{5},1+91−9​=10−8​=−54​,

which is neither −1-1−1 (what cancelling the x2x^2x2s would predict, leaving −99\frac{-9}{9}9−9​) nor 111 (what cancelling the 999s would predict, leaving x2x2\frac{x^2}{x^2}x2x2​). The instruction “simplify” never promises that something will cancel; sometimes the honest answer is that the expression is already in lowest terms.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Cancelling terms instead of factors, or cancelling before factoring. x+2x\frac{x+2}{x}xx+2​ is not 222; at x=3x=3x=3 it is 53\frac{5}{3}35​. Nothing can be divided out until the numerator and denominator are written as products: x2−1x−1\frac{x^2-1}{x-1}x−1x2−1​ shows no common factor as it stands, but (x−1)(x+1)x−1\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1}x−1(x−1)(x+1)​ does. Same trap in x2+9x+3\frac{x^2+9}{x+3}x+3x2+9​: the 999 and the 333 are not factors, and x+3x+3x+3 is not a factor of x2+9x^2+9x2+9, since (−3)2+9=18≠0(-3)^2+9 = 18 \neq 0(−3)2+9=18=0.
  • Reading the domain off the simplified form. 3x2+6xx2−4\frac{3x^2+6x}{x^2-4}x2−43x2+6x​ becomes 3xx−2\frac{3x}{x-2}x−23x​, and the restriction x≠−2x \neq -2x=−2 is nowhere to be seen in that answer. It still binds. Excluded values come from the original denominator, always.
  • Writing an unconditional equality. "x2−1x−1=x+1\frac{x^2-1}{x-1} = x+1x−1x2−1​=x+1" is false as stated, because the two sides disagree at x=1x=1x=1: one is undefined and the other is 222. Write ”x2−1x−1=x+1\frac{x^2-1}{x-1} = x+1x−1x2−1​=x+1 for x≠1x \neq 1x=1” and the statement becomes true.
  • Losing the sign on opposite factors. 3−x3-x3−x and x−3x-3x−3 are not the same factor; 3−x=−(x−3)3-x = -(x-3)3−x=−(x−3). So 3−xx−3=−1\frac{3-x}{x-3} = -1x−33−x​=−1 for x≠3x \neq 3x=3, not 111.

Key takeaways

  • A rational expression is p(x)q(x)\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}q(x)p(x)​ with ppp and qqq polynomials and qqq not the zero polynomial. Every polynomial is one, with denominator 111.
  • Its domain is every real number except the zeros of the denominator. Find those zeros from the original denominator, before any cancelling.
  • Cancellation law: acbc=ab\frac{ac}{bc} = \frac{a}{b}bcac​=ba​ whenever b≠0b \neq 0b=0 and c≠0c \neq 0c=0. It removes common factors, never common terms, which is why every simplification starts by factoring.
  • Cancelling preserves the value wherever both sides are defined, but it does not preserve the domain. The honest way to record a simplification is with its restriction attached, as in x2−1x−1=x+1\frac{x^2-1}{x-1} = x+1x−1x2−1​=x+1 for x≠1x \neq 1x=1.
  • Opposite factors differ by a sign: 3−x=−(x−3)3-x = -(x-3)3−x=−(x−3), so a factor of −1-1−1 comes out rather than nothing.
  • An expression is in lowest terms when any numerical coefficients are reduced and the numerator and denominator share no common non-constant factor. Some expressions, like x2−9x2+9\frac{x^2-9}{x^2+9}x2+9x2−9​, are there already, and some have no excluded values at all.

Why acbc=ab\dfrac{ac}{bc}=\dfrac{a}{b}bcac​=ba​: the full proof from the definition of a quotient

Cancelling a common factor: acbc=ab\dfrac{ac}{bc} = \dfrac{a}{b}bcac​=ba​ whenever b≠0b \neq 0b=0 and c≠0c \neq 0c=0#

Start from what a quotient means. For real numbers uuu and vvv with v≠0v \neq 0v=0, the quotient uv\frac{u}{v}vu​ is the unique number ttt satisfying tv=utv = utv=u. A bit of history (optional)

The word rational has nothing to do with being reasonable. That is an accident of language, and it hides a better story.

Greek geometers had no fractions in our sense. They compared two lengths instead. They asked how the first stood to the second, and their word for that comparison was logos. The same word meant a word, an account, a reckoning. The Greek theory of proportion was written down around 300 BCE, and it treated such a comparison as its basic object. Latin translators later rendered logos as ratio. That word already meant an account or a calculation. English then borrowed the one root twice, once for the arithmetic and once for clear thinking. A single word carries both meanings to this day.

So a rational number is not a sensible number. It is a number you can report as a ratio of two integers, with a nonzero denominator. Change the raw material from integers to polynomials, and the name follows without complaint. A rational expression is a ratio of two polynomials.

That is more than a naming accident. It is the reason 68\frac{6}{8}86​ and x2−1x−1\frac{x^2-1}{x-1}x−1x2−1​ reduce by exactly the same move: cancelling is a statement about ratios, not about the particular numbers or polynomials filling them in.
