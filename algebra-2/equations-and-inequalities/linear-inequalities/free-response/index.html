  1. Problem 1 A divided comparison

    Solve 5−2x3≤x+1\frac{5-2x}{3}\le x+135−2x​≤x+1 for real xxx.

    1. Hint 1

      Clear the positive denominator without reversing the direction.

    2. Hint 2

      Collect the variable terms, noting the sign of the final coefficient.

    Answer

    x≥25x\ge\frac25x≥52​.

    Full solution

    Multiply by the positive number 333.

    5−2x≤3x+32≤5x25≤x\begin{aligned}5-2x&\le3x+3\\2&\le5x\\\frac25&\le x\end{aligned}5−2x252​​≤3x+3≤5x≤x​

    The endpoint gives equality in the original; x=1x=1x=1 satisfies it and x=0x=0x=0 does not.

    Answer

    x≥25x\ge\frac25x≥52​.

    Key idea

    Positive scaling keeps an inequality pointing in the same direction.

  2. Problem 2 A bracketed condition

    Solve 7−2(3x+1)>4(x−2)7-2(3x+1)>4(x-2)7−2(3x+1)>4(x−2) for real xxx.

    1. Hint 1

      First rewrite each side as an equal expression.

    2. Hint 2

      Collecting the variable on the left gives a negative coefficient, which matters when dividing.

    Answer

    x<1310x<\frac{13}{10}x<1013​.

    Full solution

    Expand and collect.

    5−6x>4x−8−10x>−13x<1310\begin{aligned}5-6x&>4x-8\\-10x&>-13\\x&<\frac{13}{10}\end{aligned}5−6x−10xx​>4x−8>−13<1013​​

    Dividing by −10-10−10 reverses the direction.

    At the endpoint the sides are equal, so it is excluded; x=0x=0x=0 gives 5>−85>-85>−8.

    Answer

    x<1310x<\frac{13}{10}x<1013​.

    Key idea

    The sign of the actual divisor determines whether the inequality reverses.

  3. Problem 3 Two boundary rules

    Find all real xxx satisfying 2x+1≤92x+1\le92x+1≤9 and 5−x<75-x<75−x<7.

    1. Hint 1

      Both conditions must hold at once.

    2. Hint 2

      Solve each inequality separately, then retain their overlap.

    Answer

    −2<x≤4-2<x\le4−2<x≤4.

    Full solution

    The first condition gives

    2x≤8x≤4\begin{aligned}2x&\le8\\x&\le4\end{aligned}2xx​≤8≤4​

    The second gives −x<2-x<2−x<2, hence x>−2x>-2x>−2 after division by −1-1−1.

    Their intersection is −2<x≤4-2<x\le4−2<x≤4.

    The left endpoint fails the strict second condition, while the right endpoint satisfies both.

    Answer

    −2<x≤4-2<x\le4−2<x≤4.

    Key idea

    An and condition keeps the intersection of the two solution sets.

  4. Problem 4 A delivery allowance

    A courier has a budget of 42 dollars for a trip. A fixed fee is 6 dollars and the distance fee is 1.5 dollars per kilometer. A coupon removes 3 dollars from the total. Find the allowed real distances d≥0d\ge0d≥0 in kilometers.

    1. Hint 1

      The discounted total must stay at or below the budget.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the total as fixed fee plus distance fee minus coupon.

    Answer

    0≤d≤260\le d\le260≤d≤26 kilometers.

    Full solution

    The budget condition is

    6+1.5d−3≤421.5d≤39d≤26\begin{aligned}6+1.5d-3&\le42\\1.5d&\le39\\d&\le26\end{aligned}6+1.5d−31.5dd​≤42≤39≤26​

    Combine this with d≥0d\ge0d≥0.

    At 26 kilometers the total is exactly 42 dollars.

    Answer

    0≤d≤260\le d\le260≤d≤26 kilometers.

    Key idea

    A practical inequality must be combined with the domain allowed by the situation.

  5. Problem 5 Two entry routes

    An entry rule accepts real xxx when either 3(x−1)<3x−43(x-1)<3x-43(x−1)<3x−4 or 8−2x≤08-2x\le08−2x≤0. Find the accepted set.

    1. Hint 1

      An or rule accepts everything accepted by either piece.

    2. Hint 2

      Check whether the first inequality can ever be true after its variable cancels.

    Answer

    x≥4x\ge4x≥4.

    Full solution

    The first inequality reduces to −3<−4-3<-4−3<−4, which is false, so it contributes no values.

    The second gives

    −2x≤−8x≥4\begin{aligned}-2x&\le-8\\x&\ge4\end{aligned}−2xx​≤−8≥4​

    The union of the empty set and [4,∞)[4,\infty)[4,∞) is [4,∞)[4,\infty)[4,∞).

    Answer

    x≥4x\ge4x≥4.

    Key idea

    A false piece contributes nothing to an or condition.

  6. Problem 6 A ratio threshold

    Solve x+4x−2≤2\frac{x+4}{x-2}\le2x−2x+4​≤2 for real xxx, explaining how the sign of a multiplier affects the steps.

    1. Hint 1

      The denominator is forbidden at zero and has different signs on its two sides.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the resulting linear inequality separately for x>2x>2x>2 and x<2x<2x<2.

    Answer

    x<2x<2x<2 or x≥8x\ge8x≥8; x=2x=2x=2 is excluded.

    Full solution

    Exclude x=2x=2x=2.

    When x>2x>2x>2, multiplying by x−2x-2x−2 keeps the direction.

    x+4≤2x−4x≥8\begin{aligned}x+4&\le2x-4\\x&\ge8\end{aligned}x+4x​≤2x−4≥8​

    When x<2x<2x<2, multiplication reverses it: x+4≥2x−4x+4\ge2x-4x+4≥2x−4, so x≤8x\le8x≤8.

    Every x<2x<2x<2 meets this condition.

    The union is x<2x<2x<2 or x≥8x\ge8x≥8.

    Checking x=0,4,8x=0,4,8x=0,4,8 gives respectively true, false, and equality.

    Answer

    x<2x<2x<2 or x≥8x\ge8x≥8; x=2x=2x=2 is excluded.

    Key idea

    An unknown-sign denominator requires separate sign cases before clearing it in an inequality.

  7. Problem 7 Two control settings

    For real parameters ppp and qqq, solve (p−1)x>q+2(p-1)x>q+2(p−1)x>q+2 for real xxx in all parameter cases.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

    Full solution

    For p>1p>1p>1, divide by the positive coefficient p−1p-1p−1 to obtain

    x>q+2p−1.x>\frac{q+2}{p-1}.x>p−1q+2​.

    For p<1p<1p<1, it is negative, so division gives

    x<q+2p−1.x<\frac{q+2}{p-1}.x<p−1q+2​.

    At p=1p=1p=1, no division is allowed.

    This holds for every xxx if q<−2q<-2q<−2 and for no xxx if q≥−2q\ge-2q≥−2, including the strict boundary q=−2q=-2q=−2.

  8. Problem 8 A comparison of gaps

    For real a<ba<ba<b, a student claims 7−3a>7−3b7-3a>7-3b7−3a>7−3b. Is this correct? Justify the direction by comparing the difference of the two new quantities.

    1. Hint 1

      A larger quantity minus a smaller one has positive difference.

    2. Hint 2

      Compute (7−3a)−(7−3b)(7-3a)-(7-3b)(7−3a)−(7−3b) and relate it to b−ab-ab−a.

    Answer

    Yes; 7−3a>7−3b7-3a>7-3b7−3a>7−3b.

    Full solution

    The original order gives b−a>0b-a>0b−a>0.

    The new difference is

    (7−3a)−(7−3b)=3(b−a)\begin{aligned}(7-3a)-(7-3b)&=3(b-a)\end{aligned}(7−3a)−(7−3b)​=3(b−a)​

    This is positive, so the first new quantity is larger.

    The negative scaling reverses the original order, and the added 777 cancels from the comparison.

    Answer

    Yes; 7−3a>7−3b7-3a>7-3b7−3a>7−3b.

    Key idea

    Comparing the difference proves why negative scaling reverses order.

  9. Problem 9 A proposed shortcut

    A solver multiplies x+1≤4x+1\le4x+1≤4 by 000 and replaces it with 0≤00\le00≤0. Does the replacement preserve the solution set? Explain with a specific input.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the inputs allowed by the original and the replacement.

    2. Hint 2

      Find a number that is too large for the original but is still accepted by the replacement.

    Answer

    No; the original gives x≤3x\le3x≤3, while the replacement accepts every real xxx; for example x=4x=4x=4.

    Full solution

    Subtracting 111 in the original gives

    x≤3.x\le3.x≤3.

    At x=4x=4x=4, the original reads 5≤45\le45≤4, false, while 0≤00\le00≤0 is true.

    Thus multiplication by zero has admitted a value the original rejects.

    Answer

    No; the original gives x≤3x\le3x≤3, while the replacement accepts every real xxx; for example x=4x=4x=4.

    Key idea

    Multiplication by zero can erase the information that distinguishes accepted inputs from rejected ones.

  10. Problem 10 A restricted comparison

    On the domain x>3x>3x>3, a solver replaces x<5x<5x<5 by xx−3<5x−3\frac{x}{x-3}<\frac5{x-3}x−3x​<x−35​. Are the two inequalities equivalent on this domain? Would the same replacement be equivalent on the larger domain x≠3x\ne3x=3? Justify each verdict, using a counterexample if a verdict is no.

    1. Hint 1

      Determine the sign of the divisor on each stated domain.

    2. Hint 2

      For a counterexample, try an input below 333 and compare the truth of the two inequalities.

    Answer

    Yes on x>3x>3x>3; no on x≠3x\ne3x=3, with x=0x=0x=0 as a counterexample.

    Full solution

    On x>3x>3x>3, the divisor x−3x-3x−3 is positive.

    Division and its inverse, multiplication, preserve the direction, so both inequalities have the same solution set there:

    3<x<5.3<x<5.3<x<5.

    On the larger domain the divisor can be negative.

    At x=0x=0x=0, the original statement 0<50<50<5 is true, but the replacement becomes

    0<−53,0<-\frac53,0<−35​,

    which is false.

    Therefore the replacement is not equivalent on that domain.

    Answer

    Yes on x>3x>3x>3; no on x≠3x\ne3x=3, with x=0x=0x=0 as a counterexample.

    Key idea

    A domain restriction can establish the sign needed for a reversible inequality step.
