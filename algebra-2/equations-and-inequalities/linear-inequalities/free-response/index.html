    1. Three moves on one true statement, and the one that needs a proof . Foundational, 10 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 10 pts

    An inequality accepts some steps without any argument at all, and reverses direction under exactly one family of steps. This question runs three moves on the same true statement, then proves, for arbitrary numbers rather than just this example, why only one of the three families ever needs that reversal.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Starting from the true statement −9<4-9 < 4−9<4, apply each step below, and for each write the resulting statement using whichever symbol, <<< or >>>, makes it true: (i) add 111111 to both sides; (ii) multiply both sides by 666; (iii) multiply both sides by −2-2−2.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      All three steps in part A are reversible: adding 111111 is undone by subtracting 111111, multiplying by 666 by dividing by 666, and multiplying by −2-2−2 by dividing by −2-2−2. Yet only one of them reversed the direction. Prove, for arbitrary real numbers p<qp < qp<q and an arbitrary negative number kkk, that kp>kqkp > kqkp>kq must hold, arguing from the fact that q−pq - pq−p is a positive number. Then state, in one sentence, why reversibility itself cannot be the property that decides a flip.

      Complete the derivation Each line should follow from the one above it. Say what lets you take each step. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Multiplying both sides of −9<4-9 < 4−9<4 by 000 produces 0<00 < 00<0. Explain why this outcome is not merely 'an extreme case of reversal' but a different kind of failure, and name the one property every step in part A has that this one lacks.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 4

      Two different questions are hiding in these three steps: does the direction survive, and could the step be undone afterward? They do not always have the same answer.

    2. Hint 2 of 4 · Part A

      Compute the two new numbers for each step first, then check whether the naive symbol still makes a true statement or needs to be turned around.

    3. Hint 3 of 4 · Part B

      Write the negative multiplier as k=−jk = -jk=−j for a positive jjj, and compare kpkpkp and kqkqkq through their difference, the same way the lesson compared −a-a−a and −b-b−b.

    4. Hint 4 of 4 · Part C

      Ask what step, applied to 0<00 < 00<0, could possibly recover −9-9−9 and 444. Every step in part A had such a partner.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    (i) 2<152 < 152<15; (ii) −54<24-54 < 24−54<24; (iii) 18>−818 > -818>−8.

    Part B

    True for every real p<qp < qp<q and negative kkk: kp>kqkp > kqkp>kq, because kq−kp=k(q−p)kq - kp = k(q - p)kq−kp=k(q−p) is the product of a negative number and a positive number, hence negative. Reversibility is not the deciding property, since every step in part A is equally reversible and only one of them flips.

    Part C

    0<00 < 00<0 is simply false and records nothing about −9-9−9 or 444, unlike a reversal, which still reports something true about the original numbers. The missing property is invertibility: dividing by 000 is undefined, so no step could undo multiplying by 000 and recover the original inequality.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Apply each step to both sides of −9<4-9 < 4−9<4 separately, then decide the symbol from the truth of the result.

    Adding 111111: −9+11=2-9 + 11 = 2−9+11=2 and 4+11=154 + 11 = 154+11=15.

    2<152 < 152<15

    Multiplying by 666, a positive number: −9(6)=−54-9(6) = -54−9(6)=−54 and 4(6)=244(6) = 244(6)=24.

    −54<24-54 < 24−54<24

    Multiplying by −2-2−2, a negative number: −9(−2)=18-9(-2) = 18−9(−2)=18 and 4(−2)=−84(-2) = -84(−2)=−8. Keeping the symbol as <<< would claim 18<−818 < -818<−8, which is false, so the true statement reverses it.

    18>−818 > -818>−8

    Part B

    Start from what p<qp < qp<q means: the difference q−pq - pq−p is a positive number. The claim is about the products kpkpkp and kqkqkq, so compare them through their own difference, the way the lesson compared −a-a−a and −b-b−b.

    kq−kp=k(q−p)kq - kp = k(q - p)kq−kp=k(q−p)

    Because kkk is negative and q−pq - pq−p is positive, their product is negative.

    k(q−p)<0  ⟹  kq−kp<0  ⟹  kq<kpk(q - p) < 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; kq - kp < 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; kq < kpk(q−p)<0⟹kq−kp<0⟹kq<kp

    The statement kq<kpkq < kpkq<kp is exactly kp>kqkp > kqkp>kq, so the reversal holds for every choice of p<qp < qp<q and every negative kkk, not merely for the specific numbers in part A.

    Reversibility cannot be the deciding property, because every one of the three steps in part A can be undone by an opposite step of the same family, so all three are equally reversible, and still only the negative multiplication flipped the direction. What actually decides a flip is the sign of the number multiplying both sides, not whether the step has an inverse.

    Part C

    A reversal, like the one in part A, is still an accurate report about the original two numbers: 18>−818 > -818>−8 genuinely reflects what happened to −9-9−9 and 444 under multiplication by −2-2−2, and dividing by −2-2−2 recovers them exactly. Multiplying by 000 is not that.

    −9<4  →  ×0    0<0-9 < 4 \;\xrightarrow{\;\times 0\;}\; 0 < 0−9<4×0​0<0

    The result 0<00 < 00<0 is false outright and says nothing at all about −9-9−9 or 444 specifically: any pair of numbers multiplied by 000 produces that identical statement (or 0≤00 \le 00≤0, for a non-strict symbol), so no trace of the starting numbers survives.

    The property every step in part A shares is that each one is invertible: addition of 111111 is undone by subtracting 111111, multiplication by 666 by dividing by 666, and multiplication by −2-2−2 by dividing by −2-2−2. Multiplying by 000 has no such partner, because dividing by 000 is not defined, so there is no step that could take 0<00 < 00<0 back to −9<4-9 < 4−9<4. That is the real difference: a reversal changes the symbol but preserves recoverability, while multiplying by 000 destroys recoverability altogether.

    In one line

    Adding 111111 gives 2<152 < 152<15, multiplying by 666 gives −54<24-54 < 24−54<24, and multiplying by −2-2−2 gives 18>−818 > -818>−8: only the last needed the symbol reversed. In general, for any p<qp < qp<q and any negative kkk, kq−kp=k(q−p)kq - kp = k(q - p)kq−kp=k(q−p) is negative (a negative times a positive), so kp>kqkp > kqkp>kq for every such choice, not just this example; reversibility is not the deciding property, since all three moves are reversible and only one flips. Multiplying by 000 is a different failure altogether: 0<00 < 00<0 is simply false and cannot be undone, since no step recovers the original numbers once they are multiplied by 000.

    Another way: Picture each step as a move on the number line

    Adding 111111 slides both −9-9−9 and 444 the same distance in the same direction. Multiplying by 666 stretches both distances from zero without swapping which one is further right. Multiplying by −2-2−2 does something neither of those does: it reflects the whole line through zero, so whichever number sat on the left is thrown to the right, and that swap is exactly what reverses the symbol.

    When it is worth it As a quick check on whether a step should reverse a direction, before doing any arithmetic: ask whether the step slides, stretches, or reflects the line.
