    1. Aiming one equation at two different letters . Foundational, 12 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 12 pts

    A delivery drone flies a fixed distance ddd at a constant rate rrr for a time ttt, related by d=rtd = rtd=rt. Which letter counts as the unknown and which count as parameters is not written into the equation itself; it is decided by what a particular question asks for.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Solve d=rtd = rtd=rt for the rate rrr, treating ddd and ttt as the parameters, and state the restriction that your final step requires. Then find the rate of a drone that covers d=126d = 126d=126 kilometers in t=3t = 3t=3 hours.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Solve the same equation d=rtd = rtd=rt for the time ttt instead, now treating ddd and rrr as the parameters, and state the restriction that this step requires. Then find how long a different drone flying at a rate of r=25r = 25r=25 kilometers per hour takes to cover a distance of d=200d = 200d=200 kilometers.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Both rearrangements came from the identical equation d=rtd = rtd=rt. Explain what changed between part A and part B to turn one equation into two different formulas, and say why the two restrictions, t≠0t \neq 0t=0 in part A and r≠0r \neq 0r=0 in part B, are not the same condition even though both were produced by solving the same equation.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Both parts undo the same single multiplication. The only question in each is which of the three letters you have singled out as the one to isolate.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      You have already isolated r in part A by dividing by t. Isolating t instead means dividing by r.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask, in each rearrangement, which letter was doing the multiplying that a division had to undo.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    r=dtr = \dfrac{d}{t}r=td​, valid whenever t≠0t \neq 0t=0; at d=126d = 126d=126, t=3t = 3t=3 the rate is 424242 kilometers per hour.

    Part B

    t=drt = \dfrac{d}{r}t=rd​, valid whenever r≠0r \neq 0r=0; at d=200d = 200d=200, r=25r = 25r=25 the time is 888 hours.

    Part C

    The unknown changed: r in part A, t in part B, with the other two letters held fixed each time. A rearrangement's divisor is always the parameter multiplying the target, so a different letter played that role in each part, which is why the two conditions are different statements.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Here rrr is the unknown, and ddd and ttt are held as fixed but unstated numbers. The equation reads d=t⋅rd = t \cdot rd=t⋅r: one known quantity multiplying the target, so a single division isolates it.

    r=dt.r = \frac{d}{t}.r=td​.

    The divisor ttt is a letter, so the rearrangement is not unconditional: it holds provided t≠0t \neq 0t=0, which is also the natural requirement that the trip actually took some nonzero amount of time.

    Substituting the given values,

    r=1263=42,r = \frac{126}{3} = 42,r=3126​=42,

    so the drone's rate is 424242 kilometers per hour.

    Part B

    This time ttt is the letter singled out, and ddd and rrr are the ones held fixed. Read the same equation as d=r⋅td = r \cdot td=r⋅t, a known quantity multiplying the new target:

    t=dr.t = \frac{d}{r}.t=rd​.

    The divisor here is rrr, a different letter from part A's divisor, so the restriction is r≠0r \neq 0r=0, not t≠0t \neq 0t=0.

    At d=200d = 200d=200 and r=25r = 25r=25,

    t=20025=8,t = \frac{200}{25} = 8,t=25200​=8,

    so the trip takes 888 hours.

    Part C

    In part A the roles were: unknown rrr, parameters ddd and ttt. In part B the roles were: unknown ttt, parameters ddd and rrr. The equation d=rtd = rtd=rt never changed; only the assignment of roles did.

    In both cases the rule for isolating the target is the same: divide by whatever else is multiplying it. In part A that other factor was ttt, so ttt had to be shown nonzero. In part B the target was ttt itself, and the other factor doing the multiplying was rrr, so rrr had to be shown nonzero instead:

    t≠0 (part A)versusr≠0 (part B).t \neq 0 \ \text{(part A)} \qquad\text{versus}\qquad r \neq 0 \ \text{(part B)}.t=0 (part A)versusr=0 (part B).

    So the two conditions are not two versions of one fact. They are the same rule, that the divisor must be nonzero, applied to two different quantities, because the divisor in a rearrangement is always the OTHER factor multiplying the target, and a different letter held that role in each part.

    In one line

    Solving for r gives r = d/t (t not equal to zero), a rate of 42 kilometers per hour for the first drone; solving for t instead gives t = d/r (r not equal to zero), a time of 8 hours for the second. The two restrictions differ because the divisor in a rearrangement is always the parameter multiplying the target, and a different letter was chosen as the unknown in each part.

    2. How many solutions does a family of equations have? . Foundational, 12 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Foundational 12 pts

    Consider a family of equations in the parameter mmm. Reducing one to the form (coefficient of xxx) times xxx equals (constant) is the first step toward saying, for every value of mmm at once, how many solutions the equation has.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Reduce mx−5=3x+2mmx - 5 = 3x + 2mmx−5=3x+2m to the form (coefficient) x=\,x = x= (constant), where mmm is the parameter. Then say, for every value of mmm, whether the equation has exactly one solution, no solution, or infinitely many, giving the one-solution formula in mmm wherever it applies.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Do the same for mx+15=3x+5mmx + 15 = 3x + 5mmx+15=3x+5m: reduce it to the same form, then classify the solution set for every value of mmm.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      The two families share the identical coefficient, m−3m - 3m−3, and the identical boundary value, m=3m = 3m=3, yet one never has infinitely many solutions and the other never has none. Explain what, beyond the coefficient, decides which outcome is reachable at the boundary, and say what you would check, without carrying out the full classification, to predict it.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Reduce each equation to the form (coefficient) x = (constant) in m first, exactly as in the lesson's worked examples, before asking any question about how many solutions there are.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      The coefficient of x will turn out to be the same expression as in part A. Compare what happens to the constant term at the value that zeros it.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      The coefficient is identical in both families, so it cannot be the thing that tells them apart. Look at what each family's constant term equals at the one value where the coefficient is zero.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    (m−3)x=2m+5(m-3)x = 2m+5(m−3)x=2m+5. For every m≠3m \neq 3m=3 there is exactly one solution, x=2m+5m−3x = \dfrac{2m+5}{m-3}x=m−32m+5​; at m=3m = 3m=3 the equation becomes 0⋅x=110 \cdot x = 110⋅x=11, so this family never has infinitely many solutions.

    Part B

    (m−3)x=5(m−3)(m-3)x = 5(m-3)(m−3)x=5(m−3). For m≠3m \neq 3m=3: x=5x = 5x=5. At m=3m = 3m=3: 0⋅x=00\cdot x = 00⋅x=0, so the solution set is R\mathbb{R}R; this family never has no solution.

    Part C

    The coefficient only locates the boundary; the constant's value there decides the fork. Check whether the constant vanishes at the coefficient's zero: nonzero rules out infinitely many for that family, zero rules out no solution.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Collect the xxx terms on the left and the constants on the right:

    mx−3x=2m+5,(m−3)x=2m+5.mx - 3x = 2m + 5, \qquad (m-3)x = 2m+5.mx−3x=2m+5,(m−3)x=2m+5.

    The coefficient is m−3m-3m−3, zero exactly at m=3m=3m=3. For m≠3m \neq 3m=3, dividing gives

    x=2m+5m−3,x = \frac{2m+5}{m-3},x=m−32m+5​,

    exactly one solution. At m=3m = 3m=3 the coefficient vanishes and the constant becomes 2(3)+5=112(3)+5 = 112(3)+5=11, so the equation reads 0⋅x=110 \cdot x = 110⋅x=11. No value of xxx satisfies that, so there is no solution at m=3m=3m=3, and since the boundary case already fails to be an identity, no value of mmm can ever produce infinitely many solutions here.

    Part B

    Collect terms exactly as before:

    mx−3x=5m−15,(m−3)x=5(m−3).mx - 3x = 5m - 15, \qquad (m-3)x = 5(m-3).mx−3x=5m−15,(m−3)x=5(m−3).

    The coefficient is the same m−3m-3m−3 as in part A. For m≠3m \neq 3m=3, the factor m−3m-3m−3 cancels:

    x=5(m−3)m−3=5,x = \frac{5(m-3)}{m-3} = 5,x=m−35(m−3)​=5,

    the identical value 555 no matter which m≠3m \neq 3m=3 is chosen. At m=3m=3m=3 the constant is 5(3−3)=05(3-3) = 05(3−3)=0 as well, so the equation reads 0⋅x=00 \cdot x = 00⋅x=0, an identity satisfied by every real number. Since the boundary already produces an identity, no value of mmm can ever make this family have no solution.

    Part C

    Both families reduce to a coefficient of m−3m - 3m−3, so both share the same boundary value m=3m = 3m=3 and the same coefficient away from it, even though their one-solution formulas there, 2m+5m−3\dfrac{2m+5}{m-3}m−32m+5​ and 555, are different. The coefficient alone cannot be what separates the two families' behavior AT the boundary, since it is identical in both.

    What differs is the constant term at that one value:

    2(3)+5=11≠0versus5(3−3)=0.2(3) + 5 = 11 \neq 0 \qquad\text{versus}\qquad 5(3-3) = 0.2(3)+5=11=0versus5(3−3)=0.

    In the first family the constant is 111111 at the boundary, nonzero, so the boundary equation 0⋅x=110 \cdot x = 110⋅x=11 is a contradiction and no solution results there; the family can never reach infinitely many, because that would need the constant to vanish at the boundary too, and it never will, however the rest of the family is built. In the second family the constant vanishes at the boundary too, so the boundary equation is 0⋅x=00 \cdot x = 00⋅x=0, an identity; the family can never reach no solution, for the mirror reason.

    So the check that predicts the outcome without a full classification is short: evaluate the constant at the parameter value that zeros the coefficient. A nonzero result rules out infinitely many everywhere in the family; a zero result rules out no solution everywhere in the family.

    In one line

    For mx - 5 = 3x + 2m: one solution x = (2m+5)/(m-3) whenever m is not 3, and no solution at m = 3; infinitely many never occurs. For mx + 15 = 3x + 5m: one solution x = 5 whenever m is not 3, and every real number at m = 3; no solution never occurs. The constant term's value at the coefficient's zero, not the coefficient itself, decides which of the two outcomes a family can ever reach.

    3. Recovering one pump's rate from the combined job . Application, 12 points. Question 3 of 5.

    Application 12 pts

    Two pumps work on filling the same tank. Pump A alone would take aaa hours, pump B alone would take bbb hours, and working together they take ttt hours. Because rates of filling add, 1t=1a+1b\dfrac{1}{t} = \dfrac{1}{a} + \dfrac{1}{b}t1​=a1​+b1​.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Solve the combined-rate equation 1t=1a+1b\dfrac{1}{t} = \dfrac{1}{a} + \dfrac{1}{b}t1​=a1​+b1​ for bbb, the time pump B alone would take, and state the restriction that your final step requires.

    2. B

      Carry your own answer forward Substitute into whichever rearrangement you produced in part A. The credit here is for using your own formula correctly and reporting a time with its unit, not for having arrived at one particular formula.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      There is one value of aaa at which your rearranged formula from part A cannot be evaluated: the case a=ta = ta=t. Say what that would mean about pump A's rate compared with the two pumps' combined rate, and explain why no finite value of bbb could keep the records consistent there.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Treat 1/a, 1/b, and 1/t as the rates at which each option fills the tank in one hour; rates from independent workers add, and that is the whole content of the equation.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      Isolate the term 1/b first, combine the other side over one denominator, and only then take the reciprocal of both sides.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask what value of pump B's own rate, 1/b, the equation forces at a = t, and what a rate of exactly zero would mean for how long pump B alone would take.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    b=ata−tb = \dfrac{at}{a - t}b=a−tat​, valid whenever a≠ta \neq ta=t.

    Part B

    Pump B alone takes 666 hours.

    Part C

    At a=ta=ta=t, pump A alone matches the combined time, so pump B contributes nothing to the rate. No finite time for B can cause that, since two genuinely working pumps always finish at least as fast as pump A alone, strictly faster once B contributes any positive rate.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Isolate the term holding the target by subtracting 1a\dfrac{1}{a}a1​ from both sides:

    1b=1t−1a.\frac{1}{b} = \frac{1}{t} - \frac{1}{a}.b1​=t1​−a1​.

    Combine the right side over the common denominator atatat:

    1b=a−tat.\frac{1}{b} = \frac{a - t}{at}.b1​=ata−t​.

    Both sides are reciprocals of nonzero quantities exactly when a≠ta \neq ta=t, so take the reciprocal of each side to reach

    b=ata−t.b = \frac{at}{a - t}.b=a−tat​.

    The divisor a−ta - ta−t is built from two letters, so the rearrangement carries a restriction the original combined-rate equation did not visibly show: it holds only when a≠ta \neq ta=t.

    Part B

    Substitute a=12a = 12a=12 and t=4t = 4t=4 into the rearranged formula, evaluating the numerator and the denominator separately:

    b=(12)(4)12−4=488=6.b = \frac{(12)(4)}{12 - 4} = \frac{48}{8} = 6.b=12−4(12)(4)​=848​=6.

    Pump B alone would take 666 hours.

    Confirm it in the original equation:

    112+16=112+212=312=14,\frac{1}{12} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{12} + \frac{2}{12} = \frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4},121​+61​=121​+122​=123​=41​,

    which is 1t\dfrac{1}{t}t1​ for t=4t = 4t=4, the combined time given.

    Part C

    Read the equation as a statement about rates rather than times: 1t\dfrac{1}{t}t1​ is the combined rate, and 1a\dfrac{1}{a}a1​ is pump A's own rate. Rearranged, pump B's rate is

    1b=1t−1a.\frac{1}{b} = \frac{1}{t} - \frac{1}{a}.b1​=t1​−a1​.

    At a=ta = ta=t this reads

    1b=1t−1t=0.\frac{1}{b} = \frac{1}{t} - \frac{1}{t} = 0.b1​=t1​−t1​=0.

    No finite bbb satisfies 1b=0\dfrac{1}{b} = 0b1​=0, since a finite time always has a positive rate; the equation is only satisfied in the limit of an infinitely long time, which is another way of saying pump B does no real work at all.

    That is also why the situation cannot occur for an actual second pump. Adding a pump that contributes any positive rate can only shorten the combined time below pump A's own time alone, never leave it exactly equal. So a=ta = ta=t describes a pump B that is not really pumping, and the algebra's refusal to name a finite bbb there is reporting that fact honestly rather than failing to find one.

    In one line

    b = at/(a - t), valid when a is not equal to t; with a = 12 and t = 4, pump B alone takes 6 hours, confirmed by 1/12 + 1/6 = 1/4. At a = t the equation forces pump B's own rate to be zero, meaning it contributes nothing to the job, a situation no genuinely working second pump can produce, since two positive rates always combine to finish at least as fast as either alone.

    Another way: Clear every fraction before isolating

    4. Where the restriction on a break-even usage really lives . Reasoning, 11 points. Question 4 of 5.
