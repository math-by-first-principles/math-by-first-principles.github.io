  1. Problem 1 A root expression

    For real t<1t<1t<1, simplify (2t−2)2+t\sqrt{(2t-2)^2}+t(2t−2)2​+t to an expression without a root or absolute-value bars.

    1. Hint 1

      The square root of a square gives the magnitude of the original quantity.

    2. Hint 2

      Use t<1t<1t<1 to decide the sign of 2t−22t-22t−2 before removing the bars.

    Answer

    2−t2-t2−t.

    Full solution

    Since 2t−2<02t-2<02t−2<0, its magnitude is its opposite.

    (2t−2)2+t=−(2t−2)+t=2−t\begin{aligned}\sqrt{(2t-2)^2}+t&=-(2t-2)+t\\&=2-t\end{aligned}(2t−2)2​+t​=−(2t−2)+t=2−t​

    At t=0t=0t=0, both the original and simplified expressions equal 222, consistent with the sign choice.

    Answer

    2−t2-t2−t.

    Key idea

    Removing the root from a square requires the sign of the quantity being squared.

  2. Problem 2 A shifted reading

    Solve 2∣x+1∣−3=72\lvert x+1\rvert-3=72∣x+1∣−3=7 for real xxx.

    1. Hint 1

      Isolate the absolute value before interpreting its distance.

    2. Hint 2

      The inside must be either the positive isolated value or its opposite.

    Answer

    x=−6x=-6x=−6 or x=4x=4x=4.

    Full solution

    Add 333 and divide by 222.

    ∣x+1∣=5\lvert x+1\rvert=5∣x+1∣=5

    Thus x+1=5x+1=5x+1=5 or x+1=−5x+1=-5x+1=−5, giving x=4x=4x=4 or x=−6x=-6x=−6.

    Each makes the original left side 2(5)−3=72(5)-3=72(5)−3=7.

    Answer

    x=−6x=-6x=−6 or x=4x=4x=4.

    Key idea

    Isolating the absolute value makes its two sign cases easier to solve.

    2. Hint 2

    Full solution

    −4≤2x+3≤4.-4\le2x+3\le4.−4≤2x+3≤4.

    Answer

    Key idea

    A nonnegative upper bound on magnitude places the expression inside the bars in a band.

  4. Problem 4 A calibration setting

    A technician chooses a real constant ccc so that the readings ∣2x−1∣\lvert2x-1\rvert∣2x−1∣ and ∣x+c∣\lvert x+c\rvert∣x+c∣ agree at x=2x=2x=2. Find every possible ccc and, for each, all other real settings where the readings agree.

    1. Hint 1

      Substitute the recorded setting to find the possible calibration constants.

    2. Hint 2

      For each constant, equal magnitudes allow the inside expressions to be equal or opposite.

    Answer

    c=1c=1c=1, with other setting x=0x=0x=0; or c=−5c=-5c=−5, with other setting x=−4x=-4x=−4.

    Full solution

    At x=2x=2x=2, agreement requires

    ∣2+c∣=3.\lvert2+c\rvert=3.∣2+c∣=3.

    Thus 2+c=32+c=32+c=3 or 2+c=−32+c=-32+c=−3, giving c=1c=1c=1 or c=−5c=-5c=−5.

    For c=1c=1c=1, the equal-inside case gives 2x−1=x+12x-1=x+12x−1=x+1, hence x=2x=2x=2.

    The opposite case gives

    2x−1=−x−13x=0x=0\begin{aligned}2x-1&=-x-1\\3x&=0\\x&=0\end{aligned}2x−13xx​=−x−1=0=0​

    At x=0x=0x=0, both readings are 111.

    For c=−5c=-5c=−5, the equal-inside case gives 2x−1=x−52x-1=x-52x−1=x−5, hence x=−4x=-4x=−4.

    The opposite case gives

    2x−1=−x+53x=6x=2\begin{aligned}2x-1&=-x+5\\3x&=6\\x&=2\end{aligned}2x−13xx​=−x+5=6=2​

    At x=−4x=-4x=−4, both readings are 999.

    These two sign cases exhaust the possibilities; neither inside expression needs to be nonnegative.

    Answer

    c=1c=1c=1, with other setting x=0x=0x=0; or c=−5c=-5c=−5, with other setting x=−4x=-4x=−4.

    Key idea

    An observed agreement can determine several calibration constants, each with its own set of agreement settings.

  5. Problem 5 A calculation record

    A record for ∣2x−3∣=x+c\lvert2x-3\rvert=x+c∣2x−3∣=x+c lists x=−4x=-4x=−4 from the branch 2x−3=x+c2x-3=x+c2x−3=x+c and x=103x=\frac{10}{3}x=310​ from the branch −(2x−3)=x+c-(2x-3)=x+c−(2x−3)=x+c. Recover the real constant ccc, and decide which recorded candidates solve the original equation.

    1. Hint 1

      The branch equations determine the constant, but satisfying a branch alone does not settle the original equation.

    2. Hint 2

      Substitute a recorded candidate into its branch to find ccc, then check the original right side at both candidates.

    Answer

    c=−7c=-7c=−7; neither candidate solves the original equation, which has no real solution.

    Full solution

    The first record gives

    2(−4)−3=−4+c−11=−4+cc=−7\begin{aligned}2(-4)-3&=-4+c\\-11&=-4+c\\c&=-7\end{aligned}2(−4)−3−11c​=−4+c=−4+c=−7​

    The other record agrees: at x=103x=\frac{10}{3}x=310​, both sides of its branch equal −113-\frac{11}{3}−311​.

    In the original equation the right side is x−7x-7x−7.

    It is −11-11−11 at x=−4x=-4x=−4 and −113-\frac{11}{3}−311​ at

    x=103.x=\frac{10}{3}.x=310​.

    Neither can equal a nonnegative absolute value.

    Both branches have been solved, and neither candidate works, so there is no real solution.

    Answer

    c=−7c=-7c=−7; neither candidate solves the original equation, which has no real solution.

    Key idea

    Candidates from equal-or-opposite equations still need a nonnegative right side in the original absolute-value equation.

  6. Problem 6 A separation rule

    A track marker has real coordinate xxx in meters. It must be at least 5 meters from the marker at −2-2−2, and it must lie between coordinates −10-10−10 and 888, including the endpoints. Find all allowed coordinates.

    1. Hint 1

      A minimum distance leaves two possible sides of the fixed marker.

    2. Hint 2

      First solve ∣x+2∣≥5\lvert x+2\rvert\ge5∣x+2∣≥5, then keep the portion inside the track.

    Answer

    [−10,−7]∪[3,8][-10,-7]\cup[3,8][−10,−7]∪[3,8] meters.

    Full solution

    The distance rule gives x+2≤−5x+2\le-5x+2≤−5 or x+2≥5x+2\ge5x+2≥5, hence x≤−7x\le-7x≤−7 or x≥3x\ge3x≥3.

    Intersect these rays with the track interval.

    x∈[−10,−7]∪[3,8]x\in[-10,-7]\cup[3,8]x∈[−10,−7]∪[3,8]

    The inner endpoints are exactly 5 meters from −2-2−2 and all endpoints are permitted.

    Answer

    [−10,−7]∪[3,8][-10,-7]\cup[3,8][−10,−7]∪[3,8] meters.

    Key idea

    Combine distance rays with any bounds imposed by the physical setting.

  7. Problem 7 An incomplete station record

    One station on a straight path is at coordinate −1-1−1 meter and another is at an unknown real coordinate ccc meters. At each checkpoint 000 and 444, the sum of the distances to the stations is 8 meters. Find ccc, then find every checkpoint coordinate xxx with the same distance total. Justify the latter set by splitting at the station coordinates.

    1. Hint 1

      Use each recorded checkpoint to restrict the unknown station coordinate.

    2. Hint 2

      After finding the station, split the path into the intervals before, between, and after the two stations.

    Answer

    c=7c=7c=7 meters; every xxx with −1≤x≤7-1\le x\le7−1≤x≤7 meters.

    Full solution

    At checkpoint 000, the known distance is 111, so

    ∣c∣=7.\lvert c\rvert=7.∣c∣=7.

    This gives c=−7c=-7c=−7 or c=7c=7c=7.

    At checkpoint 444, the known distance is 555, so ∣4−c∣=3\lvert4-c\rvert=3∣4−c∣=3 gives c=1c=1c=1 or c=7c=7c=7.

    Only c=7c=7c=7 fits both records.

    The required total is

    ∣x+1∣+∣x−7∣=8.\lvert x+1\rvert+\lvert x-7\rvert=8.∣x+1∣+∣x−7∣=8.

    For x<−1x<-1x<−1, its left side is 6−2x6-2x6−2x, which equals 888 only at x=−1x=-1x=−1, outside that open interval.

    For −1≤x≤7-1\le x\le7−1≤x≤7, the sum is (x+1)+(7−x)=8(x+1)+(7-x)=8(x+1)+(7−x)=8, so every point works.

    For x>7x>7x>7, the sum is 2x−62x-62x−6, which equals 888 only at x=7x=7x=7, outside that open interval.

    Thus exactly −1≤x≤7-1\le x\le7−1≤x≤7 works.

  8. Problem 8 A proposed bound

    For a real parameter ccc, a student claims that 2−∣x∣<c2-\lvert x\rvert<c2−∣x∣<c is true for every real xxx exactly when c>2c>2c>2. Decide whether the claim is correct and justify the boundary.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the largest value the left side actually reaches.

    2. Hint 2

      Check the input where the absolute value is zero.

    Answer

    Correct; exactly c>2c>2c>2.

    Full solution

    Since ∣x∣≥0\lvert x\rvert\ge0∣x∣≥0,

    2−∣x∣≤2.2-\lvert x\rvert\le2.2−∣x∣≤2.

    The upper value is attained at x=0x=0x=0.

    If c>2c>2c>2, every left-side value is less than ccc.

    If c≤2c\le2c≤2, the input x=0x=0x=0 fails because it would require 2<c2<c2<c.

    Thus the strict boundary is necessary.

    Answer

    Correct; exactly c>2c>2c>2.

    Key idea

    A strict inequality holding everywhere must exceed an attained maximum strictly.

  9. Problem 9 A solver record

    A solver replaces 4−∣2x+1∣=x4-\lvert2x+1\rvert=x4−∣2x+1∣=x by 4−(2x+1)=x4-(2x+1)=x4−(2x+1)=x and reports x=1x=1x=1 as the sole real solution. Are any solutions missing? Give the full solution set and explain.

  10. Problem 10 A total distance claim

    A student says ∣x+3∣+∣x−5∣=d\lvert x+3\rvert+\lvert x-5\rvert=d∣x+3∣+∣x−5∣=d has exactly one real solution for some real ddd. Is that possible? Explain all ranges of ddd.
