    The denominator is the reciprocal case of the quotient rule: log⁡b ⁣(1b)=−log⁡bb=−1\log_b\!\left(\tfrac{1}{b}\right) = -\log_b b = -1logb​(b1​)=−logb​b=−1.

    =2−1=−2= \frac{2}{-1} = -2=−12​=−2

    That is exactly right: (1b)−2=b2=x\left(\tfrac{1}{b}\right)^{-2} = b^2 = x(b1​)−2=b2=x. Flipping the base flips the sign of every logarithm.

    An earthquake's magnitude is M=log⁡10 ⁣(IS)M = \log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{I}{S}\right)M=log10​(SI​), where III is its intensity and SSS is a fixed reference intensity. Quake A is 100100100 times as intense as quake B. How much larger is A's magnitude than B's?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Subtract the two magnitudes and let the quotient rule combine them. The reference intensity SSS cancels.

    We are told IA=100IBI_A = 100 I_BIA​=100IB​, so the ratio inside is 100100100.

    A hundredfold jump in intensity is only 222 points of magnitude, which is precisely why the scale is logarithmic: it compresses enormous ratios into small numbers.
