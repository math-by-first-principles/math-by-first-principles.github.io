  1. Problem 1 A power of the base

    For real ttt and y>0y>0y>0, expand log⁡11(11ty)\log_{11}(11^t y)log11​(11ty) and simplify.

    1. Hint 1

      Both factors are positive, so the product rule applies.

    2. Hint 2

      The logarithm of a power of its own base is that exponent.

    Answer

    t+log⁡11yt+\log_{11}yt+log11​y.

    Full solution

    The factors 11t11^t11t and yyy are both positive, so the product rule splits the logarithm into log⁡11(11t)\log_{11}(11^t)log11​(11t) plus log⁡11y\log_{11}ylog11​y.

    Since log⁡11(11t)=t\log_{11}(11^t)=tlog11​(11t)=t for every real ttt, the expansion is t+log⁡11yt+\log_{11}yt+log11​y.

    Answer

    t+log⁡11yt+\log_{11}yt+log11​y.

    Key idea

    The product rule splits off a power of the base, whose logarithm is its exponent.

  2. Problem 2 A single argument

    For t>0t>0t>0, condense log⁡13(t2+5)−32log⁡13t\log_{13}(t^2+5)-\frac32\log_{13}tlog13​(t2+5)−23​log13​t into one logarithm with coefficient one.

    1. Hint 1

      Move the outside coefficient into an exponent before combining the logarithms.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtraction places the second positive expression in the denominator.

    log⁡13(t2+5t3/2)\log_{13}\left(\frac{t^2+5}{t^{3/2}}\right)log13​(t3/2t2+5​), or equivalently log⁡13(t2+5tt)\log_{13}\left(\frac{t^2+5}{t\sqrt t}\right)log13​(tt​t2+5​).

    Full solution

    For t>0t>0t>0 both arguments are positive and t3/2t^{3/2}t3/2 is not zero.

    The power rule turns 32log⁡13t\frac32\log_{13}t23​log13​t into log⁡13(t3/2)\log_{13}(t^{3/2})log13​(t3/2), and the quotient rule gives

    log⁡13(t2+5t3/2)\log_{13}\left(\frac{t^2+5}{t^{3/2}}\right)log13​(t3/2t2+5​)

    Expanding this recovers the original coefficients.

    Answer

    log⁡13(t2+5t3/2)\log_{13}\left(\frac{t^2+5}{t^{3/2}}\right)log13​(t3/2t2+5​), or equivalently log⁡13(t2+5tt)\log_{13}\left(\frac{t^2+5}{t\sqrt t}\right)log13​(tt​t2+5​).

    Key idea

    Power and quotient rules combine logarithms while retaining their original positive-argument conditions.

  4. Problem 4 A reconstructed quantity

    Positive numbers U,VU,VU,V satisfy log⁡10U=2.4\log_{10}U=2.4log10​U=2.4 and log⁡10V=0.8\log_{10}V=0.8log10​V=0.8. Find both log⁡10((U/V)2)\log_{10}((U/V)^2)log10​((U/V)2) and (log⁡10(U/V))2(\log_{10}(U/V))^2(log10​(U/V))2.

    1. Hint 1

      The placement of the square determines whether the power rule applies.

    2. Hint 2

      First find log⁡10(U/V)\log_{10}(U/V)log10​(U/V) by subtracting the given values.

    Answer

    log⁡10((U/V)2)=3.2\log_{10}((U/V)^2)=3.2log10​((U/V)2)=3.2; (log⁡10(U/V))2=2.56(\log_{10}(U/V))^2=2.56(log10​(U/V))2=2.56.

    Full solution

    The quotient is positive and V≠0V\ne0V=0.

    Its logarithm is

    2.4−0.8=1.6.2.4-0.8=1.6.2.4−0.8=1.6.

    Squaring the argument doubles this logarithm, giving

    log⁡10((U/V)2)=3.2\log_{10}((U/V)^2)=3.2log10​((U/V)2)=3.2

    Squaring the logarithm instead gives

    (1.6)2=2.56.(1.6)^2=2.56.(1.6)2=2.56.

    The written parentheses decide which operation occurs first.

    Answer

    log⁡10((U/V)2)=3.2\log_{10}((U/V)^2)=3.2log10​((U/V)2)=3.2; (log⁡10(U/V))2=2.56(\log_{10}(U/V))^2=2.56(log10​(U/V))2=2.56.

    Key idea

    Squaring a logarithm and taking the logarithm of a square are different operations.

  5. Problem 5 A squared numerator

    Expand log⁡5((x+2)2x2+1)\log_5(\frac{(x+2)^2}{x^2+1})log5​(x2+1(x+2)2​) into logarithms with no powers on their arguments, preserving its full real domain. State that domain.

    1. Hint 1

      The numerator is positive except where its squared factor is zero.

    2. Hint 2

      Use an absolute value when moving the square outside the logarithm.

    Answer

    2log⁡5∣x+2∣−log⁡5(x2+1)2\log_5|x+2|-\log_5(x^2+1)2log5​∣x+2∣−log5​(x2+1); x≠−2x\ne-2x=−2.

    Full solution

    The denominator x2+1x^2+1x2+1 is positive for every real xxx, and the numerator is positive exactly when x≠−2x\ne-2x=−2.

    Thus that is the full domain.

    On it, ∣x+2∣>0|x+2|>0∣x+2∣>0.

    The quotient and power rules give

    log⁡5((x+2)2)=2log⁡5∣x+2∣\begin{aligned}&\log_5((x+2)^2)\\&=2\log_5|x+2|\end{aligned}​log5​((x+2)2)=2log5​∣x+2∣​

    Subtracting log⁡5(x2+1)\log_5(x^2+1)log5​(x2+1) completes the expansion without removing any allowed negative input.

    Answer

    2log⁡5∣x+2∣−log⁡5(x2+1)2\log_5|x+2|-\log_5(x^2+1)2log5​∣x+2∣−log5​(x2+1); x≠−2x\ne-2x=−2.

    Key idea

    Absolute values preserve the full domain when expanding logarithms of even powers.

  6. Problem 6 Bounds that decide

    This interval contains 161616, so the number could lie below 101610^{16}1016 (with 161616 digits) or at least 101610^{16}1016 (with 171717), and the bounds do not decide.

    The sharper bounds give 13log⁡1017>15.995213\log_{10}17>15.995213log10​17>15.9952 and

  8. Problem 8 A rule from powers

    Let M=4sM=4^sM=4s and N=4tN=4^tN=4t for real s,ts,ts,t. A student claims log⁡4(M2N)=2s+t\log_4(M^2N)=2s+tlog4​(M2N)=2s+t. Is the claim correct? Explain directly with exponent laws. Also evaluate log⁡4(M/N)\log_4(M/N)log4​(M/N) directly with exponent laws.

    1. Hint 1

      Replace M,NM,NM,N with their stated powers before taking a logarithm.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiplying powers of the same base adds their exponents.

    Answer

    Yes; log⁡4(M2N)=2s+t\log_4(M^2N)=2s+tlog4​(M2N)=2s+t. Also log⁡4(M/N)=s−t\log_4(M/N)=s-tlog4​(M/N)=s−t.

    Full solution

    The powers M,NM,NM,N are positive.

    Squaring MMM and multiplying by NNN gives

    M2N=42s+tM^2N=4^{2s+t}M2N=42s+t

    The exponent that produces this argument is 2s+t2s+t2s+t, so its base-4 logarithm is 2s+t2s+t2s+t.

    This proves the claim for every real s,ts,ts,t.

    Dividing the powers instead subtracts their exponents:

    MN=4s−t\frac MN=4^{s-t}NM​=4s−t

    so

    log⁡4(M/N)=s−t.\log_4(M/N)=s-t.log4​(M/N)=s−t.

    Answer

    Yes; log⁡4(M2N)=2s+t\log_4(M^2N)=2s+tlog4​(M2N)=2s+t. Also log⁡4(M/N)=s−t\log_4(M/N)=s-tlog4​(M/N)=s−t.

    Key idea

    Each logarithm rule is an exponent law read through the inverse operation.

  9. Problem 9 A proposed simplification

    A student replaces log⁡2(3x+1)\log_2(3x+1)log2​(3x+1) by log⁡23+log⁡2x\log_2 3+\log_2 xlog2​3+log2​x for x>0x>0x>0. Is the replacement valid for any positive xxx? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

      Two logarithms of the same valid base agree only when their positive arguments agree.

    Answer

    No positive value of xxx makes the replacement valid.

    Full solution

    The proposed right side combines to log⁡2(3x)\log_2(3x)log2​(3x).

    Equality would require

    3x+1=3x3x+1=3x3x+1=3x

    which is impossible.

    Both sides are defined for x>0x>0x>0, so the failure is not a domain issue; the arguments differ.

    Answer

    No positive value of xxx makes the replacement valid.

    Key idea

    A logarithm of a sum is not converted into a sum of logarithms by the product rule.

  10. Problem 10 A new base

    Let b>0b>0b>0, b≠1b\ne1b=1, and M>0M>0M>0. For which real rrr is log⁡brM=log⁡bMr\log_{b^r}M=\frac{\log_bM}rlogbr​M=rlogb​M​ valid? Justify both the base restriction and the formula.
