Exponential and Logarithmic Functions: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Growth factor bbb and growth rate rrr
The rate is the fraction added per step; the factor is what you multiply by, with b=1+rb = 1 + rb=1+r. A factor of 0.850.850.85 means a 15%15\%15% decrease.
Effective annual rate
The once-a-year rate matching a schedule, (1+rn)n−1\left(1 + \tfrac{r}{n}\right)^{n} - 1(1+nr​)n−1 or er−1e^{r} - 1er−1, which is how two offers are compared.
Common logarithm log⁡x\log xlogx
Base 101010, written with no base at all; a calculator's log⁡\loglog key.
eee
The irrational number 2.718281828…2.718281828\ldots2.718281828…, so every decimal you write for it is a rounding.
Argument of a logarithm
The quantity inside it. Positivity restricts the ARGUMENT, never the variable.
Extraneous solution
A candidate satisfying an equation you derived but not the one you were given: a step widened the domain.

Problem types, step by step

Find the exponential through two points

  1. Divide the two values; aaa cancels, leaving a pure power of bbb.
  2. Take the POSITIVE root for bbb, then substitute back for aaa.
  3. Check BOTH points: a wrong bbb can still satisfy one.

e.g. f(2)=18f(2) = 18f(2)=18, f(5)=486f(5) = 486f(5)=486: b3=27b^{3} = 27b3=27, so b=3b = 3b=3, a=2a = 2a=2.

Evaluate a logarithm by hand

  1. Ask the defining question: this base to what power gives the argument?
  2. Write base and argument as powers of one common number, then equate exponents.
  3. An argument below 111 needs a negative exponent.
  4. Exponentiate your answer back to confirm it.

e.g. log⁡84\log_8 4log8​4: 23y=222^{3y} = 2^{2}23y=22, so y=23y = \tfrac{2}{3}y=32​, and 82/3=48^{2/3} = 482/3=4.

Find the domain of a logarithmic expression

  1. Set every argument strictly greater than zero, never the variable itself.
  2. For a quadratic argument, factor and test the product's sign per interval.
  3. Intersect the conditions when several logarithms appear together.

e.g. log⁡5 ⁣(x2−9)\log_5\!\left(x^{2} - 9\right)log5​(x2−9) needs (x−3)(x+3)>0(x-3)(x+3) > 0(x−3)(x+3)>0, so x<−3x < -3x<−3 or x>3x > 3x>3.

Expand or condense a logarithmic expression

  1. To expand: split the quotient, then the products, then bring exponents down; roots are fractional exponents.
  2. To condense: send coefficients up as exponents FIRST, since the other rules need a coefficient of 111.
  3. Gather added logarithms into one numerator, subtracted ones into one denominator.
  4. Evaluate whatever is exact, assuming every variable inside is positive.

e.g. log⁡5 ⁣(25x3y)=2+3log⁡5x−12log⁡5y\log_5\!\left(\dfrac{25x^{3}}{\sqrt{y}}\right) = 2 + 3\log_5 x - \tfrac{1}{2}\log_5 ylog5​(y​25x3​)=2+3log5​x−21​log5​y.

Solve an exponential equation

  1. Isolate the power, then write both sides over one base and equate exponents if you can.
  2. Otherwise take log⁡\loglog or ln⁡\lnln of both sides, both positive, and use the power rule to bring the unknown down.
  3. Divide by the constant logarithm, and give the exact form and the rounded decimal separately.

e.g. 4⋅7 x−1=3004 \cdot 7^{\,x-1} = 3004⋅7x−1=300: 7 x−1=757^{\,x-1} = 757x−1=75, so x=1+log⁡75log⁡7≈3.219x = 1 + \dfrac{\log 75}{\log 7} \approx 3.219x=1+log7log75​≈3.219.

Solve a logarithmic equation

  1. Write the domain first: every argument in the ORIGINAL equation must be positive.
  2. Condense each side to a single logarithm.
  3. For log⁡bu=k\log_b u = klogb​u=k write u=bku = b^{k}u=bk; for log⁡bM=log⁡bN\log_b M = \log_b Nlogb​M=logb​N set M=NM = NM=N; then solve.
  4. Test every candidate in the ORIGINAL and discard any making an argument zero or negative.

e.g. log⁡2x+log⁡2(x−2)=3\log_2 x + \log_2(x-2) = 3log2​x+log2​(x−2)=3: x(x−2)=8x(x-2) = 8x(x−2)=8 gives x=4x = 4x=4 or −2-2−2; only 444 survives.

Solve an exponential equation that hides a quadratic

  1. Spot that one power is the square of the other, as 4x=(2x)24^{x} = \left(2^{x}\right)^{2}4x=(2x)2.
  2. Substitute uuu for the smaller power and solve the ordinary quadratic.
  3. Undo the substitution; a root u≤0u \le 0u≤0 yields no xxx, since an exponential is never zero or negative.
  4. Check each surviving xxx in the original equation.

e.g. 4x−3⋅2x−4=04^{x} - 3 \cdot 2^{x} - 4 = 04x−3⋅2x−4=0: u=4u = 4u=4 or −1-1−1, so 2x=42^{x} = 42x=4 and x=2x = 2x=2.

Build a growth or decay model and evaluate it

  1. Turn the wording into a factor: rises 30%30\%30% gives 1.301.301.30, falls 30%30\%30% gives 0.700.700.70.
  2. From two readings, divide them so the starting amount cancels, then take the root matching the elapsed time.
  3. Pick the matching form: per step, nnn periods a year, continuous, or written from a doubling time or half-life.

e.g. 500500500 grows to 800800800 in 666 years: b=1.61/6≈1.0815b = 1.6^{1/6} \approx 1.0815b=1.61/6≈1.0815, about 8.15%8.15\%8.15% a year.

Find how long a quantity takes to reach a target

  1. Set the model equal to the target and divide by the starting amount, which cancels.
  2. Take a logarithm of both sides, choosing ln⁡\lnln when the base is eee.
  3. Bring the exponent down with the power rule, divide by the constant logarithm or by rrr, then check by substituting back.

e.g. 60(12)t/12=1060\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/12} = 1060(21​)t/12=10: t12=log⁡6log⁡2≈2.585\dfrac{t}{12} = \dfrac{\log 6}{\log 2} \approx 2.58512t​=log2log6​≈2.585, so t≈31.0t \approx 31.0t≈31.0 years.

Exam traps

  • Trap Splitting the logarithm of a sum: writing log⁡(A+B)=log⁡A+log⁡B\log(A + B) = \log A + \log Blog(A+B)=logA+logB.

    Fix The product rule wants a PRODUCT inside; a sum inside does not break up at all. Test it: log⁡2(4+4)=3\log_2(4+4) = 3log2​(4+4)=3, while log⁡24+log⁡24=4\log_2 4 + \log_2 4 = 4log2​4+log2​4=4.

  • Trap Reading log⁡Mlog⁡N\dfrac{\log M}{\log N}logNlogM​ as log⁡M−log⁡N\log M - \log NlogM−logN.

    Fix A quotient of logarithms is a change of base, equal to log⁡NM\log_N MlogN​M; the difference is log⁡MN\log \tfrac{M}{N}logNM​. Compare log⁡40log⁡5≈2.292\dfrac{\log 40}{\log 5} \approx 2.292log5log40​≈2.292 with log⁡8≈0.903\log 8 \approx 0.903log8≈0.903.

  • Trap Skipping the check after combining logarithms, or discarding a candidate for being negative.

    Fix Combining widens the domain, so test each candidate in the original: every ARGUMENT must be positive. In log⁡2 ⁣(x2−9)=4\log_2\!\left(x^{2} - 9\right) = 4log2​(x2−9)=4 the root x=−5x = -5x=−5 is genuine.

  • Trap Applying log⁡b ⁣(x2)=2log⁡bx\log_b\!\left(x^{2}\right) = 2\log_b xlogb​(x2)=2logb​x without knowing the sign of xxx.

    Fix It is false for every x<0x < 0x<0, so it silently drops the negative half of a solution set. Use 2log⁡b∣x∣2\log_b |x|2logb​∣x∣ when xxx could be negative.

  • Trap Putting the rate where the factor belongs.

    Fix "Grows 6%6\%6% a year" gives b=1.06b = 1.06b=1.06. The model A=P(0.06)tA = P(0.06)^{t}A=P(0.06)t describes something losing 94%94\%94% of itself yearly.

  • Trap Adding percent changes instead of multiplying the factors.

    Fix A 30%30\%30% rise then a 30%30\%30% fall is 1.30×0.70=0.911.30 \times 0.70 = 0.911.30×0.70=0.91, a 9%9\%9% net loss rather than a wash, and (1+r)(1−r)=1−r2<1(1+r)(1-r) = 1 - r^{2} < 1(1+r)(1−r)=1−r2<1 for every r≠0r \neq 0r=0.

  • Trap Dividing an inequality by log⁡b\log blogb without flipping it, for a decaying model.

    Fix For 0<b<10 < b < 10<b<1, log⁡b\log blogb is negative, so the inequality REVERSES: (0.75)t<0.05(0.75)^{t} < 0.05(0.75)t<0.05 gives t>log⁡0.05log⁡0.75≈10.41t > \dfrac{\log 0.05}{\log 0.75} \approx 10.41t>log0.75log0.05​≈10.41, so the first whole year is 111111.

  • Trap Reading −2⋅3x-2 \cdot 3^{x}−2⋅3x as (−6)x(-6)^{x}(−6)x, or insisting every exponential has range y>0y > 0y>0.

    Fix An exponent binds only to the base beneath it, so evaluate 3x3^{x}3x then multiply. A negative aaa makes every value negative: y=−2xy = -2^{x}y=−2x stays below the axis.

  • Trap Mixing up ln⁡\lnln and log⁡\loglog, or feeding a percent straight into erte^{rt}ert.

    Fix log⁡100=2\log 100 = 2log100=2 but ln⁡100≈4.605\ln 100 \approx 4.605ln100≈4.605. And 6%6\%6% enters as r=0.06r = 0.06r=0.06: e6te^{6t}e6t inflates one year's growth from about 1.061.061.06 to over 400400400.
