  1. Problem 1 The function PPP

    Consider the real function P(x)=116x5P(x)=\frac{11}{6}x^5P(x)=611​x5.

    (a) Decide which labels apply: polynomial, rational, single power function, exponential.

    (b) Give its degree and real domain.

    1. Hint 1

      Classify by the role of the variable and the allowed kinds of coefficient, rather than by how the coefficient is written.

    2. Hint 2

      For each label, compare its defining form with the given expression. Check whether a fractional coefficient changes the powers of the variable.

    Answer

    PPP is a polynomial, a rational function, and a single power function, but is not exponential; its degree is 555 and its real domain is R\mathbb RR.

    Full solution

    (a) The formula is a single term axpax^paxp with nonzero coefficient a=11/6a=11/6a=11/6 and fixed exponent p=5p=5p=5, so it is a power function.

    The exponent is a nonnegative integer, and a polynomial may have a fractional real coefficient.

    Thus PPP is a polynomial.

    It is also a quotient of polynomials:

    P(x)=11x56.P(x)=\frac{11x^5}{6}.P(x)=611x5​.

    The input is the base of a fixed power, rather than the exponent on a fixed base, so it is not exponential.

    (b) Its degree is 555.

    Every real number can be raised to the fifth power and multiplied by 11/611/611/6, giving domain R\mathbb RR.

    Answer

    PPP is a polynomial, a rational function, and a single power function, but is not exponential; its degree is 555 and its real domain is R\mathbb RR.

    Key idea

    A family label describes a form, and more than one label may describe the same function.

  5. Problem 5 Tracking a fading signal

    At hours 000, 111, and 222, a signal has strengths 727272, 606060, and 505050 units.

    (a) Choose between a linear rule and an exponential rule to fit all three readings, and write the rule you choose.

    (b) Predict its reading at hour 333 exactly, then report that prediction to the nearest hundredth.

    (c) State whether the three readings alone prove that your rule remains valid for every later hour.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare both successive differences and successive ratios.

    2. Hint 2

      For an exponential candidate, use the hour-zero reading as the coefficient and the repeated ratio as the base.

    3. Hint 3

      What additional evidence would you want at an hour that has not been measured?

    Answer

    The exponential rule is S(t)=72(5/6)tS(t)=72(5/6)^tS(t)=72(5/6)t; it predicts the exact value S(3)=125/3S(3)=125/3S(3)=125/3 units and rounded value S(3)≈41.67S(3)\approx41.67S(3)≈41.67 units; the readings do not prove validity at all later hours.

    Full solution

    (a) The successive differences are 60−72=−1260-72=-1260−72=−12 and 50−60=−1050-60=-1050−60=−10.

    They differ, so no single linear rule fits all three equally spaced readings.

    The ratios do match: 60/72=5/660/72=5/660/72=5/6 and 50/60=5/650/60=5/650/60=5/6.

    With initial value 727272, the exponential rule is

    S(t)=72(5/6)t.S(t)=72(5/6)^t.S(t)=72(5/6)t.

    (b) Substitute the next hour into the model:

    S(3)=72(56)3.S(3)=72\left(\frac56\right)^3.S(3)=72(65​)3.

    Since (5/6)3=125/216(5/6)^3=125/216(5/6)3=125/216, this simplifies to S(3)=125/3S(3)=125/3S(3)=125/3 units.

    Rounded to the nearest hundredth, S(3)≈41.67S(3)\approx41.67S(3)≈41.67 units.

    The fraction is exact within the chosen model, while the decimal is a rounded report.

    (c) The data support the chosen fit over the observed hours.

    They do not establish that the signal keeps the same factor indefinitely, so later predictions remain model predictions.

    Answer

    The exponential rule is S(t)=72(5/6)tS(t)=72(5/6)^tS(t)=72(5/6)t; it predicts the exact value S(3)=125/3S(3)=125/3S(3)=125/3 units and rounded value S(3)≈41.67S(3)\approx41.67S(3)≈41.67 units; the readings do not prove validity at all later hours.

    Key idea

    Repeated ratios support an exponential fit, while the observed interval limits what the data establish.

  7. Problem 7 Surface area from volume

    A solid cube has positive volume VVV cubic centimeters.

    (a) Express its total surface area AAA as a function of VVV, classify that function, and state its physical domain.

    (b) If its volume is multiplied by 272727, find the factor by which its surface area changes.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the side length to connect the two geometric quantities.

    2. Hint 2

      If the side length is sss, then V=s3V=s^3V=s3 and A=6s2A=6s^2A=6s2.

    3. Hint 3

      Consider what multiplying the volume by 272727 does to the side length before considering area.

    Answer

    A(V)=6V2/3A(V)=6V^{2/3}A(V)=6V2/3 is a power function on (0,∞)(0,\infty)(0,∞); multiplying volume by 272727 multiplies surface area by 999.

    Full solution

    (a) A cube of side s>0s>0s>0 has volume s3s^3s3, so its side length is

    s=V3.s=\sqrt[3]{V}.s=3V​.

    Its six faces each have area s2s^2s2.

    A(V)=6V2/3.A(V)=6V^{2/3}.A(V)=6V2/3.

    (b) Multiplying volume by 272727 multiplies the side length by 333, since 33=273^3=2733=27.

    Answer

  8. Problem 8 A complete cyclic model

    An instrument measures a marked point's vertical position relative to the center of a unit circle as angle θ\thetaθ varies in radians. At θ=0\theta=0θ=0 its reading is 000, and at θ=π/2\theta=\pi/2θ=π/2 its reading is 111. It must give a finite reading for every real angle, always between −1-1−1 and 111.
