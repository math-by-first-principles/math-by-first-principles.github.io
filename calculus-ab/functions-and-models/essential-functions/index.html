A Catalog of Essential Functions

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

A tank that gains five liters each minute and a culture that doubles each hour both grow. Their growth has different rules. The tank adds the same amount; the culture multiplies by the same factor. Recognizing that difference helps you choose a formula before you calculate anything. A function’s family gives you clues about its graph, allowed inputs, and behavior.

Learning goals

  • Classify familiar functions by their formulas, allowing membership in more than one family
  • Compare function families using shape, equal input steps, and repeated behavior
  • Determine real domains from denominators, roots, logarithms, and the modeling context
  • Select a function model and justify it against competing family choices
  • Distinguish exact function values from rounded approximations, using appropriate notation

Fixed outputs and equal additions

Suppose a service charges a fixed 999 dollars plus 444 dollars per kilogram shipped. For packages of 111, 222, and 333 kilograms, the charges are 131313, 171717, and 212121 dollars. Each extra kilogram adds 444 dollars. The formula is C(w)=4w+9C(w)=4w+9C(w)=4w+9 on the allowed package weights.

A function family is a collection of functions sharing a form, such as f(x)=mx+bf(x)=mx+bf(x)=mx+b. Its members can have different coefficients while keeping a useful common feature.

A linear function, written f(x)=mx+bf(x)=mx+bf(x)=mx+b with real constants mmm and bbb, has this equal-addition behavior. Here we include m=0m=0m=0, which gives a constant function f(x)=bf(x)=bf(x)=b. The graph is a nonvertical straight line. If mmm is positive the line rises from left to right; if mmm is negative it falls; if m=0m=0m=0 it is horizontal. Unless a context restricts them, all real inputs are allowed.

Why do equal steps produce equal changes? In 4w+94w+94w+9, increasing www by 111 adds 444 to the product and leaves the 999 unchanged. More generally, an input increase of hhh changes mx+bmx+bmx+b by mhmhmh, independent of the starting input.

Worked example 1 Choose a model for a steady drain

Problem. A reservoir holds 747474 liters when observation begins. It drains steadily at 666 liters per minute over the next 555 minutes. Choose a function family for its volume V(t)V(t)V(t) after ttt minutes, then predict its volume after 555 minutes.

Solution. The volume loses the same 666 liters for each additional minute. This fixed additive change fits a linear model:

The predicted volume is 444444 liters.

Powers and polynomials

The area of a square of side sss is s2s^2s2. Doubling its side multiplies its area by 444 rather than adding a fixed amount. This is a different pattern from a line.

A power function has the form f(x)=axpf(x)=ax^pf(x)=axp, where the coefficient aaa is nonzero and the exponent ppp is fixed. Its real domain depends on that exponent. For example, x2x^2x2, x3x^3x3, x−1=1/xx^{-1}=1/xx−1=1/x, and x1/2=xx^{1/2}=\sqrt{x}x1/2=x​ are power functions, but they do not all permit the same inputs. For an arbitrary real exponent, positive inputs are always safe; check zero and negative inputs from the particular expression.

Consider a formula that adds several power terms:

P(x)=2x4−7x+3.P(x)=2x^4-7x+3.P(x)=2x4−7x+3.

At input 222, calculate the fourth power, multiply it by 222, then add the other terms:

P(2)=2⋅16−14+3=21.P(2)=2\cdot16-14+3=21.P(2)=2⋅16−14+3=21.

Each power of xxx is a nonnegative integer. A polynomial function is any finite sum of such terms with real coefficients. Its degree is the largest exponent with a nonzero coefficient, here 444. A nonzero constant has degree 000; the zero polynomial has no degree in our convention. Polynomials have real domain R\mathbb RR, the set of all real numbers, because their terms need no division by a variable or root extraction. A quadratic has degree 222 and a parabolic graph; a cubic has degree 333.

For positive integer powers, parity gives a useful recognition clue. Even powers such as x2x^2x2 and x4x^4x4 give equal outputs at xxx and −x-x−x. Odd powers such as x3x^3x3 and x5x^5x5 give opposite outputs there. For instance, (−2)4=16(-2)^4=16(−2)4=16, while (−2)5=−32(-2)^5=-32(−2)5=−32. A general polynomial can mix even and odd powers, so do not assign either symmetry from its degree alone.

The families overlap. The function 7x37x^37x3 is both a power function and a polynomial. The function 7x3+27x^3+27x3+2 is a polynomial, but is not one power term. The function 1/x1/x1/x is a power function but not a polynomial: its exponent is −1-1−1.

Fractions, roots, and distance

Sharing 484848 meters of ribbon equally among nnn displays gives 48/n48/n48/n meters per display. Doubling the number of displays halves each share. The expression also warns you that n=0n=0n=0 is forbidden.

A rational function is a quotient P(x)/Q(x)P(x)/Q(x)P(x)/Q(x) of polynomials, where QQQ is not the zero polynomial. Its domain excludes every input for which the written denominator is zero. The reciprocal function 1/x1/x1/x is a basic example. Its positive branch decreases as positive xxx increases, and its negative branch has negative outputs. Neither branch includes x=0x=0x=0 or has output 000.

Two rational forms can have different graph features. Compare

H(x)=x(x+2)x+2,R(x)=1x.H(x)=\frac{x(x+2)}{x+2},\qquad R(x)=\frac1x.H(x)=x+2x(x+2)​,R(x)=x1​.

For x≠−2x\ne-2x=−2, H(x)=xH(x)=xH(x)=x, but its original denominator excludes −2-2−2. Its graph is a line with a hole, a missing point, at (−2,−2)(-2,-2)(−2,−2).

The reciprocal graph has separate branches on either side of zero. Its asymptotes are the guide lines x=0x=0x=0 and y=0y=0y=0: outputs grow in magnitude as nonzero inputs get closer to zero, while larger input magnitudes make the outputs closer to zero.

Every polynomial can also be written over denominator 111, so it is rational. These labels describe forms rather than separate boxes that a function must choose between.

Root functions include x\sqrt{x}x​ and x3\sqrt[3]{x}3x​. A real square root requires a nonnegative input and returns a nonnegative output. A cube root permits any real input and keeps its sign: −273=−3\sqrt[3]{-27}=-33−27​=−3. More generally, an even root needs a nonnegative radicand, the expression under the root; an odd root accepts any real radicand.

The absolute value function f(x)=∣x∣f(x)=|x|f(x)=∣x∣ gives the distance of xxx from zero. Both −3-3−3 and 333 are three units away, so they have the same output. Its domain is R\mathbb RR and its range is [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞). Unlike x2x^2x2, its graph has straight sides meeting at a corner.

Repeated factors and logarithms

Suppose a device retains 3/43/43/4 of its stored charge after each hour. Starting with 256256256 units, its hourly readings are 256256256, 192192192, 144144144, and 108108108. The differences change, but each reading is the previous one multiplied by 3/43/43/4.

An exponential function has a fixed positive base and a variable exponent. The basic form is bxb^xbx, where b>0b>0b>0 and b≠1b\ne1b=1. A nonzero constant multiple AbxAb^xAbx is an exponential model. The basic function has domain R\mathbb RR and positive outputs. For b>1b>1b>1 it increases, and for 0<b<10<b<10<b<1 it decreases. At equal input steps of 111, successive outputs have the same factor bbb. The excluded base 111 would instead give the constant function 111.

Compare x5x^5x5 with 5x5^x5x: the first fixes the exponent and is a power function; the second fixes the base and is exponential. Their agreement at some inputs does not make them the same family.

Cycles and trigonometric functions

A point moving around a circle repeatedly returns to the same height. That repeating behavior calls for a different model from steady addition or repeated growth.

The basic trigonometric functions sin⁡x\sin xsinx and cos⁡x\cos xcosx describe such cycles. With angles measured in radians, each has domain R\mathbb RR and range [−1,1][-1,1][−1,1]. A period is a positive input shift TTT that leaves the domain unchanged and satisfies f(x+T)=f(x)f(x+T)=f(x)f(x+T)=f(x) for every input xxx in that domain. The fundamental period is the smallest such shift, when one exists. For sine and cosine it is 2π2\pi2π; multiples such as 4π4\pi4π also repeat the pattern. Here, “the period” means the fundamental period. Sine starts at sin⁡0=0\sin0=0sin0=0; cosine starts at cos⁡0=1\cos0=1cos0=1. Both remain bounded even as inputs keep growing.

Tangent is also trigonometric and periodic, but it is not bounded. Since tan⁡x=sin⁡x/cos⁡x\tan x=\sin x/\cos xtanx=sinx/cosx, its domain excludes x=π/2+kπx=\pi/2+k\pix=π/2+kπ for every integer kkk, where cosine is zero. Its period is π\piπ. Thus recognizing a trigonometric family does not by itself settle a function’s domain or range.

Answer choices

Shape is a clue rather than a proof. A small portion of a curve can look almost straight, and several families can increase. Use the formula, the input-output pattern, and the context together.

Worked example 4 Compare models for the same physical rule

Problem. A rectangular banner must have area 909090 square meters. Its width www may be any real number from 333 to 151515 meters. Let L(w)L(w)L(w) be its length in meters. Choose between a linear, reciprocal, and exponential model for LLL, write its formula, and give its physical domain.

Solution. Area is width times length, so wL=90wL=90wL=90. Solving for length gives:

L(w)=90w,3≤w≤15.L(w)=\frac{90}{w},\qquad 3\le w\le15.L(w)=w90​,3≤w≤15.

This is a reciprocal model. Doubling the width halves the corresponding length when both widths are permitted, such as 555 and 101010 meters. Their product stays fixed.

A line would use equal length changes for equal width changes. A nonconstant exponential would use equal length factors for equal width changes. Neither is the stated fixed-area rule.

The context further narrows the formula’s domain from all nonzero real numbers to [3,15][3,15][3,15].

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Treating labels as exclusive. A polynomial is also rational because it can be written over 111.
  • Confusing power and exponential forms. In x4x^4x4 the exponent is fixed; in 4x4^x4x it varies.
  • Skipping input restrictions. Denominators cannot be zero, even-root radicands must be nonnegative, and logarithm arguments must be positive.
  • Naming a family from one feature. Increasing behavior alone does not distinguish a line, a power, an exponential, or a logarithm.
  • Extending a model past its context. A formula can accept inputs that have no physical meaning for the situation.

Key takeaways

  • Linear models use equal additions over equal input steps; exponential models use repeated factors.
  • Power functions have a fixed exponent. Polynomials use finite sums of nonnegative integer powers.
  • Domains follow both the operations in a formula and the conditions of a model.
  • Sine and cosine describe bounded repeating patterns; tangent has excluded inputs and unbounded outputs.
  • Choose a model from several pieces of evidence, then check its data and context. Keep exact values until a report needs rounding.

A bit of history (optional)

Before electronic computers, a machine could predict tides by turning repeating motions into a calculation. William Thomson, later known as Lord Kelvin, designed a tide predictor built in 1873. It combined ten repeating contributions and drew the predicted water height as a curve.

William Ferrel of the U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey took a different approach. His machine, completed in 1882, showed the times and heights of high and low water directly on scales instead of tracing a curve. That difference mattered to someone preparing tide tables: a useful prediction could be a list of events rather than a continuous drawing.

The Survey’s larger second machine, completed in 1910, combined 37 contributions. Gears set the different rotation speeds, cranks produced repeating motions, and chains combined their effects. The device weighed about 2,500 pounds. Electronic computers took over U.S. tide prediction in 1966.

NOAA’s account of tide-predicting machines describes the mechanisms and preserves photographs of this mechanical way of computing.
