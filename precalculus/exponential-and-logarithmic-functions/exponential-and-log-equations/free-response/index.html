  1. Problem 1 A squared exponent

    Find every real solution of 16x2=23x+116^{x^2}=2^{3x+1}16x2=23x+1. Verify each in the original equation.

    1. Hint 1

      Express the two sides using one base before solving for the exponent.

    2. Hint 2

      The exponent equation is quadratic, so it may give two real inputs.

    Answer

    x=1x=1x=1 or x=−1/4x=-1/4x=−1/4.

    Full solution

    Since 16=2416=2^416=24, the common-base equation is

    24x2=23x+1.2^{4x^2}=2^{3x+1}.24x2=23x+1.

    The one-to-one rule gives

    4x2−3x−1=04x^2-3x-1=04x2−3x−1=0

    Factoring gives (4x+1)(x−1)=0(4x+1)(x-1)=0(4x+1)(x−1)=0, hence x=−1/4x=-1/4x=−1/4 or x=1x=1x=1.

    At x=1x=1x=1 both sides equal 16.

    At x=−1/4x=-1/4x=−1/4, the left side is 161/1616^{1/16}161/16 and the right is 21/42^{1/4}21/4; these are equal because 16=2416=2^416=24.

    Both inputs are real and the positive bases impose no additional input restriction.

    Answer

    x=1x=1x=1 or x=−1/4x=-1/4x=−1/4.

    Key idea

    Matching exponents may produce a quadratic, so one-to-oneness does not imply only one input solves the entire equation.

  4. Problem 4 Two related equations

    Find the complete real solution set for each equation, retaining its original domain.

    A. ln⁡((x−2)2)=ln⁡(5x+14)\ln((x-2)^2)=\ln(5x+14)ln((x−2)2)=ln(5x+14).

    A.

    The squared argument is positive for x≠2x\ne2x=2; the right argument is positive for x>−14/5x>-14/5x>−14/5.

    Equating arguments gives

    x2−9x−10=0x^2-9x-10=0x2−9x−10=0

    Its factorization is

    (x−10)(x+1)=0.(x-10)(x+1)=0.(x−10)(x+1)=0.

    A.

    At x=−1x=-1x=−1 both arguments are 9.

    At x=10x=10x=10 both arguments are 64.

    Each is positive, so both candidates solve A.

    B.

    The first logarithm requires x>2x>2x>2, which also makes the right argument positive.

    On that domain the power rule gives the same polynomial equation, but only 10 belongs.

    At 10, 2ln⁡8=ln⁡642\ln8=\ln642ln8=ln64, so it checks.

    The input −1-1−1 makes x−2=−3x-2=-3x−2=−3, which is forbidden in B.

    The power rule applied directly to x−2x-2x−2 needs x−2>0x-2>0x−2>0.

    Equation A also accepts negative x−2x-2x−2, so replacing its left side by that of B removes a valid solution.

    Answer

    A. x=−1x=-1x=−1 or x=10x=10x=10, domain x>−14/5x>-14/5x>−14/5 with x≠2x\ne2x=2. B. x=10x=10x=10 only, domain x>2x>2x>2. The sets differ.

    Key idea

    An even power can make a negative expression into a valid logarithm argument, while its unsquared logarithm remains undefined.

  6. Problem 6 A quotient inside a logarithm

    Find all real inputs satisfying log⁡1/4((x+1)/(x−2))≥−1\log_{1/4}((x+1)/(x-2))\ge-1log1/4​((x+1)/(x−2))≥−1. Give an exact interval answer and justify every endpoint.

    1. Hint 1

      The whole quotient must be defined and positive.

    2. Hint 2

      The logarithm decreases, so compare the quotient with (1/4)−1(1/4)^{-1}(1/4)−1 in the correct direction.

    3. Hint 3

      Split at the numerator zero and denominator zero when checking signs.

    Answer

    (−∞,−1)∪[3,∞)(-\infty,-1)\cup[3,\infty)(−∞,−1)∪[3,∞).

    Full solution

    The denominator excludes 2.

    The quotient is positive when x<−1x<-1x<−1 or x>2x>2x>2, so that is the original domain.

    Because the base is below one, the logarithm condition requires a quotient at most 4.

    Rewrite it as 1+3/(x−2)1+3/(x-2)1+3/(x−2).

    For x<−1x<-1x<−1, the added fraction is negative and the positive quotient is below 1, so every such input works.

    For x>2x>2x>2, multiply by the positive x−2x-2x−2 to get

    x+1≤4x−8.x+1\le4x-8.x+1≤4x−8.

    Thus x≥3x\ge3x≥3.

    At 3 the argument is 4 and the logarithm is −1-1−1, so include 3.

    At −1-1−1 the argument is zero; at 2 it is undefined.

    Both are excluded.

    Answer

    (−∞,−1)∪[3,∞)(-\infty,-1)\cup[3,\infty)(−∞,−1)∪[3,∞).

    A decreasing logarithm reverses the comparison, and its original positive-argument domain still decides which intervals survive.
