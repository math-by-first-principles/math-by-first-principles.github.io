    (a) Candidate A predicts 222 and 100100100, so its residuals are −1-1−1 and 000 and its squared total is 111.

    Candidate B predicts 111 and 989898, giving residuals 000 and 222 and squared total 444.

    Candidate A wins in original units.

    (b) For A, the first log residual is ln⁡1−ln⁡2=−ln⁡2\ln1-\ln2=-\ln2ln1−ln2=−ln2 and the second is zero.

    Thus

    SA=(ln⁡2)2≈0.4805S_A=(\ln2)^2\approx0.4805SA​=(ln2)2≈0.4805

    For B, the first log residual is zero.

    The second is

    ln⁡100−ln⁡98=ln⁡(50/49).\ln100-\ln98=\ln(50/49).ln100−ln98=ln(50/49).

    Thus

    SB=(ln⁡(50/49))2≈0.0004S_B=(\ln(50/49))^2\approx0.0004SB​=(ln(50/49))2≈0.0004

    Candidate B wins in log values.

    (c) In original units, a miss of two counts more than a miss of one, wherever it occurs.

    In log values, multiplying a small reading by two makes a much larger miss than changing a reading from ninety-eight to one hundred.

    The criteria therefore answer different comparison questions.

    The computations compare only two candidates at two measured times.

    They do not verify assumptions or establish what happens after the trial.

    Key idea

    Fitting in logarithms compares multiplicative misses, so it need not select the same model as fitting in original units.
