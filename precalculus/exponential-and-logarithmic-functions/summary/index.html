Exponential and Logarithmic Functions: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Exponential function
In f(x)=abxf(x)=ab^xf(x)=abx, the variable is an exponent of a fixed base: a≠0a\ne0a=0, b>0b>0b>0, b≠1b\ne1b=1. Real inputs include fractional and irrational exponents.
Factor and effective rate
The factor bbb multiplies the current positive amount over a specified period. The effective rate is the fraction gained or lost over that period, r=b−1r=b-1r=b−1.
Initial value and reference height
The initial value is the output at input zero. In abx−h+kab^{x-h}+kabx−h+k, kkk is the horizontal asymptote height; aaa is the signed distance from it at x=hx=hx=h.
Continuous coefficient kkk
In PektPe^{kt}Pekt, e=2.718281828…e=2.718281828\ldotse=2.718281828… and kkk has reciprocal time units. Positive kkk gives growth, negative kkk decay, and zero a constant amount.
Logarithm and argument
The logarithm log⁡bu\log_b ulogb​u returns the exponent producing its positive argument uuu. A missing base in log⁡\loglog means 101010; ln⁡\lnln means base eee.
Extraneous candidate
A value satisfying a derived equation but failing the original equation or its domain restrictions.
Doubling time and half-life
The time for a growing positive amount to double, or for a decaying positive amount to halve. In an offset model, these describe the exponential part.
Residual and least squares
A residual is observed minus predicted, in the stated coordinates. Least squares compares sums of squared residuals; original-output and log-output comparisons can choose different fits.
Logarithmic scale and semi-log plot
Equal ratios occupy equal distances on a logarithmic axis, which accepts only positive values. A semi-log plot has one ordinary axis and one logarithmic axis.
Interpolation and extrapolation
Interpolation predicts between measured inputs; extrapolation predicts outside their range. A formula being defined does not establish physical validity.

Problem types, step by step

Describe a graph or its inverse

  1. Identify the parent, transformations, and full original domain. For a logarithm, require the whole argument to be positive; inner expressions may add restrictions.
  2. Map useful points and asymptotes. Track direction through every reflection; substitute zero for a vertical-axis intercept when allowed.
  3. Find the range on the actual input set, using monotonicity and endpoint inclusion. Preserve holes and exclusions. For an inverse function, confirm one-to-one behavior on the stated domain, then exchange actual domain and range and swap point coordinates.

Evaluate, expand, or condense logarithms

  1. Check valid bases and every original positive-argument condition. Intersect the restrictions before rewriting.
  2. For exact values, express the base and argument as powers of a useful shared number; otherwise change base and round only at the end.
  3. To expand, separate positive products and quotients, then powers. Use absolute values when needed for squared factors; keep sums intact unless factoring creates a product.
  4. To condense, move coefficients into argument powers first, then combine products and quotients. Attach the original domain to the result.

e.g. For ln⁡(x−8)+ln⁡(8−x)\ln(x-8)+\ln(8-x)ln(x−8)+ln(8−x), find the real domain: it is empty, since the original conditions require both x>8x>8x>8 and x<8x<8x<8.

Solve an exponential or logarithmic equation

  1. Record original domains. Isolate an exponential term before taking logarithms; a positive-base power cannot equal a nonpositive target.
  2. Match one shared valid base and equate exponents, or take logarithms and use the power rule. For logarithmic equations, condense on the original domain, then exponentiate or equate arguments.
  3. For repeated powers, substitute u=bx>0u=b^x>0u=bx>0. Translate any stated input restriction, solve the polynomial, and keep only roots in the allowed substitution range.
  4. Return to the original variable. Check every candidate in the original equation, keep exact forms until rounding, and justify rejected candidates.

e.g. Given u=5xu=5^xu=5x and u(u−6)=0u(u-6)=0u(u−6)=0, recover real xxx: only x=ln⁡6/ln⁡5x=\ln6/\ln5x=ln6/ln5; the root u=0u=0u=0 is impossible.

Solve an inequality and choose endpoints

  1. Write the original domain and identify whether the base is above 111 or between 000 and 111.
  2. Find equality boundaries. Preserve order for an increasing function; reverse it for a decreasing one or when dividing by a negative number.
  3. Intersect the comparison with all original and contextual restrictions. Include equality endpoints only when allowed and defined; exclude zero arguments and undefined inputs.
  4. If inputs count whole steps, select the first accepted integer and check adjacent permitted steps in the original model.

Fit an exponential or logarithmic model

  1. State input and output units, physical domain, and the assumed family. Compare differences or ratios only over equal input intervals.
  2. For an exponential, divide two positive readings and use the elapsed-time gap. For y=A+Bln⁡xy=A+B\ln xy=A+Blnx, use two distinct positive inputs: subtract the measurement equations to find BBB, then find AAA.
  3. Check every fitting point, predict in the requested units, and test unused measurements. For an offset model, compare excesses after subtracting the reference value.

Find a model target or characteristic time

  1. Check that the target belongs to the physical range. In cooling, subtract the constant ambient temperature before comparing with the initial excess.
  2. Divide to isolate the exponential factor. Count doublings or halvings when exact; otherwise take logarithms and solve with matching time units.
  3. Check the original model and physical time domain. Distinguish the continuous boundary from a first whole posting or observation step.

Assess a model and its predictions

  1. Read both axis scales. Check the exponential or logarithmic straight-line pattern using the appropriate logged axis and a positive logged quantity.
  2. Compute observed minus predicted. Compare candidate squared residual totals in the same stated coordinates; note systematic patterns as well as size.
  3. Classify a prediction by the measured input interval. Check capacity, ambient conditions, and other assumptions before extrapolating; even a small residual total does not prove the model.

Exam traps

  • Trap Treating a sampled ratio as the base regardless of spacing or offset.

    Fix For step sss the factor is bsb^sbs. Subtract a vertical offset before forming exponential ratios; finite data alone do not establish the rule.

  • Trap Reading the coefficient as the initial value or assigning every exponential a positive range.

    Fix Substitute input zero into the complete formula. In abx−h+kab^{x-h}+kabx−h+k, the sign of aaa determines which side of kkk contains the full graph; input restrictions may narrow the range.

  • Trap Confusing a rate, a factor, and a continuous coefficient.

    Fix A period rate rrr gives factor 1+r1+r1+r; the matching continuous coefficient is ln⁡(1+r)\ln(1+r)ln(1+r). Multiply successive factors, match time units, and count completed credits.

  • Trap Checking the variable sign instead of the complete logarithm argument.

    Fix Require each original argument to be positive and each base positive and unequal to 111. Negative inputs or outputs may be valid. Undoing or canceling does not restore excluded inputs.

  • Trap Splitting a sum or moving an exponent from outside a logarithm.

    Fix Only products, quotients, and argument powers have the corresponding log laws. log⁡b(M2)\log_b(M^2)logb​(M2) and (log⁡bM)2(\log_b M)^2(logb​M)2 perform different operations; squares may need absolute values when expanded.

  • Trap Treating a quotient of logarithms as their difference.

    Fix ln⁡M/ln⁡b\ln M/\ln blnM/lnb changes base; ln⁡M−ln⁡b\ln M-\ln blnM−lnb is ln⁡(M/b)\ln(M/b)ln(M/b) for positive M,bM,bM,b. Keep the original base in the denominator of change of base.

  • Trap Keeping inequality direction or substitution roots automatically.

    Fix Bases below 111 reverse order. A substituted exponential must be strictly positive and satisfy all translated input restrictions. Test final candidates and endpoints in the original expressions.

  • Trap Halving total temperature or treating a fitted curve as confirmed future behavior.

    Fix Cooling halves excess above ambient. Check residual signs, the coordinates used for a fit, the measured interval, and physical assumptions before interpreting a prediction.

  • Trap Choosing the wrong logged axis or reading a semi-log slope as the growth factor.

    Fix Use ordinary xxx and logarithmic yyy for exponential models; use logarithmic xxx and ordinary yyy for logarithmic models. If log⁡10y=mx+c\log_{10}y=mx+clog10​y=mx+c, the factor per input unit is 10m10^m10m, not mmm or eme^mem.
