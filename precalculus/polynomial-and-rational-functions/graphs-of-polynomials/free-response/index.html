  5. Problem 5 Features of a quartic

  6. Problem 6 Counting visible turns

    A polynomial has six turning points visible in a graphing window. Both far ends are known to rise, and further turns outside the window have not been ruled out.

    Part A: What is the smallest degree not ruled out by this information? Does the information determine the exact degree? Explain.

    Part B: Decide whether G(x)=x8+1G(x)=x^8+1G(x)=x8+1 can be the polynomial described in Part A. Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      A number of turns gives a necessary lower bound on degree, rather than an exact degree or a guarantee about a particular polynomial.

    2. Hint 2

      For Part A, combine the degree inequality from six turns with the parity required by matching far-end directions.

    3. Hint 3

      For Part B, compare the eighth powers as negative inputs move toward zero and as positive inputs move away from zero. Adding one changes heights without changing their order.

    Answer

    Part A: Minimum compatible degree 888; the exact degree is undetermined. Part B: No; GGG has only the turning point (0,1)(0,1)(0,1).

    Full solution

    Part A: The six visible turns give at least six turns in total.

    The turning bound requires n−1≥6n-1\ge6n−1≥6, hence n≥7n\ge7n≥7.

    Both far ends rise, so the degree must be even and the leading coefficient positive.

    The smallest even integer at least 777 is 888.

    Larger even degrees are not ruled out, because a polynomial need not have its maximum allowed number of turns.

    These observations therefore do not determine an exact degree.

    Part B: As a negative input increases toward zero, its absolute value decreases.

    Its eighth power therefore decreases, so GGG decreases throughout the negative inputs.

    For positive inputs, a larger input has a larger eighth power, so GGG increases throughout the positive inputs.

    At zero, G(0)=1G(0)=1G(0)=1.

    Its change from decreasing to increasing gives the turning point (0,1)(0,1)(0,1).

    Strict decrease on one side and strict increase on the other leave no other turning points.

    Thus GGG cannot have the six visible turns.

    Degree 888 and upward far ends meet the necessary conditions from Part A, but they do not make this particular candidate compatible with the observed graph.

    Answer

    Part A: Minimum compatible degree 888; the exact degree is undetermined. Part B: No; GGG has only the turning point (0,1)(0,1)(0,1).

    Key idea

    A degree bound can rule out candidates, but meeting the bound and end conditions does not guarantee the observed turning pattern.
