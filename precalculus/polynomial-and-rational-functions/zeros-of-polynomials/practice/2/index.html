    Find all zeros of P(x)=4x3−5x2+28x−35P(x)=4x^3-5x^2+28x-35P(x)=4x3−5x2+28x−35 using the verified factorization P(x)=(4x−5)(x2+7)P(x)=(4x-5)(x^2+7)P(x)=(4x−5)(x2+7).

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Set each factor equal to zero. The linear factor gives 5/4. The quadratic gives a negative square.

    x2=−7x^2=-7x2=−7 x=±i7x=\pm i\sqrt7x=±i7​

    All three zeros are simple and account for degree 3. The nonreal zeros do not appear as real intercepts.

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The quadratic discriminant is negative.

    32−4(1)(5)=−113^2-4(1)(5)=-1132−4(1)(5)=−11 x=−3±i112x=\frac{-3\pm i\sqrt{11}}2x=2−3±i11​​

    The middle coefficient sets the real part -3/2. A negative discriminant contributes iii, and the constant term is subtracted in the discriminant.

  3. 3

    What are all zeros and multiplicities of 7x4−11x37x^4-11x^37x4−11x3?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Remove the largest common power of the variable before preparing a candidate list.

    7x4−11x3=x3(7x−11)7x^4-11x^3=x^3(7x-11)7x4−11x3=x3(7x−11)

    The remaining factor is nonzero at 0, and its zero is 11/7. The multiplicities 3 and 1 account for the degree 4.

  4. 4

    What are the exact zeros of x2+8x+29x^2+8x+29x2+8x+29?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Complete the square.

    x2+8x+29=(x+4)2+13x^2+8x+29=(x+4)^2+13x2+8x+29=(x+4)2+13

    At a zero, (x+4)2=−13(x+4)^2=-13(x+4)2=−13, so x+4=±i13x+4=\pm i\sqrt{13}x+4=±i13​. Subtract 4 to obtain the two answers; taking the square root does not turn 13 into 13i.

  5. 5

    A real-coefficient polynomial has degree 4 and zero −7+2i-7+2i−7+2i of multiplicity 2. Which is its complete zero list?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Real coefficients force the conjugate -7-2i with the same multiplicity. The count is

    2+2=42+2=42+2=4

    This uses the entire degree, leaving no other zero or additional copy.

  6. 6

    A polynomial with real coefficients has zero 6−3i6-3i6−3i. Which real quadratic factor is forced?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The conjugate 6+3i is also a zero. Set u=x−6u=x-6u=x−6 so the paired factors are u+3iu+3iu+3i and u−3iu-3iu−3i. Their product is a difference of squares.

    (u+3i)(u−3i)=u2+9(u+3i)(u-3i)=u^2+9(u+3i)(u−3i)=u2+9

    Substitute back and expand.

    (x−6)2+9=x2−12x+45(x-6)^2+9=x^2-12x+45(x−6)2+9=x2−12x+45

    The constant contains a sum of squares because i2=−1i^2=-1i2=−1.

  7. 7

    For P(x)=(x−9)2(x2+4x+8)P(x)=(x-9)^2(x^2+4x+8)P(x)=(x−9)2(x2+4x+8), which statement describes the real graph and the full complex count?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The quadratic is strictly positive for every real input.

    x2+4x+8=(x+2)2+4x^2+4x+8=(x+2)^2+4x2+4x+8=(x+2)2+4

    Only 9 is real, with even multiplicity 2, so the graph touches there. The quadratic supplies two nonreal zeros, giving four zeros with multiplicity altogether.

  8. 8

    A degree-3 polynomial has complex coefficients, including at least one nonreal coefficient, and 3+4i3+4i3+4i is a zero. What follows from the conjugate zero theorem?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The theorem requires every coefficient to be real. When that condition fails, it does not decide whether this particular conjugate is a zero. Losing a guarantee does not prove the opposite.

    3+4i‾=3−4i\overline{3+4i}=3-4i3+4i​=3−4i

  11. 11

    A cubic has three distinct zeros, each verified exactly. What establishes that no additional zero was missed?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    A degree-3 polynomial has three complex zeros counted with multiplicity. Three distinct verified zeros already require at least three copies. Each must therefore be simple, and no fourth zero can remain.

    1+1+1=31+1+1=31+1+1=3
  12. 12

    For R(x)=0.001(x−20)R(x)=0.001(x-20)R(x)=0.001(x−20), a proposed input 0 gives residual −0.02-0.02−0.02. What is the distance from this input to the exact zero?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The exact zero comes from the factor, not from the size of the output.

    x−20=0x-20=0x−20=0

    Thus the root is 20 and the input error is ∣20−0∣=20|20-0|=20∣20−0∣=20. A small vertical residual alone does not ensure a small horizontal error.
