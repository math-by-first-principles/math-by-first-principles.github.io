  5. Problem 5 Removing a leading term

    On R\mathbb RR, let A(x)=1100x8−30x5+2A(x)=\frac1{100}x^8-30x^5+2A(x)=1001​x8−30x5+2 and B(x)=A(x)−1100x8B(x)=A(x)-\frac1{100}x^8B(x)=A(x)−1001​x8.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

      The highest exponent controls the far ends; its parity and coefficient sign decide the two directions.

    Answer

    AAA: degree 888, leading coefficient 1100\frac1{100}1001​, both ends rise without bound. BBB: degree 555, leading coefficient −30-30−30, far left rises and far right falls without bound.

    Full solution

    For AAA, the highest power is the eighth power even though its coefficient is small.

    Its positive even leading term gives both ends rising without bound.

    The eighth-power terms cancel in BBB, leaving

    B(x)=−30x5+2B(x)=-30x^5+2B(x)=−30x5+2

    Its leading term is a negative odd power.

    On the far left the fifth power is negative, so multiplication by −30-30−30 makes the values positive and unbounded; on the far right the outputs fall without bound.

    In AAA, the size ratio of the fifth-power term to the eighth-power term is 3000/∣x∣33000/|x|^33000/∣x∣3 for nonzero xxx.

    This ratio becomes small as the input magnitude grows, so the coefficient of larger magnitude cannot keep the lower power in control.

    Answer

    AAA: degree 888, leading coefficient 1100\frac1{100}1001​, both ends rise without bound. BBB: degree 555, leading coefficient −30-30−30, far left rises and far right falls without bound.

    Key idea

    Canceling the highest term can change both degree and end behavior; coefficient size alone cannot decide either end.
