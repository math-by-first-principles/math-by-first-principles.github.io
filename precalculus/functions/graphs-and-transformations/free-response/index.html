    Consider A(x)=∣x∣A(x)=\lvert x\rvertA(x)=∣x∣ and Q(x)=x2Q(x)=x^2Q(x)=x2, both on all real numbers.

    (a) Give the outputs of both functions at x=−3x=-3x=−3, x=−1/2x=-1/2x=−1/2, and x=0x=0x=0.

    (b) Which graph has straight sides and a corner? Explain why matching outputs at x=0x=0x=0 alone cannot identify the function.

    1. Hint 1

      Use distance from zero for absolute value and multiplication for a square.

    2. Hint 2

      A graph shape describes more than the value at one input.

    Answer

    Full solution

    (a) Absolute value removes the sign of each input, giving 333, 1/21/21/2, and 000.

    Squaring gives

    (−3)2=9.(-3)^2=9.(−3)2=9.

    It also gives (−1/2)2=1/4(-1/2)^2=1/4(−1/2)2=1/4 and 02=00^2=002=0.

    (b) Absolute value follows the straight rules −x-x−x to the left and xxx to the right.

    They meet at a corner.

    Squaring gives a smooth curve.

    The shared point (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) provides no information about the rest of either graph.

    Answer

    (a) For inputs −3,−1/2,0-3,-1/2,0−3,−1/2,0, AAA gives 3,1/2,03,1/2,03,1/2,0 and QQQ gives 9,1/4,09,1/4,09,1/4,0. (b) AAA has straight sides and a corner; both give zero at x=0x=0x=0, so that value alone does not distinguish them.

  4. Problem 4 Symmetry includes the allowed inputs

    Classify each function as even, odd, both, or neither on its stated domain. Give a reason for each classification.

    (a) AAA is defined only at −4,−1,1,4-4,-1,1,4−4,−1,1,4, with outputs 2,−3,−3,22,-3,-3,22,−3,−3,2, in that order.

    (b) BBB is defined only at −3,0,3-3,0,3−3,0,3, with outputs −5,0,5-5,0,5−5,0,5, in that order.

    (c) C(x)=xC(x)=\sqrt{x}C(x)=x​ on [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞).

    1. Hint 1

      First check whether every allowed input has its negative in the domain.

    2. Hint 2

      Then compare each pair of outputs: equal for even symmetry, negatives for odd symmetry.

    Answer

    (a) Even only. (b) Odd only. (c) Neither.

    Full solution

    (a) The domain contains opposite pairs.

    The outputs at −4-4−4 and 444 are equal, as are the outputs at −1-1−1 and 111, so AAA is even.

    Its nonzero equal outputs are not negatives of one another, so it is not odd.

    (b) The outputs at −3-3−3 and 333 are negatives of one another, and B(0)=0B(0)=0B(0)=0.

    Thus BBB is odd.

    Its outputs at opposite nonzero inputs are unequal, so it is not even.

    (c) The domain includes 111 but not −1-1−1.

    It is not symmetric about zero, so CCC is neither even nor odd on this domain.

    Answer

    (a) Even only. (b) Odd only. (c) Neither.

    Key idea

    The symmetry test concerns the entire function, including its domain.

    ▾

  6. Problem 6 Peaks, valleys and endpoints

    The whole graph of fff joins (−6,2)(-6,2)(−6,2), (−3,8)(-3,8)(−3,8), (0,3)(0,3)(0,3), (4,6)(4,6)(4,6), and (7,−1)(7,-1)(7,−1) with straight segments and included endpoints. Let g(x)=10−f(x)g(x)=10-f(x)g(x)=10−f(x).

    (a) Find every interior local maximum and minimum of ggg, giving each value and input.

    (b) Find its global maximum and minimum, including endpoints in the comparison.

    1. Hint 1

      Reflecting heights turns peaks into valleys and valleys into peaks.

    2. Hint 2

      Transform all five listed heights, then compare the interior points locally and all points globally.

    Answer

    Full solution

    The five heights of ggg are 8,2,7,4,118,2,7,4,118,2,7,4,11, in order.

    The straight segments between them reveal every rise and fall.

    (a) The interior point at input 000 is above nearby outputs, giving local maximum 777.

    The interior points at inputs −3-3−3 and 444 are below their neighbors, giving local minimum values 222 and 444.

    (b) The largest output on the whole graph is 111111 at the right endpoint x=7x=7x=7.

    The smallest is 222 at x=−3x=-3x=−3.

    The endpoint is eligible for a global extremum even though this lesson reserves local extrema for interior inputs.

    Answer

    Key idea

    A global comparison includes every allowed input, even when a local comparison excludes endpoints.

  8. Problem 8 A symmetry that survives scaling

    Let fff be an even real-valued function on a nonempty domain DDD symmetric about zero. Let aaa and bbb be nonzero real numbers and kkk any real number.

    Define g(x)=af(bx)+kg(x)=a f(bx)+kg(x)=af(bx)+k wherever bxbxbx belongs to DDD. Show that ggg is even by checking both its domain and its output rule.
