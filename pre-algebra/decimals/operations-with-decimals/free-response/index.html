  1. Problem 1 Subtracting thousandths

    Find 9.3−4.6589.3-4.6589.3−4.658, then check your result by addition.

    1. Hint 1

      Give both numbers the same number of decimal places before stacking them with the points in one column.

    2. Hint 2

      The thousandths column needs a borrow, and the top number’s hundredths digit is zero, so the borrow must come from the tenths.

    Answer

    4.6424.6424.642.

    Full solution

    Pad 9.39.39.3 with two trailing zeros, which does not change its value, so both numbers reach the thousandths.

    Stack 9.3009.3009.300 over 4.6584.6584.658 with the points in one column.

    The thousandths read 0−80-80−8, and the top hundredths digit is also 000, so borrow one tenth: it becomes 999 hundredths and 101010 thousandths, and 222 tenths remain.

    The thousandths give 10−8=210-8=210−8=2, and the hundredths give 9−5=49-5=49−5=4.

    The tenths read 2−62-62−6, so borrow one whole, making 121212 tenths and 888 ones.

    The tenths give 12−6=612-6=612−6=6, and the ones give 8−4=48-4=48−4=4.

    9.300−4.658=4.6429.300-4.658=4.6429.300−4.658=4.642

    Check by adding the result to the number subtracted.

    4.642+4.658=9.3004.642+4.658=9.3004.642+4.658=9.300

    Answer

    4.6424.6424.642.

    Key idea

    When a borrow meets a zero digit, borrow from the nearest nonzero place to its left, and each zero it passes through becomes 999.

  2. Problem 2 The three-place dividend

    Compute 0.594÷0.360.594\div0.360.594÷0.36, and check your quotient by multiplication.

    1. Hint 1

      The divisor has two decimal places; decide what would make it a whole number.

    2. Hint 2

      Slide both points two places, then divide 59.459.459.4 by 363636, carrying remainders into tenths and hundredths.

    Answer

    1.651.651.65

    Full solution

    The divisor 0.360.360.36 has two decimal places, so slide both points two places right to make it whole.

    0.594÷0.36=59.4÷360.594\div0.36=59.4\div360.594÷0.36=59.4÷36

    Thirty-six fits once into 595959, leaving 232323.

    The next digit makes 234234234 tenths; six groups of 363636 leave 181818 tenths.

    Padding a zero after the 444 turns those into 180180180 hundredths, which divide into five groups.

    59.4÷36=1.6559.4\div36=1.6559.4÷36=1.65

    Check by multiplying the quotient by the divisor, which should give back the dividend.

    1.65×0.36=0.5941.65\times0.36=0.5941.65×0.36=0.594

    Answer

    1.651.651.65

    Key idea

    Sliding both points by the divisor’s decimal places makes the divisor whole; a dividend with more places than the divisor keeps a point, and the long division carries on past it, padding zeros as needed.

  3. Problem 3 The material tub

    An empty tub weighs 0.840.840.84 kg. Filled with material, it weighs 3.0753.0753.075 kg. After 0.650.650.65 kg of material is removed, how much material remains in the tub?

    1. Hint 1

      Separate the material from the weight of the tub.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the empty weight from the filled weight, then subtract the amount removed.

    Answer

    1.5851.5851.585 kg.

    Full solution

    Pad the empty weight to three places and subtract it from the filled weight.

    3.075−0.840=2.2353.075-0.840=2.2353.075−0.840=2.235

    The tub initially holds 2.2352.2352.235 kg of material.

    Subtract the 0.650.650.65 kg removed, again matching decimal places.

    2.235−0.650=1.5852.235-0.650=1.5852.235−0.650=1.585

    The remaining material weighs 1.5851.5851.585 kg.

    Adding 0.650.650.65 kg and the tub weight of 0.840.840.84 kg returns the original 3.0753.0753.075 kg.

    Answer

    1.5851.5851.585 kg.

    Key idea

    Separate the container’s weight from its contents, padding every amount to the same number of decimal places before subtracting.

  4. Problem 4 The enlarged factors

    Two positive decimal factors have an unknown product. The first factor is multiplied by 100100100, and the second by 101010. Their new product is 404040. What was the original product?

    1. Hint 1

      Track how scaling each factor affects their product.

    2. Hint 2

      Undo the combined effect of both multiplications on the new product.

    Answer

    0.040.040.04.

    Full solution

    The first change makes the product one hundred times as large, and the second change multiplies it by ten again.

    Together the two changes multiply the product by

    100×10=1000100\times10=1000100×10=1000

    Divide the new product by 100010001000 to recover the original product.

    40÷1000=0.0440\div1000=0.0440÷1000=0.04

    Checking the change forward gives 0.04×1000=400.04\times1000=400.04×1000=40, the stated new product.

    Answer

    0.040.040.04.

    Key idea

    When each factor is multiplied by a number, the product is multiplied by the product of those numbers.

  5. Problem 5 The card stacks

    A stack of 505050 identical cards is 3.93.93.9 cm thick, with no gaps between cards. How thick is one card, and how thick would a stack of 250250250 of these cards be?

    1. Hint 1

      The thickness of equal cards adds to the thickness of the stack.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide the given thickness by the number of cards in the given stack, then multiply the thickness of one card by the number of cards in the new stack.

    Answer

    One card: 0.0780.0780.078 cm; 250250250 cards: 19.519.519.5 cm.

    Full solution

    Divide the thickness of the stack by the 505050 cards in it.

    Fifty does not fit into 333 ones, so the ones digit is 000, and the 333 ones join the 999 tenths to make 393939 tenths, which 505050 does not fit into either, so the tenths digit is 000.

    Padding a zero makes 390390390 hundredths, which hold seven groups of 505050 with 404040 hundredths left over.

    Those become 400400400 thousandths, which hold exactly eight groups.

    3.9÷50=0.0783.9\div50=0.0783.9÷50=0.078

    Multiply that thickness by 250250250.

    The digit strings give 78×250=1950078\times250=1950078×250=19500, and three decimal places give

    0.078×250=19.5000.078\times250=19.5000.078×250=19.500

    Dropping the trailing zeros, the new stack is 19.519.519.5 cm thick.

    The new stack is five times the given stack of 505050 cards, and multiplying the given thickness by 555 confirms the result.

    3.9×5=19.53.9\times5=19.53.9×5=19.5

    Answer

    One card: 0.0780.0780.078 cm; 250250250 cards: 19.519.519.5 cm.

    Key idea

    Dividing a decimal total by a whole-number count gives one item’s share, and multiplying that share by a new count gives the new total.

  6. Problem 6 The roller’s progress

    A roller advances a sheet 0.3750.3750.375 meters for each turn, and a fraction of a turn advances it that same fraction of 0.3750.3750.375 meters. The sheet is 888 meters long, and the roller stops after 15.615.615.6 turns. How many meters of the sheet have not yet been advanced?

    1. Hint 1

      The advance for a number of turns, whole or not, is that number times the advance for one turn.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the advance by multiplication, counting the decimal places of both factors, then subtract it from the length of the sheet.

    Answer

    2.152.152.15 meters.

    Full solution

    The advance after 15.615.615.6 turns multiplies two decimals, 0.375×15.60.375\times15.60.375×15.6.

    The digit strings give 375×156=58500375\times156=58500375×156=58500, and the factors have 3+1=43+1=43+1=4 decimal places together.

    0.375×15.6=5.85000.375\times15.6=5.85000.375×15.6=5.8500

    Dropping the trailing zeros, the roller has advanced 5.855.855.85 meters.

    Pad the sheet’s length to hundredths and subtract, borrowing from the ones through the zero tenths.

    8.00−5.85=2.158.00-5.85=2.158.00−5.85=2.15

    Check by adding the two parts, which should give back the whole sheet.

    5.85+2.15=8.005.85+2.15=8.005.85+2.15=8.00

    Answer

    2.152.152.15 meters.

    Key idea

    Count the decimal places of both factors to place the point in the product, then pad the whole to the same places before subtracting the part from it.

  7. Problem 7 The cartridge supply

    A supply tank holds 3.123.123.12 liters of liquid. Another 0.480.480.48 liters is added. Each empty cartridge receives exactly 0.220.220.22 liters. How many cartridges can be filled completely from the tank, and how much liquid is left over?

    1. Hint 1

      Work out how much liquid is available after the addition.

    2. Hint 2

      Slide both points so the divisor is whole, and find how many whole times it fits into the dividend.

    3. Hint 3

      Multiply the number of full cartridges by 0.220.220.22, then subtract that amount from the available liquid.

    Answer

    161616 cartridges, with 0.080.080.08 liters left over.

    Full solution

    Add the amounts of liquid.

    3.12+0.48=3.603.12+0.48=3.603.12+0.48=3.60

    The number of full cartridges is the whole number of times 0.220.220.22 fits into 3.603.603.60.

    Shift both points two places right.

    3.60÷0.22=360÷223.60\div0.22=360\div223.60÷0.22=360÷22

    Since 22×16=35222\times16=35222×16=352 and 22×17=37422\times17=37422×17=374, which is more than 360360360, twenty-two fits into 360360360 sixteen whole times.

    So 161616 cartridges can be filled completely.

    Those cartridges use 16×0.22=3.5216\times0.22=3.5216×0.22=3.52 liters.

    Subtract to find what is left.

    3.60−3.52=0.083.60-3.52=0.083.60−3.52=0.08

    The 0.080.080.08 liters left is less than 0.220.220.22 liters, so no further cartridge can be filled.

    Answer

    161616 cartridges, with 0.080.080.08 liters left over.

    Key idea

    When decimal portions do not fit exactly, count the whole portions, then multiply back and subtract to find what is left over.

  8. Problem 8 Nina’s total

    Nina writes 0.74+2.8=3.540.74+2.8=3.540.74+2.8=3.54. She says that the 777 and the 888 belong in the same column of her addition. Is her result correct, and is her column choice justified? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify what each of the two named digits is worth.

    2. Hint 2

      Write both numbers to hundredths, then combine matching columns and account for any carry.

    Answer

    Both the result and the column choice are correct.

    Full solution

    The 777 in 0.740.740.74 and the 888 in 2.82.82.8 both count tenths.

    Padding the second number gives 2.802.802.80.

    Stacking with the points in one column puts tenths over tenths and hundredths over hundredths.

    0.74+2.80=3.540.74+2.80=3.540.74+2.80=3.54

    The tenths total fifteen tenths: five tenths remain and one whole is carried.

    The hundredths total four, and the carried whole raises the whole part from two to three.

    Both parts of Nina’s work are justified.

    Answer

    Both the result and the column choice are correct.

    Key idea

    Aligning decimal points puts digits that count the same place value into the same column.

  9. Problem 9 Mara’s note

    Mara claims that multiplying two numbers each written with exactly one digit after the point must leave exactly two digits after the point, even after all trailing zeros are removed. Is the claim true? Give a numerical example that supports your decision.

    1. Hint 1

      The place-counting rule positions the point before optional trailing zeros are removed.

    2. Hint 2

      Try factors whose whole-number product ends in zeros, then apply the place-counting rule.

    Answer

    No; any pair whose whole-number product ends in 000 disproves it, for example 3.2×2.5=83.2\times2.5=83.2×2.5=8 or 0.5×0.2=0.10.5\times0.2=0.10.5×0.2=0.1.

    Full solution

    Take 3.23.23.2 and 2.52.52.5, each written with one decimal place.

    Their whole-number product is

    32×25=80032\times25=80032×25=800

    Two decimal places put the product at

    3.2×2.5=8.003.2\times2.5=8.003.2×2.5=8.00

    The two trailing zeros may be removed.

    8.00=88.00=88.00=8

    The exact product has no decimal places after that removal, so it disproves Mara’s claim.

    The place-counting rule correctly located the point before the zeros were dropped.

    Any pair whose whole-number product ends in 000 also breaks the claim; for example 5×2=105\times2=105×2=10 gives 0.5×0.2=0.100.5\times0.2=0.100.5×0.2=0.10, which is 0.10.10.1.

    Answer

    No; any pair whose whole-number product ends in 000 disproves it, for example 3.2×2.5=83.2\times2.5=83.2×2.5=8 or 0.5×0.2=0.10.5\times0.2=0.10.5×0.2=0.1.

    Key idea

    Place-counting fixes the decimal point before any trailing zeros are removed.

  10. Problem 10 The paired calculations

    To evaluate 0.084÷0.0120.084\div0.0120.084÷0.012, Jo uses 84÷1284\div1284÷12 and Ren uses 840÷120840\div120840÷120. Do both calculations give the correct value? Find that value and explain.

    1. Hint 1

      A quotient is preserved when both of its numbers are multiplied by the same nonzero amount.

    2. Hint 2

      Decide how many places each proposed calculation slides the point in the dividend and in the divisor.

    Answer

    Yes; both give 777.

    Full solution

    Jo multiplies both numbers by 100010001000.

    Ren multiplies both numbers by 100001000010000.

    Each calculation scales its dividend and divisor by the same nonzero amount, so each preserves the original quotient.

    Both divisions give the same value.

    84÷12=784\div12=784÷12=7

    For Ren’s calculation,

    840÷120=7840\div120=7840÷120=7

    Checking in the original division gives

    0.012×7=0.084.0.012\times7=0.084.0.012×7=0.084.

    Answer

    Yes; both give 777.

    Key idea

    Both points may slide any number of places to the right, as long as the dividend and divisor slide the same number of places.
