    0.24×0.085=0.020400.24\times0.085=0.020400.24×0.085=0.02040

    The final zero is a trailing zero, so it may be dropped, giving 0.02040.02040.0204.

    The zero just after the point and the zero between the 222 and the 444 must stay, because they hold those digits in their columns.

    A size check agrees: 0.240.240.24 is just under a quarter, and a quarter of 0.0850.0850.085 is just over 0.020.020.02.

    Answer

    0.02040.02040.0204.

    Key idea

    Counting decimal places can call for leading zeros, and a trailing zero that the count produces may then be dropped.

  4. Problem 4 The evenly spaced marks

    A straight scale has equally spaced marks beginning at 0.180.180.18 and ending at 5.765.765.76. The difference between neighboring marks is 0.090.090.09. How many equal gaps lie between the first and last marks?

    1. Hint 1

      Find the entire change from the starting label to the ending label.

    2. Hint 2

      Each gap accounts for the same part of that change, so divide by the change per gap.

    Answer

    626262 gaps.

    Full solution

    The full change in the labels is

    5.76−0.18=5.585.76-0.18=5.585.76−0.18=5.58

    The number of equal gaps is the full change divided by 0.090.090.09.

    Scaling both numbers by one hundred gives

    5.58÷0.09=558÷95.58\div0.09=558\div95.58÷0.09=558÷9

    Now divide.

    558÷9=62558\div9=62558÷9=62

    Sixty-two gaps contribute 62×0.09=5.5862\times0.09=5.5862×0.09=5.58, and adding that change to 0.180.180.18 returns 5.765.765.76.

    Answer

    626262 gaps.

    Key idea

    A count of equal gaps comes from dividing the total change by the change per gap.

  5. Problem 5 The workshop mixtures

    Workshop A uses 0.380.380.38 liters of water for each kg of clay. Workshop B uses 0.4170.4170.417 liters for each kg of clay. Each workshop prepares 1.61.61.6 kg of clay. How many more liters of water does workshop B use than workshop A?

    1. Hint 1

      Find each workshop’s water amount for the actual mass of clay.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiply each amount per kg by the mass, then compare the two products.

    Answer

    0.05920.05920.0592 liters.

    Full solution

    For workshop A, multiply 383838 by 161616 and place three decimal digits in the product.

    0.38×1.6=0.6080.38\times1.6=0.6080.38×1.6=0.608

    For workshop B, 417×16=6672417\times16=6672417×16=6672, with four decimal places altogether.

    0.417×1.6=0.66720.417\times1.6=0.66720.417×1.6=0.6672

    Pad workshop A’s amount to four decimal places, then subtract, borrowing from the hundredths for the thousandths column.

    0.6672−0.6080=0.05920.6672-0.6080=0.05920.6672−0.6080=0.0592

  6. Problem 6 The two expansions

    Write 8.738.738.73 and 8.7268.7268.726 in expanded form, using fractions for the parts smaller than one. Then say which of the two numbers is greater.

    1. Hint 1

      Each digit contributes the digit times its column’s value, and two numbers can be matched one column at a time.

    2. Hint 2

      Match the terms of the two sums column by column, then ask whether the later terms of one can make up for a smaller earlier term.

    Answer

    8.73=8+710+31008.73=8+\frac{7}{10}+\frac{3}{100}8.73=8+107​+1003​; 8.726=8+710+2100+610008.726=8+\frac{7}{10}+\frac{2}{100}+\frac{6}{1000}8.726=8+107​+1002​+10006​; 8.738.738.73 is greater.

    Full solution

    Each digit is worth the digit times its column’s value.

    In 8.738.738.73 the 888 is ones, the 777 is tenths and the 333 is hundredths.

    8.73=8+710+31008.73=8+\frac{7}{10}+\frac{3}{100}8.73=8+107​+1003​

    In 8.7268.7268.726 the 888 is ones, the 777 is tenths, the 222 is hundredths and the 666 is thousandths.

    8.726=8+710+2100+610008.726=8+\frac{7}{10}+\frac{2}{100}+\frac{6}{1000}8.726=8+107​+1002​+10006​

    The ones terms and the tenths terms of the two sums agree, so the order rests on the terms that remain.

    For 8.738.738.73 that is 3100\frac{3}{100}1003​, which is 301000\frac{30}{1000}100030​.

    For 8.7268.7268.726 it is

    2100+61000=261000\frac{2}{100}+\frac{6}{1000}=\frac{26}{1000}1002​+10006​=100026​

    Since 30>2630>2630>26, 8.738.738.73 is the greater number, although it is written with fewer digits.

    Answer

    8.73=8+710+31008.73=8+\frac{7}{10}+\frac{3}{100}8.73=8+107​+1003​; 8.726=8+710+2100+610008.726=8+\frac{7}{10}+\frac{2}{100}+\frac{6}{1000}8.726=8+107​+1002​+10006​; 8.738.738.73 is greater.

    In expanded form each digit is worth the digit times its column’s value, and two decimals compare at the first column where they differ, not by how many digits they show.

  7. Problem 7 The sample batch

    A batch contains 100100100 identical samples, each weighing exactly 0.029950.029950.02995 grams. What is the batch’s total weight to the nearest hundredth of a gram? Use round half up for an exact tie.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the complete weight before deciding which digits to keep.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiplying by one hundred moves the point two places right; then inspect the digit after the requested hundredths place.

    Answer

    3.003.003.00 grams.

    Full solution

    The exact batch weight is

    0.02995×100=2.9950.02995\times100=2.9950.02995×100=2.995

    The thousandths digit is 555 with nothing after it, so the weight is exactly halfway between 2.992.992.99 and 3.003.003.00, and round half up sends it up.

    The carry passes through both nines to the ones column.

    2.995≈3.002.995\approx3.002.995≈3.00

    The batch weighs 3.003.003.00 grams to the nearest hundredth.

    The final two zeros record the requested place.

    Answer

    3.003.003.00 grams.

    Key idea

    An exact halfway value goes up under round half up, and a carry into a nine turns that nine into a zero and carries again into the column to its left.

  8. Problem 8 The voltage record

    A voltage rises from 1.8751.8751.875 volts to 2.042.042.04 volts. Nora records the increase as 3320\frac{33}{20}2033​ of a volt. Is her record correct? Give the increase as a decimal and as a fraction in lowest terms.

  9. Problem 9 The packing plans

    A shipment of 14.114.114.1 kg of rice is packed into bags that each hold exactly 0.470.470.47 kg, and no rice is lost. Plan A calls for 282828 full bags and Plan B calls for 303030. Estimate the bag count, rounding the total mass to the nearest whole number and the mass in each bag to the nearest tenth, then find the exact count. Which plan packs all the rice with none left over?

    1. Hint 1

      The number of bags compares the total mass of rice with the mass that each bag holds.

    2. Hint 2

      The rounded masses provide an estimate; the original masses determine whether a plan packs the rice exactly.

    Answer

    Estimate: about 282828 bags; exact count: 303030 bags; Plan B packs all the rice.

    Full solution

    The rounded total is 141414 kg, and the rounded mass in each bag is 0.50.50.5 kg.

    The estimated bag count is

    14÷0.5=2814\div0.5=2814÷0.5=28

    That estimate suggests the correct size, but it uses altered masses and does not establish the exact count.

    Now divide the original masses.

    The divisor 0.470.470.47 has two decimal places, but 14.114.114.1 has only one, so write it as 14.1014.1014.10 first.

    14.10÷0.47=1410÷4714.10\div0.47=1410\div4714.10÷0.47=1410÷47

  10. Problem 10 The possible numerators

    A fraction has denominator 909090 and a whole-number numerator from 111 through 202020. Which numerators make its decimal terminate? Justify why your list is complete.

    1. Hint 1

      Look for denominator factors that would prevent termination if they survived reduction.

    2. Hint 2

      Determine which permitted numerators cancel every factor of three in the denominator.

    Answer

    999 and 181818.

    Full solution

    The denominator factors as

    90=2×3×3×590=2\times3\times3\times590=2×3×3×5

    After reduction, a terminating decimal may have only twos and fives in its denominator.

    Both factors of three must therefore cancel, which happens exactly when the numerator is divisible by

    3×3=9.3\times3=9.3×3=9.

    The permitted multiples of nine are 999 and 181818, and both terminate.

    990=110\frac{9}{90}=\frac{1}{10}909​=101​

    Likewise,

    1890=15\frac{18}{90}=\frac{1}{5}9018​=51​

    A multiple of three that is not a multiple of nine cancels only one three, so the other survives.

    For example,

    690=115\frac{6}{90}=\frac{1}{15}906​=151​

    Every other permitted numerator cancels no three at all.

    Each of those fractions keeps a three in its reduced denominator, so none of those decimals terminates, and the list is complete.

    Answer

    999 and 181818.

    Key idea

    A fraction terminates exactly when reduction removes every denominator prime other than two and five.
