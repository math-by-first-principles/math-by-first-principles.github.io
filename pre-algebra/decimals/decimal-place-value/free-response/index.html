Problem 1 of 10

  1. Problem 1 The counter reading

    A counter starts at zero. Each click adds one tenth to its reading. What decimal does it show after 141414 clicks?

    1. Hint 1

      Count the reading as a number of tenths, including any whole amounts they make.

    2. Hint 2

      Ten tenths make one whole, so bundle them before placing the remaining tenths.

    Answer

    1.41.41.4.

    Full solution

    Fourteen clicks add fourteen tenths.

    Ten of those tenths form a whole and four tenths remain.

    1410=1+410\frac{14}{10}=1+\frac{4}{10}1014​=1+104​

    The counter therefore shows 1.41.41.4.

    It has passed one whole but has not reached two wholes.

    Answer

    1.41.41.4.

    Key idea

    Ten tenths form a whole, so a count of tenths may need digits on both sides of the point.

  2. Problem 2 The missing digit

    Find the digit that completes 1.0□9=104910001.0\square9=\frac{1049}{1000}1.0□9=10001049​.

    1. Hint 1

      The fraction counts all of the number’s thousandths, including those in its whole part.

    2. Hint 2

      Separate one thousand thousandths from the numerator, then place the remaining digits in their columns.

    Answer

    444.

    Full solution

    One thousand of the thousandths make one whole.

    The rest form the decimal part.

    10491000=1+491000\frac{1049}{1000}=1+\frac{49}{1000}10001049​=1+100049​

    Forty-nine thousandths occupy the hundredths and thousandths columns, with a zero holding the tenths column open.

    10491000=1.049\frac{1049}{1000}=1.04910001049​=1.049

    The missing digit is therefore 444.

    It contributes four hundredths, and the final nine contributes nine thousandths.

    Answer

    444.

    Key idea

    A fraction over one thousand determines the decimal’s whole part and three places after the point.

  3. Problem 3 The marked gap

    The gap from 2.62.62.6 to 2.72.72.7 on a number line is divided into ten equal parts. What decimal labels the fourth small mark to the right of 2.62.62.6?

    1. Hint 1

      Decide what fraction of one whole the entire gap represents.

    2. Hint 2

      A tenth split into ten equal parts gives a smaller place; name it, then count the steps from 2.62.62.6.

    Answer

    2.642.642.64

    Full solution

    The gap is one tenth of a whole, and splitting a tenth into ten equal parts makes each part one hundredth.

    Four steps beyond 2.62.62.6 therefore add four hundredths.

    2610+4100=264100\frac{26}{10}+\frac{4}{100}=\frac{264}{100}1026​+1004​=100264​

    The mark is labeled 2.642.642.64.

    It lies between 2.602.602.60 and 2.702.702.70, as required.

    Answer

    2.642.642.64

    Key idea

    Dividing one tenth into ten equal parts produces hundredths.

  4. Problem 4 The label pair

    Label A reads "eleven and forty thousandths." Label B shows 11.00411.00411.004. Write label A as a decimal and state which label names the larger number.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the named final place to translate between the words and decimal notation.

    2. Hint 2

      Give both decimals three places, then look for the first column where they differ.

    Answer

    A: 11.04011.04011.040, or 11.0411.0411.04; A is larger.

    Full solution

    Forty thousandths means 404040 thousandths, so the final digit of 404040 lands in the thousandths column.

    The 444 then sits in the hundredths column, and a zero holds the tenths column open.

    401000=0.040\frac{40}{1000}=0.040100040​=0.040

    Label A is therefore 11.04011.04011.040.

    Its final zero is a trailing zero, so 11.0411.0411.04 names the same number, but the zero in the tenths column cannot be dropped.

    Both labels have 111111 ones and 000 tenths.

    Label A has 444 hundredths, while label B has 000 hundredths.

    11.040>11.00411.040>11.00411.040>11.004

    The first column where the labels differ settles the comparison, so label A names the larger number.

    Answer

    A: 11.04011.04011.040, or 11.0411.0411.04; A is larger.

    Key idea

    Read the place named by the words before comparing the resulting decimals.

  5. Problem 5 The recorded amount

    A recorder describes an amount as 121212 tenths and 151515 thousandths. Write the amount as a decimal and as a sum of its nonzero place values, using fractions for the parts smaller than one.

    1. Hint 1

      Both counts are ten or more of their unit, so each one spills over into a larger place.

    2. Hint 2

      Bundle ten tenths into one whole and ten thousandths into one hundredth, then place what remains in each column.

    Answer

    1.2151.2151.215; 1+210+1100+510001+\frac{2}{10}+\frac{1}{100}+\frac{5}{1000}1+102​+1001​+10005​.

    Full solution

    Twelve tenths contain one whole and two tenths.

    1210=1+210\frac{12}{10}=1+\frac{2}{10}1012​=1+102​

    Fifteen thousandths contain one hundredth and five thousandths, because ten thousandths make one hundredth.

    151000=1100+51000\frac{15}{1000}=\frac{1}{100}+\frac{5}{1000}100015​=1001​+10005​

    Placing every piece in its column gives 111 one, 222 tenths, 111 hundredth and 555 thousandths, so the amount is 1.2151.2151.215.

    In expanded form,

    1.215=1+210+1100+510001.215=1+\frac{2}{10}+\frac{1}{100}+\frac{5}{1000}1.215=1+102​+1001​+10005​

    As a check, count everything in thousandths.

    Twelve tenths are 120012001200 thousandths.

    1200+15=12151200+15=12151200+15=1215

    That is 121512151215 thousandths, which is 1.2151.2151.215 again.

    Answer

    1.2151.2151.215; 1+210+1100+510001+\frac{2}{10}+\frac{1}{100}+\frac{5}{1000}1+102​+1001​+10005​.

    Key idea

    Bundle ten parts into the next larger place before writing the decimal digits.

  6. Problem 6 Pat's fraction

    Pat writes 7.09=791007.09=\frac{79}{100}7.09=10079​, reading the digits 777 and 999 over one hundred. Is Pat correct? Write 7.097.097.09 as one fraction with denominator 100100100.

    1. Hint 1

      Before writing 7.097.097.09 as a fraction, compare the size of Pat's fraction with the size of 7.097.097.09.

    2. Hint 2

      Find how many hundredths the whole part contributes, then add the hundredths after the point.

    Answer

    No; 7.09=7091007.09=\frac{709}{100}7.09=100709​.

    Full solution

    Pat's fraction is 797979 hundredths, which is less than one whole.

    79100=0.79\frac{79}{100}=0.7910079​=0.79

    That cannot equal 7.097.097.09, which is more than seven.

    Pat dropped the placeholder zero and treated the 777 ones as 777 tenths.

    Each whole is one hundred hundredths, so the 777 ones contribute 700700700 hundredths.

    The digits after the point contribute 999 hundredths.

    700+9=709700+9=709700+9=709

    The number is therefore 709709709 hundredths.

    7.09=7091007.09=\frac{709}{100}7.09=100709​

    Answer

    No; 7.09=7091007.09=\frac{709}{100}7.09=100709​.

    Key idea

    One fraction over 100100100 for a decimal counts the hundredths in its whole part as well as those after the point.

  7. Problem 7 The four readings

    Four readings are 2.5082.5082.508, 258100\frac{258}{100}100258​, "222 ones, 555 tenths and 777 hundredths" and 2.50802.50802.5080. List them from least to greatest, keeping all four readings and marking any equal readings with ===.

    1. Hint 1

      Turn the fraction and the place description into decimals, so that all four readings share one form.

    2. Hint 2

      Extra zeros at the far right can make all four readings the same length without changing them.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare tenths first, then hundredths, then any further columns that are needed.

    Answer

    2.508=2.5080<2.508=2.5080<2.508=2.5080< "222 ones, 555 tenths and 777 hundredths" <258100<\frac{258}{100}<100258​; in decimals, 2.508=2.5080<2.57<2.582.508=2.5080<2.57<2.582.508=2.5080<2.57<2.58.

    Full solution

    The place description puts 555 in the tenths column and 777 in the hundredths column after the 222 ones, so it names 2.572.572.57.

    2+510+7100=2.572+\frac{5}{10}+\frac{7}{100}=2.572+105​+1007​=2.57

    The fraction counts 258258258 hundredths.

    Two hundred of them make two wholes, leaving 585858 hundredths.

    258100=2.58\frac{258}{100}=2.58100258​=2.58

    Pad all four readings to four places: 2.50802.50802.5080, 2.58002.58002.5800, 2.57002.57002.5700 and 2.50802.50802.5080.

    All four readings have 555 tenths, so the hundredths decide.

    The two versions of 2.5082.5082.508 have 000 hundredths, the place description has 777 and the fraction has 888.

    The two versions of 2.5082.5082.508 agree in every column, so they have the same value.

    From least to greatest, 2.5082.5082.508 and 2.50802.50802.5080 come first and are equal, then the place description, and the fraction 258100\frac{258}{100}100258​ comes last.

    Answer

    2.508=2.5080<2.508=2.5080<2.508=2.5080< "222 ones, 555 tenths and 777 hundredths" <258100<\frac{258}{100}<100258​; in decimals, 2.508=2.5080<2.57<2.582.508=2.5080<2.57<2.582.508=2.5080<2.57<2.58.

    Key idea

    Trailing zeros preserve equality while the first differing place settles an order.

  8. Problem 8 The token exchange

    Each small token is worth one thousandth of a credit, and each large token is worth one hundredth of a credit. Kai has 333 large tokens and 555 small tokens. He trades each large token for ten small tokens and says that, because he now has more tokens, they are worth more. Is Kai correct? Give the value of his tokens before and after the trade as fractions of a credit.

    1. Hint 1

      Decide what one large token is worth in small tokens before comparing anything.

    2. Hint 2

      Write both totals as fractions of a credit with denominator 100010001000, counting each large token as ten thousandths.

    Answer

    No; his tokens are worth 351000\frac{35}{1000}100035​ of a credit, or 7200\frac{7}{200}2007​, both before and after the trade.

    Full solution

    Before the trade, the 333 large tokens are worth 333 hundredths, which is 303030 thousandths, and the 555 small tokens are worth 555 thousandths.

    301000+51000=351000\frac{30}{1000}+\frac{5}{1000}=\frac{35}{1000}100030​+10005​=100035​

    One large token is worth ten small ones, since ten thousandths make one hundredth.

    101000=1100\frac{10}{1000}=\frac{1}{100}100010​=1001​

    Trading the 333 large tokens gives 303030 small tokens, so Kai ends with 353535 small tokens.

    35×11000=35100035\times\frac{1}{1000}=\frac{35}{1000}35×10001​=100035​

    The value is the same before and after the trade.

    Kai has more tokens, but the 303030 that replaced his large tokens are each worth one tenth of a large token, so the total is unchanged and he is not correct.

    In lowest terms the total is 7200\frac{7}{200}2007​, since dividing 353535 and 100010001000 by 555 gives 777 and 200200200.

    Answer

    No; his tokens are worth 351000\frac{35}{1000}100035​ of a credit, or 7200\frac{7}{200}2007​, both before and after the trade.

    Key idea

    Trading one hundredth for ten thousandths changes how many pieces there are but not the total, because each new piece is worth one tenth as much.

  9. Problem 9 The extra zero

    A display shows 8.058.058.05. One extra zero is to be inserted after the decimal point, in one of three positions: immediately after the point, between the 000 and the 555, or after the 555. Give the number each choice produces, and say which choices keep the value.

    1. Hint 1

      Write 8.058.058.05 in expanded form first, so you know what the 555 is worth before any zero is added.

    2. Hint 2

      After each insertion, write the digits in their columns and read what the 555 is worth.

    Answer

    Immediately after the point: 8.0058.0058.005; between the 000 and the 555: 8.0058.0058.005; after the 555: 8.0508.0508.050. Only the zero after the 555 keeps the value; the other two give the smaller 8.0058.0058.005.

    Full solution

    In 8.058.058.05 the 555 sits in the hundredths column, so it is worth five hundredths.

    8.05=8+51008.05=8+\frac{5}{100}8.05=8+1005​

    A zero inserted immediately after the point, or between the 000 and the 555, leaves two zeros before the 555.

    Both choices give 8.0058.0058.005, where the 555 has moved into the thousandths column and is worth only five thousandths.

    A zero inserted after the 555 gives 8.0508.0508.050.

    It is a trailing zero, and the 555 stays in the hundredths column.

    So 8.0508.0508.050 has the same value as 8.058.058.05, while the other two choices give a smaller number.

    8.005<8.0508.005<8.0508.005<8.050

    Answer

    Immediately after the point: 8.0058.0058.005; between the 000 and the 555: 8.0058.0058.005; after the 555: 8.0508.0508.050. Only the zero after the 555 keeps the value; the other two give the smaller 8.0058.0058.005.

    Key idea

    After the decimal point, a zero inserted before a nonzero digit pushes that digit one place to the right, while a zero added after the last digit leaves the value unchanged.

  10. Problem 10 The narrow gap

    Mia says that every terminating decimal strictly between 3.213.213.21 and 3.223.223.22 needs at least three places after the point once all trailing zeros are removed. Is she correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Ask whether a number ending at the hundredths column could fit between the two endpoints.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the endpoints in thousandths to see where an extra place could fit.

    Answer

    Yes.

    Full solution

    A decimal ending at the hundredths column counts whole hundredths.

    The endpoints are 321321321 hundredths and 322322322 hundredths, with no whole count of hundredths between them.

    A shorter decimal can also be written in hundredths by padding it with zeros, so it does not create another possibility.

    An additional place does allow values in the gap.

    For example, 3.2153.2153.215 has more thousandths than 3.2103.2103.210.

    3.210<3.2153.210<3.2153.210<3.215

    It has fewer hundredths than 3.2203.2203.220.

    3.215<3.2203.215<3.2203.215<3.220

    Thus a decimal in the gap must retain at least three decimal places after its trailing zeros are removed.

    Answer

    Yes.

    Key idea

    A terminating decimal strictly between two neighboring hundredths needs a digit beyond the hundredths place.
