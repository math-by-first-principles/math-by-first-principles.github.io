Operations with Signed Rational Numbers

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

A winter thermometer reads −2.5-2.5−2.5 degrees. Owing a friend three quarters of a dollar gives a balance of −34-\tfrac{3}{4}−43​. Signs are not just for whole numbers.

This lesson joins the integer sign rules to the fraction and decimal methods. We decide the sign of a result, then calculate its size. Zero still needs special care: it cannot be a divisor.

Learning goals

  • Write a negative fraction or decimal as a fraction of two integers with a nonzero denominator
  • Compare and order signed fractions and decimals by their positions on the number line
  • Add and subtract signed rational numbers by deciding the sign, then working out the size
  • Multiply and divide signed rational numbers, and explain why zero can never be the divisor
  • Explain why −34\frac{-3}{4}4−3​, 3−4\frac{3}{-4}−43​ and −34-\frac{3}{4}−43​ all name the same number

Fractions and decimals on both sides of zero

The fraction 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​ sits three quarters of a unit to the right of zero. Its opposite, −34-\tfrac{3}{4}−43​, sits the same distance to the left, between −1-1−1 and 000. In the same way −2.5-2.5−2.5 is the opposite of 2.52.52.5, halfway between −3-3−3 and −2-2−2. A negative fraction or decimal is the mirror image of a positive one across zero, exactly as −5-5−5 mirrors 555.

Familiar numbers can be written as fractions of integers: 1.5=321.5=\tfrac{3}{2}1.5=23​ and −2=−21-2=\tfrac{-2}{1}−2=1−2​. Four copies of −34-\tfrac{3}{4}−43​ give −3-3−3. Division −3÷4-3\div4−3÷4 asks for the number that, multiplied by 444, gives −3-3−3. Thus

−34=−34.\frac{-3}{4}=-\frac{3}{4}.4−3​=−43​.

A rational number can be written as ab\tfrac{a}{b}ba​, where aaa and bbb are integers and b≠0b\ne0b=0. The bar means division, and dividing by zero never gives one definite number. Several familiar kinds meet this definition:

  • Integers: −7=−71-7=\tfrac{-7}{1}−7=1−7​.
  • Zero: 0=010=\tfrac{0}{1}0=10​. It is neither positive nor negative.
  • Terminating decimals: −2.5=−2510=−52-2.5=\tfrac{-25}{10}=\tfrac{-5}{2}−2.5=10−25​=2−5​.
  • Decimals obtained from fractions: −0.3‾=−13-0.\overline{3}=-\tfrac{1}{3}−0.3=−31​.

Algebra I shows that every repeating decimal is a fraction, even when it was not originally found by division. For calculations here, use the fraction: a finite approximation such as −0.33-0.33−0.33 is not exactly −13-\tfrac{1}{3}−31​.

Comparing and ordering

The number farther right is greater. Every negative number is less than zero and every positive number:

−18<0<0.01.-\frac{1}{8}<0<0.01.−81​<0<0.01.

Absolute value is distance from zero: ∣−0.6∣=0.6\lvert -0.6 \rvert = 0.6∣−0.6∣=0.6. For two negatives, the greater distance puts the number farther left. Since 0.6>0.450.6>0.450.6>0.45,

−0.6<−0.45.-0.6<-0.45.−0.6<−0.45.

For numbers in different forms, choose a common exact form. Compare −23-\tfrac{2}{3}−32​ and −0.6-0.6−0.6 by writing 0.6=350.6=\tfrac{3}{5}0.6=53​. Their sizes over a common denominator are 1015\tfrac{10}{15}1510​ and 915\tfrac{9}{15}159​. The first is farther left, so −23<−0.6-\tfrac{2}{3}<-0.6−32​<−0.6.

Adding and subtracting: the sign, then the size

Number-line steps can be fractions of a unit. Start at 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​ and add −56-\tfrac{5}{6}−65​: move five sixths left. The first half-unit reaches zero; the rest, 56−12=13\tfrac{5}{6}-\tfrac{1}{2}=\tfrac{1}{3}65​−21​=31​, takes you to −13-\tfrac{1}{3}−31​.

That separates two questions: which side of zero, and how far from zero? The sign rules answer the first; fraction or decimal arithmetic answers the second.

Use the same addition rules as for integers:

  • Same signs: add the sizes and keep the shared sign.
  • Different signs: subtract the smaller size from the larger; use the sign of the number farther from zero.
  • Equal sizes, opposite signs: the sum is zero.
  • Subtraction: add the opposite, a−b=a+(−b)a-b=a+(-b)a−b=a+(−b).

These rules also explain a fraction difference below zero. For 14−23\tfrac{1}{4}-\tfrac{2}{3}41​−32​, the sizes over twelfths are 312\tfrac{3}{12}123​ and 812\tfrac{8}{12}128​. The larger size belongs to the negative term:

14−23=312−812=−512.\frac{1}{4}-\frac{2}{3}=\frac{3}{12}-\frac{8}{12}=-\frac{5}{12}.41​−32​=123​−128​=−125​.

Worked example 1 A temperature of −2.5-2.5−2.5 degrees rises by 0.750.750.75 degrees. What does it read now?

A rise adds, so the new reading is −2.5+0.75-2.5 + 0.75−2.5+0.75.

The signs differ, so subtract the smaller size from the larger. Line up the points, padding 2.52.52.5 with a zero:

2.50−0.75=1.75.2.50 - 0.75 = 1.75.2.50−0.75=1.75.

The number −2.5-2.5−2.5 is farther from zero, so the answer takes its sign:

−2.5+0.75=−1.75.-2.5 + 0.75 = -1.75.−2.5+0.75=−1.75.

The thermometer reads −1.75-1.75−1.75 degrees. That makes sense: the temperature went up, but by less than it needed to reach zero.

Worked example 2 You owe 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​ of a dollar and borrow 25\tfrac{2}{5}52​ of a dollar more. What is your balance?

Owing 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​ of a dollar is a balance of −34-\tfrac{3}{4}−43​. Borrowing more adds to what you owe, so your balance with your friend goes down, and the new balance is −34−25-\tfrac{3}{4} - \tfrac{2}{5}−43​−52​.

Subtracting is adding the opposite:

−34−25=−34+(−25).-\frac{3}{4} - \frac{2}{5} = -\frac{3}{4} + \left(-\frac{2}{5}\right).−43​−52​=−43​+(−52​).

Now both numbers are negative, so add the sizes and keep the minus sign. Over the common denominator 202020, the sizes are 1520\tfrac{15}{20}2015​ and 820\tfrac{8}{20}208​:

1520+820=2320.\frac{15}{20} + \frac{8}{20} = \frac{23}{20}.2015​+208​=2023​.

So the balance is −2320-\tfrac{23}{20}−2023​ of a dollar, which is −1320-1\tfrac{3}{20}−1203​, the opposite of 13201\tfrac{3}{20}1203​ (the minus sign covers the whole mixed number). In decimals the same sum is −0.75−0.40=−1.15-0.75 - 0.40 = -1.15−0.75−0.40=−1.15, and 2320=1.15\tfrac{23}{20} = 1.152023​=1.15, so the two methods agree. You owe 1.151.151.15 dollars.

Worked example 3 Compute −0.4+23-0.4 + \tfrac{2}{3}−0.4+32​

The fraction 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ repeats as a decimal. Use fractions to keep the calculation exact: −0.4=−410=−25-0.4=-\tfrac{4}{10}=-\tfrac{2}{5}−0.4=−104​=−52​.

The signs differ. Over the common denominator 151515, the sizes are 615\tfrac{6}{15}156​ and 1015\tfrac{10}{15}1510​, so subtract the smaller from the larger:

1015−615=415.\frac{10}{15} - \frac{6}{15} = \frac{4}{15}.1510​−156​=154​.

The number 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ is farther from zero, so the answer is positive:

−0.4+23=415.-0.4 + \frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{15}.−0.4+32​=154​.

Rounding 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ to 0.670.670.67 first would give 0.270.270.27. That is close to 415\tfrac{4}{15}154​, which is about 0.2670.2670.267, but it is not equal to it. The fraction is the exact answer.

Multiplying and dividing

Multiplying two negatives was where the integers chapter needed its most careful argument.

The integer sign rules still hold for fractions and decimals#

Start with 35\tfrac{3}{5}53​ of a leftward step of size 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​. The result is still leftward, with size 35×23=25\tfrac{3}{5}\times\tfrac{2}{3}=\tfrac{2}{5}53​×32​=52​:

35×(−23)=−25.\frac{3}{5}\times\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)=-\frac{2}{5}.53​×(−32​)=−52​.

Swapping factors preserves the product, so (−23)×35=−25(-\tfrac{2}{3})\times\tfrac{3}{5}=-\tfrac{2}{5}(−32​)×53​=−52​ too.

For two negatives, use the zero pair 35+(−35)=0\tfrac{3}{5}+(-\tfrac{3}{5})=053​+(−53​)=0:

(−23)×(35+(−35))=0.\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\times \left(\frac{3}{5}+\left(-\frac{3}{5}\right)\right)=0.(−32​)×(53​+(−53​))=0.

Distribute and use the product we already found:

−25+(−23)×(−35)=0.-\frac{2}{5}+\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\times\left(-\frac{3}{5}\right)=0.−52​+(−32​)×(−53​)=0.

The second product must be 25\tfrac{2}{5}52​ to cancel −25-\tfrac{2}{5}−52​. Thus two negatives give a positive.

Division asks for a missing factor. Since 35×(−23)=−25\tfrac{3}{5}\times(-\tfrac{2}{3})=-\tfrac{2}{5}53​×(−32​)=−52​, we have

(−25)÷(−23)=35.\left(-\frac{2}{5}\right)\div\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)=\frac{3}{5}.(−52​)÷(−32​)=53​.

The reasoning does not depend on these particular fractions. A positive multiple of a leftward step stays leftward. Multiplying a zero pair and distributing forces the product of two negatives to cancel the negative product, so it is positive. Division then inherits the sign rules through its missing factor. Terminating decimals are fractions too, so the argument includes them.

∎

For two nonzero signed numbers, matching signs give a positive product or quotient; different signs give a negative one. Calculate the size with the usual fraction or decimal method.

For a product of nonzero factors, an even number of negatives gives a positive result; an odd number gives a negative result. Each pair of negatives gives a positive. Evaluate grouped sums before counting signs.

To divide by a nonzero fraction, multiply by its reciprocal. The reciprocal keeps its sign:

(−23)×(−32)=1.\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)\times\left(-\frac{3}{2}\right)=1.(−32​)×(−23​)=1.

For a left-to-right chain of multiplication and division, first ensure every divisor is nonzero. Replace each division with multiplication by the divisor’s reciprocal; then the same sign count applies.

Worked example 4 Compute −49÷(−23)-\tfrac{4}{9} \div \left(-\tfrac{2}{3}\right)−94​÷(−32​)

Settle the sign first. Both numbers are negative, so the signs match and the quotient is positive.

Now the size. Dividing by 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ means multiplying by its reciprocal 32\tfrac{3}{2}23​:

49×32=1218=23.\frac{4}{9} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{12}{18} = \frac{2}{3}.94​×23​=1812​=32​.

So −49÷(−23)=23-\tfrac{4}{9} \div \left(-\tfrac{2}{3}\right) = \tfrac{2}{3}−94​÷(−32​)=32​. Check by multiplying back, which is what division means:

23×(−23)=−49.✓\frac{2}{3} \times \left(-\frac{2}{3}\right) = -\frac{4}{9}. \checkmark32​×(−32​)=−94​.✓

Worked example 5 Compute (−0.25)×83×(−1.5)(-0.25) \times \tfrac{8}{3} \times (-1.5)(−0.25)×38​×(−1.5)

No factor is zero, so count the negative factors. There are two, −0.25-0.25−0.25 and −1.5-1.5−1.5, an even count, so the product is positive.

For the size, write the decimals as fractions so that all three factors are in one form: 0.25=140.25 = \tfrac{1}{4}0.25=41​ and 1.5=321.5 = \tfrac{3}{2}1.5=23​. Then

14×83×32=2424=1.\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{8}{3} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{24}{24} = 1.41​×38​×23​=2424​=1.

So (−0.25)×83×(−1.5)=1(-0.25) \times \tfrac{8}{3} \times (-1.5) = 1(−0.25)×38​×(−1.5)=1. The decimal 0.250.250.25 ends, so you could keep it, but 83\tfrac{8}{3}38​ repeats as a decimal. That is why fractions are the better form here.

Zero needs separate treatment. The integer chapter’s missing-factor reasoning still applies to fractions and decimals:

  • A zero factor makes a product zero: (−58)×0=0(-\tfrac{5}{8})\times0=0(−85​)×0=0.
  • Zero divided by a nonzero number is zero: 0÷(−2.5)=00\div(-2.5)=00÷(−2.5)=0.
  • A nonzero number divided by zero is undefined: for (−2.5)÷0(-2.5)\div0(−2.5)÷0, no number multiplied by zero can give −2.5-2.5−2.5.
  • 0÷00\div00÷0 is also undefined: every number multiplied by zero gives zero, so there is no unique quotient.
  • Zero has no reciprocal, because no number multiplied by zero gives one.

Where the minus sign goes

So far −34-\tfrac{3}{4}−43​ has been written with its minus sign in front, and also as −34\tfrac{-3}{4}4−3​. A third spelling puts the sign in the denominator, 3−4\tfrac{3}{-4}−43​.

Why −34\frac{-3}{4}4−3​, 3−4\frac{3}{-4}−43​ and −34-\frac{3}{4}−43​ are one number#

In −34\tfrac{-3}{4}4−3​, the signs differ, so the quotient is negative with size 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​. In 3−4\tfrac{3}{-4}−43​, the signs also differ and the size is unchanged. Both name the opposite of 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​:

−34=3−4=−34.\frac{-3}{4}=\frac{3}{-4}=-\frac{3}{4}.4−3​=−43​=−43​.

Nothing in this reasoning depended on 333 and 444. For any integers aaa and bbb, with b≠0b\ne0b=0, negating either the numerator or the denominator reverses the quotient’s sign without changing its size. If a=0a=0a=0, all the values are zero. Thus

−ab=a−b=−ab.\frac{-a}{b}=\frac{a}{-b}=-\frac{a}{b}.b−a​=−ba​=−ba​.

∎

Negating both parts leaves the quotient unchanged: −3−4=34\tfrac{-3}{-4}=\tfrac{3}{4}−4−3​=43​. A nonzero fraction is negative exactly when its numerator and denominator have different signs. A zero numerator gives zero, which is neither positive nor negative.

Writing the sign in front, or in the numerator with a positive denominator, is a useful convention. An equivalent form with a negative denominator is still valid.

Parentheses keep two signs from touching. A negative number that follows an operation sign is usually written in parentheses, as in 2.5−(−0.75)2.5 - (-0.75)2.5−(−0.75) or 3×(−12)3 \times \left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)3×(−21​). A minus sign in front of parentheses means the opposite of everything inside, so −(−34)=34-\left(-\tfrac{3}{4}\right) = \tfrac{3}{4}−(−43​)=43​. Work out the inside first: −(0.5−2)=−(−1.5)=1.5-(0.5 - 2) = -(-1.5) = 1.5−(0.5−2)=−(−1.5)=1.5.

The same idea governs a negative mixed number. The sign belongs to the whole mixed number, so −212-2\tfrac{1}{2}−221​ is the opposite of 2122\tfrac{1}{2}221​:

−212=−(2+12)=−52.-2\tfrac{1}{2} = -\left(2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = -\frac{5}{2}.−221​=−(2+21​)=−25​.

It does not mean −2+12-2 + \tfrac{1}{2}−2+21​, which is −112-1\tfrac{1}{2}−121​. On the number line, −212-2\tfrac{1}{2}−221​ lies half a unit to the left of −2-2−2, not to its right.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Reading a negative mixed number as a sum. −212-2\tfrac{1}{2}−221​ means −(2+12)-\left(2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)−(2+21​), which is −52-\tfrac{5}{2}−25​. It is not −2+12-2 + \tfrac{1}{2}−2+21​, which is −112-1\tfrac{1}{2}−121​.
  • Comparing two negatives by their sizes. 0.6>0.450.6 > 0.450.6>0.45, but −0.6<−0.45-0.6 < -0.45−0.6<−0.45, because −0.6-0.6−0.6 is farther left. Between two negatives, the one with the larger absolute value is the smaller.
  • Changing the sign when taking a reciprocal. The reciprocal of −23-\tfrac{2}{3}−32​ is −32-\tfrac{3}{2}−23​, not 32\tfrac{3}{2}23​. Their product must be 111, which is positive.
  • Treating a minus sign on top and bottom as negative. −3−4\tfrac{-3}{-4}−4−3​ is 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​, because the signs match.
  • Rounding a repeating decimal and then writing ===. −0.4+0.67-0.4 + 0.67−0.4+0.67 is 0.270.270.27, but −0.4+23-0.4 + \tfrac{2}{3}−0.4+32​ is exactly 415\tfrac{4}{15}154​. Use the fraction to get the exact answer.

Key takeaways

  • A rational number can be written as a fraction of two integers with a nonzero denominator. Integers, decimals that end, and the repeating decimals of fractions are all rational.
  • On the number line, the number farther right is the greater. Between two negatives, the one with the larger absolute value is the smaller.
  • A signed calculation splits into a sign, decided by the integer rules, and a size, worked out with fraction or decimal methods. A size of zero gives the answer zero.
  • −34=3−4=−34\tfrac{-3}{4} = \tfrac{3}{-4} = -\tfrac{3}{4}4−3​=−43​=−43​, while −3−4=34\tfrac{-3}{-4} = \tfrac{3}{4}−4−3​=43​. A reciprocal keeps the sign of the fraction it came from.
  • Zero is rational and neither positive nor negative. A product with a zero factor is zero, and dividing by zero is still undefined.

A bit of history (optional)

The words rational and reason belong to the same family. Their Latin root, ratio, could mean a reckoning or an account as well as reason. The related word rationalis could even name an accountant or treasury official. These older uses appear in the Lewis and Short Latin dictionary entries for ratio and rationalis.

The word traveled through French into English as reason. In a manuscript of Ancrene Riwle dated about 1225, it appeared as reisun. John Broome, citing the Oxford English Dictionary, dates the first recorded English use of rational to 1398, when it meant having the faculty of reason.

Ancient mathematical comparisons also looked different from ours. In his Elements, Euclid described a ratio between magnitudes of the same kind, such as two lengths. That historical setting was not yet our modern collection of signed rational numbers.
