Decimals: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Decimal point
The mark between whole-number and fractional places. Ones are immediately left; tenths immediately right. The point is not itself a place.
Expanded form
A sum of each digit times its place value: 6.209=6+210+910006.209=6+\tfrac{2}{10}+\tfrac{9}{1000}6.209=6+102​+10009​.
Terminating decimal
A decimal whose digits end. In long division, remainder zero ends the calculation: 716=0.4375\tfrac{7}{16}=0.4375167​=0.4375.
Repeating decimal, 0.3‾0.\overline{3}0.3
A decimal that never ends and eventually repeats a fixed block. The bar marks only that block: 0.16‾=0.1666…0.1\overline{6}=0.1666\ldots0.16=0.1666….
Dividend and divisor
In a÷ba\div ba÷b, aaa is the dividend and bbb the divisor. The divisor must be nonzero.
Rounding place and deciding digit
Rounding to a place replaces a number with the nearest multiple of that place's value (an exact tie goes to the larger neighbor: round half up). The rounding place is the place requested. For a nonnegative (positive or zero) decimal that ends, the deciding digit is immediately to its right.
Estimation
Using simpler nearby numbers to calculate an approximate result or check the size of an answer.
Front-end estimation
Keeping the leading digit and replacing the rest with zeros. For nonnegative inputs, the estimated sum is never more than the exact sum: 381+264381+264381+264 becomes 300+200=500300+200=500300+200=500. Front-rounding instead rounds to one nonzero digit.
Rational number
A number expressible as ab\tfrac{a}{b}ba​ for integers a,ba,ba,b with b≠0b\ne0b=0. Integers, terminating decimals, and the repeating decimals of fractions qualify. For example, −2.5=−52-2.5=\tfrac{-5}{2}−2.5=2−5​. Zero is rational, 0=010=\tfrac{0}{1}0=10​, and neither positive nor negative.
Opposite, −x-x−x
The number equally far from zero on the other side; zero is its own opposite. Before parentheses, the minus sign applies to the entire result inside.

Formulas and theorems

  • Place values to the right of the point

    1,110,1100,11000,…1, \quad \frac{1}{10}, \quad \frac{1}{100}, \quad \frac{1}{1000}, \quad \ldots1,101​,1001​,10001​,…
    Text description

    A place value chart for 6.209 naming ones, tenths, hundredths and thousandths, with each place worth one tenth of the place to its left.

    Use when Each place is one tenth of its left neighbor. To the right of the point: tenths, hundredths, thousandths.

    e.g. In 0.4040.4040.404, the first 444 contributes 0.40.40.4, which is 100100100 times the contribution of the final 444, 0.0040.0040.004.

  • Decimal to fraction by place

    0.7=7100.49=491000.013=131000\begin{gathered} 0.7 = \frac{7}{10} \\ 0.49 = \frac{49}{100} \\ 0.013 = \frac{13}{1000} \end{gathered}0.7=107​0.49=10049​0.013=100013​​

    Use when For the fractional part of a terminating decimal, use one denominator zero per digit after the point, then reduce by the GCF. Include any whole part: 2.6=2352.6=2\tfrac{3}{5}2.6=253​.

  • Reading and writing a decimal in words

    Read the whole part, say "and", then read the digits after the point as a whole number and name their last place.

    Use when For terminating decimals. The last place supplies the denominator: 0.410.410.41 is "forty-one hundredths." From words, include placeholder zeros as needed: "two and seven hundredths" is 2.072.072.07.

  • Trailing zeros versus placeholder zeros

    0.3=0.30=0.3000.3 = 0.30 = 0.3000.3=0.30=0.300

    Use when Appending zeros after the last decimal place leaves all existing digits in place. Appending to a whole number instead changes it: 3≠303\ne303=30. Removing an internal placeholder shifts later digits and can change the value: 1.205≠1.251.205\ne1.251.205=1.25.

  • Adding and subtracting decimals

    9.40− 2.656.75\begin{array}{r} 9.40 \\ -\ 2.65 \\ \hline 6.75 \end{array}9.40− 2.656.75​​

    Use when Align the points and pad with trailing zeros. Add or subtract from the right, carrying or borrowing in equal units. For signed numbers, first use the signed-number rules; when subtracting sizes, put the larger size first.

  • Multiplying decimals: the places add

    places in the product=places in one factor+places in the other\begin{gathered} \text{places in the product} \\ = \text{places in one factor} \\ + \text{places in the other} \end{gathered}places in the product=places in one factor+places in the other​

    Use when For two terminating decimals: multiply as whole numbers, then place the point using both factors. Add leading placeholder zeros if needed. Remove trailing zeros only afterward: 0.06×0.5=0.030=0.030.06\times0.5=0.030=0.030.06×0.5=0.030=0.03.

    e.g. 12×35=42012\times35=42012×35=420 gives 1.2×0.35=0.4201.2\times0.35=0.4201.2×0.35=0.420, with 1+2=31+2=31+2=3 places before trimming.

  • Multiplying or dividing by 101010, 100100100, 100010001000

    0.048×100=4.852.7÷1000=0.0527\begin{gathered} 0.048 \times 100 = 4.8 \\ 52.7 \div 1000 = 0.0527 \end{gathered}0.048×100=4.852.7÷1000=0.0527​

    Use when Move the point one place per zero in 101010, 100100100, or 100010001000: right to multiply, left to divide. Fill any empty places with zeros.

  • Dividing by a decimal: shift both

    ab=a×10b×10\frac{a}{b}=\frac{a\times10}{b\times10}ba​=b×10a×10​

    Use when The divisor bbb must be nonzero. For a terminating divisor, shift both points equally until the divisor is whole, then divide. A whole divisor needs no shift. Add trailing zeros to the dividend to continue dividing.

    e.g. 6.5÷0.13=650÷13=506.5\div0.13=650\div13=506.5÷0.13=650÷13=50.

  • Fraction to decimal: the bar means divide

    ab=a÷b\frac{a}{b} = a \div bba​=a÷b

    Use when For integers a,ba,ba,b with b≠0b\ne0b=0, divide numerator by denominator. Continue with smaller decimal units until the remainder is zero or a remainder returns; a repeated remainder forces the following digits to repeat.

    e.g. 716=7÷16=0.4375\tfrac{7}{16} = 7 \div 16 = 0.4375167​=7÷16=0.4375.

  • Scaling shortcut to a decimal

    ab=a×kb×k\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \times k}{b \times k}ba​=b×ka×k​

    Use when Reduce first and use a positive denominator bbb. A positive whole kkk can make b×kb\times kb×k equal 101010, 100100100, 100010001000, or another 111 followed by zeros only when bbb divides one of those numbers. Read the resulting fraction by its decimal places.

    e.g. 1950=38100=0.38\tfrac{19}{50} = \tfrac{38}{100} = 0.385019​=10038​=0.38.

  • Terminating test

    A fraction in lowest terms terminates exactly when its positive denominator has no prime factors other than 222 and 555.

    Use when Reduce first. A surviving prime other than 222 or 555 gives a repeating decimal. A denominator of 111 has no prime factors and also terminates.

    e.g. 1435=25\tfrac{14}{35}=\tfrac{2}{5}3514​=52​ terminates, even though the unreduced denominator has a factor of 777.

  • The rounding rule

    Under round half up, a deciding digit below 555 keeps the rounding place unchanged; 555 or above increases it by one.

    Use when For nonnegative terminating decimals. Carry through nines and keep the requested place. A deciding 555 followed only by zeros is an exact tie; nonzero later digits put it beyond halfway. Round half up sends either case upward.

    e.g. 7.857.857.85 to the nearest tenth is 7.97.97.9, and 19.9819.9819.98 to the nearest tenth is 20.020.020.0.

  • Which numbers round to a given value

    L−u2  ≤  x  <  L+u2L - \frac{u}{2} \;\le\; x \;<\; L + \frac{u}{2}L−2u​≤x<L+2u​

    Use when The input xxx is nonnegative, the rounding unit uuu is positive, and LLL is a nonnegative multiple of uuu. Under round half up, include the lower midpoint and exclude the upper one. If the lower bound is negative, start at zero.

  • Adding and subtracting signed numbers: the sign, then the size

    Same signs: add sizes and keep the sign. Different signs: subtract the smaller size from the larger, taking the sign of the number farther from zero. Subtraction: add the opposite, a−b=a+(−b)a-b=a+(-b)a−b=a+(−b).

    Use when For rational numbers. Adding zero changes nothing; equal opposite sizes cancel to zero. Use fractions to keep repeating values exact.

    e.g. −14−(−34)=−14+34=12-\tfrac{1}{4}-(-\tfrac{3}{4})=-\tfrac{1}{4}+\tfrac{3}{4}=\tfrac{1}{2}−41​−(−43​)=−41​+43​=21​.

  • Multiplying and dividing signed numbers: count the negatives

    For two nonzero numbers, matching signs give a positive product or quotient; different signs give a negative one. In a product with no zero factors, an even number of negative factors gives positive, and an odd number gives negative.

    Use when Evaluate grouped sums first and check every divisor is nonzero. In a left-to-right multiplication/division chain, replace division with multiplication by the reciprocal, which keeps its sign. Then count negative factors.

  • Zero as a factor, a dividend, and a divisor

    x×0=00÷x=0(x≠0)x÷0 is undefined\begin{gathered} x \times 0 = 0 \\ 0 \div x = 0 \quad (x \neq 0) \\ x \div 0 \text{ is undefined} \end{gathered}x×0=00÷x=0(x=0)x÷0 is undefined​

    Use when Any rational xxx, so 0÷00 \div 00÷0 is undefined too. Zero has no reciprocal.

  • Where the minus sign goes

    −ab=a−b=−ab−a−b=ab\begin{gathered} \frac{-a}{b} = \frac{a}{-b} = -\frac{a}{b} \\ \frac{-a}{-b} = \frac{a}{b} \end{gathered}b−a​=−ba​=−ba​−b−a​=ba​​

    Use when Integers aaa and bbb with b≠0b \neq 0b=0; when a=0a = 0a=0 every form is 000. A nonzero fraction is negative exactly when its top and bottom have opposite signs.

Problem types, step by step

Read a decimal: place, value, expanded form, or words

  1. Locate each digit by its column. Its place is the column name; its value is the digit times that place value.
  2. For expanded form, add those values, omitting zero terms. To read aloud, use the name of the last decimal place.

e.g. 6.209=6+210+910006.209 = 6 + \tfrac{2}{10} + \tfrac{9}{1000}6.209=6+102​+10009​, and its 999 has value 91000\tfrac{9}{1000}10009​.

Compare or order a list of values

  1. Separate negatives, zero, and positives. Among two negatives, greater absolute value means the smaller number.
  2. Choose a common exact form. Terminating decimals can be compared as decimals; use common-denominator fractions when repeating values would otherwise be rounded.
  3. For terminating decimals, pad with trailing zeros and compare from the left. The first differing place decides.

Convert a decimal that ends to a fraction in lowest terms

  1. Put the fractional digits over 111 followed by one zero for each decimal place.
  2. Reduce by the GCF and include any whole-number part.

e.g. 0.85=85100=17200.85 = \tfrac{85}{100} = \tfrac{17}{20}0.85=10085​=2017​.

Convert a fraction to a decimal

  1. Reduce, then look for a convenient denominator such as 101010, 100100100, or 100010001000.
  2. If possible, scale both parts equally to that denominator and read the decimal.
  3. Otherwise divide numerator by denominator. Stop at remainder zero, or mark a repeating block once the same remainder returns.

e.g. 1320=65100=0.65\tfrac{13}{20}=\tfrac{65}{100}=0.652013​=10065​=0.65.

Decide whether a fraction terminates or repeats, without dividing

  1. Reduce to lowest terms and prime factorize the positive denominator.
  2. Only twos and fives, or denominator one, means the decimal ends. Any other surviving prime means it repeats.

e.g. 730\tfrac{7}{30}307​ repeats because 30=2×3×530=2\times3\times530=2×3×5 still contains a 333.

Add or subtract two decimals that end

  1. Apply sign rules first if needed. Align decimal points and pad with trailing zeros.
  2. Add or subtract from the right, regrouping when needed. Keep the answer point aligned.

e.g. 5.6−2.34=5.60−2.34=3.265.6 - 2.34 = 5.60 - 2.34 = 3.265.6−2.34=5.60−2.34=3.26.

Multiply two decimals that end

  1. Multiply as whole numbers, temporarily ignoring the points.
  2. Add both factors' decimal-place counts.
  3. Place the point that many digits from the right, adding zeros if needed.

e.g. 0.25×0.30.25 \times 0.30.25×0.3: 25×3=7525 \times 3 = 7525×3=75 with 2+1=32 + 1 = 32+1=3 places, so 0.0750.0750.075.

Divide by a decimal that ends

  1. Check that the divisor is nonzero. Shift its point right until it becomes whole.
  2. Shift the dividend by the same number of places, adding zeros if needed.
  3. Divide with the quotient point above the shifted dividend point. Check using the original divisor.

e.g. 13.5÷0.05=1350÷5=27013.5 \div 0.05 = 1350 \div 5 = 27013.5÷0.05=1350÷5=270.

Round a nonnegative decimal that ends to a named place

  1. Locate the requested place and the digit immediately after it.
  2. Use round half up: below 555, keep the rounding digit; 555 or above, increase it.
  3. Carry if needed. Remove later decimal places, or replace later whole-number places with zeros.

e.g. 8.978.978.97 to the nearest tenth: the hundredths 777 rounds the 999 up, which carries to 9.09.09.0.

Find the numbers that round to a given value

  1. Find the midpoints half a rounding unit below and above the target.
  2. For nonnegative inputs under round half up, include the lower midpoint and exclude the upper.
  3. If asked to count whole numbers, use last minus first plus one.

e.g. 35≤n<4535\le n<4535≤n<45 rounds to 404040 to the nearest ten.

Estimate a calculation, or check an answer for reasonableness

  1. Round to convenient values, using the method requested.
  2. Calculate with those values and note that the result is approximate.
  3. Compare its size with the recorded answer. If the two differ by about a factor of ten, a hundred, or more, suspect a misplaced decimal point and recheck the exact work. A close estimate does not prove an exact result.

Solve a decimal word problem

  1. Choose the operation: combine totals, find a difference, multiply a rate by a count, or divide to share equally or find how many groups fit.
  2. Calculate with exact inputs. Estimate separately as a size check; round the final result only as requested. Report money to the nearest cent when the context requires cents.

Multiply or divide signed fractions and decimals

  1. Evaluate parentheses and check divisors first. Any zero divisor makes the expression undefined.
  2. With valid divisors, a zero factor or dividend makes a multiplication/division chain zero. Otherwise count negative factors after replacing divisions by reciprocals.
  3. Calculate the size, using exact fractions when a decimal repeats.

e.g. (−0.6)÷(−310)=2(-0.6)\div(-\tfrac{3}{10})=2(−0.6)÷(−103​)=2: matching signs give a positive quotient.

Solve a signed word problem

  1. Choose a reference and assign signs. A gain is positive; a loss is negative. A position below the reference can be negative, while its distance below is positive.
  2. A signed change is new value minus old value. Check that the resulting sign fits the story.

e.g. From −1.5-1.5−1.5 degrees to 2.252.252.25 degrees the change is 2.25−(−1.5)=3.752.25 - (-1.5) = 3.752.25−(−1.5)=3.75 degrees.

Exam traps

  • Trap Lining the points up to multiply, so 0.3×0.20.3 \times 0.20.3×0.2 comes out as 0.60.60.6.

    Fix Use alignment for addition and subtraction. For multiplication, count both factors' places: 0.3×0.2=0.060.3\times0.2=0.060.3×0.2=0.06.

  • Trap Ranking decimals by how many digits they show, so 0.450.450.45 looks bigger than 0.50.50.5.

    Fix Pad to equal length and read from the left: 0.450.450.45 against 0.500.500.50 is settled at the tenths, so 0.45<0.50.45 < 0.50.45<0.5.

  • Trap Omitting the zero in the tenths place, writing 0.25×0.3=0.750.25\times0.3=0.750.25×0.3=0.75.

    Fix The product needs three places. Put a zero before 757575 in the decimal places to get 0.0750.0750.075, not 0.750.750.75. The zero before the point makes the number easier to read; the tenths zero preserves its value.

  • Trap Sliding the point in the divisor only, turning 9.6÷0.049.6 \div 0.049.6÷0.04 into 9.6÷4=2.49.6 \div 4 = 2.49.6÷4=2.4.

    Fix Both points move the same number of places, so it is 960÷4=240960 \div 4 = 240960÷4=240. Moving one alone rescales the quotient, here by 100100100.

  • Trap Assuming multiplication always enlarges and division always shrinks.

    Fix A factor between 000 and 111 shrinks a positive number (0.4×0.4=0.160.4 \times 0.4 = 0.160.4×0.4=0.16), and dividing a positive number by one between 000 and 111 enlarges it (9.6÷0.04=2409.6 \div 0.04 = 2409.6÷0.04=240). A size check must allow both.

  • Trap Dividing bottom by top, computing 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​ as 4÷34 \div 34÷3.

    Fix Divide numerator by denominator: 3÷4=0.753\div4=0.753÷4=0.75. Reversing them computes a different fraction.

  • Trap Applying the 222s-and-555s test before reducing, calling 36\tfrac{3}{6}63​ a repeater on the strength of its 333.

    Fix Reduce first: 36=12\tfrac{3}{6} = \tfrac{1}{2}63​=21​, whose denominator is 222, so it terminates at 0.50.50.5.

  • Trap Rounding digit by digit from the right, so 4.4494.4494.449 to the nearest tenth becomes 4.54.54.5.

    Fix Round the original value to the requested place. The hundredths digit is 444, so 4.4494.4494.449 rounds directly to 4.44.44.4. Rounding in stages can change the result.

  • Trap Writing 13=0.33\tfrac{1}{3} = 0.3331​=0.33.

    Fix That is a rounded value, not an equality. The exact decimal is 0.3‾0.\overline{3}0.3, whose 333s never stop.

  • Trap Rounding a 999 in place, turning 2.962.962.96 to the nearest tenth into 2.102.102.10.

    Fix Ten tenths is one whole, so the carry lands in the ones: 3.03.03.0, keeping the trailing zero to show the tenths was the target.

  • Trap Rounding every input first when the question asks for an exact answer.

    Fix For an exact answer, keep the calculation and result exact. For a rounded answer, round only the final result. Round inputs first when the task asks for an estimate.

  • Trap Reading −314-3\tfrac{1}{4}−341​ as −3+14-3 + \tfrac{1}{4}−3+41​.

    Fix The sign covers the whole mixed number: −134-\tfrac{13}{4}−413​, not −234-2\tfrac{3}{4}−243​.

  • Trap Ordering negatives by size, so −0.8>−0.35-0.8 > -0.35−0.8>−0.35.

    Fix The larger absolute value lies farther left: −0.8<−0.35-0.8 < -0.35−0.8<−0.35.

  • Trap Flipping the sign with the reciprocal, sending −45-\tfrac{4}{5}−54​ to 54\tfrac{5}{4}45​.

    Fix A reciprocal keeps its sign, −54-\tfrac{5}{4}−45​, since the product must be 111.
