Decimals: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Decimal point
The mark separating the whole part from the fractional part. It is not itself a place: ones sits immediately left of it, tenths immediately right.
Expanded form
A decimal written as the sum of each digit times its place value: 6.209=6+210+910006.209 = 6 + \tfrac{2}{10} + \tfrac{9}{1000}6.209=6+102​+10009​.
Terminating decimal
A decimal whose digits stop, which is what happens when long division reaches a remainder of 000: 716=0.4375\tfrac{7}{16} = 0.4375167​=0.4375.
Repeating decimal, 0.3‾0.\overline{3}0.3
A decimal whose digits never stop but cycle through a fixed block. The overline marks the block, so 0.16‾0.1\overline{6}0.16 is 0.1666…0.1666\ldots0.1666….
Dividend and divisor
In a÷ba \div ba÷b, the dividend is aaa and the divisor is bbb.
Rounding place and deciding digit
Rounding to a place replaces a number with the nearest multiple of that place's value. The rounding place is the one named; for a nonnegative decimal that ends, the deciding digit is the single digit immediately to its right.
Estimation
Rounding the numbers of a calculation before computing, for a fast approximate answer and a size check on an exact one.
Front-end estimation
Keeping only each number's leading digit and zeroing the rest. For a sum it runs low, since chopping only discards value: 381+264≈500381 + 264 \approx 500381+264≈500, against 645645645.

Formulas and theorems

  • Place values to the right of the point

    1,110,1100,11000,…1, \quad \frac{1}{10}, \quad \frac{1}{100}, \quad \frac{1}{1000}, \quad \ldots1,101​,1001​,10001​,…
    Text description

    A place value chart for 6.209 naming ones, tenths, hundredths and thousandths, with each place worth one tenth of the place to its left.

    Use when On both sides of the point, every place is one tenth of the place to its left. First after the point is tenths, then hundredths, then thousandths.

    e.g. In 0.4040.4040.404 the left 444 is 410\tfrac{4}{10}104​ and the right 444 is 41000\tfrac{4}{1000}10004​, so the left is 100100100 times the right.

  • Decimal to fraction by place

    0.7=7100.49=491000.013=131000\begin{gathered} 0.7 = \frac{7}{10} \\ 0.49 = \frac{49}{100} \\ 0.013 = \frac{13}{1000} \end{gathered}0.7=107​0.49=10049​0.013=100013​​

    Use when Terminating decimals only. Digits after the point equal zeros in the denominator, and those digits are the numerator; then reduce by the GCF. A whole part stays put, so 2.6=2352.6 = 2\tfrac{3}{5}2.6=253​.

  • Reading and writing a decimal in words

    For a decimal that ends: read the whole part, say "and" at the point, read the digits after it as one whole number, then name the place of the LAST digit.

    Use when That last place names the denominator for the whole string, so 0.410.410.41 is "forty-one hundredths". From words, the named place fixes how many digits follow the point, sometimes needing a placeholder zero: "two and seven hundredths" is 2.072.072.07, not 2.72.72.7.

  • Trailing zeros versus placeholder zeros

    0.3=0.30=0.3000.3 = 0.30 = 0.3000.3=0.30=0.300

    Use when Zeros PAST THE POINT and right of the last nonzero digit only: a trailing zero on a whole number is a place, so 3≠303 \neq 303=30. A zero between the point and a nonzero digit holds that digit out one place, so removing it multiplies the value by ten: 0.050.050.05 against 0.50.50.5.

  • Adding and subtracting decimals

    9.40− 2.656.75\begin{array}{r} 9.40 \\ -\ 2.65 \\ \hline 6.75 \end{array}9.40− 2.656.75​​

    Use when Addition and subtraction only, for two decimals that end. Line up the points, pad with trailing zeros so no column is empty, work right to left, and drop the point straight down.

  • Multiplying decimals: the places add

    places in the product=places in one factor+places in the other\begin{gathered} \text{places in the product} \\ = \text{places in one factor} \\ + \text{places in the other} \end{gathered}places in the product=places in one factor+places in the other​

    Use when Both factors must be decimals that end. The points are NOT lined up: multiply as whole numbers, then count. Prepend zeros when the product is too short to hold the point; a trailing zero the count creates may be dropped, so 0.06×0.5=0.030=0.030.06 \times 0.5 = 0.030 = 0.030.06×0.5=0.030=0.03.

    e.g. 1.2×0.351.2 \times 0.351.2×0.35: 12×35=42012 \times 35 = 42012×35=420, and 1+2=31 + 2 = 31+2=3 places give 0.420.420.42.

  • Multiplying or dividing by 101010, 100100100, 100010001000

    0.048×100=4.852.7÷1000=0.0527\begin{gathered} 0.048 \times 100 = 4.8 \\ 52.7 \div 1000 = 0.0527 \end{gathered}0.048×100=4.852.7÷1000=0.0527​

    Use when Count the zeros: that is how many places the point moves, right to multiply and left to divide. Append zeros wherever the digits run out.

  • Dividing by a decimal: shift both

    a÷b=(a×10)÷(b×10)a \div b = (a \times 10) \div (b \times 10)a÷b=(a×10)÷(b×10)

    Use when b≠0b \neq 0b=0 and bbb must be a decimal that ends, so its point can reach whole in finitely many shifts. Shift the divisor's point right until it is whole and the dividend's the same number of places, leaving the quotient unchanged. An already-whole divisor needs no shift. Append zeros to the dividend when its digits run out.

    e.g. 6.5÷0.13=650÷13=506.5 \div 0.13 = 650 \div 13 = 506.5÷0.13=650÷13=50, while 3.5÷4=0.8753.5 \div 4 = 0.8753.5÷4=0.875 shifts nothing.

  • Fraction to decimal: the bar means divide

    ab=a÷b\frac{a}{b} = a \div bba​=a÷b

    Use when b≠0b \neq 0b=0, and the numerator is the dividend: top divided by bottom, never bottom by top. Append zeros after the point in the dividend until the remainder clears or a block of digits recurs.

    e.g. 716=7÷16=0.4375\tfrac{7}{16} = 7 \div 16 = 0.4375167​=7÷16=0.4375.

  • Scaling shortcut to a decimal

    ab=a×kb×k\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \times k}{b \times k}ba​=b×ka×k​

    Use when Available when a whole kkk makes b×kb \times kb×k a power of ten (101010, 100100100, 100010001000, or a longer string of zeros), that is when bbb divides one of them. The new numerator is then read off as the decimal digits.

    e.g. 1125=44100=0.44\tfrac{11}{25} = \tfrac{44}{100} = 0.442511​=10044​=0.44.

  • Terminating test

    ab in lowest terms terminates  ⟺  every prime factor of bis 2 or 5\begin{gathered} \frac{a}{b} \text{ in lowest terms terminates} \\ \iff \text{every prime factor of } b \\ \text{is } 2 \text{ or } 5 \end{gathered}ba​ in lowest terms terminates⟺every prime factor of bis 2 or 5​

    Use when Reduce first: the test reads the REDUCED denominator. Both directions hold there, so any surviving prime other than 222 or 555 forces a repeat, and a denominator of 111 has no prime factors at all and terminates.

    e.g. 40=2×2×2×540 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 540=2×2×2×5, so 940=0.225\tfrac{9}{40} = 0.225409​=0.225; 12=2×2×312 = 2 \times 2 \times 312=2×2×3, so 712=0.583‾\tfrac{7}{12} = 0.58\overline{3}127​=0.583.

  • The rounding rule

    deciding digit≥5  ⇒  updeciding digit<5  ⇒  down\begin{gathered} \text{deciding digit} \ge 5 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{up} \\ \text{deciding digit} < 5 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{down} \end{gathered}deciding digit≥5⇒updeciding digit<5⇒down​

    Use when For a nonnegative decimal that ends, the single digit right after the rounding place settles the round-half-up direction; everything beyond it is ignored for that. The answer keeps the rounding place, trailing zero included. When that deciding digit is exactly 555, the digits after it decide whether the number is a genuine tie or already strictly past the midpoint, though round half up sends both cases up the same way.

    e.g. 7.857.857.85 to the nearest tenth is 7.97.97.9, and 19.9819.9819.98 to the nearest tenth is 20.020.020.0.

  • Which numbers round to a given value

    L−u2  ≤  x  <  L+u2L - \frac{u}{2} \;\le\; x \;<\; L + \frac{u}{2}L−2u​≤x<L+2u​

    Use when LLL is the rounded result and uuu is one unit of the rounding place. Under round half up the lower midpoint is included and the upper one is not.

Problem types, step by step

Read a decimal: place, value, expanded form, or words

  1. Count places rightward from the point (tenths, hundredths, thousandths); the place is that column's name, the value is the digit over 101010, 100100100, or 100010001000 to match.
  2. For expanded form add every digit times its place value and drop the zero terms; for words, on a decimal that ends, name the place of the last digit.

e.g. 6.209=6+210+910006.209 = 6 + \tfrac{2}{10} + \tfrac{9}{1000}6.209=6+102​+10009​, and its 999 has value 91000\tfrac{9}{1000}10009​.

Compare or order a list of values

  1. Convert any fraction in the list to a decimal.
  2. For decimals that end, pad with trailing zeros to equal length.
  3. Compare from the leftmost place; the first place where a pair differs settles that pair.
  4. A repeating decimal cannot be padded to a finite length and its digits can mislead; compare it as a fraction instead.

e.g. 58=0.625\tfrac{5}{8} = 0.62585​=0.625 against 0.620.620.62 and 35=0.6\tfrac{3}{5} = 0.653​=0.6 gives 0.6<0.62<580.6 < 0.62 < \tfrac{5}{8}0.6<0.62<85​.

Convert a decimal that ends to a fraction in lowest terms

  1. Put the digits after the point over 111 followed by that many zeros.
  2. Divide top and bottom by their GCF, keeping any whole part beside the fraction.

e.g. 0.85=85100=17200.85 = \tfrac{85}{100} = \tfrac{17}{20}0.85=10085​=2017​.

Convert a fraction to a decimal

  1. Reduce, then check whether the denominator divides 101010, 100100100, or 100010001000.
  2. If it does, scale top and bottom to that denominator and read the digits off.
  3. If it does not, divide numerator by denominator, appending zeros after the point until the remainder clears or a block recurs.

e.g. 1320=65100=0.65\tfrac{13}{20} = \tfrac{65}{100} = 0.652013​=10065​=0.65, while 56=5÷6=0.83‾\tfrac{5}{6} = 5 \div 6 = 0.8\overline{3}65​=5÷6=0.83.

Decide whether a fraction terminates or repeats, without dividing

  1. Reduce to lowest terms.
  2. Prime factorize the reduced denominator.
  3. Only 222s and 555s terminate; any other prime repeats, and the repeating block takes an overline.

e.g. 1435=25\tfrac{14}{35} = \tfrac{2}{5}3514​=52​ terminates at 0.40.40.4, while 730\tfrac{7}{30}307​ keeps a factor of 333 and repeats.

Add or subtract two decimals that end

  1. Stack the numbers with their points in one column, padding the shorter with trailing zeros so no column is empty.
  2. Work from the right, carrying or borrowing as with whole numbers, then drop the point straight down.

e.g. 5.6−2.34=5.60−2.34=3.265.6 - 2.34 = 5.60 - 2.34 = 3.265.6−2.34=5.60−2.34=3.26.

Multiply two decimals that end

  1. Ignore both points and multiply as whole numbers.
  2. Add the decimal places of the two factors.
  3. Count that many places in from the right of the product, prepending zeros if its digits run short.

e.g. 0.25×0.30.25 \times 0.30.25×0.3: 25×3=7525 \times 3 = 7525×3=75 with 2+1=32 + 1 = 32+1=3 places, so 0.0750.0750.075.

Divide by a decimal that ends

  1. Count the decimal places in the DIVISOR and slide its point right that many places.
  2. Slide the dividend's point right the same number of places, appending zeros if needed.
  3. Divide, keeping the quotient's point above the shifted dividend's, then check by multiplying the quotient by the original divisor.

e.g. 13.5÷0.05=1350÷5=27013.5 \div 0.05 = 1350 \div 5 = 27013.5÷0.05=1350÷5=270.

Round a nonnegative decimal that ends to a named place

  1. Locate the rounding place and read the single digit immediately to its right.
  2. 555 or more raises the rounding digit; less than 555 leaves it alone.
  3. Delete every digit past the rounding place, keep the rounding place itself, and carry left if a 999 became ten.

e.g. 8.978.978.97 to the nearest tenth: the hundredths 777 rounds the 999 up, which carries to 9.09.09.0.

Find the numbers that round to a given value

  1. Halve one unit of the rounding place to get the distance out to each midpoint.
  2. Write the band from the lower midpoint up to, but not including, the upper one.
  3. To count whole numbers, take the last minus the first, then add one.

e.g. To the nearest ten, 35≤n<4535 \le n < 4535≤n<45 round to 404040: 353535 through 444444, so 44−35+1=1044 - 35 + 1 = 1044−35+1=10.

Estimate a calculation, or check an answer for reasonableness

  1. Round each number to an easy value, keeping only a leading digit for the roughest version.
  2. Do the simple arithmetic on the rounded numbers.
  3. Compare against the exact answer: a gap of a factor of ten or more means a misplaced point.

e.g. 0.62×48≈0.6×50=300.62 \times 48 \approx 0.6 \times 50 = 300.62×48≈0.6×50=30, so a reading of 297.6297.6297.6 is ten times too big.

Solve a decimal word problem

  1. Pick the operation: totals and differences add or subtract, a rate times a count multiplies, sharing or "how many fit" divides.
  2. Compute with the matching point rule, estimate separately to confirm the size, and report money to two decimal places.

e.g. Three items at 2.452.452.45 dollars paid from 202020 dollars: 3×2.45=7.353 \times 2.45 = 7.353×2.45=7.35, so the change is 12.6512.6512.65 dollars.

Exam traps

  • Trap Lining the points up to multiply, so 0.3×0.20.3 \times 0.20.3×0.2 comes out as 0.60.60.6.

    Fix Alignment belongs to addition and subtraction. For two decimals that end, multiply as whole numbers and add the places: 3×2=63 \times 2 = 63×2=6 with 1+1=21 + 1 = 21+1=2 places, giving 0.060.060.06.

  • Trap Ranking decimals by how many digits they show, so 0.450.450.45 looks bigger than 0.50.50.5.

    Fix Pad to equal length and read from the left: 0.450.450.45 against 0.500.500.50 is settled at the tenths, so 0.45<0.50.45 < 0.50.45<0.5.

  • Trap Dropping the leading zero a product needs, writing 0.25×0.3=0.750.25 \times 0.3 = 0.750.25×0.3=0.75.

    Fix Three places were counted, so the point sits three digits in from the right of 757575: it is 0.0750.0750.075, ten times smaller.

  • Trap Sliding the point in the divisor only, turning 9.6÷0.049.6 \div 0.049.6÷0.04 into 9.6÷4=2.49.6 \div 4 = 2.49.6÷4=2.4.

    Fix Both points move the same number of places, so it is 960÷4=240960 \div 4 = 240960÷4=240. Moving one alone rescales the quotient, here by 100100100.

  • Trap Assuming multiplication always enlarges and division always shrinks.

    Fix A positive factor below 111 shrinks (0.4×0.4=0.160.4 \times 0.4 = 0.160.4×0.4=0.16) and dividing a positive by one below 111 enlarges (9.6÷0.04=2409.6 \div 0.04 = 2409.6÷0.04=240). A size check must allow both.

  • Trap Dividing bottom by top, computing 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​ as 4÷34 \div 34÷3.

    Fix The bar is top divided by bottom, so 3÷4=0.753 \div 4 = 0.753÷4=0.75. The reversed order gives 1.33…1.33\ldots1.33…, above 111 where the fraction is below it.

  • Trap Applying the 222s-and-555s test before reducing, calling 36\tfrac{3}{6}63​ a repeater on the strength of its 333.

    Fix Reduce first: 36=12\tfrac{3}{6} = \tfrac{1}{2}63​=21​, whose denominator is 222, so it terminates at 0.50.50.5.

  • Trap Rounding digit by digit from the right, so 6.2496.2496.249 to the nearest tenth becomes 6.36.36.3.

    Fix Only the hundredths digit is consulted, and 4<54 < 54<5, so the answer is 6.26.26.2. The 999 never gets a vote.

  • Trap Writing 13=0.33\tfrac{1}{3} = 0.3331​=0.33.

    Fix That is a rounded value, not an equality. The exact decimal is 0.3‾0.\overline{3}0.3, whose 333s never stop.

  • Trap Rounding a 999 in place, turning 2.962.962.96 to the nearest tenth into 2.102.102.10.

    Fix Ten tenths is one whole, so the carry lands in the ones: 3.03.03.0, keeping the trailing zero to show the tenths was the target.

  • Trap Rounding every input first when the question asks for an exact answer.

    Fix Compute exactly and round the final result once. Rounding first is for estimates, where the lost accuracy buys speed.
