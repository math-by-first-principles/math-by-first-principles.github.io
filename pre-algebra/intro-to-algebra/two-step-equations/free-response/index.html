    1. Two routes through one equation . Foundational, 11 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 11 pts

    Two students are handed the equation 6x+21=576x + 21 = 576x+21=57. The first deals with the constant before touching the coefficient. The second divides both sides by 666 straight away, before anything else. Neither has done anything forbidden: the balance principle permits either operation at any moment. This question follows both routes to the end and asks what separates them.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Take the first student's route. Deal with the constant first and the coefficient second, writing each move as an operation applied to both sides, and report the value of xxx it produces. Confirm that value in the original equation.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Now take the second student's route on the same equation. Divide both sides by 666 as the very first move, then finish. Report the equation you reach immediately after that division, and the value of xxx the route produces.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Explain, from the way the side 6x+216x + 216x+21 is built up out of xxx, why the standard order removes the constant before the coefficient. Then say what it is about these particular numbers that made the second route more work, and state the condition on the numbers under which dividing first would bring in no fractions at all.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 5 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      There is no hard arithmetic anywhere in this question. It turns on the order of two moves, so begin by asking what was done to the variable first when that side was built, and what was wrapped around the result.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      Set the work up before you compute anything: write the whole of each side over the divisor, as 6x+216=576\frac{6x + 21}{6} = \frac{57}{6}66x+21​=657​, and only then split the left-hand fraction into the pieces it is made of. Simplify nothing until that line is on the page.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Compare the routes by the kind of numbers each one forces you to carry, and ask whether the constant and the number on the right are whole numbers of sixes.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    x=6x = 6x=6.

    Part B

    The division gives x+72=192x + \frac{7}{2} = \frac{19}{2}x+27​=219​, and the route produces x=6x = 6x=6.

    • x+216=576x + \frac{21}{6} = \frac{57}{6}x+621​=657​ is the same line, before the two fractions are reduced

    Part C

    The multiplication is done to xxx first and the addition wraps around it, so the constant is the outer layer and comes off first. Dividing first brings in no fractions exactly when the constant and the number on the right are both multiples of the coefficient, and here 212121 and 575757 are not multiples of 666.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The left side is the variable multiplied by 666 with 212121 added on, so the addition is the outer layer and comes off first. Subtract 212121 from both sides:

    6x+21−21=57−21  ⇒  6x=36.6x + 21 - 21 = 57 - 21 \;\Rightarrow\; 6x = 36.6x+21−21=57−21⇒6x=36.

    The variable term now stands alone, so undo the multiplication by dividing both sides by the coefficient 666:

    6x6=366  ⇒  x=6.\frac{6x}{6} = \frac{36}{6} \;\Rightarrow\; x = 6.66x​=636​⇒x=6.

    Check by substituting into the original equation, multiplying before adding as the order of operations requires:

    6(6)+21=36+21=57.6(6) + 21 = 36 + 21 = 57.6(6)+21=36+21=57.

    Both sides come to 575757, so x=6x = 6x=6. Every number met along the way was a whole number.

    Part B

    Dividing both sides by 666 means dividing every term on each side, not only the term holding the variable. On the left, 6x6x6x becomes xxx and 212121 becomes 216\frac{21}{6}621​; on the right, 575757 becomes 576\frac{57}{6}657​:

    6x+216=576  ⇒  x+216=576.\frac{6x + 21}{6} = \frac{57}{6} \;\Rightarrow\; x + \frac{21}{6} = \frac{57}{6}.66x+21​=657​⇒x+621​=657​.

    Both of those fractions simplify by a factor of 333:

    x+72=192.x + \frac{7}{2} = \frac{19}{2}.x+27​=219​.

    One subtraction finishes it. Take 72\frac{7}{2}27​ from both sides, and since the two fractions already share a denominator the numerators subtract directly:

    x=192−72=122=6.x = \frac{19}{2} - \frac{7}{2} = \frac{12}{2} = 6.x=219​−27​=212​=6.

    The value is x=6x = 6x=6, the same as before. The route is sound; it simply passed through halves to get there.

    Part C

    Read the left side as a set of instructions carried out on xxx. Under the order of operations the multiplication happens first, producing 6x6x6x, and the addition happens after that, producing 6x+216x + 216x+21. So the multiplication by 666 is the inner layer, wrapped directly around the variable, and the 212121 is the outer layer, wrapped around everything.

    Taking a construction apart means running it backwards, and the last thing wrapped on is the first thing that can be removed. While 212121 is still attached, the variable term is not alone on its side, so nothing done to 6x6x6x by itself is a legal move on the equation. Subtracting 212121 strips the outer layer and leaves exactly one operation standing between xxx and its value. That is why the standard order is subtract, then divide.

    The second route is legal too, because dividing both sides by 666 preserves the equality just as subtracting 212121 does. What it costs is arithmetic. The division reaches every term, so the constant and the number on the right are dragged into it as well, and neither is a whole number of sixes:

    216=72,576=192.\frac{21}{6} = \frac{7}{2}, \qquad \frac{57}{6} = \frac{19}{2}.621​=27​,657​=219​.

    The rest of that work is then done in halves. Nothing goes wrong, but there are two more fractions to carry and two more places to slip.

    Both routes are two moves long, here and always, so what is at stake is never the number of steps but whether fractions appear. That gives a condition worth stating exactly. Dividing 6x+b=c6x + b = c6x+b=c by 666 produces x+b6=c6x + \frac{b}{6} = \frac{c}{6}x+6b​=6c​, and those two quotients are whole precisely when 666 divides bbb and 666 divides ccc. So dividing first brings in no fractions exactly when the constant and the number on the right are both multiples of the coefficient, and it brings in fractions the moment either one is not. Divide 6x+18=486x + 18 = 486x+18=48 by 666 and you get x+3=8x + 3 = 8x+3=8 with no fraction anywhere, and x=5x = 5x=5 follows at once. It is the divisibility of the particular numbers, not the shape of the equation, that decides which route is tidier.

    In one line

    Both routes give x=6x = 6x=6. Clearing the constant first runs 6x+21=576x + 21 = 576x+21=57 into 6x=366x = 366x=36 and then x=6x = 6x=6, entirely in whole numbers, while dividing by 666 first passes through x+72=192x + \frac{7}{2} = \frac{19}{2}x+27​=219​. Both are legal, because every move keeps the two sides equal; the standard order is preferred because the constant is the outer layer, and here 212121 and 575757 are not multiples of 666, so taking it off first is what keeps every number whole.

    2. What each walker pays . Application, 10 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Application 10 pts

    A walking club runs a day trip for the 888 members who sign up. The trip's own costs, the bus and the guide together, are shared equally among those 888 members. On top of that share, every member pays the museum a separate entry charge of 151515 dollars. When the treasurer adds it up, each member has paid 414141 dollars altogether.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Model the situation Name your unknown first, then write every other quantity in terms of that one letter. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Carry your own answer forward Solve the equation you wrote in part A, whatever form it took, and name the equation you are solving before you start. The credit here is for the route through it, not for the equation itself.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      The treasurer files a note recording the trip's own costs as 8×41=3288 \times 41 = 3288×41=328 dollars. Work out what that product counts, member by member, and decide whether it is the figure the note should carry. Account for any difference as an amount of money.

      Find and correct the error Say which line first goes wrong, why it is wrong, and then do it correctly. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Follow a single member all the way through before you think about the club as a whole. What one member pays is made of two amounts, and only one of them is a share of the thing you are asked to find.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      The unknown is divided by a number before anything is added to it, which makes the addition the outer layer. Once that is gone, remember that a division is undone by multiplying.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      The treasurer's multiplication is correct arithmetic. Ask instead how many museum charges are hiding inside its result, and whether the museum's money was ever the club's to share.

    That is every hint for this question.

    • any letter may stand for the trip's own costs, and 41=c8+1541 = \frac{c}{8} + 1541=8c​+15 says the same thing
    • c÷8+15=41c \div 8 + 15 = 41c÷8+15=41 is the same equation written with a division sign

    Part B

    208208208 dollars.

    Part C

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Name the unknown first. The quantity nobody knows is the trip's own costs, so let ccc be that amount in dollars.

    Those costs are shared equally among the 888 members, so one member's share of them is the whole amount split eight ways:

    c8.\frac{c}{8}.8c​.

    The museum charge is not shared. Every member pays 151515 dollars of it, so it is added to the share rather than divided by anything. One member therefore pays c8+15\frac{c}{8} + 158c​+15 dollars, and the treasurer's figure says that this is 414141 dollars:

    c8+15=41.\frac{c}{8} + 15 = 41.8c​+15=41.

    One equation, one unknown, and every quantity the situation states appears in it exactly once.

    Part B

    The unknown is divided by 888 and then has 151515 added, so the addition is the outer layer and comes off first. Subtract 151515 from both sides:

    c8+15−15=41−15  ⇒  c8=26.\frac{c}{8} + 15 - 15 = 41 - 15 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{c}{8} = 26.8c​+15−15=41−15⇒8c​=26.

    That line already says something readable: one member's share of the trip's own costs is 262626 dollars.

    The unknown is now divided by 888 and nothing else. A division is undone by multiplying, so multiply both sides by 888:

    8⋅c8=8⋅26  ⇒  c=208.8 \cdot \frac{c}{8} = 8 \cdot 26 \;\Rightarrow\; c = 208.8⋅8c​=8⋅26⇒c=208.

    Check it against the story rather than only against the equation: 208208208 dollars split eight ways is 262626 dollars each, and 26+15=4126 + 15 = 4126+15=41 dollars, which is what each member paid. The trip's own costs are 208208208 dollars.

    Part C

    Read the product for what it counts. Each member paid 414141 dollars and there are 888 members, so

    8×41=3288 \times 41 = 3288×41=328

    is the total amount of money the members handed over. That arithmetic is right, and it answers a real question. It is simply not the question the note is asking.

    Each of those 414141 dollars is made of two amounts: a share of the trip's own costs, and a museum charge that the museum keeps. Multiplying by 888 multiplies both amounts, so the 328328328 dollars contains eight museum charges as well as the whole of the trip's costs:

    8×15=120.8 \times 15 = 120.8×15=120.

    Take those out and what is left is the trip's own costs:

    328−120=208.328 - 120 = 208.328−120=208.

    So the note overstates the trip's own costs by 120120120 dollars. The mistake is one of grouping rather than of arithmetic: the entry charge was never part of what the members were sharing, so it has to come out of a member's payment before anything is multiplied by 888. That is exactly the order the equation in part A records, with the charge added outside the division and therefore removed first.

    In one line

    One member's payment is recorded by c8+15=41\frac{c}{8} + 15 = 418c​+15=41, where ccc is the trip's own costs in dollars, and solving it gives c=208c = 208c=208 dollars. The treasurer's 8×41=3288 \times 41 = 3288×41=328 dollars is the total the members handed over, not the trip's costs: it carries eight museum charges as well, and 8×15=1208 \times 15 = 1208×15=120 dollars is exactly the amount by which the note overstates the trip.

    Foundational 9 pts

    Three equations, each of which yields to the same two moves in the same order: −5x+14=59-5x + 14 = 59−5x+14=59, then 4x+9=24x + 9 = 24x+9=2, then 0.4x−1.6=2.80.4x - 1.6 = 2.80.4x−1.6=2.8.     Worked solution

    Part A

    The constant 141414 is added to the variable term, so it is the outer layer. Subtract it from both sides:

    −5x+14−14=59−14  ⇒  −5x=45.-5x + 14 - 14 = 59 - 14 \;\Rightarrow\; -5x = 45.−5x+14−14=59−14⇒−5x=45.

    The coefficient here is −5-5−5, not 555. The minus sign belongs to the number multiplying the variable, so it is part of what you divide by. Divide both sides by −5-5−5, and a positive divided by a negative is negative:

    −5x−5=45−5  ⇒  x=−9.\frac{-5x}{-5} = \frac{45}{-5} \;\Rightarrow\; x = -9.−5−5x​=−545​⇒x=−9.

    Part C

    Take the sign first.     Now the fractions. A solution is whatever number makes the two sides equal, and nothing in that definition asks it to be whole. When 4x=−74x = -74x=−7 is divided by 444, the quotient −74-\frac{7}{4}−47​ is not a symptom of an error; it is what splitting −7-7−7 into four equal parts gives, and the −7-7−7 arrived honestly because 222 is smaller than 999.     The procedure that settles either worry is the same one, and it requires trusting none of the reasoning above. Substitute the value into the original equation and evaluate both sides under the full order of operations:

    The two sides agree, so that value is the solution, sign and fraction and all. The check is the authority here, not the tidiness of the number.

    Part A

    t+12t + 12t+12

    Part B

    The left side is a number multiplied by a sum, so clear the parentheses first. The distributive property multiplies the 777 across both terms inside:

    7(t+12)=7t+84  ⇒  7t+84=175.7(t + 12) = 7t + 84 \;\Rightarrow\; 7t + 84 = 175.7(t+12)=7t+84⇒7t+84=175.

    Both routes are legitimate, and for the same reason: it is not that one is a rule and the other a trick. Every balance move in each route is applied to both sides in full, so at no point do the two sides stop holding equal amounts, and each line therefore has the same solution as the line above it. Distributing is not even a balance move. It rewrites one side into an equal form, changing how that side is written rather than what it is worth.

    Count the moves before looking at the numbers. Dividing first clears the factor from the whole side at once and leaves t+12=25t + 12 = 25t+12=25, which one subtraction finishes: two moves. Dividing by 777 reaches every term on both sides, so it stays in whole numbers only when everything it touches is a whole number of sevens. On the left it meets a single factor of 777, which cancels exactly; on the right it meets the invoice total, and

    1757=25\frac{175}{7} = 257175​=25

    comes out whole. With 170170170 that same first move would have produced 1707\frac{170}{7}7170​ and the rest of the work would have been done in sevenths.     The general reading: when a side is a factor times a sum, dividing by that factor first is the shorter route, and the divisibility of the other side decides only whether that route stays in whole numbers along the way.
