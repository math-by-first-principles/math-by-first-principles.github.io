Solving Two-Step Equations

Intuition

In the last lesson you solved equations where exactly one thing was done to the variable, like x+5=12x + 5 = 12x+5=12 or 6x=426x = 426x=42. You solved each one by applying a single inverse operation to both sides. Most equations are not that tidy. Look at 2x+3=112x + 3 = 112x+3=11: here the variable is first multiplied by 222 and then has 333 added, so two operations stand between xxx and its value. The good news is that you do not need a new idea, just the same balance principle used twice. The only real question is which operation to undo first, and the answer falls straight out of how the expression was built. You peel the operations off in the reverse of the order of operations. Once you see why, every two-step equation comes apart in two clean moves.

Learning goals

  • Recognize ax+b=cax + b = cax+b=c as two operations on the variable
  • Undo in reverse order, clearing the constant first and undoing the multiplication or division second
  • Divide by the full coefficient, sign and all
  • Combine like terms or distribute first when a side needs cleaning
  • Leave a non-integer answer as a simplified fraction

What makes an equation a two-step equation

A two-step equation has two operations attached to the variable, so isolating the variable takes two inverse moves instead of one. The most common shape is

ax+b=c,ax + b = c,ax+b=c,

where aaa is the coefficient multiplying the variable, bbb is a constant added on, and ccc is the number the whole expression equals. In 2x+3=112x + 3 = 112x+3=11 the coefficient is a=2a = 2a=2, the constant is b=3b = 3b=3, and c=11c = 11c=11. Reading the left side the way you would evaluate it, you multiply xxx by 222 first, then add 333. Two operations went in, so two inverse operations must come out.

Nothing about the balance principle changes. An equation is still a balanced scale, and the two sides hold equal amounts. So a balance-preserving move, adding or subtracting the same number on both sides, or multiplying or dividing both sides by the same nonzero number, keeps the solution unchanged, whichever side of the equation the variable is on. What is new is that you will use that principle twice in a row, and the order in which you use it matters.

Why you undo in the reverse order

To see which operation to undo first, think about how the left side of 2x+32x + 32x+3 is built up from xxx. Then you run that construction backward.

Start with the variable xxx. The order of operations says multiplication happens before addition. So to build 2x+32x + 32x+3 you first multiply by 222, reaching 2x2x2x, and then add 333, reaching 2x+32x + 32x+3. The addition is the outermost operation: it is the last thing wrapped around the variable, sitting on the outside of everything else.

To take the expression apart you reverse that process, removing the outermost layer first, the way you cannot reach an onion’s inner skin without peeling the outer one off. So you undo the addition before the multiplication: first subtract 333 from both sides to strip off the constant, which leaves 2x2x2x alone. Then you divide by 222 to strip off the coefficient, which leaves xxx alone.

This is why the rule is “reverse the order of operations.” Evaluating works inside out, doing multiplication before addition; solving works outside in, undoing addition before multiplication. The two orders are mirror images, and that single fact decides every two-step equation.

Why the constant comes off before the coefficient#

Take the standard form ax+b=cax + b = cax+b=c with a nonzero coefficient aaa. Suppose some value of xxx makes that equation true, so the two sides are the same number.

Subtract bbb from both sides. The left side becomes ax+b−bax + b - bax+b−b, and since b−b=0b - b = 0b−b=0, that is just axaxax; the right side becomes c−bc - bc−b. Because the same amount was removed from each side, the equation ax=c−bax = c - bax=c−b has exactly the same solution as the one you started with. Notice what this step accomplished: the constant bbb is gone, and the variable term axaxax now stands by itself. This move could not wait, because while bbb was still attached by addition, the variable term was not alone on its side. With bbb still attached, dividing that whole side by aaa would not have isolated xxx either.

Now divide both sides of ax=c−bax = c - bax=c−b by aaa. The left side becomes axa=x\dfrac{ax}{a} = xaax​=x, since aa=1\dfrac{a}{a} = 1aa​=1; the right side becomes c−ba\dfrac{c - b}{a}ac−b​. Again the same operation hit both sides, so the solution is unchanged, and now xxx stands completely alone:

x=c−ba.x = \frac{c - b}{a}.x=ac−b​.

Clearing the constant first is the clean, efficient order. The constant is the outer layer, added after the multiplication. So undoing that outer layer first removes one layer at a time and leaves the variable term alone. You could divide by aaa while bbb is still present, and you would reach the same answer. But that step divides bbb by aaa too, and can turn a whole number into a fraction, making the arithmetic messier for no gain. To keep the numbers whole, undo the addition or subtraction first, then the multiplication or division.

∎

The two-step recipe

Every equation in this lesson follows the same two moves once the variable term appears on only one side:

  1. Undo the addition or subtraction. Add or subtract the constant on both sides so the variable term (axaxax or xa\tfrac{x}{a}ax​) sits by itself.
  2. Undo the multiplication or division. Divide both sides by the coefficient, or multiply both sides by the divisor, to leave the variable alone.

Then check by substituting your answer into the original equation and evaluating with the full order of operations. The check is not optional decoration. With two steps there are two places to slip, and substituting back catches almost every mistake in a single line.

Worked example 1 Solve 2x+3=112x + 3 = 112x+3=11

The variable is multiplied by 222 and then has 333 added, so undo the addition first. Subtract 333 from both sides:

2x+3−3=11−3.2x + 3 - 3 = 11 - 3.2x+3−3=11−3.

On the left, 3−3=03 - 3 = 03−3=0, leaving the variable term alone; on the right, 11−3=811 - 3 = 811−3=8:

2x=8.2x = 8.2x=8.

Now undo the multiplication by dividing both sides by the coefficient 222:

2x2=82  ⇒  x=4.\frac{2x}{2} = \frac{8}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; x = 4.22x​=28​⇒x=4.

Check by substituting 444 into the original equation, doing the multiplication before the addition just as the order of operations requires:

2(4)+3=8+3=11,11=11.  ✓2(4) + 3 = 8 + 3 = 11, \qquad 11 = 11. \;\checkmark2(4)+3=8+3=11,11=11.✓

Both sides match, so x=4x = 4x=4. Subtracting the constant first cleared the way to divide off the coefficient.

A valid but messier order

Dividing by 222 before subtracting also reaches x=4x = 4x=4, since a balance move works no matter which valid move comes first. But it splits the 333 into a fraction along the way, x+32=112x + \tfrac{3}{2} = \tfrac{11}{2}x+23​=211​, which is more arithmetic for the same answer. Clearing the constant first keeps the numbers whole, which is why this lesson leads with that order, not because dividing first is wrong.

Worked example 2 Solve 5x−4=165x - 4 = 165x−4=16

This is the subtraction form, ax−b=cax - b = cax−b=c. A constant is subtracted from the variable term, so undo that by adding 444 to both sides:

5x−4+4=16+4  ⇒  5x=20.5x - 4 + 4 = 16 + 4 \;\Rightarrow\; 5x = 20.5x−4+4=16+4⇒5x=20.

The variable term now stands alone. Divide both sides by the coefficient 555:

5x5=205  ⇒  x=4.\frac{5x}{5} = \frac{20}{5} \;\Rightarrow\; x = 4.55x​=520​⇒x=4.

Check in the original equation, multiplying before subtracting:

5(4)−4=20−4=16,16=16.  ✓5(4) - 4 = 20 - 4 = 16, \qquad 16 = 16. \;\checkmark5(4)−4=20−4=16,16=16.✓

So x=4x = 4x=4. A subtracted constant is undone by adding it back, exactly as in the one-step lesson, only now a division step follows.

Answer choices

The constant 777 is the outer layer, added after the multiplication, so undo it first by subtracting 777 from both sides.

3x+7−7=22−7  ⇒  3x=153x + 7 - 7 = 22 - 7 \;\Rightarrow\; 3x = 153x+7−7=22−7⇒3x=15

That clears the constant and leaves 3x3x3x alone, ready for the division step. Dividing by 333 first reaches the same answer, but it splits the 777 into a fraction and makes more work.

Reading the equation in a different order

It is fine, and often clearer, to read the equation in either direction. If the constant comes first, as in b+ax=cb + ax = cb+ax=c, or the whole thing is written backward, as in c=ax+bc = ax + bc=ax+b, the recipe is unchanged. In both of those forms the addition or subtraction is still the outer layer, so undo it first.

Worked example 3 Solve 4+3x=194 + 3x = 194+3x=19

Here the constant 444 is written before the variable term, but it is still added to 3x3x3x, so it is still the outer layer. Subtract 444 from both sides:

4+3x−4=19−4  ⇒  3x=15.4 + 3x - 4 = 19 - 4 \;\Rightarrow\; 3x = 15.4+3x−4=19−4⇒3x=15.

Divide both sides by the coefficient 333:

3x3=153  ⇒  x=5.\frac{3x}{3} = \frac{15}{3} \;\Rightarrow\; x = 5.33x​=315​⇒x=5.

Check in the original equation:

4+3(5)=4+15=19,19=19.  ✓4 + 3(5) = 4 + 15 = 19, \qquad 19 = 19. \;\checkmark4+3(5)=4+15=19,19=19.✓

So x=5x = 5x=5. Writing the constant first does not change anything; you still strip it off before dividing.

Worked example 4 Solve 25=7x−325 = 7x - 325=7x−3

The variable term is on the right, but the balance principle does not care which side the variable lives on. The constant −3-3−3 is the outer layer on the right, so add 333 to both sides:

25+3=7x−3+3  ⇒  28=7x.25 + 3 = 7x - 3 + 3 \;\Rightarrow\; 28 = 7x.25+3=7x−3+3⇒28=7x.

Divide both sides by the coefficient 777:

287=7x7  ⇒  4=x.\frac{28}{7} = \frac{7x}{7} \;\Rightarrow\; 4 = x.728​=77x​⇒4=x.

That is the same as x=4x = 4x=4, since an equation reads the same both ways. Check in the original:

7(4)−3=28−3=25,25=25.  ✓7(4) - 3 = 28 - 3 = 25, \qquad 25 = 25. \;\checkmark7(4)−3=28−3=25,25=25.✓

So x=4x = 4x=4. When the variable sits on the right, just work toward isolating it there; you do not need to swap the sides first.

Negatives, fractions, and decimals ride along

The recipe does not flinch when the numbers get awkward. A negative coefficient, a fractional answer, or a decimal constant changes the arithmetic of each step but not the two steps themselves. You still clear the constant, then divide by the coefficient, sign and all. For instance, 2x+1.5=5.52x + 1.5 = 5.52x+1.5=5.5 subtracts to 2x=42x = 42x=4 and divides to x=2x = 2x=2, the same two moves with decimal arithmetic in place of whole numbers.

Worked example 5 Solve −3x+8=2-3x + 8 = 2−3x+8=2

The constant 888 is added on, so subtract 888 from both sides first:

−3x+8−8=2−8  ⇒  −3x=−6.-3x + 8 - 8 = 2 - 8 \;\Rightarrow\; -3x = -6.−3x+8−8=2−8⇒−3x=−6.

Now divide both sides by the full coefficient −3-3−3, carrying its sign. A negative divided by a negative is positive, the rule from the integers chapter:

−3x−3=−6−3  ⇒  x=2.\frac{-3x}{-3} = \frac{-6}{-3} \;\Rightarrow\; x = 2.−3−3x​=−3−6​⇒x=2.

Check in the original equation:

−3(2)+8=−6+8=2,2=2.  ✓-3(2) + 8 = -6 + 8 = 2, \qquad 2 = 2. \;\checkmark−3(2)+8=−6+8=2,2=2.✓

So x=2x = 2x=2. The key is dividing by −3-3−3, not by a bare 333, so the sign of the answer comes out right.

After clearing the constant in −4x+9=1-4x + 9 = 1−4x+9=1 you reach −4x=−8-4x = -8−4x=−8. What is xxx?

Answer choices

Divide both sides by the full coefficient −4-4−4, sign included.

−4x−4=−8−4  ⇒  x=2\frac{-4x}{-4} = \frac{-8}{-4} \;\Rightarrow\; x = 2−4−4x​=−4−8​⇒x=2

A negative divided by a negative is positive, so x=2x = 2x=2, not −2-2−2. Dropping the negative sign and dividing by a bare 444 is what flips the answer's sign by mistake.

Worked example 6 Solve 4x+1=74x + 1 = 74x+1=7

Subtract the constant 111 from both sides:

4x+1−1=7−1  ⇒  4x=6.4x + 1 - 1 = 7 - 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 4x = 6.4x+1−1=7−1⇒4x=6.

Divide both sides by 444. The quotient is not a whole number, which is perfectly fine; write it as a fraction in lowest terms:

x=64=32.x = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}.x=46​=23​.

Check by substituting 32\tfrac{3}{2}23​ into the original equation:

4⋅32+1=122+1=6+1=7,7=7.  ✓4 \cdot \tfrac{3}{2} + 1 = \tfrac{12}{2} + 1 = 6 + 1 = 7, \qquad 7 = 7. \;\checkmark4⋅23​+1=212​+1=6+1=7,7=7.✓

So x=32x = \tfrac{3}{2}x=23​. A non-integer answer is not a sign of a mistake; simplify the fraction and check it the same way.

Solve 2x−5=82x - 5 = 82x−5=8 for xxx.

Answer choices

Add the constant 555 to both sides, then divide by the coefficient 222.

2x−5+5=8+5  ⇒  2x=132x - 5 + 5 = 8 + 5 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x = 132x−5+5=8+5⇒2x=13

Dividing by 222 gives x=132x = \tfrac{13}{2}x=213​. Check: 2⋅132−5=13−5=82 \cdot \tfrac{13}{2} - 5 = 13 - 5 = 82⋅213​−5=13−5=8, which is true.

The divide form: a variable split by a number

When the variable is divided by a number and then has a constant added, the equation looks like

xa+b=c.\frac{x}{a} + b = c.ax​+b=c.

The two layers are now “divide by aaa” (inner) and “add bbb” (outer), so the recipe runs the same way. That means you clear the constant first, then undo the division by multiplying both sides by aaa.

Worked example 7 Solve x3+2=9\dfrac{x}{3} + 2 = 93x​+2=9

The constant 222 is the outer layer, so subtract it from both sides:

x3+2−2=9−2  ⇒  x3=7.\frac{x}{3} + 2 - 2 = 9 - 2 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{x}{3} = 7.3x​+2−2=9−2⇒3x​=7.

The variable is now divided by 333 and nothing else, so undo that by multiplying both sides by 333:

3⋅x3=3⋅7  ⇒  x=21.3 \cdot \frac{x}{3} = 3 \cdot 7 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 21.3⋅3x​=3⋅7⇒x=21.

Check in the original equation, dividing before adding:

213+2=7+2=9,9=9.  ✓\frac{21}{3} + 2 = 7 + 2 = 9, \qquad 9 = 9. \;\checkmark321​+2=7+2=9,9=9.✓

So x=21x = 21x=21. The only change from the multiplication form is the second step: a divided variable is freed by multiplying, not dividing.

Check your understanding

After clearing the constant in x4−1=5\dfrac{x}{4} - 1 = 54x​−1=5 you reach x4=6\dfrac{x}{4} = 64x​=6. What is the correct second step?

Answer choices

The variable is divided by 444, and the inverse of dividing by 444 is multiplying by 444.

4⋅x4=4⋅6  ⇒  x=244 \cdot \frac{x}{4} = 4 \cdot 6 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 244⋅4x​=4⋅6⇒x=24

Check: 244−1=6−1=5\frac{24}{4} - 1 = 6 - 1 = 5424​−1=6−1=5, which is true. Dividing again would only shrink the variable further.

When a side needs cleaning up first

Some equations are not yet in two-step form, but a single move from earlier in this chapter turns them into one. Before you reach for an inverse operation, look at each side and simplify it first.

If one side has like terms, combine them. Recall from the simplifying lesson that 3x+2x=5x3x + 2x = 5x3x+2x=5x, because the two terms count the same thing. So 3x+2x+1=113x + 2x + 1 = 113x+2x+1=11 becomes 5x+1=115x + 1 = 115x+1=11, an ordinary two-step equation.

Worked example 8 Solve 3x+2x+1=113x + 2x + 1 = 113x+2x+1=11

The left side has two like terms, 3x3x3x and 2x2x2x. Combine them before doing anything else:

3x+2x+1=11  ⇒  5x+1=11.3x + 2x + 1 = 11 \;\Rightarrow\; 5x + 1 = 11.3x+2x+1=11⇒5x+1=11.

Now it is a standard two-step equation. Subtract the constant 111 from both sides:

5x+1−1=11−1  ⇒  5x=10.5x + 1 - 1 = 11 - 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 5x = 10.5x+1−1=11−1⇒5x=10.

Divide both sides by the coefficient 555:

5x5=105  ⇒  x=2.\frac{5x}{5} = \frac{10}{5} \;\Rightarrow\; x = 2.55x​=510​⇒x=2.

Check in the original equation, before any simplifying, so the check tests your whole solution:

3(2)+2(2)+1=6+4+1=11,11=11.  ✓3(2) + 2(2) + 1 = 6 + 4 + 1 = 11, \qquad 11 = 11. \;\checkmark3(2)+2(2)+1=6+4+1=11,11=11.✓

So x=2x = 2x=2. Combining like terms collapsed three terms into the familiar ax+b=cax + b = cax+b=c.

If a side is a number times a sum, distribute first. The distributive property from the start of this chapter says 2(x+3)=2x+62(x + 3) = 2x + 62(x+3)=2x+6, multiplying the 222 across both terms inside the parentheses. That rewrite clears the parentheses and again leaves a two-step equation. (When a single number multiplies an entire side and nothing else is added outside the parentheses, dividing both sides by that number first works too. Distributing is the move you will lean on once a side mixes parentheses with other terms.)

Worked example 9 Solve 2(x+3)=142(x + 3) = 142(x+3)=14

The left side is 222 times the sum x+3x + 3x+3. Distribute the 222 across both terms inside the parentheses:

2(x+3)=14  ⇒  2x+6=14.2(x + 3) = 14 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x + 6 = 14.2(x+3)=14⇒2x+6=14.

Now solve the two-step equation. Subtract the constant 666 from both sides:

2x+6−6=14−6  ⇒  2x=8.2x + 6 - 6 = 14 - 6 \;\Rightarrow\; 2x = 8.2x+6−6=14−6⇒2x=8.

Divide both sides by the coefficient 222:

2x2=82  ⇒  x=4.\frac{2x}{2} = \frac{8}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; x = 4.22x​=28​⇒x=4.

Check in the original equation, evaluating the parentheses first as the order of operations demands:

2(4+3)=2(7)=14,14=14.  ✓2(4 + 3) = 2(7) = 14, \qquad 14 = 14. \;\checkmark2(4+3)=2(7)=14,14=14.✓

So x=4x = 4x=4. Distributing cleared the parentheses and turned the equation into one you already know how to finish.

Which is the best first step toward solving 4x−x+2=114x - x + 2 = 114x−x+2=11?

Answer choices

The left side has two like terms, 4x4x4x and −x-x−x. Combine them first, remembering that −x-x−x means −1x-1x−1x.

4x−x=(4−1)x=3x4x - x = (4 - 1)x = 3x4x−x=(4−1)x=3x

Reading a two-step equation before you move

Every two-step equation comes down to the same short routine. First, simplify each side if it has like terms or parentheses. Then ask what is the outer operation (the addition or subtraction) and undo it on both sides. Finally ask what is the inner operation (the multiplication or division) and undo that on both sides. The table below is the whole lesson at a glance.

The pattern to lock in is the order: the outer layer, the added or subtracted constant, comes off first. The inner layer, the coefficient or divisor, comes off second, and you finish by substituting your answer back to confirm both sides are equal.

Worked example 10 Identify the moves, then solve x2−4=−1\dfrac{x}{2} - 4 = -12x​−4=−1

First read the equation. The variable is divided by 222 (inner) and then has 444 subtracted (outer), so this is the divide form. Undo the subtraction first by adding 444 to both sides:

x2−4+4=−1+4  ⇒  x2=3.\frac{x}{2} - 4 + 4 = -1 + 4 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{x}{2} = 3.2x​−4+4=−1+4⇒2x​=3.

Now undo the division by multiplying both sides by 222:

2⋅x2=2⋅3  ⇒  x=6.2 \cdot \frac{x}{2} = 2 \cdot 3 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 6.2⋅2x​=2⋅3⇒x=6.

Check in the original equation:

62−4=3−4=−1,−1=−1.  ✓\frac{6}{2} - 4 = 3 - 4 = -1, \qquad -1 = -1. \;\checkmark26​−4=3−4=−1,−1=−1.✓

So x=6x = 6x=6. Reading the form first told you to add before multiplying, and the check confirms it.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Subtracting the coefficient instead of dividing. Once you reach 2x=82x = 82x=8, the 222 is multiplied by the variable, so you divide both sides by 222. Subtracting 222 does nothing useful, because 2x2x2x is not 2+x2 + x2+x.
  • Forgetting to do both steps to both sides. Each step is a balance move: subtract the constant from both sides, then divide both sides by the coefficient. Touch only one side and the scale tips.
  • Combining unlike terms. In 5x+1=115x + 1 = 115x+1=11 the 5x5x5x and the 111 are not like terms, so they cannot be merged into 6x6x6x or 666. Only 5x5x5x and another xxx-term combine; a number combines only with another number.
  • Distributing to only one term. 2(x+3)2(x + 3)2(x+3) is 2x+62x + 62x+6, not 2x+32x + 32x+3. The factor multiplies every term inside the parentheses, not just the first.
  • Losing a negative sign. When the coefficient is −3-3−3, divide by −3-3−3, sign included, so the answer has the right sign. Dividing −6-6−6 by a bare 333 drops the negative and flips the answer.
  • Skipping the check. With two steps there are two chances to slip. Substituting your answer back into the original equation, with the full order of operations, costs one line and catches almost every error.

Key takeaways

  • A two-step equation has two operations attached to the variable, most often the form ax+b=cax + b = cax+b=c, where aaa is the coefficient and bbb is the constant.
  • Undo the operations in the reverse of the order of operations. The added or subtracted constant is the outer layer, so clear it first; the coefficient or divisor is the inner layer, so undo it second.
  • The recipe: first add or subtract to clear the constant, isolating the variable term. Then divide by the coefficient or multiply by the divisor to isolate the variable.
  • Negative, fraction, and decimal numbers change the arithmetic of each step, not the steps. Divide by the full coefficient, sign and all, and leave a non-integer answer as a simplified fraction.
  • If a side has like terms, combine them first; if a side is a number times a sum, distribute first. Either move turns a longer equation back into a two-step.
  • Always check by substituting your answer into the original equation and evaluating with the full order of operations.

It comes from a collection written long after he died, so nobody can be sure it is true. It says his boyhood lasted a sixth of his life. His beard grew after another twelfth. He married after another seventh, and his son was born five years later. The son lived half as long as his father, and Diophantus died four years after his son.

So how old was he? The riddle is an equation in disguise, though a longer one than this lesson covers: several fraction terms have to be combined before the unknown can be isolated. But the core idea is the same one you just used, undo the operations one at a time, in reverse order. The answer is 848484.

Diophantus is remembered for something that sounds small. He was among the first to give the unknown its own symbol, instead of describing it in a sentence every time. Once you can write the unknown down, you can work on it.
