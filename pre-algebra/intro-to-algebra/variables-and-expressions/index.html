Variables and Expressions

▾

Intuition

A notebook costs 333 dollars, so 444 notebooks cost 3×4=123 \times 4 = 123×4=12 dollars. If you have not chosen how many to buy, let nnn stand for the number of notebooks. Their cost is 3×n3 \times n3×n dollars. The letter lets you write the calculation before you know the number.

Learning goals

  • Use a letter for a number that is unknown or free to change
  • Distinguish expressions from equations and describe what it means to evaluate or solve
  • Translate phrases into algebra using the correct order and parentheses
  • Identify terms, coefficients, and constants, including their signs
  • Evaluate by substituting each value in parentheses

What a variable is

A variable is a letter that stands for a number that is unknown or free to change. For example, nnn can stand for an unknown number of notebooks in a bag. It can also stand for the number you choose to buy, which may change from one purchase to another.

For the notebooks that cost 333 dollars each, choosing n=10n = 10n=10 gives a cost of 3×10=303 \times 10 = 303×10=30 dollars. The number of notebooks changes, but the calculation still follows the same rule: multiply the count by 333. A variable lets one expression describe all these choices.

Choose a letter and state what it means. A letter such as nnn for a count or ttt for time can help you remember. Within one calculation, every use of the same letter stands for the same number. If n=4n = 4n=4, then n+nn + nn+n means 4+44 + 44+4, not two different numbers.

In algebra, 3n3n3n is shorthand for 3×n3 \times n3×n. Leaving out the multiplication sign keeps the expression compact and avoids confusing ×\times× with the letter xxx. Likewise, ababab means a×ba \times ba×b, and 2(x+4)2(x + 4)2(x+4) means 2×(x+4)2 \times (x + 4)2×(x+4). Placing bare digits together is different: 343434 means thirty-four, while 3(4)3(4)3(4) means 3×43 \times 43×4.

Let nnn be the number of notebooks in one pack. Each pack has 444 notebooks. What does n+nn + nn+n mean?

Answer choices

Both copies of nnn stand for the same count, 444.

n+n=4+4=8n + n = 4 + 4 = 8n+n=4+4=8

The letter can stand for a different count in another situation, but it keeps the same value throughout this calculation.

Expressions versus equations

The notation x+5x + 5x+5 describes a number plus five. It is an expression: a number, a variable, or numbers and variables combined with operations. Other examples are 777, 3n3n3n, y4\frac{y}{4}4y​, and 5x25x^25x2. An expression has no equals sign.

Writing x+5=9x + 5 = 9x+5=9 makes a claim that two amounts are equal. This is an equation: two expressions joined by an equals sign. It is true when x=4x = 4x=4, since 4+5=94 + 5 = 94+5=9. It is false when x=2x = 2x=2, since 2+5=72 + 5 = 72+5=7, not 999.

This distinction changes what you are trying to find. To evaluate an expression, work out its value using given values for its variables. For x+5x + 5x+5 with x=2x = 2x=2, you calculate 2+5=72 + 5 = 72+5=7. To solve an equation, find the value or values that make it true. For x+5=9x + 5 = 9x+5=9, that value is 444. Later lessons teach methods for solving equations.

Check your understanding

Which statement correctly describes 2n+52n + 52n+5 and 2n+5=112n + 5 = 112n+5=11?

Answer choices

An expression names an amount. To evaluate 2n+52n + 52n+5, you need a value for nnn. For example, when n=4n = 4n=4:

2(4)+5=132(4) + 5 = 132(4)+5=13

An equation states that two amounts are equal. Solving 2n+5=112n + 5 = 112n+5=11 means finding the value of nnn that makes the equality true.

Translating words into expressions

To write “seven more than a number,” let xxx stand for the number and add seven: x+7x + 7x+7. Name the number first, then decide what operations the words describe. The words in the table are clues to the operation. Read the whole phrase to decide which quantity to start with; the order of the words may differ from the order of the calculation.

“Five less than ten” means 10−510 - 510−5. In the same way, “five less than xxx” means x−5x - 5x−5, not 5−x5 - x5−x: start with xxx and take five away. “Five fewer than xxx” and “five subtracted from xxx” also mean x−5x - 5x−5.

Worked example 1 Translate three phrases into expressions

Let xxx stand for the number in each phrase.

“Eight more than twice a number.” Twice the number is 2x2x2x, and eight more than that adds 888:

2x+8.2x + 8.2x+8.

“The product of 555 and a number, decreased by 333.” The product of 555 and the number is 5x5x5x, and decreased by 333 subtracts 333:

5x−3.5x - 3.5x−3.

“A number divided by 222, then increased by 111.” Dividing by 222 gives x2\dfrac{x}{2}2x​, and increasing by 111 adds 111:

x2+1.\frac{x}{2} + 1.2x​+1.

Use parentheses when an operation applies to a whole sum or difference.

Worked example 2 Translate 'twice the sum of a number and four'

With the number 333, “twice the sum of three and four” means 2(3+4)=142(3 + 4) = 142(3+4)=14. You add first, then double the whole sum.

With xxx standing for the number, the sum is x+4x + 4x+4. Keep it together with parentheses:

2(x+4).2(x + 4).2(x+4).

Without parentheses, 2x+42x + 42x+4 doubles only xxx, then adds 444. At x=3x = 3x=3, it gives 2(3)+4=102(3) + 4 = 102(3)+4=10, rather than 141414.

The parts of an expression

In the expression below, two products are added and then seven is subtracted:

5x+3y−7.5x + 3y - 7.5x+3y−7.

Its terms are the parts added or subtracted at the main level: 5x5x5x, 3y3y3y, and −7-7−7. Subtracting 777 is the same as adding −7-7−7, so you can write 5x+3y+(−7)5x + 3y + (-7)5x+3y+(−7). Each term carries its sign, so the last term is −7-7−7, not 777.

A plus or minus inside parentheses does not split the surrounding expression into terms. For example, 2(x+4)−72(x + 4) - 72(x+4)−7 has two terms as written: 2(x+4)2(x + 4)2(x+4) and −7-7−7. The grouped sum stays inside the first term.

In 5x5x5x, the number 555 is the coefficient, the number multiplying the variable. The coefficient of 3y3y3y is 333. Plain xxx has coefficient 111, because x=1xx = 1xx=1x; likewise, −x=−1x-x = -1x−x=−1x has coefficient −1-1−1.

The term −7-7−7 is a constant, a number term with no variable. It stays −7-7−7 regardless of the values of xxx and yyy.

Check your understanding

Which statement about −k+2-k + 2−k+2 is correct?

Answer choices

Keep the minus sign with the first term. The terms are −k-k−k and 222.

−k=(−1)k-k = (-1)k−k=(−1)k

The coefficient is −1-1−1, even though the 111 is not written. The number term, 222, is the constant.

Evaluating an expression by substitution

If x=7x = 7x=7, then 5x5x5x means 5×7=355 \times 7 = 355×7=35. Replacing a variable with its given value is called substitution.

Put the substituted number in parentheses to keep the operations clear: 5x5x5x becomes 5(7)5(7)5(7), not 575757. Then follow the order of operations to evaluate the expression.

Worked example 3 Evaluate 3x+43x + 43x+4 when x=5x = 5x=5

Substitute 555 for xxx, writing it in parentheses so the multiplication stays clear:

3x+4=3(5)+4.3x + 4 = 3(5) + 4.3x+4=3(5)+4.

Now follow the order of operations: multiply before you add.

3(5)+4=15+4=19.3(5) + 4 = 15 + 4 = 19.3(5)+4=15+4=19.

Why the same expression gives different values for different inputs#

Changing the input can change the value of an expression. For 2x+12x + 12x+1, compare two inputs:

x=3:2(3)+1=7,x=5:2(5)+1=11.\begin{aligned} x = 3 &: \quad 2(3) + 1 = 7, \\ x = 5 &: \quad 2(5) + 1 = 11. \end{aligned}x=3x=5​:2(3)+1=7,:2(5)+1=11.​

Both calculations multiply the input by two and add one. The expression stays the same; the chosen input and the result change. This is why one expression can describe many calculations.

For a width of 444 and height of 222, going around adds 4+24 + 24+2 twice: 2(4+2)=122(4 + 2) = 122(4+2)=12. With letters, the two pairs of sides give (w+h)+(w+h)=2(w+h)(w + h) + (w + h) = 2(w + h)(w+h)+(w+h)=2(w+h).

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Reversing subtraction. “Five less than xxx” and “five subtracted from xxx” both mean x−5x - 5x−5.
  • Trying to solve an expression. An expression names an amount; it does not state an equality to solve.
  • Losing a sign or coefficient. In −x−7-x - 7−x−7, the coefficient of xxx is −1-1−1 and the constant is −7-7−7.
  • Changing multiplication during substitution. In 5x5x5x, for x=7x = 7x=7, write 5(7)5(7)5(7), not 575757. For x=−2x = -2x=−2, write 5(−2)5(-2)5(−2), not 5−25 - 25−2.
  • Leaving a sum ungrouped. “Twice the sum of xxx and 444” is 2(x+4)2(x + 4)2(x+4), not 2x+42x + 42x+4.

Key takeaways

  • A variable stands for a number; 3n3n3n means 3×n3 \times n3×n.
  • Evaluate an expression to find its value. Solve an equation to find values that make it true.
  • Translate the meaning of a phrase, including subtraction order and any grouping.
  • Terms carry their signs. A coefficient multiplies a variable; a constant is a number term.
  • Substitute values in parentheses, then follow the order of operations. Different inputs can give different results.

A bit of history (optional)

François Viète made his living as a lawyer and royal adviser in France. Mathematics was something he pursued alongside that work. When political enemies drove him from the royal court, he spent five years away from public duties.

In 159115911591, he published a book that used letters for both known and unknown quantities. His choice was different from today’s familiar xxx: vowels stood for unknowns, and consonants stood for known quantities. That let him describe a whole family of problems without choosing particular numbers at the start.

Viète’s years away from court became a period of major mathematical work. A political setback had given the busy adviser time to develop the ideas for which he is remembered.
