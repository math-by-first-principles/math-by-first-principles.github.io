    1. Four terms, two kinds . Foundational, 13 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 13 pts

    An expression can be shortened without ever choosing a value for its letter, provided that only terms of the same kind are gathered. This question works on 10m+8−3m−1310m + 8 - 3m - 1310m+8−3m−13 and then asks what makes the gathering legitimate in the first place.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Simplify 10m+8−3m−1310m + 8 - 3m - 1310m+8−3m−13 as far as it will go, and state how many terms your result has.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Justify the step that merges 10m10m10m and −3m-3m−3m into a single term, naming the property that permits it. Write the line of algebra that does it.

      Complete the derivation Each line should follow from the one above it. Say what lets you take each step. 5 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Explain why the four terms of 10m+8−3m−1310m + 8 - 3m - 1310m+8−3m−13 may be reordered so that the two multiples of mmm sit next to each other, and why no reordering could ever let a plain number join a multiple of mmm.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Every term carries the sign printed in front of it, so read the whole expression as a sum of four signed terms before moving anything at all.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      The statement a(b+c)=ab+aca(b + c) = ab + aca(b+c)=ab+ac is an equation, so it may be read from either end. Read from the right it turns two products into one.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask what the collecting step actually consumes. It needs a factor present in both terms, and a plain number has no letter to offer it.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    7m−57m - 57m−5, an expression of two terms.

    Part B

    10m−3m=m(10−3)=7m10m - 3m = m(10 - 3) = 7m10m−3m=m(10−3)=7m. The shared factor mmm comes out in front, and only the coefficients take part in the arithmetic.

    Part C

    Reordering is legal because subtracting is adding the opposite and a sum may be added in any order, so each term travels with its own sign. A plain number shares no factor of mmm with a multiple of mmm, so the collecting step has no shared letter to pull out and the two stay apart.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Each term carries the sign printed in front of it, so the four terms are +10m+10m+10m, +8+8+8, −3m-3m−3m and −13-13−13. Two of them are multiples of mmm and two are plain numbers, and only terms of the same kind may be gathered.

    Collect the multiples of mmm and the plain numbers separately, adding and subtracting the coefficients:

    10m−3m=7m,8−13=−5.10m - 3m = 7m, \qquad 8 - 13 = -5.10m−3m=7m,8−13=−5.

    Writing the two results side by side gives

    10m+8−3m−13=7m−5.10m + 8 - 3m - 13 = 7m - 5.10m+8−3m−13=7m−5.

    Nothing further can be done. One term is a multiple of mmm and the other is a plain number, so the two are unlike and the expression stands at two terms.

    Part B

    The distributive property says that a(b+c)=ab+aca(b + c) = ab + aca(b+c)=ab+ac. That is an equation, so it may be read from either end, and read from right to left it says something equally true: a factor common to two terms can be pulled out in front of them.

    Both terms here are built on the same factor mmm, since 10m=m⋅1010m = m \cdot 1010m=m⋅10 and 3m=m⋅33m = m \cdot 33m=m⋅3. Subtracting is adding the opposite, so the pair is m⋅10m \cdot 10m⋅10 added to m⋅(−3)m \cdot (-3)m⋅(−3), and pulling the shared mmm out in front gives

    10m−3m=m(10−3).10m - 3m = m(10 - 3).10m−3m=m(10−3).

    Inside the parentheses sits ordinary arithmetic with no letter in it at all, and 10−3=710 - 3 = 710−3=7, so

    m(10−3)=m(7)=7m.m(10 - 3) = m(7) = 7m.m(10−3)=m(7)=7m.

    No new rule was invented anywhere in that line. Combining like terms is the distributive property collecting a shared factor, with the coefficients doing the only arithmetic in sight.

    Part C

    Two separate facts are at work, and they are worth separating.

    First, the reordering. A subtraction is an addition of the opposite, so the expression is really a pure sum:

    10m+8+(−3m)+(−13).10m + 8 + (-3m) + (-13).10m+8+(−3m)+(−13).

    A sum may be added in any order and in any grouping without changing its total, which is the property proved back in chapter 1. So the four terms may be shuffled freely, provided each keeps the sign it arrived with. Placing the two multiples of mmm side by side is exactly such a shuffle, and that is why the simplified form does not depend on the order in which the terms happened to be written.

    Second, the refusal. Collecting works by pulling out a factor the two terms share, and 888 and −13-13−13 contain no mmm whatever, so a group such as 10m+810m + 810m+8 has no factor of mmm to pull out. A number can still come out, since 10m+8=2(5m+4)10m + 8 = 2(5m + 4)10m+8=2(5m+4), but that leaves a sum inside the parentheses rather than a single term, so the two have been rewritten and not merged. Treating the group as 18m18m18m would count the plain 888 as though it were eight copies of mmm, and one value shows that it is not:

    10(4)+8=48,18(4)=72.10(4) + 8 = 48, \qquad 18(4) = 72.10(4)+8=48,18(4)=72.

    So the two multiples of mmm join, the two plain numbers join, and the two groups stay where they are.

    In one line

    10m+8−3m−13=7m−510m + 8 - 3m - 13 = 7m - 510m+8−3m−13=7m−5, two terms. The merge of 10m10m10m and −3m-3m−3m is the distributive property read backward, 10m−3m=m(10−3)=7m10m - 3m = m(10 - 3) = 7m10m−3m=m(10−3)=7m. The terms may be reordered because a sum may be added in any order once each term keeps its own sign, and a plain number can never join a multiple of mmm because the two share no factor of mmm to pull out.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Substitute −2-2−2 for every www, each one inside its own parentheses so that no minus sign drifts away from the number it belongs to:

    4w2−7w+3=4(−2)2−7(−2)+3.4w^2 - 7w + 3 = 4(-2)^2 - 7(-2) + 3.4w2−7w+3=4(−2)2−7(−2)+3.

    Exponents run before multiplications. Squaring −2-2−2 multiplies a negative by a negative, which is positive, so (−2)2=4(-2)^2 = 4(−2)2=4 and the first term is 4⋅4=164 \cdot 4 = 164⋅4=16. Notice that the exponent reached the −2-2−2 only, never the coefficient 444.

    The middle term is a multiplication of two negatives as well, because the sign in front of the 777 belongs to that term:

    −7(−2)=+14.-7(-2) = +14.−7(−2)=+14.

    Only additions are left, and they run left to right:

    16+14+3=33.16 + 14 + 3 = 33.16+14+3=33.

    The value is 333333. Both places where a negative met a negative produced a positive, which is why the result is larger than the expression looks at first glance.

    Part B

    Take the expressions one at a time and let the order of operations decide what each exponent is attached to.

    In −t2-t^2−t2 the exponent sits on the letter alone, so the squaring happens first and the leading minus is applied last. At t=7t = 7t=7,

    −t2=−(7)2=−(49)=−49,-t^2 = -(7)^2 = -(49) = -49,−t2=−(7)2=−(49)=−49,

    and at t=−7t = -7t=−7 the square is positive again, so the leading minus still hands back a negative:

    −t2=−(−7)2=−(49)=−49.-t^2 = -(-7)^2 = -(49) = -49.−t2=−(−7)2=−(49)=−49.

    In (−t)2(-t)^2(−t)2 the parentheses gather the minus sign in with the letter, so the whole of −t-t−t is squared. At t=7t = 7t=7 that is (−7)2=49(-7)^2 = 49(−7)2=49, and at t=−7t = -7t=−7 the opposite of −7-7−7 is 777, so it is (7)2=49(7)^2 = 49(7)2=49:

    (−7)2=49,(7)2=49.(-7)^2 = 49, \qquad (7)^2 = 49.(−7)2=49,(7)2=49.

    The four values are −49-49−49, 494949, −49-49−49 and 494949. The first expression came out negative at both inputs and the second came out positive at both.

    Part C

    A pair of tested values can refute a claim about every ttt, but it can never establish one, so argue about the expressions themselves.

    Squaring destroys a sign. A positive times itself is positive, a negative times itself is positive, and zero times itself is zero, so no square is ever negative. Since −t-t−t and ttt differ only in sign, their squares agree, while in the other expression the leading minus is applied after the squaring:

    (−t)2=t2and−t2=−(t2).(-t)^2 = t^2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad -t^2 = -(t^2).(−t)2=t2and−t2=−(t2).

    Now read off what each one is capable of. The first is never negative. The second is the opposite of something never negative, so it is never positive. Two numbers, one of them never negative and the other never positive, can be equal only if both are zero.

    That leaves a single candidate. A square is zero only when the number squared is zero, since any other number multiplied by itself gives a positive amount. So the two expressions agree at t=0t = 0t=0 and nowhere else, and there both come to 000. Everywhere else they are opposites of each other, and the gap grows quickly: at t=7t = 7t=7 the two values already stand 989898 apart.

    In one line

    Terms combine when they are of the same kind, and the test for same kind is finer than it first looks. This question simplifies a four-term expression, then examines a rule a classmate has proposed for deciding when terms may be merged.

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    7a2−3a7a^2 - 3a7a2−3a, whose terms have variable parts a2a^2a2 and aaa.

    Part B

    The pair 4a4a4a and 4a24a^24a2 obeys the classmate's rule but does not combine. The correct test is that the variable parts match exactly, the same letters raised to the same powers, with only the coefficients free to differ.

    Part C

    Pulling the shared a2a^2a2 out of the like pair leaves plain numbers inside, which collapse to one coefficient and give a single term. Pulling a factor out of the mixed pair leaves the letter still inside the parentheses, so there is no coefficient to collapse to and two kinds remain.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Four terms are present, each carrying the sign in front of it: +9a2+9a^2+9a2, +4a+4a+4a, −2a2-2a^2−2a2 and −7a-7a−7a. Sort them by variable part. Two are multiples of a2a^2a2 and two are multiples of aaa, and those are different kinds, because the powers differ.

    Combine each kind through its coefficients, leaving the variable part exactly as it is:

    9a2−2a2=7a2,4a−7a=−3a.9a^2 - 2a^2 = 7a^2, \qquad 4a - 7a = -3a.9a2−2a2=7a2,4a−7a=−3a.

    The second group came out negative, which is ordinary enough: 4−7=−34 - 7 = -34−7=−3, and the term is written −3a-3a−3a. Putting the groups together,

    9a2+4a−2a2−7a=7a2−3a.9a^2 + 4a - 2a^2 - 7a = 7a^2 - 3a.9a2+4a−2a2−7a=7a2−3a.

    Two terms remain, one with variable part a2a^2a2 and one with variable part aaa. They are unlike, so nothing further combines.

    Part B

    The classmate's rule asks only that a letter be shared, so any pair with matching letters and different powers will test it. Take 4a4a4a and 4a24a^24a2.

    An evaluation shows what would go wrong. At a=3a = 3a=3 the two terms are 4(3)=124(3) = 124(3)=12 and 4(3)2=364(3)^2 = 364(3)2=36, so the pair is worth

    4a+4a2=12+36=48.4a + 4a^2 = 12 + 36 = 48.4a+4a2=12+36=48.

    Merging them into 8a8a8a would report 8(3)=248(3) = 248(3)=24, and merging them into 8a38a^38a3 would report 8(27)=2168(27) = 2168(27)=216. Neither lands on 484848, so neither of the mergers the classmate's rule invites is equal to the pair.

    The repair is to compare the whole variable part instead of merely spotting a letter. Two terms are of the same kind when their variable parts are identical, the same letters each raised to the same power, and only their coefficients differ. By that test 4a4a4a and 4a24a^24a2 are unlike, while 4a4a4a and 9a9a9a are like and merge into 13a13a13a.

    Part C

    Combining like terms is the distributive property read from right to left, so the question is what that reading leaves behind.

    Take two multiples of a2a^2a2, say 8a28a^28a2 and 3a23a^23a2, and pull out the shared a2a^2a2:

    8a2−3a2=a2(8−3)=a2(5)=5a2.8a^2 - 3a^2 = a^2(8 - 3) = a^2(5) = 5a^2.8a2−3a2=a2(8−3)=a2(5)=5a2.

    What sits inside the parentheses is 8−38 - 38−3, plain arithmetic with no letter in it, so it collapses to the single number 555 and the pair really does become one term.

    Now try the same move on a mixed pair, 5a25a^25a2 and 2a2a2a. They do share a factor: both contain at least one aaa, since 5a2=a⋅5a5a^2 = a \cdot 5a5a2=a⋅5a and 2a=a⋅22a = a \cdot 22a=a⋅2. Pulling that shared aaa out in front is perfectly legal:

    5a2−2a=a(5a−2).5a^2 - 2a = a(5a - 2).5a2−2a=a(5a−2).

    But look at what is left inside. It is 5a−25a - 25a−2, which still contains the letter, so it is not a number and cannot be folded into a coefficient. The expression has been rewritten, not shortened into one term, and the two kinds are still visible inside the parentheses.

    That is the whole distinction. Terms are of the same kind exactly when the variable factor they share is the entire variable part of each, so that pulling it out leaves nothing but numbers behind, and it is those numbers, the coefficients, that the arithmetic then finishes off. Whenever the letter survives inside the parentheses, no amount of rearranging will produce a single term.

    In one line

    9a2+4a−2a2−7a=7a2−3a9a^2 + 4a - 2a^2 - 7a = 7a^2 - 3a9a2+4a−2a2−7a=7a2−3a, with variable parts a2a^2a2 and aaa. The pair 4a4a4a and 4a24a^24a2 obeys the classmate's rule yet cannot be combined, so the rule must compare whole variable parts, the same letters raised to the same powers. Like terms merge because pulling out the shared variable part leaves only numbers inside the parentheses; a mixed pair leaves the letter inside, so it can be rewritten but never reduced to one term.
