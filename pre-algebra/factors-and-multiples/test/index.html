    Exactly one of these four statements is true. Which one?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    You chose A: Counts the single factor of 111 twice, once as the 111 and once as the number itself, although a prime needs two DISTINCT factors and 111 supplies one.

    You chose C: Counts the factor 222 as an extra factor of the number 222, although there it IS the number itself, so the list 1,21, 21,2 still holds only two entries.

    You chose D: Reads odd as the test for prime, although 9=3×39 = 3 \times 39=3×3 is odd and has three factors; being odd only rules out the divisor 222.

    Prime and composite are settled by a count of DISTINCT factors: exactly two makes a number prime, more than two makes it composite.

    factors of 1:  1factors of 2:  1,2factors of 9:  1,3,9\text{factors of } 1: \; 1 \qquad \text{factors of } 2: \; 1, 2 \qquad \text{factors of } 9: \; 1, 3, 9factors of 1:1factors of 2:1,2factors of 9:1,3,9

    The list for 111 holds one number, which is neither exactly two nor more than two, so 111 meets neither definition. The number 222 has exactly two factors and is prime, its evenness costing it nothing because the factor 222 is the number itself rather than a third factor. And 999 is odd with three factors, so odd numbers can be composite.

    One factor tree for 468468468 ends with the leaves 222, 222, 333, 333 and 131313. Another ends with the leaves 222, 999, 222 and 131313. Which statement is true?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    You chose A: Reads a mismatch as evidence that the route matters, although a list still holding a composite is not a finished tree to compare with.

    You chose B: Counts a product containing the composite 999 as a prime factorization, although every factor written in one has to be prime.

    You chose C: Takes a two-digit leaf to be composite because it is the largest one there, although the only factors of 131313 are 111 and 131313.

    A tree is finished exactly when every leaf is prime. In the second list the leaf 999 is not: its digits add to 999, so 333 divides it.

    9=3×3,2×9×2×13=4689 = 3 \times 3, \qquad 2 \times 9 \times 2 \times 13 = 4689=3×3,2×9×2×13=468

    Splitting that leaf turns the second list into 222, 333, 333, 222, 131313, which is the first list in a different order.

    468=22×32×13468 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 13468=22×32×13

    A whole number greater than 111 has one prime factorization apart from the order of its factors, so two finished trees can never disagree. Where a mismatch shows up, one of the trees has been stopped a branch early, which is exactly what the leaf 999 records here.

  1. Problem 1 One digit, two numbers

    The same digit fills both blanks in 4,4□44{,}4\square44,4□4 and 6□16\square16□1. The first number must be divisible by 888, and the second must NOT be divisible by 999. What digit works?

    1. Hint 1

      The digit has to meet both requirements, so start with the digits the requirement on the first number allows.

    2. Hint 2

      The test for 888 reads the number formed by the last three digits, and the test for 999 reads the digit sum, which here must not be a multiple of 999.

    Answer

    666.

    Full solution

    The test for 888 reads the number formed by the last three digits of 4,4□44{,}4\square44,4□4, which is 4□44\square44□4.

    Since 400=8×50400=8\times50400=8×50, the block 4□44\square44□4 is a multiple of 888 exactly when the two-digit number □4\square4□4 is.

    The multiples of 888 below 100100100 that end in 444 are 242424 and 646464.

    424=8×53424=8\times53424=8×53 464=8×58464=8\times58464=8×58

    So the digit is 222 or 666.

    The digit sum of 6□16\square16□1 is 777 plus the missing digit.

    With 222 the digit sum is 999, a multiple of 999, so 621621621 is divisible by 999 and the digit 222 is ruled out.

    621=9×69621=9\times69621=9×69

    With 666 the digit sum is 131313, which is not a multiple of 999, so 661661661 is not divisible by 999.

    The digit is 666, and the first number then passes the test for 888.

    4,464=8×5584{,}464=8\times5584,464=8×558

    Answer

    666.

    Key idea

    When a digit must pass one test and fail another, list the digits the first test allows and strike out those that pass the second.

  2. Problem 2 The two sides

    A card shows a prime number on each side, and the two primes add to 252525. What are the two primes?

    1. Hint 1

      Think about whether the total is odd or even, and what that says about the two sides.

    2. Hint 2

      An odd total needs one even number and one odd number, so ask which primes are even.

    Answer

    222 and 232323.

    Full solution

    Two odd numbers add to an even number, and so do two even numbers.

    The total 252525 is odd, so one side shows an even number and the other an odd number.

    The only even prime is 222, because every larger even number has 222 as a factor besides 111 and itself.

    So one side shows 222, and the other side shows the rest of the total.

    25−2=2325-2=2325−2=23

    The number 232323 is prime: it is odd, its digit sum 555 is not a multiple of 333, and the next candidate, 555, already passes it when multiplied by itself, since

    5×5=25.5\times5=25.5×5=25.

    So its only factors are 111 and 232323, and the two primes are 222 and 232323.

    Answer

    222 and 232323.

    Key idea

    An odd sum of two primes has to use the even prime, 222.

  3. Problem 3 The shared divisors

    Three numbers have prime-power forms 23×322^3\times3^223×32, 22×3×52^2\times3\times522×3×5, and 2×32×132\times3^2\times132×32×13. List every positive whole number that divides all three.

    1. Hint 1

      A factor shared by all three can contain only primes present in every number.

    2. Hint 2

      For each shared prime, a common divisor can hold no more copies than the number with the fewest; list every choice within those limits.

    Answer

    111, 222, 333 and 666.

    Full solution

    The prime 555 appears only in the second number and 131313 only in the third, so neither can be part of a divisor common to all three.

    The primes 222 and 333 appear in all three.

    The prime 222 appears three, two and one times, so a common divisor can hold at most one copy, because the third number holds only one.

    The prime 333 appears two, one and two times, so a common divisor can hold at most one copy, because the second number holds only one.

    So a common divisor holds no 222 or one 222, no 333 or one 333, and no other prime.

    The four choices give 111, 222, 333 and 2×32\times32×3, which is 666.

    The three numbers are 727272, 606060 and 234234234, and 666 divides each of them.

    72÷6=1272\div6=1272÷6=12 60÷6=1060\div6=1060÷6=10 234÷6=39234\div6=39234÷6=39

    Each of 111, 222 and 333 divides 666, so each divides all three numbers as well.

    A second copy of 222 would not divide 234234234, and a second copy of 333 would not divide 606060, so the list is complete.

    Answer

    111, 222, 333 and 666.

    Key idea

    The caps on each prime build every common divisor, and here those divisors are exactly the factors of the GCF, 666.

  4. Problem 4 The warehouse code

    A warehouse code is 3,8873{,}8873,887. Is it prime or composite? Justify your answer, and if it is composite, give its prime-power form.

    1. Hint 1

      A number is composite as soon as it has a divisor other than 111 and itself, so search for one.

    2. Hint 2

      The tests for 222, 333 and 555 all fail, so divide: one exact division proves the code composite, while reaching a prime that times itself passes 3,8873{,}8873,887 without one would prove it prime.

    3. Hint 3

      If a prime divides it, keep dividing each quotient until the quotient is itself prime.

    Answer

    Composite; 3,887=132×233{,}887=13^2\times233,887=132×23.

    Full solution

    The number 3,8873{,}8873,887 is odd, its digit sum is 262626, and it ends in 777, so 222, 333 and 555 do not divide it.

    Division by 777 and by 111111 leaves a remainder.

    3,887=7×555+23{,}887=7\times555+23,887=7×555+2 3,887=11×353+43{,}887=11\times353+43,887=11×353+4

    The next prime, 131313, divides it exactly.

    3,887÷13=2993{,}887\div13=2993,887÷13=299

    So 131313 is a factor besides 111 and 3,8873{,}8873,887, and the code is composite.

    Keep dividing.

    Any factor of 299299299 is also a factor of 3,8873{,}8873,887, so 222, 333, 555, 777 and 111111 are already ruled out, and 131313 is next.

    299÷13=23299\div13=23299÷13=23

    The quotient 232323 is prime, since neither 222 nor 333 divides it and 5×5=255\times5=255×5=25 passes 232323.

    Collect the primes.

    3,887=13×13×233{,}887=13\times13\times233,887=13×13×23 3,887=132×233{,}887=13^2\times233,887=132×23

    Multiplying back,

    169×23=3,887.169\times23=3{,}887.169×23=3,887.

    Answer

    Composite; 3,887=132×233{,}887=13^2\times233,887=132×23.

    Key idea

    Trial division by the primes in order classifies a number, and continuing it on each quotient builds the factorization.

  5. Problem 5 Divisible by both

    What is the smallest positive whole number divisible by both 282828 and 150150150? Give its ordinary value and its prime-power form.

    1. Hint 1

      The number must contain enough prime factors to include each required divisor.

    2. Hint 2

  6. Problem 6 The wooden strips

    Two wooden strips are 565656 cm and 848484 cm long. They are cut into pieces of one common whole-number length, with no waste. What is the smallest total number of pieces the two strips can be cut into, and how long is each piece?

    1. Hint 1

      The piece length must divide both original lengths.

    2. Hint 2

      Longer pieces mean fewer of them, so find the greatest length that fits both strips exactly, then count the pieces.

    Answer

    555 pieces, each 282828 cm long.

    Full solution

    Each strip is cut into equal pieces with no waste, so the piece length divides both 565656 and 848484.

    Longer pieces mean fewer of them, so the fewest pieces come from the greatest such length, the GCF of the two lengths.

    Their prime-power forms are 56=23×756=2^3\times756=23×7 and

    84=22×3×7.84=2^2\times3\times7.84=22×3×7.

    The shared primes at their lowest powers give

    22×7=282^2\times7=2822×7=28

    Thus each piece is 282828 cm long.

    The first strip gives 56÷28=256\div28=256÷28=2 pieces and the second gives 84÷28=384\div28=384÷28=3.

    2+3=52+3=52+3=5

    So the fewest pieces in all is 555.

    Answer

    555 pieces, each 282828 cm long.

    Key idea

    Asking for the fewest equal pieces is asking for the longest common length, which is the GCF.

  7. Problem 7 A test for twenty-five

    Explain why a whole number is divisible by 252525 exactly when the number formed by its last two digits is divisible by 252525. Then find every digit that can fill the blank in 4,1□54{,}1\square54,1□5 to make it divisible by 757575.

    1. Hint 1

      Split the number into its whole hundreds and the number formed by its last two digits.

    2. Hint 2

      Ask what 100100100 is a multiple of, and what that says about any whole number of hundreds.

    3. Hint 3

      Since 75=3×2575=3\times2575=3×25, and 333 and 252525 share no factor above 111, the number has to pass a test for each.

    Answer

    Only 222, giving 4,1254{,}1254,125.

    Full solution

    Write the number as its whole hundreds plus the number LLL formed by its last two digits.

    Every hundred is a multiple of 252525.

    100=4×25100=4\times25100=4×25

    So the hundreds part is a multiple of 252525, whatever its digits are.

    Adding a multiple of 252525 does not change the remainder on division by 252525.

    So the whole number and LLL leave the same remainder, and one is divisible by 252525 exactly when the other is.

    Now 75=3×2575=3\times2575=3×25, and 333 and 252525 share no factor above 111, so 4,1□54{,}1\square54,1□5 is divisible by 757575 exactly when it passes the tests for both 333 and 252525.

    Its last two digits form a number ending in 555.

    The multiples of 252525 below 100100100 are 000, 252525, 505050 and 757575, and only 252525 and 757575 end in 555, so the digit is 222 or 777.

    The digit sum of 4,1254{,}1254,125 is 121212, a multiple of 333, while that of 4,1754{,}1754,175 is 171717, which is not.

    So the digit is 222 alone.

    4,125=75×554{,}125=75\times554,125=75×55

    Answer

    Only 222, giving 4,1254{,}1254,125.

    Because 100100100 is a multiple of 252525, the last two digits alone decide divisibility by 252525.

  8. Problem 8 Sixes and twelves

    Two whole numbers greater than 111 are multiplied, and the result can be written as one or more 666s multiplied together, such as 6×6×66\times6\times66×6×6. A student says that the GCF of the two numbers times their LCM cannot be written as one or more 121212s multiplied together. Is the student right? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the GCF times the LCM of two numbers with the two numbers multiplied together.

    2. Hint 2

      Count the copies of 222 and of 333 in a product of 666s, and in a product of 121212s.

    3. Hint 3

      A whole number greater than 111 has only one prime factorization, so its count of each prime is fixed.

    Answer

    Yes, the student is right.

    Full solution

    For two numbers, the GCF times the LCM equals the two numbers multiplied together.

    So the GCF times the LCM here is the same number NNN as the product, which is a product of 666s.

    Each 6=2×36=2\times36=2×3 brings one 222 and one 333, so a product of 666s holds as many 222s as 333s.

    Each 12=22×312=2^2\times312=22×3 brings two 222s and one 333, so a product of 121212s holds twice as many 222s as 333s.

    The number NNN is greater than 111, so it has exactly one prime factorization apart from order, and that fixes how many 333s it holds.

    That count is at least one, since NNN is made of 666s, so the count of 222s cannot both equal it and be twice it.

    So NNN cannot also be a product of 121212s, and the student is right.

    For example, 444 and 999 multiply to 6×6=366\times6=366×6=36, and their GCF times LCM is 1×36=361\times36=361×36=36, which lies between 121212 and

    12×12=144.12\times12=144.12×12=144.

    Answer

    Yes, the student is right.

    Key idea

    The product rule turns a claim about the GCF and LCM of two numbers into one about their product, and unique factorization fixes the count of every prime in it.

  9. Problem 9 The screening programs

    One program accepts a positive whole number exactly when it is divisible by 222, 333, and 555. Another accepts it exactly when it is divisible by 666 and 101010. Do the two programs accept exactly the same numbers? Justify your answer.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the factors required by each program.

    2. Hint 2

      Take a number the first program accepts and ask whether it must pass each check of the second; then take a number the second accepts and ask the same about the first.

    3. Hint 3

      Two tests combine into one test for the product of their divisors exactly when those divisors share no factor above 111.

    Answer

    Yes.

    Full solution

    A number accepted by the first program has factors 222 and 333.

    Since they share no factor above 111, it is divisible by their product, 666.

    Its factors 222 and 555 likewise make it divisible by 101010.

    It therefore passes the second program.

    Conversely, a number divisible by 666 is divisible by 222 and 333, and a number divisible by 101010 is divisible by 555.

    So every number passing the second program passes all three checks in the first.

    Both directions hold, proving that the programs accept exactly the same numbers.

    Answer

    Yes.

    Key idea

    Two collections of divisibility checks are equivalent when each guarantees every check in the other.

  10. Problem 10 Two claims about three numbers

    Let A=23×3A=2^3\times3A=23×3, B=2×33B=2\times3^3B=2×33 and C=22×11C=2^2\times11C=22×11. A student makes two claims.     1. Hint 1

      Ask how many numbers each GCF and LCM is taken over, and recall how far the product rule reaches.

    2. Hint 2

      The GCF and the LCM of three numbers are read off prime by prime, just as for two numbers.

    3. Hint 3

    Answer

    The first claim is true: both sides are 57,02457{,}02457,024. The second claim is false: the GCF of all three times the LCM of all three is 4,7524{,}7524,752, while A×B×C=57,024A\times B\times C=57{,}024A×B×C=57,024.

    Full solution

    For the first claim, work with A=24A=24A=24 and B=54B=54B=54 alone.

    The prime 222 appears three times in AAA and once in BBB, and the prime 333 once in AAA and three times in BBB.

    GCF⁡(A,B)=2×3\operatorname{GCF}(A,B)=2\times3GCF(A,B)=2×3 LCM⁡(A,B)=23×33\operatorname{LCM}(A,B)=2^3\times3^3LCM(A,B)=23×33

    These are 666 and 216216216.

    6×216=1,2966\times216=1{,}2966×216=1,296 24×54=1,29624\times54=1{,}29624×54=1,296
