  1. Problem 1 A fourth color

    ★ Stretch Difficulty: 1 of 3 stars, Stretch

    A collection contains positive whole numbers of red, blue, green, and yellow tiles. The ratio of red to blue tiles is 2:32:32:3, and the ratio of blue to green tiles is 4:54:54:5. The number of red tiles plus the number of yellow tiles equals the number of green tiles.

    Find the smallest possible total number of tiles. Then describe every possible total and prove that your description is complete.

    1. Hint 1

      Make the blue entries in the two ratios agree before combining them.

    2. Hint 2

      Once the four-color ratio is known, check whether its scale factor must be a whole number.

    Answer

    The smallest total is 42. Every possible total is a positive multiple of 42; the four counts are 8t,12t,15t,7t8t,12t,15t,7t8t,12t,15t,7t for a positive integer ttt.

    Full solution

    The ratios 2:32:32:3 and 4:54:54:5 share blue, so rewrite them as 8:128:128:12 and 12:1512:1512:15.

    Thus red, blue, and green have ratio 8:12:158:12:158:12:15.

    Since red plus yellow equals green, yellow contributes the difference 15−8=715-8=715−8=7 parts.

    All four colors therefore have ratio 8:12:15:78:12:15:78:12:15:7, containing 42 parts altogether.

    We must still justify that a fractional scale cannot create a smaller collection.

    Let one part represent ttt tiles, so red and green contain 8t8t8t and 15t15t15t tiles.

    Both are whole numbers.

    Their combination 2(8t)−15t=t2(8t)-15t=t2(8t)−15t=t is consequently a whole number too.

    Positivity makes ttt a positive integer.

    Taking t=1t=1t=1 gives counts 8,12,15,78,12,15,78,12,15,7, which satisfy every condition and total 42.

    Every positive integer ttt gives a valid collection, and the argument above shows that every collection has this form.

    Hence the possible totals are exactly 42,84,126,…42,84,126,\ldots42,84,126,….

    Answer

    The smallest total is 42. Every possible total is a positive multiple of 42; the four counts are 8t,12t,15t,7t8t,12t,15t,7t8t,12t,15t,7t for a positive integer ttt.

    Key idea

    Matching ratio parts determines proportions; whole-number conditions determine the allowed scale.

  2. Problem 2 Half the time or half the distance?

    ★ Stretch Difficulty: 1 of 3 stars, Stretch

    Two carts start together and travel for the same positive amount of time. Cart A travels at 6 kilometers per hour for the first half of its travel time and at 12 kilometers per hour for the second half.

    Cart B travels at 6 kilometers per hour for the first half of its total distance and at 12 kilometers per hour for the second half. Neither cart stops.

    (a) Which cart travels farther, and by what percentage of the shorter distance?

    (b) Keep Cart B's first-half speed at 6 kilometers per hour. What second-half speed would make it travel the same distance as Cart A in the same time?

    1. Hint 1

      For Cart A, use one hour as a convenient travel time. For Cart B, use two equal distance pieces.

    2. Hint 2

      For part (b), suppose the common time is one hour. Cart B must cover 4.5 kilometers in each distance half.

    Answer

    Cart A travels 12.5%12.5\%12.5% farther. Cart B needs a second-half speed of 18 kilometers per hour to tie Cart A.

    Full solution

    The comparison does not depend on the common travel time, so use one hour.

    Cart A covers 6×12+12×12=96\times\frac12+12\times\frac12=96×21​+12×21​=9 kilometers.

    Its equal-time average speed is 9 kilometers per hour.

    For Cart B, consider a journey consisting of 6 kilometers at each speed.

    The slow half takes one hour; the fast half takes half an hour.

    Its average speed is therefore 12÷32=812\div\frac32=812÷23​=8 kilometers per hour.

    In the common one-hour period, Cart B travels 8 kilometers.

    Cart A travels 1 kilometer farther, which is 18=12.5%\frac18=12.5\%81​=12.5% of Cart B's distance.

    To tie Cart A, Cart B must travel 9 kilometers in one hour.

    Its first 4.5 kilometers at 6 kilometers per hour take 34\frac3443​ hour.

    Only 14\frac1441​ hour remains for the other 4.5 kilometers, requiring 4.5÷14=184.5\div\frac14=184.5÷41​=18 kilometers per hour.

    Averaging 6 and 12 gives the right speed only when the two speeds are used for equal times.

    Equal distances give more time to the slower speed.

    Answer

    Cart A travels 12.5%12.5\%12.5% farther. Cart B needs a second-half speed of 18 kilometers per hour to tie Cart A.

    Key idea

    An average speed must weight speeds by time, even when the journey is described by distance.

  3. Problem 3 Reordering four price changes

    ★ Stretch Difficulty: 1 of 3 stars, Stretch

    A positive price undergoes four changes: an increase of 25%25\%25%, an increase of 20%20\%20%, a decrease of 20%20\%20%, and a decrease of 25%25\%25%. Each change is applied once to the price then in effect, in any order.

    (a) Does the order affect the final price? Find the final percentage change.

    (b) Across all orders, what are the greatest and least prices that can appear immediately after a change, as percentages of the original price? Prove both bounds are attainable and cannot be exceeded.

    1. Hint 1

      Represent each change by the fraction by which it multiplies the current price.

    2. Hint 2

      To bound an intermediate price, ask which completed changes could increase its product and which could decrease it.

    Answer

    The final price is 90%90\%90% of the original, a 10%10\%10% decrease, in every order. Intermediate prices can be as high as 150%150\%150% or as low as 60%60\%60% of the original.

    Full solution

    The four multipliers are 54,65,45,34\frac54,\frac65,\frac45,\frac3445​,56​,54​,43​.

    The final price is the original price multiplied by all four.

    Multiplication can be reordered, and the product is

    54×65×45×34=910.\frac54\times\frac65\times\frac45\times\frac34=\frac9{10}.45​×56​×54​×43​=109​.

    Thus every order finishes at 90%90\%90% of the original price.

    Adding the signed percentages would incorrectly predict no change, because the percentages use changing prices as their wholes.

    At any intermediate stage, the price multiplier is the product of some of these four factors.

    A largest possible product includes both factors greater than 1 and neither factor less than 1.

    It is 54×65=32\frac54\times\frac65=\frac3245​×56​=23​, attained by applying both increases first.

    Similarly, a smallest possible product includes both decrease factors and neither increase factor.

    It is 45×34=35\frac45\times\frac34=\frac3554​×43​=53​, attained by applying both decreases first.

    Adding any increase raises this product; omitting any decrease also raises it.

    These arguments prove the 150%150\%150% and 60%60\%60% bounds for every order.

    Answer

    The final price is 90%90\%90% of the original, a 10%10\%10% decrease, in every order. Intermediate prices can be as high as 150%150\%150% or as low as 60%60\%60% of the original.

    Key idea

    Percent changes multiply, and intermediate extremes can be bounded by choosing which factors are present.

  4. Problem 4 A simultaneous exchange

    ★★ Challenge Difficulty: 2 of 3 stars, Challenge

    Jug A contains 9 liters of a well-mixed drink that is 20%20\%20% concentrate. Jug B contains 6 liters of a well-mixed drink that is 70%70\%70% concentrate. Volumes add normally when the drinks are mixed.

    The same quantity is removed from each jug into separate clean containers before either quantity is poured into the other jug. After the exchange, each jug is mixed thoroughly. The two final drinks have the same concentration.

    How many liters were exchanged in each direction, and what is the final concentration? Explain why no other exchange quantity works.

    1. Hint 1

      The exchange changes neither the total drink volume nor the total amount of concentrate.

    2. Hint 2

      Each liter exchanged increases the concentrate in Jug A by the difference between the original concentrations.

    Answer

    185=3.6\frac{18}{5}=3.6518​=3.6 liters in each direction; both final concentrations are 40%40\%40%.

    Full solution

    The original concentrate amounts are 9×0.20=1.89\times0.20=1.89×0.20=1.8 liters and 6×0.70=4.26\times0.70=4.26×0.70=4.2 liters.

    Altogether, 15 liters of drink contain 6 liters of concentrate.

    If both jugs finish at the same concentration, that concentration must therefore be 6/15=40%6/15=40\%6/15=40%.

    Jug A keeps its volume of 9 liters.

    To reach 40%40\%40%, its concentrate amount must become 3.6 liters, a gain of 1.8 liters.

    Each liter removed from A takes away 0.2 liter of concentrate; each liter received from the original mixture in B brings 0.7 liter.

    The net gain is 0.5 liter per exchanged liter.

    Consequently, the required exchange is 1.8÷0.5=3.61.8\div0.5=3.61.8÷0.5=3.6 liters.

    This is feasible because it is less than either jug's initial volume.

    Jug B then loses 1.8 liters of concentrate, leaving 4.2−1.8=2.44.2-1.8=2.44.2−1.8=2.4 liters in 6 liters of drink, also 40%40\%40%.

    Any equal final concentration must be 40%40\%40%, and only this exchange quantity produces A's required gain.

    The simultaneous-removal condition matters: both transferred portions have their original concentrations.

    Answer

    185=3.6\frac{18}{5}=3.6518​=3.6 liters in each direction; both final concentrations are 40%40\%40%.

    Key idea

    Conservation fixes the common target before any transfer calculation is needed.

  5. Problem 5 Reconstructing a rounded survey

    ★★ Challenge Difficulty: 2 of 3 stars, Challenge

    A survey of at most 100 students asks each student to choose exactly one of hiking or cycling. The reported percentage choosing hiking is 57%57\%57%, rounded to the nearest whole percent. A percentage exactly halfway between two whole percentages rounds upward.

    Later, exactly four hiking voters switch to cycling, and the two choices then have equal numbers of voters. No one else changes a vote.

    Find every possible number of surveyed students and the original counts for each choice. Prove that the list is complete.

    1. Hint 1

      Four voters changing sides reduces the difference between the groups by eight.

    2. Hint 2

      The original hiking share is one-half of the total, plus four students. A reported 57%57\%57% means the true percentage is at least 56.5%56.5\%56.5% and less than 57.5%57.5\%57.5%.

    Answer

    Possible totals are 54, 56, 58, and 60. The corresponding original hiking/cycling counts are (31,23)(31,23)(31,23), (32,24)(32,24)(32,24), (33,25)(33,25)(33,25), and (34,26)(34,26)(34,26).

    Full solution

    Let NNN be the total.

    After the switch, each group has N/2N/2N/2 students, so NNN is even.

    Originally, hiking had N/2+4N/2+4N/2+4 voters and cycling had N/2−4N/2-4N/2−4.

    Thus the true hiking share exceeds 50%50\%50% by the fraction 4/N4/N4/N of the full survey.

    To round to 57%57\%57%, that extra fraction must be at least 0.0650.0650.065 and less than 0.0750.0750.075.

    Therefore

    0.065≤4N<0.075.0.065\leq\frac4N<0.075.0.065≤N4​<0.075.

    Since NNN is positive, the upper inequality gives

    N>4/0.075=5313.N>4/0.075=53\frac13.N>4/0.075=5331​.

    The lower inequality gives

    N≤4/0.065=61713.N\leq4/0.065=61\frac7{13}.N≤4/0.065=61137​.

    The only even integers in this interval are 54,56,58,6054,56,58,6054,56,58,60.

    Using N/2+4N/2+4N/2+4 and N/2−4N/2-4N/2−4 gives the four stated pairs.

    Every pair has positive whole-number counts, the initial difference is eight, and its hiking share lies in the required rounding interval.

    Switching four voters therefore gives equality in every case.

    All possible surveys had to satisfy the interval and evenness conditions, so there are no others.

    Answer

    Possible totals are 54, 56, 58, and 60. The corresponding original hiking/cycling counts are (31,23)(31,23)(31,23), (32,24)(32,24)(32,24), (33,25)(33,25)(33,25), and (34,26)(34,26)(34,26).

    Key idea

    A rounded percentage gives an interval; exact count information can reduce that interval to a finite list.

  6. Problem 6 The final partial cycle

    ★★ Challenge Difficulty: 2 of 3 stars, Challenge

    An empty tank has two pumps and a leak. With the leak sealed, Pump A alone would fill the tank in 12 minutes and Pump B alone would fill it in 20 minutes. The leak removes water at a constant rate of one full tank per 30 minutes whenever water is available.

    The leak stays open. Starting at time zero, both pumps run together for 2 minutes, then both are off for 1 minute. This 3-minute pattern repeats until the tank first becomes full.

    Exactly how long does filling take? Justify why the tank has not already filled during an earlier pump-on interval.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the net filling rate while both pumps run, then the net gain over one complete 3-minute cycle.

    2. Hint 2

      The tank may become full before the final cycle is complete. Check the highest water level in the previous cycle.

    Answer

    162316\frac231632​ minutes, or 16 minutes 40 seconds.

    Full solution

    Measure water in tankfuls.

    While both pumps run, the net rate is

    112+120−130=110\frac1{12}+\frac1{20}-\frac1{30}=\frac1{10}121​+201​−301​=101​

    tank per minute.

    Each 2-minute pumping interval adds 1/51/51/5 of a tank.

    The following off minute removes 1/301/301/30, so a complete cycle gains 1/5−1/30=1/61/5-1/30=1/61/5−1/30=1/6 of a tank.

    Even in the first off minute there is enough water for this full leak amount.

    After five complete cycles, 15 minutes have elapsed and the tank contains 5/65/65/6 of a tank.

    The remaining 1/61/61/6 takes (1/6)÷(1/10)=5/3(1/6)\div(1/10)=5/3(1/6)÷(1/10)=5/3 minutes of pumping.

    This is less than the available 2 minutes, so filling finishes at 15+5/3=162315+5/3=16\frac2315+5/3=1632​ minutes.

    To verify that no earlier filling was overlooked, examine the fifth pumping interval, the highest of the first five.

    It starts with 4/64/64/6 and ends with 4/6+1/5=13/154/6+1/5=13/154/6+1/5=13/15, below full.

    Earlier pumping peaks are still lower.

    Thus 16 minutes 40 seconds is the first filling time.

    Answer

    162316\frac231632​ minutes, or 16 minutes 40 seconds.

    Key idea

    A net gain per cycle locates the final cycle; the within-cycle rate determines the exact finish.

  7. Problem 7 A discount with a cap

    ★★ Challenge Difficulty: 2 of 3 stars, Challenge

    Three items cost 20, 30, and 50 dollars. You have three coupons: one for 20%20\%20% off one item, one for 30%30\%30% off one item, and one for 50%50\%50% off one item. The 50%50\%50% coupon has a maximum saving of 12 dollars; the other coupons have no cap. Every coupon must be used, exactly one per item. There are no other charges.

    Find the greatest and least possible savings as percentages of the original total price. Find every coupon assignment attaining either extreme, and give a systematic argument proving completeness.

    1. Hint 1

      The capped coupon does not necessarily belong on the most expensive item. First calculate its saving on each of the three items.

    2. Hint 2

      Split into three cases according to which item receives the capped coupon. Within each case, decide how to assign the 20%20\%20% and 30%30\%30% coupons for greatest or least saving.

    Answer

    Greatest saving: 31%31\%31%, with coupon percentages (50,20,30)(50,20,30)(50,20,30) or (20,50,30)(20,50,30)(20,50,30) on the items costing 20, 30, and 50 dollars, respectively. Least saving: 24%24\%24%, with coupon percentages (30,20,50)(30,20,50)(30,20,50) in that order.

    Full solution

    The original total is 100 dollars, so the number of dollars saved is also the percentage saved.

    The capped 50%50\%50% coupon saves 10 dollars on the 20-dollar item and 12 dollars on either of the other items.

    Once that coupon is placed, only the uncapped 20%20\%20% and 30%30\%30% coupons remain.

    Giving the larger percentage to the dearer remaining item gives the larger saving.

    Swapping the two changes the saving by 10%10\%10% of the difference between those two prices, so the comparison is strict.

    If the capped coupon is on the 20-dollar item, the largest total saving is 10+6+15=3110+6+15=3110+6+15=31 dollars; the smallest is 10+9+10=2910+9+10=2910+9+10=29 dollars.

    If it is on the 30-dollar item, the largest saving is 12+4+15=3112+4+15=3112+4+15=31 dollars; the smallest is 12+6+10=2812+6+10=2812+6+10=28 dollars.

    If it is on the 50-dollar item, the largest saving is 12+4+9=2512+4+9=2512+4+9=25 dollars; the smallest is 12+6+6=2412+6+6=2412+6+6=24 dollars.

    The three cases include every location of the capped coupon, and within each case both remaining orders have been resolved.

    Thus the greatest saving is 31 dollars, attained by exactly the two assignments stated, and the least is 24 dollars, attained by exactly one assignment.

    The percentages are therefore 31%31\%31% and 24%24\%24%.

    Answer

    Greatest saving: 31%31\%31%, with coupon percentages (50,20,30)(50,20,30)(50,20,30) or (20,50,30)(20,50,30)(20,50,30) on the items costing 20, 30, and 50 dollars, respectively. Least saving: 24%24\%24%, with coupon percentages (30,20,50)(30,20,50)(30,20,50) in that order.

    Key idea

    A cap can change which assignment is best. Separate the exceptional rule into cases before using a familiar comparison.

  9. Problem 9 Both groups improve, but the total falls

    ★★★ Deep challenge Difficulty: 3 of 3 stars, Deep challenge

    Two problem-solving events each have the same total number NNN of participants. Every participant belongs to one of two categories, experienced or newer; both categories attend each event. The people and the category proportions may differ between events.
