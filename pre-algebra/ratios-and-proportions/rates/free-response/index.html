Problem 1 of 10

  1. Problem 1 Supply labels

    Label A compares 333 meters of ribbon with 222 meters of fabric. Label B compares 333 meters of ribbon with a cost of 2 dollars. Which label gives a rate?

    1. Hint 1

      Look at the units, rather than the names of the materials.

    2. Hint 2

      A rate keeps two different units in the comparison.

    Answer

    Label B.

    Full solution

    Label A compares meters with meters, so it compares two lengths in the same unit.

    Label B compares meters with dollars, which are different units.

    Label B therefore gives a rate.

    As a unit rate, in meters per dollar, it is

    32=1.5\frac{3}{2}=1.523​=1.5

    Answer

    Label B.

    Key idea

    A rate compares quantities measured in different units.

  2. Problem 2 Thread feed

    A machine feeds 5.45.45.4 meters of thread in 1.21.21.2 minutes at a steady rate. How many meters does it feed per minute?

    1. Hint 1

      The requested amount belongs to one minute.

    2. Hint 2

      Meters per minute puts meters on top, so divide the meters by the number of minutes.

    Answer

    4.54.54.5 meters per minute.

    Full solution

    Dividing the time by 1.21.21.2 gives one minute, so divide the thread amount by the same number.

    5.4÷1.2=4.55.4\div1.2=4.55.4÷1.2=4.5

    The machine feeds 4.54.54.5 meters per minute.

    Check that 4.5×1.2=5.44.5\times1.2=5.44.5×1.2=5.4, the original thread amount.

    Answer

    4.54.54.5 meters per minute.

    Key idea

    Dividing the first quantity by the second gives the amount for one unit, even when the time is not a whole number.

  3. Problem 3 Moving belt

    A belt moves steadily at 1.251.251.25 meters per second. How far does it move in 242424 seconds?

    1. Hint 1

      The rate tells how much distance belongs to each second.

    2. Hint 2

      In twenty-four seconds the belt covers twenty-four copies of that one-second distance.

    Answer

    303030 meters.

    Full solution

    Multiply the distance for one second by the number of seconds.

    1.25×24=301.25\times24=301.25×24=30

    The belt moves 303030 meters.

    Dividing the result by 242424 seconds returns 1.251.251.25 meters per second.

    Answer

    303030 meters.

    Key idea

    Multiply an amount per unit by the number of units to find the total.

  4. Problem 4 Two cyclists

    Ana and Ben each cycle at a steady speed. Ana rides 393939 miles in 333 hours, and Ben rides 505050 miles in 444 hours. Who is faster, and how far does the faster rider travel in 666 hours?

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the distance each rider covers in one hour.

    2. Hint 2

      Scale the faster rider's miles per hour up to six hours.

    Answer

    Ana is faster; she rides 787878 miles in 666 hours.

    Full solution

    In miles per hour, Ana's speed is

    39÷3=1339\div3=1339÷3=13

    In miles per hour, Ben's speed is

    50÷4=12.550\div4=12.550÷4=12.5

    For a speed, the higher number of miles per hour is faster, and 131313 is greater than 12.512.512.5, so Ana is faster.

    Scaling Ana's unit rate up to six hours gives, in miles,

    13×6=7813\times6=7813×6=78

    Answer

    Ana is faster; she rides 787878 miles in 666 hours.

    Key idea

    Speeds in the same units compare directly, and a unit rate scales up to a total for any length of time.

  5. Problem 5 Reading log

    A reader reads 969696 words during the first minute, 144144144 during the next minute, and 606060 during the third and last minute. What is the average number of words read per minute across the whole log, and what is that average restated as words per 303030 seconds?

    1. Hint 1

      The whole log supplies one total word count and one total time.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide the total words by the three minutes.

    3. Hint 3

      Thirty seconds is half a minute, so divide both quantities of the per-minute rate by two.

    Answer

    100100100 words per minute, which is 505050 words per 303030 seconds.

    Full solution

    Combine the words read in all three minutes.

    96+144+60=30096+144+60=30096+144+60=300

    The log covers three minutes, so in words per minute the average rate is

    300÷3=100300\div3=100300÷3=100

    Three minutes at that average accounts for the same 300300300 words.

    Thirty seconds is half of sixty seconds, so dividing both quantities of the rate by 222 keeps it equal.

    In words per 303030 seconds, the average is

    100÷2=50100\div2=50100÷2=50

    Like the per-minute figure, this is an average, not the count in every half minute.

    Answer

    100100100 words per minute, which is 505050 words per 303030 seconds.

    Key idea

    A total divided by its duration gives an average rate even when the amount varies from minute to minute.

  6. Problem 6 Rain barrel

    A sprinkler draws water from a rain barrel at a steady rate of 212121 liters every 141414 minutes. The barrel holds 404040 liters when the sprinkler starts. How many liters remain in the barrel after the sprinkler runs for 181818 minutes?

    1. Hint 1

      Find the water used per minute before extending the running time.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the total water used from the starting amount.

    Answer

    131313 liters.

    Full solution

    In liters per minute, the sprinkler draws

    21÷14=1.521\div14=1.521÷14=1.5

    Over eighteen minutes, in liters, it draws

    1.5×18=271.5\times18=271.5×18=27

    The barrel began with forty liters, so in liters what remains is

    40−27=1340-27=1340−27=13

    The used and remaining amounts add back to the starting forty liters.

    Answer

    131313 liters.

    Key idea

    Use a unit rate to calculate consumption before subtracting it from a starting supply.

  7. Problem 7 Fabric suppliers

    Supplier A sells 333 meters of fabric for 12.60 dollars. Supplier B sells 505050 centimeters of the same fabric for 2.30 dollars. There are 100100100 centimeters in a meter, and both suppliers sell any requested length at their stated rate. Which supplier charges less for 2.52.52.5 meters, and how much less?

    1. Hint 1

      Put both prices on the same length unit.

    2. Hint 2

      Find dollars per meter for each supplier, then price two and a half meters at each rate.

    Answer

    Supplier A, by 1.00 dollar.

    Full solution

    In dollars per meter, Supplier A charges

    12.60÷3=4.2012.60\div3=4.2012.60÷3=4.20

    Two lengths of fifty centimeters make one meter, so in dollars per meter Supplier B charges

    2.30×2=4.602.30\times2=4.602.30×2=4.60

    Supplier A has the lower rate.

    For two and a half meters, in dollars, Supplier A charges

    4.20×2.5=10.504.20\times2.5=10.504.20×2.5=10.50

    For the same length, in dollars, Supplier B charges

    4.60×2.5=11.504.60\times2.5=11.504.60×2.5=11.50

    Supplier A charges less, and in dollars the difference is

    11.50−10.50=1.0011.50-10.50=1.0011.50−10.50=1.00

    Answer

    Supplier A, by 1.00 dollar.

    Key idea

    Rates must use the same units before their numerical values can be compared.

  8. Problem 8 Bead labels

    A set of identical beads contains 242424 beads and weighs 666 grams. One label says 444 beads per gram, and another says 0.250.250.25 gram per bead. Can both labels be correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      The first label is about one gram, and the second is about one bead.

    2. Hint 2

      Calculate each rate in the order its units name.

    Answer

    Yes; both labels are correct.

    Full solution

    Dividing the bead count by the weight in grams gives, in beads per gram,

    24÷6=424\div6=424÷6=4

    Dividing the weight in grams by the bead count gives, in grams per bead,

    6÷24=0.256\div24=0.256÷24=0.25

    The two rates answer different questions and agree with one another: four beads, each weighing a quarter gram, weigh one gram in total.

    Answer

    Yes; both labels are correct.

    Key idea

    Reversing the units reverses the division and gives a second rate that describes the same beads.

  9. Problem 9 Sorting report

    A worker sorts 606060 envelopes during 101010 minutes, and the report shows only those two numbers. Rina says this proves the worker sorted exactly 666 envelopes in every minute. Is that conclusion justified? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Distinguish the average for the full interval from what happened in each minute.

    2. Hint 2

      Look for unequal whole-number minute counts whose total is sixty.

    Answer

    No. One possible log is 444 envelopes in each of five minutes and 888 in each of the other five; any whole-number minute counts that total 606060 and are not all 666 also work.

    Full solution

    In envelopes per minute, the overall average is

    60÷10=660\div10=660÷10=6

    This division does not establish that the work was steady.

    For example, the worker could sort four envelopes in each of five minutes and eight in each of five other minutes.

    5×4+5×8=605\times4+5\times8=605×4+5×8=60

    This log has the given total and duration without having six envelopes in any minute.

    Answer

    No. One possible log is 444 envelopes in each of five minutes and 888 in each of the other five; any whole-number minute counts that total 606060 and are not all 666 also work.

    Key idea

    An average rate alone does not determine the amount in each individual interval.

  10. Problem 10 Part of a minute

    A pump moves 666 liters of water in 0.750.750.75 minute at a steady rate. Jo says that in one full minute the pump must move less than 666 liters, because dividing always makes a number smaller. Is Jo right? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      One full minute is longer than 0.750.750.75 minute, so ask whether the pump moves more or less water in it.

    2. Hint 2

      Turning 0.750.750.75 minute into one full minute takes a division, so do that same division to the liters and the rate stays equal.

    3. Hint 3

      Check your per-minute amount by multiplying it by 0.750.750.75 minute.

    Answer

    No; the pump moves 888 liters per minute.

    Full solution

    Dividing both quantities of a rate by the same number keeps them matched, the equivalent-ratio move.

    Dividing the time by its own value turns it into one minute.

    0.75÷0.75=10.75\div0.75=10.75÷0.75=1

    Dividing the liters by the same number gives, in liters per minute,

    6÷0.75=86\div0.75=86÷0.75=8

    Since 0.750.750.75 is less than one, dividing by it scales both quantities up: the time from 0.750.750.75 to 111 minute, and the water from 666 to 888 liters.

    A full minute is longer than 0.750.750.75 minute, so at a steady rate the pump moves more water in it, not less.

    Jo is not right.

    As a check, in liters,

    8×0.75=68\times0.75=68×0.75=6

    Answer

    No; the pump moves 888 liters per minute.

    Key idea

    Dividing by a number between zero and one scales both quantities up, so the amount for a whole unit is larger than the amount for part of one.
