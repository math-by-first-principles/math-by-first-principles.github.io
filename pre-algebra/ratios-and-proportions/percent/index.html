Percent

Intuition

A test score of 181818 out of 202020 and a free-throw record of 343434 out of 404040 look nothing alike. At a glance it is hard to say which one is better. But if you rescale both to “out of 100100100,” they become 909090 out of 100100100 and 858585 out of 100100100. Now the comparison is instant. That single trick, forcing every ratio onto a common scale of 100100100, is what a percent is: the symbol %\%% is shorthand for “out of 100100100.” Because the denominator is always the same, percents let you compare scores, prices, and chances of all sizes without any extra work.

Learning goals

  • Read a percent as a ratio out of one hundred
  • Convert a percent to a fraction or a decimal, and back again
  • Solve all three percent tasks from one part-over-whole proportion
  • Explain why a fixed denominator makes ratios instantly comparable

Percent means “out of 100”

A percent is a ratio whose second term is fixed at 100100100. Writing 25%25\%25% is just another way of writing the ratio 25:10025 : 10025:100, or the fraction 25100\frac{25}{100}10025​:

25%=25100.25\% = \frac{25}{100}.25%=10025​.

Read it as “twenty-five per hundred,” or “twenty-five out of every hundred.” If 25%25\%25% of a class walks to school, then for every 100100100 students, 252525 of them walk. The symbol %\%% is doing one job and one job only: it stands for “divide by 100100100.” So whenever you see a percent, you can replace the %\%% sign with ÷ 100\div\, 100÷100 and nothing changes:

25%  =  25÷100  =  25100.25\% \;=\; 25 \div 100 \;=\; \frac{25}{100}.25%=25÷100=10025​.

The same idea explains why 18:2018 : 2018:20 and 34:4034 : 4034:40 could not be compared until they were rescaled above: two ratios with different denominators cannot be compared by eye. Rewriting each one with a denominator of 100100100 does for a ratio what a common denominator does for two fractions, letting you see which is larger. Percent is just the agreement that the common denominator will always be 100100100.

The grid below is one whole, cut into 100100100 equal squares. Change how many are shaded and read the four names the amount goes by, all of them counting the same squares.

Shade 252525 squares and you have filled two and a half rows: 25%25\%25%, or 25100\frac{25}{100}10025​, or 0.250.250.25. Shade 505050 and you have filled exactly half the grid, which is why 50%50\%50% and one half are the same amount. Shade all 100100100 and you have one whole, so 100%100\%100% is the whole amount. The grid stops at 100100100 squares, but a percent does not have to.

Which is larger, 920\frac{9}{20}209​ or 715\frac{7}{15}157​?

Answer choices

Comparing the numerators or denominators alone does not work, because the two ratios use different scales. Rewrite each with a denominator of 100100100: 920=45100=45%\frac{9}{20} = \frac{45}{100} = 45\%209​=10045​=45%, and 715=46.6‾100≈46.7%\frac{7}{15} = \frac{46.\overline{6}}{100} \approx 46.7\%157​=10046.6​≈46.7%. Once both sit on the same scale of 100100100, the comparison is instant: 715\frac{7}{15}157​ is larger, even though 9>79 > 79>7.

Converting between a percent and a fraction

Turning a percent into a fraction takes no new rule at all, just the definition: drop the %\%% sign and write the number over 100100100. After that, simplify the fraction to lowest terms with the greatest-common-factor method from the Fractions chapter.

40%=40100=40÷20100÷20=25.40\% = \frac{40}{100} = \frac{40 \div 20}{100 \div 20} = \frac{2}{5}.40%=10040​=100÷2040÷20​=52​.

Going the other way, from a fraction to a percent, you want the fraction rewritten with a denominator of 100100100. The reason is that a percent is a count out of 100100100. When the denominator divides into 100100100, scale the fraction up:

34=3×254×25=75100=75%.\frac{3}{4} = \frac{3 \times 25}{4 \times 25} = \frac{75}{100} = 75\%.43​=4×253×25​=10075​=75%.

This is exactly the proportion idea from the last lesson: 34\frac{3}{4}43​ and 75100\frac{75}{100}10075​ are equal ratios. When the denominator does not divide evenly into 100100100, you can still find the percent by treating it as a proportion. Solve that proportion for the numerator, which is what the worked example below does.

Worked example 1 Write 38\frac{3}{8}83​ as a percent

A percent is a number over 100100100, so try to rewrite 38\frac{3}{8}83​ with 100100100 on the bottom. This time 888 does not divide evenly into 100100100, so no whole number scales it up. Set up a proportion instead, with the unknown numerator marked by a blank:

38=?100.\frac{3}{8} = \frac{?}{100}.83​=100?​.

Cross-multiply the diagonal you know, 333 and 100100100, then divide by the 888 diagonally opposite the blank:

?=3×1008=3008=37.5.? = \frac{3 \times 100}{8} = \frac{300}{8} = 37.5.?=83×100​=8300​=37.5.

So 38=37.5%\frac{3}{8} = 37.5\%83​=37.5%. A percent does not have to be a whole number.

Converting between a percent and a decimal

The link between percents and decimals comes straight from the meaning of the two place columns after the decimal point. From the Decimals chapter, the first two decimal places are tenths and hundredths, so a two-place decimal is already a count of hundredths. Since a percent is also a count of hundredths, the two are the same idea in different clothing:

25%=25100=0.25.25\% = \frac{25}{100} = 0.25.25%=10025​=0.25.

To go from a percent to a decimal, divide by 100100100, because %\%% means “divide by 100100100.” That division moves the decimal point two places to the left:

73%=73÷100=0.73,8%=8÷100=0.08.73\% = 73 \div 100 = 0.73, \qquad 8\% = 8 \div 100 = 0.08.73%=73÷100=0.73,8%=8÷100=0.08.

To go from a decimal to a percent, do the reverse: multiply by 100100100, which moves the decimal point two places to the right. Then attach the %\%% sign:

0.6=(0.6×100)%=60%,0.045=(0.045×100)%=4.5%.0.6 = (0.6 \times 100)\% = 60\%, \qquad 0.045 = (0.045 \times 100)\% = 4.5\%.0.6=(0.6×100)%=60%,0.045=(0.045×100)%=4.5%.

Why dividing by 100100100 shifts the decimal point two places left#

A percent is a number of hundredths, so 46%46\%46% is 464646 copies of 1100\frac{1}{100}1001​:

46%=46100.46\% = \frac{46}{100}.46%=10046​.

Dividing by 100100100 drops the ones digit two place columns: the 666 that was worth 666 now sits in the hundredths place worth 6100\frac{6}{100}1006​. The tens digit drops two columns in the same way: the 444 that was worth 404040 now sits in the tenths place worth 410\frac{4}{10}104​. Reading the new places off,

46100=410+6100=0.4+0.06=0.46.\frac{46}{100} = \frac{4}{10} + \frac{6}{100} = 0.4 + 0.06 = 0.46.10046​=104​+1006​=0.4+0.06=0.46.

So 46%=0.4646\% = 0.4646%=0.46, two places to the left of where the point sat in 464646. Nothing in the argument depended on the digits being 444 and 666: place value works in powers of ten, so dividing by 100100100 drops every digit two place columns no matter what the digits are.

The three percent tasks

Almost every percent question is one of three closely related tasks, and all three come from the same relationship. Take a bag of 202020 marbles where 111111 of them are blue. Scaling that ratio to a denominator of 100100100 multiplies both terms by 555, so 555555 marbles in every 100100100 would be blue:

1120=55100=55%.\frac{11}{20} = \frac{55}{100} = 55\%.2011​=10055​=55%.

The 111111 is the part and the 202020 is the whole. 555555 is the number before the percent sign, so the percent is 55%55\%55%. Those two equal fractions are the two sides of the percent proportion, which all three tasks use. Let ppp stand for the number written before the percent sign, so the percent itself is p%p\%p%:

partwhole=p100.\frac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} = \frac{p}{100}.wholepart​=100p​.

Three numbers appear here (the part, the whole, and ppp), and each task hands you two of them and asks for the third. Once it is set up, cross-multiply and divide to solve for whichever one is missing, the same move from the Proportions lesson. The division needs a nonzero number to divide by: finding ppp divides by the whole, so the whole must be nonzero; finding the whole divides by ppp, so ppp must be nonzero. Every problem in this lesson already satisfies whichever one its own task needs.

Task A: find a percent of a number

To find 20%20\%20% of 808080, the cleanest route is to turn the percent into a decimal and multiply, because “of” with percents means multiply. Since 20%=0.220\% = 0.220%=0.2,

20% of 80=0.2×80=16.20\% \text{ of } 80 = 0.2 \times 80 = 16.20% of 80=0.2×80=16.

Why does multiplying work? The percent proportion says part80=20100\frac{\text{part}}{80} = \frac{20}{100}80part​=10020​, and solving it for the part means multiplying 808080 by 20100=0.2\frac{20}{100} = 0.210020​=0.2. The decimal method is just that proportion with the arithmetic already done. You can also reason in fraction form: 20%20\%20% is 15\frac{1}{5}51​, and 15\frac{1}{5}51​ of 808080 is 80÷5=1680 \div 5 = 1680÷5=16, the same answer.

Worked example 2 Find 15%15\%15% of 240240240

Convert the percent to a decimal first. Dividing by 100100100 moves the point two places left:

15%=0.15.15\% = 0.15.15%=0.15.

“Of” means multiply, so multiply the decimal by the number:

0.15×240=36.0.15 \times 240 = 36.0.15×240=36.

So 15%15\%15% of 240240240 is 363636. As a check, split it into friendly pieces: 10%10\%10% of 240240240 is 242424 and 5%5\%5% is half of that, 121212, and 24+12=3624 + 12 = 3624+12=36.

Task B: find what percent one number is of another

Here you know the part and the whole and you want the percent. Make the ratio of part to whole, then rewrite it over 100100100. To ask what percent 181818 is of 242424, start with the ratio 1824\frac{18}{24}2418​ and turn it into a percent by writing it over 100100100:

1824=p100.\frac{18}{24} = \frac{p}{100}.2418​=100p​.

This is a proportion with ppp as the missing numerator. Cross-multiply the known diagonal and divide by the number diagonally opposite the blank:

p=18×10024=180024=75,so 18 is 75% of 24.p = \frac{18 \times 100}{24} = \frac{1800}{24} = 75, \qquad \text{so } 18 \text{ is } 75\% \text{ of } 24.p=2418×100​=241800​=75,so 18 is 75% of 24.

A shortcut that gives the same thing: divide the part by the whole to get a decimal, then convert that decimal to a percent. Here 18÷24=0.75=75%18 \div 24 = 0.75 = 75\%18÷24=0.75=75%.

What percent of 404040 is 303030?

Answer choices

The part is 303030 and the whole is 404040. Make the ratio of part to whole and set it over 100100100: 3040=p100\frac{30}{40} = \frac{p}{100}4030​=100p​. Cross-multiply and divide by 404040.

p=30×10040=300040=75p = \frac{30 \times 100}{40} = \frac{3000}{40} = 75p=4030×100​=403000​=75

So 303030 is 75%75\%75% of 404040. Dividing also works: 30÷40=0.75=75%30 \div 40 = 0.75 = 75\%30÷40=0.75=75%.

Task C: find the whole from a part and its percent

The last task gives you a part and the percent it represents, and asks for the whole. If 303030 is 40%40\%40% of some number, here is a quick way to see the answer before any algebra: 40%40\%40% is four tenths, so one tenth of the whole is 30÷4=7.530 \div 4 = 7.530÷4=7.5, and the whole is ten of those tenths, 10×7.5=7510 \times 7.5 = 7510×7.5=75.

The percent proportion gets there directly too. 303030 is the part, p=40p = 40p=40, and the whole is the missing piece:

30whole=40100.\frac{30}{\text{whole}} = \frac{40}{100}.whole30​=10040​.

The whole sits in a denominator this time, but cross-multiplication does not care where the blank is. Multiply the diagonal you fully know, 303030 and 100100100, then divide by the 404040 diagonally opposite the blank:

whole=30×10040=300040=75.\text{whole} = \frac{30 \times 100}{40} = \frac{3000}{40} = 75.whole=4030×100​=403000​=75.

So 303030 is 40%40\%40% of 757575, matching the tenths estimate. The check is to run it forward: 40%40\%40% of 757575 is 0.4×75=300.4 \times 75 = 300.4×75=30, which matches the part you started with.

Worked example 3 A real percent problem

A basketball player made 131313 of her shots, and that was 52%52\%52% of the shots she took. How many shots did she take in all?

The 131313 made shots are the part, p=52p = 52p=52, and the total number of shots is the whole you want. Write the percent proportion with the unknown total ttt in the denominator:

13t=52100.\frac{13}{t} = \frac{52}{100}.t13​=10052​.

Multiply the known diagonal 131313 and 100100100, then divide by the 525252 diagonally opposite the blank:

t=13×10052=130052=25.t = \frac{13 \times 100}{52} = \frac{1300}{52} = 25.t=5213×100​=521300​=25.

She took 252525 shots. The check runs forward: 52%52\%52% of 252525 is 0.52×25=130.52 \times 25 = 130.52×25=13, the number she made.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting that %\%% means “divide by 100100100.” A percent is never a plain number; 50%50\%50% is 50100=0.5\frac{50}{100} = 0.510050​=0.5, not 505050. Treating 50%50\%50% as 505050 in a calculation inflates the answer a hundredfold.
  • Moving the decimal the wrong number of places. Converting a percent to a decimal moves the point two places left (8%=0.088\% = 0.088%=0.08), not one. Moving one place turns 8%8\%8% into 0.80.80.8, which is 80%80\%80%, ten times too big.
  • Mixing up the part and the whole. In “what percent of 404040 is 303030,” the whole is 404040 (the number after “of”) and the part is 303030. Dividing the wrong way, 40÷30≈1.3340 \div 30 \approx 1.3340÷30≈1.33, answers a different question, “what percent of 303030 is 404040,” not the one asked.
  • Treating the same percent as the same amount. The amount a percent stands for depends on its whole. 20%20\%20% of 505050 is 101010, but 20%20\%20% of 200200200 is 404040, so the same percent is a different amount unless the whole is the same.
  • Confusing “find the whole” with “find a percent of a number.” If 303030 is 40%40\%40% of a number, the number is larger than 303030 (it works out to 757575). Multiplying 303030 by 0.40.40.4 answers a different question; here you divide to recover the bigger whole.

Key takeaways

  • A percent is a ratio out of 100100100: 25%=2510025\% = \frac{25}{100}25%=10025​, and the %\%% sign means “divide by 100100100.” A fixed denominator of 100100100 is what makes different ratios instantly comparable.
  • Percent to fraction: write the number over 100100100 and simplify.
  • Fraction to percent: rewrite the fraction with denominator 100100100; the numerator is ppp, so the percent is p%p\%p%.
  • Percent to decimal: divide by 100100100 (move the point two places left).
  • Decimal to percent: multiply by 100100100 (move the point two places right).
  • The three tasks all come from partwhole=p100\dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}} = \dfrac{p}{100}wholepart​=100p​, where ppp is the number written before the percent sign. Those tasks are finding a percent of a number, finding what percent one number is of another, and finding the whole from a part.
  • Solve each task by cross-multiplying and dividing. Finding a percent of a number has a shortcut: convert the percent to a decimal and multiply, since “of” means multiply. Since 100%100\%100% is the whole, a percent over 100100100 is more than the whole.

A bit of history (optional)

Look closely at the sign %\%%. Two little circles and a slash: a strange shape for an idea as plain as hundredths. It began as a scribble.

Merchants in Italy wrote per cento, “for each hundred,” dozens of times on a page of accounts. A page of prices could carry those two words twenty times over. Busy clerks shortened it, the way anyone shortens a word they write all day. By the fourteen hundreds the phrase had shrunk to a letter with a small looped ending. The letter went next. The loop then split into two circles with a bar leaning between them, and there is the sign on your calculator.

So the symbol stands for no grand idea at all. It is worn-down handwriting for two ordinary words, and those two words still say what to do with it. Whenever you swap %\%% for “divide by 100100100,” you are reading that old abbreviation back out in full.
