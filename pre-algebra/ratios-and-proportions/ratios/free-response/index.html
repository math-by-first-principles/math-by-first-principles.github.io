Problem 1 of 10

  1. Problem 1 Paper shapes

    A collection of paper shapes contains only stars and circles. Stars make up 311\frac{3}{11}113​ of the collection. Write the ratio of circles to stars in simplest form.

    1. Hint 1

      Think of the whole as eleven equal parts.

    2. Hint 2

      Find how many of those parts belong to circles, then put circles first.

    Answer

    8:38:38:3.

    Full solution

    Three of the eleven equal parts are stars.

    The remaining parts are circles.

    11−3=811-3=811−3=8

    Circles therefore take eight parts for every three parts of stars, so their ratio is 8:38:38:3.

    The two numbers have no common factor above 111.

    Answer

    8:38:38:3.

    Key idea

    The order of the named groups determines the order of a ratio.

  2. Problem 2 Storage slots

    A rack has 323232 slots. Exactly 121212 are empty, and the rest are occupied. Write the ratio of occupied slots to empty slots in simplest form.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the occupied count before comparing the two groups.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide both counts by their greatest common factor.

    Answer

    5:35:35:3.

    Full solution

    Subtract the empty slots from the total.

    32−12=2032-12=2032−12=20

    The comparison is 20:1220:1220:12.

    The greatest common factor is 444, so dividing both parts by 444 gives 5:35:35:3.

    2012=53\frac{20}{12}=\frac{5}{3}1220​=35​

    Answer

    5:35:35:3.

    Key idea

    A comparison between two parts uses the two part counts, rather than the whole count.

  3. Problem 3 Cutting a strip

    A strip consists of 999 cm of dark material followed by 141414 cm of light material. It is cut into pieces that are each 0.50.50.5 cm long, with no piece crossing the boundary between materials. Write the ratio of the actual number of dark pieces to the actual number of light pieces without simplifying.

    1. Hint 1

      Each material length is divided into pieces of the same length.

    2. Hint 2

      Find each piece count by dividing its material length by the length of one piece.

    Answer

    18:2818:2818:28.

    Full solution

    Each half-centimeter piece uses 0.50.50.5 cm of material.

    The dark piece count is

    9÷0.5=189\div0.5=189÷0.5=18

    and the light piece count is

    14÷0.5=2814\div0.5=2814÷0.5=28

    The actual counts give 18:2818:2818:28, as requested.

    Both counts are twice the corresponding lengths, so this comparison is equivalent to the length ratio 9:149:149:14.

    Answer

    18:2818:2818:28.

    Key idea

    Dividing both quantities by the same positive size produces equivalent comparisons.

  4. Problem 4 Workshop funds

    A workshop has 126 dollars. It reserves 22 dollars for delivery and divides the rest between paper and tools in the ratio 3:53:53:5. How much goes to each of these two purchases?

    1. Hint 1

      The ratio applies to the amount left after delivery is reserved.

    2. Hint 2

      Count all the ratio parts, find the value of one part, and then find each share.

    Answer

    Paper: 39 dollars; tools: 65 dollars.

    Full solution

    The amount to split is the budget after delivery.

    126−22=104126-22=104126−22=104

    The ratio has 3+5=83+5=83+5=8 parts.

    Each part is worth 13 dollars.

    104÷8=13104\div8=13104÷8=13

    Paper receives three parts and tools receive five.

    3×13=393\times13=393×13=39 5×13=655\times13=655×13=65

    Their shares add to 104 dollars, and delivery brings the total back to 126 dollars.

    Answer

    Paper: 39 dollars; tools: 65 dollars.

    Key idea

    Identify the total that a ratio actually splits before valuing its parts.

  5. Problem 5 Audio programs

    Program A has 666 minutes of speech and 111111 minutes of music. Program B has 555 minutes of speech and 999 minutes of music. Which program has more speech for the same amount of music? Show why.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare speech with music in the same order for both programs.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the two comparisons as fractions with a common denominator.

    Answer

    Program B.

    Full solution

    The speech-to-music comparisons are 611\frac{6}{11}116​ and 59\frac{5}{9}95​.

    Rewrite both over 999999.

    611=5499\frac{6}{11}=\frac{54}{99}116​=9954​ 59=5599\frac{5}{9}=\frac{55}{99}95​=9955​

    Fifty-five ninety-ninths is greater than fifty-four ninety-ninths.

    Program B therefore has more speech for the same amount of music.

    Answer

    Program B.

    Key idea

    Two ratios can be compared by expressing both as fractions with the same denominator.

  6. Problem 6 Three kinds of fastener

    A box holds three kinds of fastener: screws, nails, and 121212 hooks. The ratio of screws to nails is 5:75:75:7, and there are 424242 nails. What fraction of all the fasteners are screws, in simplest form?

    1. Hint 1

      The whole box includes the hooks as well as the two groups in the ratio.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the nail count to find the screw count.

    Answer

    514\frac{5}{14}145​.

    Full solution

    Seven ratio parts account for 424242 nails, so one part contains six fasteners.

    42÷7=642\div7=642÷7=6

    The screw count is five parts.

    5×6=305\times6=305×6=30

    Include the hooks in the whole.

    30+42+12=8430+42+12=8430+42+12=84

    The required fraction simplifies by dividing top and bottom by 666.

    3084=514\frac{30}{84}=\frac{5}{14}8430​=145​

    Answer

    514\frac{5}{14}145​.

    Key idea

    A part-to-whole fraction must include every group that belongs to the whole.

  7. Problem 7 Display pieces

    A display uses 282828 small pieces and 484848 large pieces. A worker removes one quarter of the pieces of each size. Find the ratio of small to large pieces that remain, in simplest form, and the total remaining count.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the remaining share of each original count.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the remaining counts, then add them for the total.

    Answer

    7:127:127:12; 575757 pieces remain.

    Full solution

    Removing one quarter leaves three quarters of each size.

    34×28=21\frac{3}{4}\times28=2143​×28=21 34×48=36\frac{3}{4}\times48=3643​×48=36

    The remaining ratio is 21:3621:3621:36.

    Divide both parts by their greatest common factor, 333, to get 7:127:127:12.

    The remaining count is

    21+36=5721+36=5721+36=57

    Both counts were scaled by the same factor, so the original comparison is preserved.

    Answer

    7:127:127:12; 575757 pieces remain.

    Key idea

    Removing the same fractional share from both quantities preserves their ratio.

  8. Problem 8 Tile tray

    A tray holds 888 green tiles and 222222 white tiles. Ana adds 121212 green tiles and 333333 white tiles. She says the tray's green-to-white ratio has not changed. Is she right? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Two whole-number ratios are equivalent exactly when they reduce to the same simplest form.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the new green and white counts, then simplify the ratio before and after the tiles are added.

    Answer

    Yes; the green-to-white ratio is 4:114:114:11 before and after.

    Full solution

    Before the change, the ratio is 8:228:228:22.

    The greatest common factor of 888 and 222222 is 222, so it simplifies to 4:114:114:11.

    822=411\frac{8}{22}=\frac{4}{11}228​=114​

    After the change, the green count is

    8+12=208+12=208+12=20

    and the white count is

    22+33=5522+33=5522+33=55

    The greatest common factor of 202020 and 555555 is 555, so 20:5520:5520:55 also simplifies to 4:114:114:11.

    2055=411\frac{20}{55}=\frac{4}{11}5520​=114​

    Both ratios reduce to 4:114:114:11, so Ana is right.

    The added tiles explain why: 12:3312:3312:33 also reduces to 4:114:114:11, with greatest common factor 333, so Ana added three more groups of four green and eleven white tiles to the two groups already there.

    Answer

    Yes; the green-to-white ratio is 4:114:114:11 before and after.

    Key idea

    Adding amounts that are in the same ratio as the original keeps the ratio unchanged.

  9. Problem 9 Seat reservation

    A room has 636363 seats, all either reserved or available. The ratio of reserved seats to available seats is 2:72:72:7. Luis calculates the reserved count as 63×2763\times\frac{2}{7}63×72​. Is that calculation correct? Find the reserved count and explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Decide whether the denominator seven represents available seats or every seat.

    2. Hint 2

      Count the parts in the entire room before assigning two parts to reserved seats.

    Answer

    No; 141414 seats are reserved.

    Full solution

    The 777 represents available seats, while 636363 counts every seat.

    The whole has 2+7=92+7=92+7=9 parts, so the reserved fraction is 29\frac{2}{9}92​.

    63×29=1463\times\frac{2}{9}=1463×92​=14

    The other 494949 seats are available.

    Reserved to available is then 14:4914:4914:49, which reduces to 2:72:72:7 when both counts are divided by 777, and the counts add back to 636363.

    Luis's calculation gives 181818 reserved seats, which would leave 454545 available.

    Dividing both counts by 999 reduces 18:4518:4518:45 to 2:52:52:5, not 2:72:72:7, so his result does not fit the room.

    Answer

    No; 141414 seats are reserved.

    Key idea

    When the groups in a ratio make up the whole, the denominator for a share of the total is the sum of the ratio parts.

  10. Problem 10 Packing request

    A customer requests a box containing exactly 474747 whole pieces, with smooth pieces to ridged pieces in the ratio 4:54:54:5 and no other pieces. Can the request be filled exactly? Justify your answer.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the number of pieces in one complete group of the ratio.

    2. Hint 2

      Check whether forty-seven pieces can be made up entirely of complete groups of that size.

    Answer

    No.

    Full solution

    Any whole-number counts in the ratio 4:54:54:5 reduce to 4:54:54:5 when both are divided by their greatest common factor.

    So the counts are 444 and 555 times that factor, which means they come in complete groups of four smooth and five ridged pieces.

    Each complete group holds nine pieces.

    4+5=94+5=94+5=9

    Five complete groups give 454545 pieces, and six give 545454 pieces.

    The requested 474747 is not a multiple of 999, so no whole-piece counts with that total have the required ratio.

    As a check, the smooth share would be

    47×49=188947\times\frac{4}{9}=\frac{188}{9}47×94​=9188​

    which is not a whole number.

    Answer

    No.

    Key idea

    For two whole-piece counts in a simplified ratio, their total must be a multiple of the sum of the ratio parts.
