    Foundational 13 pts

    A library shelf holds 424242 hardcover books and 565656 paperback books, and nothing else stands on it. A shelf like this can be described by comparing the two kinds of book with each other, or by comparing one kind with the shelf as a whole, and those are different descriptions.

    3. C
      Part C.

      The next shelf along holds 272727 hardcover books and 363636 paperback books. Decide whether the two shelves compare hardcover with paperback in the same way, and support the decision instead of asserting it.

      Justify your claim State the claim, then give the reason it has to be true. 4 points

    4. D
      Part D.

      The lowest-terms ratio you found for the first shelf contains neither 424242 nor 565656. Say what such a ratio still records about a shelf and what it no longer records, and say what changes about the claim if the two kinds of book are named in the other order.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 4

      Two moves settle every part of this question: divide both counts by a factor they share, or add them together to build the whole.

    2. Hint 2 of 4 · Part B

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Lowest terms means dividing both parts by their greatest common factor. Break the two counts down: 42=2×3×742 = 2 \times 3 \times 742=2×3×7 and 56=2×2×2×756 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 756=2×2×2×7, so the largest factor they share is 2×7=142 \times 7 = 142×7=14.

    Divide both parts by it, never one of them alone:

    42:56=(42÷14):(56÷14)=3:4.42 : 56 = (42 \div 14) : (56 \div 14) = 3 : 4.42:56=(42÷14):(56÷14)=3:4.

    The parts 333 and 444 share no factor beyond 111, so this is as far as it reduces.

    For the second ratio nothing about the shelf has changed, only which kind of book is named first. The same factor of 141414 comes out:

    56:42=(56÷14):(42÷14)=4:3.56 : 42 = (56 \div 14) : (42 \div 14) = 4 : 3.56:42=(56÷14):(42÷14)=4:3.

    So the shelf is 3:43 : 43:4 hardcover to paperback and 4:34 : 34:3 paperback to hardcover. Both statements are true of it, and neither may be written in the other's place: the pair of numbers carries no meaning until the order of the two kinds is fixed.

    Part B

    Nothing else stands on the shelf, so the whole is the sum of the two parts:

    42+56=98 books.42 + 56 = 98 \text{ books.}42+56=98 books.

    Now compare one kind with that whole. The greatest common factor of 424242 and 989898 is 141414, since 98=2×7×798 = 2 \times 7 \times 798=2×7×7:

    42:98=(42÷14):(98÷14)=3:7.42 : 98 = (42 \div 14) : (98 \div 14) = 3 : 7.42:98=(42÷14):(98÷14)=3:7.

    A part-to-whole ratio written as a fraction puts the part on top and the whole underneath, so the hardcovers are 37\frac{3}{7}73​ of the shelf.

    Watch what the denominator is doing. In part A the second number was the other kind of book; here it is the two kinds added together, and 3+4=73 + 4 = 73+4=7 is where the 777 comes from. The check is that the two part-to-whole fractions account for the shelf exactly once: the paperbacks are 56:98=4:756 : 98 = 4 : 756:98=4:7, and

    37+47=1.\frac{3}{7} + \frac{4}{7} = 1.73​+74​=1.

    Part C

    Comparing 424242 with 272727 decides nothing, because the shelves are different sizes. Put the second shelf into lowest terms first. Here 27=3×3×327 = 3 \times 3 \times 327=3×3×3 and 36=2×2×3×336 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 336=2×2×3×3, so the greatest common factor is 999:

    27:36=(27÷9):(36÷9)=3:4.27 : 36 = (27 \div 9) : (36 \div 9) = 3 : 4.27:36=(27÷9):(36÷9)=3:4.

    The first shelf reduced to 3:43 : 43:4 as well. Two ratios are equivalent exactly when they reduce to the same lowest terms, so the two shelves do compare their books in the same way.

    Reading the same fact upwards makes it concrete. Start from 3:43 : 43:4 and scale both parts by 141414 and you get the first shelf; scale both parts by 999 and you get the second:

    3:4=42:56,3:4=27:36.3 : 4 = 42 : 56, \qquad 3 : 4 = 27 : 36.3:4=42:56,3:4=27:36.

    Each shelf is a batch of the same pattern, four paperbacks alongside every three hardcovers, taken 141414 times over on one shelf and 999 times over on the other. The totals differ, 989898 books against 27+36=6327 + 36 = 6327+36=63, and that difference is invisible to the ratio, because a ratio was never a record of how much there is.

    Part D

    The step from 42:5642 : 5642:56 to 3:43 : 43:4 divided both counts by 141414, and dividing both parts by the same number leaves the comparison untouched. So the comparison is exactly what survives: on this shelf there are four paperbacks alongside every three hardcovers.

    What does not survive is the size. The two shelves in this question reduce to the same thing:

    42:56=3:4,27:36=3:4,42 : 56 = 3 : 4, \qquad 27 : 36 = 3 : 4,42:56=3:4,27:36=3:4,

    yet one holds 989898 books and the other 636363. So 3:43 : 43:4 cannot tell them apart, and it makes no claim that any shelf holds three hardcovers. It says how the two kinds compare and stops there. To pin the counts down you need one more piece of information, either a total or one of the two counts, and then the scale factor recovers the rest.

    Order is the other thing the notation carries, and it is carried entirely by the naming. The pairs 3:43 : 43:4 and 4:34 : 34:3 are built from the same two numbers, and both are true of this shelf, but of different questions about it. Written down with no statement of which kind comes first, the pair says nothing: read as paperbacks to hardcovers, 3:43 : 43:4 would describe a shelf holding more hardcover books than paperback books, and on this shelf the hardcovers are the smaller group.

    In one line

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The three numbers in the ratio are parts, not chairs. Count them first:

    5+6+9=20 parts.5 + 6 + 9 = 20 \text{ parts.}5+6+9=20 parts.

    The delivery is cut into that many equal parts, so a single part is worth

    240÷20=12 chairs.240 \div 20 = 12 \text{ chairs.}240÷20=12 chairs.

    Now give each hall its own number of parts, at 121212 chairs a part:

    5×12=60,6×12=72,9×12=108.5 \times 12 = 60, \qquad 6 \times 12 = 72, \qquad 9 \times 12 = 108.5×12=60,6×12=72,9×12=108.

    The check is to add the three shares:

    60+72+108=240,60 + 72 + 108 = 240,60+72+108=240,

    which is the whole delivery with nothing left over and nothing invented. It is worth doing every time, because it catches the commonest slip in a three-way split, dividing by the number of halls instead of by the number of parts. Dividing 240240240 by 333 would have given 808080 chairs each, a split in the ratio 1:1:11 : 1 : 11:1:1 rather than the one that was agreed.

    Part B

    The parts are all the same size, so a hall's share of the delivery is settled by how many parts it holds out of all of them. The third hall holds 999, and the delivery is 5+6+95 + 6 + 95+6+9 parts in all:

    95+6+9=920.\frac{9}{5 + 6 + 9} = \frac{9}{20}.5+6+99​=209​.

    The denominator is the total number of parts. It is not 666, which counts another hall's parts, and the difference between those two denominators is the difference between a part-to-part comparison and a part-to-whole one.

    The same answer comes out of the counts, which is the check. The third hall takes 108108108 chairs out of 240240240, and the greatest common factor of 108108108 and 240240240 is 121212:

    108:240=(108÷12):(240÷12)=9:20.108 : 240 = (108 \div 12) : (240 \div 12) = 9 : 20.108:240=(108÷12):(240÷12)=9:20.

    So the fraction and the reduced part-to-whole ratio carry the same information written two ways, and 920\frac{9}{20}209​ of 240240240 is 9×12=1089 \times 12 = 1089×12=108, as it must be.

    Part C

    One half of the parent's statement is right and the other half is a different kind of comparison wearing the same numbers.

    Sound: the first two halls really do stand in the ratio 5:65 : 65:6. Every hall's share is its own number of parts, and all the parts are the same size, so the first two compare as 555 parts against 666 parts. Ignoring the third hall does not disturb that, because dropping a hall changes what the whole is without changing how any two shares compare with each other.

    Faulty: 5:65 : 65:6 is part-to-part. Turning it into a fraction of what the pair receives needs the whole that the pair makes, and that whole is the sum of their parts:

    5+6=11 parts,5 + 6 = 11 \text{ parts,}5+6=11 parts,

    so the first hall's share of the pair's chairs is 511\frac{5}{11}115​, not 56\frac{5}{6}65​.

    5:1,5 : 1,5:1,

    which contradicts the 5:65 : 65:6 the parent started from. A rule that destroys its own premise is wrong wherever the numbers came from.

    The counts confirm it. The first two halls hold 60+72=13260 + 72 = 13260+72=132 chairs, and

    511×132=5×12=60,\frac{5}{11} \times 132 = 5 \times 12 = 60,115​×132=5×12=60,

    Part D

    5+6+9=20 parts,5 + 6 + 9 = 20 \text{ parts,}5+6+9=20 parts,

    and at the second school a part is worth

    400÷20=20 chairs,400 \div 20 = 20 \text{ chairs,}400÷20=20 chairs,

The size of a delivery and the ratio it is split in are independent pieces of information, and only the second is what the ratio records.

Writing the ratio a:ba : ba:b as ab\frac{a}{b}ba​ and scaling both parts by kkk gives kakb\frac{ka}{kb}kbka​, and the factor kkk sits in both the top and the bottom, so it cancels:

    kakb=ab.\frac{ka}{kb} = \frac{a}{b}.kbka​=ba​.

    That cancelling is where the exclusion of zero earns its keep. Taking k=0k = 0k=0 would collapse both parts to 0:00 : 00:0, leaving no factor to cancel and no comparison of any kind, which is why the scaling rule has always been stated for a nonzero number. With zero ruled out, this half of the test is sound, and it is the rule the whole idea of an equivalent ratio rests on.

    The second promise: if two ratios are equivalent, must one be reachable from the other in that way? Here the test breaks, and the pairs already in front of you break it. The pairs 24:6024 : 6024:60 and 18:4518 : 4518:45 are equivalent, since both reduce to 2:52 : 52:5. But

    24÷18=43,18÷24=34,24 \div 18 = \frac{4}{3}, \qquad 18 \div 24 = \frac{3}{4},24÷18=34​,18÷24=43​,

    and neither is a whole number, so neither pair can be reached from the other by multiplying both parts by a whole number, and excluding zero does nothing to rescue it.

    The lowest terms show what is going on. Both pairs are built from 2:52 : 52:5, one by scaling by 121212 and the other by scaling by 999, and 121212 and 999 are not multiples of each other. The student's test looks for a ladder running from one ratio straight to the other; equivalence asks only that the two stand on the same ladder, and they can do that at rungs neither of which is above the other.

    So the verdict is that the test holds one way and fails the other, which means it is not a test of equivalence. The repair is the method used in part A: reduce both and see whether the lowest terms agree. That one does work in both directions, which is why simplifying alone was enough to settle part A.

    In one line

    24:6024 : 6024:60 and 18:4518 : 4518:45 both reduce to 2:52 : 52:5, so they say the same thing. 20:5220 : 5220:52 reduces to 5:135 : 135:13, which is different, and over the denominator 656565 the two stand as 2665\frac{26}{65}6526​ against 2565\frac{25}{65}6525​, so 24:6024 : 6024:60 is the stronger comparison. The student's test is sound one way and unsound the other: 24:6024 : 6024:60 and 18:4518 : 4518:45 are equivalent, yet 24÷1824 \div 1824÷18 is not a whole number. Equivalence requires a shared lowest-terms form, here 2:52 : 52:5, reached by scaling by 121212 and by 999.
