  1. Problem 1 Print records

    A printing record compares the first print with the second as 7:117:117:11 in length, and the second print with the first as 22:1422:1422:14 in width. Write a proportion in fraction form comparing the first print with the second in both measurements, with length on the left.

    1. Hint 1

      Keep the first print before the second in each comparison.

    2. Hint 2

      Reverse the width comparison so the first print comes first.

    Answer

    711=1422\frac{7}{11}=\frac{14}{22}117​=2214​.

    Full solution

    Length, first print to second, gives 711\frac{7}{11}117​.

    The width record lists the second print first, so reversing it to first print to second turns 22:1422:1422:14 into 14:2214:2214:22, which is 1422\frac{14}{22}2214​.

    Putting length on the left gives

    711=1422\frac{7}{11}=\frac{14}{22}117​=2214​

    This is a true proportion, since dividing both numbers of 14:2214:2214:22 by 222 gives 7:117:117:11.

    Answer

    711=1422\frac{7}{11}=\frac{14}{22}117​=2214​.

    Key idea

    A proportion writes two equal comparisons in the same order.

  2. Problem 2 Comparison cards

    Three cards show 24:4024:4024:40, 36:6036:6036:60, and 48:7548:7548:75. Which two cards give the same comparison?

    1. Hint 1

      Different counts can still represent the same comparison.

    2. Hint 2

      Reduce each ratio by the greatest common factor of its two numbers.

    Answer

    24:4024:4024:40 and 36:6036:6036:60.

    Full solution

    The greatest common factors are 888, 121212, and 333, respectively.

    Dividing each pair by its greatest common factor gives

    2440=35\frac{24}{40}=\frac{3}{5}4024​=53​ 3660=35\frac{36}{60}=\frac{3}{5}6036​=53​ 4875=1625\frac{48}{75}=\frac{16}{25}7548​=2516​

    The first two ratios have the same simplest form.

    The third differs, since 35=1525\frac{3}{5}=\frac{15}{25}53​=2515​, which is not 1625\frac{16}{25}2516​.

    Answer

    24:4024:4024:40 and 36:6036:6036:60.

    Key idea

    Two whole-number ratios agree exactly when their simplest forms agree.

  3. Problem 3 Trail mix

    A trail mix label lists raisins to peanuts as 5:65:65:6 by weight. A larger scoop of the same mix holds 151515 grams of peanuts. How many grams of raisins does it hold?

    1. Hint 1

      Write both ratios as fractions in the same order, raisins over peanuts.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiply the diagonal you know, then divide by the number facing the blank.

    Answer

    12.512.512.5 grams.

    Full solution

    With nnn for the grams of raisins in the larger scoop, writing raisins over peanuts on both sides gives

    56=n15\frac{5}{6}=\frac{n}{15}65​=15n​

    The peanut amount goes from 666 to 151515.

    The ratio 5:65:65:6 is already in lowest terms and 151515 is not a whole-number multiple of 666, so there is no whole-number factor to scale by, and cross-multiplying is the way through.

    The diagonal you know is

    5×15=755\times15=755×15=75

    This equals 666 times nnn, so divide by the 666 facing the blank.

    n=75÷6n=75\div6n=75÷6 n=12.5n=12.5n=12.5

    As a check, the other cross-product 6×12.56\times12.56×12.5 is also 757575.

    Answer

    12.512.512.5 grams.

    Key idea

    When the scale factor is not a whole number, multiply the known diagonal and divide by the number facing the blank.

  4. Problem 4 Drying cord

    Every 151515 cm of a wet craft cord becomes 121212 cm after drying. A maker cuts 404040 cm of wet cord. How long will the piece be after drying, and how many centimeters will it lose?

    1. Hint 1

      The new wet and dried lengths must pair up the same way as the given pair.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the dried length first, then subtract it from the wet length.

    Answer

    323232 cm after drying; 888 cm lost.

    Full solution

    Put wet length on top and dried length below on both sides, with ddd for the unknown dried length in cm, a positive number.

    1512=40d\frac{15}{12}=\frac{40}{d}1215​=d40​

    Since 404040 is not a whole-number multiple of 151515, multiply the known diagonal.

    12×40=48012\times40=48012×40=480

    This equals 151515 times ddd, so divide by 151515.

    d=480÷15d=480\div15d=480÷15 d=32d=32d=32

    The piece loses the difference of the two lengths.

    40−32=840-32=840−32=8

    As a check, 4032\frac{40}{32}3240​ and 1512\frac{15}{12}1215​ both reduce to 54\frac{5}{4}45​.

    Answer

    323232 cm after drying; 888 cm lost.

    Key idea

    Match corresponding amounts before finding a missing length from a proportion.

  5. Problem 5 Two processing stages

    A strip goes through two machines. The first produces 333 cm of output for every 444 cm of input. The second produces 222 cm of output for every 333 cm of its input. If a 282828 cm strip enters the first machine, what length leaves the second?

    1. Hint 1

      The first machine supplies the second machine with its input.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the output of each stage in order, keeping output over input in both comparisons.

    Answer

    141414 cm.

    Full solution

    The first machine turns each four centimeters into three.

    Its output is

    28×34=2128\times\frac{3}{4}=2128×43​=21

    cm.

    Those twenty-one centimeters enter the second machine, whose output is

    21×23=1421\times\frac{2}{3}=1421×32​=14

    cm.

    As a check, 2128=34\frac{21}{28}=\frac{3}{4}2821​=43​ and 1421=23\frac{14}{21}=\frac{2}{3}2114​=32​, matching each machine's stated ratio.

    Answer

    141414 cm.

    Key idea

    When two processes follow their stated ratios in sequence, each output becomes the input to the next process.

  6. Problem 6 Prize tickets

    An event exchanges blue tickets for gold tickets at 252525 blue for every 101010 gold, and exchanges may be made in any amounts that keep that ratio exactly. Each prize costs 666 gold tickets. How many blue tickets must Kai exchange to claim 333 prizes?

    1. Hint 1

      Find the total number of gold tickets Kai needs before using the exchange ratio.

    2. Hint 2

      Keep blue over gold on both sides, reduce the exchange ratio to lowest terms, then find the factor that takes its gold number to the gold total.

    Answer

    454545 blue tickets.

    Full solution

    Three prizes at six gold tickets each need

    3×6=183\times6=183×6=18

    gold tickets.

    Writing blue over gold on both sides, with bbb for the blue tickets, gives

    2510=b18\frac{25}{10}=\frac{b}{18}1025​=18b​

    Since 181818 is not a whole-number multiple of 101010, first reduce the exchange ratio by dividing both of its numbers by 555 (cross-multiplying would also reach 454545).

    25÷510÷5=52\frac{25\div5}{10\div5}=\frac{5}{2}10÷525÷5​=25​

    The gold number now goes from 222 to 181818, a scale factor of

    18÷2=918\div2=918÷2=9

    Multiply the blue number by the same factor.

    b=5×9b=5\times9b=5×9 b=45b=45b=45

    As a check, 4518\frac{45}{18}1845​ and 2510\frac{25}{10}1025​ both reduce to 52\frac{5}{2}25​, so 454545 blue tickets give exactly the 181818 gold tickets needed.

    Answer

    454545 blue tickets.

    Key idea

    Find the total amount required before using the exchange proportion.

  7. Problem 7 Shipping form

    A shipping service charges 7.50 dollars for a 333 kg parcel and uses the same charge per kg for every parcel, with no extra fees. There are 100010001000 grams in a kilogram. A form for a 150015001500 gram parcel records 37.50=1500c\frac{3}{7.50}=\frac{1500}{c}7.503​=c1500​, where ccc is its charge in dollars. Is this record set up correctly? If it is not, write a correct equality and use it to find the charge.

    1. Hint 1

      Check that each position holds the same quantity in the same unit on both sides.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the parcel's mass in kilograms before placing it beside the 333 kg.

    Answer

    No; a correct equality is 37.50=1.5c\frac{3}{7.50}=\frac{1.5}{c}7.503​=c1.5​ (or one with both masses in one unit, in the same order on both sides, as in 30007.50=1500c\frac{3000}{7.50}=\frac{1500}{c}7.503000​=c1500​ or 31.5=7.50c\frac{3}{1.5}=\frac{7.50}{c}1.53​=c7.50​), and the charge is 3.75 dollars.

    Full solution

    Both sides put mass over dollars, but the left mass is in kilograms and the right mass is in grams, so the record is not set up correctly.

    Since 100010001000 grams make one kilogram, the parcel's mass is

    1500÷1000=1.51500\div1000=1.51500÷1000=1.5

    kg.

    For the positive charge ccc, a correct equality is

    37.50=1.5c\frac{3}{7.50}=\frac{1.5}{c}7.503​=c1.5​

    The mass 1.51.51.5 kg is half of 333 kg, so the charge is half of 7.50 dollars.

    7.50÷2=3.757.50\div2=3.757.50÷2=3.75

    As a check, the cross-products 3×3.753\times3.753×3.75 and 7.50×1.57.50\times1.57.50×1.5 are both 11.2511.2511.25.

    The record as written would instead give 7.50×1500÷37.50\times1500\div37.50×1500÷3, which is 3750 dollars, a thousand times too much.

    Answer

    No; a correct equality is 37.50=1.5c\frac{3}{7.50}=\frac{1.5}{c}7.503​=c1.5​ (or one with both masses in one unit, in the same order on both sides, as in 30007.50=1500c\frac{3000}{7.50}=\frac{1500}{c}7.503000​=c1500​ or 31.5=7.50c\frac{3}{1.5}=\frac{7.50}{c}1.53​=c7.50​), and the charge is 3.75 dollars.

    Key idea

    Corresponding positions in a proportion need matching quantities in matching units.

  8. Problem 8 Calculator check

    A calculator reports 3147=6294\frac{31}{47}=\frac{62}{94}4731​=9462​. Jo checks 31×9431\times9431×94 and 47×6247\times6247×62 and finds that both give 291429142914. Does this establish the reported equality? Explain why or why not.

    1. Hint 1

      A common denominator lets you compare numerators directly.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the product of the two original denominators as the shared denominator.

    Answer

    Yes; both fractions become 29144418\frac{2914}{4418}44182914​.

    Full solution

    The denominators are positive, so their product is a valid common denominator.

    47×94=441847\times94=441847×94=4418

    Multiply the numerator and denominator of the left fraction by 949494, and those of the right fraction by 474747.

    3147=29144418\frac{31}{47}=\frac{2914}{4418}4731​=44182914​ 6294=29144418\frac{62}{94}=\frac{2914}{4418}9462​=44182914​

    Jo has compared the two resulting numerators.

    Their equality establishes that the original fractions are equal.

    Answer

    Yes; both fractions become 29144418\frac{2914}{4418}44182914​.

    Key idea

    Cross-products test equality because they become the numerators over a shared nonzero denominator.

  9. Problem 9 The written note

    A note claims that 40=90\frac{4}{0}=\frac{9}{0}04​=09​ is true since 4×04\times04×0 and 0×90\times90×9 both equal zero. Is the claim valid? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Before comparing two fractions, check whether each fraction names a number.

    2. Hint 2

      The common-denominator reasoning requires nonzero denominators.

    Answer

    No; both fractions are undefined.

    Full solution

    The product calculation itself is correct:

    4×0=04\times0=04×0=0 0×9=00\times9=00×9=0

    But neither displayed fraction is defined, because both have denominator zero.

    Cross-products compare fractions over a shared nonzero denominator.

    That requirement fails here, so matching products do not establish the claimed equality.

    Answer

    No; both fractions are undefined.

    Key idea

    Matching cross-products establish a proportion only when the original denominators are nonzero.

  10. Problem 10 Two tasting cups

    A small tasting cup of a drink holds 151515 mL of syrup and 333333 mL of water, and a larger tasting cup of the same drink holds 202020 mL of syrup and 444444 mL of water, so 1533=2044\frac{15}{33}=\frac{20}{44}3315​=4420​. Dana compares the two syrup amounts with each other and the two water amounts with each other, writing 1520=3344\frac{15}{20}=\frac{33}{44}2015​=4433​. Is her proportion true?

    In general, swapping the middle terms of ab=cd\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}ba​=dc​ gives ac=bd\frac{a}{c}=\frac{b}{d}ca​=db​. If the first proportion is true and bbb, ccc and ddd are nonzero, must the second be true? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the diagonal products of Dana's proportion with those of the original.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the cross-products of ac=bd\frac{a}{c}=\frac{b}{d}ca​=db​ and compare them with those of ab=cd\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}ba​=dc​.

    3. Hint 3

      Equal cross-products make a proportion true when its denominators are nonzero.

    Answer

    Yes, Dana's proportion is true. Yes, ac=bd\frac{a}{c}=\frac{b}{d}ca​=db​ must also be true.

    Full solution

    Dana's cross-products are

    15×44=66015\times44=66015×44=660 20×33=66020\times33=66020×33=660

    They are equal and her denominators are nonzero, so her proportion is true.

    Both of her ratios also reduce to 34\frac{3}{4}43​.

    Her proportion has the same two cross-products as the original one, 15×4415\times4415×44 and 33×2033\times2033×20.

    In general, the cross-products of ab=cd\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}ba​=dc​ are a×da\times da×d and b×cb\times cb×c.

    The cross-products of ac=bd\frac{a}{c}=\frac{b}{d}ca​=db​ are a×da\times da×d and c×bc\times bc×b, the same pair, since c×bc\times bc×b equals b×cb\times cb×c.

    The first proportion is true and bbb and ddd are nonzero, so a×da\times da×d equals b×cb\times cb×c.

    The swapped proportion then has equal cross-products and nonzero denominators ccc and ddd, so it is true as well.

    Answer

    Yes, Dana's proportion is true. Yes, ac=bd\frac{a}{c}=\frac{b}{d}ca​=db​ must also be true.

    Key idea

    Swapping the middle terms of a true proportion with nonzero terms keeps the same pair of cross-products, so the new proportion is true as well.
