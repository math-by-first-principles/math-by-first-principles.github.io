    1. Two programmes, and the figure each move is compared with . Foundational, 10 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 10 pts

    A leisure centre reports each spring on how its two swimming programmes have moved over the year, and it reports every move as a percent rather than as a raw count, so that a small programme and a large one can be set side by side. Weekly places on the swim school went from 646464 last spring to 888888 this spring. Weekly places in the lane sessions went from 216216216 down to 189189189.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Find the amount of change in the swim school's weekly places and express that change as a percent. State which of the two figures you divided by, and say why the report treats that figure as the baseline.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Find the percent decrease in the lane sessions' weekly places. Then set the two programmes beside each other and say which of them moved by the larger number of places, counting a rise and a fall alike, and which moved by the larger percent.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      A duty manager writes the lane sessions up as a drop of about 14.3%14.3\%14.3%, having divided the number of places lost by 189189189. Say what that figure does measure, explain why the report measures a drop against the earlier figure instead, and give the figure that should have been published.

      Find and correct the error Say which line first goes wrong, why it is wrong, and then do it correctly. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Every percent here is a comparison, and what it compares with is a moment in time rather than the bigger of two figures. Work out the raw change on a programme first, then settle which spring that change should be measured against.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      A programme can move by more places than the other and still be the smaller move once the size of the programme is taken into account. Work out both of the lane sessions' figures before deciding which comparison to make.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Only the denominator has gone astray, so ask what question the duty manager's ratio does answer. A ratio with a real amount underneath it always answers something.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    The change is 242424 places, an increase of 37.5%37.5\%37.5%. The baseline is last spring's figure of 646464, because the change is being reported as a part of what the programme already had.

    Part B

    The lane sessions fell by 272727 places, a decrease of 12.5%12.5\%12.5%. The lane sessions moved by the larger number of places, while the swim school moved by the larger percent.

    Part C

    Dividing by 189189189 compares the loss with the figure the programme ended the year on, which says the places lost are about 14.3%14.3\%14.3% of those still running. The baseline the report asks for is the figure from before the change, so the published figure should be the 12.5%12.5\%12.5% decrease.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The amount of change is the new figure minus the earlier one:

    88−64=24.88 - 64 = 24.88−64=24.

    The number of places rose, so this is an increase. Percent change measures that rise against the figure the programme started the year on, so the 242424 goes over the earlier 646464, and the ratio is rescaled to a denominator of one hundred:

    2464×100%=0.375×100%=37.5%.\frac{24}{64} \times 100\% = 0.375 \times 100\% = 37.5\%.6424​×100%=0.375×100%=37.5%.

    The swim school rose by 37.5%37.5\%37.5%, a gain of 242424 weekly places. The 646464 is the right baseline because the change grew out of it: the rise is being reported as a part of what was already there. Dividing by this spring's 888888 would answer a question nobody asked, namely what share of this year's places the gain accounts for.

    Part B

    Subtract to find the change:

    189−216=−27.189 - 216 = -27.189−216=−27.

    The programme lost 272727 weekly places, so this is a decrease, and its size is compared with last spring's figure of 216216216:

    27216×100%=0.125×100%=12.5%.\frac{27}{216} \times 100\% = 0.125 \times 100\% = 12.5\%.21627​×100%=0.125×100%=12.5%.

    The lane sessions fell by 12.5%12.5\%12.5%. Now set the two programmes side by side, and they disagree about which move was bigger. The lane sessions lost 272727 places while the swim school gained 242424, so counting a rise and a fall alike the lane sessions moved by more places. But the swim school moved by 37.5%37.5\%37.5% against the lane sessions' 12.5%12.5\%12.5%, so the swim school moved by far the larger percent.

    Both statements are true at once, and that is the whole reason the centre reports percents. The lane sessions began the year on 216216216 places against the swim school's 646464, more than three times as many, so each place lost from the lane sessions is a much smaller share of its programme than each place gained by the swim school.

    Part C

    The duty manager's arithmetic is sound; only the denominator is wrong. Dividing the loss by the figure the programme ended the year on gives

    27189×100%≈14.3%,\frac{27}{189} \times 100\% \approx 14.3\%,18927​×100%≈14.3%,

    which says the places lost are about a seventh of the places still running. That is a genuine fact about this spring's timetable, but it is not a measure of how big the move was.

    Percent change compares a change with the amount that was there before it, because that is the amount the change happened to. The programme ran 216216216 weekly places and lost 272727 of them, so the fair measure is

    27216×100%=12.5%.\frac{27}{216} \times 100\% = 12.5\%.21627​×100%=12.5%.

    The two figures differ because they sit over different denominators, and with the same change on top, the smaller denominator always produces the larger percent. Publishing 14.3%14.3\%14.3% would make the lane sessions look like a steeper decline than they suffered, which matters most in a report whose purpose is to set one programme against another.

    In one line

    The swim school gained 242424 places, and 2464×100%=37.5%\frac{24}{64} \times 100\% = 37.5\%6424​×100%=37.5%, so it rose by 37.5%37.5\%37.5% against last spring's figure of 646464. The lane sessions lost 272727 places, and 27216×100%=12.5%\frac{27}{216} \times 100\% = 12.5\%21627​×100%=12.5%, so the lane sessions moved by the larger number of places while the swim school moved by the larger percent. The duty manager's 14.3%14.3\%14.3% comes from dividing 272727 by this spring's 189189189, which measures the loss against what remains rather than against what was there before; the baseline is last spring's 216216216, so the correct report is a decrease of 12.5%12.5\%12.5%.

    2. What a multiplier is made of . Foundational, 12 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Foundational 12 pts

    A print shop keeps its prices in a spreadsheet that stores each repricing as a single number to multiply by, rather than as a percent with an instruction attached to it.     Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      A percent change always involves two amounts: the one you already had, and the one being added or taken away. Ask how a single multiplication could be made to carry both of them at once.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

    The answer

    Part A

    The increase is 18.7018.7018.70 dollars, so the poster comes to 103.70103.70103.70 dollars by either route. The spreadsheet should store 1.221.221.22.

    Part B

    It records a decrease of 22%22\%22%, since 0.780.780.78 leaves 78%78\%78% of the price standing. The frame's new price is 191.10191.10191.10 dollars.

    The stored number is 1+0.221 + 0.221+0.22. The 111 keeps the whole price already being charged, which is 100%100\%100% of itself, and the 0.220.220.22 supplies the part being added, so one multiplication carries both.     Worked solution

    Part A

    Take the two-step route first. A rise of 22%22\%22% means finding 22%22\%22% of the current price and adding it on, and 22%=0.2222\% = 0.2222%=0.22:

    0.22×85=18.70,85+18.70=103.70.0.22 \times 85 = 18.70, \qquad 85 + 18.70 = 103.70.0.22×85=18.70,85+18.70=103.70.

    1+22100=1.22,1.22×85=103.70.1 + \frac{22}{100} = 1.22, \qquad 1.22 \times 85 = 103.70.1+10022​=1.22,1.22×85=103.70.

    Both routes land on 103.70103.70103.70 dollars, because the multiplier is the two-step calculation packed into a single number.

    Part B

    A stored number below 111 shrinks whatever it multiplies, so this is a decrease. Read off how much of the price survives. Since 0.78=781000.78 = \frac{78}{100}0.78=10078​, the frame keeps 78%78\%78% of what it cost, and what was taken off is the rest:

    100%−78%=22%.100\% - 78\% = 22\%.100%−78%=22%.

    So the record describes a 22%22\%22% decrease. Applying it,

    0.78×245=191.10.0.78 \times 245 = 191.10.0.78×245=191.10.

    The frame's new price is 191.10191.10191.10 dollars. Checking by the two-step route, 22%22\%22% of 245245245 is 0.22×245=53.900.22 \times 245 = 53.900.22×245=53.90 dollars off, and 245−53.90=191.10245 - 53.90 = 191.10245−53.90=191.10, the same price.

    Part C

    Write the two-step calculation on one line, before simplifying anything:

    85+0.22×85.85 + 0.22 \times 85.85+0.22×85.

    The old price appears twice. Once it appears whole, because the shop keeps every dollar it was already charging, and once it appears multiplied by 0.220.220.22, because that is the part being added. Anything that multiplies the same amount in two places can be collected:

    85+0.22×85=(1+0.22)×85=1.22×85.85 + 0.22 \times 85 = (1 + 0.22) \times 85 = 1.22 \times 85.85+0.22×85=(1+0.22)×85=1.22×85.

    That is what the stored number is made of. The 111 is the price you keep, which is 100%100\%100% of itself, and the 0.220.220.22 is the extra 22%22\%22% laid on top, so a single multiplication carries the old price and the increase together. It is not a new rule, only the two-step calculation written without the intermediate stop.

    Multiplying by 0.220.220.22 alone throws away the first of those two pieces. It returns 18.7018.7018.70 dollars, which is the increase by itself rather than the price after the increase, and it is why a shopper who reaches for the bare percent gets an answer far too small to be a price.

    The answer

    The trade price is 198198198 dollars. The discount leaves 72%72\%72% of the list price standing, so the multiplier is 0.720.720.72.

    5. Two changes in a row, and what they come to . Reasoning, 14 points. Question 5 of 5.

    Reasoning 14 pts

The second says that two increases in a row add up, so a 5%5\%5% raise followed by a 4%4\%4% raise is a 9%9\%9% raise. Two sets of figures are enough to test them: a town of 6,4006{,}4006,400 residents that grows by 15%15\%15% one year and shrinks by 15%15\%15% the next, and a salary of 32,00032{,}00032,000 dollars that receives those two raises in successive years.
