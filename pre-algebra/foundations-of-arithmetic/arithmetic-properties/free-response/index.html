  1. Problem 1 Regroup a sum

    Rewrite 18+6+1418 + 6 + 1418+6+14 with parentheses so that 666 and 141414 are added first, keeping the numbers in their original order, and check that the total is unchanged.

    1. Hint 1

      A sum keeps its value when you change which pair is combined first without moving the terms.

    2. Hint 2

      Parentheses mark the pair that is combined first, and the term outside them is added to that pair's sum.

    Answer

    18+(6+14)18 + (6 + 14)18+(6+14); both groupings total 383838.

    Full solution

    Place the parentheses around the last two terms.

    The expression becomes 18+(6+14)18 + (6 + 14)18+(6+14).

    This changes the grouping without changing the order, as the associative property permits.

    Check that the grouped expression still has the original total.

    6+14=2018+20=38\begin{aligned} 6 + 14 &= 20 \\ 18 + 20 &= 38 \end{aligned}6+1418+20​=20=38​

    Adding the first pair instead gives 24+1424 + 1424+14, which is also 383838.

    Answer

    18+(6+14)18 + (6 + 14)18+(6+14); both groupings total 383838.

    Key idea

    The associative property allows a sum to be grouped differently while its terms stay in the same order.

  2. Problem 2 Tickets at an empty hall

    A hall gives each visitor 666 tickets, but no visitors arrive. How many tickets are handed out?

    1. Hint 1

      Multiplication can count equal groups.

    2. Hint 2

      Let the number of visitors be the number of groups and the tickets per visitor be the group size.

    Answer

    000 tickets.

    Full solution

    There are zero visitors, so there are zero groups of six tickets to hand out.

    0×6=00 \times 6 = 00×6=0

    No group is handed out, so the total is 000 tickets.

    Answer

    000 tickets.

    Key idea

    Zero groups of a quantity give a total of zero, which is one meaning of multiplication by zero.

  3. Problem 3 Keep the number unchanged

    Which whole number belongs in the box?

    (73+0)×□=73(73 + 0) \times \square = 73(73+0)×□=73

    1. Hint 1

      Look for operations that leave a number unchanged.

    2. Hint 2

      Adding nothing preserves the first number, so the missing factor must preserve that same number when multiplying.

    Answer

    111.

    Full solution

    Zero is the additive identity, so adding it leaves 737373 unchanged.

    73+0=7373 + 0 = 7373+0=73

    One is the multiplicative identity, so a factor of 111 keeps 737373 unchanged.

    73×1=7373 \times 1 = 7373×1=73

    No other whole number works: a factor of 000 gives 000, and a factor of 222 or more gives at least 146146146.

    So the missing factor is 111, and the two displayed equations check the completed expression.

    Answer

    111.

    Key idea

    The additive identity is zero and the multiplicative identity is one.

  4. Problem 4 Combine the donations

    Six groups donate 142142142, 656565, 585858, 191919, 353535 and 818181 books. Find the total by reordering and grouping the counts into helpful pairs, and show your pairs.

    1. Hint 1

      A sum keeps its value when its terms are reordered and regrouped.

    2. Hint 2

      Look for pairs whose ones digits complete a ten.

    3. Hint 3

      Pair 142142142 with 585858, then find pairs among the four remaining counts.

    Answer

    400400400 books; pairs: 142+58142 + 58142+58, 65+3565 + 3565+35, and 19+8119 + 8119+81.

    Full solution

    Reorder the counts to bring helpful pairs together, then group each pair.

    The commutative property permits the reordering and the associative property permits the grouping.

    142+58=20065+35=10019+81=100\begin{aligned} 142 + 58 &= 200 \\ 65 + 35 &= 100 \\ 19 + 81 &= 100 \end{aligned}142+5865+3519+81​=200=100=100​

    Add the three subtotals.

    200+100+100=400200 + 100 + 100 = 400200+100+100=400

    Each donation appears in exactly one pair, so the total includes all 400400400 books without adding or losing any.

    Answer

    400400400 books; pairs: 142+58142 + 58142+58, 65+3565 + 3565+35, and 19+8119 + 8119+81.

    Key idea

    Reordering and regrouping a long sum can turn several awkward additions into round subtotals.

  5. Problem 5 Count the narration time

    A museum has 555 audio devices. Each device plays a narration 777 times per day, and each play lasts 222 minutes. This continues for 333 days. Find the total minutes of narration by grouping the factors into convenient pairs, and explain why your grouping must give the same total as multiplying the factors in the order the facts are given.

    1. Hint 1

      Count the narration minutes by multiplying the number of devices, minutes per play, plays per day and days.

    2. Hint 2

      The factors may be reordered and regrouped without changing the total.

    3. Hint 3

      Pair the factors 555 and 222, then pair the remaining factors.

    Answer

    210210210 minutes, for example from (5×2)×(7×3)(5 \times 2) \times (7 \times 3)(5×2)×(7×3).

    Full solution

    The count is 5×7×2×35 \times 7 \times 2 \times 35×7×2×3: devices, plays per day, minutes per play and days.

    Reorder the factors to bring 555 and 222 together, then group the factors into two convenient pairs.

    5×2=107×3=2110×21=210\begin{aligned} 5 \times 2 &= 10 \\ 7 \times 3 &= 21 \\ 10 \times 21 &= 210 \end{aligned}5×27×310×21​=10=21=210​

    The grouped product is (5×2)×(7×3)(5 \times 2) \times (7 \times 3)(5×2)×(7×3), giving 210210210 minutes.

    Reordering and regrouping change only which partial count is made first.

    Every play of every device on every day is still counted exactly once, so no grouping can reach a different total.

    Check by counting one device over all three days: 2×72 \times 72×7 gives 141414 minutes per day, and 14×314 \times 314×3 gives 424242 minutes.

    All 555 devices then play 5×425 \times 425×42, or 210210210 minutes.

    Answer

    210210210 minutes, for example from (5×2)×(7×3)(5 \times 2) \times (7 \times 3)(5×2)×(7×3).

    Key idea

    Regrouping the factors of a repeated count changes the subtotals used without changing what is counted.

  6. Problem 6 Check the art order

    Each art kit contains 111 brush, 000 paint tubes and 666 paper sheets. A studio orders 151515 kits and 999 separate paint tubes. How many brushes, paint tubes and paper sheets will the studio receive?

    1. Hint 1

      Count each kind of item separately, using the number of kits and the contents of one kit.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiplying the number of kits by one preserves that number, while multiplying it by zero gives no items of that kind.

    3. Hint 3

      After counting the kit contents, include the paint tubes ordered separately.

    Answer

    151515 brushes, 999 paint tubes and 909090 paper sheets.

    Full solution

    There is one brush per kit, so the multiplicative identity gives 151515 brushes.

    15×1=1515 \times 1 = 1515×1=15

    Each kit has six paper sheets, giving 909090 paper sheets.

    15×6=9015 \times 6 = 9015×6=90

    Fifteen kits with no paint tubes in any kit contribute no paint tubes.

    The nine separate paint tubes must still be included.

    15×0=00+9=9\begin{aligned} 15 \times 0 &= 0 \\ 0 + 9 &= 9 \end{aligned}15×00+9​=0=9​

    The zero property counts the paint tubes in the kits, and the additive identity leaves the nine separate paint tubes unchanged.

    Every item in the order has been counted in its own category.

    Answer

    151515 brushes, 999 paint tubes and 909090 paper sheets.

    Key idea

    Multiplication by one preserves a count, while empty groups contribute zero before other items are added.

  7. Problem 7 Repair a product

    A game score is the product 16×0×1×716 \times 0 \times 1 \times 716×0×1×7. Replace exactly one of its four factors with a whole number so that the score becomes 112112112. Which factor should be replaced, and what should replace it?

    1. Hint 1

      A zero factor makes the entire product zero.

    2. Hint 2

      If you replace a factor other than zero, consider which factor will still force the score to zero.

    3. Hint 3

      Find the product of the three factors that stay, then choose a replacement that preserves it.

    Answer

    Replace the factor 000 with 111.

    Full solution

    Changing 161616, 111 or 777 leaves the zero factor in place, so each of those choices would still give a score of 000.

    The factor that must change is 000.

    The three unchanged factors already give the desired score.

    16×1=1616×7=112\begin{aligned} 16 \times 1 &= 16 \\ 16 \times 7 &= 112 \end{aligned}16×116×7​=16=112​

    The replacement must keep this product at 112112112.

    A replacement of 111 does, while 000 gives 000 and any whole number from 222 up gives at least 224224224.

    Check the repaired product.

    16×1×1×7=11216 \times 1 \times 1 \times 7 = 11216×1×1×7=112

    Answer

    Replace the factor 000 with 111.

    Key idea

    A zero factor makes a product zero, while a factor of one preserves the product of the other factors.

  8. Problem 8 Check Nora's label

    Nora rewrites (11+9)+26(11 + 9) + 26(11+9)+26 as 26+(11+9)26 + (11 + 9)26+(11+9). She calls this the associative property since the parentheses appear in a different place on the page. Is her label correct? Name the property used and explain your decision.

    1. Hint 1

      Distinguish moving a group from changing which terms belong to it.

    2. Hint 2

      Look at the pair inside the parentheses before and after the rewrite.

    3. Hint 3

      Treat the whole parenthesized sum as one number and compare its position with that of 262626.

    Answer

    No; the commutative property of addition.

    Full solution

    The terms 111111 and 999 remain grouped together in both expressions, so the grouping has not changed.

    The two things being added are the sum (11+9)(11 + 9)(11+9) and the number 262626.

    Those two things trade places, which is the commutative property of addition.

    The grouped sum is 202020.

    Both orders count that same 202020 together with the same 262626, with nothing added or removed.

    11+9=2020+26=4626+20=46\begin{aligned} 11 + 9 &= 20 \\ 20 + 26 &= 46 \\ 26 + 20 &= 46 \end{aligned}11+920+2626+20​=20=46=46​

    The equal totals check the rewrite, while the unchanged pair identifies which property was used.

    Answer

    No; the commutative property of addition.

    Key idea

    Moving a whole group past another term changes their order, even when the parentheses move on the page.

  9. Problem 9 Test the subtraction claims

    A student says that subtraction is commutative and associative, so its numbers can be swapped or regrouped freely.

    Give a numerical counterexample to each conclusion, and explain why your examples settle the claims. Use positive whole numbers. You may describe a reversed subtraction as below zero without calculating a negative answer. For your grouping example, choose numbers that keep every subtraction above zero.

    1. Hint 1

      A property must hold for every allowed choice, so one failed example can disprove it.

    2. Hint 2

      To test order, choose two different positive numbers and compare both orders.

    3. Hint 3

      To test grouping, keep three numbers in the same order and compare the two choices of first pair.

    4. Hint 4

      A large first number and two smaller numbers can keep both grouped calculations above zero.

    Answer

    One choice: 9−2=79 - 2 = 79−2=7, but 2−9<02 - 9 < 02−9<0; (18−7)−3=8(18 - 7) - 3 = 8(18−7)−3=8, but 18−(7−3)=1418 - (7 - 3) = 1418−(7−3)=14.

    Full solution

    For order, choose 999 and 222.

    9−2=79 - 2 = 79−2=7

    Reversing them asks for 999 to be taken from 222.

    That goes below zero, so it does not give 777.

    Thus 9−29 - 29−2 and 2−92 - 92−9 disagree, disproving commutativity.

    For grouping, keep 181818, 777 and 333 in that order.

    First combine the first pair.

    18−7=1111−3=8\begin{aligned} 18 - 7 &= 11 \\ 11 - 3 &= 8 \end{aligned}18−711−3​=11=8​

    Then combine the last pair instead.

    7−3=418−4=14\begin{aligned} 7 - 3 &= 4 \\ 18 - 4 &= 14 \end{aligned}7−318−4​=4=14​

    Every subtraction stays above zero, but the final results differ, disproving associativity.

    Each claimed property would have to hold for every choice of numbers.

    One counterexample to each claim is enough to show that the claimed rule fails.

    Answer

    One choice: 9−2=79 - 2 = 79−2=7, but 2−9<02 - 9 < 02−9<0; (18−7)−3=8(18 - 7) - 3 = 8(18−7)−3=8, but 18−(7−3)=1418 - (7 - 3) = 1418−(7−3)=14.

    Key idea

    A single counterexample disproves a claimed property of subtraction.

  10. Problem 10 Correct the division instructions

    An instruction card says that division allows both swapping the two numbers and regrouping three numbers freely. Show that each instruction can change the result by giving one numerical counterexample for swapping and one for regrouping, and explain your comparisons.

    Use positive whole numbers. For a reversed division, you may describe the answer as less than one without writing a fraction. In your regrouping example, every division must have a whole number result.

    1. Hint 1

      An instruction that is meant to work freely must preserve the result for every allowed choice.

    2. Hint 2

      For swapping, choose a larger number that contains several equal groups of a smaller number.

    3. Hint 3

      For regrouping, compare dividing twice with dividing once by the result of the other division.

    4. Hint 4

      Choose a large first number and two small divisors, with the middle number divisible by the last number.

    Answer

    One choice: 24÷8=324 \div 8 = 324÷8=3, but 8÷24<18 \div 24 < 18÷24<1; (72÷6)÷3=4(72 \div 6) \div 3 = 4(72÷6)÷3=4, but 72÷(6÷3)=3672 \div (6 \div 3) = 3672÷(6÷3)=36.

    Full solution

    For swapping, choose 242424 and 888.

    24÷8=324 \div 8 = 324÷8=3

    In the reverse order, 888 contains less than one whole group of 242424, so 8÷248 \div 248÷24 is less than 111 and cannot equal 333.

    Swapping changes the result.

    For regrouping, keep 727272, 666 and 333 in that order.

    Grouping the first pair gives these steps.

    72÷6=1212÷3=4\begin{aligned} 72 \div 6 &= 12 \\ 12 \div 3 &= 4 \end{aligned}72÷612÷3​=12=4​

    Grouping the last pair instead gives these steps.

    6÷3=272÷2=36\begin{aligned} 6 \div 3 &= 2 \\ 72 \div 2 &= 36 \end{aligned}6÷372÷2​=2=36​

    The results are 444 and 363636.

    Every divisor is nonzero and every division has a whole number result, yet regrouping changes the value.

    The first counterexample shows division is not commutative, and the second shows it is not associative.

    Each instruction therefore fails even though it may happen to work for some choices.

    Answer

    One choice: 24÷8=324 \div 8 = 324÷8=3, but 8÷24<18 \div 24 < 18÷24<1; (72÷6)÷3=4(72 \div 6) \div 3 = 4(72÷6)÷3=4, but 72÷(6÷3)=3672 \div (6 \div 3) = 3672÷(6÷3)=36.

    Key idea

    Division depends on both the order of its numbers and the grouping of successive divisions.
