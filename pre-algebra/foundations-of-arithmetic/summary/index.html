Foundations of Arithmetic: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Base 10 (the decimal system)
A place-value system using digits 000 through 999. Each place holds bundles of ten units from the place to its right.
Expanded form
A number written as the sum of its digit values. Zero contributions may be left out of the sum: 4,502=4,000+500+24{,}502 = 4{,}000 + 500 + 24,502=4,000+500+2.
Placeholder zero
A zero that keeps other digits in their correct places. Removing the zero from 3,2053{,}2053,205 moves the digits to its left and gives 325325325.
Period
A group of three places, counted from the right: ones, thousands, millions, billions. Commas separate periods; the first group may have fewer than three digits.
Property (of an operation)
A rule that holds for every allowed choice of numbers. A general reason supports the rule; one counterexample disproves it.
Commutative versus associative
Commutative changes order: numbers trade places. Associative changes grouping: the numbers stay in order, but a different pair is combined first.
Counterexample
An example where a proposed rule fails. It disproves a claim that the rule works for every allowed choice.
Grouping symbols
Parentheses (  )(\;)(), brackets [  ][\;][], and braces {  }\{\;\}{} mark a quantity to evaluate together. Start with the innermost group.
Greatest common factor
The greatest positive whole number that divides each of two or more positive whole numbers exactly. Pull it out when factoring a sum completely.

Formulas and theorems

  • Value of a digit

    digit value=digit×place value\text{digit value} = \text{digit} \times \text{place value}digit value=digit×place value

    Use when For a whole number, multiply the digit by its place value. A zero digit contributes zero but may still be needed as a placeholder.

    e.g. In 6,3086{,}3086,308 the 333 sits in the hundreds column, so its value is 3×100=3003 \times 100 = 3003×100=300.

  • The ten-times ladder of places

    …,100,000,10,000,1000,100,10,1\ldots, 100{,}000, 10{,}000, 1000, 100, 10, 1…,100,000,10,000,1000,100,10,1
    Text description

    A ladder of the whole-number places from ten thousands down to ones, joined by times-ten arrows, with the ones place at the bottom.

    Use when One place left multiplies a digit's value by 101010; one place right divides it by 101010. The whole-number places end at the ones.

    e.g. The 555 in 508508508 is worth 101010 times the 555 in 585858, since 500÷50=10500 \div 50 = 10500÷50=10.

  • How many of a place a number contains

    tens in n=n10hundreds in n=n100\begin{gathered} \text{tens in } n = \frac{n}{10} \\ \text{hundreds in } n = \frac{n}{100} \end{gathered}tens in n=10n​hundreds in n=100n​​

    Use when Here nnn is the whole number, not the digit in the named column. These formulas give an exact whole-number count when nnn is a multiple of the bundle size.

    e.g. 3,4003{,}4003,400 contains 340340340 tens, even though its tens digit is 000.

  • Commutative property

    a+b=b+aa×b=b×a\begin{gathered} a + b = b + a \\ a \times b = b \times a \end{gathered}a+b=b+aa×b=b×a​

    Use when For addition and multiplication of whole numbers. Swapping the inputs of subtraction or division can change the result.

    e.g. 5−3=25 - 3 = 25−3=2, while 3−53 - 53−5 takes 555 away from only 333 and cannot give 222.

  • Associative property

    (a+b)+c=a+(b+c)(a×b)×c=a×(b×c)\begin{gathered} (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) \\ (a \times b) \times c = a \times (b \times c) \end{gathered}(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)(a×b)×c=a×(b×c)​

    Use when For addition and multiplication of whole numbers. The numbers keep their order while grouping changes. Subtraction and division do not have this property.

    e.g. (10−4)−2=4(10 - 4) - 2 = 4(10−4)−2=4, but 10−(4−2)=810 - (4 - 2) = 810−(4−2)=8.

  • Identity elements and the zero property

    a+0=a,a×1=aa×0=0\begin{gathered} a + 0 = a, \qquad a \times 1 = a \\ a \times 0 = 0 \end{gathered}a+0=a,a×1=aa×0=0​

    Use when For every whole number aaa. Zero is the additive identity and one the multiplicative identity. The zero property says any product with a zero factor is zero.

    e.g. (37+58)×(6−6)=0(37 + 58) \times (6 - 6) = 0(37+58)×(6−6)=0, with the first factor never needed.

  • A chain removes the total, or divides by the product

    a−b−c=a−(b+c)a÷b÷c=a÷(b×c)\begin{gathered} a - b - c = a - (b + c) \\ a \div b \div c = a \div (b \times c) \end{gathered}a−b−c=a−(b+c)a÷b÷c=a÷(b×c)​

    Use when The chains are read left to right. Keep the starting number aaa fixed; later removals or divisors may trade places. For whole-number calculations, b+cb + cb+c must not exceed aaa, divisors must be nonzero, and each division must be exact.

    e.g. 30−12−8=30−20=1030 - 12 - 8 = 30 - 20 = 1030−12−8=30−20=10, and 720÷12÷3=720÷36=20720 \div 12 \div 3 = 720 \div 36 = 20720÷12÷3=720÷36=20.

  • The order of operations

    Grouping  →  Exponents  →×, ÷  →  +, −\begin{gathered} \text{Grouping} \;\to\; \text{Exponents} \;\to \\ \times,\ \div \;\to\; +,\ - \end{gathered}Grouping→Exponents→×, ÷→+, −​

    Use when PEMDAS recalls four tiers, not six. Multiplication and division share a tier, as do addition and subtraction; work left to right within each pair. Apply the tiers inside every group, starting innermost.

    e.g. 2×(3+1)2−24÷6=2×16−4=282 \times (3 + 1)^2 - 24 \div 6 = 2 \times 16 - 4 = 282×(3+1)2−24÷6=2×16−4=28.

  • The fraction bar as a grouping symbol

    a+bc+d=(a+b)÷(c+d)\frac{a + b}{c + d} = (a + b) \div (c + d)c+da+b​=(a+b)÷(c+d)

    Use when Evaluate the whole numerator and whole denominator before dividing. The denominator c+dc + dc+d must not be zero. Terms outside the fraction remain outside.

    e.g. 8+42+2=12÷4=3\dfrac{8 + 4}{2 + 2} = 12 \div 4 = 32+28+4​=12÷4=3.

  • Distributive property

    a(b+c)=ab+aca(b−c)=ab−ac\begin{gathered} a(b + c) = ab + ac \\ a(b - c) = ab - ac \end{gathered}a(b+c)=ab+aca(b−c)=ab−ac​
    Text description

    A rectangle of height a, cut into parts of width b and c labeled ab and ac, whose full width is b plus c.

    Use when The factor aaa multiplies every term of the sum or difference, including sums with more than two terms. For whole-number subtraction, use b≥cb \ge cb≥c. Do not apply this rule to a product inside parentheses.

    e.g. 7(100−2)=700−14=6867(100 - 2) = 700 - 14 = 6867(100−2)=700−14=686.

  • Factoring, the same rule backward

    ab+ac=a(b+c)ab + ac = a(b + c)ab+ac=a(b+c)

    Use when For positive whole-number terms, choose a factor aaa dividing every term. Factoring completely uses the greatest common factor, leaving no shared factor above 111 inside. Check by distributing back.

    e.g. 45+27=9(5+3)45 + 27 = 9(5 + 3)45+27=9(5+3), while 3(15+9)3(15 + 9)3(15+9) is a true equality that is not finished.

Problem types, step by step

Name the place, or the value, of a digit in a large number

  1. Separate periods of three digits from the right.
  2. Name the digit's place. The hundreds place within the thousands period is the hundred-thousands place.
  3. For its value, multiply the digit by the place value.
  4. To compare digit values, subtract for the difference or divide for how many times as large, provided the smaller value is not zero.

e.g. In 7,248,0917{,}248{,}0917,248,091, the 222 is in the hundred-thousands place and is worth 200,000200{,}000200,000.

Convert between a number and its expanded form

  1. For expanded form, multiply each nonzero digit by its place value and add the contributions.
  2. To rebuild the numeral, put each contribution in its place and fill any missing places with zeros.

e.g. 50,409=50,000+400+950{,}409 = 50{,}000 + 400 + 950,409=50,000+400+9.

Compare or order whole numbers

  1. Ignore leading zeros. More digits means a larger whole number.
  2. If digit counts match, compare from the left.
  3. The first differing place decides. To order several numbers, compare pairs until every number is placed.

e.g. 4,081>9914{,}081 > 9914,081>991 on digit count alone, while 3,205>3,1953{,}205 > 3{,}1953,205>3,195 is settled at the hundreds.

Build the largest or smallest number from a given set of digits

  1. Use every given digit once. For the largest number, arrange them from largest to smallest.
  2. For the smallest number with the full digit count, put the smallest nonzero digit first, then the remaining digits from smallest to largest. This requires at least one nonzero digit.

e.g. From 444, 000, 777, 111 the largest is 7,4107{,}4107,410 and the smallest is 1,0471{,}0471,047.

Evaluate a numerical expression

  1. Evaluate the innermost groups first, including the whole top and bottom of each fraction.
  2. Within each group and the remaining expression, apply powers, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction.
  3. Work left to right within a tier. Do one operation per line and copy the rest unchanged.

Place parentheses to force a target value

  1. Evaluate the expression as written and compare its value with the target.
  2. Try grouping an operation that needs to happen earlier.
  3. Re-evaluate the whole expression to check the target; the usual tiers still apply outside the group.

e.g. 2+3×42 + 3 \times 42+3×4 reads 141414; bracketing the sum gives (2+3)×4=20(2 + 3) \times 4 = 20(2+3)×4=20.

Rearrange a sum or product into an easier one

  1. Look for numbers that combine to a round total or product, such as 101010, 100100100, or 100010001000.
  2. Bring them together using the commutative property, then group them using the associative property.
  3. Calculate that pair and finish the remaining sum or product.

e.g. 8×125×7=(8×125)×7=1000×7=70008 \times 125 \times 7 = (8 \times 125) \times 7 = 1000 \times 7 = 70008×125×7=(8×125)×7=1000×7=7000.

Multiply mentally by splitting one factor

  1. Choose a nearby round number for one factor.
  2. Write that factor as the round number plus or minus a small amount; leave the other factor whole.
  3. Multiply both parts by the unchanged factor, then add or subtract the products.

e.g. 9×97=9(100−3)=900−27=8739 \times 97 = 9(100 - 3) = 900 - 27 = 8739×97=9(100−3)=900−27=873.

Factor a sum completely

  1. Find the greatest positive number dividing every term exactly.
  2. Write that factor outside parentheses and the quotients inside.
  3. Check by distributing. The terms inside should share no factor above 111.

e.g. 48+80=16(3+5)48 + 80 = 16(3 + 5)48+80=16(3+5), and 16×8=12816 \times 8 = 12816×8=128 matches.

Name the property that justifies a step

  1. Compare the expressions to see what changed.
  2. Numbers trading places use the commutative property; regrouping without changing their order uses the associative property.
  3. Adding zero or multiplying by one uses an identity. Multiplying by zero uses the zero property.
  4. Multiplying a factor into a sum or difference, or taking a shared factor out, uses the distributive property.

e.g. 8×7×5=8×5×78 \times 7 \times 5 = 8 \times 5 \times 78×7×5=8×5×7 swaps factors, so it uses the commutative property.

Exam traps

  • Trap Reading PEMDAS as six separate ranks.

    Fix M and D share a tier, as do A and S. Work left to right: 16÷4×2=816 \div 4 \times 2 = 816÷4×2=8 and 10−4+1=710 - 4 + 1 = 710−4+1=7.

  • Trap Ignoring the tiers after finishing parentheses.

    Fix The remaining expression still follows the tiers: 30−2×(3+4)=30−14=1630 - 2 \times (3 + 4) = 30 - 14 = 1630−2×(3+4)=30−14=16.

  • Trap Writing a stacked fraction on one line without grouping its top and bottom.

    Fix Keep the groups: 32+61+2=(32+6)÷(1+2)\dfrac{3^2 + 6}{1 + 2} = (3^2 + 6) \div (1 + 2)1+232+6​=(32+6)÷(1+2).

  • Trap Calling every rearrangement commutative.

    Fix (5×8)×2=5×(8×2)(5 \times 8) \times 2 = 5 \times (8 \times 2)(5×8)×2=5×(8×2) changes only grouping, so it is associative. Commutative changes order.

  • Trap Multiplying only the first term inside parentheses.

    Fix Multiply every term: 9(50+3)=450+27=4779(50 + 3) = 450 + 27 = 4779(50+3)=450+27=477, not 450+3450 + 3450+3.

  • Trap Confusing a digit's place with its value.

    Fix In 6,3086{,}3086,308, the 333 has place hundreds and value 300300300.

  • Trap Rebuilding 40,000+2,000+90+540{,}000 + 2{,}000 + 90 + 540,000+2,000+90+5 as 42,90542{,}90542,905.

    Fix There is no hundreds contribution, so put zero there: 42,09542{,}09542,095.

  • Trap Using the tens digit to count all the tens in a number.

    Fix Divide the whole number by ten: 9,060÷10=9069{,}060 \div 10 = 9069,060÷10=906 tens, although its tens digit is 666.

  • Trap Swapping the starting number in a subtraction or division chain.

    Fix Only later removals or divisors may trade places: 80−25−15=80−15−25=4080 - 25 - 15 = 80 - 15 - 25 = 4080−25−15=80−15−25=40, but the 808080 stays first. Keep removals within the starting amount, and divisions exact with nonzero divisors. Regrouping is different: 80−(25−15)=7080 - (25 - 15) = 7080−(25−15)=70.
