  1. Problem 1 A factor on the right

    Rewrite (30+8)×7(30 + 8) \times 7(30+8)×7 as a sum of two products, one for each term in the parentheses, without evaluating the products.

    1. Hint 1

      Changing the order of two factors leaves their product unchanged.

    2. Hint 2

      Move 777 to the left of the parentheses, then multiply each term inside by that factor.

    Answer

    30×7+8×730 \times 7 + 8 \times 730×7+8×7, or equivalently 7×30+7×87 \times 30 + 7 \times 87×30+7×8.

    Full solution

    Multiplication is commutative, so (30+8)×7(30 + 8) \times 7(30+8)×7 has the same value as 7(30+8)7(30 + 8)7(30+8).

    With the factor 777 now on the left, distribute it to both terms.

    7(30+8)=7×30+7×87(30 + 8) = 7 \times 30 + 7 \times 87(30+8)=7×30+7×8

    Swapping the two factors in each product changes neither product, so the sum can be written with 777 on the right.

    (30+8)×7=30×7+8×7(30 + 8) \times 7 = 30 \times 7 + 8 \times 7(30+8)×7=30×7+8×7

    Check the values: the original product is 38×738 \times 738×7, which is 266266266.

    The two products are 210210210 and 565656, and their sum is also 266266266.

    Answer

    30×7+8×730 \times 7 + 8 \times 730×7+8×7, or equivalently 7×30+7×87 \times 30 + 7 \times 87×30+7×8.

    Key idea

    An outside factor multiplies every term in a sum even when the factor is written on the right.

  2. Problem 2 Completing a distributed difference

    Find the whole number that belongs in the box: 8(□−6)=400−488(\square - 6) = 400 - 488(□−6)=400−48.

    1. Hint 1

      The two sides must describe the same difference of two products.

    2. Hint 2

      The subtracted 484848 matches 8×68 \times 68×6. Match 400400400 with 888 times the missing number.

    Answer

    505050.

    Full solution

    The factor 888 multiplies both numbers inside the parentheses.

    The second product is 8×6=488 \times 6 = 488×6=48, which matches the subtraction on the right.

    The first product must be 400400400, so divide 400400400 by the outside factor.

    400÷8=50400 \div 8 = 50400÷8=50

    Check by putting 505050 in the box.

    8(50−6)=8×44=352\begin{aligned} 8(50 - 6) &= 8 \times 44 \\ &= 352 \end{aligned}8(50−6)​=8×44=352​

    The right side also has value 400−48=352400 - 48 = 352400−48=352, so the equality is true.

    Answer

    505050.

    Key idea

    In a distributed difference, each product pairs the outside factor with one of the numbers inside the parentheses.

  3. Problem 3 Collecting the shared factor

    Write 16+4816 + 4816+48 as a product with its greatest common factor outside parentheses and a sum inside.

    1. Hint 1

      A positive common factor cannot exceed the smaller of the two positive terms.

    2. Hint 2

      Test whether the smaller term divides the larger, then divide both terms by the factor you find.

    Answer

    16(1+3)16(1 + 3)16(1+3).

    Full solution

    The number 161616 divides itself and also divides 484848.

    No number larger than 161616 divides 161616, so 161616 is the greatest common factor.

    The two quotients are 111 and 333.

    16+48=16(1+3)16 + 48 = 16(1 + 3)16+48=16(1+3)

    Check by distributing back: 16×1=1616 \times 1 = 1616×1=16 and

    16×3=48.16 \times 3 = 48.16×3=48.

    The terms inside share no factor greater than 111.

    Answer

    16(1+3)16(1 + 3)16(1+3).

    Key idea

    When the smaller positive term divides the larger, it is their greatest common factor.

  4. Problem 4 Pages for a print order

    A print shop prints 8 manuals with 102 pages in each manual. It also prints 24 single-page notices. How many pages does it print altogether?

    1. Hint 1

      Count the pages in the repeated manuals, then include the notices once.

    2. Hint 2

      For mental multiplication, split 102102102 into a round hundred and the amount left over.

    3. Hint 3

      After finding 8×1008 \times 1008×100 and 8×28 \times 28×2, add those results and the 242424 notice pages.

    Answer

    840840840 pages.

    Full solution

    Each manual has 100100100 pages plus 222 more.

    Multiply both parts by the number of manuals.

    8×102=8(100+2)=800+16=816\begin{aligned} 8 \times 102 &= 8(100 + 2) \\ &= 800 + 16 \\ &= 816 \end{aligned}8×102​=8(100+2)=800+16=816​

    The notices add 242424 pages to the manual pages.

    816+24=840816 + 24 = 840816+24=840

    Check by counting the 800800800 pages from the hundreds first.

    The 161616 extra manual pages and 242424 notice pages make 404040 more pages, giving 840840840 pages altogether.

    Answer

    840840840 pages.

    Key idea

    Splitting a factor near a round number makes the repeated part of a total easier to calculate.

  5. Problem 5 Two parts of one rectangle

    A rectangle is 6 cm high. A straight cut parallel to its sides, from its top edge to its bottom edge, divides it into two smaller rectangles. The left piece is 7 cm wide, and the right piece has an area of 54 square cm. Both pieces retain the full height.

    Find the area of the original rectangle. Explain why adding the two piece areas gives the same result as multiplying its full height by its full width.

    1. Hint 1

      The pieces cover the original rectangle without gaps or overlaps.

    2. Hint 2

      The right piece has height 666 cm, so divide its area by 666 to find its width.

    3. Hint 3

      Add the two widths for the full width, and compare the whole area with the two piece areas.

    Answer

    969696 square cm; 6(7+9)=6×7+6×96(7 + 9) = 6 \times 7 + 6 \times 96(7+9)=6×7+6×9.

    Full solution

    A rectangle's area is its height times its width.

    The right piece has height 666 cm and area 545454 square cm, so its width is 54÷6=954 \div 6 = 954÷6=9 cm.

    The full width is 7+9=167 + 9 = 167+9=16 cm.

    6×16=966 \times 16 = 966×16=96

    The left piece has area 6×7=426 \times 7 = 426×7=42 square cm, and the right piece has area 545454 square cm.

    Their sum checks the whole area.

    42+54=9642 + 54 = 9642+54=96

    The cut changes neither the amount of space nor the common height.

    Adding the areas counts each square cm of the original rectangle exactly once.

    That is why 6(7+9)6(7 + 9)6(7+9) equals 6×7+6×96 \times 7 + 6 \times 96×7+6×9.

    Answer

    969696 square cm; 6(7+9)=6×7+6×96(7 + 9) = 6 \times 7 + 6 \times 96(7+9)=6×7+6×9.

    Key idea

    Splitting a rectangle's width separates its area into two products with the same height.

  6. Problem 6 Counters after a class activity

    A teacher has 6 sets of counters, with 32 counters in each set. After removing 7 counters from each set, the teacher puts all the remaining counters into a storage box that already holds 18 counters. How many counters are now in the storage box?

    1. Hint 1

      The removal happens in every set, while the counters already in storage are counted once.

    2. Hint 2

      The remaining counters from the sets are described by 6(32−7)6(32 - 7)6(32−7).

    3. Hint 3

      Subtract the total removed from the total originally in the sets, then include the stored counters.

    Answer

    168168168 counters.

    Full solution

    Each of the 666 sets loses 777 counters, so the outside factor multiplies both the starting amount and the amount removed.

    6(32−7)=6×32−6×7=192−42=150\begin{aligned} 6(32 - 7) &= 6 \times 32 - 6 \times 7 \\ &= 192 - 42 \\ &= 150 \end{aligned}6(32−7)​=6×32−6×7=192−42=150​

    Add the 181818 counters already in storage.

    150+18=168150 + 18 = 168150+18=168

    Check by finding the remainder in one set first:

    32−7=25.32 - 7 = 25.32−7=25.

    Six sets of 252525 hold 150150150 counters, and the stored 181818 bring the total to 168168168 counters.

    Answer

    168168168 counters.

    Key idea

    When every equal group loses the same amount, the total loss is the number of groups times that amount.

  7. Problem 7 Joining colored tile panels

    A designer uses all 12 orange tiles to make one solid rectangle and all 20 purple tiles to make another. Each square tile measures 1 cm on each side, and every tile stays whole. The rectangles must have the same height and are joined along their full heights, without gaps or overlaps.

    What is the greatest possible height of the joined rectangle, and what is its width at that height?

    1. Hint 1

      The shared height must allow each color's tiles to form complete rows.

    2. Hint 2

      Look for the largest whole number that divides both 121212 and 202020.

    3. Hint 3

      Divide each tile count by the shared height to find the two widths, then add those widths.

    Answer

    Height: 444 cm; width: 888 cm.

    Full solution

    Each tile covers 111 square cm.

    A whole-number height must divide both areas.

    The possible heights for the orange piece are 111, 222, 333, 444, 666 and 121212 cm.

    Of these, 111, 222 and 444 also divide 202020, so the greatest shared height is 444 cm.

    At height 444 cm, the orange piece is 12÷4=312 \div 4 = 312÷4=3 cm wide, and the purple piece is 20÷4=520 \div 4 = 520÷4=5 cm wide.

    12+20=4(3+5)12 + 20 = 4(3 + 5)12+20=4(3+5)

    The joined width is 3+5=83 + 5 = 83+5=8 cm.

    Check: a rectangle 444 cm high and 888 cm wide has area 4×8=324 \times 8 = 324×8=32 square cm, matching all 323232 tiles.

    Answer

    Height: 444 cm; width: 888 cm.

    Key idea

    A greatest common factor can represent the greatest shared whole-number height of adjacent rectangles.

  8. Problem 8 Checking a shortcut claim

    A student knows that 7×86=6027 \times 86 = 6027×86=602 and wants to find 7×857 \times 857×85. The student says the new product is 111 less, since 858585 is 111 less than 868686. Decide whether the claim is correct, and state how much the product decreases.

    1. Hint 1

      A change in the size of every equal group affects the total once for each group.

    2. Hint 2

      Write 868686 as 85+185 + 185+1, then compare the distributed expression with 7×857 \times 857×85.

    Answer

    Incorrect; the product decreases by 777.

    Full solution

    There are 777 equal groups.

    Changing each group from 868686 to 858585 removes one from each of them.

    Distribution records all seven removals.

    7×86=7(85+1)=7×85+7×1\begin{aligned} 7 \times 86 &= 7(85 + 1) \\ &= 7 \times 85 + 7 \times 1 \end{aligned}7×86​=7(85+1)=7×85+7×1​

    So 7×857 \times 857×85 is 777 less than 602602602.

    602−7=595602 - 7 = 595602−7=595

    Check directly: 7×80=5607 \times 80 = 5607×80=560 and 7×5=357 \times 5 = 357×5=35, so 7×85=5957 \times 85 = 5957×85=595, which is 777 less than 602602602.

    The claim is incorrect.

    The decrease is seven, matching the seven single items removed.

    Answer

    Incorrect; the product decreases by 777.

    Key idea

    Reducing each equal group by one reduces the total by the number of groups.

  9. Problem 9 Boxes on shelves

    There are 222 shelves, each holding 666 boxes, with 777 balls in each box. A student writes the total as 2(6×7)2(6 \times 7)2(6×7), then rewrites it as (2×6)(2×7)(2 \times 6)(2 \times 7)(2×6)(2×7), saying that the 222 must multiply everything inside the parentheses. Is this rewrite valid? Explain, and give the correct total number of balls.

    1. Hint 1

      Distinguish groups within groups from two amounts being added.

    2. Hint 2

      Count the balls on one shelf, then multiply by the number of shelves.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare how many times the factor 222 appears before and after the rewrite.

    Answer

    The rewrite is invalid; 848484 balls.

    Full solution

    The parentheses contain a product, so the distributive rule for a sum or difference does not apply.

    One shelf has 6×7=426 \times 7 = 426×7=42 balls.

    2(6×7)=2×42=84\begin{aligned} 2(6 \times 7) &= 2 \times 42 \\ &= 84 \end{aligned}2(6×7)​=2×42=84​

    The proposed rewrite introduces a second factor of 222.

    Its value is different.

    (2×6)(2×7)=12×14=168\begin{aligned} (2 \times 6)(2 \times 7) &= 12 \times 14 \\ &= 168 \end{aligned}(2×6)(2×7)​=12×14=168​

    Check the correct count another way: 222 shelves of 666 boxes make 121212 boxes, and 12×7=8412 \times 7 = 8412×7=84 balls.

    Regrouping the original three factors counts each shelf, box and ball once.

    Answer

    The rewrite is invalid; 848484 balls.

    Key idea

    A product can be regrouped, but multiplying both inner factors by the outside factor introduces an extra copy of that factor.

  10. Problem 10 Two factoring decisions

    To factor 40+7240 + 7240+72, Mira writes 4(10+18)4(10 + 18)4(10+18) and Eli writes 8(5+9)8(5 + 9)8(5+9). Are both expressions equal to the original sum, and which one has the greatest common factor outside the parentheses? Justify your decisions.

    1. Hint 1

      A factoring can preserve the value without taking out the greatest common factor.

    2. Hint 2

      Distribute each outside factor back to check the two original terms.

    3. Hint 3

      Inspect the terms still inside each pair of parentheses for a common factor greater than one.

    Answer

    Both equal 40+7240 + 7240+72; Eli's expression has the greatest common factor, 888, outside.

    Full solution

    Mira's outside factor gives 4×10=404 \times 10 = 404×10=40 and

    4×18=72.4 \times 18 = 72.4×18=72.

    Eli's gives 8×5=408 \times 5 = 408×5=40 and

    8×9=72.8 \times 9 = 72.8×9=72.

    Both expressions therefore reproduce the original terms.

    Mira has left a factor of 222 in both inside terms.

    Taking it out combines it with the outside 444.

    4(10+18)=4×[2(5+9)]=8(5+9)\begin{aligned} 4(10 + 18) &= 4 \times [2(5 + 9)] \\ &= 8(5 + 9) \end{aligned}4(10+18)​=4×[2(5+9)]=8(5+9)​

    The terms 555 and 999 share no factor greater than 111: the only factor of 555 above 111 is 555, and it does not divide 999.

    Eli's form is therefore complete, with greatest common factor 888 outside.

    Check the values directly: 40+72=11240 + 72 = 11240+72=112, 4×28=1124 \times 28 = 1124×28=112, and

    8×14=112.8 \times 14 = 112.8×14=112.

    Answer

    Both equal 40+7240 + 7240+72; Eli's expression has the greatest common factor, 888, outside.

    Key idea

    A factored sum is complete when its inside terms share no factor greater than one.
