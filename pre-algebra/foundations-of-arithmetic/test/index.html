    The hundreds column changes, its 666 becoming a 999, and the corrected figure is 52,90852{,}90852,908.

    52,608=50,000+2,000+600+852{,}608 = 50{,}000 + 2{,}000 + 600 + 852,608=50,000+2,000+600+8, and 600+500=1,100600 + 500 = 1{,}100600+500=1,100, so the figure becomes 53,10853{,}10853,108. The thousands digit and the hundreds digit both change.

    Every other column is left exactly as it was, because nothing was added to any of them.

    Part B

    Expanded form is the sum of what each digit contributes, so every column gets its own term and the correction has an obvious home:

    52,608=50,000+2,000+600+8,600+500=1,100.52{,}608 = 50{,}000 + 2{,}000 + 600 + 8, \qquad 600 + 500 = 1{,}100.52,608=50,000+2,000+600+8,600+500=1,100.

    50,000+2,000+1,100+8=50,000+3,000+100+8=53,108.50{,}000 + 2{,}000 + 1{,}100 + 8 = 50{,}000 + 3{,}000 + 100 + 8 = 53{,}108.50,000+2,000+1,100+8=50,000+3,000+100+8=53,108.

    Part C

    Nothing about the correction is large; what matters is whether the column it lands in can hold the result. A column holds a single digit, so it tops out at nine of its own units. The first correction left six hundreds as nine hundreds and fitted:

    6+3=9.6 + 3 = 9.6+3=9.

    10×100=1,000,10 \times 100 = 1{,}000,10×100=1,000,

That is the ten-times relationship seen from the inside: each column is worth ten of the column on its right, so ten units of any column are one unit of its left-hand neighbour, and an overflow always lands exactly one column over.
