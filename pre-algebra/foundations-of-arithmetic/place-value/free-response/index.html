    1. The same digit in two different columns . Foundational, 8 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 8 pts

    A digit written on a page is only a symbol. What it is worth inside a numeral is decided by the column it occupies, so one symbol can contribute two very different amounts in the same number.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Take the number 2,7272{,}7272,727. Name the place of each of its four digits, working from left to right, and give the value that each digit contributes.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 2 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Write the four-digit whole number in which the digit 333 contributes 3,0003{,}0003,000, the digit 555 contributes 500500500, the digit 999 contributes 999, and the tens column contributes nothing at all.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      A student writes about the number 9,1409{,}1409,140: "The place of the digit 111 is 111, and its value is the hundreds." Identify the mistake, state that digit's place and its value correctly, and explain in a sentence or two how a digit's place differs from its value.

      Find and correct the error Say which line first goes wrong, why it is wrong, and then do it correctly. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Every digit contributes the digit itself multiplied by the value of the column it occupies, so begin by counting columns from the right: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      Each contribution you are given points at one column. Ask which column makes a 555 worth five hundred, and then ask what has to be written in a column that contributes nothing.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      One of the student's two answers is the name of a column and the other is an amount. Sort out which is which before deciding where each one belongs.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    The 222 in the thousands place is worth 2,0002{,}0002,000, the 777 in the hundreds place 700700700, the 222 in the tens place 202020, and the 777 in the ones place 777.

    Part B

    3,5093{,}5093,509.

    Part C

    The place of the digit 111 is the hundreds and its value is 100100100. The student named the digit itself where a place belongs, and named a column where an amount belongs, so exchanging the two answers would still leave the value wrong.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Count the columns from the right. The final 777 sits in the ones place, the 222 before it in the tens place, the next 777 in the hundreds place, and the leading 222 in the thousands place.

    A digit's value is the digit multiplied by the value of its column:

    2×1,000=2,000,7×100=700,2×10=20,7×1=7.2 \times 1{,}000 = 2{,}000, \qquad 7 \times 100 = 700, \qquad 2 \times 10 = 20, \qquad 7 \times 1 = 7.2×1,000=2,000,7×100=700,2×10=20,7×1=7.

    The two 777s are the same symbol, yet one contributes 700700700 and the other 777. Nothing about them differs except the column they stand in.

    Part B

    Each stated contribution names a column, because a contribution is a digit multiplied by that column's place value:

    3,000=3×1,000,500=5×100,9=9×1.3{,}000 = 3 \times 1{,}000, \qquad 500 = 5 \times 100, \qquad 9 = 9 \times 1.3,000=3×1,000,500=5×100,9=9×1.

    So the 333 goes in the thousands column, the 555 in the hundreds column and the 999 in the ones column. The tens column contributes nothing, but a column that contributes nothing still has to be written down, and 000 is what holds it open. Reading the four columns off in order and checking the contributions back,

    3,509=3,000+500+0+9.3{,}509 = 3{,}000 + 500 + 0 + 9.3,509=3,000+500+0+9.

    Leave that 000 out and the numeral reads 359359359, in which the 333 contributes 300300300 instead of 3,0003{,}0003,000 and the 555 contributes 505050 instead of 500500500.

    Part C

    The student has answered each question with the other kind of thing. A place is the name of a column (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands); a value is an amount, namely the digit multiplied by that column's place value.

    Count the columns of 9,1409{,}1409,140 from the right: 000 ones, 444 tens, 111 hundred, 999 thousands. So the digit 111 is in the hundreds place, and its value is

    1×100=100.1 \times 100 = 100.1×100=100.

    The student's "111" is the digit itself, not a place, and "the hundreds" is the name of a column, not an amount. It is worth noticing that simply exchanging the two answers does not repair the statement: that would give the place as the hundreds, which is right, but it would leave the value as 111, and the value is 100100100.

    In one line

    In 2,7272{,}7272,727 the digits are worth 2,0002{,}0002,000, 700700700, 202020 and 777 from left to right; the number described in part B is 3,5093{,}5093,509; and in 9,1409{,}1409,140 the digit 111 is in the hundreds place with value 100100100, where the student had named the digit itself as the place and a column name as the value.

    2. Expanded form, in both directions . Foundational, 9 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Foundational 9 pts

    Expanded form writes a whole number as the sum of what its digits contribute, which makes every column visible at once. Read in reverse, the same sum rebuilds the numeral from its contributions.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Write 9,0609{,}0609,060 in expanded form twice: first as a sum of the digit values, and then with every column shown as a digit multiplied by its place value.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      A number is described as (3×10,000)+(8×100)+(2×1)(3 \times 10{,}000) + (8 \times 100) + (2 \times 1)(3×10,000)+(8×100)+(2×1). Write that number in standard form, with digits and a comma.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      A student claims: "The zeros in 9,0609{,}0609,060 contribute nothing to the expanded sum, so 9,0609{,}0609,060 and 969696 are two ways of writing the same number." Decide whether the claim holds, and justify your decision by comparing the expanded form of each of the two numbers.

      Justify your claim State the claim, then give the reason it has to be true. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Expanded form is a sum of contributions, one term for every column, and each term is that column's digit multiplied by that column's place value.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      Every product in the description names a column and the digit that sits there. A column the description never mentions still needs something written in it.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Write both numerals out as sums of digit values and set the terms side by side. Then ask what happens to the OTHER digits when a zero is removed from a numeral.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    9,060=9,000+609{,}060 = 9{,}000 + 609,060=9,000+60, which written column by column is (9×1,000)+(0×100)+(6×10)+(0×1)(9 \times 1{,}000) + (0 \times 100) + (6 \times 10) + (0 \times 1)(9×1,000)+(0×100)+(6×10)+(0×1).

    Part B

    30,80230{,}80230,802.

    Part C

    The claim does not hold. The two numerals have different expanded forms, 9,000+609{,}000 + 609,000+60 against 90+690 + 690+6, so their digits are not standing in the same columns.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    There are four digits, so there are four columns. From the right: 000 ones, 666 tens, 000 hundreds, 999 thousands. Adding only the contributions that are not zero,

    9,060=9,000+60.9{,}060 = 9{,}000 + 60.9,060=9,000+60.

    Showing every column explicitly, zeros included,

    (9×1,000)+(0×100)+(6×10)+(0×1).(9 \times 1{,}000) + (0 \times 100) + (6 \times 10) + (0 \times 1).(9×1,000)+(0×100)+(6×10)+(0×1).

    The second form is the one that makes the zeros visible. They add nothing to the sum, and they still occupy columns.

    Part B

    Each product names one column together with the digit that belongs in it: 3×10,0003 \times 10{,}0003×10,000 puts a 333 in the ten-thousands column, 8×1008 \times 1008×100 puts an 888 in the hundreds column, and 2×12 \times 12×1 puts a 222 in the ones column.

    The thousands and the tens are never mentioned, so nothing is contributed there, and "nothing" is written 000:

    30,802=30,000+800+2.30{,}802 = 30{,}000 + 800 + 2.30,802=30,000+800+2.

    Closing the two gaps instead would give 382382382, a completely different number: the 333 drops from the ten-thousands column to the hundreds column and the 888 drops from the hundreds column to the tens column, leaving only the 222 where the description placed it.

    Part C

    Write each numeral as the sum of its digit values:

    9,060=9,000+60,96=90+6.9{,}060 = 9{,}000 + 60, \qquad 96 = 90 + 6.9,060=9,000+60,96=90+6.

    The two sums are not equal, so the two numerals do not name one number.

    The student's premise is correct and the conclusion does not follow from it. A zero digit really does contribute nothing to the sum, but contributing is not its only job: it also holds its column, and that fixes where every digit to its left lands. Delete the two zeros from 9,0609{,}0609,060 and the 999 falls from the thousands column to the tens column and the 666 falls from the tens column to the ones column, so their contributions collapse from 9,0009{,}0009,000 and 606060 to 909090 and 666.

    In one line

    9,060=9,000+60=(9×1,000)+(0×100)+(6×10)+(0×1)9{,}060 = 9{,}000 + 60 = (9 \times 1{,}000) + (0 \times 100) + (6 \times 10) + (0 \times 1)9,060=9,000+60=(9×1,000)+(0×100)+(6×10)+(0×1); the number described in part B is 30,80230{,}80230,802; and 9,0609{,}0609,060 and 969696 are different numbers, since their expanded forms put the same digits in different columns.

    Another way: Read the numeral off a place-value chart

    Draw one box per digit and label the boxes from the right (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands), then drop the digits in one at a time, starting from the right-hand end of the numeral. Once the chart is filled, the expanded form is a matter of reading rather than of counting:

    9,060=9,000+60.9{,}060 = 9{,}000 + 60.9,060=9,000+60.

    When it is worth it When a numeral carries several zeros, or is long enough that counting columns in your head starts to go wrong. It is also the fastest way to check a numeral you have just built from a description, as in part B.

    3. Where the ten in base ten comes from . Reasoning, 11 points. Question 3 of 5.

    Reasoning 11 pts

    The columns of our number system are built by bundling: a fixed number of units in one column makes a single unit of the column to its left. The storage system below is packed on that same principle, so it can be used to see what one column of a numeral is actually made of.

    1. A
      Part A.

      A game is stored as single chips, as stacks of ten chips, and as crates of ten stacks. How many chips does one crate hold, and how many chips are represented by 222 crates, 666 stacks and 555 chips?

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      The warehouse adds one more size to the same system: ten crates make one pallet. How many chips does one pallet hold, and how many chips are 333 pallets, 444 crates and 222 stacks?

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Using the way these sizes are built, explain why every place in our base-ten numerals is worth exactly ten times the place to its right. Then say where the ten in that rule comes from.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 5 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Work out what a single unit of a column is actually made of. In each of these systems, one unit of a column is one full bundle of the units in the column to its right.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      Going up one size bundles the previous size again, so the amounts grow by repeated multiplication and not by repeated addition.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask what has to be true of the ratio between two neighbouring columns when one of them is built as a bundle of the other. Then ask which part of that description the number ten actually comes from.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    One crate holds 100100100 chips, and 222 crates, 666 stacks and 555 chips represent 265265265 chips.

    Part B

    One pallet holds 1,0001{,}0001,000 chips, and 333 pallets, 444 crates and 222 stacks are 3,4203{,}4203,420 chips.

    Part C

    Each place holds one bundle of ten units of the place to its right, so one unit of the left-hand place is ten of the right-hand units; the ten comes from the size of the bundle we count in, which is what the name base ten records.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    A stack is ten chips and a crate is ten stacks, so a crate is ten groups of ten chips:

    10×10=100.10 \times 10 = 100.10×10=100.

    One crate therefore holds 100100100 chips, which is exactly what a hundreds column is worth. Now take each size on its own:

    2×100=200,6×10=60,5×1=5.2 \times 100 = 200, \qquad 6 \times 10 = 60, \qquad 5 \times 1 = 5.2×100=200,6×10=60,5×1=5.

    Those three contributions come to 265265265 chips. Notice that the digits of 265265265 are precisely the counts of crates, stacks and chips: the packing and the numeral are the same idea written two ways.

    Part B

    A crate is 100100100 chips and a pallet is ten crates, so a pallet is ten groups of one hundred chips:

    10×100=1,000.10 \times 100 = 1{,}000.10×100=1,000.

    One pallet therefore holds 1,0001{,}0001,000 chips, which is exactly what a thousands column is worth. Now take each size on its own:

    3×1,000=3,000,4×100=400,2×10=20.3 \times 1{,}000 = 3{,}000, \qquad 4 \times 100 = 400, \qquad 2 \times 10 = 20.3×1,000=3,000,4×100=400,2×10=20.

    Those contributions come to 3,4203{,}4203,420 chips, and the digits of 3,4203{,}4203,420 are once again the counts of pallets, crates, stacks and single chips.

    Part C

    Look at what one unit of a place is actually made of. The columns are built by bundling, so one ten is ten ones, one hundred is ten tens, and one thousand is ten hundreds. A single unit of any column is therefore a bundle of ten units of the column on its right, which is to say its value is ten times as much:

    10=10×1,100=10×10,1,000=10×100.10 = 10 \times 1, \qquad 100 = 10 \times 10, \qquad 1{,}000 = 10 \times 100.10=10×1,100=10×10,1,000=10×100.

    That reason did not depend on which two columns we picked, so it holds at every pair of neighbouring columns. Chips, stacks, crates and pallets are those same four columns packed in a warehouse.

    The ten itself comes from the size of the bundle, not from the digits. We bundle in tens, so each column ends up worth ten of the column to its right, and that is exactly what the name base ten records.

    In one line

    One crate holds 100100100 chips and the collection is 265265265 chips; one pallet holds 1,0001{,}0001,000 chips and 333 pallets, 444 crates and 222 stacks are 3,4203{,}4203,420 chips; and because each place is built as one bundle of ten of the units in the place to its right, every place is exactly ten times that place, with the ten coming from the size of the bundle.

    Rubric

    Try a similar problem (Optional)

    Same idea, different numbers. Work it on paper, then check yourself the same way.

    Here is the same bundling idea with a different bundle size, to test where the ten really comes from. A counting frame bundles in threes: three beads make one row, three rows make one card, and three cards make one case. How many beads is each of those four sizes worth? Then say what one step to the right would divide by, if numbers were written positionally on that frame.

    The answer

    The four sizes are worth 111, 333, 999 and 272727 beads, and one step to the right would divide by 333.

    Each size is a bundle of three of the size below, so multiply by three at every step. A bead is 111 and a row is 333 beads. A card is three rows:

    3×3=9.3 \times 3 = 9.3×3=9.

    A case is three cards:

    9×3=27.9 \times 3 = 27.9×3=27.

    So the sizes are worth 111, 333, 999 and 272727 beads. Written positionally, each column would be three times the column on its right, so a step to the right would undo one bundle of three and divide by 333, just as a step to the right divides by 101010 in our own numerals.

    4. Large figures in words and in digits . Application, 10 points. Question 4 of 5.

    Application 10 pts

    Turning a large number from words into digits, and back again, is a place-value job. The digits of a large whole number are grouped into periods of three, counting from the right, and the commas mark where one period ends and the next begins.

    1. A
      Part A.

      A census reports a country's population in words as four hundred six million, ninety thousand, twenty-five. Write that figure in digits, with the commas in place.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      A water authority reports a reservoir's capacity as 18,304,06018{,}304{,}06018,304,060 liters. Write that figure in words, as it would be read aloud, and name the period that the digits 304304304 occupy.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Explain why the digits of a large whole number are grouped into threes counting from the right rather than from the left, and say what would go wrong in reading 18,304,06018{,}304{,}06018,304,060 if the groups were counted from the left instead.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Work in blocks, marked off three digits at a time from the right. Each block is read as an ordinary number of at most three digits and is then given the name of its period: ones, thousands or millions.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      Write the three blocks separately before joining them up, and remember that a block after the leading one still has to fill three digit positions even when the words name no hundreds for it.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask which single column is in the same position no matter how long a numeral is. Then try counting the groups of the given figure from the other end and see what is left over.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    406,090,025406{,}090{,}025406,090,025.

    Part B

    Eighteen million, three hundred four thousand, sixty liters. The digits 304304304 occupy the thousands period.

    Part C

    The place values are built outward from the ones place, so the periods have to be counted from the right; counting from the left instead groups the figure as 183183183, 040040040 and 606060, which leaves a two-digit final group and would announce one hundred eighty-three million.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Take the periods one at a time. The words name 406406406 in the millions period, 909090 in the thousands period and 252525 in the ones period.

    Every period except the leading one must be filled out to three digit positions, so 909090 is written 090090090 and 252525 is written 025025025:

    406,090,025.406{,}090{,}025.406,090,025.

    Without those placeholder zeros the digits would run together as 4,069,0254{,}069{,}0254,069,025, which is a far smaller number: the 444 would sit in the millions column instead of the hundred-millions column.

    Part B

    The commas have already marked the periods off in threes from the right: 181818 in the millions period, 304304304 in the thousands period and 060060060 in the ones period.

    18,304,060=18,000,000+304,000+60.18{,}304{,}060 = 18{,}000{,}000 + 304{,}000 + 60.18,304,060=18,000,000+304,000+60.

    Read each period as an ordinary number of at most three digits followed by its period name, and read the last period with no name at all: eighteen million, three hundred four thousand, sixty. The unit goes on the end, so the capacity is eighteen million, three hundred four thousand, sixty liters.

    The middle group is the one attached to the word thousand, so 304304304 occupies the thousands period, and its 333 is a hundred-thousands digit.

    Part C

    The ones place is the anchor. Place values are built outward from it, one bundle of ten at a time, so the rightmost column is the only one whose position is fixed before you know how long the numeral is. Counting periods from the right therefore counts from the one column that every number has, whatever its length. Counting from the left has nothing fixed to start from, because the leading column changes as soon as the number gets longer.

    On the given figure, counting from the left produces the groups 183183183, 040040040 and 606060:

    183,040,60183{,}040{,}60183,040,60

    whose last group holds two digits instead of three, which is the first sign that the grouping is wrong. Read as periods, those groups would announce one hundred eighty-three million, when the figure contains only eighteen million, because the groups no longer line up with the columns the digits actually occupy.

    In one line

    The census figure is 406,090,025406{,}090{,}025406,090,025; the capacity reads as eighteen million, three hundred four thousand, sixty liters, with 304304304 in the thousands period; and the groups are counted from the right because the place values grow outward from the ones place, so counting from the left leaves a short final group and renames every period.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Digit count comes first, since none of these numerals is padded with leading zeros. 7,4817{,}4817,481 has four digits and the other three have five, so it is the smallest of the four: every five-digit number is at least 10,00010{,}00010,000, and 7,4817{,}4817,481 is below that.

All three begin with 777 and then 000, so the ten-thousands and the thousands columns tie and the hundreds column decides first: 111 in 70,14870{,}14870,148 against 444 in each of the others, so 70,14870{,}14870,148 is the least of the three.     Part B

    The two numerals have the same number of digits, so compare from the left. The thousands column is the first one that differs: 444 against 555. Split each number at that column:

    4,963=4,000+963,5,008=5,000+8.4{,}963 = 4{,}000 + 963, \qquad 5{,}008 = 5{,}000 + 8.4,963=4,000+963,5,008=5,000+8.

    900+90+9=999,900 + 90 + 9 = 999,900+90+9=999,

    4,963<5,008.4{,}963 < 5{,}008.4,963<5,008.

    Part C

    Take the pair 999999 and 100100100, and add the digits of each:

    9+9=18,1+0+0=1.9 + 9 = 18, \qquad 1 + 0 + 0 = 1.9+9=18,1+0+0=1.

    The shortcut compares 181818 with 111 and announces that 999999 is the larger number. But 100100100 has three digits and 999999 has two, so 100100100 is larger, and one pair like this is enough to retire the shortcut.

    What the shortcut throws away is the link between each digit and the column it stands in, and that link is what fixes how much the digit contributes. A digit sum treats a 999 in the tens column and a 999 in the ones column as the same contribution, when the first is worth ten times the second.
