  1. Problem 1 Finding a contribution

    The digit 666 appears twice in 681,462681{,}462681,462. What does each 666 contribute to the number?

    1. Hint 1

      A digit's contribution is the digit multiplied by the value of its place.

    2. Hint 2

      Name the place of each 666 by counting places from the right.

    Answer

    The left 666 contributes 600,000600{,}000600,000; the right 666 contributes 606060.

    Full solution

    From the right, the places are ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten-thousands and hundred-thousands.

    The left 666 occupies the hundred-thousands place.

    6×100,000=600,0006 \times 100{,}000 = 600{,}0006×100,000=600,000

    The right 666 occupies the tens place.

    6×10=606 \times 10 = 606×10=60

    The same digit contributes different amounts because it sits in different places.

    Answer

    The left 666 contributes 600,000600{,}000600,000; the right 666 contributes 606060.

    Key idea

    A digit's contribution equals the digit multiplied by its place value.

  2. Problem 2 Writing a large count

    Write three billion, fourteen as a numeral with commas separating the periods.

    1. Hint 1

      The periods, from right to left, are ones, thousands, millions and billions.

    2. Hint 2

      Keep three digits for every period after the leading period, even when an entire period has no nonzero digits.

    Answer

    3,000,000,0143{,}000{,}000{,}0143,000,000,014.

    Full solution

    The billions period is 333.

    The words name no millions and no thousands, so the millions and thousands periods are each written as 000000000.

    Fourteen fills the ones period as 014014014.

    Writing the periods in order gives

    3,000,000,0143{,}000{,}000{,}0143,000,000,014

    The nonzero periods read as three billion and fourteen, matching the words.

    Answer

    3,000,000,0143{,}000{,}000{,}0143,000,000,014.

    Key idea

    Each period after the leading period uses three digits, including zeros for empty places.

  3. Problem 3 Comparing two counts

    Compare 605,090605{,}090605,090 and 650,009650{,}009650,009 by writing a true statement with <<<, >>> or ===.

    1. Hint 1

      For whole numbers with the same number of digits, compare places from left to right.

    2. Hint 2

      The hundred-thousands digits match; inspect the next place before looking at the smaller places.

    Answer

    605,090<650,009605{,}090 < 650{,}009605,090<650,009.

    Full solution

    Both numbers have six digits and have 666 in the hundred-thousands place.

    The first difference is in the ten-thousands place: the first number has 000, and the second has 555.

    The smaller digit in that first differing place determines the comparison.

    605,090<650,009605{,}090 < 650{,}009605,090<650,009

    The digits farther right do not change that decision.

    Answer

    605,090<650,009605{,}090 < 650{,}009605,090<650,009.

    Key idea

    The first differing place from the left decides the order of two whole numbers with the same digit count.

  4. Problem 4 Restoring the separators

    A file records a whole-number count as 310060203100602031006020, with no commas. Insert commas between its periods, then write the number in words.

    1. Hint 1

      Period boundaries are found by counting groups of three digits from the right.

    2. Hint 2

      Name the rightmost group as ones and the next group as thousands before reading the leading group.

    Answer

    31,006,02031{,}006{,}02031,006,020; thirty-one million, six thousand, twenty.

    Full solution

    Starting at the right, the first group is 020020020, the next is 006006006, and the remaining leading group is 313131.

    They are the ones, thousands and millions periods, respectively.

    The numeral with separators is

    31,006,02031{,}006{,}02031,006,020

    Read the nonzero amounts in those periods as thirty-one million, six thousand, twenty.

    Removing the commas reproduces the original digit sequence.

    Answer

    31,006,02031{,}006{,}02031,006,020; thirty-one million, six thousand, twenty.

    Key idea

    Grouping digits into periods of three from the right connects a large numeral to its spoken name.

  5. Problem 5 Rebundling a stockroom count

    A stockroom has 333 sealed cartons of 1,0001{,}0001,000 tokens each, 171717 packets of 100100100 tokens each, 444 strips of 101010 tokens each, and 666 loose tokens. Write the total number of tokens as a numeral and in expanded form.

    1. Hint 1

      Ten groups of one place value can be exchanged for one group of the next larger place value.

    2. Hint 2

      Exchange ten of the hundred-token packets for one thousand-token group, then count how many groups remain at each place.

    Answer

    4,7464{,}7464,746 tokens; expanded form: 4,000+700+40+64{,}000 + 700 + 40 + 64,000+700+40+6, or (4×1,000)+(7×100)+(4×10)+(6×1)(4 \times 1{,}000) + (7 \times 100) + (4 \times 10) + (6 \times 1)(4×1,000)+(7×100)+(4×10)+(6×1).

    Full solution

    Ten of the 171717 hundred-token packets make one thousand-token group.

    10×100=1,00010 \times 100 = 1{,}00010×100=1,000

    This gives 444 thousands in total and leaves 777 hundreds, along with 444 tens and 666 ones.

    Add the contributions of the four places.

    4,746=4,000+700+40+64{,}746 = 4{,}000 + 700 + 40 + 64,746=4,000+700+40+6

    The stockroom has 4,7464{,}7464,746 tokens.

    Check the rebundling: the extra thousand and the remaining 777 hundreds account for all 171717 original packets.

    The strips and loose tokens have not changed.

    Answer

    4,7464{,}7464,746 tokens; expanded form: 4,000+700+40+64{,}000 + 700 + 40 + 64,000+700+40+6, or (4×1,000)+(7×100)+(4×10)+(6×1)(4 \times 1{,}000) + (7 \times 100) + (4 \times 10) + (6 \times 1)(4×1,000)+(7×100)+(4×10)+(6×1).

    Key idea

    Rebundling ten units of one place as one unit of the next place preserves the total.

  6. Problem 6 Two reported totals

    One counter reports 6,040,0806{,}040{,}0806,040,080 visits. Another report gives six million, four hundred thousand, eighty visits. Write the second count as a numeral, decide which report gives the larger count, and name the first place from the left where the counts differ.

    1. Hint 1

      Put both counts in the same written form before comparing their places.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the spoken count in millions, thousands and ones periods, giving every period after the first exactly three digits.

    3. Hint 3

      After comparing the millions digits, inspect the leftmost digit in the thousands period.

    Answer

    Second count: 6,400,0806{,}400{,}0806,400,080 visits. The second report is larger; the first difference is in the hundred-thousands place.

    Full solution

    The spoken count has millions period 666, thousands period 400400400, and ones period 080080080.

    Its numeral is 6,400,0806{,}400{,}0806,400,080.

    The millions digits match.

    The next place, hundred-thousands, contains 000 in the first count and 444 in the second.

    6,040,080<6,400,0806{,}040{,}080 < 6{,}400{,}0806,040,080<6,400,080

    The second report gives the larger count.

    Reading its periods back gives six million, four hundred thousand, eighty, as required.

    Answer

    Second count: 6,400,0806{,}400{,}0806,400,080 visits. The second report is larger; the first difference is in the hundred-thousands place.

    Key idea

    Translate number words into place-value periods before comparing them with a numeral.

  7. Problem 7 An incomplete counter reading

    A six-digit counter begins with the digits 284284284, but its last three digits are unreadable. What are the smallest and largest possible readings? Could its reading be greater than 285,000285{,}000285,000? Explain using place value.

    1. Hint 1

      The known digits fix the higher places, while each unreadable digit can range from 000 to 999.

    2. Hint 2

      Fill the last three places with the smallest digits for one endpoint and the largest digits for the other.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare the largest possible reading with 285,000285{,}000285,000, starting at the left.

    Answer

    Smallest: 284,000284{,}000284,000. Largest: 284,999284{,}999284,999. A reading greater than 285,000285{,}000285,000 is not possible.

    Full solution

    The first three digits contribute 284,000284{,}000284,000.

    The last three places contribute at least 000 and at most 999999999.

    284,000+0=284,000284,000+999=284,999\begin{aligned} 284{,}000 + 0 &= 284{,}000 \\ 284{,}000 + 999 &= 284{,}999 \end{aligned}284,000+0284,000+999​=284,000=284,999​

    Compare the largest reading with 285,000285{,}000285,000.

    Their first two digits match, and the thousands digits are 444 and 555.

    284,999<285,000284{,}999 < 285{,}000284,999<285,000

    So no possible reading is greater than 285,000285{,}000285,000.

    Both endpoints have six digits and begin with 284284284, as required.

    Answer

    Smallest: 284,000284{,}000284,000. Largest: 284,999284{,}999284,999. A reading greater than 285,000285{,}000285,000 is not possible.

    Key idea

    Fixed leading digits limit how much the unknown lower places can change a whole number.

  8. Problem 8 An empty middle period

    A student reads 8,000,0458{,}000{,}0458,000,045 as eight thousand, forty-five, saying that the middle group can be skipped since it contains only zeros. Is the reading correct? Give the correct reading and explain the role of the middle group.

    1. Hint 1

      A period with no spoken amount still occupies three places in the written numeral.

    2. Hint 2

      Name the periods from the right and locate the period containing the 888.

    Answer

    No; eight million, forty-five. The middle group marks an empty thousands period.

    Full solution

    From the right, 045045045 is the ones period, 000000000 is the thousands period, and 888 is the millions period.

    The number therefore reads as eight million, forty-five.

    8,000,045=8,000,000+458{,}000{,}045 = 8{,}000{,}000 + 458,000,045=8,000,000+45

    The empty thousands period keeps the 888 in the millions period.

    It has no amount to say aloud, but its positions still count.

    Deleting its three digits would produce 8,0458{,}0458,045, which does read as eight thousand, forty-five.

    8,045=8,000+458{,}045 = 8{,}000 + 458,045=8,000+45

    That is a different number.

    Answer

    No; eight million, forty-five. The middle group marks an empty thousands period.

    Key idea

    An empty period contributes no separate amount but preserves the positions of higher periods.

  9. Problem 9 Bundles, trays and boxes

    A collector packs cards in bundles of ten cards. Ten bundles fill a tray, and ten trays fill a box. A student says a box holds 202020 times as many cards as a bundle, because a box is two packing steps above a bundle and 10+10=2010 + 10 = 2010+10=20. Is the student correct? State how many cards a bundle and a full box hold, and explain how their sizes compare.

    1. Hint 1

      Work out what one packing step does to the number of cards, then do it again for the second step.

    2. Hint 2

      Count the cards in a full tray, then in a full box, and compare the box with a single bundle.

    Answer

    No. A bundle holds 101010 cards; a full box holds 1,0001{,}0001,000 cards. A box holds 100100100 times as many cards as a bundle.

    Full solution

    A tray holds ten bundles of ten cards.

    10×10=10010 \times 10 = 10010×10=100

    A full tray holds 100100100 cards.

    A box holds ten of those trays.

    10×100=1,00010 \times 100 = 1{,}00010×100=1,000

    A full box holds 1,0001{,}0001,000 cards.

    A bundle holds 101010 cards, and 1,0001{,}0001,000 cards make 100100100 bundles.

    100×10=1,000100 \times 10 = 1{,}000100×10=1,000

    So a box holds 100100100 times as many cards as a bundle, not 202020 times.

    Each packing step multiplies the count by ten, so two steps multiply it by ten twice.

    The student added the two factors of ten instead of multiplying them.

    Answer

    No. A bundle holds 101010 cards; a full box holds 1,0001{,}0001,000 cards. A box holds 100100100 times as many cards as a bundle.

    Key idea

    Two steps of bundling in tens multiply a size by ten twice, which is 100100100 times, not 202020 times.

  10. Problem 10 Does the final digit decide

    Two whole numbers each have six digits and begin with 636363. One ends in 999, and the other ends in 000. Ari says the number ending in 999 must be greater. Is that guaranteed? If it is, explain why; if it is not, give a pair of numbers meeting the conditions for which Ari is wrong, and justify their comparison.

    1. Hint 1

      The shared leading digits and the specified final digits still leave other places available.

    2. Hint 2

      Try making the thousands digits different, then fill unused places with zeros.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare your numbers from the left; the first differing place decides whether the ones digits matter.

    Answer

    No; for example, 630,009<631,000630{,}009 < 631{,}000630,009<631,000.

    Full solution

    Choose 630,009630{,}009630,009 for the number ending in 999, and 631,000631{,}000631,000 for the number ending in 000.

    Both have six digits and begin with 636363, so they meet every condition.

    The hundred-thousands and ten-thousands places match.

    In the thousands place, the number ending in 999 has 000, while the other has 111.

    630,009<631,000630{,}009 < 631{,}000630,009<631,000

    The lower places do not overturn this difference.

    This pair makes the number ending in 999 smaller, so Ari's claim is not guaranteed.

    Other pairs can work as counterexamples too.

    Answer

    No; for example, 630,009<631,000630{,}009 < 631{,}000630,009<631,000.

    Key idea

    A larger ones digit does not determine the larger whole number when a higher place differs.
