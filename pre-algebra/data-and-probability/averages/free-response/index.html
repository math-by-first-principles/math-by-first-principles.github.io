  1. Problem 1 The instrument log

    An instrument log contains 888 readings whose sum is −12-12−12. What is the mean of the readings?

    1. Hint 1

      The mean is the amount each reading would have if the total were shared equally.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide the given sum by the number of readings, keeping the sign of the sum.

    Answer

    −1.5-1.5−1.5, or −32-\tfrac32−23​.

    Full solution

    The sum and the number of readings are already known, so divide the sum by 888.

    mean=−128\text{mean}=\frac{-12}{8}mean=8−12​ mean=−1.5\text{mean}=-1.5mean=−1.5

    Replacing all eight readings by −1.5-1.5−1.5 gives the original total:

    8(−1.5)=−128(-1.5)=-128(−1.5)=−12

    Answer

    −1.5-1.5−1.5, or −32-\tfrac32−23​.

    Key idea

    The sum and the number of values are enough to determine the mean.

  2. Problem 2 The covered reading

    Six readings are 141414, 222222, 161616, 181818, 202020, and one covered value. The covered value is less than 141414. Find the median of the six readings.

    1. Hint 1

      Locate the middle positions after putting every reading in order.

    2. Hint 2

      The covered reading belongs before all five visible readings, so it still occupies one position.

    3. Hint 3

      For six readings, average the third and fourth readings in order.

    Answer

    171717.

    Full solution

    The covered reading is the smallest.

    The sorted order is the covered reading, 141414, 161616, 181818, 202020, 222222.

    The middle two readings are 161616 and 181818.

    Their mean gives the median.

    median=16+182\text{median}=\frac{16+18}{2}median=216+18​ median=17\text{median}=17median=17

    There are three readings below 171717 and three above it, regardless of the covered value.

    Answer

    171717.

    Key idea

    An unknown extreme value need not prevent you from locating the middle of an ordered list.

  3. Problem 3 The archive entry

    An archive entry gives the smallest value in a data set as −7-7−7 and the range as 191919. What is the largest value?

    1. Hint 1

      The range measures the distance from the smallest value to the largest.

    2. Hint 2

      Start at the smallest value and add the full distance to the largest.

    Answer

    121212.

    Full solution

    Add the range to the smallest value to reach the largest value.

    −7+19=12-7+19=12−7+19=12

    Check by subtracting the smallest from the largest.

    12−(−7)=1912-(-7)=1912−(−7)=19

    Answer

    121212.

    Key idea

    The largest value equals the smallest value plus the range.

  4. Problem 4 The water jars

    Five jars contain 181818, 161616, 202020, 161616, and 151515 milliliters of water. Transfer 222 milliliters from the third jar to the second jar without spilling any water. Find the mean, median, mode, and range of the five amounts after the transfer.

    1. Hint 1

      A transfer moves water between jars without changing the total, but it changes the amounts in two of them.

    2. Hint 2

      Record the five amounts that remain after the transfer before finding any summary.

    3. Hint 3

      Sort the final amounts for the median, count repeats for the mode, and compare the extremes for the range.

    Answer

    Mean 171717 milliliters; median 181818 milliliters; mode 181818 milliliters; range 333 milliliters.

    Full solution

    The third jar loses 222 milliliters and the second jar gains 222 milliliters.

    20−2=1820-2=1820−2=18 16+2=1816+2=1816+2=18

    The final amounts are 181818, 181818, 181818, 161616, and 151515 milliliters.

    Add the final amounts and divide by the five jars.

    18+18+18+16+15=8518+18+18+16+15=8518+18+18+16+15=85 mean=855\text{mean}=\frac{85}{5}mean=585​ mean=17\text{mean}=17mean=17

    The mean is 171717 milliliters.

    The sorted amounts are 151515, 161616, 181818, 181818, 181818 milliliters.

    The third value is 181818, so the median is 181818 milliliters.

    The value 181818 occurs three times and each other value once, so the mode is also 181818 milliliters.

    The range is

    18−15=318-15=318−15=3

    or 333 milliliters.

    The original amounts sort to 151515, 161616, 161616, 181818, 202020 milliliters and add to 858585 milliliters.

    The final total of 858585 milliliters matches the original total, as a transfer without spilling requires.

    Before the transfer the median and mode were both 161616 milliliters and the range was 555 milliliters, so only the mean stayed the same.

    Answer

    Mean 171717 milliliters; median 181818 milliliters; mode 181818 milliliters; range 333 milliliters.

    Key idea

    Update the affected entries before finding summaries of a changed data set.

  5. Problem 5 The decal batch

    A print shop has a batch of seven decals: comet, leaf, comet, wave, leaf, leaf, comet. It then prints one more leaf decal for the batch. Which of the mean, median, mode, and range can be found for the seven decals, and which for all eight in the finished batch? Give the value of each one that can be found.

    1. Hint 1

      Ask what each of the four summaries needs from the data before it can be found.

    2. Hint 2

      Decal designs have no numerical values and no least-to-greatest order, but they can still be counted.

    3. Hint 3

      Count each design before and after the new decal, and remember that a tie for the highest count, with another design counted less often, gives more than one mode.

    Answer

    Only the mode can be found. The seven decals have two modes, comet and leaf; the eight decals in the finished batch have one mode, leaf.

    Full solution

    The mean and range need numerical values, and the median needs a least-to-greatest order.

    Decal designs have neither.

    The mode compares how often each design occurs, so it is the only one of the four that can summarize these names.

    Among the seven decals there are three comets, three leaves, and one wave.

    Comet and leaf tie for the highest tally, and the wave has a lower tally, so the seven decals have two modes, comet and leaf.

    Printing one more leaf gives

    3+1=43+1=43+1=4

    leaves.

    The counts are now four leaves, three comets, and one wave, so leaf alone is the mode of the finished batch.

    The final counts account for the eight decals.

    4+3+1=84+3+1=84+3+1=8

    Answer

    Only the mode can be found. The seven decals have two modes, comet and leaf; the eight decals in the finished batch have one mode, leaf.

    Key idea

    The mode can summarize names by comparing how often they occur, and a tie for the highest count, while some other design is counted less often, gives more than one mode.

  6. Problem 6 The delivery report

    A delivery report covers 363636 deliveries. Their times have a mean of 282828 minutes and a median of 121212 minutes. A few deliveries included unusually long waits. A manager needs a typical delivery time and the total time for all 363636 deliveries. What should the two reported times be?

    1. Hint 1

      The two requested times serve different purposes: one describes a typical delivery, and the other accounts for every minute.

    2. Hint 2

      For the typical time, choose the center that resists the unusually long waits.

    3. Hint 3

      Recover a total from the number of values and their mean.

    Answer

    Typical time: 121212 minutes, using the median; total time: 100810081008 minutes.

    Full solution

    The mean of 282828 minutes is more than twice the median of 121212 minutes, a sign that the few unusually long waits pull the mean above the usual delivery times.

    The median depends on the middle position, so the reported median of 121212 minutes is the better typical time here.

    The total must include the long waits as well as every other delivery.

    The mean accounts for that full total.

    Multiply it by the number of deliveries.

    36×28=100836\times28=100836×28=1008

    The total is 100810081008 minutes.

    Check the total against the reported mean.

    1008÷36=281008\div36=281008÷36=28

    Answer

    Typical time: 121212 minutes, using the median; total time: 100810081008 minutes.

    Key idea

    The median can describe a typical value while the mean still determines the total.

  7. Problem 7 The two filling stations

    Station A fills 333 containers with a mean amount of 666 liters. Station B fills 555 containers with a mean amount of 101010 liters. What is the mean amount in all 888 containers together?

    1. Hint 1

      Combining the containers means combining their total amounts before sharing again.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the total at each station from its count and mean.

    3. Hint 3

      Add the two totals and divide by the combined number of containers.

    Answer

    8.58.58.5 liters, or 172\tfrac{17}{2}217​ liters.

    Full solution

    The mean times the number of containers gives each station's total.

    3×6=183\times6=183×6=18 5×10=505\times10=505×10=50

    Station A contributes 181818 liters and Station B contributes 505050 liters.

    Together there are 888 containers holding

    18+50=6818+50=6818+50=68

    liters.

    Their mean amount is

    68÷8=8.568\div8=8.568÷8=8.5

    or 8.58.58.5 liters.

    As a check, eight containers each holding the combined mean would contain the same total.

    8×8.5=688\times8.5=688×8.5=68

    Answer

    8.58.58.5 liters, or 172\tfrac{17}{2}217​ liters.

    Key idea

    To combine group means, recover each group total and divide their sum by the combined count.

  8. Problem 8 Nia's claim

    A list has four different whole numbers, three below 101010 and one above 101010. Nia says its mean could still be 101010. Is Nia right? If she is, give one such list and show its deviations from 101010; if she is not, explain why no such list exists.

    1. Hint 1

      Think of the mean as a balance point and ask what must balance on each side of it.

    2. Hint 2

      Choose three different whole numbers below 101010 and add their distances below 101010.

    3. Hint 3

      Place the remaining number that total distance above 101010.

    Answer

    Yes. One example is 666, 888, 999, 171717, with deviations −4-4−4, −2-2−2, −1-1−1, 777.

    Full solution

    Choose 666, 888, and 999 below 101010.

    Their distances below 101010 add to

    4+2+1=74+2+1=74+2+1=7

    so choose 171717, which is 777 above 101010.

    The deviations are −4-4−4, −2-2−2, −1-1−1, and 777.

    They balance:

    −4−2−1+7=0-4-2-1+7=0−4−2−1+7=0

    This makes 101010 the balance point even though three values lie below it.

    Check by finding the mean directly.

    6+8+9+17=406+8+9+17=406+8+9+17=40 40÷4=1040\div4=1040÷4=10

    Thus Nia is right.

    Other lists that meet the conditions are also valid.

    Answer

    Yes. One example is 666, 888, 999, 171717, with deviations −4-4−4, −2-2−2, −1-1−1, 777.

    Key idea

    More values may lie on one side of the mean when their total deviations balance those on the other side.

  9. Problem 9 Sam's copied list

    A data list has exactly two modes. Sam copies the entire list once and attaches the copy to the original. He says the resulting list has four modes. Is Sam right? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      A mode is a value with a highest tally, not a single appearance in the list.

    2. Hint 2

      Determine how attaching one full copy changes every tally.

    Answer

    No; the same two values remain the modes.

    Full solution

    Attaching one full copy doubles the tally of every value.

    The two values tied for the highest tally still tie, and neither is replaced by a new value.

    To check that a lower tally stays lower, let aaa be the original highest tally and bbb any lower tally.

    Multiplying both by the same positive number preserves their order.

    2a>2b2a>2b2a>2b

    No other value catches up, so there are still exactly two modes.

    Answer

    No; the same two values remain the modes.

    Key idea

    Copying an entire list the same number of times preserves which values have the highest tallies.

  10. Problem 10 Lee's claim

    Lee says two lists of three whole numbers that both have range 666 must have the same mean. Is Lee right? If he is, explain why; if he is not, give two such lists and their means.

    1. Hint 1

      The range controls the gap between the smallest and largest entries.

    2. Hint 2

      Choose a middle entry between two numbers 666 apart.

    3. Hint 3

      For the other list, move all three entries by the same amount and compare the new extremes.

    Answer

    No. One example: 000, 333, 666, mean 333; 101010, 131313, 161616, mean 131313. Both ranges are 666, but the means differ.

    Full solution

    One possible first list is 000, 333, 666.

    Its range and mean are

    6−0=66-0=66−0=6 0+3+63=3\frac{0+3+6}{3}=330+3+6​=3

    Choose 101010, 131313, 161616 for the other list.

    Its range and mean are

    16−10=616-10=616−10=6 10+13+163=13\frac{10+13+16}{3}=13310+13+16​=13

    Both lists have range 666, but their means differ, so Lee is not right.

    The range describes the distance between extremes, so it does not determine where the center lies.

    Any two lists of three whole numbers with range 666 and different means are acceptable.

    Answer

    No. One example: 000, 333, 666, mean 333; 101010, 131313, 161616, mean 131313. Both ranges are 666, but the means differ.

    Key idea

    Equal ranges can describe data sets whose centers are far apart.
