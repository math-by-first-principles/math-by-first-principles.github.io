    1. Five plots of tomatoes, and how far each sits from their mean . Foundational, 12 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 12 pts

    A community garden is divided into five plots, each looked after by a different family. At the end of the season the garden weighs what every plot produced and records, in kilograms of tomatoes, 171717, 262626, 212121, 141414 and 272727. The newsletter publishes a single figure for the season, and beside it each plot's deviation from that figure, meaning the plot's weight minus the figure itself.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Find the mean weight per plot for the season. Show the total you divided, and say what the mean tells the five families about their season.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Work out each plot's deviation from the mean, writing a plot above the mean as a positive number and one below it as a negative one. Add the five deviations, report the total, and say what that total tells you about the plots above the mean set against those below it.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      A neighbouring garden has eleven plots and a completely different set of weights, none of which you are told. Say what its eleven deviations from its own mean must add to, and explain why that must hold for any list of values at all. Start your explanation from the way the total of the values is related to the mean.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 5 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Two quantities run through the whole question: the total the five plots produced, and how many plots there are. Work out that pair first and keep the total on the page, because the closing part turns on how a total and a mean are tied to each other.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      Take the mean away from each weight, rather than the other way round, so that a heavy plot gives a positive result. Then group the five results by sign and add each group before you add the two groups together.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Adding eleven deviations means subtracting the mean eleven times in all. Ask what that repeated subtraction removes from the total of the values, and set it beside the total itself.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    The mean is 212121 kilograms per plot, from a season total of 105105105 kilograms. Shared out evenly, the whole crop would leave each of the five families with 212121 kilograms.

    Part B

    The five deviations are −4-4−4, +5+5+5, 000, −7-7−7 and +6+6+6 kilograms, and they add to 000. The plots above the mean stand 111111 kilograms clear of it in total, and those below fall 111111 kilograms short.

    Part C

    They must add to 000, as they do for every data set. The values add up to the count times the mean, so subtracting the mean once from each value takes away exactly that same total and leaves nothing behind.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The mean pools the whole season's crop and shares it out evenly, so start with the total the five plots produced.

    17+26+21+14+27=10517 + 26 + 21 + 14 + 27 = 10517+26+21+14+27=105

    There are five plots, so divide that total by five.

    mean=1055=21\text{mean} = \frac{105}{5} = 21mean=5105​=21

    The mean is 212121 kilograms per plot. Read as a fair share, it says that if the five families had tipped the season's tomatoes into one heap and divided the heap evenly, each family would have carried home 212121 kilograms. Nothing is lost or invented in that redistribution: five lots of 212121 kilograms come back to the 105105105 kilograms the garden actually grew.

    Part B

    Subtract the mean from each weight in turn, so that a plot above the mean gives a positive number and a plot below it gives a negative one.

    17−21=−4,26−21=+517 - 21 = -4, \qquad 26 - 21 = +517−21=−4,26−21=+5

    21−21=0,14−21=−721 - 21 = 0, \qquad 14 - 21 = -721−21=0,14−21=−7

    27−21=+627 - 21 = +627−21=+6

    Now add the five results, keeping every sign.

    −4+5+0−7+6=0-4 + 5 + 0 - 7 + 6 = 0−4+5+0−7+6=0

    The total is 000. Splitting the five into two camps shows what is happening. The plots above the mean are 555 and 666 kilograms clear of it, which is 111111 kilograms of surplus, while the plots below it fall short by 444 and 777 kilograms, which is 111111 kilograms of shortfall. The surplus covers the shortfall exactly, and the plot that weighed 212121 kilograms contributes nothing either way.

    Part C

    The eleven deviations must add to 000, and so must the deviations of any list of values whatever, however long the list is and whatever the values weigh.

    The reason sits inside the definition of the mean. The mean is the total of the values divided by how many values there are, so multiplying both sides of that by the count turns it around and gives a fact worth writing down.

    total of the values=count×mean\text{total of the values} = \text{count} \times \text{mean}total of the values=count×mean

    Now build the deviations. Each one is a value with the mean subtracted from it, so adding all eleven deviations adds all eleven values once and subtracts the mean eleven times over.

    (total of the values)−11×mean(\text{total of the values}) - 11 \times \text{mean}(total of the values)−11×mean

    But the bracket is exactly 111111 times the mean, by the line above, so this is a quantity minus itself, which is 000. Nothing about the particular weights was used anywhere in that argument, which is why the neighbouring garden's total is settled without anyone weighing a single tomato.

    This is the precise sense in which the mean is a balance point. Every value above it pulls one way and every value below it pulls the other, and the two pulls always come out the same size.

    In one line

    The five plots produced 105105105 kilograms in total, so the mean is 212121 kilograms per plot. The deviations from the mean are −4-4−4, +5+5+5, 000, −7-7−7 and +6+6+6 kilograms, and they add to 000: the 111111 kilograms of surplus above the mean exactly cover the 111111 kilograms of shortfall below it. The neighbouring garden's eleven deviations must add to 000 as well, because the values of any data set total the count times the mean, so subtracting the mean once from each value removes exactly that total and leaves nothing behind.

    Another way: Averaging from a nearby round number

    Every weight here sits close to 202020 kilograms, so measure from 202020 rather than from zero. Against that guess the five plots stand at −3-3−3, +6+6+6, +1+1+1, −6-6−6 and +7+7+7, and those five add to +5+5+5.

    −3+6+1−6+75=55=1\frac{-3 + 6 + 1 - 6 + 7}{5} = \frac{5}{5} = 15−3+6+1−6+7​=55​=1

    The plots average one kilogram above the guess, so the mean is 20+1=2120 + 1 = 2120+1=21 kilograms, which agrees with the direct calculation. The method works for the same reason the deviations cancel: lowering every value by 202020 lowers the mean by 202020 too, so the guess can be added back at the end.

    When it is worth it When every value is clustered near a convenient round number, this keeps the arithmetic to small numbers and is quick to carry out in your head.

    2. Six repair bills, and the one figure for the shop window . Application, 14 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Application 14 pts

    A bicycle repair shop finished six jobs last week and charged, in dollars, 464646, 525252, 494949, 585858, 454545 and 350350350. Five of the jobs were ordinary servicing. The sixth was a rebuild after a crash, which took a new wheel, a new fork and most of a day's work. The owner wants to paint one figure on the shop window, under the words "a repair here costs about".

    1. A
      Part A.

      Find the mean bill and the median bill for the six jobs. State which of the two needed the bills put in order, and why that step belongs to it.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Count how many of the six bills fall below the mean. Then find how far the rebuild sits from the mean, and how far the other five sit from it once their deviations are added together.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      The owner will paint either the mean or the median on the window. Decide which of the two answers the question a customer is asking, and give the reason it answers it better for this shop. Then describe a task in the shop's own accounts for which the other figure is the right tool, and say what it is being used to work out there.

      Justify your claim State the claim, then give the reason it has to be true. 6 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Two summaries can describe the same six bills and send a customer away expecting very different amounts. Work both of them out before deciding anything, and watch where each one lands among the six numbers.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask what a customer wants to know before handing over a bicycle, and then ask which of the two summaries one unusual job can move a long way. The summary you do not paint on the window still has work to do in the shop's accounts.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    The mean is 100100100 dollars and the median is 50.5050.5050.50 dollars. Only the median needed the bills sorted, because it is fixed by position: with six bills it is the mean of the third and fourth in order.

    Part B

    Five of the six bills fall below the mean. The rebuild stands 250250250 dollars above it, and the other five stand 250250250 dollars below it between them, so one job carries the whole balance.

    Part C

    The median belongs on the window: it sits among the ordinary bills, while the single rebuild has hauled the mean above five of the six jobs. The mean is the right tool when the owner works out what a week of jobs brings in, because the mean multiplied by the number of jobs returns the total takings.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    46+52+49+58+45+350=60046 + 52 + 49 + 58 + 45 + 350 = 60046+52+49+58+45+350=600

    mean=6006=100\text{mean} = \frac{600}{6} = 100mean=6600​=100

    median=49+522=50.5\text{median} = \frac{49 + 52}{2} = 50.5median=249+52​=50.5

    Part B

    against the rebuild's +250+250+250. One job is 250250250 dollars up on its own while five jobs are 250250250 dollars down between them, which is the balance from the lesson doing its work in an extreme case: the mean has been pushed to the one place where those two totals match.

    Part C

    Paint the median. A customer wheeling a bicycle through the door wants to know what an ordinary repair will cost them, and the median of 50.5050.5050.50 dollars sits right among the ordinary bills: three of the six jobs came within five dollars of it, and a fourth within six. The mean of 100100100 dollars is higher than five of the six bills the shop actually issued, so a customer reading it would brace for roughly twice what an ordinary service costs, and would take the bicycle elsewhere for no good reason.

    The comparison in part B says why the two figures part company. The rebuild stands 250250250 dollars above the mean and that one job is what hauled the mean up there, because the mean answers to the size of every bill. The median never felt it.     100×6=600100 \times 6 = 600100×6=600

    So the shop forecasts its takings from the mean and describes a repair to a customer with the median. Neither figure is wrong; they answer different questions, and the window is asking the customer's question.

    3. A morning of shoe sales, and the size to reorder in bulk . Foundational, 12 points. Question 3 of 5.

    Foundational 12 pts

    A shoe shop sells ten pairs of trainers in one morning. In the order they were sold, the sizes were 666, 888, 666, 777, 999, 666, 101010, 888, 666 and 888. The manager keeps that list because two decisions rest on it: which single size to reorder in bulk, and how wide a band of sizes the shelf has to carry.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Find the mean size and the median size sold that morning, showing the middle pair your median came from.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B

      Find the mode of the ten sizes and the range.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C

      The manager can reorder only one size in bulk. Name the summary that decides which size that is, and give two separate reasons neither the mean nor the median can decide it here. You may quote the figures you found earlier.

      Justify your claim State the claim, then give the reason it has to be true. 4 points

    4. D
      Part D.

      The manager also has to decide how wide a band of sizes to keep on the shelf. Say what the range tells her about that, and name something about the morning's sizes that the range leaves out and that she would still want to know.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 2 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Four different summaries can be read off these ten numbers, and each answers a different question about the morning. Work all four out first, then match each decision the manager faces to the summary that speaks to it.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      Write the ten sizes out in order before hunting for a middle. With an even count there is no single middle size, so the middle is shared between the fifth and sixth values in that ordered list.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      A shop can order only sizes that are manufactured. Look at the two figures from the first part and ask whether a customer could put either of them on their feet, then ask what question each of them was answering.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    The mean size is 7.47.47.4 and the median size is 7.57.57.5. Sorting the ten sizes puts a 777 and an 888 in the middle, and the median is the mean of that pair.

    Part B

    The mode is size 666, which sold four times, more often than any other size. The range is 444 sizes, from the smallest size sold, 666, to the largest, 101010.

    Part C

    The mode decides it, and here it is size 666. The mean and the median both landed between the whole sizes, on figures nobody bought that morning; and even had they landed on a size that sold, they describe the middle of the sizes sold rather than the size that sold most.

    Part D

    The range of 444 says every pair sold that morning fell inside a band four sizes wide, from 666 to 101010, so the shelf has to cover that whole band. It says nothing about how the ten pairs were shared out inside the band, and in particular nothing about size 666 taking four of them.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Add the ten sizes and divide by ten.

    6+8+6+7+9+6+10+8+6+8=746 + 8 + 6 + 7 + 9 + 6 + 10 + 8 + 6 + 8 = 746+8+6+7+9+6+10+8+6+8=74

    mean=7410=7.4\text{mean} = \frac{74}{10} = 7.4mean=1074​=7.4

    For the median, sort the sizes first, because the median is the middle of the data in order and not the middle of the list as the morning happened to produce it.

    6,  6,  6,  6,  7,  8,  8,  8,  9,  106, \; 6, \; 6, \; 6, \; 7, \; 8, \; 8, \; 8, \; 9, \; 106,6,6,6,7,8,8,8,9,10

    Ten is an even count, so the middle is shared by the fifth and sixth values, 777 and 888.

    median=7+82=7.5\text{median} = \frac{7 + 8}{2} = 7.5median=27+8​=7.5

    The mean size is 7.47.47.4 and the median size is 7.57.57.5.

    Part B

    The mode is settled by counting rather than by arithmetic. Size 666 was sold four times, size 888 three times, and sizes 777, 999 and 101010 once each, so the size that came up most often is 666.

    mode=6\text{mode} = 6mode=6

    The range subtracts the smallest size sold from the largest.

    range=10−6=4\text{range} = 10 - 6 = 4range=10−6=4

    The mode is size 666, and the range is 444 sizes wide. Notice that the range is not itself a size: it is a gap between two sizes.

    Part C

    The mode decides the bulk order, and for this morning that is size 666. The manager is not asking where the middle of the morning's sizes lay. She is asking which size walked out of the door most often, and counting is what answers that question.

    mean=7.4,median=7.5\text{mean} = 7.4, \qquad \text{median} = 7.5mean=7.4,median=7.5

    mode=6\text{mode} = 6mode=6

    Neither of the two measures of center can stand in for it. Both landed between the whole sizes, and neither 7.47.47.4 nor 7.57.57.5 is a size a single customer bought that morning, so neither figure names a crate she could sensibly order. That is not an accident of these particular sales either: the mean is a total shared out, and the median is the average of two middle values whenever the count is even, so both are free to land between the sizes that sold.

    Even if they had come out on a whole size, they would still be answering the wrong question. Both describe the middle of the sizes sold, and the middle of the sizes sold is not the size that sold most. Four of these ten pairs were size 666, and both measures of center sit above every one of those four. The mode is also the only one of the four summaries that would still work if the shop recorded colours instead of sizes, which is a sign of what it is for: it counts, and counting does not care what is being counted.

    Part D

    The range tells the manager how wide the band has to be, and nothing more than that. Every pair sold that morning lay between size 666 and size 101010.

    10−6=410 - 6 = 410−6=4

    So the shelf has to reach across four sizes. That is a statement about spread, which is the one thing no measure of center reports: two mornings can share a mean, a median and a mode and still stretch across bands of different widths.

    What the range leaves out is how the ten pairs were shared across the band, and she needs that as much as the width. Size 666 took four of the ten pairs on its own, while sizes 999 and 101010 took one each, so a shelf stocked to the same depth all the way across would run out at one end and gather dust at the other. The range fixes how wide the shelf must be; only the counts fix how deep each pile should be.

    In one line

    The mean size is 7.47.47.4 and the median is 7.57.57.5; the mode is size 666, sold four times, and the range is 444 sizes. The bulk reorder is decided by the mode, size 666: the mean and the median landed between the whole sizes, on figures nobody bought, and both describe the middle of the sizes sold rather than the size that sold most. The range says only that the shelf must cover sizes 666 to 101010. It says nothing about how the ten pairs were shared out inside that band, where size 666 alone took four of them.

    mode=30\text{mode} = 30mode=30

    The range subtracts the shortest from the longest.

    range=55−25=30\text{range} = 55 - 25 = 30range=55−25=30

    The mode decides the shelf. It names the length customers asked for most often, which is 303030 millimetres.     4. Two reading groups, and one figure for the whole club . Application, 14 points. Question 4 of 5.

    Application 14 pts

    A book club runs two reading groups. The Tuesday group has 444 members, who read a mean of 999 books last year. The Thursday group has 666 members, who read a mean of 141414 books. The secretary has to report one figure for all ten members, and is also planning for an eleventh member joining in January.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 5 points

      Part B.

      An eleventh member joins, and the secretary wants the mean across all eleven members to come out at 131313 books. Find how many books the new member must read.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    3. C

      Find and correct the error Say which line first goes wrong, why it is wrong, and then do it correctly. 5 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      A mean and a total are two views of one thing: the values add up to the number of them times the mean. Every part of this question gets easier once each group's total is written on the page.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    The Tuesday group read 363636 books and the Thursday group read 848484, so the ten members read 120120120 books between them and the mean for the whole club is 121212 books per member.

    Part B

    The new member must read 232323 books, comfortably above the club's current mean of 121212.

    Part C

    It is the mean of the two group means, so it gives the four Tuesday readers the same weight as the six Thursday readers. The club figure has to come from the whole total shared over all ten members. The shortcut is correct whenever the two groups hold equal numbers of members.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    A group's mean and its total are two views of the same figures, because the books read add up to the number of members times the mean. That turns each group's mean into a total.

    4×9=36,6×14=844 \times 9 = 36, \qquad 6 \times 14 = 844×9=36,6×14=84

    The two groups read

    36+84=12036 + 84 = 12036+84=120

    books between them, and the club has 4+6=104 + 6 = 104+6=10 members, so the club's mean comes from that pair.

    mean=12010=12\text{mean} = \frac{120}{10} = 12mean=10120​=12

    The Tuesday group read 363636 books, the Thursday group read 848484, and the mean across all ten members is 121212 books.

    Part B

    Work backwards from the target, using the same relationship in the other direction. Eleven members at a mean of 131313 books would have to read

    11×13=14311 \times 13 = 14311×13=143

    books between them. The ten current members have already read 120120120 of those, so the new member has to supply what is missing.

    143−120=23143 - 120 = 23143−120=23

    The eleventh member must read 232323 books. That figure sits well above the club's current mean of 121212, which is what should be expected: a mean only climbs if the value joining the set stands above it.

    Part C

    The 11.511.511.5 is a real number and the arithmetic behind it is sound, but it is the mean of the two group means rather than the mean of the ten members.

    9+142=11.5\frac{9 + 14}{2} = 11.529+14​=11.5

    That calculation counts each group once, which hands the four Tuesday readers exactly as much say as the six Thursday readers. The club's figure has to count each member once. Thursday has half as many members again as Tuesday, so its higher mean deserves to pull the club figure further than Tuesday's pulls it back, and it does: the club mean of 121212 sits above the halfway point of 11.511.511.5 and closer to the Thursday group's 141414.

    The shortcut is not always wrong, though, and it is worth knowing when it is safe. It gives the right answer whenever the two groups hold the same number of members, because then counting each group once and counting each member once come to the same thing. (It also lands on the right figure in the one uninteresting case where the groups already share a mean, since then every way of averaging returns that same figure.) Had both groups held five members, the totals would have been 454545 and 707070, which is 115115115 books over 101010 members, and the club mean would have been 11.511.511.5 after all.

    45+7010=11510=11.5\frac{45 + 70}{10} = \frac{115}{10} = 11.51045+70​=10115​=11.5

    In one line

    The Tuesday group read 4×9=364 \times 9 = 364×9=36 books and the Thursday group read 6×14=846 \times 14 = 846×14=84, so ten members read 120120120 books and the club mean is 121212 books per member. To bring the mean of eleven members to 131313, the club needs 11×13=14311 \times 13 = 14311×13=143 books, so the new member must read 143−120=23143 - 120 = 23143−120=23. Averaging the two group means gives 11.511.511.5, which weights each group equally instead of each member equally; it agrees with the club mean whenever the two groups hold the same number of members.

    5. Building a data set to order . Reasoning, 13 points. Question 5 of 5.

    Reasoning 13 pts

    A puzzle in a school magazine asks readers for five whole numbers with three properties at once: a mean of 888, a median of 666 and a range of 121212. Several readers send in answers, and the answers do not all agree with one another. Sort every set you build from least to greatest before you check it against the three conditions.

    1. A
      Part A.

    2. B
      Part B.

      Now build a set of five whole numbers that meets the same three conditions and in which some value appears more than once. State the mode of your set.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      A reader writes in with a claim: whenever a data set has a mean larger than its median, removing the single largest value will always bring the mean down to the median or below. Refute the claim with a data set of your own: work out its mean and median, remove its largest value, and work out both again. Then say what feature of your set breaks the claim.

      Construct a counterexample Give one specific case, and show it breaks the claim. 5 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      The mean and the median are pinned down by different things: one by the total of all five numbers, the other by the value sitting in the middle once they are sorted. Settle the middle value first and let the outer numbers take up the slack in the total.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      Three of the five numbers are almost decided for you once the total is known: the middle one, and the pair at the two ends whose gap is fixed. Pick the smallest number and see how much freedom is left.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      The claim blames one value for the whole gap. Try building a set in which two values, not one, stand far above the rest, and see whether taking the larger of them away settles anything.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    Answers vary. One set is 333, 555, 666, 111111, 151515: it totals 404040 for a mean of 888, its third value in order is 666, and 15−3=1215 - 3 = 1215−3=12.

    Part B

    Answers vary. One set is 444, 666, 666, 888, 161616, whose mode is 666: it totals 404040, its third value in order is 666, and 16−4=1216 - 4 = 1216−4=12.

    Part C

    The claim fails. For 444, 555, 666, 202020, 252525 the mean is 121212 and the median is 666; removing the 252525 leaves 444, 555, 666, 202020, whose mean is 8.758.758.75 and whose median is 5.55.55.5, so the mean is still the larger of the two.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Take the conditions one at a time and let each one fix part of the set. Think of the five numbers in order as a first, a second, a middle, a fourth and a fifth.

    A mean of 888 over five numbers fixes the total, because the values add up to the count times the mean.

    5×8=405 \times 8 = 405×8=40

    A median of 666 fixes the middle number itself, since with an odd count the median is the third value in order. A range of 121212 fixes the gap between the first number and the last.

    Now choose. Let the smallest be 333, and the range forces the largest to be 151515. Those two and the middle 666 account for 3+15+6=243 + 15 + 6 = 243+15+6=24 of the total, which leaves 40−24=1640 - 24 = 1640−24=16 to be split between the second and the fourth. The second has to land between 333 and 666 and the fourth between 666 and 151515, so that the two ends stay the ends. Taking 555 and 111111 does it.

    3,  5,  6,  11,  153, \; 5, \; 6, \; 11, \; 153,5,6,11,15

    Check all three conditions: the total is 404040, so the mean is 888; the third value in order is 666, so the median is 666; and 15−3=1215 - 3 = 1215−3=12, so the range is 121212. Many other sets work here, because these particular conditions leave several choices for the endpoints and the remaining pair.

    Part B

    Run the same construction again, this time steering a repeat into the set. The total stays at 404040, the middle value stays at 666, and the two ends stay 121212 apart.

    Let the smallest be 444, which forces the largest to be 161616. Those two and the middle account for 4+16+6=264 + 16 + 6 = 264+16+6=26, leaving 141414 for the second and fourth numbers, the second between 444 and 666 and the fourth between 666 and 161616. Choosing 666 and 888 puts a repeat alongside the middle value.

    4,  6,  6,  8,  164, \; 6, \; 6, \; 8, \; 164,6,6,8,16

    The total is 404040, the third value in order is 666, and 16−4=1216 - 4 = 1216−4=12, so all three conditions still hold. The value 666 now appears twice while every other value appears once.

    mode=6\text{mode} = 6mode=6

    The mode is not tied down by the other three summaries at all. A set can satisfy the same conditions with no repeated value whatever, as 333, 555, 666, 111111, 151515 does, or with a clear mode, as this one does.

    Part C

    The claim fails, and a single set of five numbers is enough to break it.

    Take 444, 555, 666, 202020 and 252525. They total 606060, so the mean is 121212, while the third value in order is 666, so the median is 666. The mean is comfortably the larger, which is exactly the situation the claim is about.

    mean=605=12,median=6\text{mean} = \frac{60}{5} = 12, \qquad \text{median} = 6mean=560​=12,median=6

    Now remove the single largest value, 252525, as the claim instructs. What is left is 444, 555, 666 and 202020, totalling 353535 over four values, and with an even count the median is the mean of the middle pair.

    mean=354=8.75\text{mean} = \frac{35}{4} = 8.75mean=435​=8.75

    median=5+62=5.5\text{median} = \frac{5 + 6}{2} = 5.5median=25+6​=5.5

    The mean has fallen, from 121212 to 8.758.758.75, but it is still well above the median, so the claim is broken.

    The reasoning behind the claim is worth naming, because it is half right. It assumes that one value is responsible for the whole gap between the mean and the median, and in the familiar picture of a single wild value among ordinary ones that is fair enough. Here two values sit far above the other three, so taking one away leaves the other still hauling the mean upward. The mean answers to the size of every value at once, and nothing says only one value at a time is allowed to be extreme.

    In one line

    One set meeting all three conditions is 333, 555, 666, 111111, 151515: it totals 404040 for a mean of 888, its third value in order is 666, and 15−3=1215 - 3 = 1215−3=12. One with a repeat is 444, 666, 666, 888, 161616, whose mode is 666. The reader's claim fails: 444, 555, 666, 202020, 252525 has a mean of 121212 and a median of 666, and removing the 252525 leaves a mean of 8.758.758.75 against a median of 5.55.55.5, so the mean is still the larger. Two values, not one, were holding it up.
