    A birdwatcher counts the goldfinches at a feeder on six mornings and records 121212, 777, 151515, 777, 999 and 101010.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

      The seventh count is larger than every one of the first six. Working from totals rather than from any single morning, explain why one added value has to overshoot the new mean by so much.

    The answer

    Part A

    The mean is 101010 goldfinches a morning, from a total of 606060. The median is 9.59.59.5 goldfinches, the mean of the middle pair once the counts are sorted.

    • The median may be written 192\frac{19}{2}219​; what is not the same is 999 or 101010 on its own, because an even count has no single middle value

    Part B

    The mode is 777 goldfinches, the only count that appears more than once. The range is 888 goldfinches, from the smallest count 777 up to the largest count 151515.

    Part C

    171717 goldfinches. Seven mornings at a mean of 111111 need a total of 777777, and the first six supply 606060.

    Part D

    Lifting the mean by one raises what each of the six earlier mornings is credited with by one, a shortfall of 666 that only the new morning can supply. So it must carry its own 111111 plus that 666. Any value added to lift a mean has to beat the new mean by the whole shortfall of the values already there.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Add the six counts and divide by six. Then sort them and average the middle pair, because with an even count no single value is central.

    mean=12+7+15+7+9+106=606=10,median=9+102=9.5\text{mean} = \frac{12 + 7 + 15 + 7 + 9 + 10}{6} = \frac{60}{6} = 10, \qquad \text{median} = \frac{9 + 10}{2} = 9.5mean=612+7+15+7+9+10​=660​=10,median=29+10​=9.5

    Sorted, the six counts run 7,7,9,10,12,157, 7, 9, 10, 12, 157,7,9,10,12,15, so the third and fourth are the pair to average.

    Part B

    The mode is whichever count occurs most often, and the range is the distance from the smallest count to the largest.

    mode=7,range=15−7=8\text{mode} = 7, \qquad \text{range} = 15 - 7 = 8mode=7,range=15−7=8

    Neither one is divided by anything. Only the mean owes a division by the number of mornings.

    Part C

    A mean is the total divided by the count, so the total is the mean times the count. Seven mornings at a mean of 111111 demand a total of 777777, and the six already recorded supply 606060.

    7×11−60=77−60=177 \times 11 - 60 = 77 - 60 = 177×11−60=77−60=17

    So the seventh morning brought 171717 goldfinches.

    Part D

    Compare the two totals the two means demand. Six mornings at a mean of 101010 need 606060; seven mornings at a mean of 111111 need 777777.

    77−60=17=11+677 - 60 = 17 = 11 + 677−60=17=11+6

    The split is the whole story. The new morning owes 111111 for itself, and it owes one more for each of the six earlier mornings, which used to be credited with 101010 each and are now credited with 111111 each. That is why a single added value can sit far above the mean it produces, and why the further the mean is dragged the further out that value has to be.

    In one line

    The six counts have mean 101010, median 9.59.59.5, mode 777 and range 888. A seventh morning of 171717 goldfinches brings the mean of all seven to 111111, and it has to be that large because lifting the mean by one credits each of the six earlier mornings with one more bird, a shortfall of 666 that only the new morning can supply on top of its own 111111.
