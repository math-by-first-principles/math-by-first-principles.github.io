    Two orchards each weighed 101010 apples. Orchard A's weights have a mean of 140140140 grams and a range of 888 grams. Orchard B's weights have a mean of 140140140 grams and a range of 606060 grams. Which statement do these figures support?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    You chose A: Reads the range as a second measure of center, so a larger range is taken to mean larger values.

    You chose B: Reverses the meaning of the range, treating a small range as a wide spread.

    You chose D: Treats an equal mean as proof of identical data, which the differing ranges already rule out.

    The two orchards share a center and differ in spread. The range is the distance from the smallest weight to the largest, so a larger range means the weights stretch further apart.

    rangeA=8 g,rangeB=60 g\text{range}_A = 8 \text{ g}, \qquad \text{range}_B = 60 \text{ g}rangeA​=8 g,rangeB​=60 g

    Equal means say the two sets balance at the same point; they say nothing about how far the values sit from it, which is exactly what the range reports.

    A fair spinner has four equal sectors numbered 111 to 444. It is spun twice and the two numbers are added. A class performs 200200200 pairs of spins and records a total of at least 666 on 868686 of those pairs. Which statement compares the record with the theoretical value correctly?

    Answer choices for question 20

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    You chose A: Computes the theoretical probability of a total below 666, which is the complement of the event recorded.

    You chose B: Treats a gap after a limited run as proof of bias, though a short run scatters around the true value.

    You chose C: Counts only the three ways of making exactly 666, leaving out the totals of 777 and 888.

    Two spins give 4×4=164 \times 4 = 164×4=16 equally likely ordered results. Totals of at least 666 come from (2,4),(3,3),(4,2)(2,4), (3,3), (4,2)(2,4),(3,3),(4,2) for a total of 666, from (3,4),(4,3)(3,4), (4,3)(3,4),(4,3) for 777, and from (4,4)(4,4)(4,4) for 888, which is 666 results.

    Answer

    19.419.419.4 seconds.

    Full solution

    The original recorded total is

    5(18)=905(18)=905(18)=90

    seconds.

    The corrected total is

    90+7=9790+7=9790+7=97

    seconds.

    The number of recordings is still 555, so the new mean is

    97÷5=19.497\div5=19.497÷5=19.4

    seconds.

    As a check, the mean rises by 7÷5=1.47\div5=1.47÷5=1.4 seconds, from 181818 to 19.419.419.4.

    Key idea

    Correcting one value changes the total, and the change in mean is shared across the unchanged count.

  2. Problem 2 The helmet stall

    A rental stall begins the day with 606060 helmets. The pictograph shows every loan and return during the day. There are no other changes. How many helmets are at the stall at the end of the day?

    Full solution

    With 121212 helmets per whole symbol, the loan count is

    3(12)+12(12)=423(12)+\tfrac12(12)=423(12)+21​(12)=42

    helmets.

    The Returns row shows 222 whole symbols and a quarter symbol, so the return count is

    2(12)+14(12)=272(12)+\tfrac14(12)=272(12)+41​(12)=27

    helmets.

    60−42+27=4560-42+27=4560−42+27=45

  3. Problem 3 The submission menu

    An app accepts either one still image or a before-and-after pair. There are 555 still images to choose from. A pair starts with one of 333 before images, P, Q, or R, and then takes an after image: P may be followed by any of 444 after images, while Q and R may each be followed by any of 222. Single-image submissions and paired submissions are different types. How many different submissions are possible?

    1. Hint 1

      Check whether the second choice in a pair offers the same number of options whatever the first choice was.

    2. Hint 2

      Count the pairs that start with P, with Q, and with R separately, then combine the pair count with the single images.

    Answer

    131313 submissions.

    Full solution

    The number of after images depends on which before image starts the pair, so the pairs are not one product of stage counts.

    Count them by their before image instead: 444 start with P, 222 with Q, and 222 with R.

    These groups do not overlap, so the number of paired submissions is

    4+2+2=84+2+2=84+2+2=8

    A submission is either one still image or one pair, and the two types do not overlap, so their counts add.

    5+8=135+8=135+8=13

    Checking, 3×4=123\times4=123×4=12 would give Q and R two extra after images each, and removing those 444 pairs leaves 12−4=812-4=812−4=8 pairs, as counted.

    Answer

    131313 submissions.

    Key idea

    Stage counts multiply when each choice leaves the same number of options at the next stage; when that number changes, count each case and add.

  4. Problem 4 The discarded delay

    Full solution

    Its two middle values are both 101010, so

    10+102=10\frac{10+10}{2}=10210+10​=10

    gives the median in minutes.

    Its range is

    30−8=2230-8=2230−8=22

    minutes.

    After deletion, the sorted list is 888, 888, 101010, 101010, 121212.

    The middle value is 101010, so the median is still 101010 minutes.

    The range is now

    12−8=412-8=412−8=4

    minutes.

    Answer

  6. Problem 6 The key order

    Four different keys, labeled A, B, C, and D, are placed in a row. Every possible ordering is equally likely. What is the probability that the first two positions hold A and B in either order? Give the probability as a fraction in lowest terms.

    1. Hint 1

      A used key is unavailable for later positions, and different orders count separately.

    2. Hint 2

      For the favorable orderings, consider the possible orders of A and B and then the possible orders of the remaining keys.

    Answer

    16\frac1661​.

    Full solution

    The available choices shrink as each key is placed.

    The total number of orderings is

    4(3)(2)(1)=244(3)(2)(1)=244(3)(2)(1)=24

    A and B can start the row as AB or BA, giving 222 choices.

    The last two keys can be CD or DC, giving 222 choices for each start.

    The favorable count is

    2(2)=42(2)=42(2)=4

    Every complete ordering is equally likely, so

    424=16\frac4{24}=\frac16244​=61​

    is the probability.

    The four favorable orderings are ABCD, ABDC, BACD, and BADC, which checks the count.

    Answer

    16\frac1661​.

    Key idea

    Count complete arrangements with shrinking choices before comparing favorable arrangements with all equally likely ones.

  7. Problem 7 The three letters

    A display selects a three-letter code from A, B, and C. Letters may repeat, and every possible code is equally likely. What is the probability that the displayed code contains exactly one A? Give a fraction in lowest terms and show your counting.

    1. Hint 1

      Treat each position as a stage, and think about where the single A can sit.

    2. Hint 2

      There are three possible positions for the A. Once its position is fixed, each other position has two choices.

    Answer

    49\frac4994​; 272727 total codes and 121212 favorable codes.

    Full solution

    Each of the three positions can contain any of the three letters, giving

    3(3)(3)=273(3)(3)=273(3)(3)=27

    equally likely codes.

    If A is first, the other positions may each be B or C, giving 2(2)=42(2)=42(2)=4 codes.

    The same count holds when A is second or third.

    These three groups do not overlap because there is exactly one A.

    Thus

    4+4+4=124+4+4=124+4+4=12

    codes are favorable.

    The requested probability is

    1227=49\frac{12}{27}=\frac492712​=94​

    The favorable count is less than the total, and the probability lies between 000 and 111.

    Answer

    49\frac4994​; 272727 total codes and 121212 favorable codes.

    Key idea

    An exact-one condition can separate favorable outcomes into groups according to the position of the required item.

  10. Problem 10 The digit record

    A random digit generator is claimed to give each digit from 000 through 999 the same chance on each independent trial. A trial qualifies when its digit is at least 333. In 100100100 trials, 626262 qualify. Find the theoretical probability that a trial qualifies under that claim, and the experimental probability from this record. A student says the record proves the generator does not give equal chances. Does the student's conclusion follow from the record? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Separate a probability obtained from the claimed model from a proportion obtained from the recorded trials.

    2. Hint 2

      Count the qualifying digits and divide by all the equally likely digits; use the trial counts separately.

    3. Hint 3

      Ask whether a generator that matches the claim could still produce a record like this one in only 100100100 trials.

    Answer

    Theoretical probability under the claim: 0.70.70.7 (or 710\tfrac{7}{10}107​). Experimental probability: 0.620.620.62 (or 3150\tfrac{31}{50}5031​). The student's conclusion does not follow from the record.

    Full solution

    Under the claim, the qualifying digits 333, 444, 555, 666, 777, 888, and 999 are 777 of the 101010 equally likely digits.

    The theoretical qualifying probability is

    7÷10=0.77\div10=0.77÷10=0.7

    The experimental probability uses the recorded successes and trials.

    62÷100=0.6262\div100=0.6262÷100=0.62

    The conclusion does not follow.

    The record falls 0.080.080.08 short of the claimed 0.70.70.7, but each trial's digit is random, so even a generator that matches the claim gives a finite record that need not land on 0.70.70.7.

    A run of 100100100 trials can come out at 0.620.620.62 without the chances being unequal.

    As independent trials pile up, a generator that matches the claim becomes increasingly likely to show a proportion close to 0.70.70.7, so a much longer record would be better evidence; these 100100100 trials do not prove the claim false.

    Answer

    Theoretical probability under the claim: 0.70.70.7 (or 710\tfrac{7}{10}107​). Experimental probability: 0.620.620.62 (or 3150\tfrac{31}{50}5031​). The student's conclusion does not follow from the record.

    Key idea

    A finite experimental proportion can differ from a model's theoretical probability, so a gap in one record does not by itself disprove the model.
