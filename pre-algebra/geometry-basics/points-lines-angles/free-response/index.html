  1. Problem 1 The marked opening

    Write both three-letter names for the opening marked by the arc in the figure.

    Text description of this figure

    Three rays share the endpoint Q. One goes horizontally to the right through point P, one rises to the upper right through point R, and one goes straight up through point S; each ray has an arrowhead beyond its labeled point. A single arc near Q starts on ray QP, passes across ray QR, and ends on ray QS, marking the whole opening between the two outer rays. No angle measures are shown.

    1. Hint 1

      An angle name identifies its two sides and their shared endpoint.

    2. Hint 2

      Put the shared endpoint in the middle, and use a point on each side of the marked opening.

    Answer

    ∠PQS\angle PQS∠PQS and ∠SQP\angle SQP∠SQP.

    Full solution

    The marked opening has sides through PPP and SSS.

    Both sides start at QQQ, so QQQ must be the middle letter.

    Reading the two sides in either order gives ∠PQS\angle PQS∠PQS or ∠SQP\angle SQP∠SQP.

    The point RRR is on the ray inside the opening, so it does not name either outer side.

    Answer

    ∠PQS\angle PQS∠PQS and ∠SQP\angle SQP∠SQP.

    Key idea

    The middle letter of a three-letter angle name is its vertex, and the other letters lie on its two sides.

  2. Problem 2 A pointer turn

    A pointer turns through 58\frac{5}{8}85​ of a full turn. Classify the angle it turns through as acute, right, obtuse, straight, or reflex.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the turn with a half turn and a full turn.

    2. Hint 2

      A full turn measures 360∘360^\circ360∘; multiply this by the given fraction.

    Answer

    Reflex.

    Full solution

    Find the amount of turn in degrees.

    58×360∘=225∘\frac{5}{8}\times360^\circ=225^\circ85​×360∘=225∘

    This is greater than 180∘180^\circ180∘ and less than 360∘360^\circ360∘, so the angle is reflex.

    The fraction is greater than one-half and less than one, which agrees with that classification.

    Answer

    Reflex.

    Key idea

    A turn greater than a half turn and less than a full turn forms a reflex angle.

  3. Problem 3 Points on the drawing

    List every group of three labeled points that is collinear in the figure.

    Text description of this figure

    Two straight lines with arrowheads at both ends cross at point B. The horizontal line carries points A, B and C from left to right, with A and C equally far from B. The other line slants up from lower left to upper right; point D lies on it below and to the left of B, and point E lies on it above and to the right of B, equally far from B. No lengths or angles are marked.

    1. Hint 1

      Collinear points lie on one straight line.

    2. Hint 2

      Follow each complete line through the crossing and collect its three point labels.

    Answer

    A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C and D,B,ED,B,ED,B,E (in any order within each group).

    Full solution

    The horizontal line contains AAA, BBB, and CCC.

    The slanted line contains DDD, BBB, and EEE.

    Each of these groups lies on one straight line.

    Any other group has a point from the horizontal line other than BBB and a point from the slanted line other than BBB.

    Two different lines share only the point BBB, so no single line holds all three.

    Therefore the complete list is A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C and D,B,ED,B,ED,B,E, in any order within each group.

    Answer

    A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C and D,B,ED,B,ED,B,E (in any order within each group).

    Key idea

    To identify collinear points, follow one straight line through all the points in a proposed group.

  4. Problem 4 A line, a ray and a segment

    For the figure, write a name for the complete line, the drawn ray, and the segment ending at HHH, and give each object's endpoints.

    Text description of this figure

    A horizontal line with arrowheads at both ends passes through points D, E and F, in that order from left to right. From point E, a vertical ray goes straight up through point G, with an arrowhead above G. A horizontal segment with solid dots at its ends and no arrowheads joins G to point H on its right. H sits directly above F, but H and F are not joined. No lengths or angles are marked.

    1. Hint 1

      Arrowheads show which directions continue beyond the drawing.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the endpoint first when naming a ray; a line has no endpoints, and a segment has two.

    Answer

    Line DF↔\overleftrightarrow{DF}DF (or any two of DDD, EEE, FFF, in either order), no endpoints; ray EG→\overrightarrow{EG}EG, endpoint EEE; segment GH‾\overline{GH}GH or HG‾\overline{HG}HG, endpoints G,HG,HG,H.

    Full solution

    The line extends in both directions through DDD, EEE, and FFF, so it has no endpoints.

    Any two of these three points name it, such as DF↔\overleftrightarrow{DF}DF.

    The upward ray starts at EEE and passes through GGG, giving EG→\overrightarrow{EG}EG with endpoint EEE.

    The horizontal piece from GGG to HHH has no arrows, so it is GH‾\overline{GH}GH with endpoints GGG and HHH.

    Answer

    Line DF↔\overleftrightarrow{DF}DF (or any two of DDD, EEE, FFF, in either order), no endpoints; ray EG→\overrightarrow{EG}EG, endpoint EEE; segment GH‾\overline{GH}GH or HG‾\overline{HG}HG, endpoints G,HG,HG,H.

    Key idea

    The arrowheads and stopping points in a drawing determine whether a straight object is a line, ray, or segment.

  5. Problem 5 An opening in two parts

    The figure shows an opening split into two parts. Find the complement and supplement of the whole opening ∠KLM\angle KLM∠KLM, and classify each of those two results as acute, right, obtuse, straight, or reflex.

    Text description of this figure

    Three rays start at point L, each with an arrowhead beyond its labeled point. Ray LK goes horizontally to the right through point K. Ray LN turns upward from LK through point N, and the arc between LK and LN is labeled 21 degrees. Ray LM turns upward further through point M, and the arc between LN and LM is labeled 27 degrees. The angles are drawn to scale, and the whole opening from LK to LM has no measure written on it.

    1. Hint 1

      The whole opening includes both marked parts.

    2. Hint 2

      Add the two parts before subtracting from 90∘90^\circ90∘ for the complement or 180∘180^\circ180∘ for the supplement.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare each resulting measure with 90∘90^\circ90∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Answer

    Complement: 42∘42^\circ42∘, acute. Supplement: 132∘132^\circ132∘, obtuse.

    Full solution

    The two non-overlapping parts fill the whole opening.

    ∠KLM=21∘+27∘\angle KLM=21^\circ+27^\circ∠KLM=21∘+27∘ ∠KLM=48∘\angle KLM=48^\circ∠KLM=48∘

    Subtract the whole opening from each required total.

    90∘−48∘=42∘90^\circ-48^\circ=42^\circ90∘−48∘=42∘ 180∘−48∘=132∘180^\circ-48^\circ=132^\circ180∘−48∘=132∘

    The complement is acute because it lies between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 90∘90^\circ90∘.

    The supplement is obtuse because it lies between 90∘90^\circ90∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Checking, 48∘+42∘=90∘48^\circ+42^\circ=90^\circ48∘+42∘=90∘ and

    48∘+132∘=180∘.48^\circ+132^\circ=180^\circ.48∘+132∘=180∘.

    Answer

    Complement: 42∘42^\circ42∘, acute. Supplement: 132∘132^\circ132∘, obtuse.

    Key idea

    Find the entire angle before calculating its complement or supplement.

  6. Problem 6 Across the two rails

    In the figure, lines aaa and bbb are parallel. Find the angles marked uuu, vvv, and www. For each one, name its relationship to the whole opening split into 28∘28^\circ28∘ and 43∘43^\circ43∘ at the upper crossing.

    Text description of this figure

    Two horizontal lines, a on top and b below, have arrowheads at both ends and matching parallel marks. One straight line rises from lower left to upper right and crosses both. At the upper crossing, a short extra ray drawn inside the lower-left opening splits it in two: the part next to the leftward direction of line a is marked 28 degrees, and the part next to the downward-left direction of the crossing line is marked 43 degrees. At the lower crossing, three angles have their own arcs and letters: u is the lower-left angle, v is the upper-right angle, and w is the upper-left angle. The lower-right angle at the lower crossing is not marked.

    1. Hint 1

      First find the full angle at the upper crossing, then compare positions at the two crossings.

    2. Hint 2

      Corresponding angles occupy matching positions; alternate interior angles lie between the parallel lines on opposite sides of the crossing line.

    3. Hint 3

      Co-interior angles lie between the parallel lines on the same side of the crossing line and add to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Answer

    u=71∘u=71^\circu=71∘, corresponding; v=71∘v=71^\circv=71∘, alternate interior; w=109∘w=109^\circw=109∘, co-interior (same-side interior).

    Full solution

    The full marked angle at the upper crossing is

    28∘+43∘=71∘28^\circ+43^\circ=71^\circ28∘+43∘=71∘

    Angle uuu has the same position as that opening, below its parallel line and to the left of the crossing line, so they are corresponding angles.

    Because the lines are parallel,

    u=71∘u=71^\circu=71∘

    Angle vvv lies between the parallel lines on the opposite side of the crossing line from the given opening.

    These are alternate interior angles, so

    v=71∘v=71^\circv=71∘

    Angle www lies between the parallel lines on the same side of the crossing line as the given opening.

    These are co-interior angles.

    w=180∘−71∘w=180^\circ-71^\circw=180∘−71∘ w=109∘w=109^\circw=109∘

    At the lower crossing, uuu and vvv are vertical angles and agree; vvv and www form a straight angle and add to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Answer

    u=71∘u=71^\circu=71∘, corresponding; v=71∘v=71^\circv=71∘, alternate interior; w=109∘w=109^\circw=109∘, co-interior (same-side interior).

    Key idea

    When a line crosses parallel lines, the positions of the angles determine whether their measures are equal or supplementary.

  7. Problem 7 The support drawing

    For the three complete lines in the figure, list every pair guaranteed to be parallel and every pair guaranteed to be perpendicular. Classify the angles where ttt meets bbb.

    Text description of this figure

    Two horizontal lines, a above b, have arrowheads at both ends and matching parallel marks. A vertical line t, also with arrowheads at both ends, crosses both of them. Where t meets a, a small right-angle square sits in the upper-right opening. Where t meets b, nothing is marked. There are no points, lengths or measures.

    1. Hint 1

      The matching marks and the small square provide different kinds of information.

    2. Hint 2

      The square gives one angle where ttt meets aaa; corresponding angles carry that information to line bbb.

    Answer

    Parallel: a,ba,ba,b. Perpendicular: a,ta,ta,t and b,tb,tb,t. All four angles where ttt meets bbb are right angles.

    Full solution

    The matching parallel marks state that aaa and bbb are parallel.

    The square at the crossing of aaa and ttt marks a 90∘90^\circ90∘ angle, so those two lines are perpendicular.

    The corresponding angle where ttt meets bbb is also 90∘90^\circ90∘, because aaa and bbb are parallel.

    Thus ttt is perpendicular to bbb.

    The opposite angle is equal, and each neighboring angle is

    180∘−90∘=90∘180^\circ-90^\circ=90^\circ180∘−90∘=90∘

    So all four angles at the lower crossing are right angles.

    Each of the three possible line pairs has now been accounted for.

    Answer

    Parallel: a,ba,ba,b. Perpendicular: a,ta,ta,t and b,tb,tb,t. All four angles where ttt meets bbb are right angles.

    Key idea

    A line perpendicular to one of two parallel lines is perpendicular to the other as well.

  8. Problem 8 The measurement record

    The figure shows two straight lines crossing. A record lists three of the four openings: top 76∘76^\circ76∘, right 106∘106^\circ106∘, and bottom 74∘74^\circ74∘. Exactly one of these entries is wrong. Which entry is it, and what should it read?

    Text description of this figure

    Two straight lines, each with arrowheads at both ends, cross at one point to form an X. Each of the four openings at the crossing is marked with its own arc and named in words: top, right, bottom and left. The top and bottom openings are narrow, acute angles, and the left and right openings are wide, obtuse angles. No angle measures, points or equality marks are shown.

    1. Hint 1

      Which pairs of openings must be equal, and which must add to 180∘180^\circ180∘?

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the top and bottom entries with each other, then check each of them against the right entry.

    3. Hint 3

      Suppose each entry in turn is the wrong one, and ask whether the other two entries then agree.

    Answer

    The top entry; it should be 74∘74^\circ74∘.

    Full solution

    The top and bottom openings are opposite each other, so they are vertical angles and must be equal.

    The record gives 76∘76^\circ76∘ and 74∘74^\circ74∘, so the top entry or the bottom entry is wrong.

    Neighboring openings form a linear pair, so each neighboring pair adds to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    The right and bottom entries pass this check, since

    106∘+74∘=180∘.106^\circ+74^\circ=180^\circ.106∘+74∘=180∘.

    The top and right entries fail it, since

    76∘+106∘=182∘.76^\circ+106^\circ=182^\circ.76∘+106∘=182∘.

    If only the right entry were wrong, the top and bottom entries would still disagree.

    If only the bottom entry were wrong, the top and right entries would still fail to add to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    So the wrong entry is the top one.

    The top opening must equal the bottom one, so it should read 74∘74^\circ74∘.

    Checking, the top and bottom now match, and

    74∘+106∘=180∘.74^\circ+106^\circ=180^\circ.74∘+106∘=180∘.

    Answer

    The top entry; it should be 74∘74^\circ74∘.

    Key idea

    At a crossing of two lines, every recorded measure must fit all of its pairs at once: opposite openings match, and neighboring openings add to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

  9. Problem 9 Two angle cards

    Two cards each show an acute angle. Nia says two acute angles can add to an obtuse angle. Omar says two acute angles must add to an obtuse angle. Who is correct? Give measures that support each decision.

    1. Hint 1

      An obtuse angle must be greater than 90∘90^\circ90∘; ask whether two acute angles are always large enough to pass it.

    2. Hint 2

      One example shows that something can happen, and one counterexample shows that it need not happen.

    3. Hint 3

      Try two acute measures that are both close to 90∘90^\circ90∘, and then two that are both small.

    Answer

    Nia is correct: for example, 64∘64^\circ64∘ and 38∘38^\circ38∘ add to an obtuse 102∘102^\circ102∘. Omar is not: for example, 22∘22^\circ22∘ and 19∘19^\circ19∘ add to an acute 41∘41^\circ41∘.

    Full solution

    Take 64∘64^\circ64∘ and 38∘38^\circ38∘.

    Each is between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 90∘90^\circ90∘, so each is acute.

    Their sum is

    64∘+38∘=102∘64^\circ+38^\circ=102^\circ64∘+38∘=102∘

    Since 102∘102^\circ102∘ is between 90∘90^\circ90∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘, the sum is obtuse.

    So two acute angles can add to an obtuse angle, and Nia is correct.

    Now take 22∘22^\circ22∘ and 19∘19^\circ19∘, which are also both acute.

    Their sum is

    22∘+19∘=41∘22^\circ+19^\circ=41^\circ22∘+19∘=41∘

    That sum is acute, not obtuse, so two acute angles need not add to an obtuse angle.

    This one counterexample shows that Omar is not correct.

    Answer

    Nia is correct: for example, 64∘64^\circ64∘ and 38∘38^\circ38∘ add to an obtuse 102∘102^\circ102∘. Omar is not: for example, 22∘22^\circ22∘ and 19∘19^\circ19∘ add to an acute 41∘41^\circ41∘.

    Key idea

    The sum of two acute angles may or may not be obtuse: an example shows what can happen, and a counterexample shows what need not happen.

  10. Problem 10 The two settings

    Two machines each have two settings. At each setting, a machine makes a first angle less than 90∘90^\circ90∘ and a second angle adjacent to it, sharing a vertex and a side with no overlap. On the first machine the second angle is the supplement of the first angle; on the second machine it is the complement.

    Sam says that on each machine the whole opening has the same angle type at both settings, even though the first angle changes. Is Sam correct? Explain, giving the type of the whole opening on each machine at each setting.

    1. Hint 1

      Consider what the words supplement and complement tell you about each pair's total.

    2. Hint 2

      Call the first angle xxx, write the whole opening on each machine in terms of xxx, and compare each total with the angle-type definitions.

    Answer

    Yes. On the first machine the whole opening is straight at both settings, and on the second machine it is right at both settings.

    Full solution

    Let the first angle measure xxx.

    Since xxx is less than 90∘90^\circ90∘, it has both a supplement and a complement.

    The two angles are adjacent with no overlap, so the whole opening is their sum.

    On the first machine, the second angle is 180∘−x180^\circ-x180∘−x, so the whole opening is

    x+(180∘−x)=180∘x+(180^\circ-x)=180^\circx+(180∘−x)=180∘

    The value of xxx cancels, so the whole opening is a straight angle at both settings.

    On the second machine, the second angle is 90∘−x90^\circ-x90∘−x, so the whole opening is

    x+(90∘−x)=90∘x+(90^\circ-x)=90^\circx+(90∘−x)=90∘

    Again xxx cancels, so the whole opening is a right angle at both settings.

    Changing the first angle changes its partner by the opposite amount, so Sam is correct about both machines.

    Answer

    Yes. On the first machine the whole opening is straight at both settings, and on the second machine it is right at both settings.

    Key idea

    An angle and its supplement always total 180∘180^\circ180∘, and an angle and its complement always total 90∘90^\circ90∘, so the first angle's size does not change the whole opening's type.
