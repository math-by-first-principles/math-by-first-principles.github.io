  1. Problem 1 A triangle named by its corners

    Triangle JKLJKLJKL has a right angle at KKK. Side JKJKJK is 121212 cm long and side KLKLKL is 353535 cm long.

    Which side is the hypotenuse, and how long is it?

    1. Hint 1

      Look for the one side that does not touch the corner where the right angle is.

    2. Hint 2

      The two sides that meet at KKK are the legs. Square each of them and add the results.

    3. Hint 3

      The total is 136913691369. Look for a whole number between 303030 and 404040 whose square is 136913691369.

    Answer

    The hypotenuse is JLJLJL, and JL=37JL = 37JL=37 cm.

    Full solution

    The right angle is at KKK, so the legs are JKJKJK and KLKLKL, the two sides that meet at KKK.

    The hypotenuse is the third side, JLJLJL.

    Add the squares of the legs.

    JL2=122+352JL^2 = 12^2 + 35^2JL2=122+352 JL2=144+1225JL^2 = 144 + 1225JL2=144+1225 JL2=1369JL^2 = 1369JL2=1369

    Since 362=129636^2 = 1296362=1296 and 372=136937^2 = 1369372=1369, the square root of 136913691369 is 373737.

    So JL=37JL = 37JL=37 cm, which is longer than both legs, as a hypotenuse must be.

    Answer

    The hypotenuse is JLJLJL, and JL=37JL = 37JL=37 cm.

    Key idea

    Find the hypotenuse by its position, across from the right angle, before any numbers go into the rule.

  2. Problem 2 Legs of 666 and 999

    A right triangle has legs of 666 cm and 999 cm.

    Find the exact length of its hypotenuse in simplest radical form. Then give the length to the nearest tenth of a centimeter.

    1. Hint 1

      The square of the hypotenuse is the squares of the two legs added together.

    2. Hint 2

      That sum is not a perfect square. Look for the largest perfect square that divides it evenly.

    3. Hint 3

      To round, try 10.810.810.8, then the halfway value 10.8510.8510.85, and see which side of 117117117 each square falls.

    Answer

    Exactly 3133\sqrt{13}313​ cm (the same as 117\sqrt{117}117​ cm), which is about 10.810.810.8 cm.

    Full solution

    Add the squares of the legs.

    c2=62+92c^2 = 6^2 + 9^2c2=62+92 c2=36+81=117c^2 = 36 + 81 = 117c2=36+81=117

    So

    c=117.c = \sqrt{117}.c=117​.

    The largest perfect square that divides 117117117 is 999, because 117=9×13117 = 9 \times 13117=9×13 and 131313 has no perfect-square factor other than 111.

    c=9×13c = \sqrt{9} \times \sqrt{13}c=9​×13​ c=313c = 3\sqrt{13}c=313​

    To estimate, 102=10010^2 = 100102=100 and 112=12111^2 = 121112=121, so ccc is between 101010 and 111111, and close to 111111.

    Next, 10.82=116.6410.8^2 = 116.6410.82=116.64 and

    10.852=117.7225.10.85^2 = 117.7225.10.852=117.7225.

    The number 117117117 lies between those two squares, so ccc is between 10.810.810.8 and 10.8510.8510.85 and rounds to 10.810.810.8 cm.

    Answer

    Exactly 3133\sqrt{13}313​ cm (the same as 117\sqrt{117}117​ cm), which is about 10.810.810.8 cm.

    Key idea

    Keep the exact root as the answer, and round only when a decimal is asked for, marking it as approximate.

  3. Problem 3 A long, thin right triangle

    A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 616161 m and one leg of 111111 m. How long is the other leg?

    1. Hint 1

      Decide which side the 616161 m is, and which kind of side is missing.

    2. Hint 2

      The missing side is a leg. Take the square of the known leg away from the square of the hypotenuse.

    3. Hint 3

      The result is a perfect square. Its root is a whole number between 505050 and 707070.

    Answer

    606060 m.

    Full solution

    The 616161 m side is the hypotenuse, so the missing side is a leg.

    Its square is what remains after taking the known leg's square away from the hypotenuse's square.

    b2=612−112b^2 = 61^2 - 11^2b2=612−112 b2=3721−121b^2 = 3721 - 121b2=3721−121 b2=3600b^2 = 3600b2=3600

    Since 60×60=360060 \times 60 = 360060×60=3600, the missing leg is b=60b = 60b=60.

    Check by running the rule forward.

    112+602=121+360011^2 + 60^2 = 121 + 3600112+602=121+3600

    That sum is 372137213721, which is 61261^2612, so the other leg is 606060 m.

    Answer

    606060 m.

    Key idea

    For a missing leg, subtract the known leg's square from the hypotenuse's square.

  4. Problem 4 Hypotenuse 888, leg 444

    A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 888 in and one leg of 444 in.

    Find the other leg exactly, in simplest radical form, and then to the nearest tenth of an inch.

    1. Hint 1

      The missing side is a leg, so the squares are subtracted rather than added.

    2. Hint 2

      After subtracting, pull the largest perfect-square factor out from under the root.

    3. Hint 3

      For the decimal, trap the number under the root between two perfect squares first, then try decimals.

    Answer

    Exactly 434\sqrt{3}43​ in (the same as 48\sqrt{48}48​ in), which is about 6.96.96.9 in.

    Full solution

    The 888 in side is the hypotenuse, so subtract the square of the known leg.

    b2=82−42b^2 = 8^2 - 4^2b2=82−42 b2=64−16=48b^2 = 64 - 16 = 48b2=64−16=48

    So

    b=48.b = \sqrt{48}.b=48​.

    The largest perfect square dividing 484848 is 161616, because 48=16×348 = 16 \times 348=16×3.

    b=16×3b = \sqrt{16} \times \sqrt{3}b=16​×3​ b=43b = 4\sqrt{3}b=43​

    For the decimal, 36<48<4936 < 48 < 4936<48<49, so bbb is between 666 and 777, and close to 777.

    Next, 6.92=47.616.9^2 = 47.616.92=47.61 and

    6.952=48.3025.6.95^2 = 48.3025.6.952=48.3025.

    The number 484848 lies between those squares, so bbb rounds to 6.96.96.9 in.

    Answer

    Exactly 434\sqrt{3}43​ in (the same as 48\sqrt{48}48​ in), which is about 6.96.96.9 in.

    Key idea

    A missing leg can be irrational, and its exact form comes from the same simplifying you use for any square root.

  5. Problem 5 The wire to the top of the pole

    A straight wire runs from the top of a pole 4.54.54.5 m tall to a stake in the ground 2.82.82.8 m from the foot of the pole. The pole stands at a right angle to the level ground.

    How long is the wire?

    Text description of this figure

    Level ground runs along the bottom of the picture. On the right, a pole rises straight up from the ground and is labeled 4.5 m. On the ground to the left is a stake, and the ground distance from the stake to the foot of the pole is labeled 2.8 m. A straight wire joins the stake to the top of the pole and is labeled with a question mark. A small square at the foot of the pole marks the right angle between the pole and the ground.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the right angle in the picture, and decide which length sits across from it.

    2. Hint 2

      The wire is across from the right angle. Square the two other lengths and add them.

    3. Hint 3

      The sum is 28.0928.0928.09. Its square root is a number with one decimal place.

    Answer

    5.35.35.3 m.

    Full solution

    The pole and the ground meet at a right angle, so the pole and the ground distance are the legs.

    The wire, across from the right angle, is the hypotenuse.

    Square the legs and add.

    w2=4.52+2.82w^2 = 4.5^2 + 2.8^2w2=4.52+2.82 w2=20.25+7.84w^2 = 20.25 + 7.84w2=20.25+7.84 w2=28.09w^2 = 28.09w2=28.09

    Since 52=255^2 = 2552=25 and 62=366^2 = 3662=36, the wire is between 555 m and 666 m long.

    Trying 5.35.35.3 gives 5.3×5.3=28.095.3 \times 5.3 = 28.095.3×5.3=28.09 exactly, so the wire is 5.35.35.3 m long.

    Answer

    5.35.35.3 m.

    Key idea

    In a measurement problem, find the right angle first, because it tells you which length is the hypotenuse.

  6. Problem 6 Is the corner square?

    A builder wants a square corner. She marks a point 333333 cm along one edge and a point 565656 cm along the other edge, both measured from the corner. The two marks turn out to be 666666 cm apart.

    Is the corner a right angle? Explain how you know, and say how far apart the marks would be if it were.

    1. Hint 1

      Suppose for a moment that the corner is a right angle. Which side of a right triangle would the gap between the marks be?

    2. Hint 2

      Work out the gap that a right angle would force, using the two marked lengths as the legs.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare the gap a right angle forces with the 666666 cm the builder actually measured.

    Answer

    No, the corner is not a right angle. A right angle would put the marks exactly 656565 cm apart, and they are 666666 cm apart.

    Full solution

    If the corner were a right angle, the two marked lengths would be the legs of a right triangle, and the gap between the marks would be its hypotenuse.

    g2=332+562g^2 = 33^2 + 56^2g2=332+562 g2=1089+3136g^2 = 1089 + 3136g2=1089+3136 g2=4225g^2 = 4225g2=4225

    Since 65×65=422565 \times 65 = 422565×65=4225, a right angle would make the gap exactly 656565 cm.

    The builder measured 666666 cm, not 656565 cm.

    A right angle at the corner forces a gap of 656565 cm, so this corner cannot be a right angle.

    Answer

    No, the corner is not a right angle. A right angle would put the marks exactly 656565 cm apart, and they are 666666 cm apart.

    Key idea

    A right angle forces the length of the third side, so any other measurement shows the corner is not square.

  7. Problem 7 A triangle with two equal sides

    A triangle has two sides of 535353 cm and a base of 565656 cm. The height drawn from the top corner meets the base at its midpoint, at a right angle.

    Find the height and the area of the triangle.

    1. Hint 1

      The height splits the triangle into two right triangles. Name the hypotenuse of one of them.

    2. Hint 2

      One leg of each small triangle is half of the base, and the height is the other leg.

    3. Hint 3

      Once you know the height, the area is half of the base times the height.

    Answer

    The height is 454545 cm, and the area is 1260 cm21260 \text{ cm}^21260 cm2.

    Full solution

    The height meets the base at its midpoint at a right angle, so it cuts the triangle into two right triangles.

    Each has a hypotenuse of 535353 cm and a leg of half the base, which is 282828 cm.

    The height is the other leg.

    h2=532−282h^2 = 53^2 - 28^2h2=532−282 h2=2809−784h^2 = 2809 - 784h2=2809−784 h2=2025h^2 = 2025h2=2025

    Since 45×45=202545 \times 45 = 202545×45=2025, the height is 454545 cm.

    The area of a triangle is half the base times the height.

    A=12×56×45A = \tfrac12 \times 56 \times 45A=21​×56×45 A=1260A = 1260A=1260

    So the area is 1260 cm21260 \text{ cm}^21260 cm2.

    Answer

    The height is 454545 cm, and the area is 1260 cm21260 \text{ cm}^21260 cm2.

    Key idea

    A height can cut a shape into right triangles, and then the theorem supplies the length an area formula needs.

  8. Problem 8 Checking a classmate's leg

    Leo is finding the missing leg of a right triangle whose hypotenuse is 858585 and whose other leg is 131313. He writes b2=852+132=7394b^2 = 85^2 + 13^2 = 7394b2=852+132=7394, so bbb is about 868686.

    Explain how you can tell that Leo's answer is wrong without redoing his arithmetic. Then find the correct leg.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the size of Leo's answer with the size of the hypotenuse.

    2. Hint 2

      In a right triangle the hypotenuse is the longest side. Which operation on the squares gives a leg?

    3. Hint 3

      Subtract the square of 131313 from the square of 858585, then look for a whole-number root.

    Answer

    A leg of about 868686 would be longer than the hypotenuse of 858585, which no leg can be. The correct leg is 848484.

    Full solution

    The hypotenuse is always the longest side of a right triangle.

    Leo's leg of about 868686 is longer than the hypotenuse of 858585, so it cannot be right.

    He added the squares, which is the method for a hypotenuse, not for a leg.

    For a leg, subtract the squares.

    b2=852−132b^2 = 85^2 - 13^2b2=852−132 b2=7225−169b^2 = 7225 - 169b2=7225−169 b2=7056b^2 = 7056b2=7056

    Since 802=640080^2 = 6400802=6400 and 852=722585^2 = 7225852=7225, the root is between 808080 and 858585.

    Trying 848484 gives 84×84=705684 \times 84 = 705684×84=7056, so b=84b = 84b=84.

    Check by running the rule forward.

    132+842=169+705613^2 + 84^2 = 169 + 7056132+842=169+7056

    That sum is 722572257225, which is 85285^2852.

    Answer

    A leg of about 868686 would be longer than the hypotenuse of 858585, which no leg can be. The correct leg is 848484.

    Key idea

    A leg must come out shorter than the hypotenuse, which gives a quick check on any answer.

  9. Problem 9 Two ways to fill one square

    A right triangle has legs of 444 cm and 777 cm. Four copies of it are placed inside a square with sides of 111111 cm, in two different ways.

    In the first way, the triangles sit in the four corners and leave a tilted square in the middle whose sides are hypotenuses. In the second way, the triangles pair up into two rectangles and leave two squares.

    Find the area left uncovered in each arrangement, explain why the two areas must be equal, and give the exact length of the hypotenuse.

    1. Hint 1

      Start with the area of the big square, and then the area of one triangle.

    2. Hint 2

      Each triangle is half of a rectangle that measures 444 cm by 777 cm.

    3. Hint 3

      In the second arrangement, the two squares left uncovered have the two legs as their sides.

    Answer

    Both arrangements leave 65 cm265 \text{ cm}^265 cm2, since each is the same 121 cm2121 \text{ cm}^2121 cm2 square minus the same four triangles. The hypotenuse is exactly 65\sqrt{65}65​ cm.

    Full solution

    The big square has area

    112=121 cm2.11^2 = 121 \text{ cm}^2.112=121 cm2.

    Each triangle is half of a 444 by 777 rectangle, so it covers 14 cm214 \text{ cm}^214 cm2, and the four triangles cover 56 cm256 \text{ cm}^256 cm2 together.

    In the first arrangement, the tilted square is all that the triangles leave.

    121−56=65121 - 56 = 65121−56=65

    In the second arrangement, the uncovered squares have sides 444 cm and 777 cm.

    16+49=6516 + 49 = 6516+49=65

    The two uncovered areas must be equal, because each is the same 121 cm2121 \text{ cm}^2121 cm2 square with the same 56 cm256 \text{ cm}^256 cm2 of triangles taken away.

    The tilted square is the square on the hypotenuse, so the hypotenuse squared is 656565.

    The number 65=5×1365 = 5 \times 1365=5×13 has no perfect-square factor other than 111, so the hypotenuse is exactly 65\sqrt{65}65​ cm, a little more than 888 cm.

    Answer

    Both arrangements leave 65 cm265 \text{ cm}^265 cm2, since each is the same 121 cm2121 \text{ cm}^2121 cm2 square minus the same four triangles. The hypotenuse is exactly 65\sqrt{65}65​ cm.

    Key idea

    The same square minus the same four triangles leaves the same area, which is why the square on the hypotenuse equals the two squares on the legs.

  10. Problem 10 The shortcut across

    Explain why the hypotenuse of any right triangle is shorter than its two legs added together.

    Then check your explanation on a right triangle whose legs are both 555 cm, giving the hypotenuse exactly and to the nearest tenth of a centimeter.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare squares instead of lengths: a bigger square always has a longer side.

    2. Hint 2

      Picture a square whose side is the two legs laid end to end, like the big square in the lesson's second arrangement.

    3. Hint 3

      That big square holds the two squares on the legs and two rectangles as well. Compare its area with the square on the hypotenuse.

    Answer

    A square with side equal to the two legs together holds both leg squares plus two rectangles, so it is bigger than the square on the hypotenuse. For legs of 555 cm, the hypotenuse is exactly 525\sqrt{2}52​ cm, about 7.17.17.1 cm.

    Full solution

    Lay the two legs end to end and build a square on that combined length.

    Cut it as in the second arrangement: it splits into the square on one leg, the square on the other leg, and two rectangles with the legs as their sides.

    So that square has more area than the two leg squares alone.

    The two leg squares together are exactly the square on the hypotenuse, so the square on the two legs together is bigger than the square on the hypotenuse.

    A bigger square has a longer side.

    So the two legs together are longer than the hypotenuse, which means the hypotenuse is shorter than the two legs added together.

    For legs of 555 cm, add the squares.

    c2=25+25=50c^2 = 25 + 25 = 50c2=25+25=50

    Since 50=25×250 = 25 \times 250=25×2, the hypotenuse is c=52c = 5\sqrt{2}c=52​ cm.

    Next, 7.052=49.70257.05^2 = 49.70257.052=49.7025 and 7.152=51.12257.15^2 = 51.12257.152=51.1225, so ccc is between them and rounds to 7.17.17.1 cm.

    That is less than 5+5=105 + 5 = 105+5=10 cm, as the explanation says it must be.

    Answer

    A square with side equal to the two legs together holds both leg squares plus two rectangles, so it is bigger than the square on the hypotenuse. For legs of 555 cm, the hypotenuse is exactly 525\sqrt{2}52​ cm, about 7.17.17.1 cm.

    Key idea

    The square on the two legs together holds the hypotenuse's square plus two rectangles, so walking across is always shorter than walking along both legs.
