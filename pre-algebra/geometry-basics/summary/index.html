Geometry Basics: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Line, segment, ray
AB↔\overleftrightarrow{AB}AB: no endpoints, forever both ways. AB‾\overline{AB}AB: two endpoints, the only one of the three with a length. AB→\overrightarrow{AB}AB: one endpoint, AAA, named first.
Plane, collinear
A plane is an endless flat surface. Points on one straight line are collinear; two different lines meet at most once, so lines that do cross share exactly one point.
Angle ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC
Two rays from a shared vertex, the middle letter naming it. Size is the turn between the arms, in degrees, however long they are drawn.
Adjacent angles, linear pair
Adjacent: sharing a vertex and a side, not overlapping. A linear pair is two adjacent angles whose outer sides form a straight line.
ℓ∥m\ell \parallel mℓ∥m and ℓ⊥m\ell \perp mℓ⊥m
Parallel lines never meet. Perpendicular lines cross at a right angle, which makes all four angles there 90∘90^\circ90∘.
Transversal
A line crossing two or more others. Its angle facts hold only when the crossed lines are parallel.
Perpendicular height hhh
Distance from a base to the opposite side or vertex, at a right angle. Never a slanted side; on an obtuse triangle it can land outside.
Circle parts
Radius rrr: center to edge, all equal. Chord: joins two edge points. Diameter ddd: the longest chord, through the center. Arc: part of the boundary. Circumference: all of it.
Prism, cylinder, face, net
Prism: two identical bases, unchanging cross-section. Cylinder: the same with a circular base. Face: one flat surface. Net: the solid unfolded flat.
Perimeter, area, volume, surface area
Distance around a flat shape; unit squares covering a region; unit cubes filling a solid; total area of every outer surface, flat or curved. Units: linear, square, cubic, square.
Ordered pair (x,y)(x, y)(x,y)
Across first (xxx, right positive), then up (yyy), so (3,2)≠(2,3)(3, 2) \neq (2, 3)(3,2)=(2,3). The origin (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0) is where the axes cross.

Formulas and theorems

    e.g. The reflex angle beside 115∘115^\circ115∘ is 245∘245^\circ245∘.

  • Complement and supplement of xxx

    complement=90∘−xsupplement=180∘−x\begin{gathered} \text{complement} = 90^\circ - x \\ \text{supplement} = 180^\circ - x \end{gathered}complement=90∘−xsupplement=180∘−x​

    Use when A complement needs x<90∘x < 90^\circx<90∘, a supplement x<180∘x < 180^\circx<180∘, or the result is zero or negative. The angles need not touch.

    e.g. 62∘62^\circ62∘: complement 28∘28^\circ28∘, supplement 118∘118^\circ118∘.

  • Angle sums: line and full turn

    Use when The angles must share the vertex and fill the line or turn with no gaps or overlaps; a linear pair is therefore supplementary.

    e.g. 110∘110^\circ110∘, 135∘135^\circ135∘, xxx fill a turn: x=115∘x = 115^\circx=115∘.

  • Vertical angles are equal

    Use when Only the opposite pairs where two straight lines cross, sharing the vertex and no side; a neighbour is supplementary to it instead.

    Use when All three need the crossed lines PARALLEL. Corresponding: matching positions. Alternate interior: opposite sides, between the lines. Co-interior: same side, between them.

    e.g. An interior 78∘78^\circ78∘ gives alternate interior 78∘78^\circ78∘ and co-interior 102∘102^\circ102∘.

    d=2r,C=πd=2πrd = 2r, \qquad C = \pi d = 2\pi rd=2r,C=πd=2πr

    Use when Halve a diameter whenever a formula wants rrr. Circumference is a length, and it goes round a WHOLE circle: a part-circle boundary adds its straight edges.

    π=Cd≈3.14\pi = \frac{C}{d} \approx 3.14π=dC​≈3.14

Problem types, step by step

Find an unknown angle from a stated relationship

  1. Name the relationship: complementary (90∘90^\circ90∘), linear pair (180∘180^\circ180∘), vertical (equal), or filling a point (360∘360^\circ360∘).
  2. Write it as an equation, with xxx the unknown or the multiplier in an expression.
  3. Solve, then check the pieces total 90∘90^\circ90∘, 180∘180^\circ180∘, or 360∘360^\circ360∘.

e.g. (3x+12)∘(3x + 12)^\circ(3x+12)∘ with x∘x^\circx∘ in a linear pair: 4x+12=1804x + 12 = 1804x+12=180, so x=42x = 42x=42.

Find an angle using parallel lines and a transversal

  1. Confirm the crossed lines are marked parallel, or none of the rules applies.
  2. Classify the pair: matching positions (corresponding), opposite sides between the lines (alternate interior), same side between them (co-interior).
  3. Set them equal, or their sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘ for co-interior, and solve.

e.g. Corresponding (4x)∘(4x)^\circ(4x)∘ and (x+60)∘(x + 60)^\circ(x+60)∘: x=20x = 20x=20, so each is 80∘80^\circ80∘.

Find an area, or work back from one to a missing length

  1. Take the shape's formula: lwlwlw, bhbhbh, 12bh\tfrac{1}{2}bh21​bh, or 12(a+b)h\tfrac{1}{2}(a + b)h21​(a+b)h.
  2. Pick the base (or the two parallel sides) and the PERPENDICULAR height, discarding any slanted side given as a distractor.
  3. Substitute, every length in one unit, and report square units.
  4. Working back to a missing length, undo instead: multiply by 222 if the formula carries a 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​, then divide.

e.g. A triangle of area 54 cm254 \text{ cm}^254 cm2 on base 121212 cm: h=2(54)12=9h = \dfrac{2(54)}{12} = 9h=122(54)​=9 cm.

Find the area of a composite figure

  1. Cut it into non-overlapping rectangles, triangles, and part-circles, or read it as one full shape with a piece removed.
  2. Recover an unlabelled side by subtracting the labelled ones running the same direction.
  3. Add the pieces, or subtract the removed piece, then split a second way as a check.

e.g. A 999 by 777 rectangle less a 444 by 333 corner: 63−12=5163 - 12 = 5163−12=51.

Find the circumference or the area of a circle

  1. Convert to the radius with r=d2r = \tfrac{d}{2}r=2d​ if a diameter was given.
  2. Around uses C=2πrC = 2\pi rC=2πr; inside uses A=πr2A = \pi r^2A=πr2, squaring rrr BEFORE multiplying by π\piπ.
  3. Halve for a semicircle, adding the diameter if its whole boundary is wanted, and report linear units for a length, square units for an area, with ≈\approx≈.

e.g. d=20d = 20d=20 cm: r=10r = 10r=10, so C≈62.8C \approx 62.8C≈62.8 cm and A≈314 cm2A \approx 314 \text{ cm}^2A≈314 cm2.

Find the volume or the surface area of a solid

  1. Volume: find the base area BBB (lwlwlw, 12bh△\tfrac{1}{2}bh_{\triangle}21​bh△​, or πr2\pi r^2πr2), multiply by the distance between the two bases, and report cubic units.
  2. Surface area of a box: compute lwlwlw, lhlhlh, and whwhwh, add, and double.
  3. Surface area of a cylinder: add the caps 2πr22\pi r^22πr2 to the unrolled side 2πrh2\pi r h2πrh; an open box or tube drops the surfaces it lacks, and the answer is square units.

e.g. A closed cube of edge 555 cm: V=125 cm3V = 125 \text{ cm}^3V=125 cm3 but S=6(25)=150 cm2S = 6(25) = 150 \text{ cm}^2S=6(25)=150 cm2.

Plot, read, and measure on the coordinate grid

  1. To plot, from the origin go across by the first number, then up or down by the second; negatives mean left and down.
  2. To read, move straight to the xxx-axis for the first coordinate (down from above it, up from below it) and straight to the yyy-axis for the second, then name the quadrant from the two signs.
  3. For an axis-aligned rectangle, subtract the xxx-coordinates of a horizontal side for the length and the yyy-coordinates of a vertical side for the width, then apply P=2(l+w)P = 2(l + w)P=2(l+w) and A=lwA = lwA=lw.

e.g. (−3,5)(-3, 5)(−3,5) is 333 left, 555 up, Quadrant II; corners (−2,1)(-2, 1)(−2,1), (5,1)(5, 1)(5,1), (5,6)(5, 6)(5,6) give P=24P = 24P=24, A=35A = 35A=35.

Exam traps

  • Trap Reporting a measurement in the wrong kind of unit, or converting 1 m21 \text{ m}^21 m2 to 100 cm2100 \text{ cm}^2100 cm2.

    Fix Count the factors of length the formula multiplies: one linear, two square, three cubic. Squaring a length squares the conversion factor, so it is 10,000 cm210{,}000 \text{ cm}^210,000 cm2.

  • Trap Putting the diameter where the radius belongs, so d=12d = 12d=12 cm gives A≈3.14×122=452.16A \approx 3.14 \times 12^2 = 452.16A≈3.14×122=452.16.

    Fix Halve first: r=6r = 6r=6 and A≈113.04 cm2A \approx 113.04 \text{ cm}^2A≈113.04 cm2. Squaring turns the factor of 222 into 444, so the slip is always four times too big.

  • Trap Answering a distance-around question with πr2\pi r^2πr2, or a space-inside question with 2πr2\pi r2πr.

    Fix Circumference carries rrr once, area carries rrr squared. At r=10r = 10r=10: about 62.862.862.8 around, 314314314 inside.

  • Trap Multiplying by the slanted side of a parallelogram, triangle, or trapezoid.

    Fix Only the perpendicular height counts: base 999, slant 666, height 555 gives 45 cm245 \text{ cm}^245 cm2, not 545454.

  • Trap Dropping the 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​ from the triangle formula, or using a+ba + ba+b instead of a+b2\tfrac{a+b}{2}2a+b​ for a trapezoid.

    Fix Either doubles the area: each shape is half of a parallelogram made from two copies of it.

  • Trap Calling a semicircle's boundary half the circumference.

    Fix The cut exposes a straight diameter, so P=πr+2rP = \pi r + 2rP=πr+2r: at r=4r = 4r=4, about 20.5620.5620.56 cm, not 12.5612.5612.56.

  • Trap Charging a closed can only its unrolled side, 2πrh2\pi r h2πrh.

    Fix Add both caps: S=2πr2+2πrhS = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r hS=2πr2+2πrh. The side alone is a tube open at each end.

  • Trap Subtracting coordinates to get the distance between two diagonal points.

    Fix It works only when the points SHARE a coordinate; (1,2)(1, 2)(1,2) and (4,6)(4, 6)(4,6) are neither 333 nor 444 apart.
