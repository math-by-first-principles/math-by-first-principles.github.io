Geometry Basics: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Line, segment, ray
A line AB↔\overleftrightarrow{AB}AB extends forever both ways. A segment AB‾\overline{AB}AB has two endpoints and a finite length. A ray AB→\overrightarrow{AB}AB starts at AAA and extends forever through BBB.
Plane, collinear
A plane is an endless flat surface. Collinear points lie on one straight line. Two distinct lines that cross share exactly one point.
Angle ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC
Two rays meet at a vertex, named by the middle letter (BBB). The angle measures the turn between them in degrees, regardless of how long the arms are drawn.
Adjacent angles, linear pair
Adjacent angles share a vertex and a side without overlapping. They form a linear pair when their outer sides make a straight line.
ℓ∥m\ell \parallel mℓ∥m and ℓ⊥m\ell \perp mℓ⊥m
Parallel lines lie in one plane and never meet. Perpendicular lines cross at a right angle, making all four angles 90∘90^\circ90∘.
Transversal
A line that crosses two or more other lines. The corresponding, alternate-interior and co-interior angle rules require the crossed lines to be parallel.
Perpendicular height hhh
The distance from a base to the opposite side or vertex, measured at a right angle to the base or its extension. A triangle's height can fall outside it.
Circle parts
A radius rrr joins the center to the circle; all radii have equal length. A chord joins two points on the circle. A diameter ddd passes through the center and is the longest chord. An arc is part of the boundary; circumference measures the whole boundary.
Prism, cylinder, face, net
A prism has two identical parallel polygon bases joined by flat faces, with matching cross-sections parallel to the bases. A cylinder has circular bases. A face is a flat surface; a net unfolds the outer surfaces into a flat pattern.
Perimeter, area, volume, surface area
Perimeter measures a flat shape's boundary in linear units. Area counts square units; volume counts cubic units. Surface area adds every outer surface, flat or curved, in square units.
Ordered pair (x,y)(x, y)(x,y)
The first coordinate gives horizontal position; the second gives vertical position. Positive means right or up. The origin (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) is where the axes cross. Order matters: (3,2)(3,2)(3,2) and (2,3)(2,3)(2,3) are different points.
Proportional, constant of proportionality kkk
A fixed nonzero constant kkk relates the variables by y=kxy=kxy=kx. For every allowed x≠0x\neq0x=0, the ratio y/xy/xy/x is kkk. If 000 is allowed, its output is 000. The unit rate kkk has units of output per unit of input.
Right triangle: legs, hypotenuse
The legs meet at the 90∘90^\circ90∘ angle. The hypotenuse is opposite that angle and is the longest side.

Formulas and theorems

  • Angle types by measure

    acute: 0∘<θ<90∘right: θ=90∘obtuse: 90∘<θ<180∘straight: θ=180∘reflex: 180∘<θ<360∘\begin{gathered} \text{acute: } 0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ \\ \text{right: } \theta = 90^\circ \\ \text{obtuse: } 90^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ \\ \text{straight: } \theta = 180^\circ \\ \text{reflex: } 180^\circ < \theta < 360^\circ \end{gathered}acute: 0∘<θ<90∘right: θ=90∘obtuse: 90∘<θ<180∘straight: θ=180∘reflex: 180∘<θ<360∘​

    Use when θ\thetaθ is measured in degrees. A full turn is 360∘360^\circ360∘. An angle strictly between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘ has a reflex partner completing that turn.

    e.g. The reflex angle beside 115∘115^\circ115∘ is 245∘245^\circ245∘.

  • Complement and supplement of xxx

    complement=90∘−xsupplement=180∘−x\begin{gathered} \text{complement} = 90^\circ - x \\ \text{supplement} = 180^\circ - x \end{gathered}complement=90∘−xsupplement=180∘−x​
    Text description

    A right angle split into x and its complement, beside a straight angle split into x and its supplement.

    Use when For positive angles, a complement exists only when x<90∘x<90^\circx<90∘, and a supplement only when x<180∘x<180^\circx<180∘. The paired angles need not touch.

    e.g. 62∘62^\circ62∘: complement 28∘28^\circ28∘, supplement 118∘118^\circ118∘.

  • Angle sums: line and full turn

    linear pair=180∘around a point=360∘\begin{gathered} \text{linear pair} = 180^\circ \\ \text{around a point} = 360^\circ \end{gathered}linear pair=180∘around a point=360∘​

    Use when The angles share a vertex and fill the straight angle or full turn without gaps or overlaps. A linear pair is supplementary.

    e.g. 110∘110^\circ110∘, 135∘135^\circ135∘, xxx fill a turn: x=115∘x = 115^\circx=115∘.

  • Vertical angles are equal

    ∠1=∠3,∠2=∠4\angle 1 = \angle 3, \qquad \angle 2 = \angle 4∠1=∠3,∠2=∠4

    Use when These are opposite angles where two straight lines cross. They share a vertex but no side. Adjacent angles at that crossing are supplementary.

  • Parallel lines cut by a transversal

    corresponding: equalalternate interior: equalco-interior: sum 180∘\begin{gathered} \text{corresponding: equal} \\ \text{alternate interior: equal} \\ \text{co-interior: sum } 180^\circ \end{gathered}corresponding: equalalternate interior: equalco-interior: sum 180∘​
    Text description

    Two parallel lines cut by a transversal, with the corresponding pair of angles marked a and the alternate interior pair marked b.

    Use when The crossed lines must be parallel. Corresponding angles occupy matching positions. Alternate interior angles lie between the lines on opposite sides of the transversal; co-interior angles lie between them on the same side.

    e.g. An interior 78∘78^\circ78∘ gives alternate interior 78∘78^\circ78∘ and co-interior 102∘102^\circ102∘.

  • Rectangle and square

    P=2(l+w),A=lwPsquare=4s,Asquare=s2\begin{gathered} P = 2(l + w), \quad A = lw \\ P_{\text{square}} = 4s, \quad A_{\text{square}} = s^2 \end{gathered}P=2(l+w),A=lwPsquare​=4s,Asquare​=s2​

    Use when l,wl,wl,w are a rectangle's side lengths and sss is a square's side length. Use one length unit throughout. Any polygon's perimeter is the sum of its boundary sides. For area conversions, square the length factor: 1 m2=10,000 cm21\text{ m}^2=10{,}000\text{ cm}^21 m2=10,000 cm2.

  • Area of a parallelogram

    A=b×hA = b \times hA=b×h

    Use when bbb is the chosen base and hhh is the perpendicular distance to the opposite side. A side slanting away from the perpendicular is longer than the height.

  • Area of a triangle

    A=12 b hA = \tfrac{1}{2} \, b \, hA=21​bh

    Use when Choose any side as bbb, then use the perpendicular height to that base or its extension. The height may fall outside an obtuse triangle. Every matching base-height choice gives the same area.

  • Area of a trapezoid

    A=12(a+b) h=a+b2×hA = \tfrac{1}{2}(a + b)\,h = \frac{a + b}{2} \times hA=21​(a+b)h=2a+b​×h

    Use when a,ba,ba,b are parallel side lengths and hhh is the perpendicular distance between them. Average the parallel sides, then multiply by the height. The inclusive definition requires at least one pair of parallel sides, so parallelograms are trapezoids.

  • Circumference, and d=2rd = 2rd=2r

    d=2r,C=πd=2πrd = 2r, \qquad C = \pi d = 2\pi rd=2r,C=πd=2πr

    Use when Use the diameter ddd or radius rrr as the formula requires. Circumference measures the whole circle in linear units. A part-circle's full boundary may also include straight edges.

  • Pi

    π=Cd≈3.14\pi = \frac{C}{d} \approx 3.14π=dC​≈3.14

    Use when Every circle has the same ratio C/dC/dC/d. Pi is irrational: its decimal never ends or settles into a repeating pattern. With exact measurements, keeping π\piπ gives an exact answer. Treat results from approximate measurements or substitutions such as 3.143.143.14 and 227\tfrac{22}{7}722​ as approximate, using ≈\approx≈.

  • Area of a circle and a semicircle

    A=πr2Asemi=12πr2Psemi=πr+2r\begin{gathered} A = \pi r^2 \\ A_{\text{semi}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2 \\ P_{\text{semi}} = \pi r + 2r \end{gathered}A=πr2Asemi​=21​πr2Psemi​=πr+2r​

    Use when Square the radius before multiplying by π\piπ. Halve a given diameter first; using it as the radius makes the area four times too large. A semicircle has half the circle's area, but its perimeter includes the straight diameter 2r2r2r.

  • Volume of a prism or a cylinder

    V=B×hVbox=lwh,Vcube=s3Vcylinder=πr2h\begin{gathered} V = B \times h \\ V_{\text{box}} = lwh, \quad V_{\text{cube}} = s^3 \\ V_{\text{cylinder}} = \pi r^2 h \end{gathered}V=B×hVbox​=lwh,Vcube​=s3Vcylinder​=πr2h​

    Use when BBB is one base's area; hhh is the perpendicular distance between the identical parallel bases. Cross-sections parallel to them stay the same size and shape. A cylinder uses its radius. Convert all lengths to one unit first. For cubic-unit conversions, cube the length factor.

    e.g. Triangular base: base 777 m and height 444 m; prism length 666 m gives B=14 m2B=14\text{ m}^2B=14 m2 and V=84 m3V=84\text{ m}^3V=84 m3.

  • Surface area from a net

    Sbox=2(lw+lh+wh)Scube=6s2Scylinder=2πr2+2πrh\begin{gathered} S_{\text{box}} = 2(lw + lh + wh) \\ S_{\text{cube}} = 6s^2 \\ S_{\text{cylinder}} = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h \end{gathered}Sbox​=2(lw+lh+wh)Scube​=6s2Scylinder​=2πr2+2πrh​

    Use when The box has three pairs of equal faces. For a right circular cylinder, add two circular caps and a rectangle of width 2πr2\pi r2πr and height hhh. These formulas include every outer surface; omit any missing surfaces of an open container. Use square units.

  • Quadrant sign patterns

    I (+,+)II (−,+)III (−,−)IV (+,−)\begin{gathered} \text{I } (+,+) \qquad \text{II } (-,+) \\ \text{III } (-,-) \qquad \text{IV } (+,-) \end{gathered}I (+,+)II (−,+)III (−,−)IV (+,−)​
    Text description

    Coordinate axes with the four quadrants numbered counterclockwise from the upper right, each labeled with the signs of its coordinates.

    Use when Number the quadrants counterclockwise from the upper right. If either coordinate is 000, the point is on an axis and in no quadrant, including the origin.

  • Horizontal and vertical distance

    horizontal ∣x2−x1∣vertical ∣y2−y1∣\begin{gathered} \text{horizontal } |x_2 - x_1| \\ \text{vertical } |y_2 - y_1| \end{gathered}horizontal ∣x2​−x1​∣vertical ∣y2​−y1​∣​

    Use when For a horizontal segment, the y-coordinates match; for a vertical segment, the x-coordinates match. Absolute value makes the distance nonnegative, regardless of subtraction order.

  • Reflections across the axes

    across the x-axis: (x,y)→(x,−y)across the y-axis: (x,y)→(−x,y)\begin{gathered} \text{across the }x\text{-axis: }(x,y)\to(x,-y)\\ \text{across the }y\text{-axis: }(x,y)\to(-x,y) \end{gathered}across the x-axis: (x,y)→(x,−y)across the y-axis: (x,y)→(−x,y)​

    Use when A reflection keeps the coordinate along the mirror axis and changes the sign of the other. A point already on that axis stays in place.

    e.g. Reflecting (3,−5)(3,-5)(3,−5) across the x-axis gives (3,5)(3,5)(3,5); across the y-axis it gives (−3,−5)(-3,-5)(−3,−5).

  • Proportional relationship y=kxy = kxy=kx

    k=yx⟹y=kxk = \frac{y}{x} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y = kxk=xy​⟹y=kx

    Use when For fixed k≠0k\neq0k=0, finding k=y/xk=y/xk=y/x also needs x≠0x\neq0x=0. With every input allowed, the graph is a straight line through (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) and (1,k)(1,k)(1,k). A context may allow only part of it. Positive kkk rises; negative kkk falls. Larger ∣k∣|k|∣k∣ is steeper when graphs share the same axis units and scales.

  • Pythagorean theorem

    a2+b2=c2c=a2+b2b=c2−a2\begin{gathered} a^2 + b^2 = c^2 \\ c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} \\ b = \sqrt{c^2 - a^2} \end{gathered}a2+b2=c2c=a2+b2​b=c2−a2​​

    Use when Use only for right triangles, with ccc the hypotenuse and all lengths in one unit. Take positive roots. When finding a leg, ccc must exceed the known leg. Without a right angle, the equation can fail: sides 2,2,22,2,22,2,2 give 8≠48\neq48=4.

Problem types, step by step

Find an unknown angle from a stated relationship

  1. Identify the relationship: complementary (90∘90^\circ90∘), supplementary or a linear pair (180∘180^\circ180∘), vertical (equal), or filling a turn (360∘360^\circ360∘).
  2. Write an equation using the unknown angle or the expressions given.
  3. Solve, then check the required sum or equality.

e.g. (3x+12)∘(3x + 12)^\circ(3x+12)∘ with x∘x^\circx∘ in a linear pair: 4x+12=1804x + 12 = 1804x+12=180, so x=42x = 42x=42.

Find an angle using parallel lines and a transversal

  1. Confirm the crossed lines are parallel before using these three angle rules.
  2. Identify corresponding, alternate interior or co-interior angles from their positions.
  3. Set corresponding or alternate interior angles equal. Set co-interior angles' sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘, then solve.

e.g. Corresponding angles (x+25)∘(x+25)^\circ(x+25)∘ and 65∘65^\circ65∘: x+25=65x+25=65x+25=65, so x=40x=40x=40 and both angles are 65∘65^\circ65∘.

Find an area, or work back from one to a missing length

  1. Choose the formula: lwlwlw, bhbhbh, 12bh\tfrac12bh21​bh, or 12(a+b)h\tfrac12(a+b)h21​(a+b)h.
  2. Identify the base, or parallel sides, and the matching perpendicular height.
  3. Put lengths in one unit, substitute and give the area in square units.
  4. To find a missing length, undo the operations: for a triangle, double the area and divide by the known base or height. For a missing parallel side of a trapezoid, double the area, divide by hhh, then subtract the known parallel side.

e.g. A triangle of area 54 cm254 \text{ cm}^254 cm2 on base 121212 cm: h=2(54)12=9h = \dfrac{2(54)}{12} = 9h=122(54)​=9 cm.

Find the area or perimeter of a composite figure

  1. Split the region into non-overlapping rectangles, triangles and parts of circles, or subtract a cutout from a larger shape.
  2. Find missing side lengths from the labeled lengths.
  3. Add the pieces' areas or subtract the cutout. A different split can check the answer.
  4. For total perimeter, trace every boundary, including hole boundaries. An outer perimeter counts only the outside boundary. Exclude shared joins between pieces.

e.g. A 999 by 777 rectangle less a 444 by 333 corner: 63−12=5163 - 12 = 5163−12=51.

Find the circumference or the area of a circle

  1. For distance around, use C=πdC=\pi dC=πd or C=2πrC=2\pi rC=2πr. For area, use A=πr2A=\pi r^2A=πr2; halve a given diameter before squaring.
  2. For a semicircle, halve the area. Its full perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter.
  3. With exact measurements, keeping π\piπ gives an exact result. Use ≈\approx≈ when measurements or π\piπ are approximated. Give linear units for lengths and square units for areas.
  4. Working backward: d=C/πd=C/\pid=C/π, r=C/(2π)r=C/(2\pi)r=C/(2π), or r=A/πr=\sqrt{A/\pi}r=A/π​ with the positive root.

e.g. d=20d = 20d=20 cm: r=10r = 10r=10, so C≈62.8C \approx 62.8C≈62.8 cm and A≈314 cm2A \approx 314 \text{ cm}^2A≈314 cm2.

Find the volume or the surface area of a solid

  1. Volume: find base area BBB, multiply by perpendicular height hhh and give cubic units.
  2. For a box's surface area, add lwlwlw, lhlhlh and whwhwh, then double. For a right circular cylinder, add the caps 2πr22\pi r^22πr2 and side 2πrh2\pi rh2πrh. Use square units.
  3. Working backward from volume, use h=V/Bh=V/Bh=V/B. For a cylinder's radius, use r=V/(πh)r=\sqrt{V/(\pi h)}r=V/(πh)​, taking the positive root.

e.g. A closed cube of edge 555 cm: V=125 cm3V = 125 \text{ cm}^3V=125 cm3 but S=6(25)=150 cm2S = 6(25) = 150 \text{ cm}^2S=6(25)=150 cm2.

Plot, read, and measure on the coordinate grid

  1. To plot, start at the origin, move horizontally by the first coordinate and vertically by the second. Negative means left or down.
  2. To read, trace straight to each axis. Use the signs to name a quadrant; points on axes have no quadrant.
  3. For an axis-aligned rectangle, take absolute coordinate differences to find its side lengths. Then use P=2(l+w)P=2(l+w)P=2(l+w) and A=lwA=lwA=lw.

e.g. (−3,5)(-3, 5)(−3,5) is 333 left, 555 up, Quadrant II; corners (−2,1)(-2, 1)(−2,1), (5,1)(5, 1)(5,1), (5,6)(5, 6)(5,6) give P=24P = 24P=24, A=35A = 35A=35.

Test for proportionality, then use y=kxy = kxy=kx

  1. In a table, all ratios y/xy/xy/x for x≠0x\neq0x=0 must equal the same nonzero kkk. Any row with x=0x=0x=0 must have y=0y=0y=0.
  2. On a graph, the points must lie on one straight line through the origin, other than an axis. A context may show only some of that line.
  3. Find k=y/xk=y/xk=y/x from a point with x≠0x\neq0x=0. Use y=kxy=kxy=kx to find an output or divide yyy by kkk to find an input. Give the rate its units.

e.g. 302=755=15\frac{30}{2} = \frac{75}{5} = 15230​=575​=15 liters per minute, so 300300300 liters take 202020 minutes.

Find a missing side of a right triangle, or check a corner

  1. Confirm the right angle and label its opposite side ccc. A ladder forms a right triangle when the wall and ground are perpendicular. Look for hidden right triangles: a rectangle's diagonal, or a perpendicular height drawn from a vertex to a base or its extension.
  2. For the hypotenuse, add the legs' squares. For a leg, subtract the known leg's square from the hypotenuse's square. Take the positive square root.
  3. Keep an irrational root as an exact radical unless rounding is requested. For a nearest-tenth answer, compare the radicand with the square of the midpoint between neighboring tenths.
  4. Place marks aaa and bbb units from the same corner along its two edges. Compare the distance between marks with a2+b2\sqrt{a^2+b^2}a2+b2​. A mismatch rules out a right angle; a match uses the converse, a separate statement.

e.g. Legs 444, 666: c=52=213≈7.2c = \sqrt{52} = 2\sqrt{13} \approx 7.2c=52​=213​≈7.2, as 7.22<52<7.2527.2^2 < 52 < 7.25^27.22<52<7.252.

Exam traps

  • Trap Using the wrong kind of unit, or converting area and volume as though they were lengths.

    Fix Area uses square units; volume uses cubic units. Square or cube the length conversion factor: 1 m2=10,000 cm21\text{ m}^2=10{,}000\text{ cm}^21 m2=10,000 cm2 and 1 m3=1,000,000 cm31\text{ m}^3=1{,}000{,}000\text{ cm}^31 m3=1,000,000 cm3.

  • Trap Using a diameter as the radius in the area formula.

    Fix For d=12d=12d=12 cm, use r=6r=6r=6 cm, giving A≈113.04 cm2A\approx113.04\text{ cm}^2A≈113.04 cm2. Using 121212 instead gives four times that area because the mistaken factor of 222 is squared.

  • Trap Confusing circumference with area.

    Fix Use 2πr2\pi r2πr for distance around and πr2\pi r^2πr2 for space inside. At r=10r=10r=10 cm, these are about 62.862.862.8 cm and 314 cm2314\text{ cm}^2314 cm2.

  • Trap Using a side that is not perpendicular to the base as the height.

    Fix Use the perpendicular distance. A parallelogram with base 999 cm, slanted side 666 cm and height 555 cm has area 45 cm245\text{ cm}^245 cm2, not 54 cm254\text{ cm}^254 cm2.

  • Trap Forgetting the 12\tfrac1221​ for a triangle or trapezoid.

    Fix That doubles the area: two copies of either shape make the parallelogram used to derive its formula.

  • Trap Using half the circumference as a semicircle's full perimeter.

    Fix Add the straight diameter: P=πr+2rP=\pi r+2rP=πr+2r. At r=4r=4r=4 cm, the perimeter is about 20.5620.5620.56 cm; the curve alone is about 12.5612.5612.56 cm.

  • Trap Counting only a closed cylinder's curved surface.

    Fix For a right circular cylinder, add both caps: S=2πr2+2πrhS=2\pi r^2+2\pi rhS=2πr2+2πrh. Use the side alone only for a tube open at both ends.

  • Trap Using one coordinate difference as the distance between diagonal points.

    Fix One absolute difference gives the distance only when the other coordinates match. Points (1,2)(1,2)(1,2) and (4,6)(4,6)(4,6) are neither 333 nor 444 units apart.

  • Trap Calling every straight-line relationship proportional.

    Fix A fee of 444 dollars plus 222 dollars per hour rises evenly, but its ratios differ. Its line meets the y-axis at (0,4)(0,4)(0,4), not the origin.

  • Trap Using x/yx/yx/y for kkk, or trying to find kkk at the origin.

    Fix Use k=y/xk=y/xk=y/x, in output units per input unit. The reversed ratio gives the reciprocal rate. At the origin, 0/00/00/0 is undefined.

  • Trap Drawing the whole line for every situation.

    Fix Use only allowed inputs. Whole pencils give separate dots. A walk allows every elapsed time from start to finish, with no negative elapsed time.

  • Trap Adding squares when the missing side is a leg.

    Fix Subtract from c2c^2c2: hypotenuse 252525, leg 777 gives 625−49=24\sqrt{625-49}=24625−49​=24. Adding would give a side longer than the hypotenuse.

  • Trap Adding side lengths instead of squares, or stopping at the squared length.

    Fix For legs 888 and 151515, calculate c=82+152=289=17c=\sqrt{8^2+15^2}=\sqrt{289}=17c=82+152​=289​=17, not 232323 or 289289289.
