Perimeter and Area

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Fencing a yard means measuring the distance around its edge. Covering that same yard with grass means measuring the space inside. These are different measurements: perimeter and area. Counting squares and rearranging pieces will show us why the area formulas work.

Learning goals

  • Add the side lengths for perimeter, and count unit squares for area
  • Derive the parallelogram, triangle, and trapezoid areas by cutting, sliding, or turning shapes
  • Use the perpendicular height, never the slant side, even when it falls outside the shape
  • Split a composite figure into pieces whose areas and boundary you know
  • Recover a missing length or height by reversing an area formula

Perimeter: the distance around

The perimeter of a shape is the total distance around its boundary. For a polygon, a closed shape made of straight sides, add all the side lengths. If a triangle has sides 555 cm, 666 cm, and 777 cm, its perimeter is

P=5+6+7=18 cm.P = 5 + 6 + 7 = 18 \text{ cm}.P=5+6+7=18 cm.

Perimeter adds lengths, so it is measured in linear units such as centimeters or meters.

Repeated side lengths give us shortcuts. A rectangle is a four-sided shape with four right angles. It has two sides of length lll and two of width www, so

P=l+w+l+w=2l+2w=2(l+w).P = l + w + l + w = 2l + 2w = 2(l + w).P=l+w+l+w=2l+2w=2(l+w).

A square is a rectangle whose four sides are all the same length sss, so the sum is just sss four times:

P=s+s+s+s=4s.P = s + s + s + s = 4s.P=s+s+s+s=4s.

Area: counting unit squares

Area measures how much flat space a shape covers. Its unit is a unit square, a square one unit long on each side. A square one centimeter on each side has an area of one square centimeter, written 1 cm21 \text{ cm}^21 cm2.

Count how many unit squares cover the shape, with no gaps or overlaps. Include parts of squares along the edges: two half-squares, for example, contribute one square unit. This lets us measure shapes with slanted or curved edges as well as rectangles.

A ruler measures length along a line; unit squares measure area across a surface. That is why perimeter uses units such as cm, while area uses square units such as cm2\text{cm}^2cm2.

Area of a rectangle

In the figure above, the squares form 333 rows of 555. Multiplication counts them:

3 rows×5 squares per row=15 squares.3 \text{ rows} \times 5 \text{ squares per row} = 15 \text{ squares}.3 rows×5 squares per row=15 squares.

Why the area of a rectangle is length times width#

A rectangle 666 units long and 444 units wide holds 444 rows of 666 unit squares. The rows cover it without gaps or overlaps, so its area is 4×6=244 \times 6 = 244×6=24 square units.

The same count works for any whole-number lengths lll and www. There are www rows, each holding lll unit squares. Every square is counted once:

A=l+l+⋯+l⏟w rows=w×l=l×w.A = \underbrace{l + l + \cdots + l}_{w \text{ rows}} = w \times l = l \times w.A=w rowsl+l+⋯+l​​=w×l=l×w.

This proves the formula for whole-number side lengths. The formula also holds for lengths that are not whole numbers. For example, a 2.52.52.5-by-333 rectangle holds 333 rows of two whole squares and a half-square, giving 3×2.5=7.53 \times 2.5 = 7.53×2.5=7.5 square units.

∎

A square has length and width sss, so its area is

A=s×s=s2.A = s \times s = s^2.A=s×s=s2.

This is why s2s^2s2 is read ”sss squared”: it gives the area of a square with side length sss.

Use the rectangle below to compare perimeter and area. Change its width and height, and watch whether the two measurements change together.

Build a 444-by-444 square: its perimeter is 161616 units and its area is 161616 square units. Then build a 777-by-111 rectangle: its perimeter is still 161616 units, but its area is 777 square units. The same length of fence can enclose different amounts of ground.

Worked example 1 Perimeter and area of a rectangle

A rectangular garden is 121212 m long and 777 m wide. Find its perimeter and its area.

For the perimeter, use P=2(l+w)P = 2(l + w)P=2(l+w):

P=2(12+7)=2×19=38 m.P = 2(12 + 7) = 2 \times 19 = 38 \text{ m}.P=2(12+7)=2×19=38 m.

For the area, multiply length by width:

A=12×7=84 m2.A = 12 \times 7 = 84 \text{ m}^2.A=12×7=84 m2.

The garden needs 383838 m of fencing and covers 848484 square meters of ground.

Area of a parallelogram

A parallelogram is a four-sided shape whose opposite sides are parallel. To find its area, we can cut and slide a piece to make a rectangle.

Choose one side as the base. Its matching height is the perpendicular distance to the opposite side, measured at a right angle to the base. A slanted side is longer than this distance and cannot replace it in the area calculation.

Why the area of a parallelogram is base times height#

Imagine the pictured parallelogram with a base of 777 units and a height of 444 units. Cut along the dashed height and slide the left triangle to the right. It becomes a 777-by-444 rectangle, with area 282828 square units. Every piece is still present, with no gaps or overlaps.

The same move works with base bbb and height hhh. The cut is perpendicular to the base. The triangle fits the other end because the parallelogram’s opposite sides are equal and parallel. The resulting rectangle has base bbb and height hhh, so both shapes have area

A=b×h.A = b \times h.A=b×h.

This one-cut picture works when the top-left corner lies above the base. If the corner hangs past the base’s far end, more than one cut is needed to rearrange the shape into a rectangle. Its area is still b×hb \times hb×h.

∎

Check your understanding

Answer choices

Every piece is kept, and the pieces fit together with no gaps or overlaps. Moving them changes the outline but not the total space they cover. The triangle's area still counts because it was moved, not discarded.

A rectangle is a parallelogram whose neighboring sides meet at right angles. Its side already gives the perpendicular height, so A=bhA = bhA=bh agrees with A=lwA = lwA=lw.

Worked example 2 Spotting the perpendicular height

A parallelogram has a base of 999 cm. Its slanted side is 666 cm, and the perpendicular height drawn straight down from the top edge to the base is 555 cm. Find its area.

Use the 555 cm perpendicular height:

A=b×h=9×5=45 cm2.A = b \times h = 9 \times 5 = 45 \text{ cm}^2.A=b×h=9×5=45 cm2.

The 666 cm slanted side would be useful for perimeter. Using it for area gives 54 cm254 \text{ cm}^254 cm2, too much because it is longer than the perpendicular height.

Area of a triangle

A triangle has three straight sides. Two identical copies can fit together to make a parallelogram. That lets us use the area rule we just found.

Why the area of a triangle is half the base times the height#

Take a triangle with base 888 units and perpendicular height 333 units. Rotate an identical copy by half a turn and join the copies along a side other than the base, as in the figure. Together they make a parallelogram with base 888 and height 333. Its area is 242424 square units, so each triangle has area 121212 square units.

For any triangle with base bbb and height hhh, the same half-turn (180∘180^\circ180∘) makes each unjoined side parallel to its copy. The outer shape is therefore a parallelogram, with base bbb and height hhh. Its area is bhbhbh, shared equally by the two triangles:

A=12×b×h.A = \frac{1}{2} \times b \times h.A=21​×b×h.

Any side can be the base, provided hhh is the perpendicular height to that chosen side. Each choice measures the same triangle, so each gives the same area.

∎

Draw the height from the opposite corner at a right angle to the base. It may lie inside the triangle without being one of its sides. In a right triangle, which has a right angle, either side meeting at that angle can be the base, and the other is its height.

If an angle at an end of the chosen base is obtuse, extend the base’s line. The height from the opposite corner meets that extension outside the triangle, as shown below. It is still the perpendicular distance to the base’s line.

Worked example 3 Area of a triangle

A triangle has a base of 141414 cm and a perpendicular height of 999 cm to that base. Find its area.

A triangle is half of a parallelogram with the same base and height, so its area is half the base times the height:

A=12×b×h=12×14×9.A = \frac{1}{2} \times b \times h = \frac{1}{2} \times 14 \times 9.A=21​×b×h=21​×14×9.

Multiply the base and height first, then take half:

A=12×126=63 cm2.A = \frac{1}{2} \times 126 = 63 \text{ cm}^2.A=21​×126=63 cm2.

So the triangle covers 636363 square centimeters. Taking half of 141414 first (=7= 7=7) and then multiplying by 999 gives the same 636363, since multiplication can be grouped in either order.

Area of a trapezoid

A trapezoid is a four-sided shape with at least one pair of parallel sides. Call those sides aaa and bbb, and their perpendicular distance apart hhh. As with a triangle, two copies will make a parallelogram whose area we can find.

Why the area of a trapezoid is the average of the parallel sides times the height#

Suppose the parallel sides are 333 units and 777 units, and the height is 555 units. Turn an identical copy upside down and join matching sides that connect the two bases, as shown. The pair makes a parallelogram with base 7+3=107 + 3 = 107+3=10 and height 555. Its area is 505050 square units, so each trapezoid has area 252525 square units.

For parallel sides aaa and bbb, the same turn puts a short side beside a long side along both the top and bottom. The outer sides are parallel because the turn makes each side parallel to its copy. With no gaps or overlaps, the two trapezoids form a parallelogram of base a+ba + ba+b and height hhh. Its area is

(a+b)×h.(a + b) \times h.(a+b)×h.

One trapezoid is half of that:

A=12(a+b) h.A = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)\, h.A=21​(a+b)h.

Reading this as a+b2×h\dfrac{a + b}{2} \times h2a+b​×h gives a useful memory aid: average the two parallel side lengths, then multiply by the perpendicular height.

∎

The formula includes two familiar cases. If the parallel sides are equal, a=ba = ba=b, the shape is a parallelogram. The formula becomes 12(b+b)h=bh\tfrac{1}{2}(b + b)h = bh21​(b+b)h=bh.

If the top side shrinks to a point, the shape becomes a triangle. Setting a=0a = 0a=0 gives 12(0+b)h=12bh\tfrac{1}{2}(0 + b)h = \tfrac{1}{2}bh21​(0+b)h=21​bh. This final shape has three sides, so it is no longer a trapezoid.

Our definition, “at least one pair of parallel sides,” includes parallelograms. Some books require exactly one pair instead, so check the definition a problem uses.

Worked example 4 Area of a trapezoid

A trapezoid has parallel sides of 666 cm and 101010 cm, with a perpendicular height of 444 cm between them. Find its area.

Average the two parallel sides, then multiply by the height:

A=a+b2×h=6+102×4.A = \frac{a + b}{2} \times h = \frac{6 + 10}{2} \times 4.A=2a+b​×h=26+10​×4.

The average of the parallel sides is

6+102=162=8 cm,\frac{6 + 10}{2} = \frac{16}{2} = 8 \text{ cm},26+10​=216​=8 cm,

so the area is

A=8×4=32 cm2.A = 8 \times 4 = 32 \text{ cm}^2.A=8×4=32 cm2.

The trapezoid covers the same space as a rectangle 888 cm wide (the average width) and 444 cm tall.

Composite figures: cut into pieces you know

A composite figure combines simpler shapes. Split it into non-overlapping rectangles and triangles, find their areas, and add. You can also start with a larger shape and subtract a missing piece. Both methods count the space the figure covers.

For perimeter, trace the boundary. The dashed line that splits the L-shape is inside the figure, so it does not count. The edges of the notch do count: they are part of the figure’s boundary.

A hole has real boundary edges too. A 666 m by 444 m rectangle has an outer perimeter of 2(6+4)=202(6+4)=202(6+4)=20 m. If a 222 m by 111 m hole lies entirely inside it, that hole adds 2(2+1)=62(2+1)=62(2+1)=6 m of boundary. The total perimeter is 262626 m, while the outer perimeter is still 202020 m. Check whether the question asks for the outside only or for every boundary.

A caution about units

Converting area units changes both dimensions of the unit square. There are 100100100 centimeters in a meter. A square meter is therefore 100100100 cm by 100100100 cm:

1 m2=100×100=10,000 cm2.1 \text{ m}^2 = 100 \times 100 = 10{,}000 \text{ cm}^2.1 m2=100×100=10,000 cm2.

The area conversion factor is the length conversion factor squared. Converting all lengths to the same unit before finding an area keeps the units clear.

Which statement is correct?

Answer choices

Area counts how many unit squares cover the region, so it uses square units like square meters (m2\text{m}^2m2).

Working a formula backward

If you know the area and need a missing length, use the formula as an equation. Substitute the known values, then undo the operations as you did when solving equations in Chapter 8: undo the last operation first. Check the length by putting it back into the area formula.

Worked example 6 Finding a missing side of a rectangle

A rectangular flag has an area of 45 cm245 \text{ cm}^245 cm2 and is 999 cm wide. Find its length.

Let lll be the length in centimeters. The rectangle rule gives an equation:

9l=45.9l = 45.9l=45.

The left side does one thing to lll: it multiplies by 999. Undo that by dividing both sides by 999:

l=459=5 cm.l = \frac{45}{9} = 5 \text{ cm}.l=945​=5 cm.

Check: 9×5=45 cm29 \times 5 = 45 \text{ cm}^29×5=45 cm2, the given area.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Confusing perimeter with area. Perimeter measures the boundary in linear units (cm, m). Area measures the space inside in square units (cm2\text{cm}^2cm2, m2\text{m}^2m2).
  • Using the slant instead of the perpendicular height. For a parallelogram, triangle, or trapezoid, the height is the perpendicular distance to the base, not the slanted side. The slant is longer and overstates the area.
  • Forgetting to halve. Both the triangle and trapezoid formulas come from two copies. Omitting the 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​ doubles the area. When finding a triangle’s base from its area, double the area before dividing by the height.
  • Converting square units like ordinary lengths. 1 m2=10,000 cm21 \text{ m}^2 = 10{,}000 \text{ cm}^21 m2=10,000 cm2, not 100100100. Square the length conversion factor, or convert all lengths to one unit before finding the area.
  • Counting an internal cut line as part of the perimeter. When you split a composite figure to find its area, the cut is not part of the boundary. Trace the actual edges, including any notch.

Key takeaways

  • Perimeter is the distance around a polygon: add the side lengths. Rectangle P=2(l+w)P = 2(l + w)P=2(l+w); square P=4sP = 4sP=4s. It is a length, measured in linear units.
  • Area counts unit squares. Rectangle A=lwA = lwA=lw; square A=s2A = s^2A=s2. Area conversions use the length conversion factor squared.
  • Rearranging shapes: parallelogram A=bhA = bhA=bh; triangle A=12bhA = \tfrac{1}{2}bhA=21​bh; trapezoid A=12(a+b)hA = \tfrac{1}{2}(a+b)hA=21​(a+b)h. Use the perpendicular height to the chosen base. In an obtuse triangle, that height can fall outside the shape.
  • Composite figures: split into rectangles and triangles, find each area, and add (or subtract a missing piece from a full rectangle). Perimeter includes all boundary edges, including the edges around a hole. Shared joins used to split the figure into pieces do not count.
  • Working backward: when the area is known and a length is not, treat the formula as an equation and undo its operations from the outside in.

A bit of history (optional)

How do you measure a field when nobody has written down a formula yet?

In medieval England, an acre was roughly the amount of ground a team of oxen could plow in a day. It measured a day’s work before it became a fixed area.

That depends on the plow, so around the year 130013001300 a royal statute defined a standard acre: a strip twenty-two yards wide and two hundred and twenty yards long. The long side had a name of its own, the furlong, meaning the length of a furrow. (Local customs kept their own versions of the acre for centuries after, but this is the shape the statute settled on.)

Why so long and thin? Turning a heavy plow and its oxen was awkward. Long strips meant fewer turns.

A square acre and that long strip cover the same amount of ground, but the strip needs much more fencing. The shape that suited a plow was not the shape that used the least fence.

The North Yorkshire Archives account of field measurements describes the acre’s connection to plowing and the dimensions of these strips.
