  1. Problem 1 The unfinished drawing

    Complete the figure by drawing a diameter with TTT as one endpoint and labeling its other endpoint UUU.

    Text description of this figure

    A circle with a dot at its center, labeled K. A point labeled T lies on the circle on its upper right, about halfway between the top of the circle and its rightmost point. A straight segment joins K to T. No other points, segments or lengths are drawn or labeled.

    1. Hint 1

      A diameter passes through the center and reaches the circle at both ends.

    2. Hint 2

      Segment KTKTKT is one radius. A diameter needs a second radius from KKK that makes a straight line with it.

    Answer

    Draw TUTUTU through KKK, with UUU on the circle opposite TTT.

    Full solution

    The center is KKK.

    A diameter starting at TTT must pass through KKK and continue to the other side of the circle.

    Put UUU at that second intersection and draw segment TUTUTU.

    The two parts TKTKTK and KUKUKU are radii of equal length, so the completed segment crosses the entire circle through its center.

    Answer

    Draw TUTUTU through KKK, with UUU on the circle opposite TTT.

    Key idea

    A diameter consists of two radii extending in opposite directions from the center.

  2. Problem 2 A pair of lengths

    A circle's diameter is 6 cm longer than its radius. Find the radius.

    1. Hint 1

      The diameter contains two copies of the radius.

    2. Hint 2

      If the radius is rrr cm, compare 2r2r2r with r+6r+6r+6.

    Answer

    6 cm.

    Full solution

    Let the radius be rrr cm.

    The diameter is both 2r2r2r cm and (r+6)(r+6)(r+6) cm.

    Equate the two descriptions and subtract rrr.

    2r=r+6r=6\begin{aligned}2r&=r+6\\r&=6\end{aligned}2rr​=r+6=6​

    A radius of 6 cm gives a diameter of 12 cm, which is 6 cm longer than the radius.

    Answer

    6 cm.

    Key idea

    The difference between a diameter and its radius is one radius.

  3. Problem 3 Pieces of the boundary

    Three arcs make up an entire circle without overlap. Their lengths are 5π5\pi5π cm, 2π2\pi2π cm, and π\piπ cm. Find the diameter.

    1. Hint 1

      The lengths of all the arcs add to the circumference.

    2. Hint 2

      The circumference is π\piπ times the diameter, so divide the total arc length by π\piπ.

    Answer

    8 cm.

    Full solution

    Add the three arc lengths to get the circumference in cm.

    C=5π+2π+πC=8π\begin{aligned}C&=5\pi+2\pi+\pi\\C&=8\pi\end{aligned}CC​=5π+2π+π=8π​

    Since C=πdC=\pi dC=πd and π\piπ is positive, divide by π\piπ.

    d=8ππd=8\begin{aligned}d&=\frac{8\pi}{\pi}\\d&=8\end{aligned}dd​=π8π​=8​

    A diameter of 8 cm has circumference 8π8\pi8π cm, equal to the three arcs together.

    Answer

    8 cm.

    Key idea

    Arcs that together make up a whole circle, without overlap, add to its circumference.

  4. Problem 4 The badge outline

    The figure shows a badge formed by a triangle and a semicircle. Find the area of the badge and its outer perimeter. Give exact answers in terms of π\piπ, keeping π\piπ as a symbol (for example, (2+5π)(2+5\pi)(2+5π) cm).

    Text description of this figure

    A badge shape, lightly shaded. A horizontal segment AB is drawn dashed across the middle of the badge, with A at the left end, B at the right end, and a point M between them; matching tick marks show that AM and MB are equal. Above AB is triangle ABC, with C directly above M. Sides AC and BC are each labeled 5 cm. A dashed segment runs from C straight down to M, is labeled 4 cm, and carries a small square showing a right angle with AB. Thin lines drop straight down from A and B past the bottom of the semicircle, and a bracket between them, below the badge, marks the distance from A to B as 6 cm. Below AB is a semicircle that has AB as its diameter, so the triangle and the semicircle share AB. The outer edge of the badge is the two sides AC and BC and the curved edge of the semicircle.

    1. Hint 1

      The shared straight edge separates the two regions but is inside the badge.

    2. Hint 2

      The semicircle's diameter is ABABAB; half of it is the radius.

    3. Hint 3

      Add the triangle and semicircle areas, then trace just the two exposed triangle sides and the curved edge.

    Answer

    Area (12+9π2)(12+\frac{9\pi}{2})(12+29π​) square cm, or (12+4.5π)(12+4.5\pi)(12+4.5π) square cm; perimeter (10+3π)(10+3\pi)(10+3π) cm.

    Full solution

    The semicircle has diameter ABABAB, which is 6 cm, so its radius is 3 cm.

    The triangle has base 6 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm.

    Its area AtA_tAt​, in square cm, is

    At=12(6)(4)At=12\begin{aligned}A_t&=\frac{1}{2}(6)(4)\\A_t&=12\end{aligned}At​At​​=21​(6)(4)=12​

    The semicircle area AsA_sAs​, in square cm, is

    As=12π(3)2As=9π2\begin{aligned}A_s&=\frac{1}{2}\pi(3)^2\\A_s&=\frac{9\pi}{2}\end{aligned}As​As​​=21​π(3)2=29π​​

    The total area is (12+9π2)(12+\frac{9\pi}{2})(12+29π​) square cm.

    The shared diameter is inside the badge, so it is not part of the outer perimeter.

    The curved edge is half the circumference of a circle of radius 3 cm, so its length is 12(2π)(3)=3π\frac{1}{2}(2\pi)(3)=3\pi21​(2π)(3)=3π cm.

    Add it to the two sides of length 5 cm.

    P=5+5+3πP=10+3π\begin{aligned}P&=5+5+3\pi\\P&=10+3\pi\end{aligned}PP​=5+5+3π=10+3π​

    The perimeter is (10+3π)(10+3\pi)(10+3π) cm.

    Answer

    Area (12+9π2)(12+\frac{9\pi}{2})(12+29π​) square cm, or (12+4.5π)(12+4.5\pi)(12+4.5π) square cm; perimeter (10+3π)(10+3\pi)(10+3π) cm.

    Key idea

    A shared edge contributes to the separate pieces but does not belong to the outer boundary of the combined shape.

  5. Problem 5 The paper disk

    A circular paper disk has diameter 22 cm. It is cut along one diameter into two semicircles, with no paper lost. Find the area of each piece and the increase in the total boundary length of the two separate pieces. Use π≈3.14\pi\approx3.14π≈3.14.

    1. Hint 1

      Cutting preserves area but creates new straight edges.

    2. Hint 2

      Each piece has half the original area, while each gets one straight edge as long as the diameter.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare the total of the two semicircle perimeters with the original circumference.

    Answer

    About 189.97 square cm per piece; the total boundary length increases by 44 cm.

    Full solution

    The radius is 11 cm.

    Halving the disk area gives the area AAA of each piece, in square cm.

    A≈12(3.14)(11)2A≈189.97\begin{aligned}A&\approx\frac{1}{2}(3.14)(11)^2\\A&\approx189.97\end{aligned}AA​≈21​(3.14)(11)2≈189.97​

    The two curved edges together are the original circumference, about 69.08 cm.

    Each semicircle also has a straight edge of 22 cm.

    Thus the new total boundary length PPP, in cm, is

    P≈69.08+2(22)P≈113.08\begin{aligned}P&\approx69.08+2(22)\\P&\approx113.08\end{aligned}PP​≈69.08+2(22)≈113.08​

    Subtracting the old circumference leaves an increase of exactly 44 cm, because the curved edges have the same total length as before.

    The two areas add to about 379.94 square cm, the original disk area.

    Answer

    About 189.97 square cm per piece; the total boundary length increases by 44 cm.

    Key idea

    A cut can preserve the total area while adding boundary to the separate pieces.

  6. Problem 6 Two sign designs

    A maker can use at most 30 cm of edging and at most 60 square cm of material for one circular sign. Design A has radius 3.4 cm, and design B has radius 4.6 cm. Which design meets both limits? Give the edging length and material area for each design, using π≈3.14\pi\approx3.14π≈3.14 and rounding to the nearest hundredth.

    1. Hint 1

      The edging follows the boundary, while the material fills the circular region.

    2. Hint 2

      For each radius, calculate both 2πr2\pi r2πr and πr2\pi r^2πr2.

    3. Hint 3

      A design qualifies only if both of its measurements are within the stated limits.

    Answer

    Design A. A: about 21.35 cm and 36.30 square cm. B: about 28.89 cm and 66.44 square cm.

    Full solution

    For design A, use radius 3.4 cm.

    The circumference CAC_ACA​ is in cm and the area AAA_AAA​ is in square cm.

    CA≈2(3.14)(3.4)CA≈21.352\begin{aligned}C_A&\approx2(3.14)(3.4)\\C_A&\approx21.352\end{aligned}CA​CA​​≈2(3.14)(3.4)≈21.352​

    This rounds to 21.35 cm.

    AA≈3.14(3.4)2AA≈36.2984\begin{aligned}A_A&\approx3.14(3.4)^2\\A_A&\approx36.2984\end{aligned}AA​AA​​≈3.14(3.4)2≈36.2984​

    This rounds to 36.30 square cm.

    Both measurements are within the limits.

    For design B, use radius 4.6 cm.

    CB≈2(3.14)(4.6)CB≈28.888\begin{aligned}C_B&\approx2(3.14)(4.6)\\C_B&\approx28.888\end{aligned}CB​CB​​≈2(3.14)(4.6)≈28.888​

    This rounds to 28.89 cm.

    AB≈3.14(4.6)2AB≈66.4424\begin{aligned}A_B&\approx3.14(4.6)^2\\A_B&\approx66.4424\end{aligned}AB​AB​​≈3.14(4.6)2≈66.4424​

    This rounds to 66.44 square cm.

    Its edging fits the 30 cm limit, but its area exceeds 60 square cm.

    Only design A meets both limits.

    Answer

    Design A. A: about 21.35 cm and 36.30 square cm. B: about 28.89 cm and 66.44 square cm.

    A circular design must satisfy its boundary and area requirements separately.

  7. Problem 7 The circular frame

    The shaded frame in the figure has area (25π−24)(25\pi-24)(25π−24) square cm. The rectangular opening lies entirely inside the circle. Find its height hhh.

    Text description of this figure

    A circle with its center marked O. A horizontal radius runs from O to the right edge of the circle and is labeled 5 cm. A rectangle with horizontal and vertical sides is centered at O and lies entirely inside the circle. A bracket below the rectangle marks its width as 4 cm, and a bracket to its left marks its height as h cm. The top right corner of the rectangle has a small square marking a right angle. The part of the disk outside the rectangle is shaded, forming the frame, and the rectangular opening is unshaded. The drawing is not to scale.

    1. Hint 1

      The frame and the opening together fill the disk.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the disk area, then subtract the stated frame area to get the opening area.

    3. Hint 3

      Divide the opening area by its width to recover the height.

    Answer

    h=6h=6h=6 cm.

    Full solution

    The radius is 5 cm, so the full disk area is 25π25\pi25π square cm.

    Subtract the frame area to get the rectangular opening area AAA, in square cm.

    A=25π−(25π−24)A=24\begin{aligned}A&=25\pi-(25\pi-24)\\A&=24\end{aligned}AA​=25π−(25π−24)=24​

    The opening is 4 cm wide, so its area is 4h4h4h square cm.

    4h=24h=6\begin{aligned}4h&=24\\h&=6\end{aligned}4hh​=24=6​

    A 4 cm by 6 cm opening has area 24 square cm, leaving the stated frame area.

    Answer

    h=6h=6h=6 cm.

    Key idea

    Recover a missing dimension of a cutout by finding its area before reversing its area formula.

  8. Problem 8 Two rows of pieces

    The figure illustrates a disk of radius rrr, with r>0r>0r>0, cut into equal sectors and rearranged into two matching strips. Each strip uses half the sectors, pointing up and down in turn. No pieces overlap or are lost.

    As the sectors get thinner, Lena says each strip approaches a parallelogram with base πr\pi rπr and height rrr, so the disk has area 2πr22\pi r^22πr2. Is her conclusion correct? Explain by finding the length that each strip's base gets closer to as the sectors get thinner, and deriving the disk area.

    Text description of this figure

    At the top, a disk is divided into 16 equal pie-slice sectors by 16 radii drawn from its center, with the sectors shaded in two alternating tints. One radius is labeled r. Below the disk are two separate, matching strips, labeled I and II, one under the other. Each strip is a row of eight of the sectors, drawn the same size as in the disk, placed side by side and pointing up and down in turn, so that neighboring pieces meet along their straight edges without overlapping. Each piece keeps its curved edge: the pieces pointing up have their curved edges along the bottom of the strip, and the pieces pointing down have their curved edges along the top. No lengths other than r are labeled.

    1. Hint 1

      The two strips must share all the curved edges from the original disk.

    2. Hint 2

      Each strip contains half the sectors, and half of its curved edges lie along its lower boundary.

    3. Hint 3

      Find the area of the parallelogram that one strip approaches, then account for both strips.

    Answer

    No. Each base approaches πr2\frac{\pi r}{2}2πr​, and the disk area is πr2\pi r^2πr2.

    Full solution

    The original boundary has length 2πr2\pi r2πr.

    Each strip receives half of those curved edges, and half of that share runs along its bottom.

    The length bbb that the base approaches is therefore

    b=14(2πr)b=πr2\begin{aligned}b&=\frac{1}{4}(2\pi r)\\b&=\frac{\pi r}{2}\end{aligned}bb​=41​(2πr)=2πr​​

    The height approaches rrr.

    Rearranging preserves area, so the disk area is the total area of the two parallelograms the strips approach.

    A=2(πr2)rA=πr2\begin{aligned}A&=2\left(\frac{\pi r}{2}\right)r\\A&=\pi r^2\end{aligned}AA​=2(2πr​)r=πr2​

    Lena gave each strip the base belonging to one strip made from all the sectors, so her total is twice the disk area.

    Answer

    No. Each base approaches πr2\frac{\pi r}{2}2πr​, and the disk area is πr2\pi r^2πr2.

    Key idea

    When pieces are divided among several strips, each strip receives only its share of the original boundary.

  9. Problem 9 A circle in mixed units

    A circle has circumference 15π15\pi15π cm and diameter 150 mm. There are 10 mm in 1 cm. Omar divides 15π15\pi15π by 150 and says the circle has a circumference-to-diameter ratio different from π\piπ. Is Omar correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      The ratio defining π\piπ compares two lengths measured in the same unit.

    2. Hint 2

      Convert the diameter to cm before dividing the numerical measurements.

    Answer

    No. The circumference-to-diameter ratio is π\piπ.

    Full solution

    Convert the diameter from mm to cm.

    150÷10=15150\div10=15150÷10=15

    The diameter is 15 cm, so the ratio of the two lengths is

    15π15=π\frac{15\pi}{15}=\pi1515π​=π

    Omar divided numbers recorded in different units.

    His calculation does not show a different value of π\piπ; using one unit gives the same ratio that every circle has.

    Answer

    No. The circumference-to-diameter ratio is π\piπ.

    Key idea

    The fixed circumference-to-diameter ratio uses lengths expressed in the same unit.

  10. Problem 10 The pair of circles

    Two circles have positive radii whose sum is 10 cm. Kai says that the sum of their circumferences is fixed even though neither individual radius is known. Is Kai correct? Justify your decision and give that sum if it is fixed.

    1. Hint 1

      Write each circumference in terms of its own radius.

    2. Hint 2

      Look for a common factor in the sum of the two circumference expressions.

    Answer

    Yes; the sum is 20π20\pi20π cm.

    Full solution

    Let the radii in cm be aaa and bbb, so a+b=10a+b=10a+b=10.

    The circumference sum CCC, in cm, is

    C=2πa+2πbC=2π(a+b)C=20π\begin{aligned}C&=2\pi a+2\pi b\\C&=2\pi(a+b)\\C&=20\pi\end{aligned}CCC​=2πa+2πb=2π(a+b)=20π​

    This depends on the radius sum, not on the separate radii, so Kai is correct.

    For example, radii 4 cm and 6 cm give circumferences 8π8\pi8π cm and 12π12\pi12π cm.

    Their sum is 20π20\pi20π cm, consistent with the result.

    Answer

    Yes; the sum is 20π20\pi20π cm.

    Key idea

    Adding circle circumferences is equivalent to multiplying the sum of the radii by 2π2\pi2π.
