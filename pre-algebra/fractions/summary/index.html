Fractions: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Numerator and denominator
In ab\frac{a}{b}ba​, the denominator bbb names the SIZE of each part (the whole was cut into bbb EQUAL parts) and the numerator aaa COUNTS how many of them you have.
Unit fraction 1b\frac{1}{b}b1​
One single equal part. Every fraction is a count of these: ab\frac{a}{b}ba​ is aaa copies of 1b\frac{1}{b}b1​.
Proper fraction
Numerator smaller than the denominator, so the value is less than 111.
Improper fraction
Numerator at least the denominator, so the value is 111 or more. 44\frac{4}{4}44​ is improper yet equals exactly 111; it is a correct value on its own, though a problem that specifically asks for a mixed number still expects that form.
Equivalent fractions
Two fractions naming the same number, such as 12\frac{1}{2}21​ and 24\frac{2}{4}42​. They share one lowest-terms form.
Lowest terms (simplest form)
The numerator and denominator share no common factor above 111, so nothing is left to divide out.
Mixed number wabw\tfrac{a}{b}wba​
A whole number beside a proper fraction, standing for their SUM: wab=w+abw\tfrac{a}{b} = w + \tfrac{a}{b}wba​=w+ba​. The space means plus, not times.

Formulas and theorems

  • The bar means divide

    ab=a÷b\frac{a}{b} = a \div bba​=a÷b

    Use when b≠0b \neq 0b=0. Top divided by bottom, so 34\frac{3}{4}43​ is 3÷43 \div 43÷4, never 4÷34 \div 34÷3.

    e.g. 5÷8=585 \div 8 = \frac{5}{8}5÷8=85​, and 123=4\frac{12}{3} = 4312​=4.

  • Zero and one inside a fraction

    nn=1,n1=n0b=0,a0 is undefined\begin{gathered} \frac{n}{n} = 1, \qquad \frac{n}{1} = n \\ \frac{0}{b} = 0, \qquad \frac{a}{0} \text{ is undefined} \end{gathered}nn​=1,1n​=nb0​=0,0a​ is undefined​

    Use when n≠0n \neq 0n=0 for nn=1\frac{n}{n} = 1nn​=1, since 00\frac{0}{0}00​ is undefined; n1=n\frac{n}{1} = n1n​=n holds for every whole number nnn, including 000. b≠0b \neq 0b=0 in the third. A numerator of 000 is fine; a denominator of 000 is not, since no number times 000 returns a nonzero aaa.

  • Comparing at a glance

    Same denominator: the larger NUMERATOR is larger. Same numerator: the SMALLER denominator is larger, because fewer cuts make bigger parts.

    Text description

    Two equal bars each with three parts shaded: the quarters on the upper bar cover far more length than the sevenths on the lower one.

    Use when One of the two numbers must genuinely match. A shared numerator must be nonzero, since 03\frac{0}{3}30​ and 05\frac{0}{5}50​ both equal 000.

    e.g. 58>38\frac{5}{8} > \frac{3}{8}85​>83​, and 34>37\frac{3}{4} > \frac{3}{7}43​>73​.

  • Building rule

    ab=a×cb×c\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times c}ba​=b×ca×c​
    Text description

    Two equal bars, one cut into halves with one part shaded and one cut into quarters with two shaded, whose shadings stop at the same point.

    Use when b≠0b \neq 0b=0 and c≠0c \neq 0c=0. The multiplier hits BOTH numbers, and it multiplies rather than adds: 1+12+1\frac{1+1}{2+1}2+11+1​ is 23\frac{2}{3}32​, a different number from 12\frac{1}{2}21​.

  • Simplifying rule

    ab=a÷cb÷c\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \div c}{b \div c}ba​=b÷ca÷c​

    Use when ccc must be a common factor, dividing BOTH aaa and bbb exactly, with c≠0c \neq 0c=0 and b≠0b \neq 0b=0. Dividing by the greatest common factor reaches lowest terms in one step.

  • Cross-product test for equality

    ab=cdexactly whena×d=b×c\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d} \quad \text{exactly when} \quad a \times d = b \times cba​=dc​exactly whena×d=b×c

    Use when b≠0b \neq 0b=0 and d≠0d \neq 0d=0. It runs both ways, so differing products prove the fractions differ. Each numerator pairs with the OTHER denominator, so a×ba \times ba×b tests nothing.

    e.g. 610\frac{6}{10}106​ and 915\frac{9}{15}159​: 6×15=90=10×96 \times 15 = 90 = 10 \times 96×15=90=10×9, so they are equal.

  • Like-denominator rule

    ac+bc=a+bc,ac−bc=a−bc\frac{a}{c} + \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a + b}{c}, \qquad \frac{a}{c} - \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a - b}{c}ca​+cb​=ca+b​,ca​−cb​=ca−b​

    Use when c≠0c \neq 0c=0, and the SAME ccc in both. The denominator never moves, and only the numerators combine.

    e.g. 911−411=511\frac{9}{11} - \frac{4}{11} = \frac{5}{11}119​−114​=115​.

  • Least common denominator

    LCD of ab and cd=lcm⁡(b,d)\text{LCD of } \tfrac{a}{b} \text{ and } \tfrac{c}{d} = \operatorname{lcm}(b, d)LCD of ba​ and dc​=lcm(b,d)

    Use when The denominators are whole numbers, so the LCD is a whole NUMBER, with b≠0b \neq 0b=0 and d≠0d \neq 0d=0. Any common multiple works, including b×db \times db×d; the least one only keeps the numbers small. When one denominator divides the other, the LCD is the larger one.

    e.g. lcm⁡(6,8)=24\operatorname{lcm}(6, 8) = 24lcm(6,8)=24, but lcm⁡(6,12)=12\operatorname{lcm}(6, 12) = 12lcm(6,12)=12.

  • Multiplication rule

    ab×cd=a×cb×d\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times d}ba​×dc​=b×da×c​

    Use when b≠0b \neq 0b=0 and d≠0d \neq 0d=0. No common denominator is wanted or needed. A whole number joins as n=n1n = \frac{n}{1}n=1n​.

  • Cancelling before multiplying

    A factor shared by any numerator and any denominator of the product divides out of both first, taken from either fraction.

    Use when The cancel pairs a NUMERATOR with a DENOMINATOR, never a top with a top. It must divide the WHOLE numerator, never one term of a sum: in a+cb\frac{a + c}{b}ba+c​ a factor of aaa alone cancels nothing.

    e.g. 29×38\frac{2}{9} \times \frac{3}{8}92​×83​: cancel 222 with 888 and 333 with 999, leaving 13×14=112\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{12}31​×41​=121​.

  • A fraction times its reciprocal

    ab×ba=1\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{b}{a} = 1ba​×ab​=1

    Use when The reciprocal swaps numerator and denominator, so it needs a≠0a \neq 0a=0 as well as b≠0b \neq 0b=0: flipping 0b\frac{0}{b}b0​ would put 000 underneath. Every number but 000 has one.

    e.g. 35×53=1\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{5}{3} = 153​×35​=1, and the reciprocal of 6=616 = \frac{6}{1}6=16​ is 16\frac{1}{6}61​.

  • Division rule (flip the divisor)

    ab÷cd=ab×dc\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}ba​÷dc​=ba​×cd​

    Use when b≠0b \neq 0b=0, d≠0d \neq 0d=0, and the divisor must be nonzero, so c≠0c \neq 0c=0. ONLY the divisor flips; the first fraction is left exactly as it is.

  • Mixed number and improper fraction

    wab=w×b+abnd=qrd   where   n=q×d+r\begin{gathered} w\tfrac{a}{b} = \frac{w \times b + a}{b} \\ \frac{n}{d} = q\tfrac{r}{d} \;\text{ where }\; n = q \times d + r \end{gathered}wba​=bw×b+a​dn​=qdr​ where n=q×d+r​
    Text description

    Two whole bars cut into quarters plus a third bar with three of its quarters shaded, numbering eleven shaded quarters in all.

    Use when b≠0b \neq 0b=0, d≠0d \neq 0d=0, and a<ba < ba<b in a mixed number's finished form, so its fraction part is proper there. The denominator never changes in either direction. A remainder is always below the divisor, so 0≤r<d0 \le r < d0≤r<d; when r=0r = 0r=0 the value is the whole number qqq. A borrow can briefly leave the fraction part improper partway through a computation; finishing the addition or subtraction brings it back to proper before the result is final.

Problem types, step by step

Name a fraction from a picture, a story, or a number line

  1. Count the equal parts the whole was cut into for the denominator, and the parts taken for the numerator.
  2. To place it, split the segment from 000 to 111 into bbb equal steps and count aaa of them, continuing past 111 for an improper fraction.
  3. Check the size: numerator below denominator lands before 111, equal on 111, above past 111.

e.g. A bar in 888 equal parts with 555 shaded is 58\frac{5}{8}85​, five steps along a 000-to-111 segment cut into eighths.

Find a fraction of a quantity

  1. Divide the quantity by the denominator for the size of one part, then multiply by the numerator.
  2. For "how much is left", subtract from the total, or take the leftover fraction instead.

e.g. 35\frac{3}{5}53​ of 454545: 45÷5=945 \div 5 = 945÷5=9, then 3×9=273 \times 9 = 273×9=27.

Compare or order fractions

  1. Take the matching-denominator or matching-numerator shortcut when one applies.
  2. Otherwise rewrite every fraction over one common denominator and compare the new numerators.
  3. List the ORIGINAL fractions in the answer, not the rewritten ones.

e.g. 56\frac{5}{6}65​ against 79\frac{7}{9}97​ over 181818: 1518>1418\frac{15}{18} > \frac{14}{18}1815​>1814​, so 56\frac{5}{6}65​ is larger.

Build an equivalent fraction with a required denominator

  1. Ask what the old denominator was multiplied by to reach the new one.
  2. Multiply the numerator by that same number.
  3. If the missing number is the denominator instead, read the multiplier off the two numerators and apply it to the bottom.

e.g. 712=?60\frac{7}{12} = \frac{?}{60}127​=60?​: since 12×5=6012 \times 5 = 6012×5=60, the numerator is 7×5=357 \times 5 = 357×5=35.

Simplify a fraction to lowest terms

  1. Find the greatest common factor of the numerator and denominator, and divide both by it in one step.
  2. Confirm the result shares no factor above 111.
  3. If you divided by a smaller common factor, the answer is correct but unfinished, so simplify again.

e.g. 5490\frac{54}{90}9054​ has GCF⁡=18\operatorname{GCF} = 18GCF=18, so 54÷1890÷18=35\frac{54 \div 18}{90 \div 18} = \frac{3}{5}90÷1854÷18​=53​.

Add or subtract two fractions

  1. Matching denominators: go straight to the numerators. Otherwise find the LCD.
  2. Rebuild each fraction over the LCD, multiplying top and bottom by the same number.
  3. Add or subtract the numerators, keep the common denominator, then simplify.
  4. A whole number enters over the common denominator, so 111 becomes cc\frac{c}{c}cc​.

e.g. 38+512\frac{3}{8} + \frac{5}{12}83​+125​ over the LCD 242424: 924+1024=1924\frac{9}{24} + \frac{10}{24} = \frac{19}{24}249​+2410​=2419​.

Multiply fractions

  1. Write any whole number over 111 and any mixed number as an improper fraction.
  2. Cancel each factor shared by a numerator and a denominator, taken from either fraction.
  3. Multiply the remaining tops together and the remaining bottoms together, then simplify anything missed.

e.g. 512×815\frac{5}{12} \times \frac{8}{15}125​×158​: cancel 444 from 888 and 121212, and 555 from 555 and 151515, giving 13×23=29\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{2}{9}31​×32​=92​.

Divide by a nonzero fraction

  1. Write any whole number over 111 and any mixed number as an improper fraction.
  2. Leave the first fraction alone and replace the nonzero divisor by its reciprocal, turning ÷\div÷ into ×\times×.
  3. Cancel, multiply across, and simplify.

e.g. 710÷1425=710×2514=12×52=54\frac{7}{10} \div \frac{14}{25} = \frac{7}{10} \times \frac{25}{14} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{5}{2} = \frac{5}{4}107​÷2514​=107​×1425​=21​×25​=45​.

Convert between an improper fraction and a mixed number

  1. Improper to mixed: divide numerator by denominator; the quotient is the whole part, the remainder is the new numerator.
  2. Mixed to improper: multiply the whole number by the denominator, then add the numerator.
  3. Keep the denominator either way, simplify the fraction part, and read a remainder of 000 as a plain whole number.

e.g. 476\frac{47}{6}647​: 47÷6=747 \div 6 = 747÷6=7 remainder 555, so 7567\tfrac{5}{6}765​; back the other way, 7×6+5=477 \times 6 + 5 = 477×6+5=47.

Multiply or divide mixed numbers

  1. Convert every mixed number to an improper fraction first, without exception.
  2. Multiply across, or flip the divisor and multiply, cancelling as you go.
  3. Simplify, then convert back to a mixed number if the question asks for that form.

e.g. 334×115=154×65=92=4123\tfrac{3}{4} \times 1\tfrac{1}{5} = \frac{15}{4} \times \frac{6}{5} = \frac{9}{2} = 4\tfrac{1}{2}343​×151​=415​×56​=29​=421​.

Add or subtract mixed numbers, carrying or borrowing

  1. Combine the whole parts, and separately combine the fraction parts over their LCD.
  2. Adding: if the fraction total reaches 111 or more, convert it and carry its whole into the whole-number total.
  3. Subtracting: if the fraction being taken away is the larger one, borrow 111 from the whole part and add it to the fraction as cc\frac{c}{c}cc​ first; the fraction part is briefly improper until the subtraction finishes.
  4. Converting both to improper fractions instead always works and skips the carry and the borrow.

e.g. 514−2235\tfrac{1}{4} - 2\tfrac{2}{3}541​−232​: over 121212 that is 5312−28125\tfrac{3}{12} - 2\tfrac{8}{12}5123​−2128​, so borrow to 41512−2812=27124\tfrac{15}{12} - 2\tfrac{8}{12} = 2\tfrac{7}{12}41215​−2128​=2127​.

Exam traps

  • Trap Adding the denominators along with the numerators, so 25+15\frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{5}52​+51​ is written as 310\frac{3}{10}103​.

    Fix The answer is 35\frac{3}{5}53​: the denominator names the piece size, and combining groups of fifths does not shrink the pieces, so only the counts add.

  • Trap Cancelling a number that sits in a SUM, such as striking the bbb's in w×b+ab\frac{w \times b + a}{b}bw×b+a​.

    Fix Cancelling divides a factor out of the whole numerator, and being one term of a sum does not make a number a factor of it. 234=2×4+34=1142\tfrac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \times 4 + 3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}243​=42×4+3​=411​, and 444 does not divide 111111; cancelling the 444s would claim 2+3=52 + 3 = 52+3=5.

  • Trap Cancelling a numerator against another numerator, or a denominator against another denominator.

    Fix Every cancel pairs one top with one bottom. In 34×38\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{3}{8}43​×83​ the two 333s are both on top, so nothing cancels and the answer is 932\frac{9}{32}329​, not 132\frac{1}{32}321​.

  • Trap Flipping the first fraction in a division, or forgetting to flip and multiplying straight across.

    Fix Only the divisor turns over: 56÷23=56×32=54\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{5}{4}65​÷32​=65​×23​=45​. Straight across gives 59\frac{5}{9}95​, and flipping the first gives 45\frac{4}{5}54​, the reciprocal of the answer.

  • Trap Ordering fractions with a shared numerator by their denominators, smallest denominator first.

    Fix More cuts make smaller pieces, so the order reverses: 34\frac{3}{4}43​, 37\frac{3}{7}73​, 310\frac{3}{10}103​ runs from greatest to least.

  • Trap Calling a fraction simplified after dividing out a common factor that was not the greatest.

    Fix Halving 1824\frac{18}{24}2418​ gives 912\frac{9}{12}129​, which still shares a factor of 333. Divide by the greatest common factor 666 once to reach 34\frac{3}{4}43​.

  • Trap Multiplying mixed numbers part by part, whole against whole and fraction against fraction.

    Fix A mixed number is a sum, and a product of sums is not the product of the parts. 214×1132\tfrac{1}{4} \times 1\tfrac{1}{3}241​×131​ is 94×43=3\frac{9}{4} \times \frac{4}{3} = 349​×34​=3, not the 21122\tfrac{1}{12}2121​ that part-by-part suggests.

  • Trap Subtracting mixed numbers by taking the smaller fraction from the larger regardless of which one you started with.

    Fix Borrow instead. 514−2235\tfrac{1}{4} - 2\tfrac{2}{3}541​−232​ is 27122\tfrac{7}{12}2127​; taking 312\tfrac{3}{12}123​ from 812\tfrac{8}{12}128​ the wrong way round returns 35123\tfrac{5}{12}3125​, too big by 56\tfrac{5}{6}65​.

  • Trap Assuming a product must be larger than its factors and a quotient smaller than what it divides.

    Fix Read ×\times× as "of" and ÷\div÷ as "how many fit". A product of two fractions each between 000 and 111 falls below both, as 34×25=310\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3}{10}43​×52​=103​ does, while 6÷34=86 \div \frac{3}{4} = 86÷43​=8 grows.
