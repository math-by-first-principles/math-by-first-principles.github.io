Fractions: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Numerator and denominator
In ab\frac{a}{b}ba​, the denominator bbb counts the equal parts in one whole, setting their size; the numerator aaa counts the parts taken. Here aaa is a nonnegative whole number and bbb is a positive whole number.
Unit fraction 1b\frac{1}{b}b1​
One equal part of a whole, with positive whole denominator bbb. The fraction ab\frac{a}{b}ba​ counts aaa copies of 1b\frac{1}{b}b1​.
Proper fraction
Numerator smaller than the denominator, so the value is less than 111.
Improper fraction
Numerator at least the positive denominator, so the value is 111 or more. For example, 44\frac4444​ is improper and equals 111. An improper fraction is a correct value as it stands; it need not be converted unless a problem asks for a mixed number.
Equivalent fractions
Two fractions naming the same number, such as 12\frac{1}{2}21​ and 24\frac{2}{4}42​. They share one lowest-terms form.
Lowest terms (simplest form)
The numerator and denominator share no common factor above 111, so nothing is left to divide out.
Mixed number wabw\tfrac{a}{b}wba​
A positive whole number plus a positive proper fraction: wab=w+abw\tfrac{a}{b}=w+\tfrac{a}{b}wba​=w+ba​. The space means plus.

Formulas and theorems

  • The bar means divide

    ab=a÷b\frac{a}{b} = a \div bba​=a÷b

    Use when The denominator is positive. Read numerator divided by denominator: 34\frac3443​ means 3÷43\div43÷4.

    e.g. 5÷8=585 \div 8 = \frac{5}{8}5÷8=85​, and 123=4\frac{12}{3} = 4312​=4.

  • Zero and one inside a fraction

    nn=1(n≠0)n1=n,0b=0(b>0)a0 is undefined\begin{gathered} \frac{n}{n}=1\quad(n\ne0)\\ \frac{n}{1}=n,\qquad\frac{0}{b}=0\quad(b>0)\\ \frac{a}{0}\text{ is undefined} \end{gathered}nn​=1(n=0)1n​=n,b0​=0(b>0)0a​ is undefined​

    Use when For nonzero aaa, no number multiplied by 000 gives aaa. For a=0a=0a=0, every number passes that check, so 0/00/00/0 has no single quotient. The rule n/1=nn/1=nn/1=n includes n=0n=0n=0.

  • Comparing at a glance

    Same denominator: the larger numerator gives the larger value. Same positive numerator: the smaller denominator gives the larger value.

    Text description

    Two equal bars each with three parts shaded: the quarters on the upper bar cover far more length than the sevenths on the lower one. The upper outline is blue and solid; the lower is red and dashed.

    Use when The wholes must be the same size and one term must match. With numerator 000, both fractions equal 000; there is no larger one.

    e.g. 58>38\frac{5}{8} > \frac{3}{8}85​>83​, and 34>37\frac{3}{4} > \frac{3}{7}43​>73​.

  • Building rule

    ab=a×cb×c\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times c}ba​=b×ca×c​
    Text description

    Two equal bars, one cut into halves with one part shaded and one cut into quarters with two shaded, whose shadings stop at the same point. The upper outline is blue and solid; the lower is red and dashed.

    Use when aaa is a nonnegative whole number; b,cb,cb,c are positive whole numbers. Multiply both terms by ccc. Adding the same number to both terms is not this rule: 1+12+1=23≠12\frac{1+1}{2+1}=\frac23\ne\frac122+11+1​=32​=21​.

  • Simplifying rule

    ab=a÷cb÷c\frac{a}{b} = \frac{a \div c}{b \div c}ba​=b÷ca÷c​

    Use when b,cb,cb,c are positive whole numbers and ccc divides both aaa and bbb. For a>0a>0a>0, dividing by the GCF reaches lowest terms. For a=0a=0a=0, the simplest form is 0/10/10/1.

  • Cross-product test for equality

    ab=cdexactly when a×d=b×c\begin{gathered} \frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}\\ \text{exactly when }a\times d=b\times c \end{gathered}ba​=dc​exactly when a×d=b×c​

    Use when b,d>0b,d>0b,d>0. Pair each numerator with the other denominator. Equal cross products mean equal fractions; unequal products mean different values.

    e.g. 610\frac{6}{10}106​ and 915\frac{9}{15}159​: 6×15=90=10×96 \times 15 = 90 = 10 \times 96×15=90=10×9, so they are equal.

  • Like-denominator rule

    ac+bc=a+bcac−bc=a−bc\begin{gathered} \frac{a}{c}+\frac{b}{c}=\frac{a+b}{c}\\ \frac{a}{c}-\frac{b}{c}=\frac{a-b}{c} \end{gathered}ca​+cb​=ca+b​ca​−cb​=ca−b​​

    Use when a,ba,ba,b are nonnegative whole numbers and c>0c>0c>0. For subtraction here, a≥ba\ge ba≥b. Keep the shared denominator while combining; simplifying afterward may change it.

    e.g. 911−411=511\frac{9}{11} - \frac{4}{11} = \frac{5}{11}119​−114​=115​.

  • Least common denominator

    For fractions with denominators bbb and ddd, the LCD is lcm⁡(b,d)\operatorname{lcm}(b,d)lcm(b,d).

    Use when b,db,db,d are positive whole numbers. Any positive common multiple works; the least keeps counts small. If one denominator divides the other, the LCD is the larger one.

    e.g. lcm⁡(6,8)=24\operatorname{lcm}(6, 8) = 24lcm(6,8)=24, but lcm⁡(6,12)=12\operatorname{lcm}(6, 12) = 12lcm(6,12)=12.

  • Multiplication rule

    ab×cd=a×cb×d\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a \times c}{b \times d}ba​×dc​=b×da×c​

    Use when Numerators are nonnegative whole numbers and denominators are positive whole numbers. A common denominator is unnecessary. Write a whole number as n/1n/1n/1.

  • Canceling before multiplying

    Divide a common factor out of one numerator and one denominator before multiplying.

    Use when The factor must divide each selected term exactly and be positive. Never pair two numerators or two denominators. A factor must divide a whole numerator: in a+cb\frac{a+c}{b}ba+c​, a factor of aaa alone cannot cancel.

    e.g. 29×38\frac{2}{9} \times \frac{3}{8}92​×83​: cancel 222 with 888 and 333 with 999, leaving 13×14=112\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{12}31​×41​=121​.

  • A fraction times its reciprocal

    ab×ba=1\frac{a}{b} \times \frac{b}{a} = 1ba​×ab​=1

    Use when a,b>0a,b>0a,b>0. The reciprocal swaps numerator and denominator. Zero has no reciprocal because flipping it would produce a zero denominator.

    e.g. 35×53=1\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{5}{3} = 153​×35​=1, and the reciprocal of 6=616 = \frac{6}{1}6=16​ is 16\frac{1}{6}61​.

  • Division rule (flip the divisor)

    ab÷cd=ab×dc\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b} \times \frac{d}{c}ba​÷dc​=ba​×cd​

    Use when a≥0a\ge0a≥0 and b,c,d>0b,c,d>0b,c,d>0 are whole numbers. The divisor must be nonzero. Flip only the divisor and leave the first fraction unchanged.

  • Mixed number and improper fraction

    wab=w×b+abn=q×d+rnd=q+rd\begin{gathered} w\tfrac{a}{b}=\frac{w\times b+a}{b}\\ n=q\times d+r\\ \frac{n}{d}=q+\frac{r}{d} \end{gathered}wba​=bw×b+a​n=q×d+rdn​=q+dr​​
    Text description

    Two whole bars cut into quarters plus a third bar with three of its quarters shaded, numbering eleven shaded quarters in all.

    Use when Use whole numbers with w>0w>0w>0, 0<a<b0<a<b0<a<b, and n≥d>0n\ge d>0n≥d>0. Division gives 0≤r<d0\le r<d0≤r<d. For r>0r>0r>0, write qrdq\tfrac rdqdr​; for r=0r=0r=0, write qqq. Keep the denominator during conversion, then simplify if needed.

Problem types, step by step

Name a fraction from a picture, a story, or a number line

  1. Count the equal parts the whole was cut into for the denominator, and the parts taken for the numerator.
  2. To place it, split the segment from 000 to 111 into bbb equal steps and count aaa of them, continuing past 111 for an improper fraction.
  3. Check the size: numerator below denominator lands before 111, equal on 111, above past 111.

e.g. A bar in 888 equal parts with 555 shaded is 58\frac{5}{8}85​, five steps along a 000-to-111 segment cut into eighths.

Find a fraction of a quantity

  1. Divide the quantity by the denominator for the size of one part, then multiply by the numerator.
  2. For "how much is left", subtract from the total, or take the leftover fraction instead.

e.g. 35\frac{3}{5}53​ of 454545: 45÷5=945 \div 5 = 945÷5=9, then 3×9=273 \times 9 = 273×9=27.

Compare or order fractions

  1. Use the same-denominator or same-positive-numerator comparison when it applies.
  2. Otherwise rewrite every fraction over a common denominator and compare the new numerators.
  3. List the original fractions in the requested order.

e.g. 56\frac{5}{6}65​ against 79\frac{7}{9}97​ over 181818: 1518>1418\frac{15}{18} > \frac{14}{18}1815​>1814​, so 56\frac{5}{6}65​ is larger.

Build an equivalent fraction with a required denominator

  1. Reduce to lowest terms first. A target can work even when it is not a multiple of the original denominator.
  2. The required positive denominator must be a whole-number multiple of the lowest-terms denominator. Otherwise no whole-number numerator works.
  3. When it is a multiple, multiply the reduced numerator by that same whole-number multiplier.
  4. For a missing denominator, use the multiplier between nonzero numerators. A zero numerator alone does not determine a denominator.

e.g. 712=?60\frac{7}{12} = \frac{?}{60}127​=60?​: since 12×5=6012 \times 5 = 6012×5=60, the numerator is 7×5=357 \times 5 = 357×5=35.

Simplify a fraction to lowest terms

  1. For a nonzero numerator, divide both terms by their GCF. For a zero numerator and positive denominator, use 0/10/10/1.
  2. Check that the terms share no factor above 1.
  3. Dividing by a smaller common factor preserves the value but may require another simplification.

e.g. 5490\frac{54}{90}9054​ has GCF⁡=18\operatorname{GCF} = 18GCF=18, so 54÷1890÷18=35\frac{54 \div 18}{90 \div 18} = \frac{3}{5}90÷1854÷18​=53​.

Add or subtract two fractions

  1. Matching denominators: go straight to the numerators. Otherwise find the LCD.
  2. Rebuild each fraction over the LCD, multiplying top and bottom by the same number.
  3. Add or subtract the numerators, keep the common denominator, then simplify.
  4. A whole number enters over the common denominator, so 111 becomes cc\frac{c}{c}cc​.

e.g. 38+512\frac{3}{8} + \frac{5}{12}83​+125​ over the LCD 242424: 924+1024=1924\frac{9}{24} + \frac{10}{24} = \frac{19}{24}249​+2410​=2419​.

Multiply fractions

  1. Write any whole number over 111 and any mixed number as an improper fraction.
  2. Cancel each factor shared by a numerator and a denominator, taken from either fraction.
  3. Multiply the remaining tops together and the remaining bottoms together, then simplify anything missed.

e.g. 512×815\frac{5}{12} \times \frac{8}{15}125​×158​: cancel 444 from 888 and 121212, and 555 from 555 and 151515, giving 13×23=29\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{2}{9}31​×32​=92​.

Divide by a nonzero fraction

  1. Write any whole number over 111 and any mixed number as an improper fraction.
  2. Leave the first fraction alone and replace the nonzero divisor by its reciprocal, turning ÷\div÷ into ×\times×.
  3. Cancel, multiply across, and simplify.

e.g. 710÷1425=710×2514=12×52=54\frac{7}{10} \div \frac{14}{25} = \frac{7}{10} \times \frac{25}{14} = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{5}{2} = \frac{5}{4}107​÷2514​=107​×1425​=21​×25​=45​.

Convert between an improper fraction and a mixed number

  1. Improper to mixed: divide numerator by denominator; the quotient is the whole part, the remainder is the new numerator.
  2. Mixed to improper: multiply the whole number by the denominator, then add the numerator.
  3. Keep the denominator during conversion, then simplify. A zero remainder gives a whole number.

e.g. 476\frac{47}{6}647​: 47÷6=747 \div 6 = 747÷6=7 remainder 555, so 7567\tfrac{5}{6}765​; back the other way, 7×6+5=477 \times 6 + 5 = 477×6+5=47.

Multiply or divide mixed numbers

  1. Convert each mixed number to an improper fraction to use the fraction multiplication or division rule.
  2. Multiply across, or flip the divisor and multiply, canceling as you go.
  3. Simplify, then convert back to a mixed number if the question asks for that form.

e.g. 334×115=154×65=92=4123\tfrac{3}{4} \times 1\tfrac{1}{5} = \frac{15}{4} \times \frac{6}{5} = \frac{9}{2} = 4\tfrac{1}{2}343​×151​=415​×56​=29​=421​.

Add or subtract mixed numbers, carrying or borrowing

  1. Rewrite the fraction parts over their LCD; add or subtract the whole parts and fraction parts separately, borrowing before subtraction when needed.
  2. For addition, carry any whole from the fraction total into the whole-number total.
  3. For subtraction, if the first fraction part is smaller than the one being removed, subtract 111 from the first whole part and add cc\frac cccc​ to its fraction part, where ccc is the common denominator. Then subtract the parts.
  4. Alternatively, convert both amounts to improper fractions, then compute and convert the answer as requested.

e.g. 715−3237\tfrac15-3\tfrac23751​−332​: rewrite in fifteenths, then borrow: 7+315=6+18157+\tfrac3{15}=6+\tfrac{18}{15}7+153​=6+1518​. Subtracting gives (6−3)+(1815−1015)=3815(6-3)+(\tfrac{18}{15}-\tfrac{10}{15})=3\tfrac8{15}(6−3)+(1518​−1510​)=3158​.

Exam traps

  • Trap Adding the denominators along with the numerators, so 25+15\frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{5}52​+51​ is written as 310\frac{3}{10}103​.

    Fix The answer is 35\frac{3}{5}53​: the denominator names the piece size, and combining groups of fifths does not shrink the pieces, so only the counts add.

  • Trap Canceling a number that sits in a sum, such as striking the bbb's in w×b+ab\frac{w \times b + a}{b}bw×b+a​.

    Fix Canceling divides a factor out of the whole numerator, and being one term of a sum does not make a number a factor of it. 234=2×4+34=1142\tfrac{3}{4} = \frac{2 \times 4 + 3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}243​=42×4+3​=411​, and 444 does not divide 111111; canceling the 444s would claim 2+3=52 + 3 = 52+3=5.

  • Trap Canceling a numerator against another numerator, or a denominator against another denominator.

    Fix Every cancel pairs one top with one bottom. In 34×38\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{3}{8}43​×83​ the two 333s are both on top, so nothing cancels and the answer is 932\frac{9}{32}329​, not 132\frac{1}{32}321​.

  • Trap Flipping the first fraction in a division, or forgetting to flip and multiplying straight across.

    Fix Only the divisor turns over: 56÷23=56×32=54\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{6} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{5}{4}65​÷32​=65​×23​=45​. Straight across gives 59\frac{5}{9}95​, and flipping the first gives 45\frac{4}{5}54​, the reciprocal of the answer.

  • Trap Listing fractions with a shared positive numerator from least to greatest by putting the smallest denominator first.

    Fix For the same positive numerator, smaller pieces give the smaller amount: 310<37<34\frac3{10}<\frac37<\frac34103​<73​<43​. Zero numerators instead give equal values.

  • Trap Calling a fraction simplified after dividing out a common factor that was not the greatest.

    Fix Halving 1824\frac{18}{24}2418​ gives 912\frac{9}{12}129​, which still shares a factor of 333. Divide by the greatest common factor 666 once to reach 34\frac{3}{4}43​.

  • Trap Multiplying mixed numbers part by part, whole against whole and fraction against fraction.

    Fix A mixed number is a sum, and a product of sums is not the product of the parts. 214×1132\tfrac{1}{4} \times 1\tfrac{1}{3}241​×131​ is 94×43=3\frac{9}{4} \times \frac{4}{3} = 349​×34​=3, not the 21122\tfrac{1}{12}2121​ that part-by-part suggests.

  • Trap Subtracting mixed numbers by taking the smaller fraction from the larger regardless of which one you started with.

    Fix Borrow before subtracting. In 715−3237\tfrac15-3\tfrac23751​−332​, regroup 7+3157+\tfrac3{15}7+153​ as 6+18156+\tfrac{18}{15}6+1518​, giving 38153\tfrac8{15}3158​. Reversing the fraction subtraction without borrowing would give 47154\tfrac7{15}4157​.

  • Trap Assuming a product must be larger than its factors and a quotient smaller than what it divides.

    Fix Multiplying two positive proper fractions gives less than either factor, as 34×25=310\frac34\times\frac25=\frac3{10}43​×52​=103​ does. Dividing a positive amount by a positive fraction below 111 increases it: 6÷34=86\div\frac34=86÷43​=8. Read multiplication as "of" and division as "how many fit": taking a proper fraction keeps only part of an amount, while smaller pieces fit more times.
