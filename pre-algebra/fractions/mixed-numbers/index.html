Mixed Numbers

Intuition

When you measure something in real life, it does not always come out even. A board comes out “two feet and a bit more,” so you naturally split the answer into two parts: the whole part and the leftover. That whole number sitting next to a fraction is exactly what a mixed number is. You already know that an amount past one whole can be written as an improper fraction such as 114\frac{11}{4}411​. A mixed number writes the same value as 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​, and that form tells you at a glance that it is a little less than three.

Learning goals

  • Read a mixed number as a whole plus a proper fraction, and explain why that space means add
  • Convert an improper fraction to a mixed number by dividing with a remainder
  • Rewrite a mixed number as an improper fraction, keeping the denominator, then simplify if needed
  • Multiply or divide mixed numbers by first converting both to improper fractions
  • Add or subtract mixed numbers, carrying or borrowing a whole when needed

What a mixed number means

A mixed number is a whole number written directly beside a proper fraction, like 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​, and it stands for their sum. By a long-standing convention, a whole number set beside a fraction means add:

234  =  2+34.2\tfrac{3}{4} \;=\; 2 + \tfrac{3}{4}.243​=2+43​.

So 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​ is read “two and three fourths,” and the word “and” is doing real work: it is the plus sign you do not write. The whole-number part counts complete wholes, and the fraction part counts the leftover that has not reached another whole. That fraction part is always a proper fraction, with a numerator smaller than its denominator. Picture three identical bars, each cut into fourths. Fill two of them completely and shade three fourths of the third, and the total shaded amount is 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​.

This is genuinely the same number as an improper fraction. Every one of those bars is cut into fourths, so count all the shaded fourths. The two full bars give 4+4=84 + 4 = 84+4=8 fourths, and the partial bar gives 333 more, for 111111 fourths in total. So the picture you just read as 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​ is the very same shaded amount as 114\frac{11}{4}411​:

234  =  114.2\tfrac{3}{4} \;=\; \frac{11}{4}.243​=411​.

Recall that 12\frac{1}{2}21​ and 24\frac{2}{4}42​ were two names for one value in the equivalent-fractions lesson. A mixed number and an improper fraction are therefore two names for one value in exactly the same way. A mixed number is easier to picture and to compare at a glance, since you can see immediately that 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​ sits between 222 and 333. An improper fraction is easier to calculate with: it is usually the more convenient form for multiplying or dividing, and addition or subtraction can use either an improper fraction or the whole and fraction parts directly.

Why “side by side” means add, not multiply

In algebra, writing a number directly beside a variable, like 2x2x2x, means multiply. Yet 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​ means 2+342 + \tfrac342+43​, an addition. Why the exception?

It is a convention, kept because it is so useful for everyday measuring. Reading 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​ as a product would give 2×34=322 \times \tfrac34 = \tfrac{3}{2}2×43​=23​, which is less than 222, an odd thing to write a "222" in front of. Reading it as a sum gives a value between 222 and 333, which matches what the leading 222 promises. That is not special to 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​. A mixed number always pairs a whole number with a proper fraction, a fraction whose value is less than 111. If the two were meant to be multiplied, the result would be smaller than the whole number, because multiplying by a proper fraction shrinks a quantity.

Common mistakes

Do not multiply the whole number by the fraction. 3123\tfrac{1}{2}321​ is 3+12=723 + \tfrac12 = \tfrac723+21​=27​, not 3×12=323 \times \tfrac12 = \tfrac323×21​=23​. When a whole number and a fraction are written side by side as a mixed number, the space between them means plus.

From an improper fraction to a mixed number

Sometimes an answer comes out as an improper fraction and you want to see its size. Going from 114\frac{11}{4}411​ to 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​ is the conversion you reach for whenever that happens. The tool is division with remainder, the same idea that the very first fractions lesson used to read the fraction bar as a division.

Recall what the bar means: 114\frac{11}{4}411​ is 11÷411 \div 411÷4. So ask the plain division question, how many whole groups of 444 fit inside 111111, and what is left over? Four goes into 111111 two times, using up 888, and leaves a remainder of 333:

11÷4=2 remainder 3.11 \div 4 = 2 \text{ remainder } 3.11÷4=2 remainder 3.

The quotient 222 counts the complete groups of four fourths, and four fourths is one whole. So 222 is the number of whole units, the whole-number part. The remainder 333 is the count of fourths that did not make it into a complete whole. So it is 333 leftover fourths, the fraction part 34\tfrac3443​. Putting the whole part and the leftover fraction together gives the mixed number:

114=234.\frac{11}{4} = 2\tfrac{3}{4}.411​=243​.

The leftover is always a proper fraction, and that is not luck. A remainder is by definition smaller than the number you divided by, so the remainder is always less than the denominator. That means the fraction part always has a numerator smaller than its denominator.

A full proof shows this division argument works for any improper fraction, not just 114\frac{11}{4}411​. You can read it in the Go deeper section at the end of this lesson.

See the general proof

To rewrite an improper fraction as a mixed number, divide the numerator by the denominator. The quotient is the whole-number part, the remainder is the new numerator, and the denominator stays the same. Then simplify the fraction part if it is not already in lowest terms, the way 146=226=213\frac{14}{6} = 2\tfrac{2}{6} = 2\tfrac{1}{3}614​=262​=231​ does. When the division comes out even, with remainder 000, the fraction is really a whole number. Then there is no fraction part at all, for example 124=3\frac{12}{4} = 3412​=3.

The grouping is worth doing with your hands. In the figure below, each dot is one of the equal parts. The row width is how many of those parts it takes to fill one whole. Set the dots to 111111 and the row width to 444 to lay out eleven fourths.

Read the mixed number straight off the rows. Two complete rows are two whole units, and the hollow dots are the fourths that could not finish a third row. Those leftover fourths are the remainder that sits over the denominator, so 114=234\frac{11}{4} = 2\tfrac{3}{4}411​=243​. Step the count down and the leftover shrinks, 333 then 222 then 111. It shrinks until at 888 dots the rows come out even, and 84\frac{8}{4}48​ is the whole number 222 with no fraction part at all.

The row width is the denominator, so changing it changes what each dot represents. Put the count back to 111111 and set the row width to 333. The same eleven dots regroup into 333 full rows with 222 left over, which is the conversion 113=323\frac{11}{3} = 3\tfrac{2}{3}311​=332​ done by eye.

17÷5=3 remainder 217 \div 5 = 3 \text{ remainder } 217÷5=3 remainder 2

The quotient 333 is the whole-number part, and the remainder 222 over the denominator 555 is the fraction part, so 175=325\frac{17}{5} = 3\tfrac{2}{5}517​=352​.

From a mixed number to an improper fraction

The reverse conversion is the one you do most often. To multiply or divide mixed numbers, you always rewrite them as improper fractions first; to add or subtract them, converting is one option, and combining the whole and fraction parts directly is often quicker.

A mixed number is a sum, 234=2+342\tfrac34 = 2 + \tfrac34243​=2+43​, so the task is to add the whole number to the fraction. The adding and subtracting lesson in this chapter showed how to add a whole number to a fraction. Write the whole number over the common denominator. Each whole is 44\frac{4}{4}44​, because four fourths make one whole, so 222 wholes is 2×4=82 \times 4 = 82×4=8 fourths:

2=2×44=84.2 = \frac{2 \times 4}{4} = \frac{8}{4}.2=42×4​=48​.

Now both terms are fourths, so add the numerators:

234=84+34=8+34=114.2\tfrac{3}{4} = \frac{8}{4} + \frac{3}{4} = \frac{8 + 3}{4} = \frac{11}{4}.243​=48​+43​=48+3​=411​.

Look at what the arithmetic actually did: it multiplied the whole number by the denominator and then added the numerator, all over the original denominator.

A full proof shows this shortcut works for any whole number and any proper fraction, not just 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​. You can read it in the Go deeper section at the end of this lesson.

See why this always works

Multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and write the result over the same denominator. For 3253\tfrac{2}{5}352​ that is 3×5+2=173 \times 5 + 2 = 173×5+2=17 over 555, giving 175\frac{17}{5}517​, which matches the checkpoint above run in reverse. In this conversion step the denominator stays the same, because rewriting a whole number in fifths does not change the size of the pieces, only how many of them you have. (As with the reverse conversion, a later simplification can still change it.)

5×6+1=315 \times 6 + 1 = 315×6+1=31

So 516=3165\tfrac{1}{6} = \frac{31}{6}561​=631​. The denominator stays 666 because rewriting the wholes in sixths does not change the size of the pieces.

Multiplying and dividing mixed numbers

Multiplication and division both use one strategy: turn each mixed number into an improper fraction, then apply the fraction rule you already know. Take 112×2131\tfrac12 \times 2\tfrac13121​×231​. Rewrite each factor as an improper fraction, 112=321\tfrac12 = \frac{3}{2}121​=23​ and 213=732\tfrac13 = \frac{7}{3}231​=37​, then multiply across and cancel where you can:

112×213=32×73=3×72×3=216=72=312.1\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2\tfrac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{2} \times \frac{7}{3} = \frac{3 \times 7}{2 \times 3} = \frac{21}{6} = \frac{7}{2} = 3\tfrac{1}{2}.121​×231​=23​×37​=2×33×7​=621​=27​=321​.

The last steps simplify 216\frac{21}{6}621​ by its greatest common factor 333 to 72\frac{7}{2}27​. They then convert back to the mixed number 3123\tfrac12321​ by dividing 7÷2=37 \div 2 = 37÷2=3 remainder 111.

For multiplication and division, converting first is the reliable method, and it always works. A tempting shortcut, multiplying the whole parts and the fraction parts separately, does not work. That is because a mixed number is a sum, and a product of sums is not the product of the parts. Division works the same way: convert, then flip and multiply, using the reciprocal of the divisor. Convert the answer back to a mixed number if the problem asks for that form.

Answer choices

Convert both to improper fractions first: 223=832\tfrac23 = \frac{8}{3}232​=38​ because 2×3+2=82 \times 3 + 2 = 82×3+2=8, and 112=321\tfrac12 = \frac{3}{2}121​=23​ because 1×2+1=31 \times 2 + 1 = 31×2+1=3. Then multiply across, cancelling.

83×32=8×33×2=246=4\frac{8}{3} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{8 \times 3}{3 \times 2} = \frac{24}{6} = 438​×23​=3×28×3​=624​=4

The product is the whole number 444. Multiplying the whole and fraction parts separately would give the wrong answer; converting first is the reliable method.

Worked example 1 Compute 312÷1163\tfrac{1}{2} \div 1\tfrac{1}{6}321​÷161​

Convert each mixed number to an improper fraction. For 3123\tfrac12321​, the shortcut gives 3×2+1=73 \times 2 + 1 = 73×2+1=7 over 222, so 312=723\tfrac12 = \frac{7}{2}321​=27​. For 1161\tfrac16161​, it gives 1×6+1=71 \times 6 + 1 = 71×6+1=7 over 666, so 116=761\tfrac16 = \frac{7}{6}161​=67​.

Dividing by 76\frac{7}{6}67​ means multiplying by its reciprocal 67\frac{6}{7}76​. Keep the first fraction and flip the second:

312÷116=72÷76=72×67.3\tfrac{1}{2} \div 1\tfrac{1}{6} = \frac{7}{2} \div \frac{7}{6} = \frac{7}{2} \times \frac{6}{7}.321​÷161​=27​÷67​=27​×76​.

Cancel before multiplying: the 777 on top cancels the 777 on the bottom. The 666 also shares a factor of 222 with the 222 on the bottom, leaving 333:

72×67=11×31=3.\frac{7}{2} \times \frac{6}{7} = \frac{1}{1} \times \frac{3}{1} = 3.27​×76​=11​×13​=3.

So 312÷116=33\tfrac12 \div 1\tfrac16 = 3321​÷161​=3, a whole number. The result reads sensibly: 1161\tfrac16161​ fits into 3123\tfrac12321​ exactly three times.

Convert both to improper fractions: 214=942\tfrac14 = \tfrac94241​=49​ and 112=321\tfrac12 = \tfrac32121​=23​. Dividing means multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor, so keep the first fraction and flip the second.

94÷32=94×23=1812=32=112\tfrac{9}{4} \div \tfrac{3}{2} = \tfrac{9}{4} \times \tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{18}{12} = \tfrac{3}{2} = 1\tfrac{1}{2}49​÷23​=49​×32​=1218​=23​=121​

Only the second fraction is flipped; multiplying without flipping gives 3383\tfrac{3}{8}383​ instead.

Adding and subtracting mixed numbers

Addition and subtraction can also be done by converting to improper fractions, and that always works. But there is a second method that is often quicker and matches how you would do it in your head. That method handles the whole parts and the fraction parts separately.

Take 214+1122\tfrac14 + 1\tfrac12241​+121​. Add the whole numbers, 2+1=32 + 1 = 32+1=3, and separately add the fractions, 14+12\tfrac14 + \tfrac1241​+21​. The fractions need a common denominator: 12=24\tfrac12 = \tfrac2421​=42​, so 14+24=34\tfrac14 + \tfrac24 = \tfrac3441​+42​=43​. Combine the whole part and the fraction part:

214+112=(2+1)+(14+24)=3+34=334.2\tfrac{1}{4} + 1\tfrac{1}{2} = (2 + 1) + \left(\tfrac{1}{4} + \tfrac{2}{4}\right) = 3 + \tfrac{3}{4} = 3\tfrac{3}{4}.241​+121​=(2+1)+(41​+42​)=3+43​=343​.

This separate-parts method is allowed because addition lets you change the order of the terms and how they are grouped without changing the total: 2+14+1+122 + \tfrac14 + 1 + \tfrac122+41​+1+21​ can be reordered and rearranged as (2+1)+(14+12)(2 + 1) + (\tfrac14 + \tfrac12)(2+1)+(41​+21​) without changing the total. When the fraction parts add up to 111 or more, the extra whole has to be carried over to the whole-number part. For instance 34+34=64=112\tfrac34 + \tfrac34 = \tfrac64 = 1\tfrac1243​+43​=46​=121​, so that whole 111 joins the others. Subtraction has a different wrinkle: the fraction you are subtracting may be bigger than the fraction you have. When that happens, borrow one whole from the whole-number part and rewrite it as a fraction first.

Worked example 2 Compute 316+2343\tfrac{1}{6} + 2\tfrac{3}{4}361​+243​

Add the whole parts and the fraction parts separately. The whole parts give 3+2=53 + 2 = 53+2=5.

For the fraction parts 16+34\tfrac16 + \tfrac3461​+43​, find the least common denominator. The least common multiple of 666 and 444 is 121212, so rewrite each fraction over 121212:

16=212,34=912,212+912=1112.\tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{2}{12}, \qquad \tfrac{3}{4} = \tfrac{9}{12}, \qquad \tfrac{2}{12} + \tfrac{9}{12} = \tfrac{11}{12}.61​=122​,43​=129​,122​+129​=1211​.

The fraction total 1112\tfrac{11}{12}1211​ is already a proper fraction, so nothing carries. Combine the whole part and the fraction part:

316+234=5+1112=51112.3\tfrac{1}{6} + 2\tfrac{3}{4} = 5 + \tfrac{11}{12} = 5\tfrac{11}{12}.361​+243​=5+1211​=51211​.

Since 111111 and 121212 share no factor above 111, the fraction part is in lowest terms, so 511125\tfrac{11}{12}51211​ is the final answer.

Worked example 3 Compute 413−1564\tfrac{1}{3} - 1\tfrac{5}{6}431​−165​

Put both fraction parts over their common denominator first, before deciding whether a borrow is needed. The least common denominator of 333 and 666 is 666, and 13=26\tfrac13 = \tfrac2631​=62​, so the subtraction reads

413−156=426−156.4\tfrac{1}{3} - 1\tfrac{5}{6} = 4\tfrac{2}{6} - 1\tfrac{5}{6}.431​−165​=462​−165​.

Now the trouble is visible: 26\tfrac2662​ is smaller than 56\tfrac5665​, so it cannot be subtracted and leave a proper fraction behind. Borrow one whole from the 444, leaving 333, and hand that whole to the fraction part. One whole is 66\tfrac6666​, so the fraction part grows from 26\tfrac2662​ to 2+66=86\tfrac{2+6}{6} = \tfrac8662+6​=68​:

426=3+66+26=386.4\tfrac{2}{6} = 3 + \tfrac{6}{6} + \tfrac{2}{6} = 3\tfrac{8}{6}.462​=3+66​+62​=368​.

The fraction part 86\tfrac8668​ is greater than 111. That is intentional: the borrow just traded one whole for six sixths, so there is now enough to subtract from. The fraction on the left is the bigger one, so the parts subtract without any further trouble. The whole parts give 3−1=23 - 1 = 23−1=2, and the fraction parts give 86−56=36\tfrac86 - \tfrac56 = \tfrac3668​−65​=63​:

386−156=236=212.3\tfrac{8}{6} - 1\tfrac{5}{6} = 2\tfrac{3}{6} = 2\tfrac{1}{2}.368​−165​=263​=221​.

Converting both numbers to improper fractions is the other route, and it needs no borrow at all. The shortcut turns 4134\tfrac13431​ into 133\frac{13}{3}313​, which is 266\frac{26}{6}626​, and turns 1561\tfrac56165​ into 116\frac{11}{6}611​. Subtracting gives 156\frac{15}{6}615​, which simplifies to 52\frac{5}{2}25​, the same 2122\tfrac12221​.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying the whole number by the fraction. A mixed number is a sum, not a product: 234=2+342\tfrac34 = 2 + \tfrac34243​=2+43​, never 2×342 \times \tfrac342×43​. The space between the whole number and the fraction means plus.
  • Converting with the wrong arithmetic. For a mixed number with whole part www and fraction part ab\tfrac{a}{b}ba​, the improper fraction is wab=w×b+abw\tfrac{a}{b} = \frac{w \times b + a}{b}wba​=bw×b+a​. Adding instead of multiplying (w+aw + aw+a over bbb) or forgetting to add the numerator both give the wrong top.
  • Changing the denominator. The denominator stays the same during the conversion step itself. When you write 423=1434\tfrac23 = \frac{14}{3}432​=314​, the bottom is still 333, because rewriting the wholes in thirds does not change the size of the pieces. If the fraction part is not already in lowest terms, a simplification afterward can change the denominator, as 146=213\frac{14}{6} = 2\tfrac13614​=231​ shows.
  • Multiplying mixed numbers part by part. 214×1132\tfrac14 \times 1\tfrac13241​×131​ is not (2×1) (14×13)(2 \times 1)\,(\tfrac14 \times \tfrac13)(2×1)(41​×31​). Convert to improper fractions first; a product of sums is not the product of the parts.
  • Forgetting to carry or borrow. When fraction parts add past one whole, carry the extra whole into the whole-number part. When a subtraction needs a bigger fraction than you have, borrow one whole. If carrying or borrowing gets messy, convert to improper fractions instead.
  • Leaving the answer unsimplified or in the wrong form. Simplify the fraction part to lowest terms. Then give the answer in the form the problem asks for, whether that is a mixed number or an improper fraction.

Key takeaways

  • A mixed number, with whole part www and fraction part ab\tfrac{a}{b}ba​, is a whole number plus a proper fraction, wab=w+abw\tfrac{a}{b} = w + \tfrac{a}{b}wba​=w+ba​. The space between them means add, and the fraction part is always less than 111.
  • A mixed number and an improper fraction are two names for the same value, so you can switch to whichever form is more convenient.
  • Improper to mixed: divide the numerator by the denominator. The quotient is the whole part, the remainder is the new numerator, and the denominator stays the same during that step; then simplify the fraction part if it is not already in lowest terms.
  • Mixed to improper: multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and keep the denominator, so wab=w×b+abw\tfrac{a}{b} = \frac{w \times b + a}{b}wba​=bw×b+a​; simplify afterward if the result is not already in lowest terms.
  • Multiply or divide mixed numbers by converting both to improper fractions first, then using multiply-across or flip-and-multiply. Never multiply the parts separately.
  • Add or subtract by combining whole parts and fraction parts separately (carrying or borrowing a whole when needed), or by converting to improper fractions. Simplify and give the requested form.

Why the division rule works for every improper fraction

The lesson turns 114\frac{11}{4}411​ into 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​ by dividing 111111 by 444 and reading the quotient and remainder back through the fraction. This shows that the same division argument works for any improper fraction, which is what makes the rule dependable rather than a pattern spotted once.

Why dividing the numerator by the denominator gives the mixed number#

Run the whole argument once on 296\frac{29}{6}629​. Six goes into 292929 four times, using up 242424, and leaves 555, so 29=4×6+529 = 4 \times 6 + 529=4×6+5, and the leftover 555 is smaller than 666. Split the numerator along that line and separate the two pieces:

296=4×6+56=4×66+56=4+56=456.\frac{29}{6} = \frac{4 \times 6 + 5}{6} = \frac{4 \times 6}{6} + \frac{5}{6} = 4 + \frac{5}{6} = 4\tfrac{5}{6}.629​=64×6+5​=64×6​+65​=4+65​=465​.

The quotient 444 became the whole part, because 4×66\frac{4 \times 6}{6}64×6​ cancels to 444. The leftover 555 became the numerator over the same 666, and it is a proper fraction because 555 is smaller than 666.

Nothing in that depended on the numbers 292929 and 666. Take any improper fraction nd\frac{n}{d}dn​, so nnn is at least ddd. There are whole numbers qqq (the quotient) and rrr (the remainder) with

n=q×d+r,0≤r<d.n = q \times d + r, \qquad 0 \le r < d.n=q×d+r,0≤r<d.

Here qqq groups of ddd account for q×dq \times dq×d, and rrr is whatever is left, necessarily less than ddd or another whole group would fit.

Using the rule for adding fractions over a common denominator, run backwards,

nd=q×d+rd=q×dd+rd.\frac{n}{d} = \frac{q \times d + r}{d} = \frac{q \times d}{d} + \frac{r}{d}.dn​=dq×d+r​=dq×d​+dr​.

The first piece simplifies, because q×dd\frac{q \times d}{d}dq×d​ divides top and bottom by ddd to give the whole number qqq. The second piece is rd\frac{r}{d}dr​, and since r<dr < dr<d, it is a proper fraction. So

nd=q+rd=q rd,\frac{n}{d} = q + \frac{r}{d} = q\,\tfrac{r}{d},dn​=q+dr​=qdr​,

which is the mixed number whose whole part is the quotient and whose fraction part is the remainder over the original denominator. That reading needs rrr to be more than zero. If rrr is zero there is no fraction part, and the answer is the whole number qqq by itself.

Why the shortcut works for every mixed number

The lesson turns 2342\tfrac{3}{4}243​ into 114\frac{11}{4}411​ by writing the 222 as 84\frac{8}{4}48​ and adding. This shows that the same two steps work for any whole number and any proper fraction, which is what makes the shortcut a rule rather than a pattern spotted once.

Why a mixed number wabw\tfrac{a}{b}wba​ equals w×b+ab\frac{w \times b + a}{b}bw×b+a​#

Run the whole argument once on 5375\tfrac{3}{7}573​, which means 5+375 + \tfrac{3}{7}5+73​. One whole is 77\tfrac{7}{7}77​, so five wholes is 5×7=355 \times 7 = 355×7=35 sevenths. Both parts are sevenths now, so add the numerators:

537=357+37=35+37=387.5\tfrac{3}{7} = \frac{35}{7} + \frac{3}{7} = \frac{35 + 3}{7} = \frac{38}{7}.573​=735​+73​=735+3​=738​.

The 353535 on top came from multiplying the whole part 555 by the denominator 777, and the 333 was the numerator that was already there.

Nothing in that depended on the numbers 555, 333 and 777. Take any mixed number wabw\tfrac{a}{b}wba​, where www is the whole-number part and ab\frac{a}{b}ba​ is a proper fraction. By the meaning of the notation it is the sum

wab=w+ab.w\tfrac{a}{b} = w + \frac{a}{b}.wba​=w+ba​.

Since one whole is bb\frac{b}{b}bb​, the whole number www is www copies of that, which is w×bb\frac{w \times b}{b}bw×b​. This is the building rule from equivalent fractions, applied to w=w1w = \frac{w}{1}w=1w​:

w=w×bb.w = \frac{w \times b}{b}.w=bw×b​.

Both terms are now over bbb, so add the numerators using the like-denominator rule:

w+ab=w×bb+ab=w×b+ab.w + \frac{a}{b} = \frac{w \times b}{b} + \frac{a}{b} = \frac{w \times b + a}{b}.w+ba​=bw×b​+ba​=bw×b+a​.

So wab=w×b+abw\tfrac{a}{b} = \frac{w \times b + a}{b}wba​=bw×b+a​: multiply the whole number by the denominator, add the numerator, and keep the denominator.

A bit of history (optional)

A Roman builder measuring a beam had no decimals and no calculator. What he had was twelfths.

The Romans cut their basic unit into twelve equal parts. One part was called an uncia. Twelve was a shrewd choice, because it divides evenly by two, three, four and six. A length or a weight was then written as so many whole units and so many twelfths. That is a whole number with a leftover attached, which is a mixed number in everything but notation.

The word itself never went away, because it became both the inch and the ounce. A foot still holds twelve inches for the same old reason.

The habit survives in any workshop. A carpenter reading a tape measure usually says two and a quarter inches, rather than nine fourths. The whole part gives the size at a glance, and the fraction gives the rest. That is the trade this lesson makes between the two forms, and both forms still have a job.
