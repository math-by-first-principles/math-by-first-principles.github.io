  1. Problem 1 A whole number in a product

    Find 23×9×512\frac23\times9\times\frac5{12}32​×9×125​ in lowest terms.

    1. Hint 1

      A whole number can be written as a fraction over 111, so the whole product becomes one fraction.

    2. Hint 2

      Look for a factor that a numerator shares with a denominator, and cancel it before multiplying.

    Answer

    52\frac5225​.

    Full solution

    Write 999 as 91\frac9119​.

    The product is then one fraction, with numerator 2×9×52\times9\times52×9×5 and denominator 3×1×123\times1\times123×1×12.

    The numerator 999 and the denominator 333 share the factor 333, leaving 333 and 111.

    The numerator 222 and the denominator 121212 share the factor 222, leaving 111 and 666.

    The remaining 333 on top and 666 below still share the factor 333, leaving 111 and 222.

    What is left is

    1×1×51×1×2=52.\frac{1\times1\times5}{1\times1\times2}=\frac52.1×1×21×1×5​=25​.

    Multiplying first instead gives 9036\frac{90}{36}3690​, which also simplifies to 52\frac5225​ by the greatest common factor 181818.

    Answer

    52\frac5225​.

    Key idea

    A whole number in a product is a fraction over 111, and a factor shared by a numerator and a denominator may be removed anywhere across the product.

  2. Problem 2 A sequence of operations

    Find (38÷910)÷256\left(\frac38\div\frac9{10}\right)\div\frac{25}6(83​÷109​)÷625​ in lowest terms.

    1. Hint 1

      Division by a nonzero fraction asks for multiplication by its reciprocal.

    2. Hint 2

      First find the value in parentheses, then divide it by the last fraction.

    Answer

    110\frac1{10}101​.

    Full solution

    Divide by 910\frac9{10}109​ by multiplying by 109\frac{10}9910​:

    38÷910=38×109.\frac38\div\frac9{10}=\frac38\times\frac{10}9.83​÷109​=83​×910​.

    Remove a factor of 333 from 333 and 999, and a factor of 222 from 101010 and 888, giving

    38×109=512.\frac38\times\frac{10}9=\frac5{12}.83​×910​=125​.

    Now divide by 256\frac{25}6625​ by multiplying by its reciprocal 625\frac6{25}256​.

    Cancel 555 from the 555 and the 252525, leaving 111 and 555, and cancel 666 from the 666 and the 121212, leaving 111 and 222.

    The leftovers give

    512×625=110.\frac5{12}\times\frac6{25}=\frac1{10}.125​×256​=101​.

    The result is in lowest terms.

    Written as one product from the start, 38×109×625\frac38\times\frac{10}9\times\frac6{25}83​×910​×256​ has numerator 180180180 and denominator 180018001800, which also gives 110\frac1{10}101​.

    Answer

    110\frac1{10}101​.

    Key idea

    In a chain of divisions by nonzero fractions, rewrite each as multiplication by the divisor’s reciprocal; the chain then becomes one product to simplify.

  3. Problem 3 A reciprocal in lowest terms

    Find the reciprocal of 3042\frac{30}{42}4230​, giving your answer in lowest terms, and check that the two numbers multiply to 111.

    1. Hint 1

      A reciprocal swaps the numerator and the denominator, and simplifying a fraction does not change its value.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide 303030 and 424242 by their greatest common factor, then turn the simpler fraction over.

    Answer

    75\frac7557​; 3042×75=1\frac{30}{42}\times\frac75=14230​×57​=1.

    Full solution

    The greatest common factor of 303030 and 424242 is 666, so

    3042=57.\frac{30}{42}=\frac57.4230​=75​.

    Swapping the numerator and denominator of 57\frac5775​ gives the reciprocal 75\frac7557​, which is in lowest terms since 777 and 555 share no factor above 111.

    Flipping first gives 4230\frac{42}{30}3042​, which also simplifies to 75\frac7557​ by the same factor 666.

    For the check, the product of 3042\frac{30}{42}4230​ and 75\frac7557​ has numerator 30×7=21030\times7=21030×7=210 and denominator

    42×5=210.42\times5=210.42×5=210.

    A fraction whose numerator equals its denominator is 111, so the two numbers multiply to 111.

    Answer

    75\frac7557​; 3042×75=1\frac{30}{42}\times\frac75=14230​×57​=1.

    Key idea

    The reciprocal of a nonzero fraction swaps its numerator and denominator, and the two multiply to 111.

  4. Problem 4 A patterned panel

    A rectangular panel is divided into six equal rows and eight equal columns. Four rows are selected. In each selected row, three spaces are left plain and the other spaces are colored. Every unselected row is left plain. What fraction of the whole panel is colored? Give the fraction in lowest terms, and write it as a product of two fractions, one for the rows and one for the spaces in a row.

    1. Hint 1

      Count colored spaces in one selected row, then count across all selected rows.

    2. Hint 2

      The denominator counts every space in the panel, including those outside the selected rows.

    3. Hint 3

      The selected rows are a fraction of all the rows, and the colored spaces in one selected row are a fraction of that row’s spaces.

    Answer

    512\frac5{12}125​, which is 46×58\frac46\times\frac5864​×85​ (or 23×58\frac23\times\frac5832​×85​).

    Full solution

    The cuts create 6×8=486\times8=486×8=48 equal spaces.

    Each selected row has 8−3=58-3=58−3=5 colored spaces, so the four selected rows contain 4×5=204\times5=204×5=20 colored spaces.

    Dividing the top and bottom by their greatest common factor 444, the fraction colored is

    2048=512.\frac{20}{48}=\frac5{12}.4820​=125​.

    The same count is the product 46×58\frac46\times\frac5864​×85​.

    Its first factor is the four selected rows out of all six rows, and its second is the five colored spaces out of the eight spaces in each selected row.

    The product’s denominator 6×86\times86×8 counts all 484848 spaces and its numerator 4×54\times54×5 counts the 202020 colored ones, with no common denominator needed.

    Answer

    512\frac5{12}125​, which is 46×58\frac46\times\frac5864​×85​ (or 23×58\frac23\times\frac5832​×85​).

    Key idea

    In an area model, the product of the numerators counts the colored spaces and the product of the denominators counts all the equal spaces.

  5. Problem 5 Clay for small models

    A workshop has 79\frac7997​ of a kilogram of clay. It sets aside 13\frac1331​ of a kilogram and uses all the rest for identical models requiring 227\frac2{27}272​ of a kilogram each. How many models can it make?

    1. Hint 1

      Find the clay available after the amount set aside is removed.

    2. Hint 2

      The model count asks how many portions of size 227\frac2{27}272​ fit in that remainder.

    Answer

    666 models.

    Full solution

    Subtract the amount set aside:

    79−13=49.\frac79-\frac13=\frac49.97​−31​=94​.

    Divide the remaining clay by the amount per model:

    49÷227=49×272.\frac49\div\frac2{27}=\frac49\times\frac{27}2.94​÷272​=94​×227​.

    The factors 272727 and 999 reduce to 333 and 111, while 444 and 222 reduce to 222 and 111.

    The product is 2×3=62\times3=62×3=6, so six models can be made.

    Checking the clay used, 6×227=496\times\frac2{27}=\frac496×272​=94​ of a kilogram, exactly the remainder.

    Answer

    666 models.

    Key idea

    Find the available amount before dividing it into portions of a specified size.

  6. Problem 6 Sam’s quotient

    Sam computes 1039÷613\frac{10}{39}\div\frac6{13}3910​÷136​ as 3910×613=95\frac{39}{10}\times\frac6{13}=\frac951039​×136​=59​. Find the quotient in lowest terms, and use multiplication by the divisor to show which of the two answers is right.

    1. Hint 1

      A correct quotient, multiplied by the divisor, gives back the number that was divided.

    2. Hint 2

      Rewrite the division as a multiplication yourself, then compare it with Sam’s first step.

    3. Hint 3

      Cancel factors shared by a numerator and a denominator before multiplying when you compute the quotient yourself.

    Answer

    The quotient is 59\frac5995​, not 95\frac9559​.

    Full solution

    Dividing by 613\frac6{13}136​ asks for the number that, multiplied by 613\frac6{13}136​, gives back 1039\frac{10}{39}3910​.

    So a correct quotient must pass the test of multiplying it by the divisor.

    In Sam’s test product no numerator shares a factor with a denominator, so

    95×613=5465.\frac95\times\frac6{13}=\frac{54}{65}.59​×136​=6554​.

    This is not 1039\frac{10}{39}3910​, since 545454 is more than half of 656565 while 101010 is less than half of 393939.

    His answer fails the test.

    Sam turned over the first fraction, 1039\frac{10}{39}3910​, and left the divisor as it was.

    The rule flips only the divisor, because multiplying by 136\frac{13}6613​ is what undoes a multiplication by 613\frac6{13}136​.

    The correct rewrite is

    1039÷613=1039×136.\frac{10}{39}\div\frac6{13}=\frac{10}{39}\times\frac{13}6.3910​÷136​=3910​×613​.

    Cancel 131313 from the 131313 and the 393939, leaving 111 and 333, and cancel 222 from the 101010 and the 666, leaving 555 and 333.

    That gives

    53×13=59.\frac53\times\frac13=\frac59.35​×31​=95​.

    The test now works.

    Canceling 333 from the 666 and the 999 leaves 53×213\frac53\times\frac2{13}35​×132​, so

    59×613=1039.\frac59\times\frac6{13}=\frac{10}{39}.95​×136​=3910​.

    Answer

    The quotient is 59\frac5995​, not 95\frac9559​.

    Key idea

    Only the divisor is flipped, and a correct quotient multiplied by the divisor gives back the number that was divided.

  7. Problem 7 Water through a filter

    Four identical pump cycles use 2120\frac{21}{20}2021​ of a liter of water altogether. In each cycle, 57\frac5775​ of its water passes through a filter. How much water passes through the filter in one cycle? Give your answer in lowest terms.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the amount in one cycle before taking the fraction that is filtered.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide the total by 444, then multiply the result by 57\frac5775​.

    Answer

    316\frac3{16}163​ of a liter.

    Full solution

    One cycle uses 2120÷4\frac{21}{20}\div42021​÷4 of a liter, which is 2120×14\frac{21}{20}\times\frac142021​×41​.

    No numerator shares a factor with a denominator here, so one cycle uses 2180\frac{21}{80}8021​ of a liter.

    Five sevenths of that amount passes through the filter.

    Canceling 777 from the 212121 and the 777, and 555 from the 555 and the 808080, gives

    2180×57=316.\frac{21}{80}\times\frac57=\frac3{16}.8021​×75​=163​.

    So 316\frac3{16}163​ of a liter is filtered in one cycle.

    As a check, all four cycles filter 4×316=344\times\frac3{16}=\frac344×163​=43​ of a liter.

    Five sevenths of the full 2120\frac{21}{20}2021​ of a liter is 105140\frac{105}{140}140105​, which is also 34\frac3443​ of a liter.

    Answer

    316\frac3{16}163​ of a liter.

    Key idea

    An equal share of a total can be found before selecting a fraction of that share.

  8. Problem 8 Two ways to shrink a product

    Noor rewrites 922×1112\frac9{22}\times\frac{11}{12}229​×1211​ as 322×114\frac3{22}\times\frac{11}4223​×411​, and Eli rewrites it as 911×116\frac9{11}\times\frac{11}6119​×611​. Which rewrite keeps the product, and what is the product in lowest terms?

    1. Hint 1

      Write the original product as one fraction to see what changed on its top and bottom.

    2. Hint 2

      For each rewrite, check whether one common factor left the top and the bottom of the single fraction.

    Answer

    Noor’s rewrite; the product is 38\frac3883​.

    Full solution

    The original product is 9×1122×12\frac{9\times11}{22\times12}22×129×11​.

    Noor divided its numerator by 333 through the first factor, turning 999 into 333, and its denominator by 333 through the second factor, turning 121212 into 444.

    Dividing the top and bottom by the same nonzero number preserves the value.

    Eli divided the denominator by 222 through each factor, turning 222222 into 111111 and 121212 into 666, and divided the numerator by nothing.

    Taking a factor from the bottom alone changes the value.

    His product gives

    911×116=9966.\frac9{11}\times\frac{11}6=\frac{99}{66}.119​×611​=6699​.

    That simplifies to 32\frac3223​, which is four times the original product, so his rewrite does not keep it.

    Noor’s product gives

    322×114=3388.\frac3{22}\times\frac{11}4=\frac{33}{88}.223​×411​=8833​.

    Dividing 333333 and 888888 by their common factor 111111 gives

    3388=38.\frac{33}{88}=\frac38.8833​=83​.

    The original product 99264\frac{99}{264}26499​ also simplifies to 38\frac3883​, by the common factor 333333.

    Answer

    Noor’s rewrite; the product is 38\frac3883​.

    Key idea

    Canceling is valid when the full product’s numerator and denominator are divided by the same nonzero factor.

  9. Problem 9 A machine’s return setting

    A machine multiplies a positive fraction by 815\frac8{15}158​. A second setting must multiply the result by one fixed fraction to restore every starting value. What fraction should that setting use, and why does it work? Check your choice with a starting value of 56\frac5665​.

    1. Hint 1

      The two multipliers together must have the effect of multiplying by 111.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the reciprocal of the first multiplier, then follow the two stages on the given starting value.

    Answer

    158\frac{15}8815​; the check returns 56\frac5665​.

    Full solution

    The required multiplier is 158\frac{15}8815​, since

    815×158=1.\frac8{15}\times\frac{15}8=1.158​×815​=1.

    Multiplying any starting value by these two factors therefore leaves that value unchanged.

    For the requested check, the first stage gives

    56×815=49.\frac56\times\frac8{15}=\frac49.65​×158​=94​.

    The second gives

    49×158=56.\frac49\times\frac{15}8=\frac56.94​×815​=65​.

    It restores the original value as required.

    Answer

    158\frac{15}8815​; the check returns 56\frac5665​.

    Key idea

    Multiplication by a nonzero fraction is undone by multiplication by its reciprocal.

  10. Problem 10 Pia’s prediction

    Pia says that a positive proper fraction multiplied by an improper fraction must give a value greater than 111. Is the claim true? Justify your answer.

    1. Hint 1

      Think about how much multiplying by an improper fraction, which is at least 111, can enlarge a positive number.

    2. Hint 2

      Try a small positive unit fraction and an improper fraction only a little above 111.

    Answer

    False; for example, 15×32=310<1\frac15\times\frac32=\frac3{10}<151​×23​=103​<1.

    Full solution

    Choose the proper fraction 15\frac1551​ and the improper fraction 32\frac3223​.

    Their product is

    15×32=310.\frac15\times\frac32=\frac3{10}.51​×23​=103​.

    The product is less than 111 because 3<103<103<10, although the second factor is improper.

    The multiplier enlarged 15\frac1551​ but did not enlarge it enough to reach a whole, so this example disproves the claim.

    Answer

    False; for example, 15×32=310<1\frac15\times\frac32=\frac3{10}<151​×23​=103​<1.

    Key idea

    A factor greater than one increases a positive value without necessarily making the product exceed one.
