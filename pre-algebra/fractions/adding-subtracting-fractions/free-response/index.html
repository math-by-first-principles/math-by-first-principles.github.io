    1. Cord measured in fifteenths . Foundational, 11 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 11 pts

    A model-making kit sells cord by the roll, and every length in its instructions is given as a fraction of one roll. The kit measures in fifteenths, so several projects come out written over the same denominator. This question combines two of them and then looks at what each number in a result is doing.

    1. A
      Part A.

      One project uses 415\frac{4}{15}154​ of a roll and a second uses 615\frac{6}{15}156​ of a roll. Give the total the two use together. Separately, a third project cuts 415\frac{4}{15}154​ of a roll from a piece measuring 1315\frac{13}{15}1513​ of a roll: give the length that is left. Put both answers in lowest terms and state what each one is a fraction of.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      The kit's instruction sheet prints the total for those first two projects as 1030\frac{10}{30}3010​ of a roll. Rewrite 1030\frac{10}{30}3010​ in fifteenths so that it can be set beside the amounts it came from, compare it with the 615\frac{6}{15}156​ that the second project uses on its own, and say what that comparison settles about the printed line.

      Find and correct the error Say which line first goes wrong, why it is wrong, and then do it correctly. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Fifteenths are the size of piece every length in this kit is measured in. Say what the denominator of one of these fractions names and what its numerator counts, and use the difference between those two jobs to say which of the two numbers an addition of fifteenths can change and which it cannot.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Two amounts can be totalled by counting only when a single kind of thing is being counted. Ask what that single thing is here before you touch any arithmetic.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      You do not need a correct total in order to test the printed one. Get the printed value into the same size of piece as the amounts it came from, and then ask whether any addition could land below one of them.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Describe one fifteenth out loud without using the number 151515, then describe four of them. Watch which of the two numbers has to change while you do it, and which one just sits there naming the piece.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    The two projects use 23\frac{2}{3}32​ of a roll together, and 35\frac{3}{5}53​ of a roll is left from the third project's piece.

    Part B

    1030=515\frac{10}{30} = \frac{5}{15}3010​=155​, which is less than the 615\frac{6}{15}156​ one project uses by itself. A total of two positive amounts cannot be smaller than either of them, so the printed line is wrong: its denominators were added as though they counted something.

    Part C

    The denominator names the size of one piece, a fifteenth of a roll, and the numerator counts how many such pieces are held. Putting two of these amounts together changes only the count, since piling pieces up does not make any piece a different size, so the numerator moves and the denominator stays.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Both calculations sit over the same denominator, so the pieces are already one size and only the counts move.

    For the total, add the counts of fifteenths and keep fifteenths as the size of the piece:

    415+615=4+615=1015.\frac{4}{15} + \frac{6}{15} = \frac{4 + 6}{15} = \frac{10}{15}.154​+156​=154+6​=1510​.

    The greatest common factor of 101010 and 151515 is 555, so the total in lowest terms is

    1015=10÷515÷5=23.\frac{10}{15} = \frac{10 \div 5}{15 \div 5} = \frac{2}{3}.1510​=15÷510÷5​=32​.

    For the piece that is left, take one count away from the other:

    1315−415=13−415=915.\frac{13}{15} - \frac{4}{15} = \frac{13 - 4}{15} = \frac{9}{15}.1513​−154​=1513−4​=159​.

    Here the greatest common factor is 333:

    915=9÷315÷3=35.\frac{9}{15} = \frac{9 \div 3}{15 \div 3} = \frac{3}{5}.159​=15÷39÷3​=53​.

    So the two projects use 23\frac{2}{3}32​ of a roll and 35\frac{3}{5}53​ of a roll is left. Each answer is a fraction of one roll, which is the only reason the numbers were comparable in the first place.

    Part B

    Put the printed value into fifteenths first, using the building rule in reverse. The denominator 303030 becomes 151515 when it is divided by 222, so the numerator is divided by 222 as well:

    1030=10÷230÷2=515.\frac{10}{30} = \frac{10 \div 2}{30 \div 2} = \frac{5}{15}.3010​=30÷210÷2​=155​.

    Now the printed total and the amounts it came from are all counted in fifteenths, so they can be compared by their counts alone:

    515<615.\frac{5}{15} < \frac{6}{15}.155​<156​.

    The printed total is smaller than the amount the second project uses on its own. Putting the first project's cord together with it cannot possibly leave less than the second project alone, so this is not a small slip in the arithmetic. The line is not a total at all.

    What produced it is visible in the numbers: 4+6=104 + 6 = 104+6=10 on top, and 15+15=3015 + 15 = 3015+15=30 underneath. The denominators were added as though they were counts. They are not: 151515 says how big one piece is, and putting two groups of fifteenths together leaves every piece exactly as big as it was.

    Part C

    A fraction such as 415\frac{4}{15}154​ makes two different statements with its two numbers.

    The denominator 151515 says how big one piece is: the roll has been cut into 151515 equal pieces, so one piece is a fifteenth of a roll. That is a description of a piece, and it tallies nothing.

    The numerator 444 says how many of those pieces are being held. That is a count, and counting is exactly what addition and subtraction do.

    So when two amounts made of fifteenths are put together, the only thing that can move is the count:

    415+615=4+615=1015.\frac{4}{15} + \frac{6}{15} = \frac{4 + 6}{15} = \frac{10}{15}.154​+156​=154+6​=1510​.

    Nothing in that step made a piece bigger or smaller. Four fifteenth-sized pieces together with six more of the same size are ten pieces of that same size, so the denominator comes through untouched while the top becomes a number that appeared in neither fraction to begin with.

    This is also why unlike denominators need work before anything else. If one amount were measured in fifteenths and the other in eighths, there would be no single piece being counted, and the two counts could not be totalled until both amounts were described in one size.

    In one line

    The two projects use 415+615=1015=23\frac{4}{15} + \frac{6}{15} = \frac{10}{15} = \frac{2}{3}154​+156​=1510​=32​ of a roll, and cutting 415\frac{4}{15}154​ from 1315\frac{13}{15}1513​ leaves 915=35\frac{9}{15} = \frac{3}{5}159​=53​ of a roll. The printed 1030\frac{10}{30}3010​ is 515\frac{5}{15}155​, smaller than the 615\frac{6}{15}156​ one project uses on its own, so it cannot be a total: its denominators were added as though they counted something. The denominator names the size of a piece and the numerator counts pieces, so combining fifteenths moves the count and leaves the size alone.

    2. Two sizes of piece, one shared size . Foundational, 11 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Foundational 11 pts

    The fractions 49\frac{4}{9}94​ and 512\frac{5}{12}125​ are built from pieces of different sizes, so their counts cannot be totalled as they stand. This question takes them through the rewriting that fixes that, and then asks why the rewriting is allowed at all.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Find the least common denominator of 49\frac{4}{9}94​ and 512\frac{5}{12}125​, and rewrite each fraction as an equivalent fraction over it. Report the multiplier you used on each fraction. Do not add anything yet.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Working over that shared denominator, compute 49+512\frac{4}{9} + \frac{5}{12}94​+125​. Say whether your result is in lowest terms, and say how many pieces of what size it counts.

      Carry your own answer forward Continue from the rewrites you produced in part A, whatever they came to. If part A did not come out, rebuild both fractions over any common multiple of 999 and 121212: the total is the same amount whichever common multiple you use, though a larger one leaves more simplifying to do at the end.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Rebuilding 49\frac{4}{9}94​ changed both of its numbers, and yet the result of part B is offered as the sum of the two fractions you were given. Explain why rebuilding a fraction that way leaves the amount alone, and say what would happen to the amount if only the denominator were multiplied.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Neither fraction can be counted alongside the other until both are described in one size of piece. The size you are looking for is one that a ninth and a twelfth can both be cut into evenly.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      You already have the tool for this from the factors chapter. Either list the multiples of the larger denominator until the smaller one divides one of them, or build the number from the prime factors each denominator needs.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask what happens to a single ninth when the denominator is multiplied by 444: into how many pieces is it cut, and how many of those pieces does each ninth you were holding become?

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    The least common denominator is 363636, with 49=1636\frac{4}{9} = \frac{16}{36}94​=3616​ (multiplier 444) and 512=1536\frac{5}{12} = \frac{15}{36}125​=3615​ (multiplier 333).

    Part B

    3136\frac{31}{36}3631​, which counts 313131 pieces each a thirty-sixth of one whole, and is already in lowest terms since 313131 and 363636 share no factor above 111.

    Part C

    Multiplying top and bottom by the same number cuts every piece into that many equal smaller pieces and counts that many more of them, so the amount is untouched and the rewrite equals the original exactly. Multiplying only the denominator would shrink the pieces while leaving the count, so the amount would shrink.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The least common denominator is the least common multiple of the two denominators, so work on 999 and 121212 first.

    By prime factorization, 9=3×39 = 3 \times 39=3×3 and 12=2×2×312 = 2 \times 2 \times 312=2×2×3. A common multiple has to carry the two 222s that 121212 needs and the two 333s that 999 needs, and the smallest number that does is

    lcm⁡(9,12)=2×2×3×3=36.\operatorname{lcm}(9, 12) = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 = 36.lcm(9,12)=2×2×3×3=36.

    Listing multiples gives the same answer: 12,24,3612, 24, 3612,24,36, and 363636 is the first of them that 999 divides.

    Now rebuild each fraction over 363636. The denominator 999 reaches 363636 when it is multiplied by 444, so the numerator is multiplied by 444 as well:

    49=4×49×4=1636.\frac{4}{9} = \frac{4 \times 4}{9 \times 4} = \frac{16}{36}.94​=9×44×4​=3616​.

    The denominator 121212 reaches 363636 when it is multiplied by 333:

    512=5×312×3=1536.\frac{5}{12} = \frac{5 \times 3}{12 \times 3} = \frac{15}{36}.125​=12×35×3​=3615​.

    Both amounts are now counted in thirty-sixths, which is what makes the next step possible.

    Part B

    Both fractions are now counted in one size of piece, so the like-denominator rule finishes the job: add the counts and keep the size.

    49+512=1636+1536=3136.\frac{4}{9} + \frac{5}{12} = \frac{16}{36} + \frac{15}{36} = \frac{31}{36}.94​+125​=3616​+3615​=3631​.

    The step in the middle is where the counting happens:

    16+1536=3136.\frac{16 + 15}{36} = \frac{31}{36}.3616+15​=3631​.

    Read the answer back: it counts 313131 pieces, each one a thirty-sixth of one whole.

    Check lowest terms before stopping. 313131 is prime, and 363636 is not a multiple of 313131, so the two share no factor above 111 and 3136\frac{31}{36}3631​ is already as simple as it can be.

    Part C

    Start from what the rebuild does to the pieces, not to the symbols.

    Taking 49\frac{4}{9}94​ to thirty-sixths multiplies the denominator by 444, which is the same as cutting each ninth into 444 equal pieces. Every ninth becomes 444 smaller pieces, so the 444 ninths being held become 161616 of those smaller pieces, and that is why the numerator is multiplied by 444 too:

    49=4×49×4=1636.\frac{4}{9} = \frac{4 \times 4}{9 \times 4} = \frac{16}{36}.94​=9×44×4​=3616​.

    Nothing was added and nothing was taken away; the same amount is simply being described in smaller pieces. So 1636\frac{16}{36}3616​ and 49\frac{4}{9}94​ are equal, not merely close, and replacing one by the other cannot change what the sum comes to. The same holds for the other fraction, so the total in part B is a total of the two amounts you were actually given.

    Multiplying only the denominator is a different thing entirely. Writing

    436\frac{4}{36}364​

    keeps the count at 444 while every piece has shrunk to a quarter of its old size, so it names a quarter of the amount you started with. Both numbers have to move, and by the same factor, precisely because one of them names the size of a piece and the other counts pieces.

    In one line

    The least common denominator is lcm⁡(9,12)=36\operatorname{lcm}(9, 12) = 36lcm(9,12)=36, where 49=1636\frac{4}{9} = \frac{16}{36}94​=3616​ and 512=1536\frac{5}{12} = \frac{15}{36}125​=3615​, so 49+512=3136\frac{4}{9} + \frac{5}{12} = \frac{31}{36}94​+125​=3631​, which counts 313131 thirty-sixths and is already in lowest terms. The rebuilding is allowed because multiplying top and bottom by the same number cuts each piece into that many smaller pieces and counts that many more of them, leaving the amount unchanged. Multiplying the denominator alone would shrink every piece while keeping the count, and so would shrink the amount.

    Another way: Use the product of the denominators and simplify at the end

    Any common multiple of the two denominators will serve, and the product 9×12=1089 \times 12 = 1089×12=108 is always one of them, which helps when the least common multiple is not obvious. Rebuild both fractions over 108108108:

    49=48108,512=45108.\frac{4}{9} = \frac{48}{108}, \qquad \frac{5}{12} = \frac{45}{108}.94​=10848​,125​=10845​.

    Add the counts:

    48108+45108=93108.\frac{48}{108} + \frac{45}{108} = \frac{93}{108}.10848​+10845​=10893​.

    The greatest common factor of 939393 and 108108108 is 333, so

    93108=93÷3108÷3=3136,\frac{93}{108} = \frac{93 \div 3}{108 \div 3} = \frac{31}{36},10893​=108÷393÷3​=3631​,

    the same amount as before. The larger denominator cost bigger numbers along the way and a simplification at the end, which is exactly what the least common denominator saves you.

    When it is worth it When the least common multiple of the two denominators is awkward to spot, or when you would rather do one extra simplification at the end than hunt for the smallest shared size of piece.

    Answer and solution

    The answer

    Part A

    45\frac{4}{5}54​ of a can of blue. Just one of the two had to be rebuilt, the 12\frac{1}{2}21​, because the tenths were already written in the size of piece both amounts could be counted in.

    Part B

    Only one fraction needs rebuilding when the two denominators differ and the larger is a multiple of the smaller, since the larger is then the least common denominator.     Worked solution

    Part A

    Look at the denominators before anything else. They are 101010 and 222, and 101010 is itself a multiple of 222, since 2×5=102 \times 5 = 102×5=10. So the least common multiple of 101010 and 222 is 101010: tenths are the shared size, and 310\frac{3}{10}103​ is already written in them.

    Only the half needs rebuilding, and 222 reaches 101010 when it is multiplied by 555:

    12=1×52×5=510.\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1 \times 5}{2 \times 5} = \frac{5}{10}.21​=2×51×5​=105​.

    Now add the counts and keep the size:

    310+12=310+510=810.\frac{3}{10} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{10} + \frac{5}{10} = \frac{8}{10}.103​+21​=103​+105​=108​.

    Finish in lowest terms. The greatest common factor of 888 and 101010 is 222:

    810=8÷210÷2=45.\frac{8}{10} = \frac{8 \div 2}{10 \div 2} = \frac{4}{5}.108​=10÷28÷2​=54​.

    The crew used 45\frac{4}{5}54​ of a can of blue over the two days.

    Part B

    Check the denominators the same way. Neither divides the other: 888 is not a multiple of 333, and 333 is not a multiple of 888. So the shared size is a new one that neither fraction is written over yet.

    Since 333 and 888 have no factor above 111 in common, their least common multiple is their product:
