  1. Problem 1 A measuring label

    A label describes 916\frac{9}{16}169​ of a liter as nine copies of one smaller amount. What is that smaller amount?

    1. Hint 1

      The denominator tells the size of each equal part.

    2. Hint 2

      How many equal parts is the whole liter cut into?

    Answer

    116\frac{1}{16}161​ of a liter.

    Full solution

    The denominator 161616 says that one liter is divided into sixteen equal parts.

    Each part is 116\frac{1}{16}161​ of a liter.

    The numerator 999 counts nine such parts.

    The part size is therefore 116\frac{1}{16}161​ of a liter.

    Nine of these parts account for the amount on the label.

    Answer

    116\frac{1}{16}161​ of a liter.

    Key idea

    The numerator counts copies of the part whose size the denominator names.

  2. Problem 2 Between two and three

    A fraction has denominator 777 and a whole-number numerator, and its value is at least 222 and less than 333. Write every such fraction.

    1. Hint 1

      Count how many sevenths make each of the two whole numbers.

    2. Hint 2

      Decide from "at least" and "less than" whether each boundary value itself is allowed.

    Answer

    147\frac{14}7714​, 157\frac{15}7715​, 167\frac{16}7716​, 177\frac{17}7717​, 187\frac{18}7718​, 197\frac{19}7719​, 207\frac{20}7720​.

    Full solution

    Seven sevenths make one whole, so fourteen make two wholes and twenty-one make three.

    147=2\frac{14}7=2714​=2 217=3\frac{21}7=3721​=3

    The value may equal 222 but must stay below 333, so the numerator must be at least 141414 and less than 212121.

    Its whole-number possibilities are 141414, 151515, 161616, 171717, 181818, 191919, and 202020, giving the seven fractions listed.

    Each is improper because its numerator is at least its denominator.

    Answer

    147\frac{14}7714​, 157\frac{15}7715​, 167\frac{16}7716​, 177\frac{17}7717​, 187\frac{18}7718​, 197\frac{19}7719​, 207\frac{20}7720​.

    Key idea

    Counting the parts in the boundary wholes gives the allowed numerators.

  3. Problem 3 A point near one

    The segment from 000 to 111 on a number line is divided into nine equal steps. A point is two of these steps to the left of 111. Name the point as a fraction with denominator 999.

    1. Hint 1

      The point can be named by the number of steps from 000.

    2. Hint 2

      Find how many of the nine steps remain after moving two steps back from 111.

    Answer

    79\frac7997​.

    Full solution

    Nine ninth-sized steps from 000 reach 111.

    Moving two steps back leaves

    9−2=79-2=79−2=7

    steps from 000.

    The point is therefore 79\frac7997​.

    It lies between 000 and 111, as expected.

    Answer

    79\frac7997​.

    Key idea

    A fraction on a number line counts equal steps from zero even when its location is described from the other end.

  4. Problem 4 A moving marker

    A marker starts at 000. Each move covers 18\frac1881​ of a unit. It makes nineteen moves right and then six moves left. Give its final position as a fraction with denominator 888, and state whether the fraction is proper or improper.

    1. Hint 1

      A right move and a left move of the same size undo each other.

    2. Hint 2

      Count the right moves that remain after pairing six of them with the left moves.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare that count of eighths with the number of eighths that make one whole.

    Answer

    138\frac{13}{8}813​; improper.

    Full solution

    Six left moves undo six of the nineteen right moves, leaving

    19−6=1319-6=1319−6=13

    right moves from 000.

    Each is one eighth of a unit, so the position is 138\frac{13}{8}813​.

    One whole takes eight moves, and 131313 is more than 888, so the marker has passed 111.

    The numerator is greater than the denominator, so the fraction is improper.

    Answer

    138\frac{13}{8}813​; improper.

    Key idea

    Equal moves can be counted first and then named using their shared part size.

  5. Problem 5 Three fabric orders

    Three clubs order lengths from identical full rolls. Club A orders 58\frac5885​ of a roll, Club B orders 512\frac5{12}125​, and Club C orders 712\frac7{12}127​. Who orders more in the comparison of A with B, and who orders more in the comparison of B with C? Explain each decision.

    1. Hint 1

      Look for the number the two fractions share in each comparison.

    2. Hint 2

      For A and B, the counts match but the parts have different sizes.

    3. Hint 3

      For B and C, the parts have the same size, so compare their counts.

    Answer

    A orders more than B; C orders more than B.

    Full solution

    A and B both take five parts.

    An eighth of a roll is larger than a twelfth of the same-sized roll, so

    58>512.\frac58>\frac5{12}.85​>125​.

    B and C both count twelfths.

    Seven twelfths is more than five twelfths, so

    712>512.\frac7{12}>\frac5{12}.127​>125​.

    These comparisons use identical roll sizes, as the statement specifies.

    Answer

    A orders more than B; C orders more than B.

    Key idea

    Equal positive counts call for comparing part sizes, while equal part sizes call for comparing counts.

  6. Problem 6 Sharing a roll of wire

    A roll of wire is shared equally among 999 spools, and each spool receives 409\frac{40}{9}940​ meters. How long was the roll, and how many complete one-meter pieces can be cut from one spool's wire?

    1. Hint 1

      Sharing among spools is a division, and the fraction bar is a division sign.

    2. Hint 2

      Write 409\frac{40}{9}940​ as a division and compare it with the roll's length divided by 999.

    3. Hint 3

      For the pieces, compare 404040 ninths with the number of ninths in each whole meter.

    Answer

    404040 meters; 444 complete pieces.

    Full solution

    Each spool receives the roll's length divided by 999.

    The fraction bar is a division sign, so

    409=40÷9.\frac{40}{9}=40\div9.940​=40÷9.

    The roll's length divided by 999 is therefore 404040 divided by 999, so the roll was 404040 meters long.

    As a check, nine shares of 404040 ninths are 9×40=3609\times40=3609×40=360 ninths, and since nine ninths make one meter, 360360360 ninths are 360÷9=40360\div9=40360÷9=40 meters, the roll's length.

    One meter is nine ninths.

    Four complete meters use 4×9=364\times9=364×9=36 ninths, and five would need 454545, more than the 404040 available.

    One spool's wire therefore gives four complete one-meter pieces, with 49\frac4994​ of a meter left over.

    Answer

    404040 meters; 444 complete pieces.

    Key idea

    When bbb equal shares are each ab\frac{a}{b}ba​, reading the bar as a÷ba\div ba÷b shows the total shared was aaa, and every bbb parts of size 1b\frac1bb1​ make one whole.

  7. Problem 7 A card counter

    A counter starts at 000 and advances once for each card packed. It reads 242424 when an empty box has been filled to 47\frac4774​ of its capacity. That box is then filled completely, and packing continues into a second, identical box until the counter reads 727272. What fraction of the second box is filled?

    1. Hint 1

      The twenty-four cards fill four equal parts of one box.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the cards in one seventh and in a full box, then count the cards that reach the second box.

    3. Hint 3

      Name the second box's cards as a number of sevenths of a box.

    Answer

    57\frac5775​ of the second box.

    Full solution

    Four sevenths of a box hold 242424 cards, so one seventh holds

    24÷4=624\div4=624÷4=6

    cards.

    A full box holds 7×6=427\times6=427×6=42 cards.

    The counter reads 424242 when the first box is full, so the second box receives

    72−42=3072-42=3072−42=30

    cards.

    Each seventh holds 666 cards, so 303030 cards fill 30÷6=530\div6=530÷6=5 sevenths, and the second box is 57\frac5775​ full.

    As a check, the 424242 cards in the first box and the 303030 in the second make 42+30=7242+30=7242+30=72, the final reading.

    Answer

    57\frac5775​ of the second box.

    Key idea

    The count in several equal parts gives the count in one part, and that tells you how many parts another count fills.

  8. Problem 8 A claim about thirteenths

    Nia says that every proper fraction with denominator 131313 and a whole-number numerator of at least 777 is closer to 111 than to 000 on the number line. Is she correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Each allowed point can be measured in thirteenth-sized steps from 000 and from 111.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the meaning of a proper fraction to list the numerators that are allowed.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare the steps from 000 with the steps to 111, starting with the smallest allowed numerator.

    Answer

    Yes.

    Full solution

    A proper fraction has a numerator smaller than its denominator, so the allowed numerators are the whole numbers from 777 to 121212.

    Thirteen steps of one thirteenth each reach

    1313=1.\frac{13}{13}=1.1313​=1.

    The point 713\frac7{13}137​ is 777 steps from 000 and 13−7=613-7=613−7=6 steps from 111, so it is closer to 111.

    Each larger allowed numerator is one more step from 000 and one step nearer to 111, so every other allowed point is also closer to 111.

    Nia is correct.

    Answer

    Yes.

    Key idea

    With a fixed denominator, comparing a point's steps from 000 with its steps to 111 shows which end it is closer to.

  9. Problem 9 Two station readings

    A recorder shows 011\frac0{11}110​ at one station and 017\frac0{17}170​ at another. Ben says the second value is smaller since seventeenths are smaller parts. Is his conclusion correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Part size matters only after you know how many parts are being counted.

    2. Hint 2

      Read each fraction as a division.

    Answer

    No; both values are 000.

    Full solution

    Both numerators are zero, so neither reading counts any parts.

    Equivalently, both divisions give zero:

    011=0\frac0{11}=0110​=0 017=0\frac0{17}=0170​=0

    Seventeenths are smaller than elevenths of the same whole, but taking none of either gives the same amount.

    The rule that the smaller denominator wins needs the shared numerator to be positive.

    Answer

    No; both values are 000.

    Key idea

    Zero copies of different-sized parts still give the same value, zero.

  10. Problem 10 A proposed location

    Leah wants to put the expression 8−85−5\frac{8-8}{5-5}5−58−8​ at the point 111 on a number line since its numerator and denominator match. Does the expression name that point? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Work out the numerator and denominator before applying a fraction rule.

    2. Hint 2

      Division must select one value whose product with the denominator gives the numerator.

    3. Hint 3

      Ask how many numbers have product 000 when multiplied by 000.

    Answer

    No; the expression is undefined.

    Full solution

    Both parts of the fraction are zero:

    8−8=08-8=08−8=0 5−5=05-5=05−5=0

    The expression would therefore be 00\frac0000​.

    A division 0÷00\div00÷0 would need one chosen number whose product with 000 is 000.

    Every number has that product, so division does not determine a single value.

    The expression is undefined and has no position on the number line.

    Equal numerator and denominator give 111 when their common value is nonzero.

    Answer

    No; the expression is undefined.

    Key idea

    A fraction with matching top and bottom equals one only when the common value is nonzero.
