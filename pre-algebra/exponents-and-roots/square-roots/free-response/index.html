  1. Problem 1 Nested symbols

    Find the value of 625\sqrt{\sqrt{625}}625​​.

    1. Hint 1

      The outer radical acts on the value produced by the inner radical.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the zero or positive number whose square is 625625625, then take the root of that result.

    Answer

    555.

    Full solution

    The inner radical is the positive square root of 625625625.

    252=62525^2=625252=625 625=25\sqrt{625}=25625​=25

    Now evaluate the outer radical.

    25=5\sqrt{25}=525​=5

    Answer

    555.

    Key idea

    For nested radicals, the inside value becomes the radicand of the outside radical.

  2. Problem 2 A number under the bar

    Fill the box with a positive whole number so that 245□=74\sqrt{\frac{245}{\square}}=\frac74□245​​=47​.

    1. Hint 1

      The number under the radical must be the square of the stated root.

    2. Hint 2

      Square the fraction on the right, then compare its numerator with 245245245.

    3. Hint 3

      The numerator 245245245 is a whole-number multiple of 494949, so scale the denominator by the same factor.

    Answer

    808080.

    Full solution

    Square the stated positive root to find the required radicand.

    (74)2=4916\left(\frac74\right)^2=\frac{49}{16}(47​)2=1649​

    The numerator 245245245 is five times 494949.

    245÷49=5245\div49=5245÷49=5

    The denominator must also be five times as large to preserve the fraction.

    5×16=805\times16=805×16=80

    The box is 808080, a positive whole number.

    Checking the fraction gives 245/80=49/16245/80=49/16245/80=49/16, whose positive square root is 7/47/47/4.

    Answer

    808080.

    Key idea

    Squaring a specified root determines its radicand, which can then be rewritten as an equivalent fraction.

  3. Problem 3 A single symbol

  4. Problem 4 A poster board

    A square poster of area 0.810.810.81 square meters is centered on a square board of area 1.211.211.21 square meters, leaving a border of the same width on all four sides. How wide is that border?

    1. Hint 1

      A square has equal side lengths, so its area is the square of one edge length.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the edge length of the poster and the edge length of the board from their areas.

    3. Hint 3

      Across the board, the extra width is shared by two borders, one on each side of the poster.

    Answer

    0.10.10.1 meters.

    Full solution

    Each edge is the positive square root of its square's area.

    0.92=0.810.9^2=0.810.92=0.81 1.12=1.211.1^2=1.211.12=1.21

    So the poster edge is 0.90.90.9 meters and the board edge is 1.11.11.1 meters.

    Across the board, the poster edge and two equal borders make up the board edge.

    1.1−0.9=0.21.1-0.9=0.21.1−0.9=0.2

    That 0.20.20.2 meters is split between the two borders on opposite sides of the poster.

    0.2÷2=0.10.2\div2=0.10.2÷2=0.1

    Each border is 0.10.10.1 meters wide.

    Answer

    0.10.10.1 meters.

    Key idea

    Side lengths found from square areas can then be combined like any other lengths.

  5. Problem 5 Three radical values

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

      Simplify the fraction inside the combined radical before finding its root.

    3. Hint 3

    Answer

    777.

    Full solution

    Combine the product in the numerator and the positive denominator under one radical.

    14×3510=7×7\frac{14\times35}{10}=7\times71014×35​=7×7

    49=7\sqrt{49}=749​=7

  6. Problem 6 A ruler marker

    Two marks lie 888 cm and 999 cm from the start of a ruler. A marker lies 74\sqrt{74}74​ cm from the start. Which mark is closer to the marker? Explain.

    3. Hint 3

    Answer

    The 999 cm mark is closer.

    Full solution

    The surrounding perfect squares locate the marker.

    82=648^2=6482=64 92=819^2=8192=81

    Since 64<74<8164<74<8164<74<81, the root lies between 888 and 999.

    The halfway point is 8.58.58.5.

    8.52=72.258.5^2=72.258.52=72.25

    This is less than 747474, so

    74>8.5.\sqrt{74}>8.5.74​>8.5.

    Answer

    Key idea

    To compare distances from a root to neighboring whole numbers, test the square of their halfway point.

  7. Problem 7 A measured cord

    A cord is exactly 684\sqrt{684}684​ cm long. It is cut into three equal pieces. Give the exact length of one piece, with the largest perfect-square factor taken out of the radical.

    1. Hint 1

      Divide the full length by the number of equal pieces.

    2. Hint 2

      Look for a perfect-square factor in the radicand before simplifying the division.

    3. Hint 3

      The perfect square 363636 is a factor of 684684684.

    Answer

    2192\sqrt{19}219​ cm.

    Full solution

    The radicand contains the perfect-square factor 363636.

    684=36×19684=36\times19684=36×19

    Split the radical with the product rule, and take the root of the perfect square, 36=6\sqrt{36}=636​=6.

    684=36×19\sqrt{684}=\sqrt{36}\times\sqrt{19}684​=36​×19​ 36×19=619\sqrt{36}\times\sqrt{19}=6\sqrt{19}36​×19​=619​

    Divide the length by three.

    Since 6196\sqrt{19}619​ means 6×196\times\sqrt{19}6×19​, dividing by 333 divides the 666 and leaves 19\sqrt{19}19​ as it is.

    6÷3=26\div3=26÷3=2 6193=219\frac{6\sqrt{19}}{3}=2\sqrt{19}3619​​=219​

    The radicand 191919 has no perfect-square factor greater than 111, so the largest one has been taken out.

    Each piece is exactly 2192\sqrt{19}219​ cm long.

    Three such lengths give 6196\sqrt{19}619​ cm, which is the original length.

    Answer

    2192\sqrt{19}219​ cm.

    Key idea

    Simplifying a radical can make division of an exact length straightforward.

  9. Problem 9 An unfinished decimal

    A student describes 17\sqrt{17}17​ as a fraction whose decimal has not been finished yet. Is that description possible? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      A decimal may go on forever even when it represents a fraction, but then a pattern repeats.

    2. Hint 2

      Decide whether 171717 is a perfect square, then recall what that tells you about the decimal of its root.

    3. Hint 3

      A decimal cut off after a few places, such as 4.123=412310004.123=\frac{4123}{1000}4.123=10004123​, is itself a fraction; ask what every fraction's decimal must do, and whether the decimal of 17\sqrt{17}17​ does it.

    Answer

    No. 17\sqrt{17}17​ is irrational: its decimal neither ends nor repeats, and no fraction equals it.

    Full solution

    A fraction has a decimal that terminates or eventually repeats.

    No whole number squares to 171717, since the perfect squares jump from 161616 to 252525.

    42=164^2=1642=16 52=255^2=2552=25

    So 171717 is not a perfect square, and 17\sqrt{17}17​ is irrational: its decimal neither terminates nor repeats.

    No fraction has a decimal like that, so 17\sqrt{17}17​ is not any fraction, with its decimal finished or not.

    A decimal cut off after a few places is only a rounded value, not the exact root.

    For example, 4.1234.1234.123 is the fraction 41231000\frac{4123}{1000}10004123​, and its square misses 171717.

    4.1232=16.9991294.123^2=16.9991294.1232=16.999129

    The exact value stays 17\sqrt{17}17​.

    Answer

    No. 17\sqrt{17}17​ is irrational: its decimal neither ends nor repeats, and no fraction equals it.

    Key idea

    An irrational root has a decimal that neither terminates nor repeats, so no fraction gives it exactly.

  10. Problem 10 A counting claim

    Rosa says exactly 222222 whole numbers nnn make n\sqrt nn​ lie strictly between 111111 and 121212, and that every one of those roots is irrational. Is she correct about both parts? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      A bigger number has a bigger root, so a condition on n\sqrt nn​ can be turned into a condition on nnn.

    2. Hint 2

      Square the two bounds, then count the whole numbers strictly between the results, with neither end included.

    3. Hint 3

      Ask whether any perfect square can lie between the squares of two neighboring whole numbers.

    Answer

    Yes. The numbers are n=122n=122n=122 to n=143n=143n=143, twenty-two in all, and each of their roots is irrational.

    Full solution

    Since a bigger number has a bigger root, n\sqrt nn​ lies strictly between 111111 and 121212 exactly when nnn lies strictly between their squares.

    112=12111^2=121112=121 122=14412^2=144122=144

    So nnn runs from 122122122 to 143143143.

    Counting from 122122122 through 143143143 includes both ends.

    143−122+1=22143-122+1=22143−122+1=22

    The next perfect square after 121121121 is 144144144, because 111111 and 121212 are neighboring whole numbers.

    So none of 122122122 to 143143143 is a perfect square.

    The square root of a whole number that is not a perfect square is irrational, so all 222222 roots are irrational.

    Both parts of Rosa's statement are correct.

    Answer

    Yes. The numbers are n=122n=122n=122 to n=143n=143n=143, twenty-two in all, and each of their roots is irrational.

    Key idea

    Squaring the bounds turns a condition on a root into a range of radicands, and every whole number strictly between two neighboring perfect squares has an irrational root.
