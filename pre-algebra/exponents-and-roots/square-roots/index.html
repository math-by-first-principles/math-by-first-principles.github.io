Square Roots

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Squaring 555 gives 252525. A square root reverses the question: which nonnegative number multiplied by itself gives 252525? The answer is 555. Picture a square made of 252525 unit tiles: it has five tiles along each side. A square root finds a square’s side length from its area.

Learning goals

  • Undo squaring to evaluate square roots of perfect squares, including the side of a square from its area
  • Explain why the radical sign returns only the non-negative root
  • Pull a perfect-square factor out with ab=ab\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}ab​=a​b​ for non-negative aaa and bbb
  • Trap a non-perfect-square root between neighboring perfect squares
  • Recognize why 2\sqrt{2}2​ is irrational, and why an exact radical is not the same as a rounded decimal

Squaring, and undoing it

A perfect square is a whole number obtained by squaring a whole number: 0,1,4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81,100,…0,1,4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81,100,\ldots0,1,4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81,100,…. A square root reverses that operation. Since 62=366^2=3662=36, the nonnegative square root of 363636 is 666.

The radical sign x\sqrt{\phantom{x}}x​ marks a square root. The number under it is the radicand. For example, 25=5\sqrt{25}=525​=5 is read “the square root of twenty-five is five.”

Squaring and square-rooting are inverse operations on nonnegative numbers: each undoes the other, just as addition can undo subtraction.

For example, 72=49=7\sqrt{7^2}=\sqrt{49}=772​=49​=7, and (49)2=72=49(\sqrt{49})^2=7^2=49(49​)2=72=49. For any a≥0a\ge0a≥0,

a2=a,(a)2=a.\sqrt{a^2}=a,\qquad (\sqrt a)^2=a.a2​=a,(a​)2=a.

Starting with a negative number is different: (−5)2=25(-5)^2=25(−5)2=25, but 25=5\sqrt{25}=525​=5, not −5-5−5. In general, x2=∣x∣\sqrt{x^2}=|x|x2​=∣x∣, the absolute value or size of xxx.

Why the root is nonnegative

Both 525^252 and (−5)2(-5)^2(−5)2 equal 252525. We need a convention so the symbol 25\sqrt{25}25​ names one number that we can use unambiguously in a calculation. The chosen value is the principal square root, the nonnegative one.

Why a\sqrt{a}a​ is defined as the non-negative root#

For example, 62=366^2=3662=36 and (−6)2=36(-6)^2=36(−6)2=36. We define 36\sqrt{36}36​ to mean 666; the other root can be written −36=−6-\sqrt{36}=-6−36​=−6.

The same issue arises for any positive number aaa. If x2=ax^2=ax2=a, its opposite also works:

(−x)2=(−x)(−x)=x2=a.(-x)^2=(-x)(-x)=x^2=a.(−x)2=(−x)(−x)=x2=a.

Choosing the nonnegative root gives the radical one value. It cannot have two different nonnegative values, because a larger nonnegative number has a larger square. For a=0a=0a=0, only 000 squares to 000, so 0=0\sqrt0=00​=0. The choice of the nonnegative root is a convention, not a claim that the negative root fails to square to aaa.

∎

Roots of negative numbers

No real number squares to a negative number: a positive or a negative number times itself is positive, and the square of zero is zero. So −9\sqrt{-9}−9​ is not a real number. We use only nonnegative radicands in this lesson.

The product rule for square roots

A product of roots is useful when a radicand contains a perfect-square factor. For 9×499\times499×49, try multiplying the separate roots, 3×7=213\times7=213×7=21. Squaring checks it: 212=441=9×4921^2=441=9\times49212=441=9×49. The next argument explains why this works beyond that example.

Why ab=a b\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\,\sqrt{b}ab​=a​b​ for non-negative aaa and bbb#

Start with 161616 and 363636, whose roots are 444 and 666. Square the product of those roots:

(4×6)2=(4×6)(4×6)=(4×4)(6×6)=16×36.\begin{aligned} (4\times6)^2&=(4\times6)(4\times6)\\ &=(4\times4)(6\times6)=16\times36. \end{aligned}(4×6)2​=(4×6)(4×6)=(4×4)(6×6)=16×36.​

Since 4×64\times64×6 is nonnegative and squares to 16×3616\times3616×36, it is the principal root of that product. The same regrouping works for any two nonnegative radicands.

Let a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0. Their principal roots exist and are nonnegative. Square their product and regroup:

(a b)2=(a)(b)(a)(b)=(a)2(b)2=ab.\begin{aligned} (\sqrt a\,\sqrt b)^2 &=(\sqrt a)(\sqrt b)(\sqrt a)(\sqrt b) \\ &=(\sqrt a)^2(\sqrt b)^2=ab. \end{aligned}(a​b​)2​=(a​)(b​)(a​)(b​)=(a​)2(b​)2=ab.​

The product a b\sqrt a\,\sqrt ba​b​ is nonnegative and its square is ababab. By the definition of the principal root,

ab=a b.\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\,\sqrt b.ab​=a​b​.

∎

Division works similarly. For example, 9/4=3/2=9/4\sqrt{9/4}=3/2=\sqrt9/\sqrt49/4​=3/2=9​/4​. In general, for a≥0a\ge0a≥0 and b>0b>0b>0, the quotient a/b\sqrt a/\sqrt ba​/b​ is nonnegative and its square is a/ba/ba/b. Therefore

ab=ab.\sqrt{\frac ab}=\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt b}.ba​​=b​a​​.

To simplify a root, split its radicand into a perfect square times the remaining factor.

Worked example 2 Simplify 12\sqrt{12}12​

Since 12=4×312=4\times312=4×3, separate the factors under the root:

12=4×3=4 3.\sqrt{12}=\sqrt{4\times3}=\sqrt4\,\sqrt3.12​=4×3​=4​3​.

Now 4=2\sqrt4=24​=2, so

12=23.\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3.12​=23​.

The notation 232\sqrt323​ means 2×32\times\sqrt32×3​. The remaining radicand 333 has no perfect-square factor greater than 111, so the root is simplified. Choosing the largest perfect-square factor finishes in one step; choosing 111 would not simplify anything.

Identical root terms can combine by the distributive property: 23+53=(2+5)3=732\sqrt3+5\sqrt3=(2+5)\sqrt3=7\sqrt323​+53​=(2+5)3​=73​. This adds the counts of the same factor; it does not split a root across addition.

Estimating roots

To locate 10\sqrt{10}10​, find the neighboring perfect squares: 9<10<169<10<169<10<16. Larger nonnegative numbers have larger squares, so their roots keep the same order:

3<10<4.3<\sqrt{10}<4.3<10​<4.

For a closer estimate, square trial decimals. Since 3.12=9.613.1^2=9.613.12=9.61 and 3.22=10.243.2^2=10.243.22=10.24, the root lies between 3.13.13.1 and 3.23.23.2. A calculator gives approximately 3.163.163.16.

The method works for any non-perfect-square whole number: find the perfect squares immediately below and above it, then take their whole-number roots as bounds.

To choose the nearest whole number, test the halfway value. For 10\sqrt{10}10​, the midpoint between 333 and 444 is 3.53.53.5. Since 3.52=12.253.5^2=12.253.52=12.25 and 10<12.2510<12.2510<12.25, the root is below 3.53.53.5 and nearer 333.

In general, let ppp be the nonnegative whole-number lower bound. If p<N<p+1p<\sqrt N<p+1p<N​<p+1, compare NNN with (p+0.5)2(p+0.5)^2(p+0.5)2. A smaller radicand puts the root nearer ppp; a larger one puts it nearer p+1p+1p+1; equality puts it exactly halfway. This works because squaring preserves order for nonnegative numbers. Square further trial decimals when you need a closer estimate.

Why some roots are irrational

A rational number is a fraction of integers with a nonzero denominator. An irrational number cannot be written as such a fraction. Its decimal neither ends nor repeats, unlike the fraction decimals studied earlier.

Why is 2\sqrt22​ irrational? It is not a whole number, since 12<2<221^2<2<2^212<2<22. A non-whole fraction in lowest terms cannot square to a whole number either: its denominator stays greater than 111. Squaring only repeats the primes already present, so it cannot create a shared prime factor to cancel. For example,

(32)2=3×32×2=94.\left(\frac32\right)^2=\frac{3\times3}{2\times2}=\frac94.(23​)2=2×23×3​=49​.

The denominator’s prime factors are still absent from the numerator. The same holds for any fraction in lowest terms. Thus no fraction squares to 222. More generally, the square root of a whole number that is not a perfect square is irrational.

Exact versus approximate

2\sqrt22​ names the exact value. The decimal 1.411.411.41 is an approximation: 1.412=1.98811.41^2=1.98811.412=1.9881, not 222, so write 2≈1.41\sqrt2\approx1.412​≈1.41. Computing more decimal digits can improve an estimate, but it does not prove irrationality.

Check your understanding

Which explanation correctly combines why 2\sqrt22​ is irrational with what 1.411.411.41 means?

Answer choices

A whole number cannot square to 222. A non-whole fraction in lowest terms cannot either: after squaring, the numerator and denominator still have no shared prime factors, so the denominator cannot disappear. Thus 2\sqrt22​ is not a fraction. Meanwhile 1.412=1.98811.41^2=1.98811.412=1.9881, so 1.411.411.41 only approximates it. A number between two whole numbers can still be a fraction, such as 3/23/23/2. Ruling out whole numbers alone is not enough: the lowest-terms argument rules out the other fractions.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Halving instead of rooting. 16=4\sqrt{16}=416​=4, not 888. Check by squaring the answer.
  • Giving both signs for a radical. 16=4\sqrt{16}=416​=4 only, though both 444 and −4-4−4 square to 161616.
  • Splitting a root over a sum. 9+16=5\sqrt{9+16}=59+16​=5, but 9+16=7\sqrt9+\sqrt{16}=79​+16​=7.
  • Treating a rounded value as exact. 2≈1.41\sqrt2\approx1.412​≈1.41; the two are not equal.

Key takeaways

  • Principal root: a\sqrt aa​ is the nonnegative number whose square is aaa, for a≥0a\ge0a≥0.
  • Inverse operations: a2=a\sqrt{a^2}=aa2​=a for a≥0a\ge0a≥0; for any real aaa, it equals ∣a∣|a|∣a∣.
  • Products: ab=a b\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\,\sqrt bab​=a​b​ for a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0. Extract perfect-square factors to simplify.
  • Quotients: a/b=a/b\sqrt{a/b}=\sqrt a/\sqrt ba/b​=a​/b​ for a≥0a\ge0a≥0 and b>0b>0b>0.
  • Estimates: neighboring perfect squares bound a root; trial squares refine the estimate.
  • Irrational roots: a non-perfect-square whole number has an irrational root. Keep its radical for an exact value; use ≈\approx≈ for rounded decimals.
  • Negative radicands: a negative number has no real square root.

Why no fraction can square to 2

Why no fraction can square to 2#

First notice what squaring does to prime factors. For example, 12=2×2×312=2\times2\times312=2×2×3, so 12212^2122 contains four factors of 222 and two of 333. Each prime’s count doubles. In particular, if a whole number’s square is even, that number must already contain a factor 222.

Suppose 2\sqrt22​ could be written as p/qp/qp/q in lowest terms, with positive whole numbers p,qp,qp,q. Squaring and multiplying by q2q^2q2 would give

p2q2=2,p2=2q2.\frac{p^2}{q^2}=2,\qquad p^2=2q^2.q2p2​=2,p2=2q2.

Thus p2p^2p2 is even, so ppp is even. Write p=2kp=2kp=2k for some whole number kkk. Substitute this into the same equality:

(2k)2=2q2,4k2=2q2.(2k)^2=2q^2,\qquad 4k^2=2q^2.(2k)2=2q2,4k2=2q2.

Divide both sides by 222:

2k2=q2.2k^2=q^2.2k2=q2.

Now q2q^2q2 is even, so qqq is even too. Both ppp and qqq are divisible by 222, which contradicts the choice of a fraction in lowest terms. Therefore no such fraction exists, and 2\sqrt22​ is irrational.

∎

A bit of history (optional)

A square one unit wide has a diagonal of length 2\sqrt22​. The line is easy to draw, yet no fraction gives its exact length. Ancient Greek mathematicians encountered such lengths while studying geometry, challenging the hope that whole-number ratios could describe every length.

One late story says the person who revealed irrational quantities drowned at sea, but does not name that person. A separate story says Hippasus, a Pythagorean thinker, drowned after revealing a secret about a twelve-faced solid. Other versions of the secrecy stories tell of expulsion instead.

Modern writers have tried to connect Hippasus with the discovery of irrational quantities. No ancient source makes that connection, so the familiar combined story is speculation. Some scholars still credit Hippasus with the discovery itself.

These accounts were written much later and disagree. Historians cannot treat the drowning tale as a reliable account of the discovery. The lasting puzzle is real, though: an ordinary drawn line can have a length that no fraction names exactly.

The Pythagoreans and the competing stories, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
