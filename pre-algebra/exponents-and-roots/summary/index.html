Exponents and Roots: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Base and exponent
In ana^{n}an with nnn a positive whole number, the base aaa is the number used as a factor and the exponent nnn counts the copies multiplied. The exponent is a count, never a factor itself; a zero or negative exponent extends the notation but is no longer a literal copy count.
Squared, cubed
a2a^{2}a2 is "aaa squared", the area of a square of side aaa; a3a^{3}a3 is "aaa cubed", the volume of a cube of edge aaa.
Perfect square
A whole number reached by squaring a whole number: 1,4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81,100,121,1441, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 1441,4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81,100,121,144.
Radical sign x\sqrt{\phantom{x}}x​ and radicand
The radical sign asks for a square root; the radicand is the number sitting under it.
Principal square root
The single non-negative value the radical returns. Both 666 and −6-6−6 square to 363636, but 36=6\sqrt{36} = 636​=6; the other is written −36-\sqrt{36}−36​.
Irrational number
A number whose decimal neither ends nor repeats, so no fraction equals it. N\sqrt{N}N​ is irrational whenever the whole number NNN is not a perfect square, so 2\sqrt{2}2​ stays in radical form.
Reciprocal
Flipping a number over: aaa gives 1a\tfrac{1}{a}a1​, and 25\tfrac{2}{5}52​ gives 52\tfrac{5}{2}25​. Only a nonzero number has one.
Coefficient
In a×10na \times 10^{n}a×10n the coefficient aaa carries the meaningful digits and 10n10^{n}10n carries the magnitude: 4.2×1094.2 \times 10^{9}4.2×109 and 4.2×10−94.2 \times 10^{-9}4.2×10−9 differ only in size.
Renormalizing
Shifting a factor of ten between the coefficient and the power until the coefficient sits in 1≤a<101 \le a < 101≤a<10: 0.4×1060.4 \times 10^{6}0.4×106 becomes 4×1054 \times 10^{5}4×105.

Formulas and theorems

  • Definition of a power

    an=a×a×⋯×a⏟n copies of aa^{n} = \underbrace{a \times a \times \cdots \times a}_{n \text{ copies of } a}an=n copies of aa×a×⋯×a​​

    Use when nnn a whole number with n≥1n \ge 1n≥1; any base. a1=aa^{1} = aa1=a, so a number with no written exponent carries an exponent of 111.

  • Powers of ten

    10n=100…0⏟n zeros10^{n} = 1\underbrace{00\ldots0}_{n \text{ zeros}}10n=1n zeros00…0​​

    Use when nnn a whole number with n≥0n \ge 0n≥0; the exponent is exactly the zero count, and for negative nnn take the reciprocal instead. The exponent is the count; the power is the number it stands for.

  • Exponents in the order of operations

    Parentheses, then exponents, then ×\times× and ÷\div÷, then +++ and −-−.

    Use when A power binds to its own base alone unless parentheses widen it: in 5×235 \times 2^{3}5×23 the exponent acts on the 222, not the product.

  • Sign of a power

    (−a)n>0 for even n(−a)n<0 for odd n−an=−(an)\begin{gathered} (-a)^{n} > 0 \text{ for even } n \\ (-a)^{n} < 0 \text{ for odd } n \\ -a^{n} = -(a^{n}) \end{gathered}(−a)n>0 for even n(−a)n<0 for odd n−an=−(an)​

    Use when a>0a > 0a>0. Only parentheses pull the minus into the base; without them the power comes first and the sign after, leaving −an-a^{n}−an negative for every nnn.

    e.g. (−3)2=9(-3)^{2} = 9(−3)2=9, (−3)3=−27(-3)^{3} = -27(−3)3=−27, and −32=−9-3^{2} = -9−32=−9.

  • Product rule

    am×an=am+na^{m} \times a^{n} = a^{m+n}am×an=am+n

    Use when The SAME base in both factors, and a≠0a \neq 0a=0 once either exponent is zero or negative. Unlike bases never merge under this rule.

    e.g. 73×72=757^{3} \times 7^{2} = 7^{5}73×72=75.

  • Quotient rule

    aman=am−n\frac{a^{m}}{a^{n}} = a^{m-n}anam​=am−n

    Use when Same base top and bottom, and a≠0a \neq 0a=0. Always subtract the denominator's exponent from the numerator's; the result may be zero or negative.

    e.g. 5853=55\dfrac{5^{8}}{5^{3}} = 5^{5}5358​=55, while 5358=5−5\dfrac{5^{3}}{5^{8}} = 5^{-5}5853​=5−5.

  • Power rule

    (am)n=am×n\left(a^{m}\right)^{n} = a^{m \times n}(am)n=am×n

    Use when Any base with integer exponents, and a≠0a \neq 0a=0 once an exponent is zero or negative.

    e.g. (43)2=46=4096\left(4^{3}\right)^{2} = 4^{6} = 4096(43)2=46=4096.

  • Power of a product or a quotient

    (ab)n=anbn,(ab)n=anbn(ab)^{n} = a^{n} b^{n}, \qquad \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{n} = \frac{a^{n}}{b^{n}}(ab)n=anbn,(ba​)n=bnan​

    Use when b≠0b \neq 0b=0 for the quotient always. Once nnn is zero or negative, every factor being raised must also be nonzero, both aaa and bbb, since a zero base to a non-positive power is undefined. The exponent lands on each factor of a PRODUCT, never on the terms of a sum.

    e.g. (2×5)3=23×53=8×125=1000(2 \times 5)^{3} = 2^{3} \times 5^{3} = 8 \times 125 = 1000(2×5)3=23×53=8×125=1000.

  • Zero exponent

    a0=1a^{0} = 1a0=1

    Use when a≠0a \neq 0a=0, and 000^{0}00 is undefined. Every other base gives 111, whole, fractional, or negative.

    e.g. 50=15^{0} = 150=1, (37)0=1\left(\tfrac{3}{7}\right)^{0} = 1(73​)0=1, and (−4)0=1(-4)^{0} = 1(−4)0=1.

  • Negative exponent

    a−n=1an,(ab)−n=(ba)na^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^{n}}, \qquad \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-n} = \left(\frac{b}{a}\right)^{n}a−n=an1​,(ba​)−n=(ab​)n

    Use when a≠0a \neq 0a=0, and b≠0b \neq 0b=0 for the fraction form. The minus sign moves the power across the fraction bar and never touches the sign of the value.

    e.g. 4−2=1164^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{16}4−2=161​ and (34)−2=169\left(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)^{-2} = \tfrac{16}{9}(43​)−2=916​.

  • Square root as the inverse of squaring

    a2=a,(a)2=a\sqrt{a^{2}} = a, \qquad \left(\sqrt{a}\right)^{2} = aa2​=a,(a​)2=a

    Use when a≥0a \ge 0a≥0. The radical returns the principal (non-negative) root, one value and never ±\pm±. A negative radicand has no real square root.

    e.g. 112=11\sqrt{11^{2}} = 11112​=11 and (11)2=11\left(\sqrt{11}\right)^{2} = 11(11​)2=11.

  • Product and quotient rules for square roots

    ab=a b,ab=ab\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\,\sqrt{b}, \qquad \sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}ab​=a​b​,ba​​=b​a​​

    Use when a≥0a \ge 0a≥0 and b≥0b \ge 0b≥0, tightened to b>0b > 0b>0 for the quotient. Products and quotients split; sums and differences do not.

    e.g. 9×49=3×7=21\sqrt{9 \times 49} = 3 \times 7 = 219×49​=3×7=21.

  • Trapping a root between whole numbers

    p2<N<q2  ⟹  p<N<qp^{2} < N < q^{2} \;\Longrightarrow\; p < \sqrt{N} < qp2<N<q2⟹p<N​<q
    Text description

    Two aligned number lines: 60 lies between 49 and 64 on the upper one, and directly below, the square root of 60 lies between 7 and 8, nearer 8.

    Use when NNN a whole number, with ppp and qqq non-negative and q=p+1q = p + 1q=p+1 for consecutive whole numbers. The root leans toward whichever perfect square NNN is nearer.

    e.g. 49<60<6449 < 60 < 6449<60<64, so 7<60<87 < \sqrt{60} < 87<60​<8, and nearer to 888.

  • Scientific notation

    N=a×10n,1≤a<10N = a \times 10^{n}, \qquad 1 \le a < 10N=a×10n,1≤a<10
    Text description

    The decimal point travels four places left to turn 45000 into 4.5 times ten to the fourth, and four places right to turn 0.00056 into 5.6 times ten to the negative fourth.

    Use when NNN positive and nnn an integer. Exactly one pair (a,n)(a, n)(a,n) fits each NNN, which makes the form unique; 30×10230 \times 10^{2}30×102 and 0.3×1040.3 \times 10^{4}0.3×104 are equal in value but not in this form.

  • Arithmetic in scientific notation

    (a×10m)(b×10n)=ab×10m+na×10mb×10n=ab×10m−n\begin{gathered} \left(a \times 10^{m}\right)\left(b \times 10^{n}\right) \\ = ab \times 10^{m+n} \\ \frac{a \times 10^{m}}{b \times 10^{n}} = \frac{a}{b} \times 10^{m-n} \end{gathered}(a×10m)(b×10n)=ab×10m+nb×10na×10m​=ba​×10m−n​

    Use when b≠0b \neq 0b=0 for the quotient. Neither result is finished until renormalized, since ababab or ab\tfrac{a}{b}ba​ can leave 1≤a<101 \le a < 101≤a<10.

  • Comparing two numbers in scientific notation

    m>n  ⟹  a×10m>b×10nm > n \;\Longrightarrow\; a \times 10^{m} > b \times 10^{n}m>n⟹a×10m>b×10n

    Use when Both positive and normalized, 1≤a<101 \le a < 101≤a<10 and 1≤b<101 \le b < 101≤b<10; an inflated coefficient breaks it. Coefficients decide only when the exponents tie.

    e.g. 3.5×106>8.1×1043.5 \times 10^{6} > 8.1 \times 10^{4}3.5×106>8.1×104, despite the smaller coefficient.

Problem types, step by step

Evaluate an expression containing powers

  1. Resolve parentheses first, including any minus sign they have captured.
  2. Evaluate every power next, multiplying the copies of the base.
  3. Then ×\times× and ÷\div÷ left to right, and finally +++ and −-−.
  4. To rank powers, evaluate each to a plain number: a bigger base need not win, since 216=655362^{16} = 65536216=65536 beats 162=25616^{2} = 256162=256.

e.g. 100−62×2=100−36×2=28100 - 6^{2} \times 2 = 100 - 36 \times 2 = 28100−62×2=100−36×2=28.

Simplify to a single power using the laws

  1. Work outward from the innermost grouping, settling each power of a power by multiplying those exponents.
  2. Combine side-by-side powers of one base by adding exponents, and a quotient of that base by subtracting them.
  3. Leave genuinely different bases side by side.

e.g. (23)3×224=21024=26\dfrac{\left(2^{3}\right)^{3} \times 2}{2^{4}} = \dfrac{2^{10}}{2^{4}} = 2^{6}24(23)3×2​=24210​=26.

Rewrite a zero or negative power with a positive exponent

  1. Combine the exponents first with the product, quotient, and power rules, carrying every minus sign.
  2. An exponent that lands on 000 turns that factor into 111.
  3. For a negative exponent, move the power across the fraction bar and drop the sign; for a fraction base, flip the fraction instead.
  4. Evaluate the positive power left behind.

e.g. a3×a−5a−4=a3−5+4=a2\dfrac{a^{3} \times a^{-5}}{a^{-4}} = a^{3-5+4} = a^{2}a−4a3×a−5​=a3−5+4=a2, for a≠0a \neq 0a=0.

Evaluate or simplify a square root

  1. If the radicand is a perfect square, name the whole number that squares to it.
  2. Otherwise split off the LARGEST perfect-square factor and take its root outside the radical, leaving the rest underneath.
  3. Check by squaring your answer back to the radicand.

e.g. 75=25×3=53\sqrt{75} = \sqrt{25 \times 3} = 5\sqrt{3}75​=25×3​=53​, and (53)2=75\left(5\sqrt{3}\right)^{2} = 75(53​)2=75 confirms it.

Estimate the root of a whole number that is not a perfect square

  1. For a whole-number radicand, find the perfect squares just below and just above it.
  2. Take roots across the inequality; those two whole-number roots bracket the answer.
  3. Decide which end it leans toward by which perfect square the radicand is nearer.
  4. For a decimal, square trial values and compare against the radicand.

e.g. 25<30<3625 < 30 < 3625<30<36, so 5<30<65 < \sqrt{30} < 65<30​<6, near the middle.

Write an ordinary number in scientific notation

  1. Place the decimal point just after the first nonzero digit; those digits become the coefficient, trailing zeros dropped.
  2. Count how many places the point moved.
  3. Moving LEFT (a number of 101010 or more) makes the exponent positive; moving RIGHT (a number below 111) makes it negative; a number already in [1,10)[1, 10)[1,10) needs no hops and takes 10010^{0}100.
  4. Confirm 1≤a<101 \le a < 101≤a<10, then multiply the power back out as a check.

e.g. 0.00056=5.6×10−40.00056 = 5.6 \times 10^{-4}0.00056=5.6×10−4, the point having moved 444 places right.

Convert scientific notation back to standard form

  1. Read the sign of the exponent: positive sends the decimal point right, negative sends it left.
  2. Move the point that many places, padding with zeros where the coefficient supplies no digit.
  3. Check the size against the sign: a positive exponent must give at least 101010, a negative one a value between 000 and 111.

e.g. 7.05×103=70507.05 \times 10^{3} = 70507.05×103=7050, and 2.5×10−4=0.000252.5 \times 10^{-4} = 0.000252.5×10−4=0.00025.

Multiply, divide, or compare in scientific notation

  1. To multiply, multiply the coefficients and ADD the exponents; to divide, divide the coefficients and SUBTRACT them.
  2. Renormalize: a coefficient of 101010 or more hands a factor of ten to the exponent, one below 111 borrows one back.
  3. To compare or order, rank by exponent first and use the coefficients only to break a tie.

e.g. (4×10−3)(5×106)=20×103=2×104\left(4 \times 10^{-3}\right)\left(5 \times 10^{6}\right) = 20 \times 10^{3} = 2 \times 10^{4}(4×10−3)(5×106)=20×103=2×104.

Exam traps

  • Trap Multiplying the base by the exponent, so 252^{5}25 is answered as 101010.

    Fix The exponent counts factors: 25=2×2×2×2×2=322^{5} = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 3225=2×2×2×2×2=32.

  • Trap Multiplying the exponents, or the bases, when two powers merely sit side by side: 23×242^{3} \times 2^{4}23×24 answered as 2122^{12}212 or 474^{7}47.

    Fix Side by side the exponents ADD and the base is untouched: 23×24=27=1282^{3} \times 2^{4} = 2^{7} = 12823×24=27=128. Exponents multiply only for a power of a power, (23)4=212\left(2^{3}\right)^{4} = 2^{12}(23)4=212.

  • Trap Combining powers of unlike bases, so 23×322^{3} \times 3^{2}23×32 becomes 656^{5}65.

    Fix The rules that add or subtract exponents (the product and quotient rules) need the SAME base; they do not apply here, since 2≠32 \neq 32=3. 23×32=8×9=722^{3} \times 3^{2} = 8 \times 9 = 7223×32=8×9=72, whereas 65=77766^{5} = 777665=7776. Unlike bases only combine under the power-of-a-product rule, and only when they already share the SAME exponent: 23×53=(2×5)3=1032^{3} \times 5^{3} = (2 \times 5)^{3} = 10^{3}23×53=(2×5)3=103.

  • Trap Using an exponent law on a SUM of powers, so 23+242^{3} + 2^{4}23+24 becomes 272^{7}27.

    Fix The laws cover multiplying and dividing only: 23+24=8+16=242^{3} + 2^{4} = 8 + 16 = 2423+24=8+16=24, while 27=1282^{7} = 12827=128.

  • Trap Splitting a power or a radical across a sum: (2+3)2(2 + 3)^{2}(2+3)2 read as 22+322^{2} + 3^{2}22+32, or 9+16\sqrt{9 + 16}9+16​ read as 3+43 + 43+4.

    Fix Combine under the grouping first: (2+3)2=25(2 + 3)^{2} = 25(2+3)2=25, not 131313, and 9+16=25=5\sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 59+16​=25​=5, not 777. Only products and quotients split.

  • Trap Reading a0a^{0}a0 as 000, so 909^{0}90 is answered as 000.

    Fix A zero exponent means no factors of the base, which leaves the multiplication at 111: 90=19^{0} = 190=1. Only 000^{0}00 is undefined.

  • Trap Treating a negative exponent as a negative value, so 2−32^{-3}2−3 is answered as −8-8−8.

    Fix It calls for a reciprocal, not a sign change: 2−3=123=182^{-3} = \tfrac{1}{2^{3}} = \tfrac{1}{8}2−3=231​=81​. A value goes negative only from a negative BASE with an odd exponent, as in (−2)−3=−18(-2)^{-3} = -\tfrac{1}{8}(−2)−3=−81​.

  • Trap Letting a bare minus sign into the base, so −42-4^{2}−42 is answered as 161616.

    Fix Only parentheses capture the sign: −42=−(4×4)=−16-4^{2} = -(4 \times 4) = -16−42=−(4×4)=−16, while (−4)2=16(-4)^{2} = 16(−4)2=16.

  • Trap Answering 16\sqrt{16}16​ with −4-4−4 or with ±4\pm 4±4.

    Fix The radical returns the principal root alone, 16=4\sqrt{16} = 416​=4. The negative solution must be asked for, as −16-\sqrt{16}−16​.

  • Trap Stopping at 18×10818 \times 10^{8}18×108 or 0.5×1030.5 \times 10^{3}0.5×103 and calling it scientific notation.

    Fix Renormalize until 1≤a<101 \le a < 101≤a<10: 18×108=1.8×10918 \times 10^{8} = 1.8 \times 10^{9}18×108=1.8×109, and 0.5×103=5×1020.5 \times 10^{3} = 5 \times 10^{2}0.5×103=5×102.
