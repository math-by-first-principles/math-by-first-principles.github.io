    1. Across the bridge in both directions . Foundational, 10 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 10 pts

    Scientific notation splits a number into a coefficient, which carries the significant digits, and a power of ten, which carries the size. Converting is a matter of parking the decimal point so that one nonzero digit stands in front of it, then recording in the exponent how far the point travelled and which way it went. These parts cross that bridge in both directions.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Write 284,000,000284{,}000{,}000284,000,000 and 0.00009050.00009050.0000905 in scientific notation. For each one, say how many places the decimal point moved and in which direction.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Write 4.06×1064.06 \times 10^{6}4.06×106 and 9.3×10−59.3 \times 10^{-5}9.3×10−5 as ordinary numbers, and say for each one how the sign of the exponent decided which way the decimal point travelled.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Someone reads you a positive number in ordinary form and asks for the sign of its exponent in scientific notation before you are allowed to count anything. Explain what feature of the number settles that sign, covering numbers of every size, and then explain what fixes the size of the exponent.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Every conversion here is the same trick: put the decimal point where exactly one nonzero digit stands in front of it, then record what that move cost.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      An exponent is an instruction for the point. Its sign says which way to walk and its size says how many steps, with zeros filling any place the digits do not reach.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask first what a number looks like when it needs no move at all, then what changes when it is bigger than that, and when it is smaller.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    284,000,000=2.84×108284{,}000{,}000 = 2.84 \times 10^{8}284,000,000=2.84×108, with the point moving 888 places left, and 0.0000905=9.05×10−50.0000905 = 9.05 \times 10^{-5}0.0000905=9.05×10−5, with the point moving 555 places right.

    Part B

    4.06×106=4,060,0004.06 \times 10^{6} = 4{,}060{,}0004.06×106=4,060,000 and 9.3×10−5=0.0000939.3 \times 10^{-5} = 0.0000939.3×10−5=0.000093.

    Part C

    Where the number sits relative to 111 and 101010 settles the sign: at least 101010 gives a positive exponent, between 000 and 111 gives a negative one, and from 111 up to but not including 101010 gives 000. The size of the exponent is the number of places the decimal point has to move.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Take the large number first. Its decimal point sits after the final zero, and exactly one nonzero digit has to end up in front of it, so slide the point left until it rests between the 222 and the 888. Counting the hops from the far right to that spot gives 888 places. Each leftward hop divides by 101010, so multiplying back by 10810^{8}108 restores the value:

    284,000,000=2.84×108.284{,}000{,}000 = 2.84 \times 10^{8}.284,000,000=2.84×108.

    The six trailing zeros are not significant digits. They were only holding the size, and the power of ten now holds it instead.

    The small number moves the other way. Its first nonzero digit is the 999 in the fifth decimal place, so the point slides 555 places right to sit just after it. Each rightward hop multiplies by 101010, so dividing back five times, which is multiplying by 10−510^{-5}10−5, leaves the value unchanged:

    0.0000905=9.05×10−5.0.0000905 = 9.05 \times 10^{-5}.0.0000905=9.05×10−5.

    The zero between the 999 and the 555 stays, because it sits among the significant digits, while the four zeros in front of the 999 were placeholders and disappear with the move. Both coefficients, 2.842.842.84 and 9.059.059.05, are at least 111 and below 101010, which is what the form demands.

    Part B

    An exponent is an instruction for the decimal point: its sign gives the direction and its size gives the number of places.

    The first exponent is positive, so the number is large and the point moves right, 666 places. After the 444 there are only two digits to pass, so four zeros fill the places the digits do not reach:

    4.06×106=4,060,000.4.06 \times 10^{6} = 4{,}060{,}000.4.06×106=4,060,000.

    The second exponent is negative, so the number is small and the point moves left, 555 places, padding with zeros as it goes:

    9.3×10−5=0.000093.9.3 \times 10^{-5} = 0.000093.9.3×10−5=0.000093.

    The check is that the direction matches the size in each case. Multiplying by 10610^{6}106 has to grow the coefficient, and 4,060,0004{,}060{,}0004,060,000 is far larger than 4.064.064.06. Multiplying by 10−510^{-5}10−5 has to shrink it, and 0.0000930.0000930.000093 is far smaller than 9.39.39.3, while still being a positive number: the minus sign lives on the exponent, not on the value.

    Part C

    The sign is decided before any counting, because it only records which way the point has to travel, and the size of the number already says that.

    If the number is 101010 or more, its first nonzero digit sits at least one place to the left of where the coefficient needs it, so the point moves left. Leftward moves shrink the number, and the power of ten has to pay that back by growing it, so the exponent is positive.

    If the number is between 000 and 111, its first nonzero digit sits to the right of the point, so the point moves right. Rightward moves grow the number, so the power of ten has to shrink it back, and the exponent is negative.

    The remaining case is a number from 111 up to but not including 101010. It is already a legal coefficient, so the point does not move at all, and there is nothing to pay back:

    7.5=7.5×100.7.5 = 7.5 \times 10^{0}.7.5=7.5×100.

    That is why the three ranges line up with the three signs, and the boundaries of the coefficient range are exactly where the sign changes.

    The size of the exponent is settled by counting, and it counts places. Every place the point moves is one factor of ten gained or lost, so moving it kkk places has to be compensated by kkk factors of ten in the other direction. That is what the exponent records, which is why an exponent of 888 and an exponent of −5-5−5 describe journeys of eight places and five places, differing only in which way the point walked.

    In one line

    284,000,000=2.84×108284{,}000{,}000 = 2.84 \times 10^{8}284,000,000=2.84×108 and 0.0000905=9.05×10−50.0000905 = 9.05 \times 10^{-5}0.0000905=9.05×10−5; going the other way, 4.06×106=4,060,0004.06 \times 10^{6} = 4{,}060{,}0004.06×106=4,060,000 and 9.3×10−5=0.0000939.3 \times 10^{-5} = 0.0000939.3×10−5=0.000093. A number at least 101010 takes a positive exponent, a number between 000 and 111 takes a negative one, and a number from 111 up to but not including 101010 takes 000, while the size of the exponent is always the number of places the decimal point moves.

    2. Coefficients in one hand, powers of ten in the other . Foundational, 11 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Foundational 11 pts

    Multiplying or dividing two numbers in scientific notation splits into two smaller jobs: one on the coefficients and one on the powers of ten. What is left afterwards is a tidying step, because the coefficient the arithmetic hands back does not always land where the form requires it to.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Compute (7×105)×(8×106)(7 \times 10^{5}) \times (8 \times 10^{6})(7×105)×(8×106) and give the result in proper scientific notation.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Compute 3.5×1027×106\dfrac{3.5 \times 10^{2}}{7 \times 10^{6}}7×1063.5×102​ and give the result in proper scientific notation.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      A student computes (6×104)×(4×103)(6 \times 10^{4}) \times (4 \times 10^{3})(6×104)×(4×103) and writes: "That is 24×10724 \times 10^{7}24×107, but 242424 is too big for a coefficient, so I make it 2.42.42.4. Making the coefficient ten times smaller means the power of ten must get ten times smaller too, so the answer is 2.4×1062.4 \times 10^{6}2.4×106." Identify exactly where that reasoning goes wrong, give the correct answer, and describe a check that would have caught the slip.

      Find and correct the error Say which line first goes wrong, why it is wrong, and then do it correctly. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Treat the coefficients and the powers of ten as two separate calculations, then look hard at whatever the first of them handed you.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      When the coefficients divide to something under 111, the power of ten has to make up the shortfall, and it does so in the opposite direction to the case where the coefficient came out too large.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Whatever you take out of the coefficient has to arrive somewhere. Ask what would happen to the value if both pieces of the product were made smaller at once.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    5.6×10125.6 \times 10^{12}5.6×1012.

    Part B

    5×10−55 \times 10^{-5}5×10−5.

    Part C

    The intermediate 24×10724 \times 10^{7}24×107 is right, but the repair pushes the value the wrong way: shrinking the coefficient has to be paid for by growing the power, so the answer is 2.4×1082.4 \times 10^{8}2.4×108. Multiplying 60,00060{,}00060,000 by 4,0004{,}0004,000 in ordinary form is the check.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Regroup so that the coefficients stand together and the powers of ten stand together, which multiplication allows:

    (7×105)×(8×106)=(7×8)×(105×106).(7 \times 10^{5}) \times (8 \times 10^{6}) = (7 \times 8) \times (10^{5} \times 10^{6}).(7×105)×(8×106)=(7×8)×(105×106).

    The coefficients give 565656, and the two powers of ten combine by adding their exponents:

    56×1011.56 \times 10^{11}.56×1011.

    That is the right value in the wrong form, because 565656 is not below 101010. Peel one factor of ten off the coefficient and hand it to the power:

    56×1011=5.6×101×1011=5.6×1012.56 \times 10^{11} = 5.6 \times 10^{1} \times 10^{11} = 5.6 \times 10^{12}.56×1011=5.6×101×1011=5.6×1012.

    The coefficient is now 5.65.65.6, safely between 111 and 101010. Nothing changed value along the way: the coefficient was divided by ten and the power was multiplied by ten in the same step, and those two cancel each other exactly.

    Part B

    Split the quotient the same way, coefficients with coefficients and powers of ten with powers of ten:

    3.5×1027×106=3.57×102106.\frac{3.5 \times 10^{2}}{7 \times 10^{6}} = \frac{3.5}{7} \times \frac{10^{2}}{10^{6}}.7×1063.5×102​=73.5​×106102​.

    The coefficients divide to 0.50.50.5, and the powers of ten divide by subtracting exponents, 2−6=−42 - 6 = -42−6=−4:

    0.5×10−4.0.5 \times 10^{-4}.0.5×10−4.

    This time the coefficient has landed too low, since 0.50.50.5 is below 111. So borrow a factor of ten from the power instead of giving one to it, which multiplies the coefficient by ten and lowers the exponent by one:

    0.5×10−4=5×10−1×10−4=5×10−5.0.5 \times 10^{-4} = 5 \times 10^{-1} \times 10^{-4} = 5 \times 10^{-5}.0.5×10−4=5×10−1×10−4=5×10−5.

    As a check, 3.5×1023.5 \times 10^{2}3.5×102 is 350350350 and 7×1067 \times 10^{6}7×106 is 7,000,0007{,}000{,}0007,000,000, and 350÷7,000,000=0.00005350 \div 7{,}000{,}000 = 0.00005350÷7,000,000=0.00005, which is exactly what 5×10−55 \times 10^{-5}5×10−5 says.

    Part C

    The first line is sound. The coefficients give 6×4=246 \times 4 = 246×4=24, and the powers of ten add their exponents to give 10710^{7}107:

    (6×104)×(4×103)=24×107.(6 \times 10^{4}) \times (4 \times 10^{3}) = 24 \times 10^{7}.(6×104)×(4×103)=24×107.

    The student is also right that 242424 cannot stand as a coefficient. The fault is in the exchange that follows. Rewriting 242424 as 2.42.42.4 divides one factor of the product by ten, and dividing one factor by ten makes the whole product ten times smaller unless the other factor is multiplied by ten to compensate. So the power of ten has to grow, not shrink:

    24×107=2.4×101×107=2.4×108.24 \times 10^{7} = 2.4 \times 10^{1} \times 10^{7} = 2.4 \times 10^{8}.24×107=2.4×101×107=2.4×108.

    The student's version is off by a factor of 100100100: one factor of ten lost by shrinking the coefficient, and a second lost by shrinking the power as well instead of growing it.

    The cheapest check is to leave scientific notation for a moment. The two numbers are 60,00060{,}00060,000 and 4,0004{,}0004,000, and their product is 240,000,000240{,}000{,}000240,000,000, which is 242424 followed by seven zeros:

    240,000,000=2.4×108.240{,}000{,}000 = 2.4 \times 10^{8}.240,000,000=2.4×108.

    The student's answer stands for 2,400,0002{,}400{,}0002,400,000, which is nowhere near it. Converting one line to ordinary form, or even counting the zeros roughly, would have caught this slip, and a misplaced factor of ten is the mistake this notation is most prone to.

    In one line

    (7×105)×(8×106)=5.6×1012(7 \times 10^{5}) \times (8 \times 10^{6}) = 5.6 \times 10^{12}(7×105)×(8×106)=5.6×1012 and 3.5×1027×106=5×10−5\dfrac{3.5 \times 10^{2}}{7 \times 10^{6}} = 5 \times 10^{-5}7×1063.5×102​=5×10−5. The student's intermediate line was correct, but the repair ran the wrong way: a coefficient made ten times smaller needs a power ten times larger, so the answer is 2.4×1082.4 \times 10^{8}2.4×108, as converting to 60,000×4,000=240,000,00060{,}000 \times 4{,}000 = 240{,}000{,}00060,000×4,000=240,000,000 confirms.

    Another way: Renormalise by counting places rather than peeling factors

    Once you have an intermediate coefficient times a power of ten, the repair can be read straight off the decimal point. Moving the point one place left in the coefficient costs a factor of ten, so the exponent goes up by one; moving it one place right gains a factor of ten, so the exponent goes down by one:

    56×1011=5.6×1012.56 \times 10^{11} = 5.6 \times 10^{12}.56×1011=5.6×1012.

    It is the same trade as peeling factors, written as a hop count instead.

    When it is worth it When the coefficient is several places out of range, so peeling one factor of ten at a time would be slow, or when you want to check a renormalisation quickly without writing anything down.

    3. How much fits in the archive . Application, 12 points. Question 3 of 5.

    Application 12 pts

    A library is digitising its collection. The storage system holds 4.8×10144.8 \times 10^{14}4.8×1014 bytes. A scanned page takes about 6×1066 \times 10^{6}6×106 bytes, while a lower-resolution image of the same page takes about 3×1043 \times 10^{4}3×104 bytes.

    1. A
      Part A.

      How many scanned pages will fit in the storage system? Give the count in proper scientific notation, and show the two halves of the division separately.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      How many of the lower-resolution images would fit in the same storage system? Give the count in proper scientific notation and also written out as an ordinary number.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Without using either count from the earlier parts, work out from the two file sizes alone how many times as many lower-resolution images as scanned pages this system can hold. Then explain why that factor can be found from the file sizes without knowing the capacity at all.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 5 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Each count is a single division: the room available divided by the room one item takes. Handle the coefficients and the powers of ten as two separate steps.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      For the written-out version, let the exponent say how many places the point travels and put a zero in every place the digits do not reach.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask how much room one item of each kind takes, then how many times over the smaller one fits into the space the larger one needs.

    That is every hint for this question.

    The answer

    Part A

    8×1078 \times 10^{7}8×107 pages.

    Part B

    1.6×10101.6 \times 10^{10}1.6×1010 images, which written out is 16,000,000,00016{,}000{,}000{,}00016,000,000,000.

    Part C

    200200200 times as many, because one scanned page takes 200200200 times the room of one image. Cutting the same room into pieces 200200200 times smaller yields 200200200 times as many of them, whatever that room happens to be; here it divides evenly, so both counts come out whole.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The number of pages is the room available divided by the room one page takes:

    4.8×10146×106=4.86×1014106.\frac{4.8 \times 10^{14}}{6 \times 10^{6}} = \frac{4.8}{6} \times \frac{10^{14}}{10^{6}}.6×1064.8×1014​=64.8​×1061014​.

    The coefficients give 4.8÷6=0.84.8 \div 6 = 0.84.8÷6=0.8, and the powers of ten subtract their exponents, 14−6=814 - 6 = 814−6=8:

    0.8×108.0.8 \times 10^{8}.0.8×108.

    The coefficient is below 111, so this is not yet proper form. Borrowing a factor of ten from the power multiplies the coefficient by ten and lowers the exponent by one:

    0.8×108=8×107.0.8 \times 10^{8} = 8 \times 10^{7}.0.8×108=8×107.

    So about 8×1078 \times 10^{7}8×107 pages fit, which is 808080 million pages. Multiplying back is the check: 8×6=488 \times 6 = 488×6=48 and 7+6=137 + 6 = 137+6=13, so the pages would take 48×101348 \times 10^{13}48×1013 bytes, and renormalising that gives 4.8×10144.8 \times 10^{14}4.8×1014 bytes, the capacity the system started with.

    Part B

    Only the size of one item has changed, so the shape of the calculation is the same:

    4.8×10143×104=4.83×1014104.\frac{4.8 \times 10^{14}}{3 \times 10^{4}} = \frac{4.8}{3} \times \frac{10^{14}}{10^{4}}.3×1044.8×1014​=34.8​×1041014​.

    The coefficients give 4.8÷3=1.64.8 \div 3 = 1.64.8÷3=1.6, and the exponents subtract to 14−4=1014 - 4 = 1014−4=10:

    1.6×1010.1.6 \times 10^{10}.1.6×1010.

    This coefficient needs no repair, since 1.61.61.6 is already at least 111 and below 101010.

    For the ordinary form, the exponent 101010 sends the decimal point 101010 places right. There is one digit after the point in 1.61.61.6, so nine zeros fill the places the digits do not reach:

    1.6×1010=16,000,000,000.1.6 \times 10^{10} = 16{,}000{,}000{,}000.1.6×1010=16,000,000,000.

    That is sixteen billion images, and the written-out version shows why the notation earns its keep: eleven digits and three separators say what four symbols said above.

    Part C

    Compare the two file sizes directly, which is a division of one size by the other:

    6×1063×104=2×102.\frac{6 \times 10^{6}}{3 \times 10^{4}} = 2 \times 10^{2}.3×1046×106​=2×102.

    So one scanned page takes 200200200 times as much room as one lower-resolution image.

    Now think about what filling the system means. The capacity is a fixed amount of room, and it is being cut into pieces of one size or the other. Pieces that are 200200200 times smaller fit into that same room 200200200 times as often, so the count rises by exactly the factor the size fell by. The capacity never enters the comparison, because it is the same room in both cases: double it and both counts double, leaving the factor between them untouched. That is why a single division of the two file sizes answers the question, and why the answer would be identical for a library with a system ten times larger or ten times smaller.

    One caution about counting whole items. The factor of 200200200 is exact for the quotients themselves, and here both quotients come out whole, since this capacity divides evenly by both file sizes. A capacity that left a few bytes unused would round each count down to a whole item, which can shift a count by one without changing the factor between the quotients.

    The counts from the earlier parts agree with this, which is a check rather than the reason. Multiplying 8×1078 \times 10^{7}8×107 by 200200200 doubles the coefficient to 161616 and raises the exponent by 222, giving 16×10916 \times 10^{9}16×109, which renormalises to 1.6×10101.6 \times 10^{10}1.6×1010.

    In one line

    The system holds about 8×1078 \times 10^{7}8×107 scanned pages and about 1.6×10101.6 \times 10^{10}1.6×1010 lower-resolution images, which is 16,000,000,00016{,}000{,}000{,}00016,000,000,000 of them. Since a scanned page takes 200200200 times the room of an image, the system holds 200200200 times as many images as pages, and that factor follows from the two file sizes alone because the same fixed capacity is being divided in both cases.

    Answer and solution

    The answer

    Part A

    Gold leaf, plastic wrap, aluminium foil, printer paper. The exponent settles every comparison involving the gold leaf; the other three share the exponent −5-5−5, so their coefficients settle the rest.

    Part B

    The conclusion does not hold. The coefficient 0.140.140.14 is below 111, so the exponent-first rule does not apply until the figure is rewritten in proper form, and rewriting it gives 1.4×10−51.4 \times 10^{-5}1.4×10−5, which lands the film between the plastic wrap and the aluminium foil.

    Part C

    The shortcut fails: 7.5×1027.5 \times 10^{2}7.5×102 has the bigger coefficient while 1.1×1051.1 \times 10^{5}1.1×105 is by far the bigger number. What works is to compare exponents first and turn to the coefficients only when the exponents are equal.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Sort by exponent first. Three of the four carry 10−510^{-5}10−5 and one carries 10−710^{-7}10−7. Since −7-7−7 lies further left on the number line than −5-5−5, 10−710^{-7}10−7 is the smaller power of ten, and with every coefficient in the range from 111 up to 101010 no coefficient can make up a whole factor of ten. So the gold leaf is thinnest outright:

    1.2×10−7<1.3×10−5.1.2 \times 10^{-7} < 1.3 \times 10^{-5}.1.2×10−7<1.3×10−5.

    It is worth seeing why the narrow gap between the coefficients 1.21.21.2 and 1.31.31.3 cannot rescue that comparison. Written out, the two thicknesses are 0.000000120.000000120.00000012 and 0.0000130.0000130.000013, and the second is over a hundred times the first. The coefficients were never in the running.

    The other three tie on the exponent, so their powers of ten are identical and the comparison falls to the coefficients alone, where 1.3<1.7<91.3 < 1.7 < 91.3<1.7<9:

    1.3×10−5<1.7×10−5<9×10−5.1.3 \times 10^{-5} < 1.7 \times 10^{-5} < 9 \times 10^{-5}.1.3×10−5<1.7×10−5<9×10−5.

    So the order from thinnest to thickest is gold leaf, plastic wrap, aluminium foil, printer paper. The exponent settled every comparison involving the gold leaf; the coefficients settled the rest, and no comparison needed both.

    Part B

    The rule the technician is using is a real one, but it carries a condition, and the condition is not met here.

    Comparing exponents first is valid only when both coefficients sit in the range from 111 up to 101010. That range is what stops a coefficient being large enough, or small enough, to cross a whole power of ten on its own. The supplier's figure has coefficient 0.140.140.14, which is below 111, so the figure is not in scientific notation at all and its exponent cannot be set against the exponents in the table.

    Put it into proper form first. Write 0.140.140.14 as 1.4×10−11.4 \times 10^{-1}1.4×10−1 and merge that spare factor of ten into the power:

    0.14×10−4=1.4×10−1×10−4=1.4×10−5.0.14 \times 10^{-4} = 1.4 \times 10^{-1} \times 10^{-4} = 1.4 \times 10^{-5}.0.14×10−4=1.4×10−1×10−4=1.4×10−5.

    Now the comparison is legitimate. The film shares the exponent −5-5−5 with three of the four materials, so their coefficients decide, and 1.3<1.4<1.7<91.3 < 1.4 < 1.7 < 91.3<1.4<1.7<9. The film is thicker than the plastic wrap and thinner than both the aluminium foil and the printer paper, which is a long way from thickest on the list.

    The general point is that an exponent reports size honestly only once the coefficient has been pinned to its range. An unnormalised coefficient can hide a whole factor of ten, and this one hid exactly one: the −4-4−4 on the supplier's sheet was doing the work of a −5-5−5.

    Part C

    One pair is enough to retire a claim of this shape, and such a pair is easy to build on purpose: take a large coefficient with a small exponent, and a small coefficient with a large one.

    7.5×102=750,1.1×105=110,000.7.5 \times 10^{2} = 750, \qquad 1.1 \times 10^{5} = 110{,}000.7.5×102=750,1.1×105=110,000.

    Both are properly written, since 7.57.57.5 and 1.11.11.1 are each at least 111 and below 101010, so the shortcut has been given every chance to work. Yet the coefficient 7.57.57.5 is the larger of the two coefficients while 750750750 is far smaller than 110,000110{,}000110,000. The shortcut is wrong.

    The reason it fails is that the coefficient carries only the significant digits, and digits alone say nothing about size. Two coefficients both confined to the same window, 7.57.57.5 and 1.11.11.1, can belong to numbers that differ by well over a hundred, because size is the job of the power of ten.

    The working rule reverses the order of the checks. Compare the exponents first: with both coefficients between 111 and 101010, the number with the larger exponent is the larger number, since the smaller-exponent number cannot climb as high as the next power of ten while the larger-exponent number is already at least that high. Only when the exponents are equal do the coefficients decide, and then they decide on their own, because the two powers of ten are identical.

    In one line

    From thinnest to thickest the materials run gold leaf, plastic wrap, aluminium foil, printer paper, with the exponent settling every comparison involving the gold leaf and the coefficients settling the rest. The technician's conclusion fails because 0.14×10−40.14 \times 10^{-4}0.14×10−4 is not in proper form; rewritten as 1.4×10−51.4 \times 10^{-5}1.4×10−5 the film sits between the plastic wrap and the aluminium foil. Comparing coefficients first fails too, as 7.5×1027.5 \times 10^{2}7.5×102 against 1.1×1051.1 \times 10^{5}1.1×105 shows: exponents come first, and coefficients only break a tie.

    Another way: Write them out and compare place value

    Any comparison in scientific notation can be settled by converting both numbers to ordinary form and reading them from the left, exactly as place value has always been compared:

    1.2×10−7=0.00000012,1.3×10−5=0.000013.1.2 \times 10^{-7} = 0.00000012, \qquad 1.3 \times 10^{-5} = 0.000013.1.2×10−7=0.00000012,1.3×10−5=0.000013.

    The first has more zeros between the point and its first significant digit, so it is the smaller of the two.

    When it is worth it As a check on a comparison that felt slippery, and as the safest route when one of the numbers is not in proper form, since writing it out removes the question of whether its exponent may be trusted.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    All three equal 72,50072{,}50072,500, and only 7.25×1047.25 \times 10^{4}7.25×104 is in scientific notation: the coefficient 725725725 is not below 101010, and the coefficient 0.07250.07250.0725 is not at least 111.

    Part B

    Each coefficient on the list is ten times the one below it, and the allowed range is exactly one factor of ten wide, so a list stepping by tens can put exactly one member inside it. A wider range would admit two neighbouring candidates for some numbers, and those numbers would have more than one form.

    Part C

    It would still give exactly one form, because the rival range is also exactly one factor of ten wide with one end included and the other excluded. What it costs is the shape of the notation: a coefficient of 101010 would be legal, so 1,0001{,}0001,000 would have to be written 10×10210 \times 10^{2}10×102 instead of 1×1031 \times 10^{3}1×103.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Evaluate them one at a time, moving the decimal point as many places right as each exponent names.

    725×102=72,500.725 \times 10^{2} = 72{,}500.725×102=72,500.

    The second needs four places, and 7.257.257.25 has only two digits after the point, so two zeros fill the rest:

    7.25×104=72,500.7.25 \times 10^{4} = 72{,}500.7.25×104=72,500.

    The third starts further left and needs six places, landing in the same spot:

    0.0725×106=72,500.0.0725 \times 10^{6} = 72{,}500.0.0725×106=72,500.

    So all three are true statements about one number, and being true is not the same as being in scientific notation. The form asks one thing of these three beyond correctness: the coefficient must be at least 111 and below 101010. Test the three coefficients against that. The coefficient 725725725 fails at the top, since it is not below 101010. The coefficient 0.07250.07250.0725 fails at the bottom, since it is not at least 111. Only 7.257.257.25 passes at both ends, so only 7.25×1047.25 \times 10^{4}7.25×104 is in scientific notation.

    Notice what the two failures have in common. Each has the decimal point parked somewhere other than just after the first nonzero digit, with the exponent adjusted to compensate. The compensation is what keeps the value right while the form stays wrong.

    Part B

    Write the list out, dividing the coefficient by ten at each step and raising the exponent by one to keep the value fixed:

    72,500×100,7,250×101,725×102,72{,}500 \times 10^{0}, \quad 7{,}250 \times 10^{1}, \quad 725 \times 10^{2},72,500×100,7,250×101,725×102,

    72.5×103,7.25×104,0.725×105.72.5 \times 10^{3}, \quad 7.25 \times 10^{4}, \quad 0.725 \times 10^{5}.72.5×103,7.25×104,0.725×105.

    The list runs on in both directions, and every step multiplies or divides the coefficient by exactly ten, because that is what shifting one place along the power of ten costs.

    Now lay the allowed range over the list. It starts at 111 and stops just short of 101010, so its top end is exactly ten times its bottom end: the window is one factor of ten wide. The coefficients march past that window in steps of ten, so as soon as one of them is inside, the next one up is at least 101010 and out the top while the next one down is below 111 and out the bottom: at most one candidate can be inside. And because the list runs on forever in both directions while the window is a full factor of ten wide, no candidate can step clean over the window without landing in it, so at least one must be inside. Exactly one candidate is inside, then, and here it is 7.257.257.25:

    72,500=7.25×104.72{,}500 = 7.25 \times 10^{4}.72,500=7.25×104.

    The width is what does the work. Suppose the rule allowed any coefficient from 111 up to 100100100, a window a hundred times wide. Then 7.257.257.25 and 72.572.572.5 would both qualify, so 7.25×1047.25 \times 10^{4}7.25×104 and 72.5×10372.5 \times 10^{3}72.5×103 would both count as correct and two people converting the same number could disagree with each other and both be right. A window narrower than a factor of ten fails the opposite way: the candidates would step straight over it and some numbers would have no expression at all. One factor of ten, with one end included and the other excluded so that no candidate is counted twice, is the width that gives every positive number exactly one form.

    Part C

    Test the rival range the way the standard one was tested. It runs from just above 111 up to and including 101010, so its top end is again exactly ten times its bottom end, and again one end is included and the other is not. Those are the only two properties the uniqueness argument used, so the argument survives the swap unchanged: candidates stepping by tens put exactly one member inside any window one factor of ten wide, wherever that window is placed.

    So the rival convention is not wrong. Every positive number would still have exactly one expression under it. What changes is which expression that is, and for most numbers it changes nothing at all. For the number on the board, 7.257.257.25 is greater than 111 and at most 101010, so both conventions choose the same expression:

    72,500=7.25×104.72{,}500 = 7.25 \times 10^{4}.72,500=7.25×104.

    The two conventions part company only at the powers of ten themselves. Under the standard rule a coefficient of 111 is allowed and a coefficient of 101010 is not, giving

    1,000=1×103,1{,}000 = 1 \times 10^{3},1,000=1×103,

    while under the rival rule 111 is barred and 101010 is allowed, forcing 1,000=10×1021{,}000 = 10 \times 10^{2}1,000=10×102.

    That is what the standard convention buys. Its coefficients always have exactly one digit in front of the decimal point, which is what makes the notation quick to read and quick to line up: the digits sit in the same place every time and the exponent alone carries the size. Under the rival rule a coefficient could have two digits before the point, and the tidy description of the form would need an exception written into it. Uniqueness is not the reason the standard range was chosen, since both ranges have it and both get it from the width. Readability is.

    In one line

    All three expressions equal 72,50072{,}50072,500, but only 7.25×1047.25 \times 10^{4}7.25×104 is in scientific notation, since 725725725 is not below 101010 and 0.07250.07250.0725 is not at least 111. The rival range from just above 111 to 101010 would be unique for the same reason; what it loses is the single digit in front of the decimal point, since it would write 1,0001{,}0001,000 as 10×10210 \times 10^{2}10×102.
