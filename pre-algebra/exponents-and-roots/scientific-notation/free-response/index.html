  1. Problem 1 A place value record

    A whole number has 222 in its millions place, 999 in its ten-thousands place, and zeros in all its other places. Write the number in scientific notation.

    1. Hint 1

      The position of the first nonzero digit determines the power of ten.

    2. Hint 2

      Keep the zero between the 222 and the 999; the zeros after the 999 are dropped.

    Answer

    2.09×1062.09\times10^62.09×106.

    Full solution

    The two place values give the ordinary number 2,000,000+90,0002{,}000{,}000+90{,}0002,000,000+90,000, which is 2,090,0002{,}090{,}0002,090,000.

    The coefficient must have one nonzero digit before its decimal point, so it is 2.092.092.09.

    The zero between the 222 and the 999 stays, and the zeros after the 999 are dropped.

    The point moves six places left, from after the last zero to just after the 222, so the exponent is 666.

    2,090,000=2.09×1062{,}090{,}000=2.09\times10^62,090,000=2.09×106

    Answer

    2.09×1062.09\times10^62.09×106.

    Key idea

    A zero between nonzero digits stays in the coefficient; zeros after the last nonzero digit are dropped.

  2. Problem 2 Where a digit sits

    When 6.03×10−76.03\times10^{-7}6.03×10−7 is written as an ordinary decimal, in which decimal place does the digit 333 sit?

    1. Hint 1

      The exponent fixes the decimal place occupied by the leading digit.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the number out in full by moving the decimal point of 6.036.036.03 the number of places the exponent gives, then count decimal places up to the 333.

    Answer

    The ninth decimal place.

    Full solution

    The exponent −7-7−7 moves the decimal point of 6.036.036.03 seven places left.

    6.03×10−7=0.0000006036.03\times10^{-7}=0.0000006036.03×10−7=0.000000603

    The leading 666 sits in the seventh decimal place.

    The 000 follows it in the eighth place, and the 333 sits in the ninth place.

    Answer

    The ninth decimal place.

    Key idea

    In scientific notation, an exponent of −n-n−n puts the leading digit in the nnnth decimal place, and the coefficient's other digits, zeros included, follow it in order.

  3. Problem 3 A missing coefficient

    Fill the box in 0.000024=□×10−50.000024=\square\times10^{-5}0.000024=□×10−5.

    1. Hint 1

      The power of ten tells you how much the coefficient is being multiplied by.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the number that multiplies 0.000010.000010.00001 to give 0.0000240.0000240.000024.

    Answer

    2.42.42.4.

    Full solution

    The power of ten is

    10−5=0.00001.10^{-5}=0.00001.10−5=0.00001.

    Divide the original value by that factor.

    0.000024÷0.00001=2.40.000024\div0.00001=2.40.000024÷0.00001=2.4

    The coefficient is in the required scientific-notation range.

    Checking the product gives

    2.4×10−5=0.0000242.4\times10^{-5}=0.0000242.4×10−5=0.000024

    Answer

    2.42.42.4.

    Key idea

    The coefficient can be recovered by dividing a number by its power-of-ten factor.

  4. Problem 4 A file transfer

    A file contains 3.2×1043.2\times10^43.2×104 bytes, including a header of 8×1028\times10^28×102 bytes. The data after the header is sent in packets of 2×1022\times10^22×102 bytes each. How many packets are needed?

    1. Hint 1

      Only the bytes after the header are divided into packets.

    2. Hint 2

      Express the file and header sizes as ordinary numbers before subtracting.

    3. Hint 3

      Divide the remaining data size by the size of one packet.

    Answer

    156156156 packets.

    Full solution

    The file contains 32,00032{,}00032,000 bytes and the header contains 800800800 bytes.

    Subtract to find the data size.

    32,000−800=31,20032{,}000-800=31{,}20032,000−800=31,200

    Each packet holds 200200200 bytes.

    31,200÷200=15631{,}200\div200=15631,200÷200=156

    Exactly 156156156 full packets are needed.

    Checking, 156×200=31,200156\times200=31{,}200156×200=31,200 bytes, and adding the header returns the file size.

    Answer

    156156156 packets.

    Key idea

    Convert representations consistently before subtracting a part and dividing the remainder into equal groups.

  5. Problem 5 A printed design

    A printer places 2.4×1032.4\times10^32.4×103 dots along each cm of a line. A design contains three lines, each 3.5×10−23.5\times10^{-2}3.5×10−2 cm long. How many dots are printed in total? Give the count in scientific notation and as an ordinary whole number.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the dot count for one line, then account for all three lines.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiply the number of dots per cm by the length, combining their coefficients and powers of ten.

    3. Hint 3

      After multiplying by three, check that the coefficient is at least 111 and less than 101010, and renormalize if it is not.

    Answer

    2.52×1022.52\times10^22.52×102 dots, and 252252252 dots as an ordinary whole number.

    Full solution

    For one line, the coefficients multiply to 2.4×3.5=8.42.4\times3.5=8.42.4×3.5=8.4, and the exponents add.

    103×10−2=10110^3\times10^{-2}=10^1103×10−2=101

    For three lines, multiply the coefficient by three.

    3×8.4=25.23\times8.4=25.23×8.4=25.2

    The total is 25.2×10125.2\times10^125.2×101 dots.

    Put the coefficient in range by moving a factor of ten into the power.

    25.2×101=2.52×10225.2\times10^1=2.52\times10^225.2×101=2.52×102

    As an ordinary count,

    2.52×102=2522.52\times10^2=2522.52×102=252

    The design contains 252252252 dots.

    Answer

    2.52×1022.52\times10^22.52×102 dots, and 252252252 dots as an ordinary whole number.

    Key idea

    To multiply in scientific notation, multiply the coefficients, add the exponents, and renormalize the coefficient at the end.

  6. Problem 6 Batches into portions

    Each of seven batches contains 3×10−13\times10^{-1}3×10−1 liter of liquid. Together, they fill 3×1043\times10^43×104 equal portions. Find the volume of one portion in scientific notation.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the total volume from all seven batches before dividing it into portions.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide the coefficients, and subtract the exponent of the portion count from the exponent of the total.

    3. Hint 3

      Make sure the final coefficient is at least 111 and less than 101010.

    Answer

    7×10−57\times10^{-5}7×10−5 liter.

    Full solution

    The seven batches hold 7×(3×10−1)7\times(3\times10^{-1})7×(3×10−1) liters.

    Multiplying the numbers in front of the power of ten gives

    7×3=217\times3=217×3=21

    The total volume is 21×10−121\times10^{-1}21×10−1 liters.

    Move one factor of ten into the power to bring the coefficient into range.

    21×10−1=2.1×10021\times10^{-1}=2.1\times10^021×10−1=2.1×100

    That is 2.12.12.1 liters.

    Divide it by 3×1043\times10^43×104 portions by dividing the coefficients and subtracting the exponents.

    The coefficients give

    2.1÷3=0.72.1\div3=0.72.1÷3=0.7

    The exponents give

    0−4=−40-4=-40−4=−4

    This gives 0.7×10−40.7\times10^{-4}0.7×10−4 liter.

    The coefficient is below 111, so multiply it by ten and decrease the exponent by one.

    0.7×10−4=7×10−50.7\times10^{-4}=7\times10^{-5}0.7×10−4=7×10−5

    One portion contains 7×10−57\times10^{-5}7×10−5 liter.

    Checking, 3×1043\times10^43×104 portions of that size hold 21×10−121\times10^{-1}21×10−1 liters, or 2.12.12.1 liters, returning the total volume.

    Answer

    7×10−57\times10^{-5}7×10−5 liter.

    Key idea

    Find a combined quantity before dividing its coefficient and power of ten among equal portions.

  7. Problem 7 Stored record sizes

    Four records have sizes 32,00032{,}00032,000, 3.1×1043.1\times10^43.1×104, 2.9×1052.9\times10^52.9×105, and 9×1039\times10^39×103 bytes. List the sizes from smallest to largest, writing every size in scientific notation.

    1. Hint 1

      Rewrite 32,00032{,}00032,000 with a coefficient at least 111 and less than 101010, so all four sizes share one form.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the exponents first; compare coefficients where exponents tie.

    Answer

    9×1039\times10^39×103, 3.1×1043.1\times10^43.1×104, 3.2×1043.2\times10^43.2×104, 2.9×1052.9\times10^52.9×105 bytes, in that order.

    Full solution

    The ordinary whole-number size converts to

    32,000=3.2×10432{,}000=3.2\times10^432,000=3.2×104

    The exponent 333 gives the smallest size and the exponent 555 gives the largest.

    The two middle sizes have exponent 444, so their coefficients decide the order.

    3.1<3.23.1<3.23.1<3.2

    The ascending order is 9×1039\times10^39×103, 3.1×1043.1\times10^43.1×104, 3.2×1043.2\times10^43.2×104, then 2.9×1052.9\times10^52.9×105 bytes.

    Answer

    9×1039\times10^39×103, 3.1×1043.1\times10^43.1×104, 3.2×1043.2\times10^43.2×104, 2.9×1052.9\times10^52.9×105 bytes, in that order.

    Key idea

    Once every coefficient is at least 111 and less than 101010, compare powers of ten first and use coefficients only to settle equal exponents.

  8. Problem 8 A marked interval

    A number line has marks at −0.000001-0.000001−0.000001, zero, and 0.0000010.0000010.000001. Sam places 9×10−89\times10^{-8}9×10−8 between zero and the positive mark. Is the placement correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      A negative exponent on ten does not turn a positive coefficient into a negative number.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the value and the positive mark with the same power of ten, or as ordinary decimals.

    Answer

    Yes. 0<0.00000009<0.0000010<0.00000009<0.0000010<0.00000009<0.000001.

    Full solution

    The indicated value is a small positive number.

    9×10−8=0.000000099\times10^{-8}=0.000000099×10−8=0.00000009

    The positive mark is 1×10−61\times10^{-6}1×10−6.

    Its exponent is larger than −8-8−8, and both coefficients are in range, so it is larger than 9×10−89\times10^{-8}9×10−8.

    The value lies between zero and the positive mark, so Sam's placement is correct.

    Answer

    Yes. 0<0.00000009<0.0000010<0.00000009<0.0000010<0.00000009<0.000001.

    Key idea

    A negative power-of-ten exponent can place a positive number very close to zero.

  9. Problem 9 A recorder rule

    A recorder accepts entries a×10na\times10^na×10n with integer nnn and 1≤a≤101\le a\le101≤a≤10, including both endpoints. Decide whether some positive number has two different accepted entries, and support your decision with an example or a reason. Then explain how requiring a<10a<10a<10 changes the situation.

    1. Hint 1

      The two endpoints of the allowed coefficient range differ by a factor of ten.

    2. Hint 2

      A factor of ten can be transferred between the coefficient and the power.

    3. Hint 3

      Test whether an entry whose coefficient is exactly 101010 has another accepted form.

    Answer

    Yes, for example 10×10−310\times10^{-3}10×10−3 and 1×10−21\times10^{-2}1×10−2. Requiring 1≤a<101\le a<101≤a<10 gives one form for each positive number.

    Full solution

    The accepted coefficient 101010 can be moved into the power of ten.

    10×10−3=1×10−210\times10^{-3}=1\times10^{-2}10×10−3=1×10−2

    Both entries equal 0.010.010.01, so including both endpoints permits two different entries for the same value.

    Requiring the coefficient to be less than 101010 excludes the first entry.

    In the range 1≤a<101\le a<101≤a<10, multiplying a coefficient by ten makes it too large, and dividing it by ten makes it too small.

    Changing the exponent by more than one multiplies or divides the coefficient by 100100100 or more, which moves it even further out of range.

    Thus changing the integer exponent cannot produce another coefficient in the allowed range for the same value.

    Answer

    Yes, for example 10×10−310\times10^{-3}10×10−3 and 1×10−21\times10^{-2}1×10−2. Requiring 1≤a<101\le a<101≤a<10 gives one form for each positive number.

    Key idea

    Excluding ten from the coefficient range prevents duplicate scientific-notation forms at the boundary.

  10. Problem 10 A revised result

    A calculation produces 0.84×1050.84\times10^50.84×105. Dev rewrites it as 8.4×1068.4\times10^68.4×106. Do the two expressions have the same value? Either way, give the proper scientific-notation form of the original result.

    1. Hint 1

      Changing the coefficient must be balanced by an opposite change in the power-of-ten factor.

    2. Hint 2

      Write both 0.84×1050.84\times10^50.84×105 and 8.4×1068.4\times10^68.4×106 as ordinary numbers and compare them.

    Answer

    No. The correct form is 8.4×1048.4\times10^48.4×104.

    Full solution

    The original result is

    0.84×105=84,0000.84\times10^5=84{,}0000.84×105=84,000

    Multiplying the coefficient by ten requires reducing the exponent by one.

    0.84×105=8.4×1040.84\times10^5=8.4\times10^40.84×105=8.4×104

    Dev increased both parts instead.

    His expression is

    8.4×106=8,400,0008.4\times10^6=8{,}400{,}0008.4×106=8,400,000

    The values are different.

    The corrected coefficient 8.48.48.4 is in the required range.

    Answer

    No. The correct form is 8.4×1048.4\times10^48.4×104.

    Key idea

    Increasing the coefficient by a factor of ten requires decreasing the exponent by one to preserve the value.
