Scientific Notation

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Earth is about 150,000,000150{,}000{,}000150,000,000 kilometers from the Sun on average. A water molecule is roughly 0.00000000030.00000000030.0000000003 meters across. Miscount one zero and the value changes by a factor of ten. Scientific notation separates the digits from the size: 1.5×1081.5\times10^81.5×108 km and 3×10−103\times10^{-10}3×10−10 m. Powers of ten carry the zeros, so the important digits are easier to compare.

Learning goals

  • Write a positive number as a×10na \times 10^{n}a×10n with 1≤a<101 \le a < 101≤a<10
  • Explain why the coefficient range makes each positive number's form unique
  • Convert between a number and its scientific notation, moving the point left or right as needed
  • Recognize that a negative power-of-ten exponent gives a small positive value, never a negative one
  • Compare magnitudes by exponent first, coefficient only to break a tie
  • Multiply and divide by combining coefficients and exponents, then renormalize

What scientific notation is

A positive number is in scientific notation when it has the form

a×10n,1≤a<10,a\times10^n,\qquad 1\le a<10,a×10n,1≤a<10,

with integer exponent nnn. The coefficient aaa carries the digits, with one nonzero digit before its decimal point. The power of ten sets the magnitude, or size. For example, 4.2×1094.2\times10^94.2×109 and 4.2×10−94.2\times10^{-9}4.2×10−9 share a coefficient but have very different sizes.

Recall that 103=100010^3=1000103=1000, 100=110^0=1100=1, and 10−3=1/1000=0.00110^{-3}=1/1000=0.00110−3=1/1000=0.001. All these powers of ten are positive.

In the chart, choose a nonzero digit such as 333 and move it from ones to thousands. Its values are 333, 303030, 300300300, and 300030003000: the coefficient stays 333 while the exponent changes from 000 to 333. Change the digit without moving columns to vary the coefficient instead. The zero digit also works for place value, but zero is outside the positive-number form used here.

Why the coefficient stays between 1 and 10

Several expressions can have the same value:

3000=3×103=30×102=0.3×104.3000=3\times10^3=30\times10^2=0.3\times10^4.3000=3×103=30×102=0.3×104.

Only 3×1033\times10^33×103 has 1≤a<101\le a<101≤a<10. This restriction makes the form unique: two people converting the same positive number get the same coefficient and exponent.

Why exactly one power of ten makes the coefficient land between 1 and 10#

Start with 47,00047{,}00047,000. Move the point left four places to get 4.74.74.7, then compensate with 10410^4104: 47,000=4.7×10447{,}000=4.7\times10^447,000=4.7×104. Moving one place farther gives coefficient 0.470.470.47, which is too small; stopping one place sooner gives 474747, which is too large.

For any positive number, place the decimal point just after its first nonzero digit. The resulting coefficient aaa satisfies 1≤a<101\le a<101≤a<10. The number and direction of places moved determines the integer exponent needed to restore the original value. If no move is needed, that exponent is zero.

Why can no other exponent work? Increasing the exponent by 111 requires dividing the coefficient by 101010 to preserve the value; then a/10<1a/10<1a/10<1. Decreasing the exponent by 111 requires multiplying the coefficient by 101010; then 10a≥1010a\ge1010a≥10. Moving farther in either direction goes still farther outside the permitted interval. Keeping the exponent fixed also fixes the coefficient. Thus exactly one coefficient/exponent pair is allowed.

∎

Expressions such as 30×10230\times10^230×102 still equal 300030003000; they are simply not in scientific notation.

Check your understanding

Both 45×10245\times10^245×102 and 4.5×1034.5\times10^34.5×103 equal 450045004500. Why is only 4.5×1034.5\times10^34.5×103 in scientific notation?

Answer choices

The condition is 1≤a<101\le a<101≤a<10, and the exponent may be any integer. Increasing the exponent would divide 4.54.54.5 by 101010 to give 0.450.450.45; decreasing it would multiply 4.54.54.5 by 101010 to give 454545. Both leave the allowed range, so the normalized form is unique.

Large numbers: a positive exponent

To write 150,000,000150{,}000{,}000150,000,000 in scientific notation, move its decimal point eight places left to make 1.51.51.5. That divides by 10810^8108, so multiply back by 10810^8108 to preserve the value:

150,000,000=1.5×108.150{,}000{,}000=1.5\times10^8.150,000,000=1.5×108.

For a number at least 101010, the exponent counts the leftward moves needed to form the coefficient.

Worked example 1 Write 48,30048{,}30048,300 in scientific notation

The point starts after the final zero. Move it four places left, to just after the 444:

48,300.  ⟶  4.8300.48{,}300.\;\longrightarrow\;4.8300.48,300.⟶4.8300.

The trailing zeros do not change the coefficient’s value: 4.8300=4.834.8300=4.834.8300=4.83. Four leftward places give exponent 444:

48,300=4.83×104.48{,}300=4.83\times10^4.48,300=4.83×104.

Check: 4.83×10,000=48,3004.83\times10{,}000=48{,}3004.83×10,000=48,300, and 1≤4.83<101\le4.83<101≤4.83<10.

Write 52,00052{,}00052,000 in scientific notation.

Answer choices

Put the decimal point after the first nonzero digit, so the coefficient is 5.25.25.2 (a single digit before the point). Then count how many places the point moved left, from the end of 52,00052{,}00052,000 to between the 555 and the 222. That is 444 places.

52,000=5.2×10452{,}000 = 5.2 \times 10^{4}52,000=5.2×104

The coefficient must satisfy 1≤a<10.1 \le a < 10.1≤a<10. This rules out 52×10352 \times 10^{3}52×103 (coefficient too big) and 0.52×1050.52 \times 10^{5}0.52×105 (coefficient too small).

Small numbers: a negative exponent

For a positive number below 111, move the point right to form the coefficient. For example, 0.000420.000420.00042 becomes 4.24.24.2 after four places. That multiplies by 10410^4104, so compensate with its reciprocal:

0.00042=4.2×10−4.0.00042=4.2\times10^{-4}.0.00042=4.2×10−4.

The value is positive: 10−4=1/10,00010^{-4}=1/10{,}00010−4=1/10,000, and 4.2/10,000=0.000424.2/10{,}000=0.000424.2/10,000=0.00042. For a normalized positive coefficient, a negative exponent makes the number smaller than 111, not negative.

Worked example 2 Write 0.03070.03070.0307 in scientific notation

Move the point two places right to get a coefficient with one nonzero digit before the point:

0.0307  ⟶  3.07.0.0307\;\longrightarrow\;3.07.0.0307⟶3.07.

The leading zeros disappear, but the zero between 333 and 777 stays: removing it would change the digits’ place values. Two rightward places give exponent −2-2−2:

0.0307=3.07×10−2.0.0307=3.07\times10^{-2}.0.0307=3.07×10−2.

Check: 3.07/100=0.03073.07/100=0.03073.07/100=0.0307.

Check your understanding

Write 0.000690.000690.00069 in scientific notation.

Answer choices

The number is between 000 and 111, so the decimal point moves right and the exponent is negative. Slide the point to just after the first nonzero digit, the 666, to get the coefficient 6.96.96.9. Then count the hops: from 0.000690.000690.00069 to 6.96.96.9 is 444 places right.

0.00069=6.9×10−40.00069 = 6.9 \times 10^{-4}0.00069=6.9×10−4

The exponent is negative because the number is small, not because it is below zero. The coefficient 696969 is not allowed (it is 101010 or more).

A number already at least 111 and below 101010 needs no shift: 6.4=6.4×1006.4=6.4\times10^06.4=6.4×100. The upper boundary matters: 10=1×10110=1\times10^110=1×101, not a coefficient of 101010.

Converting back to an ordinary number

Now start with the coefficient and apply the power of ten:

  • Positive exponent: move the point right, filling empty places with zeros. For example, 6.02×105=602,0006.02\times10^5=602{,}0006.02×105=602,000.
  • Negative exponent: move the point left. For example, 7.4×10−3=0.00747.4\times10^{-3}=0.00747.4×10−3=0.0074.
  • Zero exponent: leave the coefficient unchanged, since 100=110^0=1100=1.

Multiplying by 105=100,00010^5=100{,}000105=100,000 makes the coefficient 100,000100{,}000100,000 times as large, so its decimal point moves five places right. A negative power divides by the matching positive power, moving the point left. These moves reverse the ones used to form the coefficient.

Worked example 3 Write 9.1×1069.1 \times 10^{6}9.1×106 and 2.5×10−42.5 \times 10^{-4}2.5×10−4 as ordinary numbers

For 9.1×1069.1 \times 10^{6}9.1×106, the +6+6+6 moves the point 666 places right. After the 9.19.19.1 there is one digit past the point, so five zeros fill the rest:

9.1×106=9,100,000.9.1 \times 10^{6} = 9{,}100{,}000.9.1×106=9,100,000.

For 2.5×10−42.5 \times 10^{-4}2.5×10−4, the −4-4−4 moves the point 444 places left, which pushes the 222 into the fourth decimal place and pads the gap with zeros:

2.5×10−4=0.00025.2.5 \times 10^{-4} = 0.00025.2.5×10−4=0.00025.

Write 3.6×10−23.6 \times 10^{-2}3.6×10−2 as an ordinary number.

Answer choices

The exponent −2-2−2 is negative, so the number is small: move the decimal point 222 places to the left, padding with a zero.

3.6×10−2=0.0363.6 \times 10^{-2} = 0.0363.6×10−2=0.036

The value is a small positive number. A negative exponent shrinks the coefficient; it does not make the result negative, so −360-360−360 is wrong.

Comparing magnitudes

Compare 1.2×1031.2\times10^31.2×103 and 9.6×1029.6\times10^29.6×102. The first is at least 100010001000 because its coefficient is at least 111. The second is below 100010001000 because its coefficient is below 101010. Indeed, their values are 120012001200 and 960960960.

Thus the larger exponent wins even when it has the smaller coefficient. Here is the general reason.

Why a larger exponent beats any normalized coefficient#

Compare 4.6×1064.6\times10^64.6×106 with 8.7×1048.7\times10^48.7×104. The first is at least 106=1,000,00010^6=1{,}000{,}000106=1,000,000; the second is below 10×104=105=100,00010\times10^4=10^5=100{,}00010×104=105=100,000. Since 105≤10610^5\le10^6105≤106, we can link the bounds:

87,000<100,000,100,000≤1,000,000,1,000,000≤4,600,000.\begin{gathered} 87{,}000<100{,}000,\\ 100{,}000\le1{,}000{,}000,\\ 1{,}000{,}000\le4{,}600{,}000. \end{gathered}87,000<100,000,100,000≤1,000,000,1,000,000≤4,600,000.​

The exact coefficients did not matter: one was at least 111 and the other below 101010. The same bounds separate any two normalized numbers with different integer exponents.

Compare a×10ma\times10^ma×10m and b×10nb\times10^nb×10n, where 1≤a<101\le a<101≤a<10, 1≤b<101\le b<101≤b<10, and m,nm,nm,n are integers with m>nm>nm>n. Then m≥n+1m\ge n+1m≥n+1.

The first number is at least 10m10^m10m, since a≥1a\ge1a≥1. The second is below 10×10n=10n+110\times10^n=10^{n+1}10×10n=10n+1, since b<10b<10b<10. Also 10n+1≤10m10^{n+1}\le10^m10n+1≤10m. Therefore

b×10n<10n+1≤10m,b\times10^n<10^{n+1}\le10^m,b×10n<10n+1≤10m,

while

10m≤a×10m.10^m\le a\times10^m.10m≤a×10m.

Combining these bounds gives b×10n<a×10mb\times10^n<a\times10^mb×10n<a×10m. This includes negative exponents: all powers of ten are positive, and increasing the exponent multiplies by ten. If the exponents match instead, the powers of ten match, so the coefficients decide.

∎

Both numbers must be normalized first. For instance, 30×10230\times10^230×102 is larger than 1×1031\times10^31×103, despite its smaller exponent, because the coefficient 303030 is outside the range.

Worked example 4 Order 4×1054 \times 10^{5}4×105, 9×1049 \times 10^{4}9×104, and 2×1052 \times 10^{5}2×105 from smallest to largest

Sort by exponent first. Two numbers share the exponent 555 and one has exponent 444, so the exponent-444 number is the smallest of the three outright:

9×104<4×105,9×104<2×105.\begin{gathered} 9\times10^4<4\times10^5, \\ 9\times10^4<2\times10^5. \end{gathered}9×104<4×105,9×104<2×105.​

For the two that tie at 10510^{5}105, the exponents match, so compare the coefficients: 2<42 < 42<4. That makes 2×1052 \times 10^{5}2×105 smaller than 4×1054 \times 10^{5}4×105. Putting it together:

9×104<2×105<4×105.9 \times 10^{4} < 2 \times 10^{5} < 4 \times 10^{5}.9×104<2×105<4×105.

2.6×106=2,600,000,7.8×105=780,000.\begin{aligned}2.6\times10^6&=2{,}600{,}000,\\7.8\times10^5&=780{,}000.\end{aligned}2.6×1067.8×105​=2,600,000,=780,000.​

The coefficient 7.87.87.8 cannot make up the difference: it is less than ten times 2.62.62.6, while 10610^{6}106 is exactly ten times 10510^{5}105.

The exponents are equal (5=55 = 55=5), so this is the case where the coefficients break the tie: compare 6.26.26.2 and 4.94.94.9 directly.

6.2×105=620,000  >  490,000=4.9×1056.2 \times 10^{5} = 620{,}000 \; > \; 490{,}000 = 4.9 \times 10^{5}6.2×105=620,000>490,000=4.9×105

When the exponents match, the larger coefficient gives the larger number.

Calculating with scientific notation

Handle the coefficients and the powers of ten separately. Then renormalize: adjust the coefficient back into 1≤a<101\le a<101≤a<10 without changing the value.

Multiplying

Regroup to multiply coefficients and add exponents:

(2×103)(4×102)=(2×4)×103+2=8×105.\begin{aligned} (2\times10^3)(4\times10^2) &=(2\times4)\times10^{3+2} \\ &=8\times10^5. \end{aligned}(2×103)(4×102)​=(2×4)×103+2=8×105.​

In general, (a×10m)(b×10n)=(ab)×10m+n(a\times10^m)(b\times10^n)=(ab)\times10^{m+n}(a×10m)(b×10n)=(ab)×10m+n by the product rule.

Worked example 5 Compute (5×106)×(4×103)(5 \times 10^{6}) \times (4 \times 10^{3})(5×106)×(4×103) and fix the coefficient

Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents:

(5×106)(4×103)=(5×4)×106+3=20×109.\begin{aligned} (5\times10^6)(4\times10^3) &=(5\times4)\times10^{6+3} \\ &=20\times10^9. \end{aligned}(5×106)(4×103)​=(5×4)×106+3=20×109.​

The coefficient 202020 is too large. Write 20=2×1020=2\times1020=2×10, then combine the extra ten with 10910^9109:

20×109=2×101+9=2×1010.20\times10^9=2\times10^{1+9}=2\times10^{10}.20×109=2×101+9=2×1010.

The coefficient 222 is now in range.

Compute (6×103)×(3×105)(6 \times 10^{3}) \times (3 \times 10^{5})(6×103)×(3×105), written in proper scientific notation.

Answer choices

Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents: 6×3=186 \times 3 = 186×3=18 and 3+5=83 + 5 = 83+5=8, giving 18×10818 \times 10^{8}18×108. The coefficient 181818 is at least 101010, so renormalize by writing 18=1.8×1018 = 1.8 \times 1018=1.8×10 and folding the extra ten into the power.

18×108=1.8×101×108=1.8×109.\begin{aligned}&18\times10^8\\&=1.8\times10^1\times10^8\\&=1.8\times10^9.\end{aligned}​18×108=1.8×101×108=1.8×109.​

The proper form is 1.8×1091.8 \times 10^{9}1.8×109; 18×10818 \times 10^{8}18×108 has a coefficient that is too large.

Dividing

Divide coefficients and subtract exponents:

a×10mb×10n=ab×10m−n.\frac{a\times10^m}{b\times10^n}=\frac ab\times10^{m-n}.b×10na×10m​=ba​×10m−n.

Here aaa and bbb are positive, so the denominator is nonzero. The new coefficient may be below 111.

Worked example 6 Compute 2.4×1078×103\dfrac{2.4 \times 10^{7}}{8 \times 10^{3}}8×1032.4×107​ and fix the coefficient

Separate the coefficient division from the powers of ten:

2.4×1078×103=2.48×107−3=0.3×104.\begin{aligned} \frac{2.4\times10^7}{8\times10^3} &=\frac{2.4}{8}\times10^{7-3} \\ &=0.3\times10^4. \end{aligned}8×1032.4×107​​=82.4​×107−3=0.3×104.​

The coefficient 0.30.30.3 is below 111. Since 0.3=3×10−10.3=3\times10^{-1}0.3=3×10−1,

0.3×104=3×10−1+4=3×103.0.3\times10^4=3\times10^{-1+4}=3\times10^3.0.3×104=3×10−1+4=3×103.

The coefficient 333 is now in range. Increasing it tenfold required decreasing the exponent by one.

0.3×104=3×10−1×104=3×103.\begin{aligned}&0.3\times10^4\\&=3\times10^{-1}\times10^4\\&=3\times10^3.\end{aligned}​0.3×104=3×10−1×104=3×103.​

Adding numbers with powers of ten

When the powers match, use the distributive property: 2×104+3×104=(2+3)×104=5×1042\times10^4+3\times10^4=(2+3)\times10^4=5\times10^42×104+3×104=(2+3)×104=5×104. When they differ, rewrite using one shared power. For example,

2×104+3×103=20×103+3×103=23×103=2.3×104.\begin{aligned} 2\times10^4+3\times10^3 &=20\times10^3+3\times10^3\\ &=23\times10^3=2.3\times10^4. \end{aligned}2×104+3×103​=20×103+3×103=23×103=2.3×104.​

Add the coefficients, not the exponents. If their sum is at least 101010, renormalize as in this example. Converting both numbers to ordinary form and adding gives the same result.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the coefficient out of range. Normalize 30×10430\times10^430×104 to 3×1053\times10^53×105.
  • Reading a negative exponent as a negative number. 4.2×10−4=0.000424.2\times10^{-4}=0.000424.2×10−4=0.00042 is positive.
  • Moving the point the wrong way. To expand 6.02×1056.02\times10^56.02×105, move right because 10510^5105 makes the coefficient larger.
  • Miscounting places. 0.000420.000420.00042 needs four rightward moves to become 4.24.24.2, not three.
  • Skipping renormalization. 20×10920\times10^920×109 must become 2×10102\times10^{10}2×1010.
  • Confusing exponent and power. In 7×1037\times10^37×103, the exponent is 333; the power is 103=100010^3=1000103=1000.

Key takeaways

  • Form: a×10na\times10^na×10n with 1≤a<101\le a<101≤a<10 and integer nnn. The coefficient range makes the form unique.
  • From ordinary numbers: move the point after the first nonzero digit; compensate with the appropriate power of ten.
  • Exponent sign: positive for values at least 101010, zero for values at least 111 and below 101010, negative for values between 000 and 111.
  • Back to ordinary numbers: positive exponents move the point right; negative exponents move it left.
  • Comparison: normalize first, then compare exponents; compare coefficients when the exponents tie.
  • Multiplication/division: multiply/divide coefficients and add/subtract exponents, then renormalize.

A bit of history (optional)

What would you call a one followed by a hundred zeros? Mathematician Edward Kasner asked his nine-year-old nephew, Milton Sirotta, for a name. Milton suggested googol.

Kasner and James Newman told the story in their 1940 book Mathematics and the Imagination. The child understood that a googol was enormous but still finite. He also suggested an even larger number, a googolplex. An informal idea was to keep writing zeros until you got tired; the book instead fixed its size as a 111 followed by a googol zeros. Its size would no longer depend on who was doing the writing.

The playful name endured. A googol itself is exactly 1010010^{100}10100, with no need to write out all one hundred zeros.
