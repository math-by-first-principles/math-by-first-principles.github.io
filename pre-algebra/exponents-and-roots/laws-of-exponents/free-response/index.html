  1. Problem 1 Three missing factors

    For h>1h>1h>1, complete h×□×□×□=h16h\times\square\times\square\times\square=h^{16}h×□×□×□=h16 by writing the same power of hhh, with a positive whole-number exponent, in all three boxes.

    1. Hint 1

      Count how many copies of hhh the result needs and how many the known factor supplies; a bare hhh is one copy.

    2. Hint 2

      The three boxes supply the missing copies in three equal shares.

    Answer

    h5h^5h5 in each box.

    Full solution

    The result needs sixteen copies of hhh and the known factor supplies one, so the boxes supply fifteen copies together.

    16−1=1516-1=1516−1=15

    All three boxes hold the same power, so each supplies a third of those copies.

    15÷3=515\div3=515÷3=5

    Check the proposed factors.

    h×h5×h5×h5=h16h\times h^5\times h^5\times h^5=h^{16}h×h5×h5×h5=h16

    Because h>1h>1h>1, powers of hhh with different exponents are different numbers, so matching the count of copies is the only way.

    Answer

    h5h^5h5 in each box.

    Key idea

    The exponents of the factors in a product of powers of one base add up to the exponent of the result.

  2. Problem 2 A grouped fraction

    For numbers uuu, vvv, and www with w≠0w\ne0w=0, write (uvw)3\left(\frac{uv}{w}\right)^3(wuv​)3 as a fraction whose numerator is a product of powers of uuu and vvv and whose denominator is a power of www.

    1. Hint 1

      Each copy of the fraction supplies both numerator factors and a denominator factor.

    2. Hint 2

      Put the outer power onto the numerator and onto the denominator first. The numerator is itself a product, so its power reaches each factor.

    Answer

    u3v3w3\frac{u^3v^3}{w^3}w3u3v3​.

    Full solution

    The outer exponent applies to the entire fraction, so it reaches both the numerator and denominator.

    (uvw)3=(uv)3w3\left(\frac{uv}{w}\right)^3=\frac{(uv)^3}{w^3}(wuv​)3=w3(uv)3​

    The numerator is a product, so each factor receives the exponent.

    (uv)3=u3v3(uv)^3=u^3v^3(uv)3=u3v3

    The requested form is u3v3w3\frac{u^3v^3}{w^3}w3u3v3​.

    The denominator is nonzero because w≠0w\ne0w=0.

    Answer

    u3v3w3\frac{u^3v^3}{w^3}w3u3v3​.

    Key idea

    A power of a fraction applies to every factor in its numerator and denominator.

  3. Problem 3 Nested parentheses

    Write ((z2)3)2\bigl((z^2)^3\bigr)^2((z2)3)2 as a single power of zzz.

    1. Hint 1

      Each outer exponent counts whole groups of the factors already inside.

    2. Hint 2

      Combine the inner two exponents, then account for the outermost exponent.

    Answer

    z12z^{12}z12.

    Full solution

    Three groups of two factors give six factors.

    (z2)3=z6(z^2)^3=z^6(z2)3=z6

    The outer square uses that group twice.

    (z6)2=z12(z^6)^2=z^{12}(z6)2=z12

    Answer

    z12z^{12}z12.

    Key idea

    Nested powers multiply their exponent counts.

  4. Problem 4 Mia's product

    For q≠0q\ne0q=0, Mia writes the product q8×q7×q4q^8\times q^7\times q^4q8×q7×q4 and then divides it by qqq. Write her result as a single power of qqq.

    1. Hint 1

      Each factor in the product adds copies of the same base to the total count.

    2. Hint 2

      Total the copies from all three factors first; the division comes last.

    3. Hint 3

      The final division removes one copy of qqq.

    Answer

    q18q^{18}q18.

    Full solution

    The three factors hold eight, seven, and four copies of qqq.

    8+7+4=198+7+4=198+7+4=19

    The value before the division is q19q^{19}q19.

    Divide by the nonzero factor qqq.

    q19q=q18\frac{q^{19}}{q}=q^{18}qq19​=q18

    Answer

    q18q^{18}q18.

    Key idea

    Repeated multiplication adds factor counts, and division by a nonzero copy removes one.

  5. Problem 5 A compact product

    Write (x2y)4×(xy2)2(x^2y)^4\times(xy^2)^2(x2y)4×(xy2)2 as a product of one power of xxx and one power of yyy.

    1. Hint 1

      Each outer exponent reaches every factor inside its own parentheses.

    2. Hint 2

      After resolving both powers, combine the copies of each base separately.

    Answer

    x10y8x^{10}y^8x10y8, or y8x10y^8x^{10}y8x10.

    Full solution

    The fourth power reaches both factors in the first parentheses.

    (x2y)4=x8y4(x^2y)^4=x^8y^4(x2y)4=x8y4

    The square reaches both factors in the second parentheses.

    (xy2)2=x2y4(xy^2)^2=x^2y^4(xy2)2=x2y4

    Combine the powers of xxx by adding their exponents.

    x8×x2=x10x^8\times x^2=x^{10}x8×x2=x10

    Combine the powers of yyy the same way.

    y4×y4=y8y^4\times y^4=y^8y4×y4=y8

    The requested product is x10y8x^{10}y^8x10y8.

    Reversing the order of its two factors gives the same product.

    Answer

    x10y8x^{10}y^8x10y8, or y8x10y^8x^{10}y8x10.

    Key idea

    An exponent on a product reaches each factor before powers of matching bases are combined.

  6. Problem 6 A paper cutting

    A square sheet has side length 343^434 cm. It is cut into small squares, each with side length 333 cm, with no waste. How many small squares are made? Give the count as a single power of 333.

    1. Hint 1

      Find how many small squares fit along one side of the sheet.

    2. Hint 2

      The number of rows is the same as the number in each row.

    3. Hint 3

      Square the count along one side and simplify the resulting power.

    Answer

    363^636 small squares.

    Full solution

    Divide the large side length by the small side length to count the squares along one edge.

    343=33\frac{3^4}{3}=3^3334​=33

    There are 333^333 rows with 333^333 squares in each, so the count is

    (33)2=36(3^3)^2=3^6(33)2=36

    Checking numerically, the large side is 818181 cm and there are 272727 small squares along each edge, giving 729729729 squares, which is 363^636.

    Answer

    363^636 small squares.

    Key idea

    Counting equal squares along each edge connects division of powers with squaring a count.

  7. Problem 7 Two fractions together

    For c≠0c\ne0c=0, write c17(c5)3×(c7)2c9\frac{c^{17}}{(c^5)^3}\times\frac{(c^7)^2}{c^9}(c5)3c17​×c9(c7)2​ as a single power of ccc.

    1. Hint 1

      Each fraction can be simplified before the two results are multiplied.

    2. Hint 2

      Each fraction contains one power of a power.

    3. Hint 3

      After subtracting the exponent in each denominator, add the two remaining exponent counts.

    Answer

    c7c^7c7.

    Full solution

    In the first fraction, the denominator is a power of a power.

    (c5)3=c15(c^5)^3=c^{15}(c5)3=c15

    The base is nonzero and 17>1517>1517>15, so subtract the exponents.

    c17c15=c2\frac{c^{17}}{c^{15}}=c^2c15c17​=c2

    In the second fraction, the numerator is a power of a power.

    (c7)2=c14(c^7)^2=c^{14}(c7)2=c14

    Again 14>914>914>9, so subtract the exponents.

    c14c9=c5\frac{c^{14}}{c^9}=c^5c9c14​=c5

    Combine the two resulting powers by adding their exponents.

    c2×c5=c7c^2\times c^5=c^7c2×c5=c7

    Answer

    c7c^7c7.

    Key idea

    Simplify grouped powers and quotients before combining their remaining factor counts.

  8. Problem 8 Two expressions in ttt

    For every number ttt, do t3×t5t^3\times t^5t3×t5 and (t2)4(t^2)^4(t2)4 give the same value? Explain without choosing a particular value of ttt.

    1. Hint 1

      Both expressions can be understood by counting copies of ttt.

    2. Hint 2

      One joins two groups, while the other repeats a group four times.

    Answer

    Yes. Both equal t8t^8t8.

    Full solution

    The first expression joins three copies of ttt to five copies.

    t3×t5=t8t^3\times t^5=t^8t3×t5=t8

    The second uses four groups of two copies.

    (t2)4=t8(t^2)^4=t^8(t2)4=t8

    Both are products of eight copies of the same number, so they agree for every ttt, including zero.

    Answer

    Yes. Both equal t8t^8t8.

    Key idea

    Different groupings give equal powers when they contain the same total number of factors.

  9. Problem 9 Jo's shortcut

    A calculator finds the fifth power of its input and subtracts the cube of the input. Jo says the result is the square of the input for every number. Is Jo correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Jo's claim subtracts the exponents. Recall which operation on powers of the same base that rule belongs to.

    2. Hint 2

      At the input 222, work out the fifth power and the cube as plain numbers, subtract, and compare with the square of 222.

    Answer

    No. For example, at input 222 the calculator gives 242424, while 22=42^2=422=4.

    Full solution

    One input that fails is enough; try 222.

    Its fifth power is 25=322^5=3225=32 and its cube is 23=82^3=823=8.

    The calculator subtracts them.

    32−8=2432-8=2432−8=24

    The claimed square is

    22=42^2=422=4

    The values differ, so this input disproves the claim.

    Subtracting exponents is the rule for dividing powers of the same base, as in 2523=22\frac{2^5}{2^3}=2^22325​=22, not for subtracting their values.

    Answer

    No. For example, at input 222 the calculator gives 242424, while 22=42^2=422=4.

    Key idea

    Subtracting exponents is the rule for dividing powers of the same nonzero base, not for subtracting their values; no exponent law turns a difference of powers into one power.

  10. Problem 10 Pat's two equalities

    Pat checked (155)n=15n5n\left(\frac{15}{5}\right)^n=\frac{15^n}{5^n}(515​)n=5n15n​ and (15−5)n=15n−5n(15-5)^n=15^n-5^n(15−5)n=15n−5n at n=1n=1n=1, and both worked. Pat concludes that both work for every positive whole-number exponent. Test n=2n=2n=2 and explain whether the conclusion follows.

    1. Hint 1

      An equality working for one exponent need not work for another.

    2. Hint 2

      Evaluate the grouped base before squaring on each left side, then evaluate the corresponding right side.

    Answer

    No. At n=2n=2n=2, the quotient equality holds, with 999 on both sides, but the difference equality fails, with 100100100 on the left and 200200200 on the right.

    Full solution

    For the quotient equality, evaluate the quotient before squaring on the left.

    (155)2=9\left(\frac{15}{5}\right)^2=9(515​)2=9

    The right side divides the separate squares, 225225225 by 252525.

    15252=9\frac{15^2}{5^2}=952152​=9

    For the difference equality, evaluate the difference before squaring on the left.

    (15−5)2=100(15-5)^2=100(15−5)2=100

    The right side subtracts the separate squares, 252525 from 225225225.

    152−52=20015^2-5^2=200152−52=200

    The power-of-a-quotient law proved in the lesson makes the first equality hold for every positive whole-number exponent, but this check shows that the exponent does not spread across the difference.

    Working at n=1n=1n=1 did not establish the claim for all exponents.

    Answer

    No. At n=2n=2n=2, the quotient equality holds, with 999 on both sides, but the difference equality fails, with 100100100 on the left and 200200200 on the right.

    Key idea

    Checking one exponent does not establish a rule for every exponent.
