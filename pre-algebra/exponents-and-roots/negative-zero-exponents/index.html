Negative and Zero Exponents

Intuition

So far a power has meant one thing: ana^nan is nnn copies of the base aaa multiplied together. That definition needs nnn to be a positive whole number, since you cannot multiply something “zero times” or “negative three times” in any obvious way. Yet the laws of exponents you just proved keep producing exactly those forbidden cases. Divide 232^323 by 232^323 and the quotient rule hands you 202^020; divide 232^323 by 252^525 and it hands you 2−22^{-2}2−2. Rather than declare these meaningless, we let the laws tell us what they must equal. The same move keeps every rule from the last lesson working without exception.

Learning goals

  • Show why the quotient rule forces a0a^0a0 to equal one
  • Read a−na^{-n}a−n as the reciprocal of the matching positive power
  • Evaluate a zero or negative power to a definite number
  • Separate the sign of the exponent from the sign of the base
  • Extend the exponent laws to every integer exponent for a nonzero base

The gap left by the quotient rule

The quotient rule says that to divide powers of the same nonzero base you subtract the exponents: aman=am−n\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}anam​=am−n, for a≠0a \neq 0a=0. Last lesson we only allowed m>nm > nm>n, so the answer always had a positive exponent and matched the “repeated multiplication” definition. But nothing stops you from writing a quotient where the exponents are equal or where the bottom is larger.

Take 2323\dfrac{2^3}{2^3}2323​. The exponents are equal, so the rule produces 23−3=202^{3-3} = 2^023−3=20. Take 2325\dfrac{2^3}{2^5}2523​. The bottom is larger, so the rule produces 23−5=2−22^{3-5} = 2^{-2}23−5=2−2. Neither 202^020 nor 2−22^{-2}2−2 means anything yet under the old definition.

We could declare these expressions off-limits, or we could define them so the quotient rule keeps holding. The second choice is far better, because each quotient above is also an ordinary fraction we can simplify directly. That simplification tells us the only value the new power is allowed to have.

Zero as an exponent

Start with the equal-exponent case, which forces the meaning of a zero exponent. The expression 2323\dfrac{2^3}{2^3}2323​ has the same thing on top and bottom, and any nonzero number over itself is 111. So the value is settled before any exponent rule is used:

2323=88=1.\frac{2^3}{2^3} = \frac{8}{8} = 1.2323​=88​=1.

Now read the same quotient through the quotient rule, which subtracts the exponents:

2323=23−3=20.\frac{2^3}{2^3} = 2^{3-3} = 2^0.2323​=23−3=20.

The left sides are identical, so the right sides must be equal: 20=12^0 = 120=1. The same argument runs with any nonzero base, which gives the definition.

Why a0=1a^0 = 1a0=1 for every base a≠0a \neq 0a=0#

Take the quotient of 343^434 with itself, and evaluate it both ways. First, directly: 343^434 is 818181, and 818181 divided by 818181 is 111. Second, through the quotient rule: subtracting the exponents makes that same quotient 34−4=303^{4-4} = 3^034−4=30.

3434=8181=1and3434=34−4=30.\frac{3^4}{3^4} = \frac{81}{81} = 1 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{3^4}{3^4} = 3^{4-4} = 3^0.3434​=8181​=1and3434​=34−4=30.

One quotient cannot have two different values, so 30=13^0 = 130=1 follows from the arithmetic alone. Neither the 333 nor the 444 did any work of its own. The argument needed only a nonzero base and the same power above and below.

Take any nonzero number aaa and any positive whole-number exponent nnn. Look at the quotient of ana^nan with itself, and evaluate it two ways.

First, directly. The numerator and denominator are the same number, and a nonzero number divided by itself is 111:

anan=1.\frac{a^n}{a^n} = 1.anan​=1.

Second, through the quotient rule, which subtracts the exponents:

anan=an−n=a0.\frac{a^n}{a^n} = a^{n-n} = a^0.anan​=an−n=a0.

Both expressions are the value of the one quotient anan\dfrac{a^n}{a^n}anan​, so they are equal to each other. That forces

a0=1.a^0 = 1.a0=1.

The base aaa had to be nonzero for the very first step, since 00\dfrac{0}{0}00​ is not 111 (it is not any number). So a0=1a^0 = 1a0=1 holds for every base except 000, and 000^000 is left undefined.

∎

There is a second way to see it that needs no algebra, just the staircase of powers. Each time the exponent drops by one, the value is divided by the base, because you remove one factor. Watch the powers of 222 come down:

24=16,23=8,22=4,21=2.2^4 = 16, \quad 2^3 = 8, \quad 2^2 = 4, \quad 2^1 = 2.24=16,23=8,22=4,21=2.

Every step to the right divides by 222: from 161616 to 888, from 888 to 444, from 444 to 222. To stay on the pattern, the next step must divide 222 by 222 as well, and 2÷2=12 \div 2 = 12÷2=1. The slot below 212^121 is 202^020, so 20=12^0 = 120=1, in perfect agreement with the algebra.

A zero exponent is not a zero value

Reading a0a^0a0 as 000 is the single most common error in this chapter. A zero exponent does not mean “the value is zero”; it means “no factors of the base.” A product of no factors is 111, the number that leaves any multiplication unchanged. So 50=15^0 = 150=1, 1000=1100^0 = 11000=1, and (37)0=1\left(\tfrac{3}{7} \right)^0 = 1(73​)0=1. Only the base 000 is excluded.

70=17^0 = 170=1

A zero exponent means no factors of the base, and a product of no factors is 111, not 000.

Negative exponents

Now push past zero. The case 2325\dfrac{2^3}{2^5}2523​ has a larger exponent on the bottom, and the quotient rule produces a negative exponent, 23−5=2−22^{3-5} = 2^{-2}23−5=2−2. As before, the same quotient is also an ordinary fraction we can simplify by cancelling, and that pins down the value.

Write top and bottom as factors and cancel the three 222‘s they share:

2325=2×2×22×2×2×2×2=2×2×22×2×2×2×2=12×2=122.\frac{2^3}{2^5} = \frac{2 \times 2 \times 2}{2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2} = \frac{\cancel{2} \times \cancel{2} \times \cancel{2}}{\cancel{2} \times \cancel{2} \times \cancel{2} \times 2 \times 2} = \frac{1}{2 \times 2} = \frac{1}{2^2}.2523​=2×2×2×2×22×2×2​=2​×2​×2​×2×22​×2​×2​​=2×21​=221​.

Three factors cancel from both, leaving nothing on top but 111 and two factors of 222 on the bottom. So the plain arithmetic gives 122\dfrac{1}{2^2}221​, while the quotient rule gives 2−22^{-2}2−2, and the two must agree:

2−2=122=14.2^{-2} = \frac{1}{2^2} = \frac{1}{4}.2−2=221​=41​.

A negative exponent is an instruction to take the reciprocal of the matching positive power. The reciprocal of a number is 111 divided by that number. The minus sign in the exponent flips the power between the numerator and the denominator; it does nothing to the sign of the value. The base has to be something other than zero, because 10n\tfrac{1}{0^n}0n1​ would ask you to divide by zero.

Why a−n=1ana^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}a−n=an1​ for every base a≠0a \neq 0a=0#

Divide 424^242 by 454^545, where the bottom carries three more factors of 444 than the top does. Through the quotient rule, subtracting the exponents gives 42−5=4−34^{2-5} = 4^{-3}42−5=4−3. Directly, expand and cancel: the two factors on top cancel two of the five below, leaving three factors of 444 under a 111.

4245=4×44×4×4×4×4=14×4×4=143=164.\frac{4^2}{4^5} = \frac{4 \times 4}{4 \times 4 \times 4 \times 4 \times 4} = \frac{1}{4 \times 4 \times 4} = \frac{1}{4^3} = \frac{1}{64}.4542​=4×4×4×4×44×4​=4×4×41​=431​=641​.

One quotient cannot have two different values, so 4−3=1434^{-3} = \tfrac{1}{4^3}4−3=431​. The 444, the 222 and the 333 were not special. Every factor on top cancels because there are fewer of them, and the base only had to be nonzero.

Now take a nonzero base aaa and a positive whole-number exponent nnn. Build a quotient whose exponents differ by exactly nnn, with the larger exponent on the bottom: divide ama^mam by am+na^{m+n}am+n for any positive mmm.

Through the quotient rule, subtract the exponents:

amam+n=am−(m+n)=a−n.\frac{a^m}{a^{m+n}} = a^{m-(m+n)} = a^{-n}.am+nam​=am−(m+n)=a−n.

Directly, expand and cancel. The numerator has mmm factors of aaa and the denominator has m+nm + nm+n factors of aaa. So all mmm factors on top cancel with mmm of the factors on the bottom, leaving 111 above and the remaining nnn factors below:

amam+n=a×⋯×a⏞ma×⋯×a⏟m+n=1a×⋯×a⏟n=1an.\frac{a^m}{a^{m+n}} = \frac{\overbrace{a \times \cdots \times a}^{m}}{\underbrace{a \times \cdots \times a}_{m+n}} = \frac{1}{\underbrace{a \times \cdots \times a}_{n}} = \frac{1}{a^n}.am+nam​=m+na×⋯×a​​a×⋯×a​m​​=na×⋯×a​​1​=an1​.

Both readings describe the same quotient, so they are equal:

a−n=1an.a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}.a−n=an1​.

The base must be nonzero so the cancelling is honest (each aa=1\tfrac{a}{a} = 1aa​=1) and so the reciprocal 1an\tfrac{1}{a^n}an1​ is not a division by zero. Reading the equation in reverse is just as useful: 1an=a−n\dfrac{1}{a^n} = a^{-n}an1​=a−n, so a reciprocal power can always be rewritten with a negative exponent.

∎

The staircase confirms this too. Keep dividing by the base as the exponent drops below zero, starting from 21=22^1 = 221=2 and 20=12^0 = 120=1:

21=2,20=1,2−1=12,2−2=14,2−3=18.2^1 = 2, \quad 2^0 = 1, \quad 2^{-1} = \tfrac{1}{2}, \quad 2^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{4}, \quad 2^{-3} = \tfrac{1}{8}.21=2,20=1,2−1=21​,2−2=41​,2−3=81​.

Each step right still divides by 222: 1÷2=121 \div 2 = \tfrac{1}{2}1÷2=21​, then 12÷2=14\tfrac{1}{2} \div 2 = \tfrac{1}{4}21​÷2=41​, then 14÷2=18\tfrac{1}{4} \div 2 = \tfrac{1}{8}41​÷2=81​. The values never turn negative; they turn into fractions, shrinking toward zero, which is exactly what reciprocals of growing powers do.

Check your understanding

Write 3−23^{-2}3−2 as a fraction.

Answer choices

A negative exponent means the reciprocal of the matching positive power, so flip 323^232 under a 111.

3−2=132=193^{-2} = \frac{1}{3^2} = \frac{1}{9}3−2=321​=91​

The value is positive: the minus sign moves the power to the denominator, it does not make the number negative, so 3−23^{-2}3−2 is not −9-9−9.

Evaluating zero and negative powers

To turn a negative power into a plain number, do two separate jobs in order. First take the reciprocal the minus sign calls for, then evaluate the positive power that remains. Keeping the steps apart is what prevents the usual sign slip.

Worked example 1 Evaluate 2−32^{-3}2−3, 505^050, and 10−210^{-2}10−2

For 2−32^{-3}2−3, the negative exponent flips it to a reciprocal, then evaluate the cube:

2−3=123=18.2^{-3} = \frac{1}{2^3} = \frac{1}{8}.2−3=231​=81​.

For 505^050, a zero exponent on a nonzero base is 111 outright:

50=1.5^0 = 1.50=1.

For 10−210^{-2}10−2, take the reciprocal of 10210^2102:

10−2=1102=1100=0.01.10^{-2} = \frac{1}{10^2} = \frac{1}{100} = 0.01.10−2=1021​=1001​=0.01.

When the base is a fraction, taking its reciprocal simply turns the fraction upside down.

Worked example 2 Evaluate (12)−1\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-1}(21​)−1 and (23)−2\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-2}(32​)−2

A power of −1-1−1 asks for one reciprocal and nothing more, so it just flips the base over.

For (12)−1\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-1}(21​)−1, take the reciprocal of 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​:

(12)−1=1(12)1=112=2.\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{-1} = \frac{1}{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{1}} = \frac{1}{\tfrac{1}{2}} = 2.(21​)−1=(21​)11​=21​1​=2.

Dividing 111 by 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​ gives 222, so a positive base smaller than 111 raised to a negative power comes out larger than 111. For (23)−2\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-2}(32​)−2, flip the fraction first, then square it:

(23)−2=(32)2=3222=94.\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-2} = \left(\tfrac{3}{2}\right)^{2} = \frac{3^2}{2^2} = \frac{9}{4}.(32​)−2=(23​)2=2232​=49​.

Flipping the base turns the −2-2−2 into a +2+2+2, which is the quickest route for a fraction raised to a negative power.

Evaluate 10−310^{-3}10−3 as a fraction.

Answer choices

The negative exponent takes the reciprocal of 10310^3103, and 103=100010^3 = 1000103=1000.

10−3=1103=1100010^{-3} = \frac{1}{10^3} = \frac{1}{1000}10−3=1031​=10001​

The value is a small positive fraction, not a negative number, so 10−310^{-3}10−3 is not −1000-1000−1000.

The sign of the base is a separate question

A negative exponent and a negative base are two different things that beginners often blur together. The exponent’s minus sign decides whether you take a reciprocal; the base’s minus sign decides whether the factors carry a negative.

Consider (−2)−2(-2)^{-2}(−2)−2. The parentheses make −2-2−2 the base, and the −2-2−2 exponent calls for the reciprocal of (−2)2(-2)^2(−2)2. First square the base, where two negatives cancel to a positive, then take the reciprocal:

(−2)−2=1(−2)2=1(−2)×(−2)=14.(-2)^{-2} = \frac{1}{(-2)^2} = \frac{1}{(-2)\times(-2)} = \frac{1}{4}.(−2)−2=(−2)21​=(−2)×(−2)1​=41​.

The answer is positive 14\tfrac{1}{4}41​, even though both the base and the exponent wore a minus sign. An odd power keeps the base’s sign, so (−2)−3=1(−2)3=1−8=−18(-2)^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{(-2)^3} = \dfrac{1}{-8} = -\dfrac{1}{8}(−2)−3=(−2)31​=−81​=−81​. That value is negative, but it is negative because the base is negative and the power is odd, never because the exponent is negative.

Two minus signs, two jobs

The minus sign in the exponent flips the power into its reciprocal: a−n=1ana^{-n} = \tfrac{1}{a^n}a−n=an1​. The minus sign on the base is what can make a value negative, and only when the exponent is odd. So 2−2=142^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{4}2−2=41​ (positive), (−2)2=4(-2)^{2} = 4(−2)2=4 (positive), (−2)−2=14(-2)^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{4}(−2)−2=41​ (positive), and (−2)−3=−18(-2)^{-3} = -\tfrac{1}{8}(−2)−3=−81​ (negative, from the odd power of a negative base). Parentheses still matter just as they did for positive powers. Without them the leading minus stays outside the power, so −2−2=−(2−2)=−14-2^{-2} = -(2^{-2}) = -\tfrac{1}{4}−2−2=−(2−2)=−41​, the opposite sign from (−2)−2=14(-2)^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{4}(−2)−2=41​.

Check your understanding

Evaluate (−3)−2(-3)^{-2}(−3)−2.

Answer choices

Answer two separate questions. The exponent's sign is negative, so take a reciprocal of (−3)2(-3)^2(−3)2. The exponent's size, 222, is even, so squaring the negative base cancels its sign.

(−3)−2=1(−3)2=19(-3)^{-2} = \frac{1}{(-3)^2} = \frac{1}{9}(−3)−2=(−3)21​=91​

The result is positive even though both the base and the exponent carry a minus sign: the exponent's sign only decides whether to reciprocate, and the base's sign only survives an odd exponent.

Every law still holds

The whole reason these definitions were chosen is that they keep the laws of exponents intact. Those laws now hold for a nonzero base and every integer exponent, rather than only the positive ones. Both a0a^0a0 and a−na^{-n}a−n were defined to be exactly the values the quotient rule forces.

Worked example 3 Show 23×2−5=142^3 \times 2^{-5} = \tfrac{1}{4}23×2−5=41​ two ways

First use the product rule, adding the exponents straight through, including the negative one:

23×2−5=23+(−5)=2−2=122=14.2^3 \times 2^{-5} = 2^{3 + (-5)} = 2^{-2} = \frac{1}{2^2} = \frac{1}{4}.23×2−5=23+(−5)=2−2=221​=41​.

Now check it the long way, with no negative-exponent rule at all. Rewrite 2−52^{-5}2−5 as its reciprocal first, then multiply the fractions:

23×2−5=8×125=832=14.2^3 \times 2^{-5} = 8 \times \frac{1}{2^5} = \frac{8}{32} = \frac{1}{4}.23×2−5=8×251​=328​=41​.

Both routes land on 14\tfrac{1}{4}41​. The product rule still says “add the exponents,” and adding 3+(−5)=−23 + (-5) = -23+(−5)=−2 gives the same answer as grinding through the arithmetic.

The quotient rule now works with no restriction on which exponent is larger, because a negative result is a legitimate power. So 3236=32−6=3−4=134=181\dfrac{3^2}{3^6} = 3^{2-6} = 3^{-4} = \dfrac{1}{3^4} = \dfrac{1}{81}3632​=32−6=3−4=341​=811​, with the rule applied in one clean step instead of the side condition m>nm > nm>n we needed before.

Worked example 4 Simplify a2a5\dfrac{a^2}{a^5}a5a2​ using only a positive exponent (with a≠0a \neq 0a=0)

The base is shared, so subtract the exponents, even though the bottom one is larger:

a2a5=a2−5=a−3.\frac{a^2}{a^5} = a^{2-5} = a^{-3}.a5a2​=a2−5=a−3.

That is already a single power, but a negative exponent is usually rewritten with a positive one by moving the power to the denominator:

a−3=1a3.a^{-3} = \frac{1}{a^3}.a−3=a31​.

So a2a5=1a3\dfrac{a^2}{a^5} = \dfrac{1}{a^3}a5a2​=a31​. Reading it directly agrees: two factors of aaa over five factors cancel two pairs, leaving three factors of aaa on the bottom.

52×5−4=52+(−4)=5−2=1525^2 \times 5^{-4} = 5^{2 + (-4)} = 5^{-2} = \frac{1}{5^2}52×5−4=52+(−4)=5−2=521​

The negative exponent then moves the power to the denominator. The form asked for a positive exponent, so 5−25^{-2}5−2 is rewritten as 152\tfrac{1}{5^2}521​.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Reading a0a^0a0 as 000. A zero exponent gives 111, not 000: 90=19^0 = 190=1 and 10000=11000^0 = 110000=1. “No factors of the base” leaves the multiplication at its starting value, which is 111. Only 000^000 is undefined.
  • Thinking a negative exponent makes the value negative. 2−3=182^{-3} = \tfrac{1}{8}2−3=81​, a positive number, not −8-8−8 and not −18-\tfrac{1}{8}−81​. The minus sign in the exponent flips the power into a reciprocal; it never touches the sign of the result.
  • Putting the wrong piece in the denominator. a−n=1ana^{-n} = \tfrac{1}{a^n}a−n=an1​, so only the power moves below the line. For example 2−3=123=182^{-3} = \tfrac{1}{2^3} = \tfrac{1}{8}2−3=231​=81​, not 12×3\tfrac{1}{2 \times 3}2×31​ or 32\tfrac{3}{2}23​. Evaluate the positive power, then put it under 111.
  • Confusing a negative base with a negative exponent. They are separate decisions: (−2)−2=14(-2)^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{4}(−2)−2=41​ is positive, because squaring a negative base is positive and the reciprocal keeps it positive. A negative result needs a negative base and an odd exponent, as in (−2)−3=−18(-2)^{-3} = -\tfrac{1}{8}(−2)−3=−81​.
  • Subtracting exponents the wrong way past zero. In 3236\tfrac{3^2}{3^6}3632​ the answer is 32−6=3−43^{2-6} = 3^{-4}32−6=3−4, not 36−2=343^{6-2} = 3^436−2=34. Always subtract the denominator’s exponent from the numerator’s, and let the result be negative when it is.

Key takeaways

  • a0=1a^0 = 1a0=1 for every base a≠0a \neq 0a=0, because anan\tfrac{a^n}{a^n}anan​ is 111 directly while the quotient rule makes the same quotient a0a^0a0. The value 000^000 is left undefined.
  • a−n=1ana^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}a−n=an1​ for every base a≠0a \neq 0a=0: a negative exponent means the reciprocal of the matching positive power.
  • A negative exponent never makes a value negative. It moves the power between numerator and denominator; the sign of the result is decided only by the base and whether the exponent is odd.
  • For a fraction base with a≠0a \neq 0a=0 and b≠0b \neq 0b=0, a negative exponent flips the fraction: (ab)−n=(ba)n\left(\tfrac{a}{b}\right)^{-n} = \left(\tfrac{b}{a}\right)^{n}(ba​)−n=(ab​)n, so (23)−2=94\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-2} = \tfrac{9}{4}(32​)−2=49​.
  • With a0a^0a0 and a−na^{-n}a−n defined this way, every law of exponents holds for a nonzero base and all integer exponents. So 23×2−5=2−2=142^3 \times 2^{-5} = 2^{-2} = \tfrac{1}{4}23×2−5=2−2=41​, and the quotient rule no longer needs m>nm > nm>n.

A bit of history (optional)

What could it mean to use a number as a factor zero times, or negative three times?

Nicolas Chuquet, a doctor in France with a serious interest in numbers, wrote a manuscript about them in 1484. In it, every power carried a small raised counter, much like the ones you write today. He let that counter be zero, and he let it be negative, and worked with the results as ordinary quantities.

The manuscript stayed unprinted for centuries after Chuquet wrote it.

This lesson gives one reason those meanings must hold: the quotient rule leaves no freedom at all about a0a^0a0 or a−na^{-n}a−n, so those meanings are forced rather than chosen.
