    Which statement about the number 000 is true?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    You chose A: Counts zero among the positive numbers, although zero is the dividing point between the two sides of the line and belongs to neither.

    You chose B: Reads the writable form −0-0−0 as a second, negative number, although −0-0−0 and 000 name the same point on the line.

    You chose D: Requires an integer to record a countable amount, although the integers are the whole numbers together with all their negatives, and zero is one of them.

    Zero sits at the single dividing point of the line: every positive number lies to its right and every negative number to its left, so it is on neither side.

    −0=0-0 = 0−0=0

    Putting a minus sign in front of it names the same point, which is exactly what it means for a number to be its own opposite. Zero is an integer, and it is the only integer that is neither positive nor negative.

  1. Problem 1 Four labeled marks

    On a number line, mark A is halfway between −2-2−2 and −1-1−1. Mark B is the reflection of 000 across zero. Mark C is nineteen one-unit steps left of zero, and mark D is twenty-four one-unit steps right of zero. Which marks sit at integers? Give the integer at each.

      Integers are the whole numbers and their negatives, including zero.

    2. Hint 2

      Check whether each position is reached by a whole number of one-unit steps from zero; reflection across zero preserves distance from zero.

    Answer

    B: 000; C: −19-19−19; D: 242424. A does not sit at an integer.

    Full solution

    A lies strictly between two neighboring integer ticks, so it does not sit at an integer.

    B is at the point where the line is folded, so reflection keeps it at zero.

    −0=0-0=0−0=0

    C is at −19-19−19, since it is nineteen unit steps to the left.

    D is at 242424, since it is twenty-four unit steps to the right.

    Those two numbers and 000 are integers.

    As a check, B, C and D can all be reached from zero in a whole number of unit steps; A cannot.

    Answer

    B: 000; C: −19-19−19; D: 242424. A does not sit at an integer.

    Key idea

    Whole numbers and their negatives are integers, and reflecting zero leaves it at zero.

    Clock A is now 8 seconds behind.

    Set B back by subtracting 444 from its offset.

    −6−4=−10-6-4=-10−6−4=−10

    Clock B is now 10 seconds behind.

    Compare the new offsets.

    −8>−10-8>-10−8>−10

    Clock A has the greater offset, so it displays the later time.

    Check by undoing the adjustments.

    −8−9=−17−10+4=−6\begin{aligned}-8-9&=-17\\-10+4&=-6\end{aligned}−8−9−10+4​=−17=−6​

    The original offsets are recovered.

    Answer

    A: −8-8−8 seconds. B: −10-10−10 seconds. Clock A shows the later time.

    Key idea

    Update each signed quantity before comparing the resulting positions.

    Answer

    Key idea

    Finish grouped calculations, products and quotients before combining their signed results.

  6. Problem 6 A two-part expression

    Evaluate ∣−12+5∣−∣3−11∣\lvert-12+5\rvert-\lvert3-11\rvert∣−12+5∣−∣3−11∣.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

  7. Problem 7 Two display entries

    A programmer replaces ∣(−8)×(−11)∣\lvert(-8)\times(-11)\rvert∣(−8)×(−11)∣ with ∣−8∣×(−11)\lvert-8\rvert\times(-11)∣−8∣×(−11), claiming that the value stays the same. Find both values and assess the claim.

  8. Problem 8 Two moving markers

    Markers A and B begin at integer positions on a number line. Reflecting A's starting position across zero gives B's starting position. A moves 7 one-unit steps to the right, while B moves 4 one-unit steps to the left.

    Can the sum of their final labels be found without knowing where the markers start? If so, give the sum and explain, including the case when both markers start at zero.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the reflection to relate the two starting labels.

    2. Hint 2

      Separate each starting label from the change made to it, then consider the total of the starting labels.

    3. Hint 3

      Combine the two changes after accounting for the starting pair.

    Answer

    Yes; the sum is 333 for every starting pair, including when both markers start at zero.

    Full solution

    The reflection means the starting labels are opposites.

    Write them as aaa and −a-a−a, where aaa is an integer.

    They sum to zero.

    a+(−a)=0a+(-a)=0a+(−a)=0

    The first movement adds 777 and the second adds −4-4−4.

    Reordering and grouping the additions brings the two starting labels together and the two changes together.

    The starting labels contribute zero, and the changes contribute

    7+(−4)=37+(-4)=37+(−4)=3

    The final total is therefore

    0+3=30+3=30+3=3

    The letter aaa no longer appears, so the sum is the same for every starting pair.

    If both markers start at zero, their final labels are 777 and −4-4−4, with sum 333.

    For another check, start at −10-10−10 and 101010: the final labels are −3-3−3 and 666, which also sum to 333.

    Answer

    Yes; the sum is 333 for every starting pair, including when both markers start at zero.

    Key idea

    When the starting labels are opposites, the sum of the final labels depends only on the two changes.

  9. Problem 9 A trail report

    A straight trail has signed distance markers in kilometers, with positive positions east of zero and negative positions west of zero. A cabin is at −25-25−25 kilometers and a mast is at −8-8−8 kilometers.

    A report says, "The mast is closer to zero, so it is west of the cabin." Assess the report and give the distance between the cabin and the mast.

    1. Hint 1

      A position determines east or west, while a distance records the size of a gap.

    2. Hint 2

      Place both negative labels on the western side of zero and decide which is farther right.

    3. Hint 3

      Count the gap between the two positions rather than adding their distances from zero.

    Answer

    The report's conclusion is wrong: the mast is closer to zero, but it is east of the cabin. They are 17 kilometers apart.

    Full solution

    The cabin is 25 kilometers west of zero, while the mast is 8 kilometers west.

    The mast is farther right on the line.

    −8>−25-8>-25−8>−25

    Thus the mast is east of the cabin, although both are west of zero.

    The segment from the cabin to zero consists of the cabin-to-mast gap followed by 8 kilometers from the mast to zero.

    Subtract those 8 kilometers from the full 25 kilometers.

    25−8=1725-8=1725−8=17

    They are 17 kilometers apart.

    Check by joining the two segments again.

    17+8=2517+8=2517+8=25

    Being closer to zero on the negative side places the mast farther east, so the report has the direction wrong.

    Answer

    The report's conclusion is wrong: the mast is closer to zero, but it is east of the cabin. They are 17 kilometers apart.

    Key idea

    For two negative positions, the one closer to zero is the greater and lies farther east, and the gap between them is the difference of their distances from zero.

  10. Problem 10 Two spreadsheet entries

    Two spreadsheet entries are written as −8+13−5−(−5)\frac{-8+13}{-5-(-5)}−5−(−5)−8+13​ and −8+8−5−(−5)\frac{-8+8}{-5-(-5)}−5−(−5)−8+8​. A reviewer says that each entry has value 000. Is the reviewer right about either entry? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      A quotient must specify one number that recovers the numerator when multiplied by the denominator.

    2. Hint 2

      Evaluate both numerators and the common denominator before judging the claim.

    3. Hint 3

      Consider how many numbers could satisfy each corresponding multiplication.

    Answer

    The reviewer is wrong about both entries: neither has a value, and each is undefined.

    Full solution

    The common denominator is found by subtracting a negative.

    −5−(−5)=0-5-(-5)=0−5−(−5)=0

    The numerators are

    −8+13=5−8+8=0\begin{aligned}-8+13&=5\\-8+8&=0\end{aligned}−8+13−8+8​=5=0​

    The first expression would require a number qqq satisfying

    q×0=5q\times0=5q×0=5

    Every product with a zero factor equals zero, so no number satisfies this condition.

    The first expression is undefined.

    The second expression would require

    q×0=0q\times0=0q×0=0

    Every number satisfies that condition.

    For example, both q=0q=0q=0 and q=2q=2q=2 work, so the condition does not select a unique quotient.

    The second expression is also undefined.

    Neither expression permits division, since its denominator is zero.

    In the first case there is no matching factor, and in the second there is no single matching factor; reporting zero is invalid in both cases.

    Answer

    The reviewer is wrong about both entries: neither has a value, and each is undefined.

    Key idea

    A denominator that works out to zero leaves no single quotient, so the expression has no value whatever its numerator.
