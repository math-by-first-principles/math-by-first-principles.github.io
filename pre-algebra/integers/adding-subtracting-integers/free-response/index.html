    1. Two walks from the same starting point . Foundational, 10 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 10 pts

    A sum of two integers is a walk on the number line. You stand on the first number, and the second number says which way to step and how far. Everything below is read off that walk, so no rule has to be remembered.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Two walks begin at −11-11−11. Compute (−11)+(−9)(-11) + (-9)(−11)+(−9) and (−11)+9(-11) + 9(−11)+9, and for each one say which way the second step goes and how far it travels before you give the landing point.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      This time the two ends of a walk are given and the step is missing. One walk starts at 141414 and ends at 666; another starts at −3-3−3 and ends at 555. Find the number added in each case.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Suppose the two numbers being added have different signs, so the two steps go opposite ways. Explain what decides which side of zero the walk ends on, what decides how far from zero it ends, and what happens if the two distances are equal. Argue from the steps themselves rather than quoting a rule.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      A sum is a walk. Stand on the first number and let the second number tell you which way to face and how many units to travel; the place you stop is the answer.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      Here you are handed the two ends of the walk instead of the step. Ask how many units apart the ends are, and which way you have to face to get from the first to the second.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Pair one unit of the leftward move with one unit of the rightward move and ask what that pair does to the walk. Then ask which move still has units to spare once the pairing stops.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    (−11)+(−9)=−20(-11) + (-9) = -20(−11)+(−9)=−20 and (−11)+9=−2(-11) + 9 = -2(−11)+9=−2.

    Part B

    The first walk adds −8-8−8 and the second adds 888.

    Part C

    The side is decided by whichever number is farther from zero, since only that move has units left once the pairing stops, and the distance from zero is the count of those leftovers, the larger distance less the smaller. Equal distances cancel completely and land on 000, which is on neither side.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Both walks stand on −11-11−11, which is eleven units to the left of zero. Only the direction of the second step is different.

    Adding −9-9−9 steps nine units further left, so the two leftward moves pile up and the distances add:

    (−11)+(−9)=−(11+9)=−20.(-11) + (-9) = -(11 + 9) = -20.(−11)+(−9)=−(11+9)=−20.

    Adding 999 steps nine units to the right instead. Those nine right-units cancel nine of the eleven left-units on the way back toward zero, and two left-units survive:

    (−11)+9=−(11−9)=−2.(-11) + 9 = -(11 - 9) = -2.(−11)+9=−(11−9)=−2.

    The second walk never reaches zero, which is why the landing point is still to the left of it.

    Part B

    A step is fixed by two things, how long it is and which way it points, and the two ends of a walk supply both.

    From 141414 to 666 the walk covers eight units and moves toward zero, that is to the left. A step to the left is what adding a negative does, so the number added is −8-8−8:

    14+(−8)=6.14 + (-8) = 6.14+(−8)=6.

    From −3-3−3 to 555 the walk again covers eight units, since three of them reach zero and five more carry on past it, but this time the movement is to the right, so the number added is 888:

    −3+8=5.-3 + 8 = 5.−3+8=5.

    The two steps are the same length and point opposite ways, which is exactly the relationship between −8-8−8 and 888.

    Part C

    Pair one unit of the leftward move against one unit of the rightward move. Each such pair is a step out and a step straight back, so it returns the walk to where it began and contributes nothing. That is the zero-pair fact a+(−a)=0a + (-a) = 0a+(−a)=0 applied one unit at a time.

    The pairing continues until one of the two moves is used up, and the one that runs out first is the shorter one. So every surviving unit belongs to the longer move, that is to whichever number is farther from zero, and every surviving unit points that number's way. The walk therefore finishes on that number's side of zero, whatever the other number is.

    How far from zero it finishes is the same count read as a distance: the longer move, less the part of it that was cancelled. A single pair of examples shows both halves at once:

    (−18)+7=−(18−7)=−11,18+(−7)=18−7=11.(-18) + 7 = -(18 - 7) = -11, \qquad 18 + (-7) = 18 - 7 = 11.(−18)+7=−(18−7)=−11,18+(−7)=18−7=11.

    If the two distances happen to be equal, nothing survives the pairing at all. The walk ends on zero, which is neither to the left nor to the right, and the sum has no sign to inherit.

    In one line

    (−11)+(−9)=−20(-11) + (-9) = -20(−11)+(−9)=−20 and (−11)+9=−2(-11) + 9 = -2(−11)+9=−2; the missing steps are −8-8−8 and 888; and when the signs differ the units pair off, so the walk ends on the side of whichever number is farther from zero, at a distance equal to the larger distance less the smaller, while equal distances cancel completely and land on 000, which is on neither side.

    2. Partners that bring a number to zero . Foundational, 11 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Foundational 11 pts

    One sum matters more than any other in this chapter: the one that lands exactly on zero. Once you can find the number that does it, a sum whose signs disagree becomes a matter of cancelling what will cancel and reading off what is left.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Find the number that must be added to −13-13−13 to land exactly on zero, and the number that must be added to 777 to land exactly on zero. Then say in a phrase what each of those numbers is to its partner.

    2. B
      Part B.

      Split 151515 into two whole-number pieces so that one piece forms a zero pair with −9-9−9. Then use that split to write −9+15-9 + 15−9+15 as zero plus a single leftover, and state the sum.

      Evaluate −12+7+12+(−3)-12 + 7 + 12 + (-3)−12+7+12+(−3) by spotting a zero pair among the four terms and adding that pair first. Then say why you are allowed to add those two terms before the others, and what finding the pair saved you.

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      A walk that must stop exactly on zero has no freedom left in it: where you start decides both how long the step has to be and which way it has to point.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      Look inside the larger number for a piece that is the partner of the smaller one, then use the freedom to add in whichever grouping you please.

    The answer

    Part A

    131313 brings −13-13−13 to zero and −7-7−7 brings 777 to zero. Each number of a pair is the opposite of the other, the same distance from zero on the other side.

    Part B

    15=9+615 = 9 + 615=9+6, so −9+15=(−9+9)+6=0+6=6-9 + 15 = (-9 + 9) + 6 = 0 + 6 = 6−9+15=(−9+9)+6=0+6=6.

    Part C

    The zero pair is −12-12−12 and 121212, so −12+7+12+(−3)=(−12+12)+(7+(−3))=0+4=4-12 + 7 + 12 + (-3) = (-12 + 12) + (7 + (-3)) = 0 + 4 = 4−12+7+12+(−3)=(−12+12)+(7+(−3))=0+4=4. Additions may be taken in any order and grouped in any way, so the pair can be added first, and it contributes nothing to the total.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Read each one as a walk that has to stop exactly on zero.

    Start at −13-13−13, thirteen units to the left. Only a rightward step of thirteen units arrives, and a rightward step of thirteen units is what adding 131313 does:

    −13+13=0.-13 + 13 = 0.−13+13=0.

    Start at 777, seven units to the right. Now the step must point left and must be seven units long, so the number added is −7-7−7:

    7+(−7)=0.7 + (-7) = 0.7+(−7)=0.

    In each pair the two numbers sit the same distance from zero on opposite sides, which is what it means for one to be the opposite of the other. Two numbers that add to zero in this way are called a zero pair.

    Part B

    The point of splitting is to make a zero pair appear. The partner of −9-9−9 is 999, so take a 999 out of the 151515:

    15=9+6.15 = 9 + 6.15=9+6.

    Addition may be regrouped freely, so the pair can be put together and added first:

    −9+15=−9+(9+6)=(−9+9)+6=0+6=6.-9 + 15 = -9 + (9 + 6) = (-9 + 9) + 6 = 0 + 6 = 6.−9+15=−9+(9+6)=(−9+9)+6=0+6=6.

    The walk says the same thing. The first nine of the fifteen rightward units are spent climbing back to zero, and the remaining six carry on past it. That is the whole of the different-signs case: cancel what cancels, then read off what is left.

    Part C

    Look through the four terms for two that are opposites. Here −12-12−12 and 121212 are a zero pair.

    Additions may be taken in any order and grouped in any way, so those two terms can be brought together and added first:

    −12+7+12+(−3)=(−12+12)+(7+(−3)).-12 + 7 + 12 + (-3) = (-12 + 12) + (7 + (-3)).−12+7+12+(−3)=(−12+12)+(7+(−3)).

    The pair adds to nothing, so only the other two terms are left. Their signs disagree, the distances are 777 and 333, and the number farther from zero is positive:

    0+(7+(−3))=0+4=4.0 + (7 + (-3)) = 0 + 4 = 4.0+(7+(−3))=0+4=4.

    What the zero pair saved is two stops on the walk. Going straight through from the left reaches the same landing point by a longer route: −12+7=−5-12 + 7 = -5−12+7=−5, then −5+12=7-5 + 12 = 7−5+12=7, then 7+(−3)=47 + (-3) = 47+(−3)=4.

    In one line

    The partners are 131313 and −7-7−7; splitting 151515 as 9+69 + 69+6 gives −9+15=(−9+9)+6=6-9 + 15 = (-9 + 9) + 6 = 6−9+15=(−9+9)+6=6; and the zero pair −12-12−12 and 121212 gives −12+7+12+(−3)=0+4=4-12 + 7 + 12 + (-3) = 0 + 4 = 4−12+7+12+(−3)=0+4=4.

    Another way: Cancel with counters instead of walking

    Lay out one counter for each unit: nine counters marked negative for −9-9−9, and fifteen marked positive for 151515. Now sweep away pairs, one negative with one positive, since each such pair adds to nothing. Nine sweeps exhaust the negatives, and what is left on the table is the sum:

    −9+15=6.-9 + 15 = 6.−9+15=6.

    When it is worth it When you want the cancelling to happen all at once rather than one step at a time, and as an independent check on a sum whose two numbers have different signs. It also makes the count of leftover counters, rather than a rule about signs, the thing that decides the answer.

    3. A night at the weather station . Application, 12 points. Question 3 of 5.

    Application 12 pts

    An automated station records a temperature at midnight and then logs each rise and each fall over the hours that follow. The site is cold enough in winter that readings below zero are ordinary rather than remarkable.

    1. A
      Part A.

      The midnight reading is −7-7−7 degrees Celsius. Over the next hour the temperature falls 666 degrees, and over the hour after that it rises 444 degrees. Give the reading after each of those two changes.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Later that day the station reads −5-5−5 degrees Celsius at 6 a.m. and 888 degrees Celsius at noon. Write the change from the 6 a.m. reading to the noon reading as a single signed number, showing both the subtraction that produces it and the rewrite you use to evaluate it.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      The log records changes as signed numbers rather than in words such as rose or fell. Explain what the sign of a recorded change tells a reader and what its size tells them, and explain how a change can be farther from zero than either of the two readings it connects.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 5 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Record every rise and every fall as a signed step first, then walk the readings forward in the order the log lists them.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      A change answers the question of what must be added to the earlier reading to arrive at the later one, so build it by taking one reading away from the other.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Split the walk between two readings at the moment it passes zero and ask how long each of the two pieces is.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    −13-13−13 degrees Celsius after the fall, then −9-9−9 degrees Celsius after the rise.

    Part B

    8−(−5)=8+5=138 - (-5) = 8 + 5 = 138−(−5)=8+5=13, a rise of 131313 degrees Celsius.

    Part C

    The sign gives the direction, warmer to the right and colder to the left, and the size gives how many degrees the reading travelled. When the two readings lie on opposite sides of zero, the walk covers the distance up to zero and the distance beyond it, and those two legs add, carrying it farther from zero than either reading.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Each logged change is a step along the line: a fall steps left, a rise steps right.

    Start at −7-7−7 degrees Celsius. A fall of six degrees adds −6-6−6, and since the position and the step are both on the left of zero, the distances add:

    −7+(−6)=−13.-7 + (-6) = -13.−7+(−6)=−13.

    From −13-13−13 degrees Celsius a rise of four degrees adds 444, a step of four units to the right. The signs disagree now, so four of the thirteen left-units cancel and nine survive:

    −13+4=−9.-13 + 4 = -9.−13+4=−9.

    So the station reads −13-13−13 degrees Celsius after the fall and −9-9−9 degrees Celsius after the rise. The later reading is the warmer of the two even though both are below zero, because it lies nearer zero on the line.

    Part B

    A change is what has to be added to the earlier reading to arrive at the later one, so it is the later reading with the earlier one taken away:

    8−(−5).8 - (-5).8−(−5).

    Subtracting a number is adding its opposite, and the opposite of −5-5−5 is 555:

    8−(−5)=8+5=13.8 - (-5) = 8 + 5 = 13.8−(−5)=8+5=13.

    The change is a rise of thirteen degrees Celsius, and the number line confirms it directly: the walk from −5-5−5 to 888 climbs five units to reach zero and eight more beyond it, and those two distances add because the walk crosses zero.

    Taking the readings in the other order, −5−8-5 - 8−5−8, answers a different question, where the earlier reading stands relative to the later one, and it reports a fall of thirteen degrees instead.

    Part C

    A change is a step, and one signed number carries both things a step has. Its sign is the direction: a positive change means the reading moved to the right along the line, toward warmer, and a negative change means it moved to the left, toward colder. Its size is how far the reading travelled, counted in degrees.

    That is why a change is not a reading and must not be read as one. A word such as rose would give the direction and lose the size; a bare size would give the distance and lose the direction. The signed number keeps both.

    A change farther from zero than both of its readings is then no puzzle. When the two readings lie on opposite sides of zero, the walk between them is made of two legs, one up to zero and one beyond it, and the legs add. From −5-5−5 degrees Celsius to 888 degrees Celsius those legs are five units and eight units:

    5+8=13.5 + 8 = 13.5+8=13.

    No reading of thirteen degrees ever appeared on the thermometer. The thirteen is a distance travelled, not a place on the scale, and only a change that crosses zero can finish farther from zero than both of the readings it connects.

    In one line

    The station reads −13-13−13 degrees Celsius and then −9-9−9 degrees Celsius; the change from −5-5−5 to 888 is 8−(−5)=138 - (-5) = 138−(−5)=13, a rise of 131313 degrees Celsius; and a recorded change carries a direction in its sign and a distance travelled in its size, which is why one that crosses zero can be farther from zero than either reading it connects.

    Part A

    The rewrite gives 191919, and 19+(−7)=1219 + (-7) = 1219+(−7)=12, so 191919 is right. Subtracting −7-7−7 undoes a step of adding −7-7−7, so putting that step back has to return you to the number you started from.

    Part C

    Reordering moves the two terms an addition already has, so a+(−b)a + (-b)a+(−b) becomes (−b)+a(-b) + a(−b)+a, which is still a−ba - ba−b. Reaching b+(−a)b + (-a)b+(−a) would mean swapping each term for its opposite, which reordering never permits. For instance 4−15=−114 - 15 = -114−15=−11 while 15−4=1115 - 4 = 1115−4=11.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Trade each subtraction for an addition of the opposite, then use what you already know about adding.

    The opposite of 666 is −6-6−6, so the first becomes an addition of two negatives and the distances add:

    −14−6=−14+(−6)=−20.-14 - 6 = -14 + (-6) = -20.−14−6=−14+(−6)=−20.

    Part C

    Both ingredients are sound on their own. Subtraction really can be rewritten, and an addition really can be taken in either order. What fails is the last step, and the reason is what reordering is allowed to move.

    Make the rewrite:

    a−b=a+(−b).a - b = a + (-b).a−b=a+(−b).

    The terms of that addition are aaa and −b-b−b. Reordering moves those two terms and nothing else, so it produces (−b)+a(-b) + a(−b)+a, which has the same value and is still a−ba - ba−b. To arrive at b−ab - ab−a, which is b+(−a)b + (-a)b+(−a), each term would have to be exchanged for its opposite: aaa for −a-a−a and −b-b−b for bbb. That is a different move, and no rule about adding allows it.

    One pair of numbers settles the claim:

    4−15=4+(−15)=−11,15−4=15+(−4)=11.4 - 15 = 4 + (-15) = -11, \qquad 15 - 4 = 15 + (-4) = 11.4−15=4+(−15)=−11,15−4=15+(−4)=11.

    The two results are −11-11−11 and 111111, so the student's conclusion is wrong: swapping the two numbers has not left the answer alone, it has moved it to the other side of zero.

    The answer

    Part A

    In order: negative, neither positive nor negative, negative, positive.

    Part B

    −11+20−7−(−3)=5-11 + 20 - 7 - (-3) = 5−11+20−7−(−3)=5. The rightward travel totals 232323 units against 181818 units leftward, so the result is positive.

    Part C

    The sums are −18-18−18, 181818 and −43-43−43. Only the third, whose two signs match, lands farther from zero than both its numbers. When the signs disagree the units pair off, so what survives is only part of the longer move, and the walk can never finish farther out than the farther number.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Each verdict comes from the two directions and the two distances alone.

    (−23)+18(-23) + 18(−23)+18: the signs disagree, so the units pair off, and the number farther from zero is −23-23−23. The survivors lie to the left, so the sum is negative.

    17+(−17)17 + (-17)17+(−17): the two numbers are opposites, a zero pair, so every unit finds a partner and none survives. The walk stops on zero, which is neither positive nor negative.

    (−12)+(−31)(-12) + (-31)(−12)+(−31): both steps go left, so they pile up and the walk can only finish farther left than it began. The sum is negative.

    25+(−19)25 + (-19)25+(−19): the signs disagree and the number farther from zero is 252525, so the survivors lie to the right and the sum is positive.

    Evaluating afterwards confirms all four calls, though none of them needed the values:

    −5,0,−43,6.-5, \qquad 0, \qquad -43, \qquad 6.−5,0,−43,6.

    Part B

    Turn every subtraction into an addition of the opposite, so the chain becomes one string of steps in the same operation:

    −11+20−7−(−3)=−11+20+(−7)+3.-11 + 20 - 7 - (-3) = -11 + 20 + (-7) + 3.−11+20−7−(−3)=−11+20+(−7)+3.

    Now the sign can be settled before any adding. The rightward steps are 202020 and 333, which come to twenty-three units of travel to the right. The leftward steps are −11-11−11 and −7-7−7, which come to eighteen units to the left. More of the walk goes right than left, so it finishes to the right of zero and the answer is positive.

    The pairing off then leaves the difference of those two totals:

    23+(−18)=5.23 + (-18) = 5.23+(−18)=5.

    A left-to-right check agrees: −11+20=9-11 + 20 = 9−11+20=9, then 9+(−7)=29 + (-7) = 29+(−7)=2, then 2+3=52 + 3 = 52+3=5.

    Part C

    Work out the three sums first.

    (−30)+12=−18,30+(−12)=18,(−12)+(−31)=−43.(-30) + 12 = -18, \qquad 30 + (-12) = 18, \qquad (-12) + (-31) = -43.(−30)+12=−18,30+(−12)=18,(−12)+(−31)=−43.

    In the first two the signs disagree, and 181818 is nearer zero than 303030 is, so neither lands farther out than both of its numbers. In the third both numbers are negative, and −43-43−43 is farther from zero than −12-12−12 and farther than −31-31−31.

    Now the reason behind the first two. When the signs disagree, one step goes right and the other left, so their units pair off, and each pair leaves the walk where it was. The pairing stops when the shorter move runs out. What survives is part of the longer move and never more than all of it, so the landing point is never farther from zero than the farther of the two numbers. Two numbers at equal distances are the extreme case: every unit finds a partner, and the walk stops on zero.

    Matching signs behave the other way, which is what the third sum shows. Two steps that go the same way pile up, so the walk can only finish farther out than either number.

    In one line

    The four sums are negative, neither positive nor negative, negative and positive; the chain comes to 555, with twenty-three units of rightward travel against eighteen leftward; and the three sums are −18-18−18, 181818 and −43-43−43, of which only the matching-sign one lands farther from zero than both its numbers, because units pointing opposite ways pair off and leave at most part of the longer move.
