  1. Problem 1 Four corners, one objective

    A bounded feasible region includes its boundary and has corners (0,2)(0,2)(0,2), (5,0)(5,0)(5,0), (7,3)(7,3)(7,3), and (3,6)(3,6)(3,6). For the objective P=2x+4yP=2x+4yP=2x+4y, find the value at each corner and state which corner gives the maximum.

    1. Hint 1

      The value at a corner depends only on that corner's own coordinates.

    2. Hint 2

      Work through the four corners one at a time, then compare the four results.

    Answer

    P(0,2)=8P(0,2)=8P(0,2)=8, P(5,0)=10P(5,0)=10P(5,0)=10, P(7,3)=26P(7,3)=26P(7,3)=26, and P(3,6)=30P(3,6)=30P(3,6)=30; the maximum 30 is at (3,6)(3,6)(3,6).

    Full solution

    Each corner value is twice the first coordinate plus four times the second.

    The four corners give

    P(0,2)=8P(0,2)=8P(0,2)=8 P(5,0)=10P(5,0)=10P(5,0)=10 P(7,3)=26P(7,3)=26P(7,3)=26 P(3,6)=30P(3,6)=30P(3,6)=30

    Comparing the four results, the largest is 30, at the corner (3,6)(3,6)(3,6).

    The region is bounded and includes its boundary, so a corner does attain the maximum, and no other feasible point can beat 30.

    Answer

    P(0,2)=8P(0,2)=8P(0,2)=8, P(5,0)=10P(5,0)=10P(5,0)=10, P(7,3)=26P(7,3)=26P(7,3)=26, and P(3,6)=30P(3,6)=30P(3,6)=30; the maximum 30 is at (3,6)(3,6)(3,6).

    Key idea

    On a bounded region that includes its boundary, evaluating a linear objective at every corner and comparing the values locates the maximum.

  2. Problem 2 A liquid order

The order must contain at least 5 liters in all and at least as much A as B. Write a complete linear model for minimizing the cost, including the conditions that keep both amounts from going below zero.

      Separate the quantity being minimized from the conditions that make an order acceptable.

    2. Hint 2

      Add each liquid cost for the objective and translate each quantity condition separately.

    Answer

    Minimize C=4x+7yC=4x+7yC=4x+7y dollars, subject to x+y≥5x+y\ge5x+y≥5, x≥yx\ge yx≥y, x≥0x\ge0x≥0, and y≥0y\ge0y≥0.

    Full solution

    The total cost in dollars is

    C=4x+7yC=4x+7yC=4x+7y

    This is the objective to minimize.

    The total-volume condition is x+y≥5x+y\ge5x+y≥5, and the comparison of liquids is x≥yx\ge yx≥y.

    Both amounts must be zero or positive, so x≥0x\ge0x≥0 and y≥0y\ge0y≥0 are also required.

    The order (5,0)(5,0)(5,0) satisfies all four conditions, confirming that the model permits an order.

    Answer

    Minimize C=4x+7yC=4x+7yC=4x+7y dollars, subject to x+y≥5x+y\ge5x+y≥5, x≥yx\ge yx≥y, x≥0x\ge0x≥0, and y≥0y\ge0y≥0.

    2(5)+3(4)=22.2(5)+3(4)=22.2(5)+3(4)=22.

  5. Problem 5 A program schedule

    A program spends xxx hours on video work and yyy hours on text work. Any real durations are allowed. Each video hour uses 1 unit of studio capacity and each text hour uses 2 units, with at most 12 units available. Video work can receive at most 6 hours, and text work must receive at least 1 hour. Each video hour earns 4 points and each text hour earns 3 points. Write the model and find the greatest score, checking every feasible corner.

    1. Hint 1

      Studio capacity, the video ceiling, and the text minimum are three separate conditions.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the resulting corners to compare the score objective.

    Answer

    Maximize P=4x+3yP=4x+3yP=4x+3y with x+2y≤12x+2y\le12x+2y≤12, x≤6x\le6x≤6, y≥1y\ge1y≥1, x≥0x\ge0x≥0. Maximum 33 points at (6,3)(6,3)(6,3).

    Each video hour uses 1 unit of capacity and each text hour 2, so the capacity condition is x+2y≤12x+2y\le12x+2y≤12.

    The objective is P=4x+3yP=4x+3yP=4x+3y, and the other conditions are x≤6x\le6x≤6, y≥1y\ge1y≥1, and x≥0x\ge0x≥0; here y≥0y\ge0y≥0 follows from y≥1y\ge1y≥1.

    The included-boundary region has vertices (0,1)(0,1)(0,1), (6,1)(6,1)(6,1), (6,3)(6,3)(6,3), and (0,6)(0,6)(0,6).

    The corner (6,3)(6,3)(6,3) is where x=6x=6x=6 meets x+2y=12x+2y=12x+2y=12, and (0,6)(0,6)(0,6) is where x=0x=0x=0 meets that same line.

    The objective values are

    P(0,1)=3P(0,1)=3P(0,1)=3 P(6,1)=27P(6,1)=27P(6,1)=27 P(6,3)=33P(6,3)=33P(6,3)=33 P(0,6)=18P(0,6)=18P(0,6)=18

    That schedule spends 6 hours on video and 3 on text.

    It uses 6+2(3)=126+2(3)=126+2(3)=12 units of capacity, the whole allowance, and it clears the one-hour text minimum.

    Answer

    Maximize P=4x+3yP=4x+3yP=4x+3y with x+2y≤12x+2y\le12x+2y≤12, x≤6x\le6x≤6, y≥1y\ge1y≥1, x≥0x\ge0x≥0. Maximum 33 points at (6,3)(6,3)(6,3).

  6. Problem 6 A strip with no ceiling

    Over real variables satisfying 0≤x≤40\le x\le40≤x≤4 and y≥2y\ge2y≥2, find the maximum and minimum of each objective P=3x−yP=3x-yP=3x−y and Q=y−3xQ=y-3xQ=y−3x, if they exist. Explain how the unbounded direction affects them.

    1. Hint 1

      Determine whether larger heights increase or decrease each objective.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the finite coordinate limits for one extreme, then examine what happens when the second coordinate grows without bound.

    Answer

    P: maximum 10 at (4,2)(4,2)(4,2), no minimum. Q: minimum −10-10−10 at (4,2)(4,2)(4,2), no maximum.

    Full solution

    Since x≤4x\le4x≤4 and y≥2y\ge2y≥2,

    3x−y≤12−23x-y\le12-23x−y≤12−2 P≤10P\le10P≤10

    Equality occurs at (4,2)(4,2)(4,2), which is feasible.

    Thus the maximum is 10.

    The points (0,2+t)(0,2+t)(0,2+t) are feasible for every t≥0t\ge0t≥0 and give

    P=−2−tP=-2-tP=−2−t

    This decreases without bound as ttt grows, so there is no minimum.

    The unbounded direction lowers the objective rather than raising it.

    The second objective is the negative of the first:

    Q=−PQ=-PQ=−P

    Hence Q≥−10Q\ge-10Q≥−10, with equality at (4,2)(4,2)(4,2).

    Along the same feasible points (0,2+t)(0,2+t)(0,2+t),

    Q=2+tQ=2+tQ=2+t

    This grows without bound, so Q has no maximum.

    The allowed upward direction lowers P and raises Q.

    Answer

    P: maximum 10 at (4,2)(4,2)(4,2), no minimum. Q: minimum −10-10−10 at (4,2)(4,2)(4,2), no maximum.

    Key idea

    An unbounded direction can destroy a maximum or a minimum depending on the signs in the objective.

  7. Problem 7 An allocation request

  8. Problem 8 An additional requirement

    For 0≤x≤30\le x\le30≤x≤3 and 0≤y≤20\le y\le20≤y≤2, the objective is P=2x+yP=2x+yP=2x+y. A new requirement x+y≥4x+y\ge4x+y≥4 is added. A student claims the maximum value stays the same. Decide whether the claim is correct, and justify your answer.

    Answer

    Yes; the maximum remains 8 at (3,2)(3,2)(3,2).

    P≤2(3)+2P\le2(3)+2P≤2(3)+2 P≤8P\le8P≤8

    The new feasible region is contained in the old one, so it cannot produce an objective value above 8.

    Since the old maximizing point survives, 8 remains the maximum despite removal of other corners.
