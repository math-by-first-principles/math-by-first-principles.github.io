    Suppose a<ba < ba<b. Which of these moves is NOT guaranteed to leave a true statement behind?

    Answer choices for question 14

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    You chose B: Treats a negative addend as a trigger for the reversal; a shift moves both sides equally, so their order survives.

    You chose C: Counts division as riskier than multiplication, although scaling by a positive number preserves the order either way.

    You chose D: Counts a negative multiplier as unsafe even when the symbol is reversed alongside it, which is exactly the pairing that keeps the statement true.

    The direction of an inequality survives a shift, and it survives scaling by a positive number; scaling by a negative number reverses it. So a multiplier decides which of two outcomes is correct, and that decision is made by its sign alone. With the sign of ttt unknown, both outcomes are still live:

    t=2:  2a<2b,t=−2:  −2a>−2bt = 2: \; 2a < 2b, \qquad t = -2: \; -2a > -2bt=2:2a<2b,t=−2:−2a>−2b

    So the move is not that multiplying by a variable is forbidden; it is that you cannot tell whether to reverse the symbol until the sign is settled.

Where you stand

  • Move a one-variable solution set between words, an inequality symbol, a number-line picture, and interval notation, choosing an included or excluded endpoint correctly and always a parenthesis at infinity. Review: Inequality Basics
  • Decide which rearranging moves preserve an inequality's direction and which reverse it, and locate the reversal at the step that multiplies or divides both sides by a negative number. Review: Inequality Basics , Solving Linear Inequalities
  • Solve a multi-step linear inequality in one variable, clearing parentheses and collecting variable terms, and report the solution set, including translating a described situation into an inequality and reading the bound back into that situation. Review: Solving Linear Inequalities
  • Handle a compound condition: an "and" that keeps only what both parts allow, an "or" that keeps what either allows, and the case where the variable cancels and the surviving statement decides. Review: Inequality Basics , Solving Linear Inequalities
  • Graph a two-variable linear inequality: draw the boundary dashed or solid from the symbol, pick the shaded side with a test point that is not on the line, and decide by substitution whether a given point is a solution. Review: Graphing Inequalities

  3. Problem 3 A refill decision

    A large container starts with 3 liters of liquid. A refill adds xxx liters, then one quarter of the resulting liquid is removed. At least 6 liters must remain. For real x≥0x\ge0x≥0, what refills meet the requirement?

    1. Hint 1

      Express the volume left after the removal.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare that expression with the required final amount and retain the input restriction.

    Answer

    x≥5x\ge5x≥5 liters.

    Full solution

    After the refill, the volume is 3+x3+x3+x liters.

    Keeping three quarters requires

    34(3+x)≥6\frac34(3+x)\ge643​(3+x)≥6

    Multiply by 4/34/34/3, which is positive:

    3+x≥83+x\ge83+x≥8 x≥5x\ge5x≥5

    This already meets x≥0x\ge0x≥0.

    At 5 liters added, the container has 8 liters before removal and 6 afterward.

    A smaller refill leaves less than 6 liters.

    Answer

    x≥5x\ge5x≥5 liters.

    Key idea

    A retained fraction of a changing total leads to an inequality for the amount added.
