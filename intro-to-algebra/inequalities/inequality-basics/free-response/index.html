  1. Problem 1 Which number is larger

    Compare −74-\frac74−47​ and −1.8-1.8−1.8 by placing <<< or >>> between them.

    1. Hint 1

      Put both numbers in a form that makes their positions easy to compare.

    2. Hint 2

      Among negative numbers, the one closer to zero is greater.

    Answer

    −74>−1.8-\frac74>-1.8−47​>−1.8.

    Full solution

    Convert the fraction exactly:

    −74=−1.75-\frac74=-1.75−47​=−1.75

    Since −1.75-1.75−1.75 is closer to zero than −1.8-1.8−1.8,

    −74>−1.8-\frac74>-1.8−47​>−1.8

    As a check, their difference is −1.75−(−1.8)=0.05-1.75-(-1.8)=0.05−1.75−(−1.8)=0.05, which is positive.

    Answer

    −74>−1.8-\frac74>-1.8−47​>−1.8.

    Key idea

    Comparing equivalent decimal forms can clarify the order of negative fractions.

  2. Problem 2 An interval from two clues

    An interval extends without bound in one direction and has its only real endpoint at 5. It contains 9 and excludes 5. Write the interval.

    1. Hint 1

      The member you are given tells you which side of the endpoint the interval lies on.

    2. Hint 2

      Match each end of the interval to its fence: one end is a number that is not a member, the other is not a number at all.

    Answer

    (5,∞)(5,\infty)(5,∞).

    Full solution

    The member 9 is larger than the endpoint 5, so the interval extends right without bound.

    Because 5 itself is excluded, the real endpoint takes a parenthesis, and an unbounded end always takes one too:

    (5,∞)(5,\infty)(5,∞)

    This contains 9 and excludes 5, as required.

    Answer

    (5,∞)(5,\infty)(5,∞).

    Key idea

    Endpoint membership and one interior value can identify the direction and fences of an unbounded interval.

  3. Problem 3 The lift and the wind

    A ski lift runs only when the wind speed www, measured in miles per hour, is at most 35. A wind speed is never negative. Give the possible values of www while the lift is running, as an inequality and as an interval.

    1. Hint 1

      Decide whether a wind speed of exactly 35 miles per hour still lets the lift run.

    2. Hint 2

      The condition carries a lower bound as well as an upper one, and each end takes the fence its own symbol calls for.

    Answer

    0≤w≤350\le w\le350≤w≤35; [0,35][0,35][0,35].

    Full solution

    Two conditions hold at once.

    A wind speed is never negative, which gives w≥0w\ge0w≥0, and the lift runs when the speed is at most 35, which allows exactly 35 and everything below it, giving w≤35w\le35w≤35.

    Written as one line with the smaller bound on the left, that is

    0≤w≤350\le w\le350≤w≤35

    Both bounds are allowed, so both ends take brackets, and the interval is [0,35][0,35][0,35].

    A speed of 35 still runs the lift, while 36 does not.

    Answer

    0≤w≤350\le w\le350≤w≤35; [0,35][0,35][0,35].

    Key idea

    A phrase such as at most keeps its boundary, so that end of the interval takes a bracket.

  4. Problem 4 Two acceptance bands

    The figure shows the numbers accepted by devices A and B. Give the numbers xxx accepted by both devices as a compound inequality and an interval, and shade them on the blank number line C.

    Text description of this figure

    Three number lines are drawn one above another and labeled A, B, and C. Each runs from negative 6 to 5, with a tick and a label at every integer and an arrowhead at each end. On line A an open circle sits at negative 5 and a filled circle sits at 1, and the stretch between them is shaded. On line B a filled circle sits at negative 2 and an open circle sits at 4, and the stretch between them is shaded. Line C carries no circles and no shading.

    1. Hint 1

      A number must lie in both shaded bands to be accepted by both devices.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the stricter lower bound and the stricter upper bound, then check those endpoints in both bands.

    Answer

    −2≤x≤1-2\le x\le1−2≤x≤1; [−2,1][-2,1][−2,1]; filled circles at −2-2−2 and 1, shaded between.

    Full solution

    A accepts numbers above −5-5−5 through 1, while B accepts numbers from −2-2−2 up to but not including 4.

    Their common part starts at −2-2−2, which A accepts, and ends at 1, which B accepts.

    Thus the common condition is

    −2≤x≤1-2\le x\le1−2≤x≤1

    The interval is [−2,1][-2,1][−2,1].

    Shade between filled circles at −2-2−2 and 1 on C; a number such as 0 checks in both original bands.

    Answer

    −2≤x≤1-2\le x\le1−2≤x≤1; [−2,1][-2,1][−2,1]; filled circles at −2-2−2 and 1, shaded between.

    Key idea

    Two acceptance bands overlap where both lower and upper conditions hold.

  5. Problem 5 The second display

    A dial reading xxx can be any real number with −4<x≤2-4<x\le2−4<x≤2. A second display reports r=−3xr=-3xr=−3x. Give the possible values of rrr as an interval, and draw them on the blank number line in the figure.

    Text description of this figure

    A single number line with an arrowhead at each end. It carries a tick and a label every 2 units, at negative 8, negative 6, negative 4, negative 2, 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 14. The line carries no circles and no shading.

    1. Hint 1

      A negative scale reverses the order of the endpoint readings.

    2. Hint 2

      Track which original endpoint is included when computing the two new bounds.

    Answer

    [−6,12)[-6,12)[−6,12); filled circle at −6-6−6, open circle at 12, shaded between.

    Full solution

    The condition −4<x≤2-4<x\le2−4<x≤2 is two conditions at once.

    From x≤2x\le2x≤2, multiplying by −3-3−3 reverses the direction and gives −3x≥−6-3x\ge-6−3x≥−6.

    From x>−4x>-4x>−4, the same multiplier gives −3x<12-3x<12−3x<12.

    Both hold together, so

    −6≤r<12.-6\le r<12.−6≤r<12.

    Dividing any rrr in that range by −3-3−3 gives back an allowed xxx, so every one of those values does occur.

    The possible readings are [−6,12)[-6,12)[−6,12).

    Draw a filled circle at −6-6−6 and an open circle at 12, shading between.

    Input 0 is permitted and gives output 0 inside the result.

    Answer

    [−6,12)[-6,12)[−6,12); filled circle at −6-6−6, open circle at 12, shaded between.

    Key idea

    A negative scale reverses endpoint order while preserving which endpoint values are included.

  6. Problem 6 A gauge with a cutoff

    A gauge takes a real input xxx and reports r=x−5r=x-5r=x−5. A monitor rejects every reading with r≥2r\ge2r≥2 and accepts all the others. Describe the accepted values of xxx in words, as an inequality, and as an interval, and draw them on the blank number line in the figure.

    Text description of this figure

    A single number line with an arrowhead at each end. It carries a tick and a label at every integer from 1 through 10. The line carries no circles, no shading, and no other marks.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the input that puts the reading exactly at the cutoff value, then decide which side of that input survives.

    2. Hint 2

      Now decide whether the boundary input itself is accepted, and let that choose its fence.

    Answer

    Numbers less than 7; x<7x<7x<7; (−∞,7)(-\infty,7)(−∞,7); open circle at 7, shaded left.

    Full solution

    A reading is rejected exactly when r≥2r\ge2r≥2, that is when

    x−5≥2x-5\ge2x−5≥2

    Adding 5 to both sides leaves the direction alone, so the rejected inputs are those with x≥7x\ge7x≥7.

    Every other input is accepted, so the accepted values are the numbers less than 7:

    x<7x<7x<7

    The boundary 7 is itself rejected, so it is excluded, and the accepted values run down without a lower bound, giving (−∞,7)(-\infty,7)(−∞,7).

    Draw an open circle at 7 and shade left with an arrow.

    As a check, x=6x=6x=6 gives r=1r=1r=1, which is accepted, while x=7x=7x=7 gives r=2r=2r=2, which is rejected.

    Answer

    Numbers less than 7; x<7x<7x<7; (−∞,7)(-\infty,7)(−∞,7); open circle at 7, shaded left.

    Key idea

    The accepted values are everything the rejection rule leaves out, so an inclusive rejection boundary becomes an excluded acceptance boundary.

  7. Problem 7 A shift and a scale

    Two readings satisfy a<ba<ba<b. A processor subtracts 8 from each reading and then divides each result by −2-2−2. State the comparison between the two processed readings and say whether their order has changed from a<ba<ba<b, justifying each step.

    1. Hint 1

      Handle the two moves one after the other, and ask what each one does to the order.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the shifted readings first, then look at the sign of the divisor before writing the second comparison.

    Answer

    a−8−2>b−8−2\frac{a-8}{-2}>\frac{b-8}{-2}−2a−8​>−2b−8​; the order has reversed.

    Full solution

    Subtracting 8 shifts both readings equally, leaving their difference unchanged:

    (b−8)−(a−8)=b−a(b-8)-(a-8)=b-a(b−8)−(a−8)=b−a

    This is positive, so a−8<b−8a-8<b-8a−8<b−8 and the first move keeps the direction.

    Dividing by −2-2−2 scales by a negative number, which reverses the direction:

    a−8−2>b−8−2\frac{a-8}{-2}>\frac{b-8}{-2}−2a−8​>−2b−8​

    So the processed readings compare the opposite way round from a<ba<ba<b.

    Take a=0a=0a=0 and b=2b=2b=2 as a check.

    Subtracting 8 gives −8<−6-8<-6−8<−6, the same direction.

    Dividing by −2-2−2 gives 4 and 3, and 4>34>34>3, so the order has reversed.

    Answer

    a−8−2>b−8−2\frac{a-8}{-2}>\frac{b-8}{-2}−2a−8​>−2b−8​; the order has reversed.

    Key idea

    An equal shift leaves the order alone, so a negative scale applied after it is what reverses the comparison.

  8. Problem 8 A list of members

    A student lists −1-1−1, 0 and 1 and claims these are all the real numbers in [−1,1][-1,1][−1,1]. Is the claim correct? If not, give two numbers of the interval that are missing from the list, and say what kind of number the list leaves out.

    1. Hint 1

      Decide whether an interval of real numbers can be listed one member at a time.

    2. Hint 2

      Look for a value between two of the listed numbers and test it against both bounds.

    Answer

    No; for example 14\frac1441​ and 12\frac1221​ are missing, and any list of integers leaves out the non-integer values between them.

    Full solution

    The claim is not correct.

    Both 14\frac1441​ and 12\frac1221​ satisfy the bounds:

    −1≤14≤1-1\le\frac14\le1−1≤41​≤1 −1≤12≤1-1\le\frac12\le1−1≤21​≤1

    Neither appears in the list.

    Between any two such values lie further real numbers, so a list of integers leaves out every non-integer value the interval holds.

    Answer

    No; for example 14\frac1441​ and 12\frac1221​ are missing, and any list of integers leaves out the non-integer values between them.

    Key idea

    An interval of real numbers holds every value between its bounds, not only the integers a student can list.

  9. Problem 9 Swapped fences

    A student says that [−1,2)[-1,2)[−1,2) and (−1,2](-1,2](−1,2] have exactly the same members except for the two endpoints. Is the claim correct? Describe precisely what changes.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the endpoint fences separately from the interior.

    2. Hint 2

      Test each endpoint in both interval records.

    Answer

    Yes; −1-1−1 belongs only to [−1,2)[-1,2)[−1,2), and 2 belongs only to (−1,2](-1,2](−1,2].

    Full solution

    Both intervals contain every number strictly between −1-1−1 and 2, and both exclude every number outside those bounds.

    The first interval includes −1-1−1 and excludes 2; the second makes the opposite endpoint choices.

    Therefore membership changes at exactly those two numbers.

    A shared interior value such as 0 remains a member of both.

    Answer

    Yes; −1-1−1 belongs only to [−1,2)[-1,2)[−1,2), and 2 belongs only to (−1,2](-1,2](−1,2].

    Key idea

    Changing endpoint fences changes endpoint membership without changing the interior of an interval.

  10. Problem 10 A zero multiplier

    A student starts with a<ba<ba<b and multiplies both sides by zero, claiming the result is still a strict inequality in one direction or the other. Is this possible? State the comparison between the two products and justify it.

    1. Hint 1

      The rules for keeping or reversing a direction were stated for positive and for negative multipliers.

    2. Hint 2

      Work out each product separately, then ask which of <<<, >>> and === can hold between them.

    Answer

    No; both products are 0, and the comparison is 0=00=00=0.

    Full solution

    Both products equal zero, so the resulting comparison is

    0=00=00=0

    Neither 0<00<00<0 nor 0>00>00>0 is true.

    Multiplication by zero removes the distinction between the original numbers rather than preserving or reversing a strict order.

    Answer

    No; both products are 0, and the comparison is 0=00=00=0.

    Key idea

    Multiplication by zero collapses unequal real numbers to equal products.
