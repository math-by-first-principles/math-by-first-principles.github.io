    1. Two two-step inequalities, and the one move that tells them apart . Foundational, 11 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 11 pts

    Solving a linear inequality undoes operations the same way solving an equation does, except for one move that can reverse the symbol. This question solves two two-step inequalities and then asks precisely which move was responsible for the difference between them.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Solve 4x+15>34x + 15 > 34x+15>3. Show each step.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Solve 20−3x≤520 - 3x \le 520−3x≤5. Show each step.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      One of your two answers came out negative and one positive, and exactly one of the two solves needed a flip. State the exact rule that decides whether an inequality's symbol reverses, and explain, using the two parts above, why the sign of the final answer plays no role in that rule.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 4

      Exactly one kind of move reverses an inequality's direction. Everything else, no matter how the numbers along the way happen to look, leaves the direction exactly as it was.

    2. Hint 2 of 4 · Part A

      Undo the addition first, then divide. Check the sign of the number you are about to divide by before deciding whether anything needs to flip.

    3. Hint 3 of 4 · Part B

      Once the constant is out of the way, the coefficient left on xxx is negative. That is the number the next step divides by.

    4. Hint 4 of 4 · Part C

      Look back at the divisor actually used in each part, not at whether the final answer came out positive or negative, and compare the two.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    x>−3x > -3x>−3.

    Part B

    x≥5x \ge 5x≥5.

    Part C

    Only multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the symbol; the sign of the result is irrelevant, since part A divided by a positive number and still ended negative, while part B divided by a negative number and still ended positive.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Undo the addition first, then the multiplication, exactly as for an equation. Subtract 151515 from both sides, which never disturbs the direction:

    4x+15−15>3−15⟹4x>−12.4x + 15 - 15 > 3 - 15 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 4x > -12.4x+15−15>3−15⟹4x>−12.

    Divide both sides by the positive number 444, so the symbol stays put:

    4x4>−124⟹x>−3.\frac{4x}{4} > \frac{-12}{4} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x > -3.44x​>4−12​⟹x>−3.

    The final answer is negative, but no flip occurred anywhere: the only division in this solve was by a positive number.

    Part B

    Isolate the term with xxx before dividing anything. Subtract 202020 from both sides, which does not touch the direction:

    20−3x−20≤5−20⟹−3x≤−15.20 - 3x - 20 \le 5 - 20 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad -3x \le -15.20−3x−20≤5−20⟹−3x≤−15.

    −3x−3≥−15−3⟹x≥5.\frac{-3x}{-3} \ge \frac{-15}{-3} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x \ge 5.−3−3x​≥−3−15​⟹x≥5.

    The final answer is positive this time, and a flip did happen, at the division by −3-3−3.

    Part C

    State the rule first, then test it against the two parts. Multiplying or dividing both sides of an inequality by a negative number reverses the symbol; adding, subtracting, or scaling by a positive number never does.

    Part A's only scaling step divided by 444, a positive number, so no flip was licensed there, and none occurred, even though −3-3−3 is a negative number.

    Part B's only scaling step divided by −3-3−3, a negative number, so a flip was required there, and one occurred, even though 555 is a positive number.

    trigger=sign of the divisor,not the sign of the answer.\text{trigger} = \text{sign of the divisor}, \qquad \text{not the sign of the answer}.trigger=sign of the divisor,not the sign of the answer.

    A rule keyed to the final answer would have gotten both parts backwards: it would flip the one that should not flip and leave alone the one that should. The two parts were built to disagree on the sign of the answer while agreeing on nothing about the rule, which is exactly what separates the real trigger from a coincidence a learner might otherwise latch onto.

    Another way: Sidestep part B's division by moving the variable instead

    Add 3x3x3x to both sides of 20−3x≤520 - 3x \le 520−3x≤5 instead of dividing by −3-3−3: 20≤5+3x20 \le 5 + 3x20≤5+3x. Subtract 555: 15≤3x15 \le 3x15≤3x. Divide by the positive number 333: 5≤x5 \le x5≤x, the same x≥5x \ge 5x≥5, reached with no flip at all.
