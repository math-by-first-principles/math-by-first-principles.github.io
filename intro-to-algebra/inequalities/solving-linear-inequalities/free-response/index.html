  1. Problem 1 Decimal coefficients on both sides

    Solve 1.2x−3.5<0.7x+11.2x-3.5<0.7x+11.2x−3.5<0.7x+1 over the real numbers.

    1. Hint 1

      Decimal coefficients need no new rule: the same inverse moves isolate the variable.

    2. Hint 2

      Gather the variable terms on the left, where the coefficient is larger, then divide.

    Answer

    x<9x<9x<9, the interval (−∞,9)(-\infty,9)(−∞,9).

    Full solution

    Subtracting 0.7x0.7x0.7x from both sides keeps the direction:

    0.5x−3.5<10.5x-3.5<10.5x−3.5<1

    Adding 3.5 to both sides gives

    0.5x<4.50.5x<4.50.5x<4.5

    Dividing by the positive number 0.5 leaves the symbol pointing the same way:

    x<9x<9x<9

    Clearing the decimals first is a second route: multiplying both sides by 10 gives 12x−35<7x+1012x-35<7x+1012x−35<7x+10, which leads to 5x<455x<455x<45 and the same bound.

    At x=9x=9x=9 both original sides equal 7.3, so the strict symbol leaves 9 out.

    At x=0x=0x=0 the comparison reads −3.5<1-3.5<1−3.5<1, true, while at x=10x=10x=10 it reads 8.5<88.5<88.5<8, false.

    Answer

    x<9x<9x<9, the interval (−∞,9)(-\infty,9)(−∞,9).

    Key idea

    Decimal coefficients follow the same inverse moves, and a positive divisor keeps the direction.

  2. Problem 2 An expression in parentheses

    Solve 8−3(2x−1)≥298-3(2x-1)\ge298−3(2x−1)≥29 over the real numbers.

    1. Hint 1

      Remove the parentheses first, taking care with the sign of each product.

    2. Hint 2

      Isolate the variable term, then watch what dividing by its negative coefficient does to the symbol.

    Answer

    x≤−3x\le-3x≤−3, the interval (−∞,−3](-\infty,-3](−∞,−3].

    Full solution

    Distributing −3-3−3 across the parentheses gives

    8−6x+3≥298-6x+3\ge298−6x+3≥29

    Combining the constants on the left gives

    11−6x≥2911-6x\ge2911−6x≥29

    Subtracting 11 from both sides keeps the direction:

    −6x≥18-6x\ge18−6x≥18

    Dividing both sides by the negative number −6-6−6 reverses the symbol:

    x≤−3x\le-3x≤−3

    The earlier subtraction reversed nothing; only the negative divisor does.

    At x=−3x=-3x=−3 the left side is 8−3(−7)=298-3(-7)=298−3(−7)=29, so the endpoint is included.

    At x=−4x=-4x=−4 it is 35, which is at least 29, while at x=0x=0x=0 it is 11, which is not.

    Answer

    x≤−3x\le-3x≤−3, the interval (−∞,−3](-\infty,-3](−∞,−3].

    Key idea

    Distribute first, and reverse the symbol only at a step that multiplies or divides both sides by a negative number.

  3. Problem 3 A fraction and a linear expression

    Solve x−22<2x+5\frac{x-2}{2}<2x+52x−2​<2x+5 over the real numbers.

    1. Hint 1

      Clear the positive denominator first.

    2. Hint 2

      The larger variable coefficient is on the right, so remove the left variable term.

    Answer

    x>−4x>-4x>−4.

    Full solution

    Multiply by 2, preserving the direction:

    x−2<4x+10x-2<4x+10x−2<4x+10

    Subtract xxx from both sides and then subtract 10:

    −12<3x-12<3x−12<3x

    Dividing by positive 3 gives −4<x-4<x−4<x, or

    x>−4x>-4x>−4

    At x=0x=0x=0, the original comparison is −1<5-1<5−1<5, true.

    At x=−5x=-5x=−5, it is −7/2<−5-7/2<-5−7/2<−5, false.

    No step scales by a negative number.

    Answer

    x>−4x>-4x>−4.

    Key idea

    Collecting on the side with the larger coefficient can leave a positive final divisor.

  4. Problem 4 Comparing two rental offers

    For a rental lasting hhh hours, offer A costs 8+3h8+3h8+3h dollars and offer B costs 14+2h14+2h14+2h dollars. Any real duration h≥0h\ge0h≥0 is allowed. For which durations does A cost no more than B? Give an interval.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the complete costs, including the starting charges.

    2. Hint 2

      Solve the cost comparison and combine it with the nonnegative duration restriction.

    Answer

    [0,6][0,6][0,6] hours.

    Full solution

    Offer A costs no more when

    8+3h≤14+2h8+3h\le14+2h8+3h≤14+2h

    Subtracting 2h2h2h and 8 gives

    h≤6h\le6h≤6

    Together with h≥0h\ge0h≥0, this gives the interval [0,6][0,6][0,6] hours.

    At 6 hours both offers cost 26 dollars.

    At 0 hours A costs 8 dollars and B costs 14 dollars; at 7 hours A costs 29 dollars and B costs 28 dollars, confirming the bound.

    Answer

    [0,6][0,6][0,6] hours.

    Key idea

    A cost comparison must be combined with the allowed domain of the duration.

  5. Problem 5 Sensor settings

    A sensor with a real setting x≥0x\ge0x≥0 reports r=5−2x3r=\frac{5-2x}{3}r=35−2x​. It accepts readings from −1-1−1 inclusive to 3 exclusive. Find all allowed settings that produce accepted readings, as an interval.

    1. Hint 1

      Put the reported expression between the two acceptance bounds.

    2. Hint 2

      Operate on all three parts, reversing both symbols when dividing by a negative, then apply x≥0x\ge0x≥0.

    Answer

    [0,4][0,4][0,4].

    Full solution

    The reading condition is

    −1≤5−2x3<3-1\le\frac{5-2x}{3}<3−1≤35−2x​<3

    Multiplying by 3 and subtracting 5 throughout gives

    −8≤−2x<4-8\le-2x<4−8≤−2x<4

    Dividing all parts by −2-2−2 reverses both symbols, giving

    4≥x>−24\ge x>-24≥x>−2

    Rewriting in increasing order gives −2<x≤4-2<x\le4−2<x≤4.

    With the setting restriction x≥0x\ge0x≥0, the answer is [0,4][0,4][0,4].

    Setting 4 gives reading −1-1−1, included; setting 0 gives 5/35/35/3, also accepted.

    Answer

    [0,4][0,4][0,4].

    Key idea

    A compound output requirement and an input restriction must both hold.

  6. Problem 6 Two entrance rules

    A real score sss is accepted when s−4≥2s-4\ge2s−4≥2 or 3s+1>103s+1>103s+1>10. Find the set of accepted scores, describe it as simply as you can, and justify that description.

    1. Hint 1

      An or condition keeps a score that satisfies either rule.

    2. Hint 2

      Solve each rule, then notice whether one accepted ray is contained in the other.

    Answer

    (3,∞)(3,\infty)(3,∞), that is s>3s>3s>3.

    Full solution

    The first rule gives s≥6s\ge6s≥6.

    The second gives

    3s>93s>93s>9 s>3s>3s>3

    Every score at least 6 is already greater than 3, so the union is just (3,∞)(3,\infty)(3,∞).

    Score 4 passes only the second rule and is accepted.

    Score 6 passes both.

    Score 3 passes neither, so the endpoint stays open.

    Answer

    (3,∞)(3,\infty)(3,∞), that is s>3s>3s>3.

    Key idea

    The union of nested rays is the larger ray.

  7. Problem 7 A parameter limit

    A positive real input xxx must satisfy 2(x+a)≤2x+62(x+a)\le2x+62(x+a)≤2x+6, where aaa is fixed. Determine which values of aaa permit an input, and give the permitted input set in each case.

    1. Hint 1

      Expand and compare the variable terms before attempting to isolate the input.

    2. Hint 2

      After the input cancels, the remaining comparison decides whether the positive-input condition is allowed or impossible.

    Answer

    If a≤3a\le3a≤3, inputs are (0,∞)(0,\infty)(0,∞); if a>3a>3a>3, there are no inputs.

    Full solution

    Expand and subtract 2x2x2x:

    2a≤62a\le62a≤6 a≤3a\le3a≤3

    This condition is independent of xxx.

    If it holds, every positive input satisfies the original comparison.

    If it fails, no input can change that failure.

    For example, at a=3a=3a=3 both original sides agree for every xxx, while a=4a=4a=4 makes the left side 2 larger for every xxx.

    The required positivity then gives precisely (0,∞)(0,\infty)(0,∞) in the first case.

    Answer

    If a≤3a\le3a≤3, inputs are (0,∞)(0,\infty)(0,∞); if a>3a>3a>3, there are no inputs.

    Key idea

    When the variable cancels, a parameter condition decides whether the remaining domain survives.

  8. Problem 8 A requested solution set

    Write one inequality of the form ax+b<cax+b<cax+b<c, where aaa is negative, whose solution set is x>−2x>-2x>−2. Show that your inequality has exactly this solution set.

    1. Hint 1

      Build backward from the desired solution using a negative scale.

    2. Hint 2

      After multiplying by a negative number, add a constant to both sides.

    Answer

    For example, −3x+1<7-3x+1<7−3x+1<7.

    Full solution

    Start with x>−2x>-2x>−2.

    Multiplying by −3-3−3 reverses the direction:

    −3x<6-3x<6−3x<6

    Adding 1 gives

    −3x+1<7-3x+1<7−3x+1<7

    Conversely, subtracting 1 and dividing by −3-3−3 in the constructed inequality returns x>−2x>-2x>−2.

    Thus no solutions were added or lost, and the coefficient of xxx is negative as required.

    Answer

    For example, −3x+1<7-3x+1<7−3x+1<7.

    Key idea

    Reversible inequality operations can construct a problem with a chosen solution set.

  9. Problem 9 Scaling by two negatives

    A student multiplies both sides of an inequality by −2-2−2 and then divides both sides by −5-5−5. The student says the final comparison symbol points the same way as the original. Is this correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Track the direction at each negative scaling step.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the two-step process with multiplication by their combined factor.

    Answer

    Yes; the direction reverses twice and returns to its original orientation.

    Full solution

    Multiplication by −2-2−2 reverses the comparison.

    Division by −5-5−5 reverses it again.

    The combined scale factor is

    −2−5=25\frac{-2}{-5}=\frac25−5−2​=52​

    This is positive, agreeing with preservation of the original direction.

    For example, 1<31<31<3 becomes −2>−6-2>-6−2>−6 and then 2/5<6/52/5<6/52/5<6/5.

    Inclusive comparisons behave the same way, with equality still included.

    Answer

    Yes; the direction reverses twice and returns to its original orientation.

    Key idea

    Two negative scale changes combine to a positive scale change.

  10. Problem 10 And versus or

    A student claims that replacing "and" by "or" between two different-looking linear inequalities must change the solution set. Is the claim correct? Justify your answer with a specific pair of inequalities.

    1. Hint 1

      Ask what each joining word demands of a number before deciding the two demands must differ.

    2. Hint 2

      Choose one inequality and produce another by a reversible operation that preserves its solutions.

    Answer

    No; for example, 2x+6>02x+6>02x+6>0 and x>−3x>-3x>−3 give (−3,∞)(-3,\infty)(−3,∞) with either joining word.

    Full solution

    Take 2x+6>02x+6>02x+6>0 and x>−3x>-3x>−3.

    The first simplifies as follows:

    2x>−62x>-62x>−6 x>−3x>-3x>−3

    Both inequalities therefore describe the same ray.

    Requiring both of two identical solution conditions, or requiring either one, gives exactly (−3,∞)(-3,\infty)(−3,∞).

    Thus different printed expressions do not ensure different solution sets, so the claim is not correct.

    Answer

    No; for example, 2x+6>02x+6>02x+6>0 and x>−3x>-3x>−3 give (−3,∞)(-3,\infty)(−3,∞) with either joining word.

    Key idea

    And and or give the same result when the two conditions have the same solution set.
