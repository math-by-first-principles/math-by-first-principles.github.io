  1. Problem 1 The marked point

    The figure shows a boundary line with neither side shaded, and a point P. Write a linear inequality whose graph includes the boundary and shades the side containing P.

    Text description of this figure

    A square coordinate grid. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative 2 to 5 and the vertical y-axis runs from negative 2 to 5, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number, equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. One unbroken straight line is drawn across the grid with an arrowhead at each end. It falls steeply from left to right, crossing the vertical axis four units above the origin and crossing the horizontal axis two units to the right of the origin. A single filled point, labeled with the letter P only, is plotted one unit left of the vertical axis and one unit below the horizontal axis. Neither side of the line is shaded, no coordinate pair is printed, and no equation is written on the line.

    1. Hint 1

      Read two points on the boundary to identify its equation.

    2. Hint 2

      Test the coordinates of P to choose the inclusive comparison direction.

    Answer

    y≤−2x+4y\le-2x+4y≤−2x+4, or 2x+y≤42x+y\le42x+y≤4, or any positive multiple of these.

    Full solution

    The boundary crosses the axes at (0,4)(0,4)(0,4) and (2,0)(2,0)(2,0), so it drops 2 units for every unit to the right and its equation is y=−2x+4y=-2x+4y=−2x+4.

    The marked point is P(−1,−1)P(-1,-1)P(−1,−1), and at x=−1x=-1x=−1 the boundary has height

    −2(−1)+4=6-2(-1)+4=6−2(−1)+4=6

    Since −1<6-1<6−1<6, P lies below the line.

    Shade that lower side and include the boundary, giving y≤−2x+4y\le-2x+4y≤−2x+4.

    The equivalent form 2x+y≤42x+y\le42x+y≤4 has the same boundary and accepts P, since 2(−1)+(−1)=−32(-1)+(-1)=-32(−1)+(−1)=−3, and −3≤4-3\le4−3≤4.

    Answer

    y≤−2x+4y\le-2x+4y≤−2x+4, or 2x+y≤42x+y\le42x+y≤4, or any positive multiple of these.

    Key idea

    A boundary equation, one point off the boundary, and whether the boundary is included fix a half-plane.

  2. Problem 2 Two coordinate terms

    Graph 2x−y<3−y2x-y<3-y2x−y<3−y on the blank coordinate grid in the figure.

    Text description of this figure

    A blank square coordinate grid. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative 2 to 5 and the vertical y-axis runs from negative 3 to 3, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number, equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. The plotting area is empty: no points, lines, regions or shading are drawn.

    1. Hint 1

      First check which coordinates remain restricted after simplifying.

    2. Hint 2

      Adding yyy to both sides keeps the direction and reveals a single-coordinate bound.

    Answer

    Dashed vertical boundary x=32x=\frac32x=23​, shaded to the left.

    Full solution

    Adding yyy gives

    2x<32x<32x<3

    Dividing by positive 2 gives

    x<32x<\frac32x<23​

    The second coordinate is free.

    Draw a dashed vertical line halfway between 1 and 2 and shade its left side.

    The origin gives 0<30<30<3, true.

    The point (2,0)(2,0)(2,0) gives 4<34<34<3, false, confirming the chosen side.

    Answer

    Dashed vertical boundary x=32x=\frac32x=23​, shaded to the left.

    Key idea

    A canceled coordinate is unrestricted, leaving a vertical or horizontal half-plane.

  3. Problem 3 A recorded location

    The point (2,4)(2,4)(2,4) lies on the boundary of ax+y≤6ax+y\le6ax+y≤6. Find aaa.

    1. Hint 1

      Points on a boundary satisfy the matching equation.

    2. Hint 2

      Insert the coordinates into ax+y=6ax+y=6ax+y=6.

    Answer

    a=1a=1a=1.

    Full solution

    Substitution gives

    2a+4=62a+4=62a+4=6

    Subtracting 4 from both sides and dividing by 2 gives

    a=1a=1a=1

    With this coefficient, the boundary is x+y=6x+y=6x+y=6, and 2+4=62+4=62+4=6 confirms the point lies on it.

    Answer

    a=1a=1a=1.

    Key idea

    A boundary point can determine a missing coefficient through the matching equality.

  4. Problem 4 A region request

    The feasible region satisfies x≥−3x\ge-3x≥−3, y≥0y\ge0y≥0, and x+3y≤6x+3y\le6x+3y≤6. Graph this region on the blank grid in the figure, and give the part of the vertical line x=3x=3x=3 that belongs to it.

    Text description of this figure

    A blank coordinate grid, wider than it is tall. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative 4 to 7 and the vertical y-axis runs from negative 1 to 4, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number, equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. The plotting area is empty: no points, lines, regions or shading are drawn.

    1. Hint 1

      Keep the overlap of the three permitted half-planes.

    2. Hint 2

      For the requested slice, insert x=3x=3x=3 into the remaining height conditions.

    Answer

    Closed triangle, all three edges solid, with corners (−3,0)(-3,0)(−3,0), (6,0)(6,0)(6,0), (−3,3)(-3,3)(−3,3); slice from (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) to (3,1)(3,1)(3,1), including both ends.

    Full solution

    The solid boundaries are x=−3x=-3x=−3, y=0y=0y=0, and x+3y=6x+3y=6x+3y=6.

    Keep points right of the first, above the second, and below the third.

    Their pairwise crossings that satisfy every condition are (−3,0)(-3,0)(−3,0), (6,0)(6,0)(6,0), and (−3,3)(-3,3)(−3,3).

    Shade this triangle including its edges.

    On x=3x=3x=3, the third inequality becomes

    3+3y≤63+3y\le63+3y≤6

    Subtracting 3 from both sides and dividing by 3 gives y≤1y\le1y≤1, and y≥0y\ge0y≥0 still applies.

    The slice is the segment from (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) to (3,1)(3,1)(3,1) with both endpoints included, each of which satisfies all three conditions.

    Answer

    Closed triangle, all three edges solid, with corners (−3,0)(-3,0)(−3,0), (6,0)(6,0)(6,0), (−3,3)(-3,3)(−3,3); slice from (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) to (3,1)(3,1)(3,1), including both ends.

    Key idea

    A vertical slice of a feasible region fixes the input and leaves a one-variable height range.

  5. Problem 5 Four posted rules

    Graph the feasible region satisfying x≥0x\ge0x≥0, y≥1y\ge1y≥1, x+y≤6x+y\le6x+y≤6, and y≥x−2y\ge x-2y≥x−2 on the blank grid in the figure. Give all its corners and state whether (3,2)(3,2)(3,2) belongs to it.

    Text description of this figure

    A blank square coordinate grid. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative 1 to 7 and the vertical y-axis runs from negative 1 to 7, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number, equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. The plotting area is empty: no points, lines, regions or shading are drawn.

    1. Hint 1

      Treat each inequality as a half-plane and keep their common portion.

    2. Hint 2

      Find boundary crossings, then reject any that fail one of the other conditions.

    Answer

    Corners (0,1)(0,1)(0,1), (0,6)(0,6)(0,6), (4,2)(4,2)(4,2), (3,1)(3,1)(3,1), all included; (3,2)(3,2)(3,2) belongs.

    Full solution

    All four boundaries are solid.

    The vertical boundary gives corners (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) and (0,6)(0,6)(0,6).

    The lower horizontal boundary meets y=x−2y=x-2y=x−2 at (3,1)(3,1)(3,1).

    The two slanted boundaries meet where

    x+(x−2)=6x+(x-2)=6x+(x−2)=6 x=4x=4x=4

    Then y=2y=2y=2, giving (4,2)(4,2)(4,2).

    Shade the quadrilateral inside these four edges.

    Each listed corner satisfies every constraint.

    At (3,2)(3,2)(3,2), the four checks are 3≥03\ge03≥0, 2≥12\ge12≥1, 5≤65\le65≤6, and 2≥3−22\ge3-22≥3−2, so the point belongs.

    Answer

    Corners (0,1)(0,1)(0,1), (0,6)(0,6)(0,6), (4,2)(4,2)(4,2), (3,1)(3,1)(3,1), all included; (3,2)(3,2)(3,2) belongs.

    Key idea

    A boundary crossing is a feasible corner only when it satisfies every remaining constraint.

  6. Problem 6 A screen filter

    A screen occupies 0≤x≤60\le x\le60≤x≤6 and 0≤y≤40\le y\le40≤y≤4. A filter hides points with x+2y≥8x+2y\ge8x+2y≥8. On the blank grid in the figure, shade the visible part of the screen, state which of the screen's four corners the filter hides, and write the system of inequalities that describes the visible part.

    Text description of this figure

    A blank coordinate grid, wider than it is tall. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative 1 to 7 and the vertical y-axis runs from negative 1 to 5, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number, equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. The plotting area is empty: no points, lines, regions or shading are drawn.

    1. Hint 1

      Visible points must be on the screen and fail the hiding condition.

    2. Hint 2

      The visible side leaves the filter boundary out, so that cut is dashed; find where it meets the screen edges.

    Answer

    The filter hides the screen corners (0,4)(0,4)(0,4) and (6,4)(6,4)(6,4). The visible part is 0≤x≤60\le x\le60≤x≤6, 0≤y≤40\le y\le40≤y≤4, and x+2y<8x+2y<8x+2y<8: the screen with a dashed cut from (0,4)(0,4)(0,4) to (6,1)(6,1)(6,1), both cut ends excluded.

    Full solution

    A point is visible when it is on the screen and fails x+2y≥8x+2y\ge8x+2y≥8, that is when

    x+2y<8x+2y<8x+2y<8

    The symbol is strict, so this cut is dashed and the points on it are hidden.

    The origin gives 0<80<80<8, so keep its side.

    The cut meets x=6x=6x=6 where y=1y=1y=1, and meets y=4y=4y=4 where x=0x=0x=0.

    Shade the quadrilateral with vertices (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), (6,0)(6,0)(6,0), (6,1)(6,1)(6,1), and (0,4)(0,4)(0,4), leaving the dashed edge from (0,4)(0,4)(0,4) to (6,1)(6,1)(6,1) out, so those two vertices are not visible themselves.

    Testing the four screen corners, (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) gives 0 and (6,0)(6,0)(6,0) gives 6, both under 8, while (6,4)(6,4)(6,4) gives 14 and (0,4)(0,4)(0,4) gives exactly 8.

    So the filter hides (6,4)(6,4)(6,4) and (0,4)(0,4)(0,4).

    Answer

    The filter hides the screen corners (0,4)(0,4)(0,4) and (6,4)(6,4)(6,4). The visible part is 0≤x≤60\le x\le60≤x≤6, 0≤y≤40\le y\le40≤y≤4, and x+2y<8x+2y<8x+2y<8: the screen with a dashed cut from (0,4)(0,4)(0,4) to (6,1)(6,1)(6,1), both cut ends excluded.

    Key idea

    Because the hiding rule is inclusive, the visible condition is strict, so its cut is dashed and a corner sitting exactly on that cut is not visible.

  7. Problem 7 A point relocation

    A point starts at S(3,0)S(3,0)S(3,0) and may move only upward. Find the shortest move that places it in the region 2x−y≤32x-y\le32x−y≤3 and y<7y<7y<7. State its new coordinates and check both conditions.

    1. Hint 1

      Keep the first coordinate fixed and find the allowed heights.

    2. Hint 2

      The nearest allowed height is the lowest one at or above the starting height.

    Answer

    Move up 3 units to (3,3)(3,3)(3,3).

    Full solution

    With x=3x=3x=3, the first condition becomes

    6−y≤36-y\le36−y≤3

    Adding yyy to both sides and then subtracting 3 gives

    y≥3y\ge3y≥3

    Combined with y<7y<7y<7, the allowed heights are 3≤y<73\le y<73≤y<7.

    The smallest upward move from height 0 reaches height 3, a move of 3 units.

    At (3,3)(3,3)(3,3) the first condition gives 2(3)−3=32(3)-3=32(3)−3=3, which sits exactly on the boundary 2x−y=32x-y=32x−y=3, and the inclusive symbol counts that boundary as part of the region; the second gives 3<73<73<7.

    A shorter upward move would leave y<3y<3y<3 and fail the first condition.

    Answer

    Move up 3 units to (3,3)(3,3)(3,3).

    Key idea

    A constrained point movement can be solved by finding the allowed coordinate interval.

  8. Problem 8 Opposite sides

    Two points lie strictly on opposite sides of one straight boundary. A student claims that both can satisfy the same single linear inequality with that boundary. Is this possible? Explain, distinguishing the boundary from its two sides.

    1. Hint 1

      The solution region of one linear inequality occupies one half-plane.

    2. Hint 2

      Including the boundary does not add the other half-plane.

    Answer

    No; one of the two points must fail the inequality.

    Full solution

    A single linear inequality selects one side of its boundary.

    For a nonvertical boundary, points above it and below it give opposite signs for their height difference from the line.

    A vertical boundary separates smaller and larger first coordinates in the same way.

    The two points lie strictly on different sides, so exactly one lies on the selected side.

    Changing between strict and inclusive symbols affects the boundary itself, not either of these off-boundary points.

    Answer

    No; one of the two points must fail the inequality.

    Key idea

    One linear inequality selects a single side of its boundary, whether or not the boundary is included.

  9. Problem 9 One more condition

    A feasible region is drawn for several inequalities. One more inequality is added while all the original requirements remain. A student says the new feasible region cannot contain a point outside the old one. Is this correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the two lists of demands a point has to meet.

    2. Hint 2

      Think of the new region as the common part of the old region and another half-plane.

    3. Hint 3

      A point in the new region must still meet each original condition.

    Answer

    Yes; every new feasible point was already in the old region.

    Full solution

    Any point satisfying the enlarged list satisfies every inequality in the original list.

    Therefore it lies in the original feasible region.

    The new condition can remove old points or leave the region unchanged, but it cannot make a previously failing original condition true.

    Thus no point outside the old region can enter the new one.

    Answer

    Yes; every new feasible point was already in the old region.

    Key idea

    Adding a constraint can reduce or preserve a feasible region, but cannot enlarge it.

  10. Problem 10 Two coordinate limits

    Let A be the region x≤2x\le2x≤2 and B the region y≤2y\le2y≤2. Does either region contain the other completely? Justify your answer with specific points.

    1. Hint 1

      Each region limits a different coordinate.

    2. Hint 2

      Keep one coordinate below its limit while placing the other above its limit.

    Answer

    Neither contains the other; for example, (0,3)(0,3)(0,3) is in A only and (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) is in B only.

    Full solution

    The point (0,3)(0,3)(0,3) satisfies 0≤20\le20≤2 but fails 3≤23\le23≤2, so it belongs to A and not B.

    The point (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) fails A but satisfies B.

    These two points disprove both possible containments.

    A is the half-plane on or left of a vertical boundary; B is the half-plane on or below a horizontal boundary.

    Answer

    Neither contains the other; for example, (0,3)(0,3)(0,3) is in A only and (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) is in B only.

    Key idea

    Bounds on different coordinates can overlap without either half-plane containing the other.
