    1. The boundary, the test point, and the check . Foundational, 9 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 9 pts

    Consider the inequality 2x+y>42x + y > 42x+y>4. This question builds its graph piece by piece, then reads the finished picture in the opposite direction.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Write the boundary equation of 2x+y>42x + y > 42x+y>4, give its slope and yyy-intercept, and state whether the boundary should be drawn dashed or solid, with a reason.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Using (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0) as a test point, confirm it does not sit on your boundary, substitute it into the ORIGINAL inequality, and state which side of the boundary ends up shaded.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Without substituting again, use the shading you found in part B to decide whether (3,−1)(3, -1)(3,−1) is a solution of 2x+y>42x + y > 42x+y>4, and say in one sentence what feature of the point told you.

      Carry your own answer forward Use whichever side you shaded in part B, even if it turns out to be the wrong one; credit here is for correctly relating (3,−1)(3, -1)(3,−1) to YOUR shaded side, not for matching the answer key.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 2 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 4

      A two-variable inequality always has a matching equation, its boundary; the strict-or-inclusive symbol fixes how to draw that boundary, and a single off-line test point settles which entire side is the answer.

    2. Hint 2 of 4 · Part A

    3. Hint 3 of 4 · Part B

    4. Hint 4 of 4 · Part C

      Find the boundary's y-value at x = 3 the way you would read a value off any line, then compare that number with -1 to see which side of the line the point falls on.

    The answer

    Part A

    y=−2x+4y = -2x + 4y=−2x+4: slope −2-2−2, yyy-intercept (0,4)(0, 4)(0,4). Dashed, because the inequality is strict.

    Part B

    (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0) is not on the boundary. Substituting gives 2(0)+0>42(0) + 0 > 42(0)+0>4, that is 0>40 > 40>4, which is false, so the shaded side is the one that does NOT contain the origin.

    Part C

    (3,−1)(3, -1)(3,−1) IS a solution: at x=3x = 3x=3 the boundary sits at y=−2y = -2y=−2, and −1-1−1 is above that, so the point lies on the same side as the shaded region found in part B.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Replace the inequality symbol with an equals sign to get the boundary equation, then solve it for yyy.

    2x+y=4  ⟹  y=−2x+4.2x + y = 4 \;\Longrightarrow\; y = -2x + 4.2x+y=4⟹y=−2x+4.

    Reading the slope-intercept form off directly gives slope −2-2−2 and yyy-intercept (0,4)(0, 4)(0,4). The original symbol is the strict >>>, so points ON this line are not solutions, which means the boundary is drawn dashed.

    Part B

    Check first that the test point is legal: the boundary y=−2x+4y = -2x + 4y=−2x+4 passes through (0,4)(0, 4)(0,4), not through (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0), so the origin is a valid choice.

    Substitute it into the ORIGINAL inequality, not the boundary equation:

    which is false. A false result means the origin is not a solution, so the entire side it sits on is excluded, and the shaded side is the other one.

    Part C

    The boundary's height at x=3x = 3x=3 comes from its equation:

    y=−2(3)+4=−2.y = -2(3) + 4 = -2.y=−2(3)+4=−2.

    The point (3,−1)(3, -1)(3,−1) has y=−1y = -1y=−1, which is greater than −2-2−2, so (3,−1)(3, -1)(3,−1) sits above the boundary at that xxx-value.     4. Four lines about a system, and where it breaks . Application, 12 points. Question 4 of 5.

    Application 12 pts

    Below are four lines that work through the system 2x+y≤62x + y \le 62x+y≤6 and y>x−1y > x - 1y>x−1. Exactly one of the four lines is not fully justified; the other three are correct as written.

    Line 1: The boundary of 2x+y≤62x + y \le 62x+y≤6 is 2x+y=62x + y = 62x+y=6; since the symbol is ≤\le≤, this boundary is drawn solid.

    Line 2: Testing the origin in 2x+y≤62x + y \le 62x+y≤6 gives 2(0)+0≤62(0) + 0 \le 62(0)+0≤6, that is 0≤60 \le 60≤6, which is true, so the shaded side of this boundary is the one containing the origin.

    Line 3: The boundary of y>x−1y > x - 1y>x−1 is y=x−1y = x - 1y=x−1; since the symbol is >>>, this boundary is drawn dashed. Testing the origin gives 0>0−10 > 0 - 10>0−1, that is 0>−10 > -10>−1, which is true, so the shaded side of this boundary is also the one containing the origin.

    Line 4: Since both individual shaded regions contain the origin, the solution of the system is the union of the two shaded half-planes: every point that satisfies at least one of the two inequalities.

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 4

    2. Hint 2 of 4 · Part A

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A
