    Part A

    Point-slope form is y−y1=m(x−x1)y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)y−y1​=m(x−x1​), so write the template down first and only then decide what each letter is. Here the point supplies x1=6x_1 = 6x1​=6 and y1=−4y_1 = -4y1​=−4, and the slope supplies m=−12m = -\tfrac{1}{2}m=−21​. Substituting all three at once:

    Part B

    Look at the two coordinate pairs before reaching for any formula. They differ in their second coordinate but agree in their first, so both points sit in the same vertical column and the line through them runs straight up and down.

    The slope formula would ask for the difference of the two first coordinates on the bottom, and that difference is −5−(−5)=0-5 - (-5) = 0−5−(−5)=0, so there is no slope to compute and nothing for the mmm in point-slope form to receive.     x=−5.x = -5.x=−5.

    Both given points satisfy it, since each has first coordinate −5-5−5, and no point outside that column does.

    Part C

    Read the template as a list of what it needs: y−y1=m(x−x1)y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)y−y1​=m(x−x1​) asks for a point, which every line has plenty of, and for a number mmm, which not every line owns.

    A line that is not vertical has a slope, so both ingredients exist and the form can be written down. Running that the other way, any equation of the shape y−y1=m(x−x1)y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)y−y1​=m(x−x1​) describes a line of slope mmm, and a line with a slope is not vertical. So the lines point-slope form can express are exactly the non-vertical ones, with nothing left over on either side.

    A vertical line is the single exception. Its slope is undefined, not zero, so there is no number to put in the mmm position at all, and the form never gets started. Such a line is written directly as x=cx = cx=c instead, as in part B.

    A slope of 000 is a different situation entirely, and the form handles it without complaint. Substituting m=0m = 0m=0 makes the whole right side vanish:

    y−y1=0(x−x1),y−y1=0,y=y1.y - y_1 = 0(x - x_1), \qquad y - y_1 = 0, \qquad y = y_1.y−y1​=0(x−x1​),y−y1​=0,y=y1​.

    That is the horizontal line through the point, whose second coordinate never changes. So zero slope is a case the form covers, and undefined slope is a case it cannot reach; the two are opposite ends of the situation, not the same one.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Decide first which quantity plays the horizontal role. Time is what is being read off the clock, so let ttt run horizontally and VVV vertically. Each reading then becomes an ordered pair: at 333 minutes, (3,26)(3, 26)(3,26); at 888 minutes, (8,46)(8, 46)(8,46).

    Worked solution

    Part A

    An equation of a line is the rule its points obey: a pair (x,y)(x, y)(x,y) satisfies the equation exactly when the point sits on the line. So any point the line is required to pass through must satisfy any correct equation for it, and that gives a test costing one substitution and no rederivation.

    The line is required to pass through (−3,4)(-3, 4)(−3,4). Substituting:

    2(−3)−3(4)=−6−12=−18.2(-3) - 3(4) = -6 - 12 = -18.2(−3)−3(4)=−6−12=−18.

    The right-hand side is −6-6−6, and −18≠−6-18 \neq -6−18=−6, so the pair (−3,4)(-3, 4)(−3,4) does not satisfy Rowan's equation. That means the point does not lie on the line Rowan's equation describes, and the line described was required to pass through it. One failure is enough, because a correct equation would have to pass every such test, not most of them.

    Notice what this test does not tell you: it says the answer is wrong without saying where it went wrong. That is the next part's job.

    Part B

    The second coordinate is positive, so the left side keeps its minus sign. To reach standard form, clear the fraction by multiplying both sides by 333, then distribute:

    3y−12=2(x+3)=2x+6.3y - 12 = 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6.3y−12=2(x+3)=2x+6.
