  1. Problem 1 Three directions

    Line A has slope −52-\frac52−25​. Line B is perpendicular to A, and line C is perpendicular to B. Find the slope of C.

    1. Hint 1

      Each right-angle change requires the negative reciprocal.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the slope of B before applying the relationship to C.

    Answer

    −52-\frac52−25​.

    Full solution

    The slope of B is 2/52/52/5, since

    −52⋅25=−1-\frac52\cdot\frac25=-1−25​⋅52​=−1

    Taking its negative reciprocal gives

    mC=−52m_C=-\frac52mC​=−25​

    Checking 25(−52)=−1\frac25(-\frac52)=-152​(−25​)=−1 confirms the second right angle.

    Answer

    −52-\frac52−25​.

    Key idea

    Applying the negative reciprocal twice returns the original nonzero slope.

  2. Problem 2 Footpath across a road

    A park map is drawn on a coordinate grid measured in meters, with east as the positive xxx direction and north as the positive yyy direction. A straight road on the map has slope −34-\frac34−43​, and a straight footpath crosses the road at a right angle. Walking along the footpath, Maya moves 6 m north while also moving east. How far east does she move?

    1. Hint 1

      The footpath's direction on the map is fixed by its right angle with the road.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the footpath's slope, then treat the 6 m north as a rise and solve for the run east.

    Answer

    92\frac9229​ m, or 4.5 m.

    Full solution

    The footpath is perpendicular to the road, so its slope is the negative reciprocal of −34-\frac34−43​, which is 43\frac4334​.

    On the map, a move north is a rise and a move east is a run.

    If the run is rrr meters, then

    6r=43\frac6r=\frac43r6​=34​

    The run is positive, so multiplying both sides by 3r3r3r is valid:

    18=4r18=4r18=4r r=92r=\frac92r=29​

    Thus Maya moves 92\frac9229​ m, or 4.5 m, east.

    Her move has slope 6/(9/2)=4/36/(9/2)=4/36/(9/2)=4/3, the footpath's slope, as required.

    Answer

    92\frac9229​ m, or 4.5 m.

    Key idea

    A known perpendicular slope fixes the ratio of rise to run, so one known leg of a move determines the other.

    Full solution

    Take c=0c=0c=0, giving

    y=−2xy=-2xy=−2x

    All three slopes are −2-2−2, while the intercept heights are 000, 111, and −4-4−4.

    Therefore the new line is distinct from, and parallel to, both given lines.

    Any real ccc other than 1 and −4-4−4 also works.

    Answer

    Any y=−2x+cy=-2x+cy=−2x+c with c≠1c\ne1c=1 and c≠−4c\ne-4c=−4; for example, y=−2xy=-2xy=−2x.

  4. Problem 4 A parallel through CCC

    The figure shows segments ABABAB and BCBCBC. Write the equation of the line through CCC parallel to ABABAB, in point-slope form using CCC. Determine whether this new line is perpendicular to BCBCBC.

    Text description of this figure

    A square coordinate grid with equal unit lengths on both axes. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative three to four and the vertical y-axis from negative two to five, with gridlines, tick marks and number labels at every whole number, arrowheads on both axes, and the origin labeled 0.     1. Hint 1

      Use the two endpoints of each segment to find its direction.

    y−3=12(x−1)y-3=\frac12(x-1)y−3=21​(x−1); yes, it is perpendicular to BCBCBC.

    Full solution

    The points read from the grid are A(−2,−1)A(-2,-1)A(−2,−1), B(2,1)B(2,1)B(2,1), and C(1,3)C(1,3)C(1,3).

    The slope of ABABAB is

    mAB=1−(−1)2−(−2)m_{AB}=\frac{1-(-1)}{2-(-2)}mAB​=2−(−2)1−(−1)​ mAB=12m_{AB}=\frac12mAB​=21​

    Using CCC gives

    y−3=12(x−1)y-3=\frac12(x-1)y−3=21​(x−1)

    The slope of BCBCBC is

    (3−1)/(1−2)=−2.(3-1)/(1-2)=-2.(3−1)/(1−2)=−2.

    Since 12(−2)=−1\frac12(-2)=-121​(−2)=−1, the new line is perpendicular to BCBCBC.

    The new equation holds at CCC, and its intercept 5/25/25/2 differs from the intercept 0 of ABABAB.

    Answer

    y−3=12(x−1)y-3=\frac12(x-1)y−3=21​(x−1); yes, it is perpendicular to BCBCBC.

    A new line can inherit one segment direction while meeting another at a right angle.

  5. Problem 5 Shared meeting point

    Lines 2x−y=−12x-y=-12x−y=−1 and x+y=7x+y=7x+y=7 meet at PPP. Write the equation of the line through PPP perpendicular to the first line, in slope-intercept form.

    1. Hint 1

      The meeting point supplies the location required for the new line.

    2. Hint 2

      Solve each equation for yyy, so that each slope can be read and the two heights can be set equal.

    3. Hint 3

      Find the common point, then combine it with the negative reciprocal of the first slope.

    Answer

    y=−12x+6y=-\frac12x+6y=−21​x+6.

    Full solution

    Subtracting 2x2x2x from both sides of 2x−y=−12x-y=-12x−y=−1 and then multiplying by −1-1−1 gives

    y=2x+1y=2x+1y=2x+1

    Subtracting xxx from both sides of x+y=7x+y=7x+y=7 gives

    y=−x+7y=-x+7y=−x+7

    At the meeting point the two heights agree, so

    2x+1=−x+72x+1=-x+72x+1=−x+7

    Adding xxx and subtracting 1 gives

    3x=63x=63x=6 x=2x=2x=2

    Either line then gives y=5y=5y=5, so P=(2,5)P=(2,5)P=(2,5).

    The first line has slope 2, so the new slope is −1/2-1/2−1/2.

    Point-slope form gives

    y−5=−12(x−2)y-5=-\frac12(x-2)y−5=−21​(x−2) y=−12x+6y=-\frac12x+6y=−21​x+6

    Substitution at x=2x=2x=2 gives 5, and the slopes multiply to −1-1−1.

    Answer

    y=−12x+6y=-\frac12x+6y=−21​x+6.

    Key idea

    An intersection can supply the point needed to construct a line with a required direction.

  6. Problem 6 Three full lines

    The figure shows full lines A, B, and C. Classify each pair as parallel, perpendicular, or neither, and for each pair say whether the test m1⋅m2=−1m_1\cdot m_2=-1m1​⋅m2​=−1 can be applied.

    Text description of this figure

    A square coordinate grid with equal unit lengths on both axes. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative five to six and the vertical y-axis from negative three to five, with gridlines, tick marks and number labels at every whole number, arrowheads on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. Three full lines are drawn, each with arrowheads at both ends and labeled with its letter only. Line A is horizontal, two units above the x-axis, through y equals two. Line B is vertical, three units left of the y-axis, through x equals negative three. Line C slants up to the right, rising one unit for each unit to the right, and passes through the grid points negative four, negative three; zero, one; and four, five. No points are marked, and no coordinates, equations or angle marks are shown.

    1. Hint 1

      Decide first which of the three lines has a slope number and which does not.

    2. Hint 2

      Read each nonvertical line's slope from two grid points on it; a vertical line has no slope number.

    3. Hint 3

      Where both slopes exist, compare the slopes and their product; where a line is vertical, decide by sight.

    Answer

    A and B: perpendicular, and the test cannot be applied; A and C: neither, and the test can be applied; B and C: neither, and the test cannot be applied.

    Full solution

    A is the horizontal line y=2y=2y=2, with slope 0.

    B is the vertical line x=−3x=-3x=−3, whose slope is undefined.

    C passes through (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) and (1,2)(1,2)(1,2), so it rises 1 for each 1 across and has slope 1.

    A and B are a horizontal line and a vertical line, so they are perpendicular by sight.

    The test cannot be applied to them, because B has no slope number.

    A and C both have real slopes, so the test applies.

    The slopes 0 and 1 are not equal, so the lines are not parallel, and their product is

    0⋅1=00\cdot1=00⋅1=0

    This is not −1-1−1, so A and C are neither parallel nor perpendicular.

    B is vertical and C is slanted, so the test cannot be applied to them.

    A vertical line and a slanted line cross, but not at a right angle, so B and C are neither.

    Answer

    A and B: perpendicular, and the test cannot be applied; A and C: neither, and the test can be applied; B and C: neither, and the test cannot be applied.

    Key idea

    When both lines have slopes, compare the slopes and their product; when one line is vertical, classify the pair by sight.

  7. Problem 7 Two unknown slopes

    The lines y=mx+4y=mx+4y=mx+4 and y=nx−2y=nx-2y=nx−2 both contain (2,1)(2,1)(2,1). Find mmm and nnn, then classify the lines as parallel, perpendicular, or neither.

    1. Hint 1

      The shared point fixes each missing slope separately.

    2. Hint 2

      After substituting, compare the slopes and their product.

    Answer

    m=−32m=-\frac32m=−23​ and n=32n=\frac32n=23​; neither.

    Full solution

    The first line gives 1=2m+41=2m+41=2m+4, so

    m=−32m=-\frac32m=−23​

    The second gives 1=2n−21=2n-21=2n−2, so

    n=32n=\frac32n=23​

    The slopes differ and their product is

    −32⋅32=−94-\frac32\cdot\frac32=-\frac94−23​⋅23​=−49​

    This is not −1-1−1, so the lines are neither parallel nor perpendicular.

    Substitution of both values returns height 1 at input 2.

    Answer

    m=−32m=-\frac32m=−23​ and n=32n=\frac32n=23​; neither.

    Key idea

    Two slopes that differ only in sign give perpendicular lines only when they are 111 and −1-1−1; otherwise the lines are neither parallel nor perpendicular.

  8. Problem 8 Repeated height gap

    Two nonvertical lines, A and B, have heights that differ by 5 units at x=−1x=-1x=−1, with A above B. Their heights again differ by 5 units at x=4x=4x=4, with A above B. Must A and B be parallel? Justify your answer.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare each line's rise over the same horizontal interval.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtracting heights whose differences agree makes the two rises agree.

    Answer

    Yes, A and B must be parallel.

    Full solution

    Let B have heights uuu and vvv at the two inputs.

    A then has heights u+5u+5u+5 and v+5v+5v+5.

    Both runs are 5, which is nonzero.

    B has slope (v−u)/5(v-u)/5(v−u)/5, and A has slope

    (v+5)−(u+5)5\frac{(v+5)-(u+5)}55(v+5)−(u+5)​

    This simplifies to the same value (v−u)/5(v-u)/5(v−u)/5.

    The lines have equal slopes and are distinct, since their heights differ at either input.

    Therefore they are parallel.

    Answer

    Yes, A and B must be parallel.

    Key idea

    When one nonvertical line stays the same height above another at two distinct inputs, the two lines have equal slopes.

    y=−13x+73y=-\frac13x+\frac73y=−31​x+37​

    A point and a slope determine one line, so the two equations describe the same line.

    Equal slopes alone do not establish distinctness.

    Here the common point makes their intercepts equal as well.

    Answer

    No; both equations describe the same line, y−1=−13(x−4)y-1=-\frac13(x-4)y−1=−31​(x−4), or y=−13x+73y=-\frac13x+\frac73y=−31​x+37​.

  10. Problem 10 Swapped coordinates

    Line A passes through (−2,1)(-2,1)(−2,1) and (3,5)(3,5)(3,5). Line B is drawn through the points formed by swapping the two coordinates of each of these points. A student claims A and B are perpendicular. Decide whether the claim is correct and explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Write the swapped points explicitly before calculating their slope.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the product of the two slopes with the condition for a right angle.

    Answer

    No; the slopes are 45\frac4554​ and 54\frac5445​.

    Full solution

    The swapped points are (1,−2)(1,-2)(1,−2) and (5,3)(5,3)(5,3).

    The original slope is

    mA=5−13−(−2)m_A=\frac{5-1}{3-(-2)}mA​=3−(−2)5−1​ mA=45m_A=\frac45mA​=54​

    The new slope is

    mB=3−(−2)5−1m_B=\frac{3-(-2)}{5-1}mB​=5−13−(−2)​ mB=54m_B=\frac54mB​=45​

    The product of the slopes is

    45⋅54=1\frac45\cdot\frac54=154​⋅45​=1

    This is 1, not −1-1−1, so A and B are not perpendicular.

    Swapping the coordinates exchanged rise and run without supplying the sign change required for perpendicular slopes.

    Answer

    No; the slopes are 45\frac4554​ and 54\frac5445​.

    Key idea

    For a line with nonzero slope, exchanging rise and run gives the reciprocal slope, while two slanted lines are perpendicular only when their slopes are negative reciprocals.
