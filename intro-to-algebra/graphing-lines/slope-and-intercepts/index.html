Slope and Intercepts

Intuition

You can already write a line’s equation from a point and its slope, and you can graph a line by plotting a couple of points. This lesson introduces the single most useful way to write a line’s equation, slope-intercept form y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b, which keeps the slope and one key point in plain sight. Read off mmm and you know the tilt. Read off bbb and you know exactly where the line crosses the yyy-axis. From those two numbers you can graph the line in seconds. Running the process backward, you can rewrite almost any linear equation in this form to expose its slope and intercept at a glance.

Learning goals

  • Read mmm and bbb straight off y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b
  • Derive the form from point-slope using (0,b)(0, b)(0,b)
  • Graph by plotting the intercept, then stepping by the slope
  • Convert a linear equation by solving for yyy
  • Find the xxx-intercept by setting y=0y = 0y=0
  • Handle the horizontal, vertical and through-the-origin special cases

From point-slope to slope-intercept form

Point-slope form asks for a point and a slope, and any point on the line will serve. Some points, though, are more convenient than others. The most convenient point of all is the one where the line crosses the yyy-axis, because its xxx-coordinate is 000. That zero coordinate makes the algebra collapse to almost nothing. Building point-slope form on that point produces the cleanest equation of a line there is.

That crossing point has a name. The y-intercept is the point where the line crosses the yyy-axis. Every point on the yyy-axis has x=0x = 0x=0, so the yyy-intercept has the form (0,b)(0, b)(0,b) for some number bbb. That number bbb is the height at which the line meets the axis.

Deriving slope-intercept form from point-slope form#

Take a line with slope 444 that crosses the yyy-axis at (0,3)(0, 3)(0,3). Point-slope form with that point and slope reads y−3=4(x−0)y - 3 = 4(x - 0)y−3=4(x−0). The x−0x - 0x−0 collapses to xxx, leaving y−3=4xy - 3 = 4xy−3=4x, and adding 333 to both sides gives y=4x+3y = 4x + 3y=4x+3. The slope 444 and the crossing height 333 both show up right where you would expect. The same three steps work for any slope mmm and any crossing point (0,b)(0, b)(0,b), so repeat them with letters instead of numbers.

Start with a line whose slope is mmm and which crosses the yyy-axis at the point (0,b)(0, b)(0,b). That crossing point is an ordinary point on the line, so point-slope form applies to it. Substitute the point (x1,y1)=(0,b)(x_1, y_1) = (0, b)(x1​,y1​)=(0,b) and the slope mmm into y−y1=m(x−x1)y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)y−y1​=m(x−x1​):

y−b=m(x−0).y - b = m(x - 0).y−b=m(x−0).

The run x−0x - 0x−0 is just xxx, and that is the whole trick: the zero coordinate erases the parenthesis and leaves

y−b=mx.y - b = mx.y−b=mx.

Add bbb to both sides to get yyy alone:

y=mx+b.y = mx + b.y=mx+b.

This is slope-intercept form. Reading it back, mmm is the slope of the line and bbb is the yyy-coordinate of the point where the line meets the yyy-axis. Both facts are guaranteed by the way the equation was built, but they are worth confirming straight from y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b. Setting x=0x = 0x=0 gives y=by = by=b, so the point (0,b)(0, b)(0,b) really does lie on the line, which makes bbb the yyy-intercept. And taking any other point (x, mx+b)(x,\ mx + b)(x, mx+b) on the line, the slope between it and (0,b)(0, b)(0,b) is

(mx+b)−bx−0=mxx=m,\frac{(mx + b) - b}{x - 0} = \frac{mx}{x} = m,x−0(mx+b)−b​=xmx​=m,

so the line really does have slope mmm. The single equation y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b carries the line’s slope and its yyy-intercept on its face.

∎

Reading the slope and the intercept

Once an equation is written as y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b, the slope and the intercept require no work at all. In that form the slope mmm is the number multiplying xxx, and bbb is the constant, which fixes the yyy-intercept at (0,b)(0, b)(0,b). The only care needed is to read each number with its own sign, and to make sure the equation is actually in this form before you read it.

Worked example 1 Read the slope and yyy-intercept from an equation

Each line below is written as y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b (or can be with one small step), so match the pieces to mmm and bbb.

EquationSlope mmmyyy-intercept (0,b)(0, b)(0,b)
y=3x−4y = 3x - 4y=3x−4333(0,−4)(0, -4)(0,−4)
y=−2x+5y = -2x + 5y=−2x+5−2-2−2(0,5)(0, 5)(0,5)
y=5−2xy = 5 - 2xy=5−2x−2-2−2(0,5)(0, 5)(0,5)
y=23xy = \tfrac{2}{3}xy=32​x23\tfrac{2}{3}32​(0,0)(0, 0)(0,0)
y=7y = 7y=7000(0,7)(0, 7)(0,7)

Several of these rows hide a small trap. In y=3x−4y = 3x - 4y=3x−4 the constant is −4-4−4, so the intercept is (0,−4)(0, -4)(0,−4), not (0,4)(0, 4)(0,4): carry the minus sign. The line y=5−2xy = 5 - 2xy=5−2x lists its terms out of order, and the slope is the coefficient of xxx, which is −2-2−2, not the leading 555. Rewriting it as y=−2x+5y = -2x + 5y=−2x+5 puts the slope back where you expect it. In y=23xy = \tfrac{2}{3}xy=32​x there is no visible constant, so b=0b = 0b=0 and the line passes through the origin. And y=7y = 7y=7 can be read as y=0x+7y = 0x + 7y=0x+7, a slope of 000 with intercept (0,7)(0, 7)(0,7).

Writing an equation is the same reading in reverse. If you are told a line’s slope and its yyy-intercept, you have mmm and bbb already, so drop them into y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b and you are done.

That reversal is what the figure below is for. Its three controls are mmm and bbb themselves, with the slope split into the rise and the run that make it. The figure’s readout writes the equation back out for whatever you set. So you can work the lesson in either direction: choose the numbers and read the equation, or pick an equation and hunt for the numbers that produce it.

Start at a rise of 333 over a run of 444 with the crossing at −2-2−2, and the readout says y=(3/4)x−2y = (3/4)x - 2y=(3/4)x−2. Now step the crossing up to 000: the constant disappears from the equation, because a line through the origin has b=0b = 0b=0. The equation then reads y=(3/4)xy = (3/4)xy=(3/4)x with nothing added. Take the rise down to 000 next. The xxx term vanishes instead and the readout reads y=0y = 0y=0, a flat line along the horizontal axis. That is the same shape as the table’s y=7y = 7y=7 row, a slope of 000 with the constant left standing. But this figure’s crossing point stops at 666, so the constant it can reach is not 777 itself. Finally set the rise negative and watch the minus sign move into the coefficient rather than the constant. Those are the three places a sign or a missing term can hide.

Worked example 2 Write y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b from a slope and a yyy-intercept

Suppose a line has slope −4-4−4 and crosses the yyy-axis at (0,3)(0, 3)(0,3). Here m=−4m = -4m=−4 and b=3b = 3b=3, so substitute directly:

y=−4x+3.y = -4x + 3.y=−4x+3.

Now suppose a graph shows a line crossing the yyy-axis at (0,−2)(0, -2)(0,−2) and climbing with slope 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​. Read the two numbers off the picture, b=−2b = -2b=−2 and m=12m = \tfrac{1}{2}m=21​, and write

y=12x−2.y = \tfrac{1}{2}x - 2.y=21​x−2.

No point-slope step is needed, because the yyy-intercept is the one point the form is built to use.

Check your understanding

Written as y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b, what are the slope and yyy-intercept of the line y=5−2xy = 5 - 2xy=5−2x?

Answer choices

Slope-intercept form lists the xxx-term first, so reorder the equation before reading it.

y=5−2x=−2x+5.y = 5 - 2x = -2x + 5.y=5−2x=−2x+5.

Now the slope is the coefficient of xxx, which is −2-2−2, and the constant is 555, so the yyy-intercept is (0,5)(0, 5)(0,5). The trap is to read the leading 555 as the slope; it is the intercept.

Graphing from slope-intercept form

Slope-intercept form is built for fast graphing, because the two numbers it hands you are exactly the two things you need to draw a line. Those two things are a point to start from and a direction to head in. The recipe is short. Plot the yyy-intercept (0,b)(0, b)(0,b) first, since it sits right on the yyy-axis and takes no calculation. Then read the slope as rise over run and step from that point to a second one. That step means moving across by the run and up or down by the rise. Two points fix a line, so lay a ruler across them and draw.

Worked example 3 Graph y=12x+1y = \tfrac{1}{2}x + 1y=21​x+1 from slope-intercept form

Match the equation to y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b: the slope is m=12m = \tfrac{1}{2}m=21​ and the intercept is b=1b = 1b=1. Plot the yyy-intercept (0,1)(0, 1)(0,1) on the yyy-axis. Then read the slope 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​ as a run of 222 and a rise of 111, and step from (0,1)(0, 1)(0,1) two units right and one unit up:

(0,1)  ⟶  (0+2, 1+1)=(2,2).(0, 1) \;\longrightarrow\; (0 + 2,\ 1 + 1) = (2, 2).(0,1)⟶(0+2, 1+1)=(2,2).

Plot (2,2)(2, 2)(2,2), then draw the straight line through the two points and extend it both ways. That line is the graph of y=12x+1y = \tfrac{1}{2}x + 1y=21​x+1, exactly the figure above. If you want a check, step once more, right 222 and up 111 from (2,2)(2, 2)(2,2) to reach (4,3)(4, 3)(4,3), and confirm it too lands on the line.

Check your understanding

Starting from the yyy-intercept (0,2)(0, 2)(0,2), which point do you reach by stepping the slope −34-\tfrac{3}{4}−43​ of the line y=−34x+2y = -\tfrac{3}{4}x + 2y=−43​x+2?

Answer choices

Read the slope −34-\tfrac{3}{4}−43​ as a run of 444 and a rise of −3-3−3, a step right 444 and down 333.

(0,2)  ⟶  (0+4, 2−3)=(4,−1).(0, 2) \;\longrightarrow\; (0 + 4,\ 2 - 3) = (4, -1).(0,2)⟶(0+4, 2−3)=(4,−1).

The minus sign moves with the rise, so the step goes down instead of up; the run itself stays positive.

Converting to slope-intercept form

Not every equation arrives in slope-intercept form. Standard form Ax+By=CAx + By = CAx+By=C and point-slope form y−y1=m(x−x1)y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)y−y1​=m(x−x1​) both hide the slope and intercept until you rearrange them. Getting to y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b is always the same goal: solve the equation for yyy, so that yyy stands alone on one side with a coefficient of 111. Then the slope and intercept read straight off.

Worked example 4 Convert 2x+3y=122x + 3y = 122x+3y=12 to slope-intercept form

Slope-intercept form has yyy by itself, so solve the equation for yyy. First subtract 2x2x2x from both sides to isolate the yyy-term:

3y=−2x+12.3y = -2x + 12.3y=−2x+12.

The coefficient of yyy is 333, so divide every term by 333:

y=−23x+4.y = -\tfrac{2}{3}x + 4.y=−32​x+4.

Now the slope and intercept are in plain sight: the slope is m=−23m = -\tfrac{2}{3}m=−32​ and the yyy-intercept is (0,4)(0, 4)(0,4). As a check, the original equation at x=0x = 0x=0 gives 3y=123y = 123y=12, so y=4y = 4y=4, matching bbb.

A point-slope equation converts the same way, by distributing the slope and then moving the constant across. Take y−3=2(x−1)y - 3 = 2(x - 1)y−3=2(x−1). Distribute the 222 on the right, then add 333 to both sides:

y−3=2x−2,y=2x+1.y - 3 = 2x - 2, \qquad y = 2x + 1.y−3=2x−2,y=2x+1.

The line has slope 222 and yyy-intercept (0,1)(0, 1)(0,1). Both forms describe one line; slope-intercept form just displays the slope and intercept that point-slope form kept folded up.

Check your understanding

Rewrite 4x+2y=104x + 2y = 104x+2y=10 in slope-intercept form.

Answer choices

Solve for yyy. Subtract 4x4x4x from both sides, then divide every term by 222.

2y=−4x+10,y=−2x+5.2y = -4x + 10, \qquad y = -2x + 5.2y=−4x+10,y=−2x+5.

The division by 222 must reach every term, turning −4x-4x−4x into −2x-2x−2x and 101010 into 555. Stopping at 2y=−4x+102y = -4x + 102y=−4x+10 leaves the line only half solved.

Finding the intercepts

The lesson is named for slope and intercepts, and slope-intercept form already hands you one of the two intercepts for free. The yyy-intercept is (0,b)(0, b)(0,b), read straight from the constant, or found by setting x=0x = 0x=0 in any form of the equation. The other crossing, the x-intercept, is the point where the line meets the xxx-axis. Every point on the xxx-axis has y=0y = 0y=0, so to find it you set y=0y = 0y=0 and solve for xxx. The two rules are mirror images: zero the coordinate of the axis you are crossing, which is always the opposite letter from the one you keep.

Worked example 5 Find both intercepts of a line

Start with y=2x−6y = 2x - 6y=2x−6, already in slope-intercept form. The yyy-intercept is the constant, so it is (0,−6)(0, -6)(0,−6). For the xxx-intercept, set y=0y = 0y=0 and solve for xxx:

0=2x−6,2x=6,x=3,0 = 2x - 6, \qquad 2x = 6, \qquad x = 3,0=2x−6,2x=6,x=3,

so the xxx-intercept is (3,0)(3, 0)(3,0).

Now take 4x−5y=204x - 5y = 204x−5y=20, which is in standard form. The intercepts are just as quick without first solving for yyy. Set y=0y = 0y=0 for the xxx-intercept:

4x−5(0)=20,4x=20,x=5,4x - 5(0) = 20, \qquad 4x = 20, \qquad x = 5,4x−5(0)=20,4x=20,x=5,

so the xxx-intercept is (5,0)(5, 0)(5,0). Set x=0x = 0x=0 for the yyy-intercept:

4(0)−5y=20,−5y=20,y=−4,4(0) - 5y = 20, \qquad -5y = 20, \qquad y = -4,4(0)−5y=20,−5y=20,y=−4,

so the yyy-intercept is (0,−4)(0, -4)(0,−4). With both crossings in hand, you can plot the line from its intercepts, the quick two-point method from the graphing lesson.

Check your understanding

What is the xxx-intercept of the line y=3x−12y = 3x - 12y=3x−12?

Answer choices

The xxx-intercept is where the line crosses the xxx-axis, so set y=0y = 0y=0 and solve for xxx.

0=3x−12,3x=12,x=4.0 = 3x - 12, \qquad 3x = 12, \qquad x = 4.0=3x−12,3x=12,x=4.

So the xxx-intercept is (4,0)(4, 0)(4,0). Setting x=0x = 0x=0 instead gives the yyy-intercept (0,−12)(0, -12)(0,−12).

Special cases

Three lines stretch slope-intercept form to its edges, or past them, and each is worth a moment.

A horizontal line has slope 000. In slope-intercept form that reads y=0x+by = 0x + by=0x+b, which collapses to y=by = by=b. So a horizontal line is its own yyy-intercept turned into an equation: it meets the yyy-axis at (0,b)(0, b)(0,b) and never tilts. When bbb is not 000, the line has no xxx-intercept, because it holds the height bbb and never reaches the xxx-axis. When b=0b = 0b=0, the line is the xxx-axis itself and lies flat along it.

A vertical line is the one case slope-intercept form cannot describe. Its slope is undefined, so there is no mmm to write, and its equation is x=cx = cx=c instead. A vertical line has an xxx-intercept at (c,0)(c, 0)(c,0) but no yyy-intercept, unless c=0c = 0c=0, when the line is the yyy-axis.

A line through the origin has yyy-intercept (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0), so its bbb is 000 and its equation is simply y=mxy = mxy=mx. When the slope mmm is not 000, the xxx-intercept and the yyy-intercept are the same point, the origin, because setting x=0x = 0x=0 and setting y=0y = 0y=0 both land there. Because both intercepts land on that same point, plotting such a line from intercepts alone is not enough; step out to a second point using the slope. A slope of 000 instead gives y=0y = 0y=0, the flat line from the horizontal case above, which has no single xxx-intercept at all.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Reading the wrong number as the slope in y=5−2xy = 5 - 2xy=5−2x. The slope is the coefficient of xxx, which is −2-2−2, not the leading 555. Rewrite it as y=−2x+5y = -2x + 5y=−2x+5 first, and the slope sits where you expect it.
  • Dropping the sign of the intercept. In y=3x−4y = 3x - 4y=3x−4 the yyy-intercept is (0,−4)(0, -4)(0,−4), not (0,4)(0, 4)(0,4). Read the constant with its own sign.
  • Swapping the two intercept rules. The xxx-intercept comes from setting y=0y = 0y=0, and the yyy-intercept from setting x=0x = 0x=0. Zero the coordinate of the axis you are crossing, which is the opposite letter.
  • Only solving halfway when converting. From 3y=−2x+123y = -2x + 123y=−2x+12 you must still divide every term by 333 to reach y=−23x+4y = -\tfrac{2}{3}x + 4y=−32​x+4. Slope-intercept form needs yyy alone with a coefficient of 111.
  • Trying to force a vertical line into y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b. A vertical line has no slope, so it has no slope-intercept form at all; write it directly as x=cx = cx=c.
  • Confusing the number bbb with the point. The intercept value bbb is a single number, the height. The yyy-intercept as a point is (0,b)(0, b)(0,b), sitting on the yyy-axis.

Key takeaways

  • Slope-intercept form of a line is y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b, where mmm is the slope and bbb is the yyy-coordinate where the line crosses the yyy-axis.
  • It comes from point-slope form using the yyy-intercept (0,b)(0, b)(0,b) as the point: y−b=m(x−0)y - b = m(x - 0)y−b=m(x−0) simplifies to y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b.
  • Read mmm and bbb straight off once yyy is alone. In y=5−2xy = 5 - 2xy=5−2x the slope is −2-2−2 and the intercept is (0,5)(0, 5)(0,5); reorder the terms if they distract you.
  • Graph from it by plotting the yyy-intercept (0,b)(0, b)(0,b), then stepping by the slope’s rise over run to a second point.
  • Convert to it by solving for yyy: isolate the yyy-term and divide through so yyy stands alone with coefficient 111.
  • The yyy-intercept (0,b)(0, b)(0,b) is read from bbb or found by setting x=0x = 0x=0; the xxx-intercept is found by setting y=0y = 0y=0 and solving for xxx.
  • Special cases: a horizontal line y=by = by=b has slope 000 and no xxx-intercept unless b=0b = 0b=0; a vertical line x=cx = cx=c has undefined slope and no slope-intercept form. A line through the origin with a nonzero slope has b=0b = 0b=0, and its two intercepts coincide at (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0).

A bit of history (optional)

Picture yourself taking an algebra exam in Stockholm. The line on the page is written y=kx+my = kx + my=kx+m, and you were taught last week that mmm is the slope. Here it is the height at which the line crosses the axis.

The form y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b can feel like a law of nature, because it is what every classroom in the United States writes. It is not a law. It is a local costume. British textbooks prefer y=mx+cy = mx + cy=mx+c, taking ccc for the constant term. Swedish and Finnish ones prefer y=kx+my = kx + my=kx+m, which moves the slope onto kkk and hands mmm over to the intercept. Three traditions, three sets of letters, one line. The packaging is younger than you would guess, too: naming and drilling these forms is the work of standardized textbooks over the last century and a half.

What the traditions never disagree about is the mathematics. Every version says the identical thing, that a nonvertical straight line is pinned down by exactly two numbers. One number fixes how steeply it tilts. The other fixes where it crosses the vertical axis. Settle those two and the line has nowhere else to go. That is why you can graph it by plotting the intercept and then stepping off the slope, and why solving for yyy hands you both numbers at once.

The lettering is a habit. The pair of numbers is the lesson.
