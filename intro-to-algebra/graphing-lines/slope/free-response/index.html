  1. Problem 1 Grid measurements

    Find the slope of the line in the figure.

    Text description of this figure

    A coordinate grid drawn with the same length for one unit on both axes. The x-axis runs from negative 4 to 6, with vertical gridlines and numbered ticks at negative 4, negative 2, 0, 2, 4 and 6, so the vertical gridlines are 2 units apart. The y-axis runs from negative 3 to 9, with horizontal gridlines and numbered ticks at negative 3, 0, 3, 6 and 9, so the horizontal gridlines are 3 units apart. The origin is labeled 0. A straight line with arrowheads at both ends falls from left to right across the grid, from the top left corner of the grid at negative 4, 9, through the y-axis at 3, down to the bottom edge at 4, negative 3. Two points on the line are marked with dots and carry no labels: one where the gridlines x equals negative 2 and y equals 6 meet, the point (negative 2, 6), and one on the x-axis at the point (2, 0). No slope triangle or slope label is drawn.

    1. Hint 1

      Use coordinate changes, not just the number of drawn grid spaces.

    2. Hint 2

      Read the coordinates of the two marked points and divide the signed rise by the signed run.

    Answer

    m=−32m=-\frac32m=−23​.

    Full solution

    The marked points are (−2,6)(-2,6)(−2,6) and (2,0)(2,0)(2,0).

    Moving from the first to the second gives a rise of −6-6−6 and a run of 444:

    m=−64m=\frac{-6}{4}m=4−6​ m=−32m=-\frac32m=−23​

    The line falls to the right, agreeing with the negative sign.

    Answer

    m=−32m=-\frac32m=−23​.

    Key idea

    Slope compares changes measured in coordinate units.

  2. Problem 2 Negative coordinates

    Find the slope of the line through (−7,4)(-7,4)(−7,4) and (5,−5)(5,-5)(5,−5).

    1. Hint 1

      Slope is the change in yyy divided by the change in xxx, both taken in the same order.

    2. Hint 2

      With (−7,4)(-7,4)(−7,4) as the first point, the rise is −5−4-5-4−5−4 and the run is 5−(−7)5-(-7)5−(−7).

    Answer

    m=−34m=-\frac34m=−43​, or −0.75-0.75−0.75.

    Full solution

    Take (−7,4)(-7,4)(−7,4) as the first point and (5,−5)(5,-5)(5,−5) as the second.

    The rise is −5−4-5-4−5−4, which is −9-9−9.

    The run is 5−(−7)5-(-7)5−(−7), which is 121212.

    So the slope is

    m=−912m=\frac{-9}{12}m=12−9​ m=−34m=-\frac34m=−43​

    Moving right from (−7,4)(-7,4)(−7,4) to (5,−5)(5,-5)(5,−5), the height drops, so the line falls to the right, which agrees with the negative sign.

    Answer

    m=−34m=-\frac34m=−43​, or −0.75-0.75−0.75.

    Key idea

    Subtracting a negative coordinate adds its size, so write each subtraction out in full before simplifying.

  3. Problem 3 Point restrictions

    Classify the slope of each line described by its points: A has second coordinate 777 for every first coordinate; B has first coordinate −4-4−4 for every second coordinate; C has yyy decrease whenever xxx increases.

    1. Hint 1

      Slope depends on whether the rise or the run is zero, and on the signs of nonzero changes.

    2. Hint 2

      A fixed second coordinate gives no rise; a fixed first coordinate gives no run.

    Answer

    A: zero; B: undefined; C: negative.

    Full solution

    For A, two distinct points have zero rise and nonzero run, so

    mA=0m_A=0mA​=0

    For B, distinct points have zero run, and division by zero has no value.

    For C, a move to the right has positive run and negative rise, so the ratio is negative.

    These descriptions respectively give a horizontal line, a vertical line, and a line falling to the right.

    Answer

    A: zero; B: undefined; C: negative.

    Key idea

    A fixed height gives zero slope, a fixed column gives undefined slope, and a fall to the right gives negative slope.

  4. Problem 4 Water readings

    Water drains from a tank. Its height is 12 cm at 0 minutes, 9 cm at 3 minutes and 5 cm at 7 minutes. Do the three readings lie on one straight-line graph? If they do, and the height keeps changing at the same rate, find the height at 10 minutes.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the height change per minute in each interval.

    2. Hint 2

      If the two rates agree, continue at that rate for the three minutes after the last reading.

    Answer

    Yes; the height at 10 minutes is 2 cm.

    Full solution

    The first interval gives

    m1=9−123−0m_1=\frac{9-12}{3-0}m1​=3−09−12​ m1=−1m_1=-1m1​=−1

    The second gives

    m2=5−97−3m_2=\frac{5-9}{7-3}m2​=7−35−9​ m2=−1m_2=-1m2​=−1

    The two intervals have the same slope and share the reading at 3 minutes, so the three readings lie on one straight line.

    Three more minutes lower the height by 3 cm, so it reaches 5−3=25-3=25−3=2 cm.

    From the starting reading, 12−10=212-10=212−10=2 cm gives an independent check.

    Answer

    Yes; the height at 10 minutes is 2 cm.

    Key idea

    Equal slopes over unequal time intervals support one constant rate of change.

  5. Problem 5 Route drawing

    The figure joins AAA to BBB and BBB to CCC. Find the slope of each segment and decide whether the route follows one straight line.

    Text description of this figure

    A coordinate grid with the same length for one unit on both axes and a gridline at every whole number. The x-axis runs from negative 5 to 4 and the y-axis from negative 3 to 4, both with arrowheads at each end, a numbered tick at every whole number, and the origin labeled 0. A segment joins A to B and a second segment joins B to C; neither is extended past its endpoints. No angles, slope triangles or coordinates are shown.

    1. Hint 1

      Each part of one nonvertical straight line must have the same slope.

    2. Hint 2

      Read the coordinates and keep the subtraction order consistent on each segment.

    Answer

    mAB=34m_{AB}=\frac34mAB​=43​ and mBC=23m_{BC}=\frac23mBC​=32​; the route does not follow one straight line.

    Full solution

    The points are A(−4,−3)A(-4,-3)A(−4,−3), B(0,0)B(0,0)B(0,0), and C(3,2)C(3,2)C(3,2).

    Their slopes are

    mAB=0−(−3)0−(−4)m_{AB}=\frac{0-(-3)}{0-(-4)}mAB​=0−(−4)0−(−3)​ mAB=34m_{AB}=\frac34mAB​=43​ mBC=2−03−0m_{BC}=\frac{2-0}{3-0}mBC​=3−02−0​ mBC=23m_{BC}=\frac23mBC​=32​

    Answer

  6. Problem 6 Ramp measurements

    A straight ramp rises 45 cm over a horizontal distance of 3 meters. Find its slope, then find its rise over the first 1.2 meters of horizontal distance.

    1. Hint 1

      Convert the two measurements to the same unit before taking their ratio.

    2. Hint 2

      The same rise-to-run ratio holds over a shorter part of the straight ramp.

    Answer

    Slope 0.150.150.15 (or 320\frac{3}{20}203​); rise 0.180.180.18 meters (18 cm).

    Full solution

    In meters, the full rise is 0.450.450.45 and the run is 333, so

    m=0.453m=\frac{0.45}{3}m=30.45​ m=0.15m=0.15m=0.15

    Measuring both in centimeters gives 45300\frac{45}{300}30045​, the same slope.

    The shorter rise is

    0.15(1.2)=0.180.15(1.2)=0.180.15(1.2)=0.18

    It is 0.180.180.18 meters, or 18 cm.

    The shorter run is 0.40.40.4 of the full run, and 0.4(0.45)=0.180.4(0.45)=0.180.4(0.45)=0.18 checks the rise.

    Answer

    Slope 0.150.150.15 (or 320\frac{3}{20}203​); rise 0.180.180.18 meters (18 cm).

    Key idea

    Slope requires consistent units and gives proportional rises along a straight ramp.

  7. Problem 7 Grid locations

    The figure shows a line through two marked points. List every point on the line inside the displayed grid whose two coordinates are integers. Explain how the slope helps you make a complete list.

    Text description of this figure

    A coordinate grid with the same length for one unit on both axes and a gridline at every whole number. The x-axis runs from negative 5 to 6 and the y-axis from negative 4 to 4, both with arrowheads at each end, a numbered tick at every whole number, and the origin labeled 0. A straight line falls from left to right across the whole grid and stops at its edges, with an arrowhead at each end: it enters at the left edge, x equals negative 5, between y equals 3 and 4, and leaves at the right edge, x equals 6, between y equals negative 3 and negative 4. Two points on the line are marked with dots and carry no letters or coordinates: (negative 4, 3) and (2, negative 1). No other points are marked.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the slope from the two marked points; every step with that rise-to-run ratio stays on the line.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the smallest whole-number run that gives a whole-number rise, then step both ways to the grid edges.

    Answer

    (−4,3)(-4,3)(−4,3), (−1,1)(-1,1)(−1,1), (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1), and (5,−3)(5,-3)(5,−3); they are spaced by runs of 3 and rises of −2-2−2, the smallest whole-number step for slope −23-\frac23−32​.

    Full solution

    The marked points are (−4,3)(-4,3)(−4,3) and (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1), giving

    m=−1−32−(−4)m=\frac{-1-3}{2-(-4)}m=2−(−4)−1−3​ m=−23m=-\frac23m=−32​

    Each step of 3 right and 2 down reaches another point with integer coordinates.

    Starting at (−4,3)(-4,3)(−4,3) gives (−1,1)(-1,1)(−1,1), (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1), and (5,−3)(5,-3)(5,−3).

    A step left leaves the grid, as does the next step right.

    A run of 1 or 2 produces a noninteger rise, so there are no skipped integer-coordinate points.

    Answer

    (−4,3)(-4,3)(−4,3), (−1,1)(-1,1)(−1,1), (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1), and (5,−3)(5,-3)(5,−3); they are spaced by runs of 3 and rises of −2-2−2, the smallest whole-number step for slope −23-\frac23−32​.

  8. Problem 8 Shifted coordinate records

    For every point on a nonvertical line, a recorder adds 5 to the first coordinate and subtracts 2 from the second. A student claims this changes the slope. Is the claim correct? Justify your answer for any two distinct original points.

    1. Hint 1

      Slope uses coordinate differences rather than the individual coordinates.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare what happens when the same adjustment appears in both terms of a difference.

    Answer

    No; the slope is unchanged.

    Full solution

    Let the original points be (a,b)(a,b)(a,b) and (c,d)(c,d)(c,d) with a≠ca\ne ca=c.

    The new rise is

    (d−2)−(b−2)=d−b(d-2)-(b-2)=d-b(d−2)−(b−2)=d−b

    The new run is

    (c+5)−(a+5)=c−a(c+5)-(a+5)=c-a(c+5)−(a+5)=c−a

    Both changes equal their original values.

    The run is nonzero because the original line is nonvertical and the points are distinct.

    Thus both slopes are (d−b)/(c−a)(d-b)/(c-a)(d−b)/(c−a), and the recorded line has the same slope.

    Answer

    No; the slope is unchanged.

    Key idea

    Adding fixed amounts to coordinates preserves the differences that determine slope.

  9. Problem 9 Two distinct points

    A student says, "If a slope calculation has no value, the two points do not determine a line." Test this claim using two distinct points that you choose, and explain what happens to the slope calculation.

    1. Hint 1

      Which kind of line makes the slope formula fail?

    2. Hint 2

      Two different points can share an xxx-coordinate while lying at different heights.

    Answer

    False; for example, (2,1)(2,1)(2,1) and (2,4)(2,4)(2,4) determine x=2x=2x=2, whose slope is undefined.

    Full solution

    Take (2,1)(2,1)(2,1) and (2,4)(2,4)(2,4).

    Their slope calculation is

    4−12−2=30\frac{4-1}{2-2}=\frac302−24−1​=03​

    Division by zero has no value.

    Nevertheless, both points lie on the vertical line x=2x=2x=2, which they determine uniquely.

    The missing slope number reflects a zero run, not the absence of a line.

    Answer

    False; for example, (2,1)(2,1)(2,1) and (2,4)(2,4)(2,4) determine x=2x=2x=2, whose slope is undefined.

    Key idea

    A vertical line exists even though its rise cannot be divided by its zero run.

  10. Problem 10 Joined movements

    A path is made of two straight moves, one after the other, both going right. Their runs are rrr and sss, both positive, and each move has slope mmm. Does the move from the start of the path to its end also have slope mmm, even when the runs differ? Justify your answer using rrr, sss and mmm.

    1. Hint 1

      Turn each slope statement into a rise using its own run.

    2. Hint 2

      Add the rises and add the runs before forming the combined ratio.

    Answer

    Yes; the combined slope is mmm.

    Full solution

    The rises are mrmrmr and msmsms, while the combined run is r+sr+sr+s.

    Since both runs are positive, r+sr+sr+s is nonzero and division is valid.

    The combined slope is

    mr+msr+s\frac{mr+ms}{r+s}r+smr+ms​

    Factoring the numerator gives

    m(r+s)r+s=m\frac{m(r+s)}{r+s}=mr+sm(r+s)​=m

    Thus unequal runs still produce the same slope.

    This also applies to a negative or zero mmm, since the rises carry its sign.

    Answer

    Yes; the combined slope is mmm.

    Key idea

    Adding moves with the same slope preserves that slope when the combined run is nonzero.
