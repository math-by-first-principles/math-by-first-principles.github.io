    1. One line, two points, one number . Foundational, 9 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 9 pts

    A line passes through P(−5,−2)P(-5, -2)P(−5,−2) and Q(3,4)Q(3, 4)Q(3,4). Unless a part says otherwise, work with the points in that order: PPP is point one and QQQ is point two.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Find the slope of the line through PPP and QQQ. Show the substitution into the slope formula, and give the slope in lowest terms.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Starting from Q(3,4)Q(3, 4)Q(3,4), step along the line to one further point on the right of QQQ and one further point on the left of QQQ. Do not write an equation for the line.

      Carry your own answer forward Step with the slope you found in part A, whatever value that came out as. The credit here is for reading a slope as a run and a rise and moving by both, not for landing on one particular pair of points.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Suppose you had labelled the same two points the other way round, calling QQQ point one and PPP point two. Explain why the formula cannot return a different value. Then say what goes wrong instead if you subtract in one order on the top and in the other order on the bottom.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Every part of this question comes out of one ratio: the change in height over the change across, measured between the same two points in the same order. Settle that order before you write a single number down.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      A slope written as a fraction is a pair of walking instructions. The number underneath says how far across to go, the number on top says how far up. Travelling the opposite way reverses both instructions, not just one of them.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Compare the two versions symbol by symbol rather than number by number. Ask what happens to a fraction when the top and the bottom both change sign, and then what happens when only one of them does.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    m=34m = \tfrac{3}{4}m=43​, from a rise of 666 over a run of 888.

    • 0.750.750.75 is the same number, and so is 68\tfrac{6}{8}86​, but a slope is normally left in lowest terms

    Part B

    (7,7)(7, 7)(7,7) lies on the line to the right of QQQ, and (−1,1)(-1, 1)(−1,1) lies on it to the left of QQQ.

    Part C

    Swapping the labels changes the sign of the top and of the bottom together, and a fraction with both signs changed keeps its value. Mixing the orders changes only one of the two signs, so the reported slope comes out with the wrong sign and describes a line tilting the other way.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Label the points and hold the order: (x1,y1)=(−5,−2)(x_1, y_1) = (-5, -2)(x1​,y1​)=(−5,−2) and (x2,y2)=(3,4)(x_2, y_2) = (3, 4)(x2​,y2​)=(3,4). Substitute, subtracting in the same order on the top and on the bottom.

    m=y2−y1x2−x1=4−(−2)3−(−5)=68=34.m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{4 - (-2)}{3 - (-5)} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}.m=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​=3−(−5)4−(−2)​=86​=43​.

    Both subtractions ran into a negative coordinate and both turned into an addition: 4−(−2)=64 - (-2) = 64−(−2)=6 and 3−(−5)=83 - (-5) = 83−(−5)=8. So the rise is 666, the run is 888, and once the fraction is reduced the line climbs 333 units of height for every 444 units across.

    Part B

    Read the slope as walking instructions: the denominator is the run and the numerator is the rise, so one step to the right moves 444 across and 333 up.

    (3,4)⟶(3+4,  4+3)=(7,7).(3, 4) \longrightarrow (3 + 4,\; 4 + 3) = (7, 7).(3,4)⟶(3+4,4+3)=(7,7).

    Stepping the other way reverses both moves, 444 to the left and 333 down:

    (3,4)⟶(3−4,  4−3)=(−1,1).(3, 4) \longrightarrow (3 - 4,\; 4 - 3) = (-1, 1).(3,4)⟶(3−4,4−3)=(−1,1).

    Both are on the line, and the second one can be checked against PPP: from P(−5,−2)P(-5, -2)P(−5,−2) to (−1,1)(-1, 1)(−1,1) the rise is 1−(−2)=31 - (-2) = 31−(−2)=3 and the run is −1−(−5)=4-1 - (-5) = 4−1−(−5)=4, so

    m=34,m = \frac{3}{4},m=43​,

    which is the slope the line already had.

    Part C

    Swapping the labels replaces y2−y1y_2 - y_1y2​−y1​ by y1−y2y_1 - y_2y1​−y2​ and x2−x1x_2 - x_1x2​−x1​ by x1−x2x_1 - x_2x1​−x2​. Each is the negative of what it was, so the new ratio is

    y1−y2x1−x2=−(y2−y1)−(x2−x1)=y2−y1x2−x1,\frac{y_1 - y_2}{x_1 - x_2} = \frac{-(y_2 - y_1)}{-(x_2 - x_1)} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1},x1​−x2​y1​−y2​​=−(x2​−x1​)−(y2​−y1​)​=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​,

    since −a−b=ab\frac{-a}{-b} = \frac{a}{b}−b−a​=ba​: two minus signs, one above and one below, cancel each other. On the points of this question,

    −2−4−5−3=−6−8=34,\frac{-2 - 4}{-5 - 3} = \frac{-6}{-8} = \frac{3}{4},−5−3−2−4​=−8−6​=43​,

    the same value as before. Nothing was risked by choosing an order, which is why the only rule is to keep the one you chose.

    Mixing the orders is a different matter, because then only one of the two signs flips. Taking y2−y1y_2 - y_1y2​−y1​ on the top but x1−x2x_1 - x_2x1​−x2​ underneath gives

    4−(−2)−5−3=6−8=−34.\frac{4 - (-2)}{-5 - 3} = \frac{6}{-8} = -\frac{3}{4}.−5−34−(−2)​=−86​=−43​.

    One sign changed and the other did not, so the ratio changed sign. That value belongs to a line falling to the right, the mirror image of the line you were given, so the error is not a small one: it reverses the direction the line reports.

    In one line

    The slope is m=34m = \tfrac{3}{4}m=43​; stepping from Q(3,4)Q(3, 4)Q(3,4) reaches (7,7)(7, 7)(7,7) on the right and (−1,1)(-1, 1)(−1,1) on the left; and the labelling cannot matter, because swapping the points changes the sign of the top and the bottom together, while mixing the two subtraction orders changes only one of them and so flips the sign of the answer.

    Another way: Count the triangle instead of substituting

    With coordinates this small the slope can be counted rather than computed. Plot P(−5,−2)P(-5, -2)P(−5,−2) and Q(3,4)Q(3, 4)Q(3,4), then travel from PPP to QQQ in two moves: across from x=−5x = -5x=−5 to x=3x = 3x=3 is a run of 888, and up from y=−2y = -2y=−2 to y=4y = 4y=4 is a rise of 666. Those two legs are the slope triangle, and

    m=riserun=68=34.m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}.m=runrise​=86​=43​.

    The count and the formula are the same arithmetic; the formula just does the two subtractions for you.

    When it is worth it When the points are close enough together to plot, and especially as a check: a counted triangle makes a sign error obvious, because you can see whether the trip went up or down.

    2. A mountain road, section by section . Application, 10 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Application 10 pts

    A mountain road is surveyed in straight sections. The first section climbs steadily from an elevation of 820820820 m to an elevation of 103010301030 m while covering a horizontal distance of 350035003500 m. The second section begins where the first one ends, runs steadily downhill, covers a horizontal distance of 150015001500 m, and has a slope of −225-\tfrac{2}{25}−252​. Road signs quote a slope as a percent grade: a grade of 5%5\%5% means the road changes elevation by 555 m for every 100100100 m of horizontal distance.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Find the slope of the first section as a fraction in lowest terms, and state it as a percent grade.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Find the elevation of the road at the end of the second section.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Which of the two sections is steeper? Give a reason that still works when one slope is positive and the other is negative, and say what the sign of each slope tells a driver.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Two of these parts are the same computation run in opposite directions. One hands you a rise and a run and wants their ratio; the other hands you a ratio and a run and wants the rise. Decide which is which before you start either.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      A grade is nothing more exotic than the slope written as a change in height per one hundred units of travel. Get the slope as a fraction first, then rewrite that fraction with one hundred underneath it.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      One of these two slopes is negative, so the usual smaller-and-larger comparison is about to answer a question nobody asked. Decide first which quantity actually measures how steep a stretch of road is.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    The slope is 350\tfrac{3}{50}503​, which is a grade of 6%6\%6%.

    • 0.060.060.06 is the same slope written as a decimal, and 6100\tfrac{6}{100}1006​ is the same fraction before it is reduced

    Part B

    The second section ends at an elevation of 910910910 m.

    Part C

    The second section is the steeper one: steepness is the size of the slope, and 225\tfrac{2}{25}252​ is bigger than 350\tfrac{3}{50}503​. The signs report direction only, the first section gaining elevation and the second losing it.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The rise is the change in elevation and the run is the horizontal distance travelled while that change happens.

    m=riserun=1030−8203500=2103500.m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{1030 - 820}{3500} = \frac{210}{3500}.m=runrise​=35001030−820​=3500210​.

    Divide the top and the bottom by 707070:

    2103500=350.\frac{210}{3500} = \frac{3}{50}.3500210​=503​.

    A grade is the same slope written with one hundred underneath, so scale the fraction instead of recomputing anything:

    350=6100=6%.\frac{3}{50} = \frac{6}{100} = 6\%.503​=1006​=6%.

    Notice that the metres of rise and the metres of run cancel. A slope is a pure number, which is exactly why a grade can be posted on a sign with no unit attached to it.

    Part B

    A slope is the rise per unit of run, so the rise over a given run is the slope multiplied by that run.

    rise=−225×1500=−300025=−120.\text{rise} = -\frac{2}{25} \times 1500 = -\frac{3000}{25} = -120.rise=−252​×1500=−253000​=−120.

    The rise is negative because this section runs downhill, and −120-120−120 is a drop of 120120120 m. The section starts where the first one finished, at 103010301030 m, so

    1030−120=910.1030 - 120 = 910.1030−120=910.

    The road is 910910910 m above sea level at the end of the second section. This is stepping along a line: one point was known, the slope supplied the rise for the run travelled, and the second point followed.

    Part C

    Compare sizes, not positions on the number line. Put both slopes over the same denominator:

    350=350,−225=−450.\frac{3}{50} = \frac{3}{50}, \qquad -\frac{2}{25} = -\frac{4}{50}.503​=503​,−252​=−504​.

    The first section changes elevation by 333 units for every 505050 of run; the second changes it by 444 units for every 505050. Ignoring direction, 444 in 505050 is the bigger tilt, so the second section is the steeper one, even though −450-\tfrac{4}{50}−504​ is the smaller of the two numbers. As grades, the comparison is 6%6\%6% against 8%8\%8%.

    That is the trap this part is built on. Ordering the two slopes as numbers puts the negative one below, and reading that as gentler is a mistake: the size measures steepness and the sign does not.

    The signs carry the other half of the information. A positive slope means the road gains elevation as the driver moves forward along the surveyed direction, and a negative slope means it loses elevation. So a driver on the second section is descending, and descending the steeper of the two stretches.

    In one line

    The first section has slope 350\tfrac{3}{50}503​, a grade of 6%6\%6%; the second section drops 120120120 m and ends at an elevation of 910910910 m; and the second section is the steeper of the two, because steepness is the size of the slope and 450\tfrac{4}{50}504​ beats 350\tfrac{3}{50}503​, with the signs saying only that the first climbs and the second descends.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    Counting from the left marked point: a run of 888, a rise of 666, and a slope of 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​.

    • a smaller triangle counted between other grid corners of the same line gives a different run and rise, such as 444 and 333, but it must give the same slope

    Part B

    Counting from the left marked point: a run of 666, a rise of −8-8−8, and a slope of −43-\tfrac{4}{3}−34​.

    Part C

    Line rrr has slope 000, a genuine number, because its rise is 000 while its run is not. Line sss has no slope at all, because its run is 000 and the division that defines a slope cannot be carried out.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Start at the left marked point, at (−4,−2)(-4, -2)(−4,−2), and count in two moves.

    The run comes first: from x=−4x = -4x=−4 across to x=4x = 4x=4 is 888 units to the right. Now the rise: from y=−2y = -2y=−2 up to y=4y = 4y=4 is 666 units up, and up counts as positive.

    m=riserun=68=34.m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}.m=runrise​=86​=43​.

    The formula agrees, as it must, since counting a triangle and subtracting coordinates are the same two differences:

    m=4−(−2)4−(−4)=68=34.m = \frac{4 - (-2)}{4 - (-4)} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}.m=4−(−4)4−(−2)​=86​=43​.

    A smaller triangle on the same line works just as well. Between (0,1)(0, 1)(0,1) and (4,4)(4, 4)(4,4) the run is 444 and the rise is 333, and 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​ comes out again.

    Part B

    Start at the left marked point of qqq, at (−3,4)(-3, 4)(−3,4), and count the run first: across to x=3x = 3x=3 is 666 units to the right.

    Now the rise. To get back onto the line you go from y=4y = 4y=4 down to y=−4y = -4y=−4, a drop of 888, and a drop is a negative rise:

    m=riserun=−86=−43.m = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{-8}{6} = -\frac{4}{3}.m=runrise​=6−8​=−34​.

    Counting the run to the right keeps the run positive, so the whole sign of the slope comes from the rise, and here the rise fell. That matches the picture: qqq goes downhill from left to right.

    Size and sign are separate readings. Comparing ∣−43∣\left|-\tfrac{4}{3}\right|​−34​​ with 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​, line qqq is the steeper of the two lines, and it is the falling one.

    Part C

    Run each pair through the formula and watch which of the two differences collapses.

    For rrr, the two heights are equal, so the rise is what collapses:

    m=6−65−(−2)=07=0.m = \frac{6 - 6}{5 - (-2)} = \frac{0}{7} = 0.m=5−(−2)6−6​=70​=0.

    Zero divided by seven is a perfectly ordinary division with a perfectly ordinary answer. Line rrr is horizontal, and 000 is its slope.

    For sss, the two xxx-coordinates are equal, so it is the run that collapses:

    m=7−(−1)4−4=80.m = \frac{7 - (-1)}{4 - 4} = \frac{8}{0}.m=4−47−(−1)​=08​.

    This is not a division with an unusual answer; it is not a division at all. No number multiplied by 000 gives 888, so nothing can be the value of this expression. Line sss is vertical, and its slope is undefined.

    That is the difference in kind. One line has a slope, and the number happens to be 000. The other has no slope for the formula to report. The two are opposite ends of the four cases, not neighbours, and the temptation to call a vertical line's slope infinite is worth resisting: infinity is not a number, and the division has no result rather than an enormous one.

    In one line

    Line ppp has a run of 888 and a rise of 666, so its slope is 34\tfrac{3}{4}43​; line qqq has a run of 666 and a rise of −8-8−8, so its slope is −43-\tfrac{4}{3}−34​; line rrr has slope 000, since its rise is 000; and line sss has no slope at all, since its run is 000 and dividing by zero has no result.
