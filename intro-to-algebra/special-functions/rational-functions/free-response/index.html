  1. Problem 1 Where the denominator vanishes

    Find the domain of f(x)=4x−3x2+5x−14f(x)=\frac{4x-3}{x^2+5x-14}f(x)=x2+5x−144x−3​.

    1. Hint 1

      A rational function's domain excludes only the inputs that make its denominator zero.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the quadratic denominator as two linear factors, then set each factor to zero.

    Answer

    Domain: every real number except −7-7−7 and 222.

    Full solution

    Factor the denominator:

    x2+5x−14=(x+7)(x−2)x^2+5x-14=(x+7)(x-2)x2+5x−14=(x+7)(x−2)

    This product is zero at x=−7x=-7x=−7 and at x=2x=2x=2, so those are the two excluded inputs.

    A numerator never restricts a domain, so no other input is excluded.

    Answer

    Domain: every real number except −7-7−7 and 222.

    Key idea

    The zeros of a rational function's denominator are exactly the inputs its domain excludes.

  2. Problem 2 The two kinds of gap

    For f(x)=x2−3x−10x2−x−20f(x)=\frac{x^2-3x-10}{x^2-x-20}f(x)=x2−x−20x2−3x−10​, name every input the domain excludes and say for each whether the graph has a hole or a vertical asymptote there. Give the height of any hole.

    1. Hint 1

      An excluded input gives a hole only when every copy of its factor cancels away.

    2. Hint 2

      Factor the top and the bottom, and cancel what you can.

    Answer

    Excluded: 555 and −4-4−4. Hole at (5,79)\left(5,\frac{7}{9}\right)(5,97​); vertical asymptote x=−4x=-4x=−4.

    Full solution

    The denominator factors as (x−5)(x+4)(x-5)(x+4)(x−5)(x+4), so the domain excludes 555 and −4-4−4.

    The numerator factors as (x−5)(x+2)(x-5)(x+2)(x−5)(x+2).

    The factor x−5x-5x−5 cancels, leaving x+2x+4\frac{x+2}{x+4}x+4x+2​ at every input except 555 and −4-4−4.

    No copy of x−5x-5x−5 is left below, so 555 gives a hole, while the copy of x+4x+4x+4 that is left makes x=−4x=-4x=−4 a vertical asymptote.

    The height of the hole is the value of the reduced form there,

    5+25+4=79\frac{5+2}{5+4}=\frac{7}{9}5+45+2​=97​

    Answer

    Excluded: 555 and −4-4−4. Hole at (5,79)\left(5,\frac{7}{9}\right)(5,97​); vertical asymptote x=−4x=-4x=−4.

    Key idea

    Cancel as far as it goes: a factor that disappears leaves a hole at the reduced height, and a copy still below leaves a vertical asymptote.

  3. Problem 3 The numerator record

    A rational function has a numerator of degree 555 and a denominator with leading term 3x53x^53x5. Its horizontal asymptote is y=−2y=-2y=−2. Find the numerator leading term.

    1. Hint 1

      Equal degrees make the horizontal asymptote the ratio of the leading coefficients.

    2. Hint 2

      Only the two leading terms matter far out; write the equation the ratio of leading coefficients must satisfy.

    Answer

    −6x5-6x^5−6x5.

    Full solution

    If the numerator leading coefficient is aaa, equal degrees give

    a3=−2\frac a3=-23a​=−2

    Multiplying by 333 gives a=−6a=-6a=−6.

    The degree is stated to be 555, so the leading term is −6x5-6x^5−6x5.

    Its ratio to 3x53x^53x5 has the required height −2-2−2.

    Answer

    −6x5-6x^5−6x5.

    Key idea

    A horizontal asymptote and a known denominator leading term can determine an unknown numerator leading term.

  4. Problem 4 The unfinished branches

    The figure marks two points of y=−2/xy=-2/xy=−2/x. Complete the graph in the window, then give the coordinates of its point with x=−4x=-4x=−4 and of its point with y=−4y=-4y=−4, and explain why no point of the graph lies on either axis.

    Text description of this figure

    Coordinate axes on a square grid, with x and y each running from negative four to four and a number at every whole unit. Short extra ticks mark every half unit along both axes, so a height of one half can be read off. Two points are marked with filled dots and labeled with their coordinates: the point (1, negative 2) below the x-axis on the right, and the point (negative 1, 2) above the x-axis on the left. Nothing else is drawn, and no curve passes through the two points.

    1. Hint 1

      Each allowed input has one output, and dividing −2-2−2 by a positive input gives a negative result.

    2. Hint 2

      Plot a few more pairs, such as the outputs at 222 and at −2-2−2, before joining anything; the input 000 is not allowed.

    3. Hint 3

      Think about what would make the output zero, and whether that can happen here.

    Answer

    Two branches, in the second and fourth quadrants, approaching both axes; the points are (−4,12)\left(-4,\frac{1}{2}\right)(−4,21​) and (12,−4)\left(\frac{1}{2},-4\right)(21​,−4); the graph meets neither axis.

    Full solution

    A positive input gives a negative output and a negative input gives a positive output, so the graph lies in the fourth and second quadrants.

    Besides the two marked points, (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1) and (−2,1)(-2,1)(−2,1) are on it.

    As the size of xxx grows, the output shrinks toward 000; as xxx nears 000, the output grows without bound in size.

    So each branch flattens toward the x-axis at one end and races along the y-axis at the other, and the two branches stay apart because the input 000 is missing.

    At x=−4x=-4x=−4 the output is −2/(−4)-2/(-4)−2/(−4), so one requested point is (−4,12)\left(-4,\frac{1}{2}\right)(−4,21​).

    An output of −4-4−4 needs −2/x=−4-2/x=-4−2/x=−4, that is x=12x=\frac{1}{2}x=21​, so the other requested point is (12,−4)\left(\frac{1}{2},-4\right)(21​,−4).

    No point of the graph has x=0x=0x=0, because that input is excluded.

    No point has y=0y=0y=0 either, because a fraction is zero only where its numerator is zero, and this numerator is the constant −2-2−2.

    Answer

    Two branches, in the second and fourth quadrants, approaching both axes; the points are (−4,12)\left(-4,\frac{1}{2}\right)(−4,21​) and (12,−4)\left(\frac{1}{2},-4\right)(21​,−4); the graph meets neither axis.

    Key idea

    An excluded input splits the domain in two, so a reciprocal graph comes in two separate branches, and a constant nonzero numerator keeps it off both axes.

  5. Problem 5 The guide lines

    A function has the form f(x)=a/(x−h)+kf(x)=a/(x-h)+kf(x)=a/(x−h)+k, with a≠0a\ne0a=0. Its graph in the figure has the shown asymptotes and marked point. Find its rule and the exact x-intercept.

    Text description of this figure

    A coordinate grid numbered at every whole unit, with x running from negative six to four and y from negative three to eight. A dashed vertical line labeled x equals negative 2 and a dashed horizontal line labeled y equals 3 cross inside the window. A curve labeled f has two separate branches. The left branch lies above the dashed horizontal line: it comes down from the top edge between negative 3 and negative 2, and flattens toward the dashed horizontal line as it runs left, reaching a height of 4 at the left edge. A filled dot labeled (0, 1) marks that point on the y-axis.

    1. Hint 1

      The asymptotes give hhh and kkk.

    2. Hint 2

      Substitute the marked point to find aaa, then solve for output zero.

    Answer

    f(x)=−4x+2+3f(x)=-\frac4{x+2}+3f(x)=−x+24​+3; x-intercept (−23,0)(-\frac23,0)(−32​,0).

    Full solution

    The asymptotes are x=−2x=-2x=−2 and y=3y=3y=3, so h=−2h=-2h=−2 and k=3k=3k=3.

    The point (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) gives

    1=a2+31=\frac a2+31=2a​+3

    4x+2=3\frac4{x+2}=3x+24​=3

    The rule also returns 111 at input 000, checking the given point.

    The asymptotes locate a shifted reciprocal, while one point sets its scale.

  6. Problem 6 The faster ride

    A cycle path is 484848 km long, so riding it at a steady speed of xxx km per hour takes 48x\frac{48}{x}x48​ hours. State the one speed the rule 48x\frac{48}{x}x48​ excludes, and say what the ride time does as the speed drops toward it. Riding 444 km per hour faster would save 111 hour: find the slower speed.

    1. Hint 1

      A fixed distance divided by a smaller and smaller speed gives a larger and larger time.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the two ride times, set their difference equal to 111 hour, and multiply through by both denominators.

    Answer

    The rule excludes x=0x=0x=0, and the time grows without bound as the speed drops toward 000; the slower speed is 121212 km per hour.

    Full solution

    The rule 48x\frac{48}{x}x48​ has no value at x=0x=0x=0, the one speed it excludes.

    As the speed drops toward 000 the time grows without bound: 242424 hours at 222 km per hour, 484848 hours at 111 km per hour, and 969696 hours at 12\frac{1}{2}21​ km per hour.

    The faster ride takes 48x+4\frac{48}{x+4}x+448​ hours, so saving one hour means

    48x−48x+4=1\frac{48}{x}-\frac{48}{x+4}=1x48​−x+448​=1

    A speed is positive, so neither denominator is zero and multiplying every term by x(x+4)x(x+4)x(x+4) is safe.

    That leaves

    48(x+4)−48x=x(x+4)48(x+4)-48x=x(x+4)48(x+4)−48x=x(x+4)

    The left side is 192192192, so the quadratic is x2+4x−192=0x^2+4x-192=0x2+4x−192=0, which factors as

    (x−12)(x+16)=0.(x-12)(x+16)=0.(x−12)(x+16)=0.

    The candidate −16-16−16 is not a speed, so the slower speed is 121212 km per hour.

    Checking, the ride takes 4812=4\frac{48}{12}=41248​=4 hours at that speed and 4816=3\frac{48}{16}=31648​=3 hours at 161616 km per hour, one hour less.

    Answer

    The rule excludes x=0x=0x=0, and the time grows without bound as the speed drops toward 000; the slower speed is 121212 km per hour.

    Key idea

    A fixed distance over a varying speed is a rational rule that bars the speed 000, and a candidate from clearing its denominators counts only if the situation allows it.

  7. Problem 7 Clearing the denominators

    Solve xx−7−7x+7=98x2−49\frac{x}{x-7}-\frac{7}{x+7}=\frac{98}{x^2-49}x−7x​−x+77​=x2−4998​, checking every candidate in the original equation.

    1. Hint 1

      The third denominator is a difference of squares, and two inputs are barred before any work is done.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiply every term by (x−7)(x+7)(x-7)(x+7)(x−7)(x+7), then compare what the resulting equation gives with those barred inputs.

    Answer

    No solution: the candidates are 777 and −7-7−7, and the original equation excludes both.

    Full solution

    Since x2−49=(x−7)(x+7)x^2-49=(x-7)(x+7)x2−49=(x−7)(x+7), the three denominators bar 777 and −7-7−7 from the start.

    At every other input, multiplying each term by (x−7)(x+7)(x-7)(x+7)(x−7)(x+7) leaves

    x(x+7)−7(x−7)=98x(x+7)-7(x-7)=98x(x+7)−7(x−7)=98

    The left side is x2+7x−7x+49x^2+7x-7x+49x2+7x−7x+49, so the equation becomes x2=49x^2=49x2=49, with candidates 777 and −7-7−7.

    Those two candidates are exactly the barred inputs, so neither solves the original equation and it has no solution.

    A sample allowed input confirms it: at x=9x=9x=9 the two sides are 6516\frac{65}{16}1665​ and 4916\frac{49}{16}1649​, which differ.

    Answer

    No solution: the candidates are 777 and −7-7−7, and the original equation excludes both.

    Key idea

    Clearing denominators can hand back only the inputs the original fractions bar, and the equation then has no solution at all.

  8. Problem 8 Bo's comparison

    For f(x)=x2+xx2+1f(x)=\frac{x^2+x}{x^2+1}f(x)=x2+1x2+x​, Bo says the graph never reaches its horizontal asymptote. Find that horizontal asymptote, determine every point, if any, at which the graph meets it, and then decide whether Bo is right.

    1. Hint 1

      The degrees and leading coefficients identify the horizontal asymptote.

    2. Hint 2

      Set the function equal to that height and check whether the resulting input is allowed.

    Answer

    Bo is wrong; the asymptote is y=1y=1y=1, and the graph meets it at (1,1)(1,1)(1,1).

    Full solution

    The degrees are equal and the leading coefficients are both 111, so the horizontal asymptote is y=1y=1y=1.

    The denominator is positive for every real input.

    Equality with the asymptote requires

    x2+x=x2+1x^2+x=x^2+1x2+x=x2+1

    Hence x=1x=1x=1.

    Substitution gives f(1)=2/2=1f(1)=2/2=1f(1)=2/2=1, so the point is (1,1)(1,1)(1,1).

    A horizontal asymptote describes distant behavior and need not be avoided at ordinary inputs.

    Answer

    Bo is wrong; the asymptote is y=1y=1y=1, and the graph meets it at (1,1)(1,1)(1,1).

    Key idea

    A rational graph may meet its horizontal asymptote even though its far-out behavior approaches that same height.

  9. Problem 9 The paired factors

    Let P,QP,QP,Q be nonzero polynomials, and suppose P/QP/QP/Q has a horizontal asymptote. For a real number aaa, Ana says multiplying both its numerator and denominator by x−ax-ax−a leaves that horizontal asymptote unchanged. Is she correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      For every input where both expressions are defined, the added factors cancel.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare degrees and leading coefficients before and after the multiplication.

    Answer

    Yes; the horizontal asymptote stays unchanged.

    Full solution

    The added factor increases both degrees by 111 and multiplies both leading coefficients by 111.

    Therefore the degree comparison is unchanged.

    If the numerator degree was smaller, it remains smaller and the asymptote stays y=0y=0y=0.

    If the degrees were equal, they remain equal and the same leading-coefficient ratio sets the asymptote.

    The new expression excludes x=ax=ax=a if it was previously allowed, but that single finite input does not change the distant behavior.

    Answer

    Yes; the horizontal asymptote stays unchanged.

    Key idea

    Adding the same nonzero polynomial factor above and below preserves the degree comparison and leading-coefficient ratio that set a horizontal asymptote.

  10. Problem 10 The missing numerator

    Find a quadratic p(x)p(x)p(x) in standard form so that f(x)=p(x)/(x2−2x−8)f(x)=p(x)/(x^2-2x-8)f(x)=p(x)/(x2−2x−8) has a hole at (4,2)(4,2)(4,2) and a vertical asymptote at x=−2x=-2x=−2. The leading coefficient of ppp is 111.

    1. Hint 1

      A hole at input 444 requires a factor x−4x-4x−4 in the numerator.

    2. Hint 2

      Write p(x)=(x−4)(x+c)p(x)=(x-4)(x+c)p(x)=(x−4)(x+c) and use the stated height after cancellation.

    p(x)=x2+4x−32p(x)=x^2+4x-32p(x)=x2+4x−32.

    Full solution

    The original denominator factors as (x−4)(x+2)(x-4)(x+2)(x−4)(x+2) and excludes both 444 and −2-2−2.

    A quadratic with leading coefficient 111 canceling x−4x-4x−4 has the form (x−4)(x+c)(x-4)(x+c)(x−4)(x+c).

    The reduced value at 444 must be 222, so

    4+c6=2\frac{4+c}{6}=264+c​=2

    giving c=8c=8c=8.

    Expanding yields

    p(x)=x2+4x−32p(x)=x^2+4x-32p(x)=x2+4x−32

    The reduced function is (x+8)/(x+2)(x+8)/(x+2)(x+8)/(x+2), away from the exclusions.

    At 444 its value would be 222, so the original has the required hole.

    At −2-2−2 the numerator is 6≠06\ne06=0, leaving the vertical asymptote.

    Answer

    p(x)=x2+4x−32p(x)=x^2+4x-32p(x)=x2+4x−32.
