  1. Problem 1 The partial record

    A real number has floor −4-4−4 and fractional part 0.350.350.35. Find the number.

    1. Hint 1

      The fractional part measures the distance above the floor.

    2. Hint 2

      Add the recorded floor and fractional part.

    Answer

    −3.65-3.65−3.65.

    Full solution

    By definition,

    x−⌊x⌋=0.35.x-\lfloor x\rfloor=0.35.x−⌊x⌋=0.35.

    Substituting the floor gives

    x=−4+0.35x=-4+0.35x=−4+0.35

    Hence x=−3.65x=-3.65x=−3.65.

    This lies between −4-4−4 and −3-3−3, so its floor is indeed −4-4−4, and subtracting that floor leaves 0.350.350.35.

    Answer

    −3.65-3.65−3.65.

    Key idea

    A number is reconstructed by adding its floor and its fractional part.

  2. Problem 2 The average reading

    Find the floor of the average of −6.8-6.8−6.8 and −4.4-4.4−4.4.

    1. Hint 1

      The average must be calculated before the integer rounding.

    2. Hint 2

      Locate the average between neighboring integers, then choose the lower integer.

    Answer

    −6-6−6.

    Full solution

    The average is

    (−6.8)+(−4.4)2=−5.6\frac{(-6.8)+(-4.4)}{2}=-5.62(−6.8)+(−4.4)​=−5.6

    It lies strictly between −6-6−6 and −5-5−5.

    The greatest integer at or below it is therefore

    ⌊−5.6⌋=−6\lfloor-5.6\rfloor=-6⌊−5.6⌋=−6

    Answer

    −6-6−6.

    Key idea

    A floor is taken after the input is fully computed, and it returns the greatest integer at or below that input, which for a negative noninteger lies further from zero.

  3. Problem 3 The upward adjustment

    How much must be added to −2.6-2.6−2.6 to reach its ceiling?

    1. Hint 1

      First locate the least integer at or above the given number.

    2. Hint 2

      The needed increase is that integer minus the original number.

    Answer

    0.60.60.6.

    Full solution

    The ceiling is −2-2−2, since

    −3<−2.6<−2.-3<-2.6<-2.−3<−2.6<−2.

    The required increase is

    −2−(−2.6)=0.6-2-(-2.6)=0.6−2−(−2.6)=0.6

    Adding 0.60.60.6 returns exactly −2-2−2, checking both the direction and amount.

    Answer

    0.60.60.6.

    Key idea

    The increase to a ceiling is the ceiling minus the original input.

  5. Problem 5 The open carton

    There are 737373 items still to pack. An already partly filled carton has room for 333 more items and must be filled first. Every new carton holds 888 items. How many new cartons are needed, and how many further items could arrive without increasing that number of new cartons?

    1. Hint 1

      Remove the items that fit into the existing carton before counting new cartons.

    2. Hint 2

      Once the count is rounded up, compare the space those cartons hold with the number of items being packed.

    Answer

    999 new cartons; 222 further items could arrive without increasing that number.

    Full solution

    Filling the existing carton leaves

    73−3=7073-3=7073−3=70

    items.

    All items need space, so

    ⌈708⌉=9\left\lceil\frac{70}{8}\right\rceil=9⌈870​⌉=9

    new cartons are needed.

    Those nine cartons hold

    9(8)=729(8)=729(8)=72

    items, which is 222 more than the 707070 being packed.

    So two further items still fit, while a third would bring the count to 737373 and force a tenth carton.

    Answer

    999 new cartons; 222 further items could arrive without increasing that number.

    Key idea

    A ceiling counts the containers needed, and the space they hold beyond the contents is the room left before one more container is required.

  6. Problem 6 The two bracket records

    Find all real xxx for which ⌊x+0.4⌋=−2\lfloor x+0.4\rfloor=-2⌊x+0.4⌋=−2 and ⌈x−0.2⌉=−2\lceil x-0.2\rceil=-2⌈x−0.2⌉=−2.

    1. Hint 1

      Translate each bracket record into its complete interval of inputs.

    2. Hint 2

      Keep the inclusive and exclusive endpoints when shifting, then take the overlap.

    Answer

    −2.4≤x≤−1.8-2.4\le x\le-1.8−2.4≤x≤−1.8.

    Full solution

    The floor record means −2≤x+0.4<−1-2\le x+0.4<-1−2≤x+0.4<−1, giving

    −2.4≤x<−1.4-2.4\le x<-1.4−2.4≤x<−1.4

    The ceiling record means −3<x−0.2≤−2-3<x-0.2\le-2−3<x−0.2≤−2, giving

    −2.8<x≤−1.8-2.8<x\le-1.8−2.8<x≤−1.8

    Their overlap is

    −2.4≤x≤−1.8.-2.4\le x\le-1.8.−2.4≤x≤−1.8.

    At x=−2.4x=-2.4x=−2.4 the shifted floor input is −2-2−2, and −2.4-2.4−2.4 lies strictly inside −2.8<x≤−1.8-2.8<x\le-1.8−2.8<x≤−1.8; at x=−1.8x=-1.8x=−1.8 the shifted ceiling input is −2-2−2, and −1.8-1.8−1.8 lies strictly inside

    −2.4≤x<−1.4.-2.4\le x<-1.4.−2.4≤x<−1.4.

    So each endpoint satisfies both records.

    Answer

    −2.4≤x≤−1.8-2.4\le x\le-1.8−2.4≤x≤−1.8.

    Key idea

    Several floor and ceiling records constrain an input to the overlap of intervals with carefully preserved endpoint inclusion.

  7. Problem 7 The separate strips

    Three separate strips are 282828 cm, 232323 cm and 191919 cm long. Each is cut into complete 444 cm pieces, and leftover lengths cannot be joined. The resulting pieces are packed into bags holding at most 777 pieces each. Find the number of complete pieces, total unused length, and number of bags needed.

    1. Hint 1

      Count complete pieces from each strip separately before adding the counts.

    2. Hint 2

      After the cutting count is known, round upward for the bags needed to hold all pieces.

    Answer

    161616 pieces; 666 cm unused; 333 bags.

    Full solution

    The strips supply ⌊28/4⌋=7\lfloor28/4\rfloor=7⌊28/4⌋=7, ⌊23/4⌋=5\lfloor23/4\rfloor=5⌊23/4⌋=5 and ⌊19/4⌋=4\lfloor19/4\rfloor=4⌊19/4⌋=4 pieces, so

    7+5+4=167+5+4=167+5+4=16

    pieces are made.

    Their leftovers are 000 cm, 333 cm and 333 cm, so

    0+3+3=60+3+3=60+3+3=6

    cm remains unused.

    Two bags hold 141414 pieces, so one further bag is needed for the last two.

    Equivalently,

    ⌈167⌉=3\left\lceil\frac{16}{7}\right\rceil=3⌈716​⌉=3

    bags.

    Answer

    161616 pieces; 666 cm unused; 333 bags.

    Key idea

    Lengths that cannot be joined force a separate rounding down on each strip, and only the pieces that result are grouped by a ceiling.

  8. Problem 8 Two floors added

    Zoe claims ⌊u⌋+⌊v⌋=⌊u+v⌋\lfloor u\rfloor+\lfloor v\rfloor=\lfloor u+v\rfloor⌊u⌋+⌊v⌋=⌊u+v⌋ for every pair of real inputs. Decide whether she is correct and justify your decision.

    1. Hint 1

      Adding fractional parts may carry into the next integer step.

    2. Hint 2

      Write each input as its floor plus its fractional part, then try a negative noninteger pair whose fractional parts add to at least 111.

    Answer

    No; u=v=−0.4u=v=-0.4u=v=−0.4 give left side −2-2−2 and right side −1-1−1.

    Full solution

    Take u=v=−0.4u=v=-0.4u=v=−0.4.

    Each has floor −1-1−1, so the left side is

    −1+(−1)=−2-1+(-1)=-2−1+(−1)=−2

    The sum is −0.8-0.8−0.8, which has floor −1-1−1.

    Thus the two sides differ, so the claim fails.

    Answer

    No; u=v=−0.4u=v=-0.4u=v=−0.4 give left side −2-2−2 and right side −1-1−1.

    Key idea

    Rounding before addition can differ from rounding the completed sum.

  9. Problem 9 The whole-number shift

    For every real xxx and every integer kkk, Eli claims ⌊x+k⌋=⌊x⌋+k\lfloor x+k\rfloor=\lfloor x\rfloor+k⌊x+k⌋=⌊x⌋+k. Is he correct? Justify your decision.

    1. Hint 1

      An integer shift moves both neighboring integer bounds by the same amount.

    2. Hint 2

      Name n=⌊x⌋n=\lfloor x\rfloorn=⌊x⌋ and add kkk to the inequality defining that floor.

    Answer

    Yes, for every real xxx and integer kkk.

    Full solution

    Let n=⌊x⌋n=\lfloor x\rfloorn=⌊x⌋, so

    n≤x<n+1.n\le x<n+1.n≤x<n+1.

    Adding the integer kkk gives

    n+k≤x+k<n+k+1n+k\le x+k<n+k+1n+k≤x+k<n+k+1

    The lower bound n+kn+kn+k is an integer, so it is exactly the floor of x+kx+kx+k.

    Therefore ⌊x+k⌋=n+k\lfloor x+k\rfloor=n+k⌊x+k⌋=n+k, proving the claim.

    Answer

    Yes, for every real xxx and integer kkk.

    Key idea

    An integer shift moves a number and its floor together without changing their separation.

  10. Problem 10 The doubled reading

    A real number xxx has fractional part 0.60.60.6. Arun says 2x2x2x must have fractional part 1.21.21.2. Decide whether he is right and find the fractional part of 2x2x2x.

    1. Hint 1

      A fractional part is at least zero and less than one.

    2. Hint 2

      Write xxx as an integer plus 0.60.60.6 and see whether doubling crosses another integer.

    Answer

    Arun is wrong; the fractional part is 0.20.20.2.

    Full solution

    Write x=n+0.6x=n+0.6x=n+0.6, where nnn is an integer.

    Then

    2x=2n+1.22x=2n+1.22x=2n+1.2

    This is the integer 2n+12n+12n+1 plus 0.20.20.2.

    Thus the floor of 2x2x2x is 2n+12n+12n+1, and its fractional part is 0.20.20.2.

    The extra whole unit in 1.21.21.2 belongs to the floor, not the fractional part.

    Answer

    Arun is wrong; the fractional part is 0.20.20.2.

    Key idea

    Scaling a fractional part may create a whole-number contribution that must be removed to find the new fractional part.
