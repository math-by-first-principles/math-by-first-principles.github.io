  1. Problem 1 Bars alone first

    Solve 4∣x−7∣−5=154|x-7|-5=154∣x−7∣−5=15, and check each value in the original equation.

    1. Hint 1

      The bars are not alone yet, so the two-case rule does not apply to the equation as written.

    2. Hint 2

      Undo the subtraction and then the multiplication, so that the absolute value stands by itself.

    3. Hint 3

      Once the right side is a positive number, the inside equals that number or its opposite.

    Answer

    x=2x=2x=2 or x=12x=12x=12.

    Full solution

    Add 555 to both sides and divide by 444, which leaves the bars alone:

    ∣x−7∣=5|x-7|=5∣x−7∣=5

    The right side is positive, so the inside is 555 or −5-5−5.

    One case is

    x−7=5x=12\begin{aligned}x-7&=5\\x&=12\end{aligned}x−7x​=5=12​

    The other case is

    x−7=−5x=2\begin{aligned}x-7&=-5\\x&=2\end{aligned}x−7x​=−5=2​

    Substituting x=12x=12x=12 gives 4∣12−7∣−5=154|12-7|-5=154∣12−7∣−5=15, and substituting x=2x=2x=2 gives 4∣2−7∣−5=154|2-7|-5=154∣2−7∣−5=15, so both values satisfy the original equation.

    Answer

    x=2x=2x=2 or x=12x=12x=12.

    Key idea

    Isolate the absolute value before splitting into cases, since splitting first solves a different equation.

  2. Problem 2 The moving labels

    Two labels lie at 2a+12a+12a+1 and 1−2a1-2a1−2a on a number line, where aaa is real. Write their distance as a constant multiple of ∣a∣|a|∣a∣.

    1. Hint 1

      Distance is the absolute value of the difference of the coordinates.

    2. Hint 2

      Simplify that difference before taking its nonnegative size.

    Answer

    4∣a∣4|a|4∣a∣.

    Full solution

    The difference of the coordinates is

    (2a+1)−(1−2a)=4a(2a+1)-(1-2a)=4a(2a+1)−(1−2a)=4a

    Its absolute value is ∣4a∣|4a|∣4a∣.

    Multiplication by the positive number 444 scales distance by 444, so

    ∣4a∣=4∣a∣|4a|=4|a|∣4a∣=4∣a∣

    This also gives zero when the labels coincide at a=0a=0a=0.

    Answer

    4∣a∣4|a|4∣a∣.

    Key idea

    The distance between two moving coordinates is found by subtracting their rules before taking the absolute value.

  3. Problem 3 The paired records

    The equation ∣x−h∣=3|x-h|=3∣x−h∣=3 has solutions x=1x=1x=1 and x=7x=7x=7. Find hhh.

    1. Hint 1

      The two solutions are equally far from the center hhh.

    2. Hint 2

      The center lies midway between them.

    Answer

    h=4h=4h=4.

    Full solution

    The center is the average of the two locations:

    h=1+72h=\frac{1+7}{2}h=21+7​

    Thus h=4h=4h=4.

    Checking, both ∣1−4∣|1-4|∣1−4∣ and ∣7−4∣|7-4|∣7−4∣ equal 333, as required.

    Answer

    h=4h=4h=4.

    Key idea

    The two solutions of a positive-distance equation lie equally far on opposite sides of its center.

  4. Problem 4 The two requirements

    Find all real numbers satisfying both ∣x−2∣<4|x-2|<4∣x−2∣<4 and ∣x+1∣>2|x+1|>2∣x+1∣>2.

    1. Hint 1

      Each requirement gives an allowed region, and the input must belong to both.

    2. Hint 2

      The first gives a band; the second gives two outside rays.

    Answer

    1<x<61<x<61<x<6.

    Full solution

    The first inequality gives

    −2<x<6-2<x<6−2<x<6

    The second gives x<−3x<-3x<−3 or x>1x>1x>1.

    The ray x<−3x<-3x<−3 does not meet the band.

    Intersecting the other ray with the band leaves

    1<x<61<x<61<x<6

    Both ends are excluded because one original inequality becomes equality there.

    Answer

    1<x<61<x<61<x<6.

    Key idea

    Simultaneous absolute-value inequalities require the overlap of their bands and rays.

  6. Problem 6 The allowed positions

    A marker has coordinate xxx on a number line. It must stay at least 222 units and at most 444 units from the location 111. On the axes in the figure, sketch the graph whose height is the distance from the marker to 111, then find all allowed coordinates.

    Text description of this figure

    Blank coordinate axes with equal unit lengths on both axes and a light square grid. The horizontal axis is labeled x and runs from negative 5 to 7. The vertical axis is labeled distance from 1, in units, and runs from negative 1 to 7. Both axes carry tick marks and number labels at every whole number, and the origin is labeled 0. Nothing is plotted: there is no graph, no marked point, no guide line, no highlighted tick and no shaded region.

    1. Hint 1

      Express the distance from location 111 as a nonnegative function of xxx.

    2. Hint 2

      Draw its two branches from the point where that distance is zero, then keep the inputs whose graph heights lie between 222 and 444, including both heights.

    Answer

    Graph y=∣x−1∣y=|x-1|y=∣x−1∣, a V with vertex (1,0)(1,0)(1,0); allowed coordinates [−3,−1]∪[3,5][-3,-1]\cup[3,5][−3,−1]∪[3,5].

    Full solution

    Distance from 111 is

    y=∣x−1∣y=|x-1|y=∣x−1∣

    Plot the vertex (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) and draw two straight branches rising away from it, with slope −1-1−1 on the left and 111 on the right.

    Points (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) and (2,1)(2,1)(2,1) check the branches.

    The upper distance bound gives

    −3≤x≤5.-3\le x\le5.−3≤x≤5.

    The lower bound gives x≤−1x\le-1x≤−1 or x≥3x\ge3x≥3.

    Their overlap is

    [−3,−1]∪[3,5][-3,-1]\cup[3,5][−3,−1]∪[3,5]

    These are the inputs where the V lies between heights 222 and 444.

    Each of the four interval endpoints gives one of those allowed heights, so all endpoints are included.

    Answer

    Graph y=∣x−1∣y=|x-1|y=∣x−1∣, a V with vertex (1,0)(1,0)(1,0); allowed coordinates [−3,−1]∪[3,5][-3,-1]\cup[3,5][−3,−1]∪[3,5].

    Key idea

    Graphing distance from a fixed location gives a V whose heights select the allowed positions.

  7. Problem 7 The stored condition

    A real input satisfies ∣4x+6∣=10|4x+6|=10∣4x+6∣=10. Find every possible value of ∣x+9∣|x+9|∣x+9∣.

    1. Hint 1

      First recover every input allowed by the stored condition.

    2. Hint 2

      Carry both signed cases into the new expression.

    Answer

    555 or 101010.

    Full solution

    The inside of the given absolute value is either 101010 or −10-10−10.

    One case is

    4x+6=10x=1\begin{aligned}4x+6&=10\\x&=1\end{aligned}4x+6x​=10=1​

    The other case is

    4x+6=−10x=−4\begin{aligned}4x+6&=-10\\x&=-4\end{aligned}4x+6x​=−10=−4​

    The new expression gives ∣1+9∣=10|1+9|=10∣1+9∣=10 or ∣−4+9∣=5|-4+9|=5∣−4+9∣=5.

    Both inputs satisfy the original condition, so both outputs are possible.

    Answer

    555 or 101010.

    Key idea

    A condition with two signed cases can lead to two different values of a later expression.

  8. Problem 8 The opposite readings

    Two nonzero numbers uuu and vvv have opposite signs. Kai claims that ∣u+v∣|u+v|∣u+v∣ equals the distance between ∣u∣|u|∣u∣ and ∣v∣|v|∣v∣. Is Kai correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      Adding numbers of opposite signs subtracts their sizes.

    2. Hint 2

      Name the positive sizes AAA and BBB, then work out what the sum is in each of the two sign arrangements.

    Answer

    Yes; ∣u+v∣=∣∣u∣−∣v∣∣|u+v|=\big||u|-|v|\big|∣u+v∣=​∣u∣−∣v∣​.

    Full solution

    Write the positive sizes as A=∣u∣A=|u|A=∣u∣ and B=∣v∣B=|v|B=∣v∣.

    Since the signs are opposite, their sum is either A−BA-BA−B or B−AB-AB−A.

    These two differences are opposites and have the same absolute value, so

    ∣u+v∣=∣A−B∣|u+v|=|A-B|∣u+v∣=∣A−B∣

    The right side is exactly the distance between the two sizes.

    Kai is correct.

    Answer

    Yes; ∣u+v∣=∣∣u∣−∣v∣∣|u+v|=\big||u|-|v|\big|∣u+v∣=​∣u∣−∣v∣​.

    Key idea

    For opposite signs, the size of the sum is the distance between the two input sizes.

  9. Problem 9 Nora's comparison

    Nora says ∣x−2∣|x-2|∣x−2∣ and ∣x∣−2|x|-2∣x∣−2 agree only at x=2x=2x=2. Decide whether she is right and find every input where they agree.

    1. Hint 1

      The signs of xxx and x−2x-2x−2 split the line into three regions.

    2. Hint 2

      On each region, rewrite both expressions without bars, then set the two results equal and keep only solutions lying in that region.

    Answer

    Nora is wrong; they agree for every x≥2x\ge2x≥2.

    Full solution

    For x<0x<0x<0, the expressions are 2−x2-x2−x and −x−2-x-2−x−2, which differ by 444.

    For 0≤x<20\le x<20≤x<2, equality would require

    2−x=x−22-x=x-22−x=x−2

    giving x=2x=2x=2, outside that region.

    For x≥2x\ge2x≥2, both expressions are exactly x−2x-2x−2.

    Thus they agree throughout that ray, including the boundary 222.

    Answer

    Nora is wrong; they agree for every x≥2x\ge2x≥2.

    Key idea

    Comparing shifted absolute values requires accounting for each interval where the inside signs are fixed.

  10. Problem 10 Elena's comparison

    For a real constant ccc, the equation 3∣x−1∣+c=03|x-1|+c=03∣x−1∣+c=0 has two distinct real solutions. Elena claims that replacing ccc by 2c2c2c doubles the distance between the solutions and leaves their midpoint unchanged. Decide whether each claim is correct and justify your decisions.

    1. Hint 1

      Two distinct solutions force the isolated absolute value to be positive.

    2. Hint 2

      Name the original distance from 111 and compare it with the distance after the constant changes.

    3. Hint 3

      Write each pair as its center plus or minus its distance, then compare the two separations.

    Answer

    Both claims are correct; the distance doubles and the midpoint remains 111.

    Full solution

    Two distinct solutions require c<0c<0c<0.

    Set d=−c/3d=-c/3d=−c/3, so d>0d>0d>0.

    The original solutions are 1−d1-d1−d and 1+d1+d1+d, with distance

    (1+d)−(1−d)=2d(1+d)-(1-d)=2d(1+d)−(1−d)=2d

    Their midpoint is 111.

    Replacing ccc by 2c2c2c gives

    ∣x−1∣=2d|x-1|=2d∣x−1∣=2d

    The new solutions are 1−2d1-2d1−2d and 1+2d1+2d1+2d.

    Their distance is 4d4d4d, twice the original distance, and their midpoint is still 111.

    Both claims are therefore correct, and the new solutions remain distinct since d>0d>0d>0.

    Answer

    Both claims are correct; the distance doubles and the midpoint remains 111.

    Key idea

    Scaling the positive distance in an absolute-value equation changes the separation of its solutions while preserving their center.
