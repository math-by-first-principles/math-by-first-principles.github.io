  1. Problem 1 The function machine

    Let f(x)=x+2f(x)=x+2f(x)=x+2 for x<0x<0x<0, and f(x)=3−xf(x)=3-xf(x)=3−x for x≥0x\ge0x≥0. Find f(f(−1))f(f(-1))f(f(−1)).

    1. Hint 1

      The first output becomes the input for the second application.

    2. Hint 2

      Choose a piece separately for each input rather than keeping the first choice.

    Answer

    222.

    Full solution

    The input −1-1−1 is negative, so

    f(−1)=−1+2f(-1)=-1+2f(−1)=−1+2

    giving 111.

    That output is nonnegative, so the other piece applies next:

    f(1)=3−1f(1)=3-1f(1)=3−1

    Thus the requested value is 222.

    Answer

    222.

    Key idea

    Each application of a piecewise function chooses its piece from its own current input.

  2. Problem 2 The hidden threshold

    A function fff returns 555 when x<tx<tx<t and −1-1−1 when x≥tx\ge tx≥t, where ttt is real. The records say f(2)=5f(2)=5f(2)=5 and f(3)=−1f(3)=-1f(3)=−1. Find all possible ttt.

    1. Hint 1

      Each recorded output tells you which condition its input satisfies.

    2. Hint 2

      The input 222 must lie below the threshold, while 333 must be at or above it.

    Answer

    2<t≤32<t\le32<t≤3.

    Full solution

    The output 555 at input 222 requires 2<t2<t2<t.

    The output −1-1−1 at input 333 requires 3≥t3\ge t3≥t.

    Together the conditions give

    2<t≤32<t\le32<t≤3

    Every threshold in this interval assigns both inputs their recorded outputs.

    Answer

    2<t≤32<t\le32<t≤3.

    Key idea

    Recorded outputs of constant pieces can constrain an unknown switching threshold.

  3. Problem 3 Which piece owns the input

    Let f(x)=−x−1f(x)=-x-1f(x)=−x−1 for x≤−3x\le-3x≤−3, let f(x)=x2−2f(x)=x^2-2f(x)=x2−2 for −3<x≤1-3<x\le1−3<x≤1, and let f(x)=2x+3f(x)=2x+3f(x)=2x+3 for x>1x>1x>1. Find f(−5)f(-5)f(−5), f(−3)f(-3)f(−3), f(0)f(0)f(0), f(1)f(1)f(1), and f(4)f(4)f(4).

    1. Hint 1

      Settle which condition an input satisfies before substituting it into any formula.

    2. Hint 2

      At each boundary, exactly one condition carries an equality sign, and that piece owns the input.

    Answer

    f(−5)=4f(-5)=4f(−5)=4, f(−3)=2f(-3)=2f(−3)=2, f(0)=−2f(0)=-2f(0)=−2, f(1)=−1f(1)=-1f(1)=−1, f(4)=11f(4)=11f(4)=11.

    Full solution

    Both −5-5−5 and −3-3−3 satisfy x≤−3x\le-3x≤−3, so the first piece −x−1-x-1−x−1 applies to each of them.

    Negating the input turns −5-5−5 into 555 and −3-3−3 into 333, so the outputs are 5−15-15−1 and 3−13-13−1:

    f(−5)=4f(−3)=2\begin{aligned}f(-5)&=4\\f(-3)&=2\end{aligned}f(−5)f(−3)​=4=2​

    Both 000 and 111 satisfy −3<x≤1-3<x\le1−3<x≤1, so the middle piece x2−2x^2-2x2−2 applies.

    Their squares are 000 and 111, so the outputs are 0−20-20−2 and 1−21-21−2:

    f(0)=−2f(1)=−1\begin{aligned}f(0)&=-2\\f(1)&=-1\end{aligned}f(0)f(1)​=−2=−1​

    The boundary 111 lands here because this condition includes it, while the boundary −3-3−3 went to the first piece for the same reason.

    The input 444 satisfies x>1x>1x>1, so the last piece gives 2(4)+32(4)+32(4)+3, that is

    f(4)=11f(4)=11f(4)=11

    Answer

    f(−5)=4f(-5)=4f(−5)=4, f(−3)=2f(-3)=2f(−3)=2, f(0)=−2f(0)=-2f(0)=−2, f(1)=−1f(1)=-1f(1)=−1, f(4)=11f(4)=11f(4)=11.

    Key idea

    Reading the condition first, and noticing which side of a boundary carries the equality sign, decides every value of a piecewise function.

  5. Problem 5 The three segments

    The figure shows all of a piecewise function. Write its rule with one formula for each drawn segment, and state its domain and range.

    Text description of this figure

    A square coordinate grid with equal unit lengths on both axes, gridlines, tick marks and number labels at every whole number, the horizontal x-axis running from negative five to five and the vertical y-axis from negative two to six, the origin labeled 0 and arrowheads on both ends of each axis. The third rises from that same filled dot at the point (2, 3) up to a filled dot at the point (4, 5). Each of those five endpoints is labeled with its coordinates. Nothing else is drawn: no formulas, no domain or range labels and no graph outside these three segments.

    1. Hint 1

      Each straight segment is fixed by its two labeled endpoints: get its slope from them, then its rule.

    2. Hint 2

      Combine the input intervals for the domain and the attained heights for the range.

    Answer

    f(x)=−x−2f(x)=-x-2f(x)=−x−2 on [−4,−1)[-4,-1)[−4,−1); f(x)=3f(x)=3f(x)=3 on [−1,2][-1,2][−1,2]; f(x)=x+1f(x)=x+1f(x)=x+1 on (2,4](2,4](2,4], or equivalently f(x)=3f(x)=3f(x)=3 on [−1,2)[-1,2)[−1,2) and f(x)=x+1f(x)=x+1f(x)=x+1 on [2,4][2,4][2,4]. Domain [−4,4][-4,4][−4,4]; range (−1,2]∪[3,5](-1,2]\cup[3,5](−1,2]∪[3,5].

    Full solution

    The sloping left segment passes through (−4,2)(-4,2)(−4,2) and approaches (−1,−1)(-1,-1)(−1,−1), so its slope is −1-1−1 and its rule is −x−2-x-2−x−2 on

    −4≤x<−1.-4\le x<-1.−4≤x<−1.

    Its heights satisfy −1<y≤2-1<y\le2−1<y≤2.

    The middle segment is y=3y=3y=3 on

    −1≤x≤2.-1\le x\le2.−1≤x≤2.

    The right segment has slope 111 and rule x+1x+1x+1 on 2<x≤42<x\le42<x≤4, reaching heights 3<y≤53<y\le53<y≤5.

    The conditions cover

    −4≤x≤4-4\le x\le4−4≤x≤4

    Combining the attained heights gives

    (−1,2]∪[3,5](-1,2]\cup[3,5](−1,2]∪[3,5]

    The height 333 is included by the middle piece.

    The point (2,3)(2,3)(2,3) may instead be assigned to the right piece and excluded from the middle piece; that choice gives the same function.

    Answer

    f(x)=−x−2f(x)=-x-2f(x)=−x−2 on [−4,−1)[-4,-1)[−4,−1); f(x)=3f(x)=3f(x)=3 on [−1,2][-1,2][−1,2]; f(x)=x+1f(x)=x+1f(x)=x+1 on (2,4](2,4](2,4], or equivalently f(x)=3f(x)=3f(x)=3 on [−1,2)[-1,2)[−1,2) and f(x)=x+1f(x)=x+1f(x)=x+1 on [2,4][2,4][2,4]. Domain [−4,4][-4,4][−4,4]; range (−1,2]∪[3,5](-1,2]\cup[3,5](−1,2]∪[3,5].

    Key idea

    A piecewise graph supplies separate equations and endpoint conditions whose output ranges must be combined.

  6. Problem 6 The limited display

    A display takes any real reading xxx. It reports −2-2−2 if x<−2x<-2x<−2, reports 333 if x>3x>3x>3, and otherwise reports the reading unchanged. Write a piecewise rule, find the displayed values for inputs −5-5−5, 111, and 444, and state the range.

    1. Hint 1

      The unchanged region includes the two boundary readings.

    2. Hint 2

      Use a separate condition for each constant output and for the middle identity rule.

    Answer

    f(x)=−2f(x)=-2f(x)=−2 if x<−2x<-2x<−2, xxx if −2≤x≤3-2\le x\le3−2≤x≤3, and 333 if x>3x>3x>3; outputs −2,1,3-2,1,3−2,1,3; range [−2,3][-2,3][−2,3].

    Full solution

    The instructions translate directly into three mutually exclusive conditions: x<−2x<-2x<−2 gives −2-2−2, −2≤x≤3-2\le x\le3−2≤x≤3 gives xxx, and x>3x>3x>3 gives 333.

    The three requested inputs belong to the left, middle, and right regions respectively, so

    f(−5)=−2f(1)=1f(4)=3\begin{aligned}f(-5)&=-2\\f(1)&=1\\f(4)&=3\end{aligned}f(−5)f(1)f(4)​=−2=1=3​

    The middle piece reaches every value from −2-2−2 to 333, including both ends.

    The outer pieces add no further heights, so the range is [−2,3][-2,3][−2,3].

    Answer

    f(x)=−2f(x)=-2f(x)=−2 if x<−2x<-2x<−2, xxx if −2≤x≤3-2\le x\le3−2≤x≤3, and 333 if x>3x>3x>3; outputs −2,1,3-2,1,3−2,1,3; range [−2,3][-2,3][−2,3].

    Key idea

    A display that caps extreme readings is described by constant outer pieces and an unchanged middle piece.

  7. Problem 7 The step labels

    A function equals −2-2−2 on −2<x≤−1-2<x\le-1−2<x≤−1, −1-1−1 on −1<x≤0-1<x\le0−1<x≤0, 000 on 0<x≤10<x\le10<x≤1, and 111 on 1<x≤21<x\le21<x≤2. Write one formula using a ceiling and state its domain. Explain how its endpoint ownership agrees with that formula.

    1. Hint 1

      Each interval includes its right integer endpoint and excludes its left one.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare each output with the ceiling of an input in its interval.

    Answer

    f(x)=⌈x⌉−1f(x)=\lceil x\rceil-1f(x)=⌈x⌉−1; domain −2<x≤2-2<x\le2−2<x≤2.

    Full solution

    On the four intervals, the ceiling values are −1-1−1, 000, 111, and 222.

    Subtracting 111 gives exactly the four specified outputs.

    Thus

    f(x)=⌈x⌉−1f(x)=\lceil x\rceil-1f(x)=⌈x⌉−1

    on −2<x≤2-2<x\le2−2<x≤2.

    Ceiling pieces own their right endpoints, matching every stated interval, and the domain must remain restricted as given.

    Answer

    f(x)=⌈x⌉−1f(x)=\lceil x\rceil-1f(x)=⌈x⌉−1; domain −2<x≤2-2<x\le2−2<x≤2.

    Key idea

    A finite piecewise staircase can match a shifted ceiling on its stated domain.

  8. Problem 8 The alternative rule

    Let g(x)=x−1g(x)=x-1g(x)=x−1 for x≥1x\ge1x≥1, and g(x)=1−xg(x)=1-xg(x)=1−x for x<1x<1x<1. Tariq claims that g(x)=∣x∣−1g(x)=|x|-1g(x)=∣x∣−1 for every real input. Is he correct? Explain.

    No; at x=0x=0x=0 the pieces give 111, while ∣0∣−1=−1|0|-1=-1∣0∣−1=−1.

    Full solution

    The input 000 satisfies x<1x<1x<1, so the second piece applies and

    g(0)=1g(0)=1g(0)=1

    The claimed rule gives ∣0∣−1=−1|0|-1=-1∣0∣−1=−1 at that same input.

    One disagreeing input settles the question, so the claim is false.

    The pieces keep x−1x-1x−1 when it is nonnegative and negate it when it is negative, so g(x)=∣x−1∣g(x)=|x-1|g(x)=∣x−1∣, the distance from xxx to 111.

    The claimed ∣x∣−1|x|-1∣x∣−1 instead measures the distance from xxx to 000 and then lowers every height by one.

    No; at x=0x=0x=0 the pieces give 111, while ∣0∣−1=−1|0|-1=-1∣0∣−1=−1.

    The number inside absolute value bars marks where the two sign pieces switch, and is not a change made to the outputs.

  9. Problem 9 Ben's adjustment

    A function agrees with ⌊x⌋\lfloor x\rfloor⌊x⌋ at every real input except 000, where its value is changed to −1/2-1/2−1/2. Ben claims this change joins the two floor steps on either side of 000. Is he correct? Explain using the nearby pieces.

    1. Hint 1

      Ask what heights the graph actually takes just to the left and just to the right of zero.

    2. Hint 2

      A step is a whole horizontal piece rather than a single point, so ask what one point at x=0x=0x=0 can and cannot fill.

    Answer

    No; the neighboring step heights remain −1-1−1 and 000.

    Full solution

    On −1≤x<0-1\le x<0−1≤x<0, the unchanged rule gives −1-1−1.

    On 0<x<10<x<10<x<1, it gives 000.

    These different heights remain separated, regardless of the assigned value at the single input 000.

    The new point is

    (0,−1/2)(0,-1/2)(0,−1/2)

    It lies on neither neighboring horizontal step, so the graph still does not connect at zero.

    Answer

    No; the neighboring step heights remain −1-1−1 and 000.

    Key idea

    Changing one boundary output does not join neighboring constant pieces that approach different heights.

  10. Problem 10 The studio rental fee

    A studio charges a flat bbb dollars for any rental of up to and including 333 hours, and 4h−34h-34h−3 dollars for a rental of hhh hours when h>3h>3h>3, where bbb is a positive constant and h>0h>0h>0. Find the value of bbb that makes the cost graph connect at h=3h=3h=3, and describe what happens to the cost at 333 hours if the studio sets b=10b=10b=10 instead.

    1. Hint 1

      Connecting at a boundary means the two pieces settle on the same height there.

    2. Hint 2

      Work out the height the second formula approaches at 333 hours, then compare it with the flat fee.

    Answer

    b=9b=9b=9. With b=10b=10b=10 the cost is 101010 dollars at exactly 333 hours but just above 999 dollars for slightly longer rentals, a drop of about a dollar.

    Full solution

    The flat piece owns every rental time up to and including 333 hours, so its height at the boundary is bbb dollars.

    The second formula approaches

    4(3)−3=94(3)-3=94(3)−3=9

    as the rental time comes down toward 333 hours.

    The two heights agree exactly when

    b=9b=9b=9

    so that fee joins the pieces and the cost graph connects at 333 hours.

    With b=10b=10b=10 there is a closed dot at (3,10)(3,10)(3,10) and an open dot at (3,9)(3,9)(3,9), so the cost drops by about a dollar just past 333 hours.

    A customer who books a few minutes over 333 hours then pays less than one who books exactly 333 hours.

    Answer

    b=9b=9b=9. With b=10b=10b=10 the cost is 101010 dollars at exactly 333 hours but just above 999 dollars for slightly longer rentals, a drop of about a dollar.

    Key idea

    Matching the two pieces at the threshold fixes the constant in a tiered price, and any other value leaves a step at the boundary.
