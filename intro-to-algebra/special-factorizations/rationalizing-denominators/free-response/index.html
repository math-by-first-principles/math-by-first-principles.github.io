  1. Problem 1 A single root in the way

    Rationalize and simplify 414\frac4{\sqrt{14}}14​4​, so that no radical is left in the denominator.

    1. Hint 1

      Multiplying a fraction by a nonzero quantity divided by itself changes its form but not its value.

    2. Hint 2

      A square root multiplied by itself gives back the number under it, so use 14\sqrt{14}14​ on both the numerator and the denominator, then look for a common factor.

    Answer

    2147\frac{2\sqrt{14}}77214​​.

    Full solution

    Multiply the numerator and the denominator by the nonzero number 14\sqrt{14}14​, which is multiplication by one.

    The denominator becomes 141414 and the numerator becomes 4144\sqrt{14}414​, so

    414=41414.\frac4{\sqrt{14}}=\frac{4\sqrt{14}}{14}.14​4​=14414​​.

    The numbers 444 and 141414 share a factor of 222, so the fraction reduces to 2147\frac{2\sqrt{14}}77214​​.

    As a check, 4/14≈1.0694/\sqrt{14}\approx1.0694/14​≈1.069 and 214/7≈1.0692\sqrt{14}/7\approx1.069214​/7≈1.069.

    Answer

    2147\frac{2\sqrt{14}}77214​​.

    Key idea

    Multiplying by a root over itself clears a single root from a denominator, and the result may still reduce.

    Full solution

  3. Problem 3 A denominator with two roots

    Write 210−7\frac2{\sqrt{10}-\sqrt7}10​−7​2​ in simplest exact form with no radical in its denominator.

    1. Hint 1

      Multiplying by just one of these roots still leaves a root in the denominator, because 10\sqrt{10}10​ and 7\sqrt77​ do not simplify to the same root.

    2. Hint 2

      Pair the denominator with the same two roots joined by the opposite sign. Their product is a difference of squares, and the numerator must be multiplied by that same expression.

    Answer

    2(10+7)3\frac{2(\sqrt{10}+\sqrt7)}332(10​+7​)​, or equivalently 210+273\frac{2\sqrt{10}+2\sqrt7}33210​+27​​.

    Full solution

    The conjugate of 10−7\sqrt{10}-\sqrt710​−7​ is 10+7\sqrt{10}+\sqrt710​+7​, which is nonzero because both roots are positive.

    Multiplying the numerator and the denominator by it makes the denominator a difference of squares,

    10−7=3.10-7=3.10−7=3.

    The numerator becomes 2(10+7)2(\sqrt{10}+\sqrt7)2(10​+7​), whose coefficient 222 shares no factor above 111 with 333, so the fraction does not reduce, and the result is 2(10+7)3\frac{2(\sqrt{10}+\sqrt7)}332(10​+7​)​.

    As a check, 2/(10−7)≈3.8722/(\sqrt{10}-\sqrt7)\approx3.8722/(10​−7​)≈3.872 and 2(10+7)/3≈3.8722(\sqrt{10}+\sqrt7)/3\approx3.8722(10​+7​)/3≈3.872.

    Answer

    2(10+7)3\frac{2(\sqrt{10}+\sqrt7)}332(10​+7​)​, or equivalently 210+273\frac{2\sqrt{10}+2\sqrt7}33210​+27​​.

    Key idea

    The conjugate turns a two-root denominator into the plain difference of the two radicands.

  4. Problem 4 Combined measurements

    Two lengths are 420\frac4{\sqrt{20}}20​4​ cm and 345\frac3{\sqrt{45}}45​3​ cm. Find their total length in simplest exact form with no radical in a denominator.

    1. Hint 1

      Simplify each radical before adding the lengths.

    2. Hint 2

      Rationalize the reduced fractions so their matching radical terms can be combined.

    Answer

    355\frac{3\sqrt5}5535​​ cm.

    Full solution

    The radicals simplify to 252\sqrt525​ and 353\sqrt535​.

    The lengths become 2/52/\sqrt52/5​ cm and 1/51/\sqrt51/5​ cm.

    Multiplying both parts of each fraction by the nonzero number 5\sqrt55​ gives 25/52\sqrt5/525​/5 cm and 5/5\sqrt5/55​/5 cm.

    The two rationalized lengths add to

    255+55=355.\frac{2\sqrt5}5+\frac{\sqrt5}5=\frac{3\sqrt5}5.525​​+55​​=535​​.

    The total length is therefore 355\frac{3\sqrt5}5535​​ cm.

    Multiplying that total by 5\sqrt55​ gives 333, and multiplying the unrationalized sum 3/53/\sqrt53/5​ by 5\sqrt55​ gives 333 as well, so the two forms agree.

    Answer

    355\frac{3\sqrt5}5535​​ cm.

    Key idea

    Simplifying each radical first turns two unlike fractions into like terms that can be added.

  5. Problem 5 A rectangle from its area

    A rectangle has area 151515 square cm and width 11−5\sqrt{11}-\sqrt511​−5​ cm. Find its perimeter in simplest exact form with no radical in a denominator.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    711+357\sqrt{11}+3\sqrt5711​+35​ cm.

    Full solution

    The width is positive because 11>511>511>5.

    Its conjugate product is

    11−5=6.11-5=6.11−5=6.

    Multiply the numerator and the denominator of 15/(11−5)15/(\sqrt{11}-\sqrt5)15/(11​−5​) by 11+5\sqrt{11}+\sqrt511​+5​.

    The numerator becomes 15(11+5)15(\sqrt{11}+\sqrt5)15(11​+5​), and dividing by the denominator 666 gives a length of 5(11+5)2\frac{5(\sqrt{11}+\sqrt5)}225(11​+5​)​ cm.

    Twice that length is 511+555\sqrt{11}+5\sqrt5511​+55​ cm and twice the width is 211−252\sqrt{11}-2\sqrt5211​−25​ cm, so the perimeter is 711+357\sqrt{11}+3\sqrt5711​+35​ cm.

    Checking the area, the length times the width is 5⋅62=15\frac{5\cdot6}2=1525⋅6​=15 square cm.

    Answer

    711+357\sqrt{11}+3\sqrt5711​+35​ cm.

    Key idea

    Rationalizing an area-to-width quotient can produce an exact perimeter with simplified radical terms.

  7. Problem 7 One less than rrr

    A number rrr is given by r=7+27−2r=\frac{\sqrt7+2}{\sqrt7-2}r=7​−27​+2​. Find r−1r-1r−1 in simplest exact form with a rational denominator.

  8. Problem 8 A formula range

    A student claims that 1n+2=n−2n−4\frac1{\sqrt n+2}=\frac{\sqrt n-2}{n-4}n​+21​=n−4n​−2​ for every positive integer nnn. Decide whether the claim is correct, and state exactly which positive integers allow the equality. Explain what happens at any excluded input.

    Answer

  9. Problem 9 Neighboring radical values

    For every positive integer nnn, Ada claims that 1n+1+n=n+1−n\frac1{\sqrt{n+1}+\sqrt n}=\sqrt{n+1}-\sqrt nn+1​+n​1​=n+1​−n​. Decide whether the claim is correct, including when one of the square roots is an integer.

  10. Problem 10 A claim about the only method

    A student says that 1300−75\frac1{\sqrt{300}-\sqrt{75}}300​−75​1​ can be rationalized only by using a conjugate. Rationalize the expression and decide whether the student is right.

    1. Hint 1

      Look at the two radicands before choosing how to start: each one has a perfect-square factor.

    2. Hint 2

    Full solution

    Simplifying first, 300=103\sqrt{300}=10\sqrt3300​=103​ and 75=53\sqrt{75}=5\sqrt375​=53​, so the denominator is the single root 535\sqrt353​.

    Multiplying both parts by 3\sqrt33​ makes the denominator 5⋅3=155\cdot3=155⋅3=15 and the numerator 3\sqrt33​, so

    153=315.\frac1{5\sqrt3}=\frac{\sqrt3}{15}.53​1​=153​​.

    Multiply both parts of the original fraction by the nonzero conjugate 300+75\sqrt{300}+\sqrt{75}300​+75​.

    The numerator becomes 103+53=15310\sqrt3+5\sqrt3=15\sqrt3103​+53​=153​ and the denominator becomes 300−75=225300-75=225300−75=225, so

    153225=315.\frac{15\sqrt3}{225}=\frac{\sqrt3}{15}.225153​​=153​​.

    Simplifying like radicals may leave a single-root denominator, so a conjugate is not the only way to clear a two-term denominator.
