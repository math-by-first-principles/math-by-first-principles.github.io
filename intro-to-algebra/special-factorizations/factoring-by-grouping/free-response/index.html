  1. Problem 1 A cubic in four terms

    Factor 3x3−21x2−5x+353x^3-21x^2-5x+353x3−21x2−5x+35 by grouping, and check your factorization by expanding it.

    1. Hint 1

      Grouping only finishes when both pairs leave the same binomial behind, so watch what each pair leaves.

    2. Hint 2

      The second pair leads with a negative term; choose its common factor so that its binomial matches the first pair's.

    Answer

    (x−7)(3x2−5)(x-7)(3x^2-5)(x−7)(3x2−5).

    Full solution

    Pair the first two terms and the last two terms.

    The first pair gives 3x2(x−7)3x^2(x-7)3x2(x−7).

    In the second pair, taking out +5+5+5 would leave 5(7−x)5(7-x)5(7−x), the opposite of x−7x-7x−7, so take out −5-5−5 instead and get −5(x−7)-5(x-7)−5(x−7).

    Both pairs now carry x−7x-7x−7, so that binomial factors out:

    (x−7)(3x2−5).(x-7)(3x^2-5).(x−7)(3x2−5).

    Expanding gives 3x3−5x−21x2+353x^3-5x-21x^2+353x3−5x−21x2+35, which reorders to the original polynomial.

    The factor 3x2−53x^2-53x2−5 has no factorization with integer coefficients, since neither 333 nor 555 is a perfect square, so this is complete.

    Answer

    (x−7)(3x2−5)(x-7)(3x^2-5)(x−7)(3x2−5).

    Key idea

    A pair that leads with a negative term needs a negative common factor so its binomial matches the other pair's.

  2. Problem 2 A shared binomial in letters

    Write A(2x−5)+B(5−2x)A(2x-5)+B(5-2x)A(2x−5)+B(5−2x) as a product of two binomials for all real A,B,xA,B,xA,B,x.

    1. Hint 1

      Nothing can be pulled out until both groups carry the same binomial, so compare the two parenthesized expressions first.

    2. Hint 2

      Factor a negative from the reversed expression before extracting the common binomial.

    Answer

    (A−B)(2x−5)(A-B)(2x-5)(A−B)(2x−5).

    Full solution

    The reversal gives

    5−2x=−(2x−5).5-2x=-(2x-5).5−2x=−(2x−5).

    Thus the expression becomes A(2x−5)−B(2x−5)A(2x-5)-B(2x-5)A(2x−5)−B(2x−5).

    The common binomial factors out to give

    (A−B)(2x−5).(A-B)(2x-5).(A−B)(2x−5).

    Distributing it reproduces both original groups with their signs intact.

    Answer

    (A−B)(2x−5)(A-B)(2x-5)(A−B)(2x−5).

    Key idea

    Opposite binomials become a shared factor when one group contributes a negative multiplier.

  3. Problem 3 A harder trinomial

    Factor 10x2−9x−710x^2-9x-710x2−9x−7 by the AC method, and check your factorization by expanding it.

    1. Hint 1

      The middle coefficient splits into two pieces whose product is acacac, the first coefficient times the constant.

    2. Hint 2

      Look for two integers with product −70-70−70 and sum −9-9−9, then split the middle term and group the four terms.

    3. Hint 3

      When a pair factors down to a bare binomial, keep the 111 in front of it; it becomes a coefficient in the answer.

    Answer

    (5x−7)(2x+1)(5x-7)(2x+1)(5x−7)(2x+1).

    Full solution

    Here a=10a=10a=10, b=−9b=-9b=−9 and c=−7c=-7c=−7, so the product to aim for is ac=−70ac=-70ac=−70.

    Two integers with product −70-70−70 and sum −9-9−9 are −14-14−14 and 555.

    Split the middle term into −14x+5x-14x+5x−14x+5x:

    10x2−14x+5x−7.10x^2-14x+5x-7.10x2−14x+5x−7.

    The first pair gives 2x(5x−7)2x(5x-7)2x(5x−7), and the second gives 1(5x−7)1(5x-7)1(5x−7), keeping the 111.

    Factoring out the shared binomial leaves

    (5x−7)(2x+1).(5x-7)(2x+1).(5x−7)(2x+1).

    Expanding gives 10x2+5x−14x−710x^2+5x-14x-710x2+5x−14x−7, which collects to 10x2−9x−710x^2-9x-710x2−9x−7.

    Answer

    (5x−7)(2x+1)(5x-7)(2x+1)(5x−7)(2x+1).

    Key idea

    Splitting the middle term with the pair whose product is acacac turns a trinomial into a grouping problem.

  4. Problem 4 An equality of totals

    Find every real solution of 3x3+15x=4x2+203x^3+15x=4x^2+203x3+15x=4x2+20, showing a grouping of the four terms after moving them to one side.

    1. Hint 1

      Place all four terms on the same side before looking for shared factors.

    2. Hint 2

      Once the polynomial is written as a product, ask which of its factors can be zero for a real xxx.

    Answer

    x=43x=\frac43x=34​, the only real solution.

    Full solution

    Moving every term to the left gives

    3x3−4x2+15x−20=0.3x^3-4x^2+15x-20=0.3x3−4x2+15x−20=0.

    The first pair gives x2(3x−4)x^2(3x-4)x2(3x−4), and the second gives 5(3x−4)5(3x-4)5(3x−4).

    Factoring out the shared binomial produces

    (3x−4)(x2+5)=0.(3x-4)(x^2+5)=0.(3x−4)(x2+5)=0.

    A product is zero only when one of its factors is zero.

    The factor x2+5x^2+5x2+5 is never zero for a real xxx, because x2x^2x2 is never negative, so x2+5x^2+5x2+5 is at least 555.

    The only real solution therefore comes from 3x−4=03x-4=03x−4=0, which gives x=43x=\frac43x=34​.

    Substituting that value gives 649+20\frac{64}{9}+20964​+20 on each side of the original equation.

    Answer

    x=43x=\frac43x=34​, the only real solution.

    Key idea

    Grouping turns an equation into a product, and only the factors that can vanish contribute real solutions.

  5. Problem 5 A missing coefficient

    The polynomial 6x3+Cx2−5x−206x^3+Cx^2-5x-206x3+Cx2−5x−20 has the factor x+4x+4x+4. Find CCC and give its complete factorization with integer coefficients.

    1. Hint 1

      The last two terms already have the required binomial factor after removing their common factor.

    2. Hint 2

      Determine which coefficient lets the first two terms share the same binomial, then inspect the remaining factor.

    Answer

    C=24C=24C=24; (x+4)(6x2−5)(x+4)(6x^2-5)(x+4)(6x2−5).

    Full solution

    The last pair is

    −5x−20=−5(x+4).-5x-20=-5(x+4).−5x−20=−5(x+4).

    For the two pairs to leave the same binomial, the first pair x2(6x+C)x^2(6x+C)x2(6x+C) must be a multiple of x+4x+4x+4 as well, and a linear expression that is a multiple of x+4x+4x+4 is just a constant times x+4x+4x+4.

    Matching the leading 666 makes that constant 666, so 6x+C=6(x+4)6x+C=6(x+4)6x+C=6(x+4) and

    C=24.C=24.C=24.

    Substituting x=−4x=-4x=−4 into 6x3+24x2−5x−206x^3+24x^2-5x-206x3+24x2−5x−20 gives 000, confirming the factor.

    The groups combine to

    (x+4)(6x2−5).(x+4)(6x^2-5).(x+4)(6x2−5).

    The factor 6x2−56x^2-56x2−5 has no factorization with integer coefficients, since neither 666 nor 555 is a perfect square, so this is complete.

    Answer

    C=24C=24C=24; (x+4)(6x2−5)(x+4)(6x^2-5)(x+4)(6x2−5).

    Key idea

    Matching the binomial one pair leaves can pin down a missing coefficient in the other pair.

  6. Problem 6 Two ways to pair

    For 6u3+10u2−15u−256u^3+10u^2-15u-256u3+10u2−15u−25, grouping 6u3−256u^3-256u3−25 together leaves no shared binomial with the remaining pair. Give two different pairings that do work and the resulting product with integer coefficients.

    1. Hint 1

      A pairing works only when both pairs leave the same binomial, so look for pairs whose two terms share a factor.

    2. Hint 2

      There are three ways to split four terms into two pairs, and the statement has already ruled one of them out.

    3. Hint 3

      Factor each pair carefully, taking a negative out when needed so the binomials match.

    Answer

    Pairings: (6u3+10u2)+(−15u−25)(6u^3+10u^2)+(-15u-25)(6u3+10u2)+(−15u−25) and (6u3−15u)+(10u2−25)(6u^3-15u)+(10u^2-25)(6u3−15u)+(10u2−25); product (3u+5)(2u2−5)(3u+5)(2u^2-5)(3u+5)(2u2−5).

    Full solution

    The first working pairing becomes

    2u2(3u+5)−5(3u+5),2u^2(3u+5)-5(3u+5),2u2(3u+5)−5(3u+5),

    giving (3u+5)(2u2−5)(3u+5)(2u^2-5)(3u+5)(2u2−5).

    The other becomes

    3u(2u2−5)+5(2u2−5),3u(2u^2-5)+5(2u^2-5),3u(2u2−5)+5(2u2−5),

    giving the same product.

    Expanding either product gives 6u3+10u2−15u−256u^3+10u^2-15u-256u3+10u2−15u−25.

    Thus the initially unsuccessful pairing did not rule out grouping.

    Answer

    Pairings: (6u3+10u2)+(−15u−25)(6u^3+10u^2)+(-15u-25)(6u3+10u2)+(−15u−25) and (6u3−15u)+(10u2−25)(6u^3-15u)+(10u^2-25)(6u3−15u)+(10u2−25); product (3u+5)(2u2−5)(3u+5)(2u^2-5)(3u+5)(2u2−5).

    Key idea

    Different pairings can expose different common binomials while producing the same complete product.

  7. Problem 7 A divided quantity

    For x≠−52x\neq-\frac52x=−25​, a quantity 4x2+4x−154x^2+4x-154x2+4x−15 is divided by 2x+52x+52x+5. Simplify the quotient by showing an AC split and grouping, and find its value at x=3x=3x=3.

    1. Hint 1

      The split coefficients must have product −60-60−60 and sum 444.

    2. Hint 2

      After grouping, cancel the denominator factor, which is nonzero on the given domain.

    Answer

    Quotient 2x−32x-32x−3; value at x=3x=3x=3: 333.

    Full solution

    The AC pair is 10,−610,-610,−6.

    Split the polynomial as 4x2+10x−6x−154x^2+10x-6x-154x2+10x−6x−15 and group:

    2x(2x+5)−3(2x+5).2x(2x+5)-3(2x+5).2x(2x+5)−3(2x+5).

    The result is (2x+5)(2x−3)(2x+5)(2x-3)(2x+5)(2x−3).

    The condition makes 2x+52x+52x+5 nonzero, so the shared factor cancels and the quotient is 2x−32x-32x−3.

    At x=3x=3x=3, this is 333; the original quotient is 33/11=333/11=333/11=3.

    Answer

    Quotient 2x−32x-32x−3; value at x=3x=3x=3: 333.

    Key idea

    An AC split can expose a nonzero denominator factor and simplify a quotient.

  8. Problem 8 A constant rule

    For positive real numbers p,qp,qp,q, a student claims that choosing r=p+qr=p+qr=p+q makes x3+px2+qx+rx^3+px^2+qx+rx3+px2+qx+r group into (x+p)(x2+q)(x+p)(x^2+q)(x+p)(x2+q). Is the rule correct? State the condition on rrr that actually makes that product an identity.

    1. Hint 1

      Expand the proposed product and compare all four coefficients.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiply x+px+px+p by each term of x2+qx^2+qx2+q, then compare the constant term of that expansion with rrr.

    Answer

    False in general; the required condition is r=pqr=pqr=pq.

    Full solution

    The product expands as x3+px2+qx+pqx^3+px^2+qx+pqx3+px2+qx+pq.

    Therefore matching the given constant requires

    r=pq.r=pq.r=pq.

    The suggested rule fails, for example, when p=2,q=3p=2,q=3p=2,q=3: it supplies r=5r=5r=5, but the product has constant 666.

    Some choices may have pq=p+qpq=p+qpq=p+q, but the sum is not the general rule.

    Answer

    False in general; the required condition is r=pqr=pqr=pq.

    Key idea

    The shared-binomial structure of a cubic fixes its constant as a product, not as a sum.

  9. Problem 9 The order of a split

    For 12x2+x−612x^2+x-612x2+x−6, the AC pair is 9,−89,-89,−8. Jules claims that either order of these two middle terms leaves the same shared binomial once the pairs are factored. Is the claim correct? Show both orders.

    1. Hint 1

      The shared binomial is whatever both pairs leave behind, so a claim about it is settled by carrying out both groupings.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the middle term as 9x−8x9x-8x9x−8x and then as −8x+9x-8x+9x−8x+9x.

    3. Hint 3

      Factor the adjacent pairs in each version, keeping any negative common factor.

    Answer

    No; the first order leaves 4x+34x+34x+3 and the second leaves 3x−23x-23x−2, though both orders give the factorization (4x+3)(3x−2)(4x+3)(3x-2)(4x+3)(3x−2).

    Full solution

    With 9x9x9x first, the four terms are 12x2+9x−8x−612x^2+9x-8x-612x2+9x−8x−6.

    The first pair gives 3x(4x+3)3x(4x+3)3x(4x+3) and the second gives −2(4x+3)-2(4x+3)−2(4x+3), so the shared binomial is 4x+34x+34x+3 and the product is

    (4x+3)(3x−2).(4x+3)(3x-2).(4x+3)(3x−2).

    With −8x-8x−8x first, the four terms are 12x2−8x+9x−612x^2-8x+9x-612x2−8x+9x−6.

    Now the first pair gives 4x(3x−2)4x(3x-2)4x(3x−2) and the second gives 3(3x−2)3(3x-2)3(3x−2), so the shared binomial is 3x−23x-23x−2 instead.

    Its product is (3x−2)(4x+3)(3x-2)(4x+3)(3x−2)(4x+3), the same two factors in the other order.

    Both orders expand to 12x2+x−612x^2+x-612x2+x−6, so Jules is wrong about the shared binomial even though the final factorization does not depend on the order.

    Answer

    No; the first order leaves 4x+34x+34x+3 and the second leaves 3x−23x-23x−2, though both orders give the factorization (4x+3)(3x−2)(4x+3)(3x-2)(4x+3)(3x−2).

    Key idea

    Reversing a valid AC split can change the visible shared binomial while preserving the final factors.

  10. Problem 10 Two cubic designs

    Create two four-term cubic polynomials with integer coefficients whose x3x^3x3 coefficient is 111. One must group into three linear factors with a repeated factor, and the other must group into three distinct linear factors. Give each expanded polynomial and its complete product, and show the grouping that produces each product.

    1. Hint 1

      Begin with a product of a linear binomial and a difference of squares.

    2. Hint 2

      For the repeated case, make the linear binomial match one of the factors of the difference; choose a different one for the distinct case.

    Answer

    Examples: x3+3x2−9x−27=(x+3)2(x−3)x^3+3x^2-9x-27=(x+3)^2(x-3)x3+3x2−9x−27=(x+3)2(x−3); x3+x2−4x−4=(x+1)(x−2)(x+2)x^3+x^2-4x-4=(x+1)(x-2)(x+2)x3+x2−4x−4=(x+1)(x−2)(x+2).

    Full solution

    For the repeated case, choose (x+3)(x2−9)(x+3)(x^2-9)(x+3)(x2−9), which expands to x3+3x2−9x−27x^3+3x^2-9x-27x3+3x2−9x−27.

    Grouping its four terms gives

    x2(x+3)−9(x+3).x^2(x+3)-9(x+3).x2(x+3)−9(x+3).

    Pulling out the shared x+3x+3x+3 and then factoring the difference of squares gives

    (x+3)2(x−3).(x+3)^2(x-3).(x+3)2(x−3).

    For the distinct case, choose (x+1)(x2−4)(x+1)(x^2-4)(x+1)(x2−4), which expands to x3+x2−4x−4x^3+x^2-4x-4x3+x2−4x−4.

    Grouping gives

    x2(x+1)−4(x+1),x^2(x+1)-4(x+1),x2(x+1)−4(x+1),

    and factoring the difference of squares gives

    (x+1)(x−2)(x+2).(x+1)(x-2)(x+2).(x+1)(x−2)(x+2).

    Its three binomials are all different.

    Any pair of examples meeting the stated conditions is valid.

    Answer

    Examples: x3+3x2−9x−27=(x+3)2(x−3)x^3+3x^2-9x-27=(x+3)^2(x-3)x3+3x2−9x−27=(x+3)2(x−3); x3+x2−4x−4=(x+1)(x−2)(x+2)x^3+x^2-4x-4=(x+1)(x-2)(x+2)x3+x2−4x−4=(x+1)(x−2)(x+2).

    Key idea

    Designing a grouping product allows control over whether its final linear factors repeat.
