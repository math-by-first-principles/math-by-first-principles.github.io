    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    5u−2v5u-2v5u−2v.

    Full solution

    The squared terms are (5u)2(5u)^2(5u)2 and (2v)2(2v)^2(2v)2.

    The required factor is 5u−2v5u-2v5u−2v, since

    (5u+2v)(5u−2v)=25u2−4v2(5u+2v)(5u-2v)=25u^2-4v^2(5u+2v)(5u−2v)=25u2−4v2

    The cross products −10uv-10uv−10uv and 10uv10uv10uv cancel.

    Answer

    5u−2v5u-2v5u−2v.

    Key idea

    A missing opposite-sign factor is recovered from the square roots of both complete terms.

  3. Problem 3 A comparison offset

    Subtract (3x+2)(3x−2)(3x+2)(3x-2)(3x+2)(3x−2) from 9x2+19x^2+19x2+1 and simplify.

    1. Hint 1

      The two factors have matching terms and opposite signs.

    2. Hint 2

      After their cross terms cancel, subtract the entire resulting expression.

    Answer

    555.

    Full solution

    The product is

    (3x+2)(3x−2)=9x2−4.(3x+2)(3x-2)=9x^2-4.(3x+2)(3x−2)=9x2−4.

    Subtracting it from 9x2+19x^2+19x2+1 gives 1−(−4)=51-(-4)=51−(−4)=5.

    Thus the difference is independent of xxx.

    Answer

    555.

    Key idea

    Recognizing canceling cross terms can reveal that the difference between two expressions is constant.

  6. Problem 6 A sum beside a difference

    Consider P=3x2+48y2P=3x^2+48y^2P=3x2+48y2 and Q=3x4−48y4Q=3x^4-48y^4Q=3x4−48y4. Write each as a completely factored product with integer coefficients, and justify in each case that no further factoring is possible over the real numbers.

    1. Hint 1

      Each expression has the same numerical factor in both of its terms; look at what is left once that factor is gone.

    2. Hint 2

      Inside each pair of parentheses, decide whether the two perfect squares are added or subtracted, taking each coefficient with its variable.

    3. Hint 3

      A fourth power is the square of a square, so a first pass on QQQ can leave a factor that is itself a difference of squares.

    Answer

    P=3(x2+16y2)P=3(x^2+16y^2)P=3(x2+16y2) and Q=3(x2+4y2)(x+2y)(x−2y)Q=3(x^2+4y^2)(x+2y)(x-2y)Q=3(x2+4y2)(x+2y)(x−2y).

    Full solution

    Both expressions share the numerical common factor 333, which gives

    P=3(x2+16y2)P=3(x^2+16y^2)P=3(x2+16y2)

    and

    Q=3(x4−16y4).Q=3(x^4-16y^4).Q=3(x4−16y4).

    In PPP the two perfect squares inside are (x)2(x)^2(x)2 and (4y)2(4y)^2(4y)2, since 16y2=(4y)216y^2=(4y)^216y2=(4y)2, and they are added.

    A sum of squares has no factorization over the real numbers, so PPP stops there.

    Inside QQQ the two perfect squares are (x2)2(x^2)^2(x2)2 and (4y2)2(4y^2)^2(4y2)2, since 16y4=(4y2)216y^4=(4y^2)^216y4=(4y2)2, and they are subtracted.

    One pass with roots x2x^2x2 and 4y24y^24y2 gives

    Q=3(x2+4y2)(x2−4y2).Q=3(x^2+4y^2)(x^2-4y^2).Q=3(x2+4y2)(x2−4y2).

    That is not yet complete, because x2−4y2x^2-4y^2x2−4y2 is again a difference of squares, with roots xxx and 2y2y2y:

    x2−4y2=(x+2y)(x−2y).x^2-4y^2=(x+2y)(x-2y).x2−4y2=(x+2y)(x−2y).

    Assembling the pieces gives

    Q=3(x2+4y2)(x+2y)(x−2y)Q=3(x^2+4y^2)(x+2y)(x-2y)Q=3(x2+4y2)(x+2y)(x−2y)

    as the complete factorization.

    The factor x2+4y2x^2+4y^2x2+4y2 is a sum of squares and the last two factors are linear, so the work stops.

    Expanding the final pair returns x2−4y2x^2-4y^2x2−4y2, because the cross terms −2xy-2xy−2xy and 2xy2xy2xy cancel.

    Answer

    P=3(x2+16y2)P=3(x^2+16y^2)P=3(x2+16y2) and Q=3(x2+4y2)(x+2y)(x−2y)Q=3(x^2+4y^2)(x+2y)(x-2y)Q=3(x2+4y2)(x+2y)(x−2y).

    Key idea

    Pull out the common factor first, then keep checking each new factor: a sum of squares stops, while a difference of squares comes apart again.

  8. Problem 8 Reversing both factors

    Dev says that (7−x)(7+x)(7-x)(7+x)(7−x)(7+x) equals (x−7)(x+7)(x-7)(x+7)(x−7)(x+7) for every real xxx, since the terms were only reordered. Decide whether this is correct and find every input where the two products do agree.

    which gives x2=49x^2=49x2=49.

    Thus the agreeing inputs are −7-7−7 and 777, where both products are zero.

    Answer

  9. Problem 9 Signs of two factors

    For real numbers p,qp,qp,q, suppose p2−q2>0p^2-q^2>0p2−q2>0. Mei claims that p+qp+qp+q and p−qp-qp−q must have the same sign. Is the claim correct? Justify your answer.

    1. Hint 1

      Express the given positive quantity as a product.

    2. Hint 2

      Ask what the signs of the two factors can be if their product is positive, and whether either factor may be zero.

    Answer

    The claim is correct.

    Full solution

    The given condition can be rewritten as

    (p+q)(p−q)>0.(p+q)(p-q)>0.(p+q)(p−q)>0.

    Neither factor can be zero, since that would make the product zero.

    Opposite signs would make the product negative, so both factors are positive or both are negative.

    This proves Mei is correct.

    Answer

    The claim is correct.

    Key idea

    A positive difference of squares imposes a same-sign condition on its two linear factors.
