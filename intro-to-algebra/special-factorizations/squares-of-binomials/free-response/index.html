  1. Problem 1 Squaring, then subtracting a square

    Write (2t+1)2−(2t)2(2t+1)^2-(2t)^2(2t+1)2−(2t)2 as a polynomial with like terms combined.

    1. Hint 1

      The subtracted term is the square of one of the two terms being squared.

    2. Hint 2

      The two 4t24t^24t2 terms cancel, so only the other two terms of the expansion survive.

    Answer

    4t+14t+14t+1.

    Full solution

    The square of the sum is

    (2t+1)2=4t2+4t+1.(2t+1)^2=4t^2+4t+1.(2t+1)2=4t2+4t+1.

    Subtracting (2t)2=4t2(2t)^2=4t^2(2t)2=4t2 leaves 4t+14t+14t+1.

    At t=1t=1t=1, the original expression is 9−4=59-4=59−4=5 and the polynomial is 4+1=54+1=54+1=5.

    Answer

    4t+14t+14t+1.

    Key idea

    Subtracting the square of a binomial's first term from the square of the whole binomial leaves twice the product of the two terms and the square of the last term.

  2. Problem 2 Factoring a trinomial completely

    Factor 12x2+60x+7512x^2+60x+7512x2+60x+75 completely.

    1. Hint 1

      A trinomial should be in lowest terms before its outer terms are tested against the square pattern.

    2. Hint 2

      Once a numerical factor is out in front, find the terms whose squares are the outer terms and compare twice their product with the middle term.

    Answer

    3(2x+5)23(2x+5)^23(2x+5)2.

    Full solution

    Every coefficient is a multiple of 333, so pulling that factor out leaves

    3(4x2+20x+25).3(4x^2+20x+25).3(4x2+20x+25).

    Inside the parentheses the outer terms are squares, since 4x2=(2x)24x^2=(2x)^24x2=(2x)2 and 25=5225=5^225=52, and twice the product of 2x2x2x and 555 is 20x20x20x, which matches the middle term.

    That makes the trinomial in the parentheses (2x+5)2(2x+5)^2(2x+5)2, so the complete factorization is

    3(2x+5)2.3(2x+5)^2.3(2x+5)2.

    The 333 stays in front, because it is not the square of a whole number.

    Expanding 3(2x+5)23(2x+5)^23(2x+5)2 returns the original trinomial, since 333 times 4x2+20x+254x^2+20x+254x2+20x+25 rebuilds each coefficient.

    Answer

    3(2x+5)23(2x+5)^23(2x+5)2.

    Key idea

    Removing a common numerical factor first can reveal a perfect square that the original coefficients hide.

  3. Problem 3 An unknown coefficient

    In the expansion of (ax+4)2(ax+4)^2(ax+4)2, where aaa is a number, the coefficient of xxx is 484848. Find the coefficient of x2x^2x2.

    1. Hint 1

      Of the three coefficients in the expansion, only one is built from both terms of the binomial.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand with aaa left in place, match the coefficient of xxx against 484848 to find aaa, then square the whole first term.

    Answer

    363636.

    Full solution

    Expanding with aaa left in place gives

    a2x2+8ax+16.a^2x^2+8ax+16.a2x2+8ax+16.

    Matching the coefficient of xxx gives 8a=488a=488a=48, so a=6a=6a=6.

    The coefficient of x2x^2x2 is a2a^2a2, which is 363636.

    Squaring 6x+46x+46x+4 gives 36x2+48x+1636x^2+48x+1636x2+48x+16, whose coefficient of xxx is the given 484848.

    Answer

    363636.

    Key idea

    The doubled middle term pins down the coefficient inside the binomial, and squaring that coefficient gives the leading one.

  4. Problem 4 A placard order

    Three identical square placards have a combined area of 12x2−48x+4812x^2-48x+4812x2−48x+48 square cm, where x>2x>2x>2. Find the side length of one placard as a linear expression in xxx.

    1. Hint 1

      Three identical squares share the total equally, and a side length belongs to one square, not to the total.

    2. Hint 2

      Remove a numerical common factor and recognize the remaining trinomial as a square; the side length must be positive.

    Answer

    2x−42x-42x−4 cm, or equivalently 2(x−2)2(x-2)2(x−2) cm.

    Full solution

    One placard has area 4x2−16x+164x^2-16x+164x2−16x+16 square cm.

    Factoring gives

    4x2−16x+16=4(x−2)2.4x^2-16x+16=4(x-2)^2.4x2−16x+16=4(x−2)2.

    This is (2x−4)2(2x-4)^2(2x−4)2.

    Because x>2x>2x>2, 2x−42x-42x−4 is positive and is the side length.

    Three copies of its square reproduce the given combined area.

    Answer

    2x−42x-42x−4 cm, or equivalently 2(x−2)2(x-2)2(x−2) cm.

    Key idea

    Pull out a numerical common factor before testing for a perfect square, and let the stated domain choose the positive root.

  5. Problem 5 A student's expansion

    A student writes (3y−7)2=3y2−42y−49(3y-7)^2=3y^2-42y-49(3y−7)2=3y2−42y−49. Identify both errors in that expansion, write the correct expansion, and use one value of yyy to show that the student's version is wrong.

    1. Hint 1

      A coefficient belongs to the term being squared, and the square of a negative number is positive.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand with the difference pattern, then compare the result term by term with what the student wrote.

    Answer

    The coefficient 333 was not squared, so the leading term is 9y29y^29y2, and the last term is +49+49+49, not −49-49−49; the correct expansion is 9y2−42y+499y^2-42y+499y2−42y+49. At y=1y=1y=1 it gives 161616, while the student's version gives −88-88−88.

    Full solution

    Squaring 3y−73y-73y−7 squares the whole first term, so the leading term is (3y)2=9y2(3y)^2=9y^2(3y)2=9y2 and not 3y23y^23y2: the student squared the variable but left the coefficient behind.

    The last term is the square of −7-7−7, which is +49+49+49, so the sign of the student's −49-49−49 is the second error.

    The middle term is correct, since

    −2(3y)(7)=−42y.-2(3y)(7)=-42y.−2(3y)(7)=−42y.

    The correct expansion is

    9y2−42y+49.9y^2-42y+49.9y2−42y+49.

    At y=1y=1y=1 the original expression is (3−7)2=16(3-7)^2=16(3−7)2=16, and the correct expansion gives

    9−42+49=16.9-42+49=16.9−42+49=16.

    The student's version gives 3−42−49=−883-42-49=-883−42−49=−88, so it is not equal to (3y−7)2(3y-7)^2(3y−7)2.

    Answer

    The coefficient 333 was not squared, so the leading term is 9y29y^29y2, and the last term is +49+49+49, not −49-49−49; the correct expansion is 9y2−42y+499y^2-42y+499y2−42y+49. At y=1y=1y=1 it gives 161616, while the student's version gives −88-88−88.

    Key idea

    Squaring a term squares its coefficient too, and squaring a negative last term leaves it positive.

  6. Problem 6 When two expressions agree

    Solve (2x−3)2=4x2−27(2x-3)^2=4x^2-27(2x−3)2=4x2−27 for xxx.

    1. Hint 1

      A squared binomial carries a linear term that the other expression does not have.

    2. Hint 2

      The squared terms cancel from the equation, leaving a linear condition.

    Answer

    x=3x=3x=3.

    Full solution

    Expanding the left side gives

    4x2−12x+9=4x2−27.4x^2-12x+9=4x^2-27.4x2−12x+9=4x2−27.

    Subtracting 4x24x^24x2 and then 999 gives

    −12x=−36.-12x=-36.−12x=−36.

    Thus x=3x=3x=3.

    At x=3x=3x=3 the two sides are 32=93^2=932=9 and 36−27=936-27=936−27=9, confirming equality.

    Answer

    x=3x=3x=3.

    Key idea

    An equation containing two quadratic expressions may become linear after their squared terms cancel.

  7. Problem 7 A square's measurements

    A square has area 64t2+80t+2564t^2+80t+2564t2+80t+25 square cm and perimeter 606060 cm, where t>0t>0t>0. Find ttt.

    1. Hint 1

      The perimeter of a square is four times its side length.

    2. Hint 2

      The area is a squared linear expression, which gives the positive side length.

    Answer

    t=54t=\tfrac54t=45​, or 1.251.251.25.

    Full solution

    The area factors as

    (8t+5)2.(8t+5)^2.(8t+5)2.

    Since t>0t>0t>0, the side is 8t+58t+58t+5 cm.

    A perimeter of 606060 cm means each side is 151515 cm, so

    8t+5=15.8t+5=15.8t+5=15.

    This gives

    t=54.t=\tfrac54.t=45​.

    Substitution gives side 151515 cm, area 225225225 square cm, and the required perimeter.

    Answer

    t=54t=\tfrac54t=45​, or 1.251.251.25.

    Key idea

    Recognizing an area as a square can turn a perimeter condition into a linear equation.

  8. Problem 8 Lin's average

    For real numbers u,vu,vu,v, Lin claims that the average of (u+v)2(u+v)^2(u+v)2 and (u−v)2(u-v)^2(u−v)2 is u2+v2u^2+v^2u2+v2. Decide whether the claim is correct and justify your answer.

    1. Hint 1

      Squaring a sum and squaring a difference produce the same two outer squares.

    2. Hint 2

      Their middle terms have opposite signs; track them when the two expansions are added.

    Answer

    The claim is correct for all real u,vu,vu,v.

    Full solution

    The expansions are u2+2uv+v2u^2+2uv+v^2u2+2uv+v2 and u2−2uv+v2u^2-2uv+v^2u2−2uv+v2.

    Their sum is

    2u2+2v2.2u^2+2v^2.2u2+2v2.

    Dividing by 222 gives u2+v2u^2+v^2u2+v2.

    No sign restriction was used, so the claim holds for every pair of real numbers.

    Answer

    The claim is correct for all real u,vu,vu,v.

    Key idea

    Averaging squares with opposite cross terms leaves the sum of the two individual squares.

  9. Problem 9 A size prediction

    Bo claims that (a+b)2(a+b)^2(a+b)2 is greater than a2+b2a^2+b^2a2+b2 whenever aaa and bbb are nonzero real numbers. Decide whether the claim is correct, and state exactly when the first expression is greater.

    1. Hint 1

      The two expressions differ only by the cross term that the expansion produces.

    2. Hint 2

      The sign of the remaining product depends on the signs of aaa and bbb.

    Answer

    False; it is greater exactly when ab>0ab>0ab>0, meaning a,ba,ba,b have the same sign.

    Full solution

    Expanding and subtracting leaves

    D=2ab,D=2ab,D=2ab,

    where DDD is the first expression minus the second.

    Thus D>0D>0D>0 exactly when ab>0ab>0ab>0.

    Nonzero numbers of opposite signs give D<0D<0D<0: for a=1,b=−1a=1,b=-1a=1,b=−1, the first expression is 000 and the second is 222.

    Answer

    False; it is greater exactly when ab>0ab>0ab>0, meaning a,ba,ba,b have the same sign.

    Key idea

    The cross term determines whether a squared sum is greater or smaller than the sum of the separate squares.

  10. Problem 10 Two formula labels

    Find every real kkk for which (kx+4)2(kx+4)^2(kx+4)2 and (kx−4)2(kx-4)^2(kx−4)2 are equal for every real xxx, and explain why no other value works.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the two expansions as polynomials in xxx.

    2. Hint 2

      The squared and constant terms agree already; equality for every input also requires the linear terms to agree.

    Answer

    k=0k=0k=0.

    Full solution

    The expansions are k2x2+8kx+16k^2x^2+8kx+16k2x2+8kx+16 and k2x2−8kx+16k^2x^2-8kx+16k2x2−8kx+16.

    Their difference is

    16kx.16kx.16kx.

    For this to vanish at every real xxx, it must vanish at x=1x=1x=1, forcing k=0k=0k=0.

    With k=0k=0k=0, both expressions are 161616 for every input, so the condition is sufficient as well.

    Answer

    k=0k=0k=0.

    Key idea

    An identity requires matching cross terms at every input, not agreement at a single special input.
