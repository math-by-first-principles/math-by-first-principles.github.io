    1. Comparing a squared sum and a squared difference . Foundational, 7 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 7 pts

    The square of a sum and the square of a difference share more than they differ. Expand two binomials built from the same numbers, one added and one subtracted, and see exactly which parts of the answer move and which stay fixed.

    2. Squaring the whole term, coefficient included . Foundational, 10 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Foundational 10 pts

    The pattern for a squared binomial does not care whether its two pieces are bare letters or carry a coefficient. What has to change is care: the WHOLE term gets squared, coefficient and letter together, not just the letter.

    The answer

    Part A

    (3x+7)2=9x2+42x+49(3x+7)^2 = 9x^2 + 42x + 49(3x+7)2=9x2+42x+49.

    Part B

    (4a−3b)2=16a2−24ab+9b2(4a-3b)^2 = 16a^2 - 24ab + 9b^2(4a−3b)2=16a2−24ab+9b2.

    3. C

    Answer and solution

    The answer

    Part A

    (x+9)2=x2+18x+81(x+9)^2 = x^2 + 18x + 81(x+9)2=x2+18x+81 square feet.

    Part B

    The four tile areas are x2x^2x2, 9x9x9x, 9x9x9x, and 818181, and their sum matches part A exactly: the lone tile gives the first square, the corner tile gives the last square, and the two strips together give the middle term.

    Part C

    The missing area is 9x9x9x square feet, one whole strip tile. In general, the middle term of (a+b)2(a+b)^2(a+b)2 is 2ab2ab2ab because there are genuinely TWO cross-product regions, one for each order the two side lengths meet; leaving out either one leaves the total short by a full ababab.

    Part A

    4. A claim that looks like the other pattern . Reasoning, 11 points. Question 4 of 5.

    Reasoning 11 pts

    Here is a claim, stated the way a claim about ALL real numbers should be stated: for all real numbers aaa and bbb, (a−b)2=a2−b2(a-b)^2 = a^2 - b^2(a−b)2=a2−b2. It is tempting to believe, since it looks like a natural cousin of the difference-of-squares shape. It is false.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    Take a=6a=6a=6, b=2b=2b=2. Then (a−b)2=42=16(a-b)^2 = 4^2 = 16(a−b)2=42=16, while a2−b2=36−4=32a^2-b^2 = 36-4=32a2−b2=36−4=32. The two sides give 161616 and 323232, so the claim is false as stated.

    Part B

    (a−b)2=a2−2ab+b2(a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2(a−b)2=a2−2ab+b2. The claim leaves out the middle term −2ab-2ab−2ab entirely, and it assigns the last term the sign −b2-b^2−b2, when a square can never be negative: it must be +b2+b^2+b2.

    Part C

    The claim holds exactly when b=0b=0b=0 or a=ba=ba=b; on every other pair, such as the one from part A, it fails. Those special cases do not rescue it, since a claim quantified over ALL real numbers is already false once a single counterexample exists.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    A claim quantified over ALL real numbers is destroyed by a single pair on which it fails, so pick one and evaluate both sides honestly.

    Take a=6a=6a=6 and b=2b=2b=2. The left side of the claim:

    (a−b)2=(6−2)2=42=16.(a-b)^2 = (6-2)^2 = 4^2 = 16.(a−b)2=(6−2)2=42=16.

    The right side of the claim:

    a2−b2=62−22=36−4=32.a^2 - b^2 = 6^2 - 2^2 = 36 - 4 = 32.a2−b2=62−22=36−4=32.

    The two sides give 161616 and 323232, which are not equal, so this pair refutes the claim.

    Part B

    Apply the squared-difference pattern:

    (a−b)2=a2−2ab+b2.(a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2.(a−b)2=a2−2ab+b2.

    Compare this correct expansion against the claim, a2−b2a^2-b^2a2−b2. Two things are wrong with the claim: it has no middle term at all, when the true expansion has −2ab-2ab−2ab, and its last term is written as −b2-b^2−b2, when b2b^2b2 is a square and squares are never negative, so the true last term is +b2+b^2+b2.

    Part C

    a2−2ab+b2=a2−b2.a^2 - 2ab + b^2 = a^2 - b^2.a2−2ab+b2=a2−b2.
