Sum and Difference of Cubes

Intuition

You already saw that a difference of squares always splits into (a+b)(a−b)(a + b)(a - b)(a+b)(a−b). Cubes have their own pair of patterns, one for a sum and one for a difference, and unlike squares, a sum of cubes factors too. Whenever you meet two perfect cubes added or subtracted, like x3+8x^3 + 8x3+8 or 27x3−6427x^3 - 6427x3−64, the expression comes apart into a binomial times a trinomial. You can check both patterns the same way you already trust: multiply the proposed factors out and watch most of the terms cancel. Learn where the signs go and you can factor any sum or difference of cubes.

Learning goals

  • Factor a sum or difference of cubes into a binomial times a trinomial
  • Place the three signs correctly using the SOAP pattern
  • Explain why the middle terms must cancel to prove each identity
  • Pull out the greatest common factor before checking for a hidden cube pattern

Why a sum or difference of cubes factors

Try a specific case first: x3+8x^3 + 8x3+8. Since 8=238 = 2^38=23, both terms are perfect cubes, and the claim is that this factors as (x+2)(x2−2x+4)(x + 2)(x^2 - 2x + 4)(x+2)(x2−2x+4). Check that claim the way you check any proposed factoring, by expanding the product:

(x+2)(x2−2x+4)=x3−2x2+4x+2x2−4x+8.(x + 2)(x^2 - 2x + 4) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 4x + 2x^2 - 4x + 8.(x+2)(x2−2x+4)=x3−2x2+4x+2x2−4x+8.

The −2x2-2x^2−2x2 and +2x2+2x^2+2x2 cancel, and so do +4x+4x+4x and −4x-4x−4x, leaving x3+8x^3 + 8x3+8. Nothing about xxx and 222 was special there; the same cancellation happens for any two terms aaa and bbb, which gives two general identities, one for each sign:

a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2),a3−b3=(a−b)(a2+ab+b2).a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2), \qquad a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2).a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2),a3−b3=(a−b)(a2+ab+b2).

Each is a binomial times a trinomial, and neither asks to be taken on faith. The proof below confirms both in general, the same way you already confirmed the difference of squares: expand the right-hand side, multiplying every term of the binomial by every term of the trinomial, and see what survives.

Why a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)#

Expand the product on the right, taking each term of a+ba + ba+b against each term of a2−ab+b2a^2 - ab + b^2a2−ab+b2:

(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)=a3−a2b+ab2+a2b−ab2+b3.(a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2) = a^3 - a^2 b + a b^2 + a^2 b - a b^2 + b^3.(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)=a3−a2b+ab2+a2b−ab2+b3.

Just like the concrete case above, the two middle pairs, −a2b-a^2 b−a2b with +a2b+a^2 b+a2b and +ab2+a b^2+ab2 with −ab2-a b^2−ab2, are exact opposites and cancel, for every aaa and bbb, not just xxx and 222:

a3−a2b+ab2+a2b−ab2+b3=a3+b3.a^3 - a^2 b + a b^2 + a^2 b - a b^2 + b^3 = a^3 + b^3.a3−a2b+ab2+a2b−ab2+b3=a3+b3.

Read right to left, that is the factoring rule for a sum of cubes. The difference identity works the same way:

(a−b)(a2+ab+b2)=a3+a2b+ab2−a2b−ab2−b3=a3−b3.(a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2) = a^3 + a^2 b + a b^2 - a^2 b - a b^2 - b^3 = a^3 - b^3.(a−b)(a2+ab+b2)=a3+a2b+ab2−a2b−ab2−b3=a3−b3.

The same two middle pairs cancel, only this time the surviving last term is −b3-b^3−b3.

∎

Nothing about that cancellation is arbitrary, and it is also what forces every sign in the two identities, which is the next thing to pin down.

The SOAP sign pattern

The two identities differ only in where the signs go, and there is a tidy way to remember the pattern. It is often called SOAP, for Same, Opposite, Always Positive, read across the three signs you have to fill in:

  • Same. The binomial takes the same sign as the original expression. A sum of cubes gives (a+b)(a + b)(a+b); a difference gives (a−b)(a - b)(a−b).
  • Opposite. The middle term of the trinomial takes the opposite sign. A sum gives a2−ab+b2a^2 - ab + b^2a2−ab+b2; a difference gives a2+ab+b2a^2 + ab + b^2a2+ab+b2.
  • Always Positive. The sign before the trinomial’s last term, b2b^2b2, is always plus, in both identities.

These are not three rules to accept blindly; the proof above forces all three. Same: the sign on bbb decides whether the product ends in +b3+b^3+b3 or −b3-b^3−b3. Opposite: that sign on the middle term is exactly what makes the four cross terms cancel instead of piling up. Always Positive: that sign work all happens on the binomial’s bbb, so the trinomial’s last term keeps a plain plus in both identities.

Check your understanding

In a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2), why must the trinomial's middle term be −ab-ab−ab and not +ab+ab+ab?

Answer choices

Expanding (a+b)(a2−ab+b2)(a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)(a+b)(a2−ab+b2) gives a3−a2b+ab2+a2b−ab2+b3a^3 - a^2b + ab^2 + a^2b - ab^2 + b^3a3−a2b+ab2+a2b−ab2+b3. The −ab-ab−ab sign is exactly what makes −a2b-a^2b−a2b pair with +a2b+a^2b+a2b, and +ab2+ab^2+ab2 pair with −ab2-ab^2−ab2, so all four middle terms cancel and only a3+b3a^3 + b^3a3+b3 survives. A +ab+ab+ab middle term would make those same pairs add instead of cancel, leaving unwanted middle terms behind. The sign is forced by this cancellation, not chosen by convention.

One warning the pattern makes visible: the middle term is a single ababab, not 2ab2ab2ab. The doubled middle term belongs to a perfect-square trinomial like (a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2(a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2, which is a different animal. The cube trinomial never doubles it.

Recognizing perfect cubes

Before you can use either identity you have to see two perfect cubes. A perfect cube is a whole number, or a term with a whole-number coefficient, that is something cubed. The perfect cubes from 111 through 101010 are worth knowing on sight:

1=13,8=23,27=33,64=43,125=53,216=63,343=73,512=83,729=93,1000=103.\begin{aligned} 1 &= 1^3, &\qquad 8 &= 2^3, \\ 27 &= 3^3, &\qquad 64 &= 4^3, \\ 125 &= 5^3, &\qquad 216 &= 6^3, \\ 343 &= 7^3, &\qquad 512 &= 8^3, \\ 729 &= 9^3, &\qquad 1000 &= 10^3. \end{aligned}127125343729​=13,=33,=53,=73,=93,​8642165121000​=23,=43,=63,=83,=103.​

Variable terms can be cubes too, but a term needs two things to qualify: its coefficient must be a perfect cube, and every variable’s exponent must be a multiple of 333 (since cubing multiplies an exponent by 333). A bare power like x3x^3x3 is (x)3(x)^3(x)3, and 8x3=(2x)38x^3 = (2x)^38x3=(2x)3, 27x3=(3x)327x^3 = (3x)^327x3=(3x)3, and 64x3=(4x)364x^3 = (4x)^364x3=(4x)3 all pass both tests. But 8x28x^28x2 is not a perfect cube, even though 888 is: the exponent 222 is not a multiple of 333. This lesson sticks to that plain case, exponent 333, so aaa is always a single variable term like xxx or 2x2x2x. To set up a factorization, take the cube root of each term to find aaa and bbb. The cube root of 8x38x^38x3 is 2x2x2x, not 8x8x8x, because you must cube both the 222 and the xxx to rebuild 8x38x^38x3.

Worked example 1 Which expressions are sums or differences of cubes?

Ask two things of each expression: are both terms perfect cubes, and are they added or subtracted?

x3−27x^3 - 27x3−27 is a difference of cubes. Both terms are cubes, since x3=(x)3x^3 = (x)^3x3=(x)3 and 27=3327 = 3^327=33, and they are subtracted, so a=xa = xa=x and b=3b = 3b=3.

8x3+18x^3 + 18x3+1 is a sum of cubes, since 8x3=(2x)38x^3 = (2x)^38x3=(2x)3 and 1=131 = 1^31=13. Here a=2xa = 2xa=2x and b=1b = 1b=1.

x3+10x^3 + 10x3+10 is neither. The first term is a cube, but 101010 is not a perfect cube, since no whole number cubes to 101010.

x2−64x^2 - 64x2−64 is not a difference of cubes. The number 64=4364 = 4^364=43 is a cube, but x2x^2x2 is a square, not a cube, so the pattern does not apply. This one is a difference of squares instead.

Answer choices

A difference of cubes is two perfect-cube terms joined by a minus sign. In x3−27x^3 - 27x3−27, both x3=(x)3x^3 = (x)^3x3=(x)3 and 27=3327 = 3^327=33 are cubes, and they are subtracted.

x3−27=(x−3)(x2+3x+9)x^3 - 27 = (x - 3)(x^2 + 3x + 9)x3−27=(x−3)(x2+3x+9)

The expression x3+27x^3 + 27x3+27 is a sum, x2x^2x2 is a square rather than a cube, and 202020 is not a perfect cube.

Factoring cubes that carry coefficients

Nothing in the pattern requires bare variables. Once you can take the cube root of each term, coefficient and all, you just read off aaa and bbb and let SOAP place the signs.

Worked example 2 Factor 8x3+278x^3 + 278x3+27

Take the cube root of each term. Since 8x3=(2x)38x^3 = (2x)^38x3=(2x)3, you have a=2xa = 2xa=2x, and since 27=3327 = 3^327=33, you have b=3b = 3b=3. This is a sum, so the binomial keeps the same sign, (2x+3)(2x + 3)(2x+3), the trinomial’s middle term takes the opposite sign, and the last term is positive:

8x3+27=(2x)3+33=(2x+3)(4x2−6x+9).8x^3 + 27 = (2x)^3 + 3^3 = (2x + 3)(4x^2 - 6x + 9).8x3+27=(2x)3+33=(2x+3)(4x2−6x+9).

The trinomial terms are a2=(2x)2=4x2a^2 = (2x)^2 = 4x^2a2=(2x)2=4x2, then ab=(2x)(3)=6xab = (2x)(3) = 6xab=(2x)(3)=6x with a minus in front, then b2=32=9b^2 = 3^2 = 9b2=32=9. The middle term is 6x6x6x, a single ababab, not 12x12x12x.

Worked example 3 Factor x3−64x^3 - 64x3−64 and 27−x327 - x^327−x3

For x3−64x^3 - 64x3−64, the cube roots are xxx and 643=4\sqrt[3]{64} = 4364​=4 (the small 333 marks a cube root instead of a square root, so 643\sqrt[3]{64}364​ means the number that cubes to 646464), so a=xa = xa=x and b=4b = 4b=4. It is a difference, so the binomial is (x−4)(x - 4)(x−4), the middle sign is opposite that minus (so plus), and the last term is positive:

x3−64=(x−4)(x2+4x+16).x^3 - 64 = (x - 4)(x^2 + 4x + 16).x3−64=(x−4)(x2+4x+16).

For 27−x327 - x^327−x3, read the terms in the order they appear as 33−x33^3 - x^333−x3, a difference with a=3a = 3a=3 and b=xb = xb=x. The number comes first, and the pattern does not mind:

27−x3=(3−x)(9+3x+x2).27 - x^3 = (3 - x)(9 + 3x + x^2).27−x3=(3−x)(9+3x+x2).

The binomial (3−x)(3 - x)(3−x) matches the difference, the middle term +3x+3x+3x is opposite that minus, and the last term x2x^2x2 is positive, exactly as SOAP predicts.

Factor 8x3+18x^3 + 18x3+1.

Answer choices

Take the cube root of each term: 8x3=(2x)38x^3 = (2x)^38x3=(2x)3 and 1=131 = 1^31=13, so a=2xa = 2xa=2x and b=1b = 1b=1. This is a sum, so the binomial keeps the plus sign and the trinomial's middle term takes the opposite (minus) sign.

8x3+1=(2x+1)(4x2−2x+1)8x^3 + 1 = (2x + 1)(4x^2 - 2x + 1)8x3+1=(2x+1)(4x2−2x+1)

The first term of the trinomial is (2x)2=4x2(2x)^2 = 4x^2(2x)2=4x2, so a 2x22x^22x2 is wrong, and the middle sign must be minus for a sum.

Why the trinomial does not factor further

After you factor a sum or difference of cubes, you are left with a trinomial like x2+4x+16x^2 + 4x + 16x2+4x+16 or x2−3x+9x^2 - 3x + 9x2−3x+9. It is tempting to keep going and try to factor that trinomial as well; try it on x2+4x+16x^2 + 4x + 16x2+4x+16 and see what happens.

To factor x2+4x+16x^2 + 4x + 16x2+4x+16 into (x+p)(x+q)(x + p)(x + q)(x+p)(x+q) you would need two integers whose product is 161616 and whose sum is 444. Since both 161616 and 444 are positive, ppp and qqq must both be positive too (two negatives would multiply to a positive but add to a negative). The positive integer pairs that multiply to 161616 are 111 and 161616, 222 and 888, and 444 and 444, which sum to 171717, 101010, and 888. None of them sum to 444, so no such factoring exists.

That is the pattern for every sum or difference of cubes in this lesson: once you have removed any common factor and applied the cube identity, you have the final, complete factorization with integer coefficients.

Pulling out a common factor first

An expression that is not a sum or difference of cubes as written can turn into one after you factor out a common factor. So always check for a common factor first. Take 2x3+162x^3 + 162x3+16. Neither 2x32x^32x3 nor 161616 is a perfect cube, so the pattern does not apply yet. But both terms are even, and once you pull out the 222, what is left is a clean sum of cubes:

2x3+16=2(x3+8)=2(x+2)(x2−2x+4).2x^3 + 16 = 2(x^3 + 8) = 2(x + 2)(x^2 - 2x + 4).2x3+16=2(x3+8)=2(x+2)(x2−2x+4).

The habit is to factor out the greatest common factor first, then check whether what remains is a sum or difference of cubes. Skip that step and you either miss the cubes entirely or leave the answer half done.

Worked example 4 Factor 3x4−24x3x^4 - 24x3x4−24x completely

Find the greatest common factor of 3x43x^43x4 and 24x24x24x first. They share a factor of 333 and a factor of xxx, so the common factor is 3x3x3x:

3x4−24x=3x(x3−8).3x^4 - 24x = 3x(x^3 - 8).3x4−24x=3x(x3−8).

What is left inside is x3−8=x3−23x^3 - 8 = x^3 - 2^3x3−8=x3−23, a difference of cubes with a=xa = xa=x and b=2b = 2b=2:

x3−8=(x−2)(x2+2x+4).x^3 - 8 = (x - 2)(x^2 + 2x + 4).x3−8=(x−2)(x2+2x+4).

Keeping the common factor out front, the complete factorization is

3x4−24x=3x(x−2)(x2+2x+4).3x^4 - 24x = 3x(x - 2)(x^2 + 2x + 4).3x4−24x=3x(x−2)(x2+2x+4).

There was no cube to see until the 3x3x3x came out.

Worked example 5 Factor 54x3+1654x^3 + 1654x3+16 completely

The terms 54x354x^354x3 and 161616 are both even, and their greatest common factor is 222:

54x3+16=2(27x3+8).54x^3 + 16 = 2(27x^3 + 8).54x3+16=2(27x3+8).

Now 27x3+8=(3x)3+2327x^3 + 8 = (3x)^3 + 2^327x3+8=(3x)3+23 is a sum of cubes with a=3xa = 3xa=3x and b=2b = 2b=2:

27x3+8=(3x+2)(9x2−6x+4).27x^3 + 8 = (3x + 2)(9x^2 - 6x + 4).27x3+8=(3x+2)(9x2−6x+4).

So the complete factorization is

54x3+16=2(3x+2)(9x2−6x+4).54x^3 + 16 = 2(3x + 2)(9x^2 - 6x + 4).54x3+16=2(3x+2)(9x2−6x+4).

Pulling out the 222 is what turned 54x3+1654x^3 + 1654x3+16, which is not itself a sum of cubes, into one that is.

2x3−128=2(x3−64)=2(x−4)(x2+4x+16)2x^3 - 128 = 2(x^3 - 64) = 2(x - 4)(x^2 + 4x + 16)2x3−128=2(x3−64)=2(x−4)(x2+4x+16)

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Doubling the middle term. The cube trinomial is a2∓ab+b2a^2 \mp ab + b^2a2∓ab+b2 with a single ababab, so x3−64=(x−4)(x2+4x+16)x^3 - 64 = (x - 4)(x^2 + 4x + 16)x3−64=(x−4)(x2+4x+16), not (x−4)(x2+8x+16)(x - 4)(x^2 + 8x + 16)(x−4)(x2+8x+16). The doubled 2ab2ab2ab belongs to a perfect-square trinomial, a different pattern.
  • Getting the middle sign wrong. By SOAP the middle sign is opposite the binomial. A sum gives a2−ab+b2a^2 - ab + b^2a2−ab+b2; a difference gives a2+ab+b2a^2 + ab + b^2a2+ab+b2.
  • Thinking a sum of cubes will not factor. A sum of squares does not factor over the reals, but a sum of cubes does.
  • Confusing a3+b3a^3 + b^3a3+b3 with (a+b)3(a + b)^3(a+b)3. Expanding gives (a+b)3=a3+3a2b+3ab2+b3(a + b)^3 = a^3 + 3a^2 b + 3ab^2 + b^3(a+b)3=a3+3a2b+3ab2+b3, which carries extra middle terms the identity does not have.
  • Trying to factor the trinomial further. Once you reach the binomial-times-trinomial stage, you stop; these trinomials do not factor further over the integers.
  • Forgetting the common factor. An expression like 2x3+162x^3 + 162x3+16 is not a sum of cubes as written. Pull out the 222 first to reveal 2(x+2)(x2−2x+4)2(x + 2)(x^2 - 2x + 4)2(x+2)(x2−2x+4).

Key takeaways

  • The two identities, a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2) and a3−b3=(a−b)(a2+ab+b2)a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)a3−b3=(a−b)(a2+ab+b2), hold because four middle terms cancel in matched pairs when you expand the right side.
  • SOAP places the signs: the binomial takes the Same sign, the trinomial’s middle term takes the Opposite sign, and the sign before its last term is Always Positive.
  • Recognize perfect cubes (8=238 = 2^38=23, 27=3327 = 3^327=33, 64=4364 = 4^364=43, …) and monomial cubes like 8x3=(2x)38x^3 = (2x)^38x3=(2x)3; cube-root each term to get aaa and bbb.
  • The trinomial’s middle term is a single ababab, never 2ab2ab2ab; pull out a common factor first when there is one. For every sum or difference of cubes in this lesson, the resulting trinomial does not factor further over the integers.

A bit of history (optional)

Numbers one below a power of two look like promising hunting ground for primes. Three is prime, and so is seven. Fifteen is not, though, and neither is sixty-three. Something is choosing the winners here, and it is not chance.

Look at sixty-three, which is 26−12^6 - 126−1. Rewrite 262^626 as 434^343 and the 111 as 131^313, and a difference of cubes is staring back at you. The identity from this lesson delivers the factor 4−14 - 14−1 at once. So sixty-three is three times twenty-one. The same move disposes of every exponent divisible by three, and a close relative handles every other exponent that is not prime.

Marin Mersenne, a friar who kept half the mathematicians of Europe writing to one another, put the fact to work. In 164416441644 he published a list of the exponents he believed produced primes. Every entry on it was itself a prime. He never needed to test the others, because the factoring pattern had ruled them out in advance. His list turned out to contain errors, and settling the last of them took nearly three centuries.

These numbers still carry his name, and the largest prime anyone has ever found is one of them. Each new search still begins by discarding composite exponents, using this identity for the ones divisible by three and its close relatives for the rest.
