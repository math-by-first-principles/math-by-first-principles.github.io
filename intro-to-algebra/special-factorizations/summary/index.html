Special Factorizations: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Perfect square (of a term)
A whole number, or a term with whole-number coefficients, that is something squared: 49=7249 = 7^249=72, 9x2=(3x)29x^2 = (3x)^29x2=(3x)2, x4=(x2)2x^4 = (x^2)^2x4=(x2)2.
Perfect-square trinomial
A trinomial that equals a squared binomial: one exactly when its first and last terms are perfect squares and its middle term is ±2\pm 2±2 times the product of their roots.
Sum of squares a2+b2a^2 + b^2a2+b2
Two perfect squares joined by a plus. The difference-of-squares identity needs a minus, and a sum like x2+9x^2 + 9x2+9 has no real factorization at all, so a plus is a full stop for this identity.
SOAP
Same, Opposite, Always Positive: the mnemonic for the three signs of a cube factorization, read across the binomial's sign, the trinomial's middle sign, and the trinomial's last sign.
Conjugate
A two-term expression with the sign between its terms flipped: the conjugate of a+ba + \sqrt{b}a+b​ is a−ba - \sqrt{b}a−b​. Multiplying the two gives a difference of squares.
Rationalizing a denominator
Rewriting a fraction so no square root is left in the denominator, by multiplying by a form of 111. The value never changes, only the way it is written.
Greatest common factor (GCF)
The largest factor every term shares, number and variable part together: the GCF of 3x33x^33x3 and 12x12x12x is 3x3x3x. Pull it out before testing any pattern.
Factoring completely
Factoring until no remaining factor can be factored again with integer coefficients.
Factoring by grouping
The method for a four-term polynomial: pair the terms, factor each pair, and factor out the shared binomial both pairs leave behind.

Formulas and theorems

  • Square of a sum

    (a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2(a+b)2=a2+2ab+b2
    Text description

    The square on a plus b splits into an a by a square, a b by b square, and two shaded a by b rectangles, and those two rectangles are the middle term 2ab.

    Use when Holds for any real aaa and bbb, either of which may be a term with a coefficient, in which case the whole term is squared.

    e.g. (2x+3)2=4x2+12x+9(2x + 3)^2 = 4x^2 + 12x + 9(2x+3)2=4x2+12x+9.

  • Square of a difference

    (a−b)2=a2−2ab+b2(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2(a−b)2=a2−2ab+b2

    Use when Holds for any real aaa and bbb. Only the middle term is negative; the last term is +b2+b^2+b2, since squaring −b-b−b gives +b2+b^2+b2.

  • Difference of squares

    a2−b2=(a+b)(a−b)a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)a2−b2=(a+b)(a−b)
    Text description

    The square on a with a b by b corner removed cuts into two pieces that slide together into a rectangle of width a plus b and height a minus b.

    Use when The identity holds for any real aaa and bbb. To use it for recognition and factoring in this course, both terms must be perfect squares of whole-number coefficients, and the sign between them a minus. Which square is written first does not matter: 25−4x2=(5+2x)(5−2x)25 - 4x^2 = (5 + 2x)(5 - 2x)25−4x2=(5+2x)(5−2x).

    e.g. 9x2−16=(3x+4)(3x−4)9x^2 - 16 = (3x + 4)(3x - 4)9x2−16=(3x+4)(3x−4).

  • Sum of cubes

    a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)

    Use when The identity holds for any real aaa and bbb; unlike a sum of squares, this always factors. For factoring in this course, aaa and bbb are whole numbers or single variable terms with a whole-number coefficient and exponent 111 (a monomial's cube root, such as 2x2x2x from 8x38x^38x3); the trinomial carries a single ababab, never 2ab2ab2ab, and within that scope does not factor further over the integers.

    e.g. 8x3+27=(2x+3)(4x2−6x+9)8x^3 + 27 = (2x + 3)(4x^2 - 6x + 9)8x3+27=(2x+3)(4x2−6x+9).

  • Difference of cubes

    a3−b3=(a−b)(a2+ab+b2)a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)a3−b3=(a−b)(a2+ab+b2)

    Use when Holds for any real aaa and bbb; the same scope as the sum of cubes (whole numbers or exponent-111 variable terms) applies for factoring in this course. The binomial's sign flips from the sum's, and the trinomial's middle sign flips with it; the last term stays +b2+b^2+b2.

    e.g. x3−64=(x−4)(x2+4x+16)x^3 - 64 = (x - 4)(x^2 + 4x + 16)x3−64=(x−4)(x2+4x+16).

  • Perfect cubes to recognize

    1=13,8=23,27=3364=43,125=53,216=631000=103\begin{gathered} 1 = 1^3, \quad 8 = 2^3, \quad 27 = 3^3 \\ 64 = 4^3, \quad 125 = 5^3, \quad 216 = 6^3 \\ 1000 = 10^3 \end{gathered}1=13,8=23,27=3364=43,125=53,216=631000=103​

    Use when A monomial is a perfect cube when its coefficient is a perfect cube too and every variable's exponent is a multiple of 333: 8x3=(2x)38x^3 = (2x)^38x3=(2x)3, 27x3=(3x)327x^3 = (3x)^327x3=(3x)3. This chapter only cube-roots monomials whose exponent is exactly 333, which always gives an exponent-111 term for aaa or bbb; coefficient included, cube-root the whole term.

  • Clearing a single square root

    ab=abb\frac{a}{\sqrt{b}} = \frac{a\sqrt{b}}{b}b​a​=bab​​

    Use when b>0b > 0b>0. Multiply the numerator too.

  • Conjugate product, one rational term

    (a+b)(a−b)=a2−b(a + \sqrt{b})(a - \sqrt{b}) = a^2 - b(a+b​)(a−b​)=a2−b

    Use when b≥0b \ge 0b≥0: the difference of squares with b\sqrt{b}b​ as the second term. On a denominator it also needs a2≠ba^2 \neq ba2=b, since a2=ba^2 = ba2=b makes one of the two factors 000.

    e.g. (2−3)(2+3)=4−3=1(2 - \sqrt{3})(2 + \sqrt{3}) = 4 - 3 = 1(2−3​)(2+3​)=4−3=1.

  • Conjugate product, two square roots

    (a+b)(a−b)=a−b(\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}) = a - b(a​+b​)(a​−b​)=a−b

    Use when a≥0a \ge 0a≥0 and b≥0b \ge 0b≥0; both squares collapse to plain numbers. On a denominator it also needs a≠ba \neq ba=b.

    e.g. (7−5)(7+5)=7−5=2(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{5})(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{5}) = 7 - 5 = 2(7​−5​)(7​+5​)=7−5=2.

  • A shared binomial factors out

    ka+kb=k(a+b)ka + kb = k(a + b)ka+kb=k(a+b)

    Use when Any expressions kkk, aaa, bbb; kkk may be a whole binomial, not just a number.

  • Splitting the middle term (the AC method)

    Two numbers with product a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c and sum bbb split bxbxbx into two terms, turning ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c into a four-term polynomial that groups.

    Use when a≠0a \neq 0a=0, integer coefficients, and any overall common factor pulled out first. If no integer pair has both the right product and the right sum, the trinomial does not factor over the integers.

  • Zero of a difference of squares

    a2−b2=0a^2 - b^2 = 0a2−b2=0 exactly when a=ba = ba=b or a=−ba = -ba=−b: factor to (a+b)(a−b)=0(a + b)(a - b) = 0(a+b)(a−b)=0 and use the zero-product property.

    Use when Real aaa and bbb. When b≠0b \neq 0b=0 the two solutions are opposites, so this shape has two answers, not one.

    e.g. x2−9=0x^2 - 9 = 0x2−9=0 gives (x+3)(x−3)=0(x + 3)(x - 3) = 0(x+3)(x−3)=0, so x=3x = 3x=3 or x=−3x = -3x=−3.

  • Mental-math shortcut

    A product of two numbers sitting the same distance ddd either side of a round nnn is n2−d2n^2 - d^2n2−d2.

    Use when Any nnn and ddd. Worth spotting when a product is written as two numbers straddling a multiple of ten.

    e.g. 52×48=502−22=2500−4=249652 \times 48 = 50^2 - 2^2 = 2500 - 4 = 249652×48=502−22=2500−4=2496.

Problem types, step by step

Expand a squared binomial

  1. Name aaa and bbb as the two whole terms, coefficients included.
  2. Write a2a^2a2, then the middle term 2ab2ab2ab, then b2b^2b2.
  3. Take the middle sign from the binomial, plus for a sum and minus for a difference; the last term is positive either way.

e.g. (5−2x)2=52−2⋅5⋅2x+(2x)2=25−20x+4x2(5 - 2x)^2 = 5^2 - 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 2x + (2x)^2 = 25 - 20x + 4x^2(5−2x)2=52−2⋅5⋅2x+(2x)2=25−20x+4x2.

Factor a perfect-square trinomial

  1. Check that the first and last terms are perfect squares and take their roots AAA and BBB.
  2. Compute 2AB2AB2AB and compare it with the middle term, ignoring the sign.
  3. If they match, write (A+B)2(A + B)^2(A+B)2 for a positive middle term and (A−B)2(A - B)^2(A−B)2 for a negative one.
  4. If the middle term does not match, it is not a perfect square: use the AC method. If there is no middle term at all, it is not one either; test it against the difference of squares.

e.g. 25x2+20x+425x^2 + 20x + 425x2+20x+4: A=5xA = 5xA=5x, B=2B = 2B=2, and 2AB=20x2AB = 20x2AB=20x matches, so it is (5x+2)2(5x + 2)^2(5x+2)2.

Factor a difference of squares completely

  1. Pull out the greatest common factor first; the squares often appear only once it is gone.
  2. Confirm two terms, both perfect squares, joined by a minus.
  3. Take the root of each term to get AAA and BBB, then write (A+B)(A−B)(A + B)(A - B)(A+B)(A−B).
  4. Inspect each new factor: one that is again a difference of squares factors again, while a sum of squares stops over the reals.

e.g. 16x4−81=(4x2+9)(4x2−9)=(4x2+9)(2x+3)(2x−3)16x^4 - 81 = (4x^2 + 9)(4x^2 - 9) = (4x^2 + 9)(2x + 3)(2x - 3)16x4−81=(4x2+9)(4x2−9)=(4x2+9)(2x+3)(2x−3).

Factor a sum or difference of cubes

  1. Pull out the greatest common factor first.
  2. Cube-root each term to get aaa and bbb.
  3. Write the binomial with the same sign as the original expression.
  4. Write the trinomial a2∓ab+b2a^2 \mp ab + b^2a2∓ab+b2: the middle sign is opposite the binomial's, the middle term is a single ababab, and the last term is +b2+b^2+b2.
  5. Stop there: the trinomial does not factor over the integers.

e.g. 54x3+16=2(27x3+8)=2(3x+2)(9x2−6x+4)54x^3 + 16 = 2(27x^3 + 8) = 2(3x + 2)(9x^2 - 6x + 4)54x3+16=2(27x3+8)=2(3x+2)(9x2−6x+4).

Rationalize a single-root denominator

  1. Simplify the radical first if it is not in simplest form.
  2. Multiply the numerator and the denominator by that square root.
  3. Reduce any common factor top and bottom.

e.g. 82=822=42\frac{8}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{8\sqrt{2}}{2} = 4\sqrt{2}2​8​=282​​=42​.

Rationalize a two-term denominator with the conjugate

  1. Write the conjugate: the same two terms with the sign between them flipped.
  2. Multiply the numerator and the denominator by it.
  3. Expand the denominator as a difference of squares, a2−ba^2 - ba2−b for one root and a−ba - ba−b for two.
  4. Multiply out the numerator, or leave it factored when a common factor may cancel.
  5. Cancel common factors and simplify.

e.g. 45+1=4(5−1)5−1=5−1\frac{4}{\sqrt{5} + 1} = \frac{4(\sqrt{5} - 1)}{5 - 1} = \sqrt{5} - 15​+14​=5−14(5​−1)​=5​−1.

Factor a four-term polynomial by grouping

  1. Pull out any overall common factor.
  2. Split the four terms into two pairs and factor the GCF out of each pair.
  3. When the second pair leads with a negative term, factor out a negative so its binomial matches the first pair's.
  4. If both pairs leave the same binomial, factor it out; the leftovers form the second factor.
  5. If they differ, reorder the terms (usually descending by degree) and retry; some four-term polynomials do not group under any ordering.
  6. Expand to check.

e.g. x3−4x2−2x+8=x2(x−4)−2(x−4)=(x−4)(x2−2)x^3 - 4x^2 - 2x + 8 = x^2(x - 4) - 2(x - 4) = (x - 4)(x^2 - 2)x3−4x2−2x+8=x2(x−4)−2(x−4)=(x−4)(x2−2).

Factor ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c by the AC method

  1. Pull out any overall common factor.
  2. Compute a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c.
  3. Find two numbers with product a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c and sum bbb; they have opposite signs when a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c is negative.
  4. Rewrite bxbxbx as those two terms, making a four-term polynomial.
  5. Group, factor out the shared binomial, and expand to check.

e.g. 6x2+7x−36x^2 + 7x - 36x2+7x−3: ac=−18ac = -18ac=−18, so use 999 and −2-2−2, giving 3x(2x+3)−1(2x+3)=(2x+3)(3x−1)3x(2x + 3) - 1(2x + 3) = (2x + 3)(3x - 1)3x(2x+3)−1(2x+3)=(2x+3)(3x−1).

Choose the pattern and factor completely

  1. Factor out the greatest common factor first, always.
  2. Two terms: difference of squares, then sum or difference of cubes.
  3. Three terms: test for a perfect square, otherwise the AC method.
  4. Four terms: group.
  5. Re-examine every factor and repeat until nothing factors again.

Exam traps

  • Trap Squaring a sum term by term, (a+b)2=a2+b2(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2(a+b)2=a2+b2.

    Fix The cross-product 2ab2ab2ab is part of the answer and vanishes only when aaa or bbb is 000: (3+4)2=49(3 + 4)^2 = 49(3+4)2=49 while 32+42=253^2 + 4^2 = 2532+42=25, and the missing 242424 is exactly 2ab2ab2ab.

  • Trap Squaring only the variable, or matching a term to the wrong square root: (2x)2=2x2(2x)^2 = 2x^2(2x)2=2x2, or reading 9x29x^29x2 as (9x)2(9x)^2(9x)2.

    Fix The coefficient comes along: (2x)2=4x2(2x)^2 = 4x^2(2x)2=4x2, and 9x29x^29x2 is the square of 3x3x3x, not 9x9x9x, since (3x)2=9x2(3x)^2 = 9x^2(3x)2=9x2.

  • Trap Giving both difference-of-squares factors the same sign: a2−b2=(a−b)(a−b)a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a - b)a2−b2=(a−b)(a−b).

    Fix One plus and one minus. (a−b)2(a - b)^2(a−b)2 is the three-term a2−2ab+b2a^2 - 2ab + b^2a2−2ab+b2, a different expression.

  • Trap Assuming a sum of cubes will not factor, by analogy with a sum of squares.

    Fix Only the sum of SQUARES refuses over the reals. A sum of cubes always factors: a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)a3+b3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2).

  • Trap Reading a3+b3a^3 + b^3a3+b3 as (a+b)3(a + b)^3(a+b)3.

    Fix They differ: (a+b)3=a3+3a2b+3ab2+b3(a + b)^3 = a^3 + 3a^2 b + 3ab^2 + b^3(a+b)3=a3+3a2b+3ab2+b3 has two extra middle terms.

  • Trap Doubling the cube trinomial's middle term: x3−64=(x−4)(x2+8x+16)x^3 - 64 = (x - 4)(x^2 + 8x + 16)x3−64=(x−4)(x2+8x+16).

    Fix The cube trinomial carries a single ababab, so it is (x−4)(x2+4x+16)(x - 4)(x^2 + 4x + 16)(x−4)(x2+4x+16). The doubled 2ab2ab2ab belongs to the perfect-square trinomial, a different pattern.

  • Trap The sign trap in the second pair: factoring +3+3+3 out of −3x+6-3x + 6−3x+6 gives 3(2−x)3(2 - x)3(2−x), whose binomial does not match the x−2x - 2x−2 from the first pair.

    Fix Factor out −3-3−3 instead, so both pairs carry x−2x - 2x−2: x2(x−2)−3(x−2)=(x−2)(x2−3)x^2(x - 2) - 3(x - 2) = (x - 2)(x^2 - 3)x2(x−2)−3(x−2)=(x−2)(x2−3).

  • Trap Dropping the 111 when a pair reduces to a bare binomial, reading 2x(x+3)+(x+3)2x(x + 3) + (x + 3)2x(x+3)+(x+3) as (x+3)(2x)(x + 3)(2x)(x+3)(2x).

    Fix The bare binomial carries a coefficient of 111: 2x(x+3)+1(x+3)=(x+3)(2x+1)2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3) = (x + 3)(2x + 1)2x(x+3)+1(x+3)=(x+3)(2x+1).

  • Trap Choosing the AC split by the sum alone, as with 10x=5x+5x10x = 5x + 5x10x=5x+5x for 3x2+10x+83x^2 + 10x + 83x2+10x+8.

    Fix The pair must also multiply to a⋅c=24a \cdot c = 24a⋅c=24, and 5⋅5=255 \cdot 5 = 255⋅5=25, so the working split is 666 and 444.

  • Trap Stopping while a factor still factors, leaving 2(x2−9)2(x^2 - 9)2(x2−9) or (x+2)(4x2+6)(x + 2)(4x^2 + 6)(x+2)(4x2+6) as the answer.

    Fix Finish both: 2(x+3)(x−3)2(x + 3)(x - 3)2(x+3)(x−3), and 2(x+2)(2x2+3)2(x + 2)(2x^2 + 3)2(x+2)(2x2+3).

  • Trap Adding under a conjugate: (6−5)(6+5)=6+5=11(\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{5})(\sqrt{6} + \sqrt{5}) = 6 + 5 = 11(6​−5​)(6​+5​)=6+5=11.

    Fix The middle terms cancel and the squares subtract, giving 6−5=16 - 5 = 16−5=1.
