  1. Problem 1 Two possible stops

    The figure marks a starting point AAA and possible stops BBB and CCC. Which stop is closer to AAA by straight-line distance?

    Text description of this figure

    A square coordinate grid. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative four to three and the vertical y-axis runs from negative two to five, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number, equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. Three points are plotted, each marked with a filled dot and labeled with its letter only: A sits three units left of the y-axis and one unit above the x-axis; B sits one unit right of the y-axis and four units above the x-axis; C sits two units right of the y-axis and one unit below the x-axis. One thin straight segment joins A to B and another thin straight segment joins A to C. No lengths, right triangles or other guide lines are drawn.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the two distances using their horizontal and vertical gaps.

    2. Hint 2

      Comparing squared distances is enough to identify the shorter distance.

    Answer

    BBB is closer to AAA.

    Full solution

    The points read from the grid are A=(−3,1)A=(-3,1)A=(−3,1), B=(1,4)B=(1,4)B=(1,4), and C=(2,−1)C=(2,-1)C=(2,−1).

    The squared distance to BBB is

    AB2=42+32AB^2=4^2+3^2AB2=42+32

    Thus AB2=25AB^2=25AB2=25 and AB=5AB=5AB=5.

    The squared distance to CCC is

    AC2=52+(−2)2AC^2=5^2+(-2)^2AC2=52+(−2)2

    This is 292929, so

    AC=29>5.AC=\sqrt{29}>5.AC=29​>5.

    Therefore BBB is closer.

    Answer

    BBB is closer to AAA.

    Key idea

    Straight-line distances can be ordered by comparing their squared coordinate gaps.

  2. Problem 2 From diameter to equation

    The equation (x+2)2+(y−5)2=R(x+2)^2+(y-5)^2=R(x+2)2+(y−5)2=R describes a circle whose diameter is 666 units. Find RRR.

    1. Hint 1

      The radius is half the diameter.

    2. Hint 2

      The right side of standard form is the square of the radius.

    Answer

    R=9R=9R=9.

    Full solution

    The radius is 6/2=36/2=36/2=3 units.

    Standard form therefore requires

    R=32R=3^2R=32

    Hence R=9R=9R=9.

    The circle of radius 333 has diameter 2×3=62\times3=62×3=6, as required.

    Answer

    R=9R=9R=9.

    Key idea

    The right side of standard form is one quarter of the squared diameter.

  3. Problem 3 A center on a line

    A circle has radius 333 units. Its center lies on the line y=2x−1y=2x-1y=2x−1 and has x-coordinate −2-2−2. Write the circle equation in standard form.

    1. Hint 1

      First determine the missing center coordinate from the line.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract each center coordinate inside its square and square the radius.

    Answer

    (x+2)2+(y+5)2=9(x+2)^2+(y+5)^2=9(x+2)2+(y+5)2=9.

    Full solution

    The center height is

    y=2(−2)−1y=2(-2)-1y=2(−2)−1

    This is −5-5−5, so the center is (−2,−5)(-2,-5)(−2,−5).

    Substituting this center and radius 333 gives

    (x+2)2+(y+5)2=9(x+2)^2+(y+5)^2=9(x+2)2+(y+5)2=9

    Answer

    (x+2)2+(y+5)2=9(x+2)^2+(y+5)^2=9(x+2)2+(y+5)2=9.

    Key idea

    A condition locating the center can be combined with the radius to write a circle equation.

  4. Problem 4 Two boundary points

    The figure shows A=(−1,2)A=(-1,2)A=(−1,2) and B=(5,4)B=(5,4)B=(5,4). A circle through both points has its center on the x-axis. Find its center and write its equation in standard form.

    Text description of this figure

    A coordinate grid. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative three to nine and the vertical y-axis runs from negative five to five, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number, equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. Two points are plotted, each marked with a filled dot and labeled with its letter and its coordinates: A at negative one, two, and B at five, four. Nothing else appears on the grid: no circle, no center, no radius, no midpoint and no construction lines.

    1. Hint 1

      Write the center as (h,0)(h,0)(h,0) and use equal squared distances to the two boundary points.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the two distance expressions to find hhh, then recover the radius squared.

    Answer

    Center (3,0)(3,0)(3,0); (x−3)2+y2=20(x-3)^2+y^2=20(x−3)2+y2=20.

    Full solution

    Both points are the same distance from (h,0)(h,0)(h,0), so

    (h+1)2+4=(h−5)2+16(h+1)^2+4=(h-5)^2+16(h+1)2+4=(h−5)2+16

    Expand both squares and cancel h2h^2h2.

    2h+5=−10h+412h+5=-10h+412h+5=−10h+41

    This gives h=3h=3h=3.

    The squared distance to AAA is (−1−3)2+22=20(-1-3)^2+2^2=20(−1−3)2+22=20, so the equation is

    (x−3)2+y2=20(x-3)^2+y^2=20(x−3)2+y2=20

    For BBB, the squared distance is 22+42=202^2+4^2=2022+42=20 too, confirming that both points lie on the circle.

    Answer

    Center (3,0)(3,0)(3,0); (x−3)2+y2=20(x-3)^2+y^2=20(x−3)2+y2=20.

    Key idea

    A center constrained to a line can be found by equating distances to two points on the circle.

  5. Problem 5 Two circular outlines

    Two outlines have equations x2+y2+6x−4y+9=0x^2+y^2+6x-4y+9=0x2+y2+6x−4y+9=0 and (x+3)2+(y−2)2=9(x+3)^2+(y-2)^2=9(x+3)2+(y−2)2=9. On the blank grid, sketch both circles. State each circle's center and radius, and the difference between the radii.

    Text description of this figure

    A blank coordinate grid to sketch on. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative eight to two and the vertical y-axis runs from negative three to seven, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number, equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. Nothing is plotted on the grid: no curves, no points, no center and no labels other than the axis numbers.

    1. Hint 1

      Put the first equation in center-radius form by completing both squares.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the two radii, then use four radius steps around each center to sketch that circle.

    Answer

    Both centers are (−3,2)(-3,2)(−3,2); the first circle has radius 222 and the second radius 333; difference 111; the smaller sketch passes through (−5,2)(-5,2)(−5,2), (−1,2)(-1,2)(−1,2), (−3,0)(-3,0)(−3,0), (−3,4)(-3,4)(−3,4) and the larger through (−6,2)(-6,2)(−6,2), (0,2)(0,2)(0,2), (−3,−1)(-3,-1)(−3,−1), (−3,5)(-3,5)(−3,5).

    Full solution

    Group the variable terms in the first equation and move the constant across.

    (x2+6x)+(y2−4y)=−9(x^2+6x)+(y^2-4y)=-9(x2+6x)+(y2−4y)=−9

    Add 999 and 444 to both sides to complete the squares, so the right side becomes −9+9+4-9+9+4−9+9+4.

    (x+3)2+(y−2)2=4(x+3)^2+(y-2)^2=4(x+3)2+(y−2)2=4

    The first right side is 444, so the first radius is 4=2\sqrt{4}=24​=2, and the second right side is 999, so the second radius is

    9=3.\sqrt{9}=3.9​=3.

    Both centers are (−3,2)(-3,2)(−3,2), and their radii differ by 111.

    For the smaller sketch, use the points (−5,2)(-5,2)(−5,2), (−1,2)(-1,2)(−1,2), (−3,0)(-3,0)(−3,0), and (−3,4)(-3,4)(−3,4).

    For the larger, use (−6,2)(-6,2)(−6,2), (0,2)(0,2)(0,2), (−3,−1)(-3,-1)(−3,−1), and (−3,5)(-3,5)(−3,5).

    Draw round curves through each group.

    Answer

    Both centers are (−3,2)(-3,2)(−3,2); the first circle has radius 222 and the second radius 333; difference 111; the smaller sketch passes through (−5,2)(-5,2)(−5,2), (−1,2)(-1,2)(−1,2), (−3,0)(-3,0)(−3,0), (−3,4)(-3,4)(−3,4) and the larger through (−6,2)(-6,2)(−6,2), (0,2)(0,2)(0,2), (−3,−1)(-3,-1)(−3,−1), (−3,5)(-3,5)(−3,5).

    Key idea

    Completing both squares can reveal that differently written circle equations share a center.

  6. Problem 6 A changed circular sign

    A circular sign has boundary (x−1)2+(y+2)2=9(x-1)^2+(y+2)^2=9(x−1)2+(y+2)2=9. A new sign has twice its radius, and its center is 444 units left and 111 unit up from the old center. Write the new boundary equation and give its center and radius.

    1. Hint 1

      Read the original center and take the square root to find the original radius.

    2. Hint 2

      Change the two center coordinates separately, then square the new radius in the equation.

    Answer

    (x+3)2+(y+1)2=36(x+3)^2+(y+1)^2=36(x+3)2+(y+1)2=36; center (−3,−1)(-3,-1)(−3,−1); radius 666 units.

    Full solution

    The original center is (1,−2)(1,-2)(1,−2) and radius is 333.

    Moving left four and up one puts the new center at (−3,−1)(-3,-1)(−3,−1).

    Doubling the radius gives 666.

    The new standard equation is

    (x+3)2+(y+1)2=36(x+3)^2+(y+1)^2=36(x+3)2+(y+1)2=36

    The center change is (−4,1)(-4,1)(−4,1) and the radius ratio is 6/3=26/3=26/3=2, checking the requested changes.

    Answer

    (x+3)2+(y+1)2=36(x+3)^2+(y+1)^2=36(x+3)2+(y+1)2=36; center (−3,−1)(-3,-1)(−3,−1); radius 666 units.

    Key idea

    Change a circle’s center and radius before translating them back into its equation.

  7. Problem 7 A changing constant

    For each of k=8k=8k=8, k=10k=10k=10, and k=12k=12k=12, describe the real graph of 2x2+2y2−8x+4y+k=02x^2+2y^2-8x+4y+k=02x2+2y2−8x+4y+k=0. For each value, say whether the graph is a circle, a single point, or empty, and give the center and radius, or the point's coordinates, wherever there is one.

    1. Hint 1

      Divide by the common leading coefficient before completing both squares.

    2. Hint 2

      Inspect whether the resulting right side is positive, zero, or negative for each value.

    Answer

    k=8k=8k=8: circle, center (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1), radius 111. k=10k=10k=10: point (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1). k=12k=12k=12: empty (no real points).

    Full solution

    Divide by 222, then complete the squares in xxx and yyy.

    (x−2)2+(y+1)2=5−k2(x-2)^2+(y+1)^2=5-\frac k2(x−2)2+(y+1)2=5−2k​

    At k=8k=8k=8, the right side is 111, giving a circle centered at (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1) with radius 111.

    At k=10k=10k=10, it is 000, forcing both squares to zero and giving the point (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1).

    At k=12k=12k=12, it is −1-1−1, impossible for a sum of real squares.

    Answer

    k=8k=8k=8: circle, center (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1), radius 111. k=10k=10k=10: point (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1). k=12k=12k=12: empty (no real points).

    Key idea

    The right side after completing both squares determines whether the graph is a circle, a point, or empty.

  8. Problem 8 A proposed center

    A student says that whenever a circle passes through two different points, their midpoint is its center. Is the claim correct? Give a circle and two points that justify your conclusion.

    1. Hint 1

      The midpoint is the center when the two points are endpoints of a diameter.

    2. Hint 2

      Choose two points on a circle that are not opposite each other.

    Answer

    False; x2+y2=9x^2+y^2=9x2+y2=9 passes through (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) and (0,3)(0,3)(0,3), whose midpoint (3/2,3/2)(3/2,3/2)(3/2,3/2) is not its center.

    Full solution

    The circle x2+y2=9x^2+y^2=9x2+y2=9 has center (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) and radius 333.

    The points (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) and (0,3)(0,3)(0,3) both satisfy its equation.

    Their midpoint is (3/2,3/2)(3/2,3/2)(3/2,3/2), which differs from (0,0)(0,0)(0,0).

    They are not the ends of a diameter, so their midpoint need not be the center.

    Answer

    False; x2+y2=9x^2+y^2=9x2+y2=9 passes through (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) and (0,3)(0,3)(0,3), whose midpoint (3/2,3/2)(3/2,3/2)(3/2,3/2) is not its center.

    Key idea

    The midpoint rule locates a circle’s center when the given segment is a diameter.

  9. Problem 9 Inside, on, or outside

    A circle has equation (x+2)2+(y−3)2=29(x+2)^2+(y-3)^2=29(x+2)2+(y−3)2=29. Without taking any square root, decide whether each of (0,8)(0,8)(0,8), (1,1)(1,1)(1,1), and (3,7)(3,7)(3,7) is inside, on, or outside the circle. Then explain why comparing squared distances gives the same verdict as comparing the distances themselves.

    1. Hint 1

      A point's place depends only on how its distance from the center compares with the radius.

    2. Hint 2

      Work out each point's squared distance from the center and compare it with the right side.

    Answer

    (0,8)(0,8)(0,8) lies on the circle, (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) lies inside it, and (3,7)(3,7)(3,7) lies outside it; squaring preserves the order of nonnegative numbers, so the squared comparison gives the same verdict as comparing the distances.

    Full solution

    The center is (−2,3)(-2,3)(−2,3) and the right side is the radius squared, 292929.

    A point is inside, on, or outside according to whether its squared distance from the center is less than, equal to, or greater than 292929.

    Squaring keeps the order of two nonnegative numbers, and a distance and a radius are both nonnegative, so this squared comparison gives the same verdict as comparing the distance itself with the radius.

    For (0,8)(0,8)(0,8) the squared distance from the center is

    (0+2)2+(8−3)2=29(0+2)^2+(8-3)^2=29(0+2)2+(8−3)2=29

    That matches the radius squared, so (0,8)(0,8)(0,8) lies on the circle.

    For (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) the squared distance is

    (1+2)2+(1−3)2=13(1+2)^2+(1-3)^2=13(1+2)2+(1−3)2=13

    Since 13<2913<2913<29, the point (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) lies inside.

    For (3,7)(3,7)(3,7) the squared distance is

    (3+2)2+(7−3)2=41(3+2)^2+(7-3)^2=41(3+2)2+(7−3)2=41

    Since 41>2941>2941>29, the point (3,7)(3,7)(3,7) lies outside.

    Answer

    (0,8)(0,8)(0,8) lies on the circle, (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) lies inside it, and (3,7)(3,7)(3,7) lies outside it; squaring preserves the order of nonnegative numbers, so the squared comparison gives the same verdict as comparing the distances.

    Key idea

    Comparing a point's squared distance with the radius squared decides inside, on, or outside with no square root.

  10. Problem 10 An altered right side

    A circle equation in standard form has right side 161616. A student adds 999 to that right side and says the radius increases by 333. Is the claim correct? State the actual increase.

    1. Hint 1

      The radius is the square root of the entire right side.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the two radii separately before subtracting them.

    Answer

    False; the radius increases by 111 unit.

    Full solution

    The original radius is

    16=4.\sqrt{16}=4.16​=4.

    The altered right side is 252525, with radius

    25=5.\sqrt{25}=5.25​=5.

    The increase is

    5−4=15-4=15−4=1

    Adding 999 to the squared radius does not add 9\sqrt99​ to the radius.

    Answer

    False; the radius increases by 111 unit.

    Key idea

    A change in radius squared must be interpreted through the new square root, not by adding separate roots.
