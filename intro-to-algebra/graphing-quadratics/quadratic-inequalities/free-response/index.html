    1. Hint 1

    Answer

    ≤\le≤.

    Full solution

    Between −2-2−2 and 111, one factor is positive and the other negative, so the product is negative.

    For example, at x=0x=0x=0,

    (0+2)(0−1)=−2(0+2)(0-1)=-2(0+2)(0−1)=−2

    The filled endpoints include the two roots, where the product is zero.

    The selected values therefore make the product negative or zero, requiring ≤\le≤.

    Answer

    ≤\le≤.

    Key idea

    A shaded interval and its endpoint markers determine the inequality symbol once the sign of the leading coefficient is known.

  2. Problem 2 A sign record

    A quadratic has distinct real roots −4-4−4 and 222, and its value at x=0x=0x=0 is negative. Determine whether its value at x=3x=3x=3 is positive or negative.

    1. Hint 1

      The two roots divide the number line into three sign intervals.

    2. Hint 2

      The known value lies between the roots, while the requested input lies to their right.

    Answer

    Positive.

    Full solution

    Write the quadratic as a(x+4)(x−2)a(x+4)(x-2)a(x+4)(x−2), with a≠0a\ne0a=0.

    Its value at x=0x=0x=0 is

    a(0+4)(0−2)=−8aa(0+4)(0-2)=-8aa(0+4)(0−2)=−8a

    This is negative, so a>0a>0a>0.

    Its value at x=3x=3x=3 is

    a(3+4)(3−2)=7aa(3+4)(3-2)=7aa(3+4)(3−2)=7a

    That is positive, as expected for the interval to the right of both roots.

    Answer

    Positive.

    Key idea

    Knowing the sign in one interval determines the other intervals of a quadratic with two distinct roots.

  4. Problem 4 Comparing two expressions

    Two expressions are 2x2−x+32x^2-x+32x2−x+3 and x2+4x+9x^2+4x+9x2+4x+9. Find all real xxx for which the first is at most the second, and show the solution on the blank number line in the figure.

    Text description of this figure

    A single number line with an arrowhead at each end. It carries a tick and a label at every integer from negative 3 through 8. The line carries no circles, no shading, and no other marks.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the difference of the two expressions with zero.

    2. Hint 2

      Find its roots and test one point in each interval.

    3. Hint 3

      The inclusive comparison determines whether the endpoints are included.

    Answer

    −1≤x≤6-1\le x\le6−1≤x≤6; on the number line, filled circles at −1-1−1 and 666 with the segment between them shaded.

    Full solution

    Subtract the second expression from both sides.

    x2−5x−6≤0x^2-5x-6\le0x2−5x−6≤0

    Its factorization is

    (x+1)(x−6)≤0(x+1)(x-6)\le0(x+1)(x−6)≤0

    Test x=−2x=-2x=−2, 000, and 777 in the three intervals.

    The products are 888, −6-6−6, and 888, respectively, so the middle interval is negative and the outer intervals positive.

    Include roots −1-1−1 and 666 because equality is allowed.

    On the number line, fill these endpoints and shade between them.

    Answer

    −1≤x≤6-1\le x\le6−1≤x≤6; on the number line, filled circles at −1-1−1 and 666 with the segment between them shaded.

    Key idea

    Subtracting one expression from another turns a comparison into the sign of a single quadratic.

  5. Problem 5 A moving point

    A circle has equation (x−1)2+(y+2)2=25(x-1)^2+(y+2)^2=25(x−1)2+(y+2)2=25. A point has coordinates (t,1)(t,1)(t,1), with real ttt. Find all values of ttt for which the point is strictly outside the circle.

    1. Hint 1

      Outside means the squared distance from the center is greater than the squared radius.

    2. Hint 2

      Substitute the fixed y-coordinate, then find the roots that separate the permitted horizontal positions.

    Answer

    t<−3t<-3t<−3 or t>5t>5t>5.

    Full solution

    The center is (1,−2)(1,-2)(1,−2).

    The outside condition is

    (t−1)2+9>25(t-1)^2+9>25(t−1)2+9>25

    Subtract 161616 after collecting constants.

    (t−1)2−16>0(t-1)^2-16>0(t−1)2−16>0

    This factors as (t+3)(t−5)>0(t+3)(t-5)>0(t+3)(t−5)>0, with roots −3-3−3 and 555.

    The upward quadratic is positive outside these roots.

    The roots themselves put the point on the circle, so exclude them.

    Answer

    t<−3t<-3t<−3 or t>5t>5t>5.

    Key idea

    A point constrained to a horizontal line can be tested against a circle using a quadratic inequality.

  6. Problem 6 A limit on diagonals

    A convex polygon with nnn sides has n(n−3)2\tfrac{n(n-3)}{2}2n(n−3)​ diagonals, where nnn is a whole number with n≥3n\ge3n≥3. Find every nnn for which the polygon has at most 202020 diagonals.

    Full solution

  7. Problem 7 An exact boundary

    Solve x(2x+3)>4x(2x+3)>4x(2x+3)>4. Give exact endpoints.

      Expand the product first, then gather every term on one side.

    2. Hint 2

    2x2+3x−4>02x^2+3x-4>02x2+3x−4>0

    The discriminant is 9+32=419+32=419+32=41, so there are two real roots.

    x=−3±414x=\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{41}}4x=4−3±41​​

    The parabola opens upward, so the expression is positive outside the roots, and the strict symbol excludes them.

    Since 41≈6.40\sqrt{41}\approx6.4041​≈6.40, the roots are about −2.35-2.35−2.35 and 0.850.850.85.

    A check confirms the choice of intervals.

    At x=0x=0x=0, which lies between the roots, the left side of the original inequality is 000 and the right side is 444, so that middle interval is correctly left out.

    Answer

  8. Problem 8 A divided product

    A student solves (x−1)(x+4)>0(x-1)(x+4)>0(x−1)(x+4)>0 by dividing by x−1x-1x−1 and reporting x>−4x>-4x>−4. Decide whether this solution is correct, and give the complete solution set.

    1. Hint 1

      The divisor can be positive, negative, or zero.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the roots of the original product and test the sign in each interval.

    Answer

    Incorrect; x<−4x<-4x<−4 or x>1x>1x>1.

    Full solution

    The roots are −4-4−4 and 111.

    At x=−5x=-5x=−5 the product is 6>06>06>0, at x=0x=0x=0 it is −4<0-4<0−4<0, and at x=2x=2x=2 it is 6>06>06>0.

    The correct solution is the two outer intervals, excluding the roots.

    Division by x−1x-1x−1 is invalid at x=1x=1x=1 and reverses the inequality when x<1x<1x<1.

    Ignoring those conditions loses valid inputs such as −5-5−5 and includes invalid ones such as 000.

    Answer

    Incorrect; x<−4x<-4x<−4 or x>1x>1x>1.

    Key idea

    Dividing an inequality by a variable expression requires knowing both its nonzero status and its sign.

  10. Problem 10 Two possible instructions
