  1. Problem 1 Two chords locate the vertex

    ★ Stretch Difficulty: 1 of 3 stars, Stretch

    A parabola of the form y=ax2+bx+cy=ax^2+bx+cy=ax2+bx+c, with a≠0a\ne0a=0, passes through (−1,6)(-1,6)(−1,6), (5,6)(5,6)(5,6), (1,−2)(1,-2)(1,−2), and (3,−2)(3,-2)(3,−2).

    Find its equation without solving three simultaneous equations. Then find the area of the triangle whose vertices are (−1,6)(-1,6)(−1,6), (5,6)(5,6)(5,6), and the vertex of the parabola. Explain why your parabola is the only possibility.

    Builds on Parabolas

    1. Hint 1

      Equal heights reveal a symmetry line before any coefficients are known.

    2. Hint 2

      Write y=a(x−h)2+ky=a(x-h)^2+ky=a(x−h)2+k. Compare the heights at horizontal distances 333 and 111 from the symmetry line.

    Answer

    y=(x−2)2−3y=(x-2)^2-3y=(x−2)2−3; the triangle has area 272727 square units.

    Full solution

    For a quadratic, two distinct inputs with equal outputs lie equally far from its axis.

    Indeed, subtracting their equations gives (u−v)[a(u+v)+b]=0(u-v)[a(u+v)+b]=0(u−v)[a(u+v)+b]=0, so their midpoint is −b/(2a)-b/(2a)−b/(2a).

    Both given pairs have midpoint 222.

    Thus the equation must be

    y=a(x−2)2+k.y=a(x-2)^2+k.y=a(x−2)2+k.

    The outer pair gives 9a+k=69a+k=69a+k=6; the inner pair gives a+k=−2a+k=-2a+k=−2.

    Subtraction gives 8a=88a=88a=8, so a=1a=1a=1 and k=−3k=-3k=−3.

    These values satisfy all four points, and the two independent height equations allow no other values.

    The vertex is (2,−3)(2,-3)(2,−3).

    The triangle has horizontal base length 666 and perpendicular height 6−(−3)=96-(-3)=96−(−3)=9, so its area is

    12⋅6⋅9=27.\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot9=27.21​⋅6⋅9=27.

    The height is a vertical distance, not the distance from the vertex to either end of the chord.

    Answer

    y=(x−2)2−3y=(x-2)^2-3y=(x−2)2−3; the triangle has area 272727 square units.

    Key idea

    Use equal-output pairs to expose symmetry before expanding an equation.

  2. Problem 2 A direction with no preferred axis

    ★ Stretch Difficulty: 1 of 3 stars, Stretch

    A circle passes through (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), (6,0)(6,0)(6,0), and (0,8)(0,8)(0,8). Find the greatest possible value of x+yx+yx+y for a point (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) on this circle, and find every point attaining it. Justify the maximum without calculus.

  3. Problem 3 Exactly one meeting point

    ★ Stretch Difficulty: 1 of 3 stars, Stretch

    Find every nonvertical line through (0,−1)(0,-1)(0,−1) that meets the parabola y=x2−4x+3y=x^2-4x+3y=x2−4x+3 in exactly one distinct real point. Give each line and its meeting point, and prove your list is complete.

    Builds on Parabolas

    1. Hint 1

      Write every allowed line using a single unknown slope.

    2. Hint 2

      After substitution, exactly one meeting point means that a quadratic has a repeated real root.

    Answer

    y=−1y=-1y=−1, meeting at (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1); and y=−8x−1y=-8x-1y=−8x−1, meeting at (−2,15)(-2,15)(−2,15).

    Full solution

    Every nonvertical line through (0,−1)(0,-1)(0,−1) has the form y=mx−1y=mx-1y=mx−1 for a real number mmm.

    A point is on both graphs precisely when x2−4x+3=mx−1x^2-4x+3=mx-1x2−4x+3=mx−1, or

    x2−(m+4)x+4=0.x^2-(m+4)x+4=0.x2−(m+4)x+4=0.

    The coefficient of x2x^2x2 never vanishes, so the usual discriminant test applies for every slope.

    There is exactly one distinct real solution if and only if

    (m+4)2−16=0.(m+4)^2-16=0.(m+4)2−16=0.

    Thus m+4=4m+4=4m+4=4 or m+4=−4m+4=-4m+4=−4, giving m=0m=0m=0 or m=−8m=-8m=−8.

    For m=0m=0m=0 the equation is (x−2)2=0(x-2)^2=0(x−2)2=0 and the common point is (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1).

    For m=−8m=-8m=−8 it is (x+2)2=0(x+2)^2=0(x+2)2=0 and the common point is (−2,15)(-2,15)(−2,15).

    Both points satisfy their original line and parabola equations.

    Since every permitted line was represented by mmm, no other line can qualify.

    The nonvertical condition matters: the vertical line x=0x=0x=0 would also have exactly one meeting point.

    Answer

    y=−1y=-1y=−1, meeting at (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1); and y=−8x−1y=-8x-1y=−8x−1, meeting at (−2,15)(-2,15)(−2,15).

    Key idea

    Translate a geometric count into the number of distinct real roots, and check excluded line types.

  4. Problem 4 Counting integer points below the axis

    ★★ Challenge Difficulty: 2 of 3 stars, Challenge

    For a real parameter kkk, consider the parabola y=x2−5x+ky=x^2-5x+ky=x2−5x+k.

    (a) Find all kkk for which exactly four integer values of xxx give a point strictly below the xxx-axis.

    (b) Can exactly five integer values of xxx give such a point? Prove your answer.

      Reflection across the symmetry line pairs integer inputs.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the heights at x=1,4x=1,4x=1,4 with those at x=0,5x=0,5x=0,5. Remember that points on the axis do not count.

    (a) 0≤k<40\le k<40≤k<4. (b) No; the number is always even.

    Full solution

    Write

    y=(x−52)2+k−254.y=(x-\tfrac52)^2+k-\tfrac{25}{4}.y=(x−25​)2+k−425​.

    The axis is x=5/2x=5/2x=5/2, so the two inputs xxx and 5−x5-x5−x always give the same height.

    No integer is fixed by this pairing.

    There are only finitely many points below the axis because the square eventually becomes larger than any fixed number.

    They therefore occur in pairs, proving part (b).

    For exactly four qualifying integers, the nearest four integers to 5/25/25/2 must qualify and all others must fail.

    These are 1,2,3,41,2,3,41,2,3,4.

    The height at 111 and 444 is k−4k-4k−4, so they qualify exactly when k<4k<4k<4.

    The next pair, 000 and 555, has height kkk, so it must satisfy k≥0k\ge0k≥0.

    If 0≤k<40\le k<40≤k<4, the inner pair 2,32,32,3 has height k−6<0k-6<0k−6<0, while every integer outside 1,2,3,41,2,3,41,2,3,4 is at least as far from the axis as 000 or 555 and has nonnegative height.

    At k=0k=0k=0 the outer pair lies on the axis and does not count; at k=4k=4k=4 the pair 1,41,41,4 no longer counts.

    Answer

    (a) 0≤k<40\le k<40≤k<4. (b) No; the number is always even.

    Key idea

    Symmetry can force a parity restriction on a count before any roots are calculated.
