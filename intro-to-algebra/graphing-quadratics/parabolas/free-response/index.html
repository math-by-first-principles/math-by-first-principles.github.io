  1. Problem 1 A missing coefficient

    The graph of y=3x2+bx+2y=3x^2+bx+2y=3x2+bx+2 has axis of symmetry x=−2x=-2x=−2. Find bbb.

    1. Hint 1

      The axis location depends on the leading and linear coefficients.

    2. Hint 2

      Substitute the known axis coordinate into −b/(2a)-b/(2a)−b/(2a).

    Answer

    b=12b=12b=12.

    Full solution

    Here a=3a=3a=3, so the given axis requires

    −b6=−2-\frac b6=-2−6b​=−2

    Multiplying by −6-6−6 gives b=12b=12b=12.

    Checking the axis of 3x2+12x+23x^2+12x+23x2+12x+2 gives −12/6=−2-12/6=-2−12/6=−2.

    Answer

    b=12b=12b=12.

    Key idea

    An axis of symmetry can determine a missing linear coefficient.

  2. Problem 2 A marked curve

  3. Problem 3 A vertex condition

    The highest point of y=−2(x−3)2+ky=-2(x-3)^2+ky=−2(x−3)2+k also lies on the line y=2x+1y=2x+1y=2x+1. Find kkk.

    1. Hint 1

      The squared term identifies the input at the highest point.

    2. Hint 2

      Use that input in the line equation to find the shared height.

    Answer

    k=7k=7k=7.

    Full solution

    The negative coefficient makes the vertex the highest point.

    Its coordinates are (3,k)(3,k)(3,k).

    Because that vertex lies on the line, its height must equal the line's height at x=3x=3x=3.

    k=2(3)+1k=2(3)+1k=2(3)+1

    Therefore k=7k=7k=7.

    At x=3x=3x=3, the squared term vanishes and both equations give 777.

    Answer

    k=7k=7k=7.

    Key idea

    A condition on the vertex can recover the constant outside a squared binomial.

  4. Problem 4 A product graph

    For y=(x−3)(3x+3)+4y=(x-3)(3x+3)+4y=(x−3)(3x+3)+4, give the vertex form, vertex, axis of symmetry, opening direction, and y-intercept. Also state the number of x-intercepts.

    1. Hint 1

      Expand the product before identifying the quadratic coefficients.

    2. Hint 2

      Complete the square to locate the vertex and axis, and use the leading coefficient for the direction.

    3. Hint 3

      Set x=0x=0x=0 for the y-intercept and inspect the discriminant for the x-intercept count.

    Answer

    y=3(x−1)2−8y=3(x-1)^2-8y=3(x−1)2−8; vertex (1,−8)(1,-8)(1,−8); axis x=1x=1x=1; opens upward; y-intercept (0,−5)(0,-5)(0,−5); two x-intercepts.

    Full solution

    Expansion gives

    y=3x2−6x−5y=3x^2-6x-5y=3x2−6x−5

    Factor 333 from the two xxx terms.

    y=3(x2−2x)−5y=3(x^2-2x)-5y=3(x2−2x)−5

    Half of −2-2−2 is −1-1−1, and (−1)2=1(-1)^2=1(−1)2=1, so

    x2−2x=(x−1)2−1.x^2-2x=(x-1)^2-1.x2−2x=(x−1)2−1.

    Multiplying that −1-1−1 by 333 and combining with −5-5−5 gives −8-8−8.

    y=3(x−1)2−8y=3(x-1)^2-8y=3(x−1)2−8

    The vertex is (1,−8)(1,-8)(1,−8) and the axis is x=1x=1x=1.

    Since a=3>0a=3>0a=3>0, the curve opens upward.

    At x=0x=0x=0, y=−5y=-5y=−5, so the y-intercept is (0,−5)(0,-5)(0,−5).

    The discriminant is

    D=(−6)2−4(3)(−5)D=(-6)^2-4(3)(-5)D=(−6)2−4(3)(−5)

    This is 96>096>096>0, so there are two x-intercepts, consistent with an upward curve whose lowest point lies below the x-axis.

    Answer

    y=3(x−1)2−8y=3(x-1)^2-8y=3(x−1)2−8; vertex (1,−8)(1,-8)(1,−8); axis x=1x=1x=1; opens upward; y-intercept (0,−5)(0,-5)(0,−5); two x-intercepts.

    Key idea

    Expanding and completing the square expose different features of the same parabola.

  5. Problem 5 An unfinished graph

    The figure shows the part of y=12x2+2x−2y=\tfrac12x^2+2x-2y=21​x2+2x−2 with x≤−4x\le-4x≤−4. Complete the graph for x>−4x>-4x>−4 on the same grid, and label the vertex and y-intercept.

    Text description of this figure

    A coordinate grid with equal unit lengths on both axes. The horizontal x-axis runs from negative seven to three and the vertical y-axis from negative five to five, with gridlines, tick marks and number labels at every whole number, the origin labeled 0, and arrowheads at both ends of each axis. Only part of a curve is drawn, and all of it lies to the left of the vertical line four units left of the origin. It enters the grid at the top edge, a little past six units to the left of the origin, falls steadily to the right, and stops at the point four units left of the origin and two units below the x-axis. The rest of the grid is blank: nothing is drawn to the right of that end point, and there is no marked vertex, no marked intercept, no axis of symmetry line, no coordinates and no equation.

    1. Hint 1

      The two sides of a parabola are mirror images of each other across its axis of symmetry.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the axis from the coefficients, then use equal horizontal distances to place matching points.

    Answer

    Vertex (−2,−4)(-2,-4)(−2,−4); y-intercept (0,−2)(0,-2)(0,−2); the right half mirrors the left half across x=−2x=-2x=−2.

    Full solution

    The axis is

    x=−22(1/2)x=-\frac{2}{2(1/2)}x=−2(1/2)2​

    This is x=−2x=-2x=−2.

    Substitution gives vertex height −4-4−4.

    Equivalently,

    y=12(x+2)2−4y=\frac12(x+2)^2-4y=21​(x+2)2−4

    Plot the vertex, then reflect each shown point across x=−2x=-2x=−2.

    For example, the drawn end point (−4,−2)(-4,-2)(−4,−2) matches (0,−2)(0,-2)(0,−2), and (−6,4)(-6,4)(−6,4) matches (2,4)(2,4)(2,4).

    Draw a smooth curve from (−4,−2)(-4,-2)(−4,−2) down through the vertex and up through (0,−2)(0,-2)(0,−2) and (2,4)(2,4)(2,4).

    At x=0x=0x=0, the equation gives −2-2−2, checking the y-intercept.

    Answer

    Vertex (−2,−4)(-2,-4)(−2,−4); y-intercept (0,−2)(0,-2)(0,−2); the right half mirrors the left half across x=−2x=-2x=−2.

    Key idea

    Once the axis is known, the vertex and mirrored points together complete a partly drawn parabola.

  6. Problem 6 Two horizontal levels

    Consider the parabola y=−(x+1)2+4y=-(x+1)^2+4y=−(x+1)2+4. Find the horizontal distance between its two points at height y=0y=0y=0, and the horizontal distance between its two points at height y=3y=3y=3. Explain which level gives the wider span.

    1. Hint 1

      At each given height, solve for the two x-coordinates.

    2. Hint 2

      A horizontal distance is the larger x-coordinate minus the smaller.

    Answer

    At y=0y=0y=0: 444 units. At y=3y=3y=3: 222 units. The span at y=0y=0y=0 is wider.

    Full solution

    At height zero, (x+1)2=4(x+1)^2=4(x+1)2=4, so the x-coordinates are −3-3−3 and 111.

    Their horizontal separation is

    1−(−3)=41-(-3)=41−(−3)=4

    At height three, (x+1)2=1(x+1)^2=1(x+1)2=1, so the x-coordinates are −2-2−2 and 000.

    Their separation is

    0−(−2)=20-(-2)=20−(−2)=2

    The lower level has the wider span because it lies farther below this downward parabola’s highest point.

    Answer

    At y=0y=0y=0: 444 units. At y=3y=3y=3: 222 units. The span at y=0y=0y=0 is wider.

    Key idea

    Horizontal spans of a parabola come from the separation of equal-height inputs.

  7. Problem 7 Two constant terms

    Compare y=2x2−16x+35y=2x^2-16x+35y=2x2−16x+35 with y=2x2−16x+30y=2x^2-16x+30y=2x2−16x+30. Find the axis of symmetry and the number of x-intercepts of each graph.

    1. Hint 1

      An axis depends on aaa and bbb, which are unchanged.

    2. Hint 2

      Compute each discriminant with its own signed constant term.

    Answer

    Common axis x=4x=4x=4. First graph: no x-intercepts. Second graph: two x-intercepts.

    Full solution

    For both graphs, the axis is

    x=−−162(2)x=-\frac{-16}{2(2)}x=−2(2)−16​

    Thus the shared axis is x=4x=4x=4.

    With a=2a=2a=2 and b=−16b=-16b=−16, the first discriminant is (−16)2−4(2)(35)(-16)^2-4(2)(35)(−16)2−4(2)(35), which is 256−280=−24256-280=-24256−280=−24, so the first graph has no x-intercepts.

    The second discriminant is (−16)2−4(2)(30)(-16)^2-4(2)(30)(−16)2−4(2)(30), which is 256−240=16256-240=16256−240=16, so the second graph has two.

    Substituting x=4x=4x=4 gives vertex heights 333 and −2-2−2, respectively, which confirms the counts for upward parabolas.

    Answer

    Common axis x=4x=4x=4. First graph: no x-intercepts. Second graph: two x-intercepts.

    Key idea

    A change in the constant can alter intercept counts without moving the axis of symmetry.

  8. Problem 8 Matching graph features

    A student says that two parabolas with the same width and the same y-intercept must have the same vertex. Is that correct? Justify your answer.

    1. Hint 1

      The leading coefficient controls width, while the constant controls the y-intercept.

    2. Hint 2

      Try keeping those coefficients fixed while changing the linear coefficient.

    Answer

    False; y=x2y=x^2y=x2 and y=x2−4xy=x^2-4xy=x2−4x have vertices (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) and (2,−4)(2,-4)(2,−4).

    Full solution

    Choose equations y=x2y=x^2y=x2 and y=x2−4xy=x^2-4xy=x2−4x.

    Both have leading coefficient 111 and y-intercept (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), so their widths and y-intercepts match.

    The second equation rewrites as

    y=(x−2)2−4y=(x-2)^2-4y=(x−2)2−4

    Its vertex is (2,−4)(2,-4)(2,−4), whereas the first vertex is (0,0)(0,0)(0,0).

    These unequal vertices disprove the claim.

    Answer

    False; y=x2y=x^2y=x2 and y=x2−4xy=x^2-4xy=x2−4x have vertices (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) and (2,−4)(2,-4)(2,−4).

    Key idea

    Equal width and y-intercept do not determine the linear coefficient or the vertex.

  9. Problem 9 A vertex on an axis

    Let y=ax2+bx+cy=ax^2+bx+cy=ax2+bx+c with real coefficients and a≠0a\ne0a=0. Explain what the vertex lying on the y-axis forces bbb to be, and give the vertex's coordinates in that case.

      A point on the y-axis has x-coordinate zero.

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    b=0b=0b=0; the vertex is (0,c)(0,c)(0,c), which is the y-intercept.

    Full solution

    The vertex x-coordinate must satisfy

    −b2a=0-\frac b{2a}=0−2ab​=0

    Because a≠0a\ne0a=0, multiplication by 2a2a2a is valid and gives −b=0-b=0−b=0.

    Hence b=0b=0b=0.

    With that value, the axis is indeed x=0x=0x=0.

    Substituting x=0x=0x=0 into y=ax2+bx+cy=ax^2+bx+cy=ax2+bx+c leaves y=cy=cy=c.

    So the vertex is (0,c)(0,c)(0,c), which is also the y-intercept.

    Answer

    b=0b=0b=0; the vertex is (0,c)(0,c)(0,c), which is the y-intercept.

    Key idea

    A quadratic vertex on the y-axis forces the linear coefficient to vanish, and the vertex is then the y-intercept.

  10. Problem 10 A scaled equation

    A quadratic y=ax2+bx+cy=ax^2+bx+cy=ax2+bx+c has discriminant DDD. Every coefficient is multiplied by −2-2−2 to make a new graph. A student says that the new graph has the same number of x-intercepts. Is the claim correct? Explain using the new discriminant.

    1. Hint 1

      Substitute the scaled coefficients into the discriminant expression.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the sign of the new discriminant with the sign of the original.

    Answer

    Yes; the new discriminant is 4D4D4D.

    Full solution

    The new coefficients are −2a-2a−2a, −2b-2b−2b, and −2c-2c−2c.

    Dnew=4b2−16acD_{\text{new}}=4b^2-16acDnew​=4b2−16ac

    Factor out 444.

    Dnew=4DD_{\text{new}}=4DDnew​=4D

    Multiplication by positive 444 preserves whether the discriminant is positive, zero, or negative.

    The number of x-intercepts therefore stays the same, although the opening direction reverses.

    The same argument covers any nonzero multiplier ttt.

    Scaling every coefficient by ttt gives

    Dnew=t2b2−4t2acD_{\text{new}}=t^2b^2-4t^2acDnew​=t2b2−4t2ac

    That expression equals t2Dt^2Dt2D, and t2>0t^2>0t2>0 for every nonzero ttt, so the sign of the discriminant never changes.

    Answer

    Yes; the new discriminant is 4D4D4D.

    Key idea

    Scaling every coefficient by a nonzero number preserves the number of real zeros.
