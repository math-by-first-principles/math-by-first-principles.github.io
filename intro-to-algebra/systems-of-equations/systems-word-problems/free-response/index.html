  1. Problem 1 The filter box

    A box holds xxx packs of three filters and yyy loose filters. Counting each pack and each loose filter as one item, the box holds 121212 items and 262626 filters altogether. Write the two equations that record these facts.

    1. Hint 1

      Count items and filters separately.

    2. Hint 2

      Each pack counts as one item but holds three filters.

    Answer

    x+y=12x+y=12x+y=12 and 3x+y=263x+y=263x+y=26.

    Full solution

    The item count includes each pack and each loose filter once.

    x+y=12x+y=12x+y=12

    The filter count includes three filters per pack and one per loose filter.

    3x+y=263x+y=263x+y=26

    Here xxx and yyy are counts, so they must be whole numbers that are zero or positive.

    For example, seven packs and five loose filters check both stated totals.

    Answer

    x+y=12x+y=12x+y=12 and 3x+y=263x+y=263x+y=26.

    Key idea

    One object can contribute differently to two different totals.

  2. Problem 2 The refund entry

    A booth sold aaa small kits at 555 dollars each and bbb large kits at 888 dollars each. A customer then received a refund of 666 dollars without returning a kit. The booth kept 102102102 dollars. Write the equation that records the money kept.

    1. Hint 1

      Attach each price to the count of the matching kit.

    2. Hint 2

      The money kept is the sales total less the refund.

    Answer

    5a+8b−6=1025a+8b-6=1025a+8b−6=102, or equivalently 5a+8b=1085a+8b=1085a+8b=108.

    Full solution

    Small kits bring in 5a5a5a dollars and large kits bring in 8b8b8b dollars.

    The refund reduces the amount kept by 666 dollars, so

    5a+8b−6=1025a+8b-6=1025a+8b−6=102

    Adding the refund back gives sales of 108108108 dollars, consistent with the equivalent equation 5a+8b=1085a+8b=1085a+8b=108.

    Answer

    5a+8b−6=1025a+8b-6=1025a+8b−6=102, or equivalently 5a+8b=1085a+8b=1085a+8b=108.

    Key idea

    An adjustment after a sale enters the value equation as its own term.

  3. Problem 3 The two class plans

    A studio offers two payment plans. Plan A charges a 202020 dollar sign-up fee plus 666 dollars per class. Plan B charges a 505050 dollar sign-up fee plus 444 dollars per class. Let ccc be the number of classes and yyy the total cost in dollars. Write each plan's cost as an equation, then find the number of classes at which the two plans cost the same, and that cost.

    1. Hint 1

      The plans cost the same where one pair (c,y)(c, y)(c,y) fits both equations.

    2. Hint 2

      Each total is the sign-up fee plus the per-class price times ccc.

    3. Hint 3

      Both equations are solved for yyy, so set the two expressions equal.

    Answer

    Plan A: y=20+6cy=20+6cy=20+6c. Plan B: y=50+4cy=50+4cy=50+4c. The two plans cost the same at 151515 classes, where each costs 110110110 dollars.

    Full solution

    Each plan's total is its sign-up fee plus its per-class price times the number of classes.

    y=20+6cy=20+6cy=20+6c y=50+4cy=50+4cy=50+4c

    Both equations are solved for yyy, so set the two expressions equal.

    20+6c=50+4c20+6c=50+4c20+6c=50+4c

    Subtracting 4c4c4c and 202020 from both sides gives

    2c=302c=302c=30 c=15c=15c=15

    Plan A then costs 20+6(15)=11020+6(15)=11020+6(15)=110 dollars.

    Check with Plan B: 50+4(15)=11050+4(15)=11050+4(15)=110 dollars, the same total.

    So both plans cost 110110110 dollars for 151515 classes.

    Answer

    Plan A: y=20+6cy=20+6cy=20+6c. Plan B: y=50+4cy=50+4cy=50+4c. The two plans cost the same at 151515 classes, where each costs 110110110 dollars.

    Key idea

    The equal-cost point of two plans is the one pair that satisfies both cost equations.

  4. Problem 4 The prepared batch

    A lab tank already has 444 liters of a 25%25\%25% dye solution. A technician adds a 5%5\%5% dye solution and a 35%35\%35% dye solution to make 101010 liters of a 28%28\%28% dye solution. Assume the volumes add. Find the liters added from each source. Organize the amount, dye fraction, and dye content for the existing batch and both additions in a table on paper before writing the two equations.

    1. Hint 1

      Total the liquid and the dye separately, including what is already present.

    2. Hint 2

      If the added amounts are xxx and yyy, only six liters remain to be added.

    3. Hint 3

      The existing batch supplies one liter of dye.

    Answer

    111 liter of the 5%5\%5% solution and 555 liters of the 35%35\%35% solution.

    Full solution

    Let xxx and yyy be the liters added from the 5%5\%5% and 35%35\%35% sources.

    The existing-batch row has amount 444, fraction 0.250.250.25, and content 111, with amounts and contents in liters.

    The two addition rows have amount xxx, fraction 0.050.050.05, content 0.05x0.05x0.05x, and amount yyy, fraction 0.350.350.35, content 0.35y0.35y0.35y.

    The final row has amount 101010, fraction 0.280.280.28, and content 2.82.82.8.

    The amount column gives

    4+x+y=104+x+y=104+x+y=10

    and the content column gives

    1+0.05x+0.35y=2.81+0.05x+0.35y=2.81+0.05x+0.35y=2.8

    Subtracting the existing batch from each gives

    x+y=6x+y=6x+y=6 0.05x+0.35y=1.80.05x+0.35y=1.80.05x+0.35y=1.8

    Substitute x=6−yx=6-yx=6−y.

    0.05(6−y)+0.35y=1.80.05(6-y)+0.35y=1.80.05(6−y)+0.35y=1.8 0.30+0.30y=1.80.30+0.30y=1.80.30+0.30y=1.8 0.30y=1.50.30y=1.50.30y=1.5 y=5y=5y=5

    Hence x=1x=1x=1.

    The liquid totals 4+1+5=104+1+5=104+1+5=10 liters.

    The dye totals 1+0.05+1.75=2.81+0.05+1.75=2.81+0.05+1.75=2.8 liters, which is 28%28\%28% of the finished batch.

    Answer

    111 liter of the 5%5\%5% solution and 555 liters of the 35%35\%35% solution.

    Key idea

    An existing mixture contributes to both totals before any new ingredients are added.

  5. Problem 5 The patrol distances

    A boat maintains the same speed in still water on two river patrols while the current stays constant. It travels downstream for 222 hours and upstream for 333 hours. The two distances total 505050 miles, and the downstream distance is 141414 miles longer. Find the boat speed in still water and the current speed, both in miles per hour.

    1. Hint 1

      Express each distance using its own travel time and ground speed.

    2. Hint 2

      With boat speed bbb and current speed ccc, the distances are 2(b+c)2(b+c)2(b+c) and 3(b−c)3(b-c)3(b−c).

    3. Hint 3

      Write equations for their total and difference.

    Answer

    Boat: 111111 miles per hour. Current: 555 miles per hour.

    Full solution

    Let bbb and ccc be the boat and current speeds in miles per hour.

    The distance facts give

    2(b+c)+3(b−c)=502(b+c)+3(b-c)=502(b+c)+3(b−c)=50 2(b+c)−3(b−c)=142(b+c)-3(b-c)=142(b+c)−3(b−c)=14

    Adding these equations gives

    4(b+c)=644(b+c)=644(b+c)=64 b+c=16b+c=16b+c=16

    The downstream distance is therefore 2(16)=322(16)=322(16)=32 miles, so the upstream distance is 50−32=1850-32=1850−32=18 miles.

    Then

    3(b−c)=183(b-c)=183(b−c)=18 b−c=6b-c=6b−c=6

    Adding the speed equations gives 2b=222b=222b=22, hence b=11b=11b=11.

    Then c=16−11=5c=16-11=5c=16−11=5.

    The boat is faster than the current, as required to travel upstream.

    The downstream distance checks as 2(11+5)=322(11+5)=322(11+5)=32 miles and the upstream distance as 3(11−5)=183(11-5)=183(11−5)=18 miles.

    They total 505050 miles and differ by 141414 miles.

    Answer

    Boat: 111111 miles per hour. Current: 555 miles per hour.

    Key idea

    Total and difference information about two journeys can be translated through their separate distance equations.

  6. Problem 6 The damaged cards

    A fundraiser began with 181818 gift cards, each worth either 444 dollars or 777 dollars. Two of the 444 dollar cards were damaged and discarded. The remaining cards are worth 979797 dollars. Find how many cards of each value the fundraiser began with.

    1. Hint 1

      Define the unknowns as the original counts.

    2. Hint 2

      Only the count of the lower-value cards changes before the remaining value is totaled.

    Answer

    777 cards worth 444 dollars each and 111111 cards worth 777 dollars each.

    Full solution

    Let xxx and yyy be the original counts of 444 dollar and 777 dollar cards.

    x+y=18x+y=18x+y=18 4(x−2)+7y=974(x-2)+7y=974(x−2)+7y=97

    Rearranging the value equation gives

    4x+7y=1054x+7y=1054x+7y=105

    Multiply the count equation by 444 and subtract it from the value equation.

    3y=333y=333y=33 y=11y=11y=11

    Thus x=18−11=7x=18-11=7x=18−11=7.

    The original count is 7+11=187+11=187+11=18.

    After discarding two lower-value cards, the value is 5(4)+11(7)=975(4)+11(7)=975(4)+11(7)=97 dollars.

    Both original counts are whole numbers, and there are enough lower-value cards to discard two.

    Answer

    777 cards worth 444 dollars each and 111111 cards worth 777 dollars each.

    Key idea

    Keep original counts separate from changed counts when matching a later value total.

  7. Problem 7 The sample display

    A maker produces a batch of pins. Four pins are kept as display samples, and each of the remaining pins is sold for 999 dollars. The fixed cost is 848484 dollars and materials cost 333 dollars for every pin produced, including the samples. How many pins must be produced for revenue to equal cost, and how many are sold?

    1. Hint 1

      The number produced and the number sold differ by four.

    2. Hint 2

      Let xxx be the number produced and yyy the common cost and revenue in dollars.

    Answer

    202020 pins produced; 161616 pins sold.

    Full solution

    Let xxx be the number produced.

    At break-even the common amount yyy, in dollars, satisfies

    y=84+3xy=84+3xy=84+3x y=9(x−4)y=9(x-4)y=9(x−4)

    Equating the expressions gives

    84+3x=9x−3684+3x=9x-3684+3x=9x−36 120=6x120=6x120=6x x=20x=20x=20

    There are 20−4=1620-4=1620−4=16 pins sold.

    Revenue is 9(16)=1449(16)=1449(16)=144 dollars, and cost is 84+3(20)=14484+3(20)=14484+3(20)=144 dollars, so the batch breaks even.

    Answer

    202020 pins produced; 161616 pins sold.

    Key idea

    Break-even models must distinguish the items that incur costs from those that produce revenue.

  8. Problem 8 The paired recipes

    Recipe A mixes 111 liter of a 10%10\%10% solution with 333 liters of a 30%30\%30% solution. Recipe B reverses those amounts, using 333 liters of the 10%10\%10% solution and 111 liter of the 30%30\%30% solution. Assume volumes add. Kim says combining both complete recipes produces a 20%20\%20% solution. Is Kim correct? Justify the decision using amounts and content.

    1. Hint 1

      Track how much comes from each original source after both recipes are combined.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the total liters and the total dissolved content of the combined batch, then divide.

    Answer

    Yes; the combined solution is 20%20\%20%.

    Full solution

    Together the recipes use 444 liters of each source.

    The total liquid is 888 liters, and the total dissolved content is

    0.10(4)+0.30(4)=1.60.10(4)+0.30(4)=1.60.10(4)+0.30(4)=1.6

    in liters.

    The content fraction is

    1.68=0.20\frac{1.6}{8}=0.2081.6​=0.20

    Thus the concentration is 20%20\%20%, and Kim is correct.

    The result follows from equal combined amounts of the two sources, even though neither recipe uses equal amounts by itself.

    Answer

    Yes; the combined solution is 20%20\%20%.

    Key idea

    A combined batch's concentration is its total content divided by its total amount, however its parts were grouped.

  9. Problem 9 The delivery receipts

    A seller charges a fixed delivery fee plus a fixed price per bottle. Two bottles delivered cost 181818 dollars, while five bottles delivered cost 303030 dollars. Eli says the delivery fee cannot be found because neither receipt lists it separately. Is Eli correct? Find the bottle price and delivery fee if the records determine them.

    1. Hint 1

      The same delivery fee appears once in each total.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the receipt equations to remove that fee.

    Answer

    No. Each bottle costs 444 dollars; delivery costs 101010 dollars.

    Full solution

    Let ppp be the bottle price and fff the delivery fee, both in dollars.

    2p+f=182p+f=182p+f=18 5p+f=305p+f=305p+f=30

    Subtracting gives

    3p=123p=123p=12 p=4p=4p=4

    The first receipt then gives f=18−8=10f=18-8=10f=18−8=10.

    The charges check as 2(4)+10=182(4)+10=182(4)+10=18 dollars and 5(4)+10=305(4)+10=305(4)+10=30 dollars.

    The two different bottle counts provide enough independent information to recover the shared fee, so Eli is incorrect.

    Answer

    No. Each bottle costs 444 dollars; delivery costs 101010 dollars.

    Key idea

    A charge shared by two records can be recovered even when it is not itemized.

  10. Problem 10 The matched increase

    A shop makes and sells the same number of items. It has a fixed cost F>0F>0F>0, a material cost of ccc dollars per item, and a selling price of ppp dollars per item, with p>cp>cp>c. Sam claims that adding the same positive amount hhh dollars to both the material cost and selling price leaves the break-even number unchanged. Is Sam correct? Explain algebraically.

    1. Hint 1

      Write cost equals revenue before and after the two increases.

    2. Hint 2

      The extra cost and extra revenue are each hxhxhx for xxx items.

    Answer

    Yes; the break-even number remains Fp−c\frac{F}{p-c}p−cF​.

    Full solution

    For xxx items, the original break-even condition is

    F+cx=pxF+cx=pxF+cx=px F=(p−c)xF=(p-c)xF=(p−c)x

    Since p>cp>cp>c, the difference is nonzero and

    x=Fp−cx=\frac{F}{p-c}x=p−cF​

    After the increases, the condition is

    F+(c+h)x=(p+h)xF+(c+h)x=(p+h)xF+(c+h)x=(p+h)x

    Subtracting hxhxhx from both sides returns the original condition.

    Thus exactly the same item count gives break-even, so Sam is correct.

    Answer

    Yes; the break-even number remains Fp−c\frac{F}{p-c}p−cF​.

    Key idea

    Equal changes to per-item cost and per-item revenue preserve their difference and the break-even condition.
