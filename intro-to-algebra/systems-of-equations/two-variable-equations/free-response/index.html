  1. Problem 1 Variables on both sides

    Write 2(x+3y)=5(x−1)+y2(x+3y)=5(x-1)+y2(x+3y)=5(x−1)+y in the form ax+by=cax+by=cax+by=c, with integer coefficients having no common factor greater than 111 and with aaa positive.

    1. Hint 1

      The target form has every variable term on the left and a single number on the right; notice that both xxx and yyy appear on both sides.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand both groups first, then move every variable term to one side and every constant to the other.

    3. Hint 3

      If the coefficient of xxx comes out negative, multiply both sides by −1-1−1.

    Answer

    3x−5y=53x-5y=53x−5y=5.

    Full solution

    Expanding both groups gives

    2x+6y=5x−5+y2x+6y=5x-5+y2x+6y=5x−5+y

    Subtract 5x5x5x and yyy from both sides.

    −3x+5y=−5-3x+5y=-5−3x+5y=−5

    Multiply both sides by −1-1−1 so that the coefficient of xxx is positive.

    3x−5y=53x-5y=53x−5y=5

    The coefficients 333, −5-5−5 and 555 are integers with no common factor greater than 111.

    As a check, (0,−1)(0,-1)(0,−1) satisfies 3x−5y=53x-5y=53x−5y=5, since

    3(0)−5(−1)=5.3(0)-5(-1)=5.3(0)−5(−1)=5.

    In the original equation it makes both sides −6-6−6, since 2(0−3)=−62(0-3)=-62(0−3)=−6 and

    5(0−1)+(−1)=−6.5(0-1)+(-1)=-6.5(0−1)+(−1)=−6.

    Answer

    3x−5y=53x-5y=53x−5y=5.

    Key idea

    Expanding and then collecting like terms turns an equation with variables on both sides into standard form.

  2. Problem 2 The shifted reading

    A reading is stored as (x,y)(x,y)(x,y). The instrument adds 111 to its first coordinate and leaves its second coordinate unchanged. Which original reading, (1,5)(1,5)(1,5) or (2,8)(2,8)(2,8), produces a new pair satisfying 4x−y=34x-y=34x−y=3?

    1. Hint 1

      Test the pair after the instrument changes it.

    2. Hint 2

      The new first coordinate belongs in the xxx position.

    Answer

    (1,5)(1,5)(1,5).

    Full solution

    The original reading (1,5)(1,5)(1,5) becomes (2,5)(2,5)(2,5).

    4(2)−5=34(2)-5=34(2)−5=3

    It satisfies the equation.

    The original reading (2,8)(2,8)(2,8) becomes (3,8)(3,8)(3,8).

    4(3)−8=44(3)-8=44(3)−8=4

    It does not satisfy the equation.

    Answer

    (1,5)(1,5)(1,5).

    Key idea

    Test the coordinates of the transformed pair in their stated order.

  3. Problem 3 A pair to choose

    Give one solution of 2x−3y=−122x-3y=-122x−3y=−12 whose first coordinate is negative and whose second coordinate is positive.

    1. Hint 1

      Choose a negative value for the first coordinate, then solve for the second.

    2. Hint 2

      Try a small negative integer, then check the sign of the resulting yyy.

    Answer

    For example, (−3,2)(-3,2)(−3,2).

    Full solution

    Choose x=−3x=-3x=−3 and substitute.

    −6−3y=−12-6-3y=-12−6−3y=−12 −3y=−6-3y=-6−3y=−6 y=2y=2y=2

    Thus (−3,2)(-3,2)(−3,2) is one valid answer.

    Checking, 2(−3)−3(2)=−122(-3)-3(2)=-122(−3)−3(2)=−12, and its coordinates have the required signs.

    Any other pair with a negative first coordinate and a positive second coordinate that satisfies the equation is also accepted.

    Answer

    For example, (−3,2)(-3,2)(−3,2).

    Key idea

    A chosen first coordinate produces a solution when the matching second coordinate is found from the equation.

  4. Problem 4 The paper strip

    A maker starts with a red strip of length rrr cm and a blue strip of length bbb cm. Three copies of the red strip are each extended by 222 cm, and four copies of the blue strip are each shortened by 111 cm. The seven finished strips total 404040 cm. Write an equation relating rrr and bbb in standard form, with the rrr term first and integer coefficients having no common factor greater than 111, then give two possible original pairs (r,b)(r,b)(r,b) with r>0r>0r>0 and b>1b>1b>1.

    1. Hint 1

      Each copy contributes its finished length to the total.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand 3(r+2)+4(b−1)3(r+2)+4(b-1)3(r+2)+4(b−1), then choose convenient values for one length.

    Answer

    3r+4b=383r+4b=383r+4b=38; for example, (10,2)(10,2)(10,2) and (2,8)(2,8)(2,8), in cm.

    Full solution

    The finished strips give

    3(r+2)+4(b−1)=403(r+2)+4(b-1)=403(r+2)+4(b−1)=40 3r+6+4b−4=403r+6+4b-4=403r+6+4b−4=40 3r+4b=383r+4b=383r+4b=38

    Choosing b=2b=2b=2 gives 3r=303r=303r=30, so

    (r,b)=(10,2).(r,b)=(10,2).(r,b)=(10,2).

    Choosing b=8b=8b=8 gives 3r=63r=63r=6, so

    (r,b)=(2,8).(r,b)=(2,8).(r,b)=(2,8).

    Both pairs satisfy the length restrictions.

    The finished totals are 3(12)+4(1)=403(12)+4(1)=403(12)+4(1)=40 cm and 3(4)+4(7)=403(4)+4(7)=403(4)+4(7)=40 cm.

    Any two distinct pairs with r>0r>0r>0 and b>1b>1b>1 satisfying the equation are accepted.

    Answer

    3r+4b=383r+4b=383r+4b=38; for example, (10,2)(10,2)(10,2) and (2,8)(2,8)(2,8), in cm.

    Key idea

    Write the amount contributed by each changed item before collecting a total equation.

  5. Problem 5 The two blank marks

    The line drawn is the graph of the equation x−y=−3x-y=-3x−y=−3. Check that both marked points satisfy the equation. Then use the equation to find the solution whose first coordinate is 101010, which lies beyond the grid, and explain why it is on the graph even though it is not drawn.

    Text description of this figure

    A square coordinate grid. The horizontal x-axis and the vertical y-axis each run from negative four to six, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number and equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. One straight line with an arrowhead at each end rises from the lower left to the upper right across the whole grid. It enters at the left edge of the grid, at the point (negative 4, negative 1), and leaves at the top edge, at the point (3, 6). Two points on the line are marked with solid dots and carry no labels: one on the x-axis at (negative 3, 0), and one at (1, 4). No equation, coordinate labels or guide lines are shown.

    1. Hint 1

      Read each point across first, then up, and write it as an ordered pair.

    2. Hint 2

      Substitute the first coordinate for xxx and the second for yyy in x−yx-yx−y, and compare the result with −3-3−3.

    3. Hint 3

      Put x=10x=10x=10 into x−y=−3x-y=-3x−y=−3 and solve for yyy; then recall what the graph of an equation contains.

    Answer

    (−3,0)(-3,0)(−3,0) and (1,4)(1,4)(1,4) both satisfy the equation; the solution with first coordinate 101010 is (10,13)(10,13)(10,13), and it lies on the graph.

    Full solution

    The marked points are (−3,0)(-3,0)(−3,0) and (1,4)(1,4)(1,4).

    Substituting the first gives

    −3−0=−3-3-0=-3−3−0=−3

    Substituting the second gives

    1−4=−31-4=-31−4=−3

    Both pairs make the equation true.

    Put x=10x=10x=10 into the equation.

    10−y=−310-y=-310−y=−3

    Subtract 101010 from both sides.

    −y=−13-y=-13−y=−13 y=13y=13y=13

    The solution is (10,13)(10,13)(10,13), and checking gives 10−13=−310-13=-310−13=−3.

    The graph of an equation is the set of all its solutions, so every solution is a point on the line, whether or not it fits in the picture.

    The arrowheads show that the line continues past the grid, and (10,13)(10,13)(10,13) is one of its points out there.

    Answer

    (−3,0)(-3,0)(−3,0) and (1,4)(1,4)(1,4) both satisfy the equation; the solution with first coordinate 101010 is (10,13)(10,13)(10,13), and it lies on the graph.

    Key idea

    The graph of an equation contains every one of its solutions, including those beyond the part that is drawn.

  6. Problem 6 An added condition

    A pair (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) must satisfy 3x+y=113x+y=113x+y=11. A recorder can add either condition A, x=2x=2x=2, or condition B, 6x+2y=226x+2y=226x+2y=22. Which condition fixes exactly one pair, and what is that pair? Explain what the other condition contributes.

    1. Hint 1

      Ask whether each condition adds information to the first equation.

    2. Hint 2

      One condition gives a coordinate directly; for the other, ask whether it can be obtained from 3x+y=113x+y=113x+y=11 by doing the same thing to both sides.

    Answer

    Condition A fixes (2,5)(2,5)(2,5). Condition B adds no new restriction.

    Full solution

    With condition A, substituting x=2x=2x=2 gives

    3(2)+y=113(2)+y=113(2)+y=11 y=5y=5y=5

    Thus A fixes (2,5)(2,5)(2,5), and 3(2)+5=113(2)+5=113(2)+5=11 checks it.

    On the graph, the line x=2x=2x=2 crosses the line 3x+y=113x+y=113x+y=11 at that one point.

    Condition B is twice the original equation.

    Every pair satisfying 3x+y=113x+y=113x+y=11 already satisfies 6x+2y=226x+2y=226x+2y=22, so B leaves the same whole line of possible pairs.

    Answer

    Condition A fixes (2,5)(2,5)(2,5). Condition B adds no new restriction.

    Key idea

    A second equation that is a multiple of the first adds no restriction, while one whose line crosses the first line once fixes a single pair.

  7. Problem 7 Describing every solution

    Find every solution pair of 4x+2y=4x+104x+2y=4x+104x+2y=4x+10, where xxx and yyy may be any real numbers. Give two of them, and describe the full graph.

    1. Hint 1

      The equal xxx terms can be removed from both sides.

    2. Hint 2

      After finding yyy, decide whether the equation places any restriction on xxx.

    Answer

    Every pair (x,5)(x,5)(x,5), for any real xxx; for example, (0,5)(0,5)(0,5) and (2,5)(2,5)(2,5); the graph is the horizontal line y=5y=5y=5.

    Full solution

    Subtract 4x4x4x from both sides.

    2y=102y=102y=10 y=5y=5y=5

    No condition on xxx remains.

    Thus every pair (x,5)(x,5)(x,5) is a solution.

    For example, (0,5)(0,5)(0,5) gives 10=1010=1010=10 in the original equation, and (2,5)(2,5)(2,5) gives 18=1818=1818=18.

    All the points sit 555 units above the horizontal axis, forming the horizontal line y=5y=5y=5.

    Answer

    Every pair (x,5)(x,5)(x,5), for any real xxx; for example, (0,5)(0,5)(0,5) and (2,5)(2,5)(2,5); the graph is the horizontal line y=5y=5y=5.

    Key idea

    If one variable cancels from an equation, its coordinate may remain free while the other coordinate is fixed.

  8. Problem 8 Rae's one solution

    Rae says, "The equation 5x+3y=225x+3y=225x+3y=22 has just one solution, (2,4)(2,4)(2,4)." Is this correct when xxx and yyy may be any real numbers? Is it correct when both must be positive whole numbers? Explain both decisions.

    1. Hint 1

      The kind of numbers allowed changes which pairs count as solutions.

    2. Hint 2

      For real numbers, choose a different value of xxx and solve for the matching yyy.

    3. Hint 3

      For positive whole numbers, yyy is at least 111, so 5x5x5x is at most 191919; test each whole number value of xxx that this allows.

    Answer

    Any real numbers: no; for example, (5,−1)(5,-1)(5,−1) also works. Positive whole numbers: yes, (2,4)(2,4)(2,4) is the only one.

    Full solution

    For real numbers, Rae is incorrect.

    The pair (5,−1)(5,-1)(5,−1) is another solution, since

    5(5)+3(−1)=225(5)+3(-1)=225(5)+3(−1)=22

    In fact, any chosen value of xxx gives a matching yyy, so there are infinitely many solutions.

    For positive whole numbers, yyy is at least 111, so 3y3y3y is at least 333 and 5x5x5x is at most 191919.

    So xxx can only be 111, 222 or 333.

    If x=1x=1x=1, then 3y=173y=173y=17, and 171717 is not a multiple of 333, so yyy is not a whole number.

    If x=2x=2x=2, then 3y=123y=123y=12, so y=4y=4y=4.

    If x=3x=3x=3, then 3y=73y=73y=7, and 777 is not a multiple of 333.

    So (2,4)(2,4)(2,4) is the only solution in positive whole numbers, and Rae is correct under that restriction.

    Answer

    Any real numbers: no; for example, (5,−1)(5,-1)(5,−1) also works. Positive whole numbers: yes, (2,4)(2,4)(2,4) is the only one.

    Key idea

    Distinguish the full solution set of an equation from the pairs left by a separate restriction on the allowed numbers.

  9. Problem 9 Jo's new pairs

    A pair (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) satisfies 3x−y=43x-y=43x−y=4. Jo makes a new pair (x+t,y+mt)(x+t,y+mt)(x+t,y+mt) by adding a real number ttt to the first coordinate and mtmtmt to the second, where mmm is a fixed number. For which value of mmm does the new pair also satisfy the equation for every real ttt? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

      The new pair is a solution exactly when the change it makes to 3x−y3x-y3x−y adds up to nothing.

    2. Hint 2

      Substitute the new pair into the left side of the equation, remembering that 3x−y3x-y3x−y is already known, then group the terms that contain ttt apart from the rest.

    3. Hint 3

      The left side must equal 444 whatever ttt is; try t=1t=1t=1 to see what the terms containing ttt must add to.

    Answer

    m=3m=3m=3.

    Full solution

    Substitute the new pair into the left side.

    3(x+t)−(y+mt)3(x+t)-(y+mt)3(x+t)−(y+mt)

    Expanding and grouping the terms that contain ttt gives

    3x−y+3t−mt3x-y+3t-mt3x−y+3t−mt

    Since 3x−y=43x-y=43x−y=4 and 3t−mt=(3−m)t3t-mt=(3-m)t3t−mt=(3−m)t, the left side equals

    4+(3−m)t4+(3-m)t4+(3−m)t

    If m=3m=3m=3, then (3−m)t=0(3-m)t=0(3−m)t=0 for every ttt, so the left side is 444 and the new pair is always a solution.

    If mmm is any other number, choose t=1t=1t=1.

    The left side is then 4+(3−m)4+(3-m)4+(3−m), which is not 444 because 3−m3-m3−m is not 000, so the new pair fails.

    Therefore m=3m=3m=3.

    For example, (1,−1)(1,-1)(1,−1) satisfies the equation, and t=3t=3t=3 with m=3m=3m=3 gives (4,8)(4,8)(4,8), where 3(4)−8=43(4)-8=43(4)−8=4.

    Answer

    m=3m=3m=3.

    Key idea

    From one solution of 3x−y=43x-y=43x−y=4, a step of ttt in xxx keeps the equation true only with a step of 3t3t3t in yyy, which is why the solutions line up.

  10. Problem 10 Liv's verification

    A system consists of x+2y=8x+2y=8x+2y=8 and 3x−y=63x-y=63x−y=6. Liv verifies (2,3)(2,3)(2,3) in the first equation and (2,0)(2,0)(2,0) in the second, then says the system is solved. Is this sufficient? Explain, and decide whether either checked pair solves the system.

    1. Hint 1

      A system requires the same pair to satisfy every equation.

    2. Hint 2

      Test each checked pair in the equation Liv did not use for it.

    Answer

    No; neither (2,3)(2,3)(2,3) nor (2,0)(2,0)(2,0) solves the system.

    Full solution

    The first pair does satisfy the first equation, since 2+2(3)=82+2(3)=82+2(3)=8.

    But in the second equation it gives

    3(2)−3=33(2)-3=33(2)−3=3

    This is not 666, so the first pair fails the second equation.

    The second pair satisfies 3(2)−0=63(2)-0=63(2)−0=6, but gives 2+2(0)=22+2(0)=22+2(0)=2 in the first equation, not 888.

    Liv's checks are not sufficient: a solution of the system is one pair that satisfies both equations, and neither checked pair does.

    Answer

    No; neither (2,3)(2,3)(2,3) nor (2,0)(2,0)(2,0) solves the system.

    Key idea

    A system is solved by a common pair, rather than separate pairs chosen for its separate equations.
