  1. Problem 1 The first move

    Solve the system for (x,y)(x,y)(x,y).

    4x+5y=64x+5y=64x+5y=6 2y−x=52y-x=52y−x=5

    1. Hint 1

      Look for a variable whose coefficient is 111 or −1-1−1 in either equation.

    2. Hint 2

      Isolate xxx in the second equation, then put that expression into the first.

    Answer

    (−1,2)(-1,2)(−1,2).

    Full solution

    In 2y−x=52y-x=52y−x=5 the variable xxx has coefficient −1-1−1, so isolating it needs a sign flip and no division.

    Adding xxx to both sides and subtracting 555 gives

    x=2y−5x=2y-5x=2y−5

    Substitute into the first equation.

    4(2y−5)+5y=64(2y-5)+5y=64(2y−5)+5y=6 8y−20+5y=68y-20+5y=68y−20+5y=6 13y=2613y=2613y=26 y=2y=2y=2

    Back-substitution gives x=2(2)−5x=2(2)-5x=2(2)−5, so x=−1x=-1x=−1.

    Checking the original equations gives 4(−1)+5(2)=64(-1)+5(2)=64(−1)+5(2)=6 and 2(2)−(−1)=52(2)-(-1)=52(2)−(−1)=5, both true.

    Answer

    (−1,2)(-1,2)(−1,2).

    Key idea

    Isolating a variable whose coefficient is −1-1−1 needs no division, so that first step brings in no fraction.

  2. Problem 2 The repeated group

    Solve the system for (x,y)(x,y)(x,y).

    y−2=3xy-2=3xy−2=3x 2(y−2)+x=282(y-2)+x=282(y−2)+x=28

    1. Hint 1

      The entire group y−2y-2y−2 already equals a simple expression.

    2. Hint 2

      Replace that group in the second equation, then return to the first.

    Answer

    (4,14)(4,14)(4,14).

    Full solution

    Replace y−2y-2y−2 by 3x3x3x in the second equation.

    2(3x)+x=282(3x)+x=282(3x)+x=28 7x=287x=287x=28 x=4x=4x=4

    The first equation then gives

    y−2=12y-2=12y−2=12 y=14y=14y=14

    In the original equations, 14−2=3(4)14-2=3(4)14−2=3(4) and 2(14−2)+4=282(14-2)+4=282(14−2)+4=28, so both hold.

    Answer

    (4,14)(4,14)(4,14).

    Key idea

    An equal expression may replace a whole repeated group as well as a single variable.

  3. Problem 3 The collected terms

    Solve the system for (x,y)(x,y)(x,y).

    2x+y=8+x2x+y=8+x2x+y=8+x y−1=3(x−1)y-1=3(x-1)y−1=3(x−1)

    1. Hint 1

      Rearrange an equation until one variable is alone.

    2. Hint 2

      The first equation gives yyy without introducing a fraction; put that expression in the second.

    3. Hint 3

      The value of xxx need not be a whole number; use its exact value when you back-substitute.

    Answer

    (52,112)\left(\frac52,\frac{11}{2}\right)(25​,211​), or (2.5,5.5)(2.5,5.5)(2.5,5.5).

    Full solution

    Subtracting xxx from the first equation and isolating yyy gives

    y=8−xy=8-xy=8−x

    Substitute into the second equation.

    8−x−1=3(x−1)8-x-1=3(x-1)8−x−1=3(x−1) 7−x=3x−37-x=3x-37−x=3x−3 10=4x10=4x10=4x x=52x=\frac52x=25​

    Back-substitution gives y=8−52y=8-\frac52y=8−25​, so

    y=112.y=\frac{11}{2}.y=211​.

    In the first original equation, both sides equal 212\frac{21}{2}221​, since 2(52)+112=2122\left(\frac52\right)+\frac{11}{2}=\frac{21}{2}2(25​)+211​=221​ and

    8+52=212.8+\frac52=\frac{21}{2}.8+25​=221​.

    In the second, both sides equal 92\frac9229​, since 112−1=92\frac{11}{2}-1=\frac92211​−1=29​ and

    3(52−1)=92.3\left(\frac52-1\right)=\frac92.3(25​−1)=29​.

    Answer

    (52,112)\left(\frac52,\frac{11}{2}\right)(25​,211​), or (2.5,5.5)(2.5,5.5)(2.5,5.5).

    Key idea

    Simplifying an equation that has variables on both sides before isolating a variable keeps the substitution short, and the solution pair need not be whole numbers.

  4. Problem 4 The requested crossing

    Two lines are described by x−y=2x-y=2x−y=2 and 4x+by=144x+by=144x+by=14, where bbb is a fixed but missing number. Their crossing is required to have y=1y=1y=1. Find bbb and the crossing pair, and check the pair in the completed equations.

    1. Hint 1

      The requested yyy value determines xxx in the first equation.

    2. Hint 2

      Use both coordinates in the second equation to recover its missing coefficient.

    Answer

    b=2b=2b=2; crossing (3,1)(3,1)(3,1).

    Full solution

    With y=1y=1y=1, the first equation gives

    x−1=2x-1=2x−1=2 x=3x=3x=3

    Substituting the pair into the second equation gives

    4(3)+b(1)=144(3)+b(1)=144(3)+b(1)=14 b=2b=2b=2

    The completed system is x−y=2x-y=2x−y=2 and 4x+2y=144x+2y=144x+2y=14.

    The pair (3,1)(3,1)(3,1) gives 3−1=23-1=23−1=2 and

    4(3)+2(1)=14.4(3)+2(1)=14.4(3)+2(1)=14.

    Replacing xxx by y+2y+2y+2 in the second equation leaves 6y=66y=66y=6, so this is its only crossing.

    Answer

    b=2b=2b=2; crossing (3,1)(3,1)(3,1).

    Key idea

    A specified coordinate at a crossing can determine a missing coefficient in one of the two equations.

  5. Problem 5 The two panels

    The figure offers two candidate graphs for the system y=2x−1y=2x-1y=2x−1 and x+y=5x+y=5x+y=5. Which panel shows the correct two lines? Find the exact crossing pair by substitution, then use it to justify your choice.

    Text description of this figure

    Two square coordinate grids, one above the other. The top grid is headed Panel A and the bottom grid Panel B. In each grid the x-axis and the y-axis run from negative 1 to 6 with equal scales, every whole number is labeled, and the origin is labeled 0. Each grid shows two solid straight lines with arrowheads where they leave the grid, and no equations, point labels or dots. In both panels one line, drawn in the text color, falls from the top-left corner (negative 1, 6) through (0, 5) and (5, 0) to the bottom-right corner (6, negative 1). In Panel A the other line, drawn in blue, rises 2 units for every 1 unit to the right: it leaves the bottom edge at (0, negative 1), crosses the x-axis halfway between 0 and 1, and leaves the top edge halfway between x equals 3 and x equals 4. In Panel B the blue line also rises 2 units for every 1 unit to the right: it leaves the bottom edge halfway between x equals 1 and x equals 2, crosses the x-axis at 2, and leaves the top edge at (5, 6). In each panel the two lines cross once inside the grid, and the crossing is not marked or labeled.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the common pair before comparing the panels.

    2. Hint 2

      Put the expression for yyy into x+y=5x+y=5x+y=5.

    3. Hint 3

      In each panel, read a crossing as xxx across and yyy up; swapping the two coordinates names a different point.

    Answer

    Panel A; (2,3)(2,3)(2,3).

    Full solution

    Substitution gives

    x+(2x−1)=5x+(2x-1)=5x+(2x−1)=5 3x=63x=63x=6 x=2x=2x=2

    Then y=2(2)−1y=2(2)-1y=2(2)−1, so y=3y=3y=3.

    The pair checks in both original equations: 3=2(2)−13=2(2)-13=2(2)−1 and 2+3=52+3=52+3=5.

    Panel B's lines cross at (3,2)(3,2)(3,2), which fails y=2x−1y=2x-1y=2x−1 because 2(3)−12(3)-12(3)−1 is 555, not 222.

    So Panel B cannot show this system.

    Panel A's lines cross at (2,3)(2,3)(2,3).

    Its rising line passes through (0,−1)(0,-1)(0,−1) and (2,3)(2,3)(2,3), which both satisfy y=2x−1y=2x-1y=2x−1, and its falling line passes through (0,5)(0,5)(0,5) and (5,0)(5,0)(5,0), which both satisfy x+y=5x+y=5x+y=5.

    Therefore Panel A is correct.

    Answer

    Panel A; (2,3)(2,3)(2,3).

    Key idea

    The common ordered pair found algebraically identifies the crossing in a graph.

  6. Problem 6 Mina's rewritten rule

    A pair (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) satisfies both rules x=(y+7)/3x=(y+7)/3x=(y+7)/3 and 2x−3y=−72x-3y=-72x−3y=−7. Mina first rewrites the first rule as 3x−y=73x-y=73x−y=7. Continue from that form using an isolation that introduces no fractions, find the pair, and check it in the two original rules.

    1. Hint 1

      In Mina's form, the coefficient of yyy is −1-1−1.

    2. Hint 2

      Isolate yyy in Mina's form, then put that expression into 2x−3y=−72x-3y=-72x−3y=−7 inside parentheses.

    Answer

    (4,5)(4,5)(4,5).

    Full solution

    Isolating xxx or yyy in 2x−3y=−72x-3y=-72x−3y=−7 would mean dividing by 222 or 333, and isolating xxx in Mina's form would mean dividing by 333.

    Only yyy in Mina's form has coefficient −1-1−1, so isolate it.

    y=3x−7y=3x-7y=3x−7

    Replace yyy in the second rule, keeping the parentheses so the minus sign reaches both terms.

    2x−3(3x−7)=−72x-3(3x-7)=-72x−3(3x−7)=−7 2x−9x+21=−72x-9x+21=-72x−9x+21=−7 −7x=−28-7x=-28−7x=−28 x=4x=4x=4

    Back-substitution gives

    y=3(4)−7y=3(4)-7y=3(4)−7 y=5y=5y=5

    Checking the original rules gives 4=(5+7)/34=(5+7)/34=(5+7)/3 and 2(4)−3(5)=−72(4)-3(5)=-72(4)−3(5)=−7, both true.

    Answer

    (4,5)(4,5)(4,5).

    Key idea

    Choosing a variable with coefficient one or negative one can avoid fractions even when a given rule contains a fraction.

  7. Problem 7 Two closing reports

    The first equation in each system is y=3x+2y=3x+2y=3x+2. In system A, the second equation is 2(y−1)=6x+22(y-1)=6x+22(y−1)=6x+2. In system B, it is 2(y−1)=6x+82(y-1)=6x+82(y−1)=6x+8. Decide how many solutions each system has and describe how its two lines sit.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the same expression for yyy in each second equation.

    2. Hint 2

      An identity keeps every pair on the first line; a false numerical statement keeps none.

    Answer

    A: infinitely many, the same line. B: no solution, distinct parallel lines.

    Full solution

    For A, replacement gives

    2(3x+2−1)=6x+22(3x+2-1)=6x+22(3x+2−1)=6x+2 6x+2=6x+26x+2=6x+26x+2=6x+2

    This is true for every xxx, so A has all pairs (x,3x+2)(x,3x+2)(x,3x+2) and its equations describe the same line.

    For B, replacement gives

    2(3x+2−1)=6x+82(3x+2-1)=6x+82(3x+2−1)=6x+8 6x+2=6x+86x+2=6x+86x+2=6x+8 2=82=82=8

    This is impossible.

    The second equation can also be written y=3x+5y=3x+5y=3x+5, so B has two distinct parallel lines.

    Answer

    A: infinitely many, the same line. B: no solution, distinct parallel lines.

    Key idea

    After substitution cancels the variable, the remaining constants distinguish one repeated line from two parallel lines.

  8. Problem 8 Teo's repair

    The pair (3,2)(3,2)(3,2) fits y=5−xy=5-xy=5−x but fails 2x−y=12x-y=12x−y=1. Teo keeps y=2y=2y=2 and changes only xxx until the second equation holds. He claims this repairs the solution of the system. What pair does he obtain, and is his claim correct?

    1. Hint 1

      Decide what a pair must satisfy to solve the whole system.

    2. Hint 2

      With y=2y=2y=2, solve the second equation for xxx, then test the changed pair in the first.

    Answer

    (32,2)\left(\frac32,2\right)(23​,2), or (1.5,2)(1.5,2)(1.5,2); the claim is incorrect.

    Full solution

    Keeping y=2y=2y=2 in the second equation gives

    2x−2=12x-2=12x−2=1 2x=32x=32x=3 x=32x=\frac32x=23​

    Thus Teo obtains (32,2)\left(\frac32,2\right)(23​,2).

    This pair satisfies the second equation, but the first would require

    y=5−32y=5-\frac32y=5−23​ y=72y=\frac72y=27​

    Its actual second coordinate is 222, so the pair fails the first equation.

    Fixing the second condition alone has disturbed the first, and the system is not solved.

    Answer

    (32,2)\left(\frac32,2\right)(23​,2), or (1.5,2)(1.5,2)(1.5,2); the claim is incorrect.

    Key idea

    A repaired coordinate must be tested in every original condition, including one the earlier pair satisfied.

  9. Problem 9 The second route

    For x+2y=9x+2y=9x+2y=9 and y=4−xy=4-xy=4−x, Arun replaces yyy in the first equation. Bea first rewrites the second equation as x=4−yx=4-yx=4−y and replaces xxx in the first equation. Bea says both routes must lead to the same pair. Is she correct? Carry out both routes, then explain why two valid routes could not give different pairs.

    1. Hint 1

      Each expression used for replacement comes from the same second equation.

    2. Hint 2

      One route leaves only xxx, while the other leaves only yyy.

    Answer

    Yes; both routes give (−1,5)(-1,5)(−1,5).

    Full solution

    Arun obtains

    x+2(4−x)=9x+2(4-x)=9x+2(4−x)=9 8−x=98-x=98−x=9 x=−1x=-1x=−1

    The second equation then gives y=5y=5y=5.

    Bea obtains

    (4−y)+2y=9(4-y)+2y=9(4−y)+2y=9 4+y=94+y=94+y=9 y=5y=5y=5

    Her expression for xxx then gives x=−1x=-1x=−1.

    Each route replaces a quantity by an equal one, so its one-variable equation, together with the second equation, is satisfied by exactly the pairs that solve the system.

    Both routes therefore find the solutions of the same system, so two valid routes could not give different pairs.

    The pair checks because −1+2(5)=9-1+2(5)=9−1+2(5)=9 and 5=4−(−1)5=4-(-1)5=4−(−1), so Bea is correct.

    Answer

    Yes; both routes give (−1,5)(-1,5)(−1,5).

    Key idea

    Valid isolations of the same equation can produce different one-variable routes to the same common pair.

  10. Problem 10 A shifted system

    A system has equations y=4x−7y=4x-7y=4x−7 and x+2y=4x+2y=4x+2y=4. In a new system, they become y=4x−5y=4x-5y=4x−5 and x+2y=8x+2y=8x+2y=8. Noor says every solution of the old system becomes a solution of the new one by keeping xxx and adding 222 to yyy. Is this correct, and can the change be reversed? Explain without solving either system.

    1. Hint 1

      Write a new pair as (x,y+2)(x,y+2)(x,y+2) using an old solution (x,y)(x,y)(x,y).

    2. Hint 2

      Check each new equation using the corresponding old equation, then consider subtracting 222.

    Answer

    Yes; adding 222 to yyy works, and subtracting 222 reverses it.

    Full solution

    For an old solution, y=4x−7y=4x-7y=4x−7, so

    y+2=4x−5y+2=4x-5y+2=4x−5

    Thus the changed pair satisfies the first new equation.

    The old pair also has x+2y=4x+2y=4x+2y=4.

    Adding 444 to both sides gives x+2y+4=8x+2y+4=8x+2y+4=8, which is

    x+2(y+2)=8x+2(y+2)=8x+2(y+2)=8

    so the changed pair satisfies the second new equation.

    Conversely, if (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) solves the new system, then y−2=4x−7y-2=4x-7y−2=4x−7 and x+2(y−2)=4x+2(y-2)=4x+2(y−2)=4, so subtracting 222 from the second coordinate gives an old solution.

    The change therefore matches the solution pairs in both directions.

    Answer

    Yes; adding 222 to yyy works, and subtracting 222 reverses it.

    Key idea

    Check a proposed change of coordinates in every equation and in reverse to establish that it matches two solution sets.
