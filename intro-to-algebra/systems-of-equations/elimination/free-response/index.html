  1. Problem 1 The erased row

    A system starts with 5x+2y=195x+2y=195x+2y=19. Twice this equation minus the second equation gives 7x=217x=217x=21. What was the second equation, in standard form?

    1. Hint 1

      Write twice the first equation with both sides doubled.

    2. Hint 2

      The erased left and right sides are what must be subtracted to leave the recorded row.

    Answer

    3x+4y=173x+4y=173x+4y=17.

    Full solution

    Twice the first equation is

    10x+4y=3810x+4y=3810x+4y=38

    To leave 7x7x7x on the left, the subtracted left side must be 3x+4y3x+4y3x+4y.

    To leave 212121 on the right, the subtracted right side must be 171717.

    Therefore the second equation was 3x+4y=173x+4y=173x+4y=17.

    Checking the combination gives

    10x+4y−(3x+4y)=7x10x+4y-(3x+4y)=7x10x+4y−(3x+4y)=7x

    and 38−17=2138-17=2138−17=21, as required.

    Answer

    3x+4y=173x+4y=173x+4y=17.

    Key idea

    A recorded equation combination determines an erased equation when the other equation and the combination are known.

  2. Problem 2 Terms on both sides

    Solve the system for (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) and check the pair in both original equations.

    7x=3y+177x=3y+177x=3y+17 3y=1−2x3y=1-2x3y=1−2x

    1. Hint 1

      Columns only line up when every variable term sits on the same side of each equation.

    2. Hint 2

      Rewrite each equation in the form ax+by=cax+by=cax+by=c, then compare the two coefficients of yyy.

    Answer

    (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1).

    Full solution

    Subtracting 3y3y3y from both sides of the first equation gives

    7x−3y=177x-3y=177x−3y=17

    Adding 2x2x2x to both sides of the second equation gives

    2x+3y=12x+3y=12x+3y=1

    The coefficients of yyy are now opposites, so adding the two equations cancels yyy.

    9x=189x=189x=18 x=2x=2x=2

    Back-substituting into 2x+3y=12x+3y=12x+3y=1 gives 4+3y=14+3y=14+3y=1, so 3y=−33y=-33y=−3 and y=−1y=-1y=−1.

    In the first original equation, 7(2)=147(2)=147(2)=14 and 3(−1)+17=143(-1)+17=143(−1)+17=14, so it holds.

    In the second, 3(−1)=−33(-1)=-33(−1)=−3 and 1−2(2)=−31-2(2)=-31−2(2)=−3, so it holds too.

    Answer

    (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1).

    Key idea

    Putting both equations in standard form lines up the columns, so a matching pair of coefficients becomes visible.

  3. Problem 3 The paired totals

    Solve the system for (x,y)(x,y)(x,y).

    2(x+y)+3y=162(x+y)+3y=162(x+y)+3y=16 3(x+y)−2y=113(x+y)-2y=113(x+y)−2y=11

    1. Hint 1

      Match the coefficients of the whole group x+yx+yx+y.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiply the first equation by 333 and the second by 222, then subtract.

    Answer

    (3,2)(3,2)(3,2).

    Full solution

    The scaled equations are

    6(x+y)+9y=486(x+y)+9y=486(x+y)+9y=48 6(x+y)−4y=226(x+y)-4y=226(x+y)−4y=22

    Subtracting gives

    13y=2613y=2613y=26 y=2y=2y=2

    The first original equation becomes 2(x+2)+6=162(x+2)+6=162(x+2)+6=16, so 2x=62x=62x=6 and x=3x=3x=3.

    Checking gives 2(3+2)+3(2)=162(3+2)+3(2)=162(3+2)+3(2)=16 and

    3(3+2)−2(2)=11.3(3+2)-2(2)=11.3(3+2)−2(2)=11.

    Answer

    (3,2)(3,2)(3,2).

    Key idea

    Scaling can match coefficients of an entire shared group, after which elimination works as usual.

  4. Problem 4 Two rows of boards

    Boards of lengths aaa cm and bbb cm are laid end to end in two rows. Two boards of length aaa and three of length bbb make a row 242424 cm long, so 2a+3b=242a+3b=242a+3b=24. Four boards of length aaa and one of length bbb make a row 282828 cm long, so 4a+b=284a+b=284a+b=28. Find aaa and bbb, then find how the length of either row changes when one board of length aaa is replaced by one board of length bbb.

    1. Hint 1

      Solving the two equations together gives both lengths, once one variable's coefficients are matched by scaling.

    2. Hint 2

      Doubling the first equation gives it the same aaa coefficient as the second, so subtracting then cancels aaa.

    3. Hint 3

      After finding both lengths, a replacement changes a row by the new length minus the old length.

    Answer

    a=6a=6a=6 cm and b=4b=4b=4 cm; either row becomes 222 cm shorter.

    Full solution

    Double the first equation.

    4a+6b=484a+6b=484a+6b=48

    Subtract the second original equation.

    5b=205b=205b=20 b=4b=4b=4

    Back-substitute into 4a+b=284a+b=284a+b=28.

    4a+4=284a+4=284a+4=28 a=6a=6a=6

    The checks are 2(6)+3(4)=242(6)+3(4)=242(6)+3(4)=24 and 4(6)+4=284(6)+4=284(6)+4=28.

    Replacing one board of length aaa by one of length bbb changes a row by 4−6=−24-6=-24−6=−2 cm, whichever row it is.

    So either row becomes 222 cm shorter: the first becomes 6+3(4)+4=226+3(4)+4=226+3(4)+4=22 cm and the second 3(6)+4+4=263(6)+4+4=263(6)+4+4=26 cm.

    Answer

    a=6a=6a=6 cm and b=4b=4b=4 cm; either row becomes 222 cm shorter.

    Key idea

    After solving for component amounts, use their difference to evaluate a replacement.

  5. Problem 5 The calibration pair

      Clear the denominators in each whole equation first.

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    Full solution

    The fractional checks are

    42+63=4\frac42+\frac63=424​+36​=4 43−62=−53\frac43-\frac62=-\frac5334​−26​=−35​

  6. Problem 6 The adjustable coefficient

    In the system kx−4y=21kx-4y=21kx−4y=21 and 3x−2y=83x-2y=83x−2y=8, the letter kkk stands for a fixed number. Find the value of kkk for which the system has no solution, and state how many solutions the system has for every other value of kkk.

    1. Hint 1

      A system has no solution exactly when elimination leaves a false statement with no variable in it.

    2. Hint 2

      Double the second equation so its yyy term matches the first, subtract, and collect the xxx terms into one product.

    3. Hint 3

      Dividing by an expression that contains kkk is allowed only for the values of kkk that keep it nonzero.

    Answer

    k=6k=6k=6 gives no solution; every other value of kkk gives exactly one solution.

    Full solution

    Doubling the second equation gives

    6x−4y=166x-4y=166x−4y=16

    Subtracting it from the first equation cancels yyy.

    kx−6x=21−16kx-6x=21-16kx−6x=21−16 (k−6)x=5(k-6)x=5(k−6)x=5

    If k=6k=6k=6, the left side is 0x0x0x, so the equation says 0=50=50=5.

    That is false for every pair, so no pair satisfies both equations, and the system has no solution.

    If kkk is any other number, then k−6k-6k−6 is not zero, so dividing by it is allowed.

    x=5k−6x=\frac{5}{k-6}x=k−65​

    That single value of xxx then gives a single value of yyy from 3x−2y=83x-2y=83x−2y=8.

    That pair satisfies the first equation as well, because twice the second equation added to the combined equation gives back the first: (k−6)x+2(3x−2y)(k-6)x+2(3x-2y)(k−6)x+2(3x−2y) simplifies to kx−4ykx-4ykx−4y, and 5+2(8)=215+2(8)=215+2(8)=21.

    So the system has exactly one solution.

    For example, k=7k=7k=7 gives x=5x=5x=5, and then 15−2y=815-2y=815−2y=8 gives y=72y=\frac72y=27​.

    The first equation checks: 7(5)=357(5)=357(5)=35, 4(72)=144\left(\frac72\right)=144(27​)=14, and 35−14=2135-14=2135−14=21.

    Answer

    k=6k=6k=6 gives no solution; every other value of kkk gives exactly one solution.

    Key idea

    When a coefficient is unknown, elimination shows which value makes both variables cancel, and a false leftover statement then means no solution.

  7. Problem 7 The two formats

    The same system is recorded in two formats. Format A is y=11−3xy=11-3xy=11−3x and 2x+y=72x+y=72x+y=7. Format B is 3x+y=113x+y=113x+y=11 and 2x+y=72x+y=72x+y=7. Give a short opening step suited to each format, and finish each route.

    1. Hint 1

      One format already has a variable isolated; the other has matching coefficients.

    2. Hint 2

      In format B, compare the coefficients of yyy to choose between adding and subtracting; either format then leaves one equation in xxx to solve and back-substitute.

    Answer

    A: replace yyy by 11−3x11-3x11−3x. B: subtract one equation from the other. Both give (4,−1)(4,-1)(4,−1).

    Full solution

    For A, replacement gives

    2x+(11−3x)=72x+(11-3x)=72x+(11−3x)=7 11−x=711-x=711−x=7 x=4x=4x=4

    Thus

    y=11−3(4)=−1.y=11-3(4)=-1.y=11−3(4)=−1.

    For B, the coefficients of yyy are equal, so subtracting the second equation from the first gives

    x=4x=4x=4

    Subtracting the first from the second instead gives −x=−4-x=-4−x=−4, the same value.

    Back-substitution into 2x+y=72x+y=72x+y=7 gives y=−1y=-1y=−1.

    The results agree.

    Checking the common pair gives −1=11−3(4)-1=11-3(4)−1=11−3(4), 2(4)−1=72(4)-1=72(4)−1=7, and 3(4)−1=113(4)-1=113(4)−1=11, so either format represents the same solution.

    Answer

    A: replace yyy by 11−3x11-3x11−3x. B: subtract one equation from the other. Both give (4,−1)(4,-1)(4,−1).

    Key idea

    The form in which a system is written can make replacement or cancellation the shorter opening.

  8. Problem 8 Both rows replaced

    A system is x+3y=13x+3y=13x+3y=13 and 2x−y=52x-y=52x−y=5. Sol replaces both equations: one by their sum, 3x+2y=183x+2y=183x+2y=18, and the other by the first minus the second, −x+4y=8-x+4y=8−x+4y=8. He claims the new system has exactly the same solutions as the original. Is he correct? Justify your answer, then solve the new system.

    1. Hint 1

      Each new equation is a sum or difference of true equations, so every solution of the original system satisfies it.

    2. Hint 2

      For the other direction, two quantities are fixed once their sum and their difference are both known; combine the new equations so an original left side reappears, then scale.

    Answer

    Yes. Both systems have the single solution (4,3)(4,3)(4,3).

    Full solution

    A pair that solves the original system makes both original equations true.

    Adding or subtracting true equations gives a true equation, so the pair satisfies 3x+2y=183x+2y=183x+2y=18 and −x+4y=8-x+4y=8−x+4y=8.

    No solution is lost.

    Now take any pair that solves the new system.

    Adding the two new equations gives

    2x+6y=262x+6y=262x+6y=26

    Halving both sides gives

    x+3y=13x+3y=13x+3y=13

    Subtracting the second new equation from the first gives

    4x−2y=104x-2y=104x−2y=10

    Halving both sides gives

    2x−y=52x-y=52x−y=5

    So every solution of the new system also satisfies both original equations, and no solution is invented.

    Sol is correct.

    To solve the new system, double 3x+2y=183x+2y=183x+2y=18 to get 6x+4y=366x+4y=366x+4y=36, then subtract −x+4y=8-x+4y=8−x+4y=8.

    7x=287x=287x=28 x=4x=4x=4

    Back-substituting gives 12+2y=1812+2y=1812+2y=18, so y=3y=3y=3.

    The pair (4,3)(4,3)(4,3) checks in all four equations: 4+9=134+9=134+9=13, 8−3=58-3=58−3=5, 12+6=1812+6=1812+6=18 and −4+12=8-4+12=8−4+12=8.

    Answer

    Yes. Both systems have the single solution (4,3)(4,3)(4,3).

    Key idea

    Replacing equations by combinations keeps the solutions when the original equations can be rebuilt from the new ones.

  9. Problem 9 The two zero rows

    After valid elimination, system A becomes x−2y=4x-2y=4x−2y=4 together with 0=00=00=0. System B becomes x−2y=4x-2y=4x−2y=4 together with 0=30=30=3. Elena says both systems have no solution, because neither last row contains a variable. Is she correct? Describe the solutions of each system and give two examples when possible.

    1. Hint 1

      Ask whether the last row accepts every pair or rejects every pair.

    2. Hint 2

      If it accepts every pair, the retained equation still restricts the coordinates.

    Answer

    No. A: infinitely many solutions, every pair (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) with x−2y=4x-2y=4x−2y=4 (equivalently x=2y+4x=2y+4x=2y+4), such as (4,0)(4,0)(4,0) and (6,1)(6,1)(6,1); any two such pairs are accepted. B: no solution.

    Full solution

    For A, 0=00=00=0 imposes no additional restriction.

    The retained equation gives x=2y+4x=2y+4x=2y+4, so each choice of yyy gives a matching xxx, and every pair on the line x−2y=4x-2y=4x−2y=4 is a solution.

    For example, (4,0)(4,0)(4,0) and (6,1)(6,1)(6,1) both satisfy x−2y=4x-2y=4x−2y=4.

    For B, 0=30=30=3 is false for every pair.

    Consequently no pair satisfies both rows.

    The absence of variables does not make the two outcomes alike: one row is always true, and the other is never true.

    So Elena's conclusion holds for B, but not because the row lacks a variable; for A it is wrong.

    Answer

    No. A: infinitely many solutions, every pair (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) with x−2y=4x-2y=4x−2y=4 (equivalently x=2y+4x=2y+4x=2y+4), such as (4,0)(4,0)(4,0) and (6,1)(6,1)(6,1); any two such pairs are accepted. B: no solution.

    Key idea

    After cancellation, the truth of the remaining numerical statement determines whether the retained equation describes solutions or none exist.

  10. Problem 10 The sum that kept both variables

    For 4x+5y=284x+5y=284x+5y=28 and 4x+2y=164x+2y=164x+2y=16, Ivo adds the equations and gets 8x+7y=448x+7y=448x+7y=44. He says this new equation must be false, because adding did not remove a variable. Is he correct? Explain what adding did and did not achieve, then solve the system.

    1. Hint 1

      Can the sum of two true equations ever be false? Ask separately what adding was meant to achieve here.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the two coefficients of xxx: are they opposites or equal, and which operation cancels a pair like that?

    Answer

    No: 8x+7y=448x+7y=448x+7y=44 is true for every solution of the system, but it removes no variable. The solution is (2,4)(2,4)(2,4).

    Full solution

    A pair that solves the system makes both equations true, and adding true equations gives a true equation.

    So 8x+7y=448x+7y=448x+7y=44 holds for every solution, and Ivo is wrong to call it false.

    Adding did not eliminate anything, though.

    The coefficients of xxx are 444 and 444, equal rather than opposite, so adding doubled the xxx term instead of canceling it, and the new equation still has two unknowns.

    Because the coefficients of xxx are equal, subtracting the second equation from the first cancels xxx.

    3y=123y=123y=12 y=4y=4y=4

    Back-substituting into 4x+2y=164x+2y=164x+2y=16 gives 4x+8=164x+8=164x+8=16, so x=2x=2x=2.

    The pair checks, since 4(2)+5(4)=284(2)+5(4)=284(2)+5(4)=28 and

    4(2)+2(4)=16.4(2)+2(4)=16.4(2)+2(4)=16.

    It satisfies Ivo's equation too, since

    8(2)+7(4)=44.8(2)+7(4)=44.8(2)+7(4)=44.

    Answer

    No: 8x+7y=448x+7y=448x+7y=44 is true for every solution of the system, but it removes no variable. The solution is (2,4)(2,4)(2,4).

    Key idea

    Every sum of true equations is true, but only adding opposite coefficients or subtracting equal ones eliminates a variable.
