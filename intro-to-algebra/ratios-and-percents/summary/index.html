Ratios, Percents, and Proportion: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Common multiplier sss
The size of one equal part: a ratio a:b:ca : b : ca:b:c describes amounts asasas, bsbsbs, cscscs, sharing one sss. A ratio hides exactly this one unknown.
Part-to-part and part-to-whole
A ratio a:ba : ba:b compares the parts to each other. The first quantity is aa+b\frac{a}{a + b}a+ba​ of the total, not ab\frac{a}{b}ba​: the whole is the sum of the parts.
Conversion factor
A fraction whose top and bottom name equal amounts, like 12 in1 ft\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft12 in​, so it equals 111. Multiplying by it changes units, not the amount.
Dimensional analysis
Arranging conversion factors so unwanted units cancel. The surviving units are the check: a stray ft2/in\text{ft}^2/\text{in}ft2/in means a factor is upside down.
Rate rrr (a percent as a decimal)
The percent divided by 100100100, so 30%30\%30% gives r=0.30r = 0.30r=0.30. Percent formulas take this decimal, never the whole number 303030.
Percent change factor
The single multiplier that performs a change: 1+r1 + r1+r for an increase, 1−r1 - r1−r for a decrease, each applied to the original amount.
Constant of proportionality kkk
The one fixed number governing a variation relationship. One complete set of matching values determines it, as long as every directly varying quantity in that set is nonzero, and it then answers every other question.
Direct variation
yyy varies directly with xxx when y=kxy = kxy=kx, so the ratio y/xy/xy/x stays constant. Doubling xxx doubles yyy, and the graph is a line through the origin.
Inverse variation
yyy varies inversely with xxx when y=kxy = \frac{k}{x}y=xk​, so the product xyxyxy stays constant. Doubling xxx halves yyy.
Work rate
The fraction of a job finished per unit of time: a job taking TTT alone runs at rate 1T\frac{1}{T}T1​, so rate and time are reciprocals. A drain is a negative rate.

Formulas and theorems

  • Finding the common multiplier sss

    A ratio a:b:ca : b : ca:b:c describes asasas, bsbsbs, cscscs. Total: (a+b+c)s=N(a + b + c)s = N(a+b+c)s=N. Difference of two terms: (a−b)s=D(a - b)s = D(a−b)s=D, with a>ba > ba>b. One known amount: as=Aas = Aas=A.

    Use when Every term shares the SAME sss, never one letter per term. The total form needs the listed terms to make up all of NNN; each form is one linear equation in the single unknown sss.

    e.g. 7:47 : 47:4 with a difference of 151515: 3s=153s = 153s=15, so s=5s = 5s=5 and the amounts are 353535 and 202020.

  • A conversion factor equals 111

    1 ft=12 in  ⟹  12 in1 ft=1 ft12 in=1\begin{gathered} 1 \text{ ft} = 12 \text{ in} \\ \implies \frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}} = \frac{1 \text{ ft}}{12 \text{ in}} = 1 \end{gathered}1 ft=12 in⟹1 ft12 in​=12 in1 ft​=1​

    Use when The two sides must name genuinely equal amounts; an invented ratio like 10 in1 ft\frac{10 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft10 in​ is not 111. Both orientations exist: use the one putting the unwanted unit opposite itself.

  • Squared and cubed units

    (12 in1 ft)n=11 ft2=144 in21 ft3=1728 in3\begin{gathered} \left(\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}\right)^{n} = 1 \\ 1 \text{ ft}^2 = 144 \text{ in}^2 \\ 1 \text{ ft}^3 = 1728 \text{ in}^3 \end{gathered}(1 ft12 in​)n=11 ft2=144 in21 ft3=1728 in3​
    Text description

    On the left, a 12 inch square ruled into a 12 by 12 grid of 144 unit squares, with one corner square shaded. On the right, a 12 inch cube drawn as a plain outline in three quarter view, with no interior lines: its front face carries one shaded square a twelfth of an edge wide, marking the unit length rather than a unit volume, since a flat square cannot represent a cube. The count of 1728 unit cubes is stated in the caption rather than drawn, since 12 layers of 12 rows of 12 would not read at this size.

    Use when A unit with the nnnth power hides nnn lengths, so raise the length factor to the nnnth power. It is still 111, so the amount is untouched.

    e.g. 2 yd3×(3 ft1 yd)3=2×27=54 ft32 \text{ yd}^3 \times \left(\frac{3 \text{ ft}}{1 \text{ yd}}\right)^3 = 2 \times 27 = 54 \text{ ft}^32 yd3×(1 yd3 ft​)3=2×27=54 ft3.

  • The percent relationship

    p=r w,w=pr,r=pwp = r\,w, \qquad w = \frac{p}{r}, \qquad r = \frac{p}{w}p=rw,w=rp​,r=wp​

    Use when rrr is the percent as a decimal, www the whole (the number after "of"; for a commission, the sales). Dividing needs r≠0r \neq 0r=0 for the whole, w≠0w \neq 0w=0 for the rate.

    e.g. 272727 out of 454545: r=2745=0.6=60%r = \frac{27}{45} = 0.6 = 60\%r=4527​=0.6=60%.

  • Percent change and its reversal

    new=(1±r) w,w=new1±r\text{new} = (1 \pm r)\,w, \qquad w = \frac{\text{new}}{1 \pm r}new=(1±r)w,w=1±rnew​

    Use when 1+r1 + r1+r for tax, tip, or markup (a markup is figured on the cost); 1−r1 - r1−r for a discount, with r<1r < 1r<1 so the reversal can divide. The rate is measured against the original www, so undoing divides by that same factor.

    e.g. 8%8\%8% tax on 250250250 gives 1.08×250=2701.08 \times 250 = 2701.08×250=270.

  • Successive percent changes

    final=f1f2 w(1+r1)(1+r2)=1+r1+r2+r1r2\begin{gathered} \text{final} = f_1 f_2 \, w \\ (1 + r_1)(1 + r_2) \\ = 1 + r_1 + r_2 + r_1 r_2 \end{gathered}final=f1​f2​w(1+r1​)(1+r2​)=1+r1​+r2​+r1​r2​​

    Use when Each fff is 1+r1 + r1+r or 1−r1 - r1−r, acting on the amount the previous change produced. Rates add only when taken of the same base.

    e.g. 1.10×1.10=1.211.10 \times 1.10 = 1.211.10×1.10=1.21, so two 10%10\%10% raises make a 21%21\%21% raise, not 20%20\%20%.

  • Simple interest

    I=Prt,A=P(1+rt)I = P r t, \qquad A = P(1 + rt)I=Prt,A=P(1+rt)

    Use when rrr is a decimal rate per period and ttt counts those same periods (an annual rate needs ttt in years). The principal PPP stays fixed, which is what makes it simple, not compound, so the balance grows in a straight line.

    e.g. 120012001200 at 4%4\%4% for 555 years: I=1200×0.04×5=240I = 1200 \times 0.04 \times 5 = 240I=1200×0.04×5=240, so A=1440A = 1440A=1440.

  • Direct and inverse variation

    y=kx,equivalentlyyx=ky=kx,equivalentlyxy=k\begin{gathered} y = kx, \quad \text{equivalently} \quad \frac{y}{x} = k \\ y = \frac{k}{x}, \quad \text{equivalently} \quad xy = k \end{gathered}y=kx,equivalentlyxy​=ky=xk​,equivalentlyxy=k​
    Text description

    Side by side, direct variation graphs as a straight line through the origin while inverse variation graphs as a falling curve that never touches either axis.

    Use when Direct needs the ratio y/xy/xy/x steady across every matching pair, inverse needs the product xyxyxy steady. Both need x≠0x \neq 0x=0 to divide.

    e.g. (2,3)(2, 3)(2,3) and (4,6)(4, 6)(4,6) share y/x=1.5y/x = 1.5y/x=1.5, so direct; (2,6)(2, 6)(2,6) and (4,3)(4, 3)(4,3) share xy=12xy = 12xy=12, so inverse.

  • Joint and combined variation

    y=kxz,y=kxzy = kxz, \qquad y = \frac{kx}{z}y=kxz,y=zkx​

    Use when A quantity varied with directly belongs on top, one varied with inversely on the bottom, and z≠0z \neq 0z=0. One complete set of matching values fixes kkk, as long as every directly varying quantity in it is nonzero.

  • Work rates add

    1T1+1T2=1T\frac{1}{T_1} + \frac{1}{T_2} = \frac{1}{T}T1​1​+T2​1​=T1​

    Use when Steady paces on the same whole job, all times in one unit, nobody in the way. An opposing agent enters with a minus sign, and the job finishes only if the net rate is positive.

    e.g. 333 hours and 666 hours alone: 13+16=12\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{2}31​+61​=21​, so together T=2T = 2T=2 hours.

  • Distance, rate, and time

    d=rt,r=dt,t=drd = rt, \qquad r = \frac{d}{t}, \qquad t = \frac{d}{r}d=rt,r=td​,t=rd​

    Use when The speed must be steady across the stretch measured, and the units must agree (mph with hours). A rate needs t≠0t \neq 0t=0, a time needs r≠0r \neq 0r=0.

  • Average speed

    average speed=total distancetotal time\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}average speed=total timetotal distance​

    Use when The one steady speed that would cover the same distance in the same total time, for any mix of speeds. On a two-leg trip it equals the mean of the two speeds only when the speeds are equal or the two TIMES are equal; over equal distances at two different speeds it lands below the mean.

Problem types, step by step

Split a total or a difference by a ratio

  1. Write every quantity as a multiple of one part: asasas, bsbsbs, cscscs.
  2. Turn the extra fact into one equation: a sum, a difference, or a single known term.
  3. Solve for sss, then multiply it back through every term.
  4. Check the amounts add or differ as stated and reduce to the original ratio.

e.g. 2:3:52 : 3 : 52:3:5 sharing 606060: 10s=6010s = 6010s=60, so s=6s = 6s=6 and the amounts are 121212, 181818, 303030.

Combine two ratios into a three-term ratio

  1. Find the quantity the two ratios share.
  2. Scale each ratio so the shared term becomes the least common multiple of its two values.
  3. Line the scaled ratios up as one three-term ratio.
  4. Given an actual amount, set that term equal to it and solve for sss.

e.g. A:B=2:3A : B = 2 : 3A:B=2:3 and B:C=4:5B : C = 4 : 5B:C=4:5 scale to 8:128 : 128:12 and 12:1512 : 1512:15, so A:B:C=8:12:15A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15A:B:C=8:12:15.

Before and after: the ratio changes

  1. Write the starting amounts as asasas and bsbsbs.
  2. Apply each change to the amount, not to the ratio number.
  3. Set the changed pair equal to the new ratio and cross-multiply.
  4. Solve the linear equation for sss, multiply back, and check both ratios.

e.g. 3s3s3s red and 2s2s2s green, 101010 reds added, new ratio 2:12 : 12:1: 3s+10=4s3s + 10 = 4s3s+10=4s, so s=10s = 10s=10.

Convert a unit, a chain of units, or a rate

  1. Write the starting quantity as a fraction, units attached.
  2. For each unit to remove, attach a factor with that unit on the opposite side of the bar.
  3. Raise a factor to the nnnth power for any unit carrying the nnnth power.
  4. Cancel, confirm only the target units survive, then multiply the tops and divide by the bottoms.

e.g. 90 km/h×1000 m1 km×1 h3600 s=25 m/s90 \text{ km/h} \times \frac{1000 \text{ m}}{1 \text{ km}} \times \frac{1 \text{ h}}{3600 \text{ s}} = 25 \text{ m/s}90 km/h×1 km1000 m​×3600 s1 h​=25 m/s.

Find the part, the whole, or the percent

  1. Convert the percent to a decimal rate.
  2. Label which of ppp, rrr, www is missing; the whole follows "of".
  3. Write p=r wp = r\,wp=rw and solve for that letter.
  4. Turn a rate answer back into a percent by multiplying by 100100100.

e.g. 454545 is 30%30\%30% of what number? 0.30 w=450.30\,w = 450.30w=45, so w=150w = 150w=150.

Apply, reverse, or stack a percent change

  1. Build a factor for each change: 1+r1 + r1+r to add, 1−r1 - r1−r to remove.
  2. Going forward, multiply the original by each factor in order.
  3. Going back from an after amount, divide by the factor instead.
  4. Multiply the factors together to read the single net change.

e.g. A shirt is 636363 after a 10%10\%10% discount, so the original was 630.90=70\frac{63}{0.90} = 700.9063​=70.

Solve a variation problem

  1. Decide the type from the wording, or from a table: steady y/xy/xy/x is direct, steady xyxyxy is inverse.
  2. Write the equation with kkk unknown, substitute the one complete set of values, and solve for kkk.
  3. Rewrite with kkk filled in, then substitute the new values.
  4. Check the direction: with a positive kkk, direct answers move with xxx and inverse answers move against it.

e.g. yyy inverse with xxx and y=12y = 12y=12 at x=5x = 5x=5: k=60k = 60k=60, so at x=8x = 8x=8, y=7.5y = 7.5y=7.5.

Work together, or find a missing worker's time

  1. Turn every stated time into a rate 1T\frac{1}{T}T1​.
  2. Add the rates, subtracting any that oppose the job.
  3. For an unknown solo time, set the sum equal to the known combined rate and solve for the missing rate.
  4. Take the reciprocal of the rate you end with; with nothing opposing, the team beats the fastest worker alone.

e.g. 110+1T2=16\frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{T_2} = \frac{1}{6}101​+T2​1​=61​ gives 1T2=115\frac{1}{T_2} = \frac{1}{15}T2​1​=151​, so T2=15T_2 = 15T2​=15 hours.

Average speed over a multi-leg trip

  1. Find each leg's distance and its time, using t=drt = \frac{d}{r}t=rd​ for a missing time.
  2. Add all the distances, then add all the times.
  3. Divide total distance by total time.
  4. Check the answer sits between the slowest and fastest leg speeds, nearer the speed of the leg that took more time.

e.g. 120120120 miles at 606060 mph then 120120120 miles at 404040 mph: 2402+3=48\frac{240}{2 + 3} = 482+3240​=48 mph.

Exam traps

  • Trap Treating a percent increase and an equal percent decrease as cancelling out.

    Fix Factors multiply: (1+r)(1−r)=1−r2(1 + r)(1 - r) = 1 - r^2(1+r)(1−r)=1−r2, below 111 for every r>0r > 0r>0. Up 20%20\%20% then down 20%20\%20% gives 0.960.960.96, a 4%4\%4% net loss, in either order.

  • Trap Reading "20%20\%20% more than 505050" as "20%20\%20% of 505050".

    Fix "Of" is the part alone, 0.20×50=100.20 \times 50 = 100.20×50=10; "more than" is the whole plus the part, 1.20×50=601.20 \times 50 = 601.20×50=60.

  • Trap Reversing a change by taking the percent off the new amount.

    Fix The percent was figured on the original, so divide by the factor: 929292 after 15%15\%15% tax undoes to 921.15=80\frac{92}{1.15} = 801.1592​=80, not to 92−0.15×92=78.2092 - 0.15 \times 92 = 78.2092−0.15×92=78.20.

  • Trap Averaging the two speeds of a there-and-back trip.

    Fix Use total distance over total time. Over equal distances at two different speeds the true average always falls below the mean, since more time is spent on the slow leg.

  • Trap Averaging or adding the workers' solo times, or answering with the combined rate.

    Fix Only rates add. Sum 1T1+1T2\frac{1}{T_1} + \frac{1}{T_2}T1​1​+T2​1​, then flip it: 512\frac{5}{12}125​ of the job per hour means 125\frac{12}{5}512​ hours.

  • Trap Handling an inverse relationship by adding or subtracting the change, or by reaching for y=kxy = kxy=kx.

    Fix Inverse variation scales: 444 workers taking 999 days give k=36k = 36k=36, so 666 workers take 366=6\frac{36}{6} = 6636​=6 days, not 9−2=79 - 2 = 79−2=7.

  • Trap Converting a squared or cubed unit with the length factor used once.

    Fix Area hides two lengths and volume three, so square or cube the whole factor: 122=14412^2 = 144122=144 and 123=172812^3 = 1728123=1728, never 121212.

  • Trap Multiplying by the bottom unit's factor when converting a rate.

    Fix A unit in the denominator has to leave the denominator, so its factor goes in flipped: 909090 km/h needs ×1 h3600 s\times \frac{1 \text{ h}}{3600 \text{ s}}×3600 s1 h​, a division by 360036003600, not a multiplication.
