    1. Three snacks, one multiplier . Foundational, 8 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 8 pts

    A trail mix recipe combines cashews, almonds, and pretzels in the ratio 2:3:42 : 3 : 42:3:4. A batch made for a hiking club contains 636363 pieces in all.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Write the number of cashews, almonds, and pretzels as multiples of one part kkk, using the ratio 2:3:42 : 3 : 42:3:4. Then use the total of 636363 pieces to find kkk, and report how many of each snack the batch contains.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Check your three counts two ways: that they add to the stated total, and that they still reduce to the ratio 2:3:42 : 3 : 42:3:4. Then explain in one sentence why every term of the ratio has to share the SAME multiplier kkk, rather than each getting its own.

      Carry your own answer forward Use the three counts you found in part A, whatever they came out to, for both checks below.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 2

      Every snack in this mix is some number of equal-sized parts, one count for cashews, one for almonds, one for pretzels, all built from parts of the same size. Name that one part and use the stated total to pin its size down before you report any count.

    2. Hint 2 of 2 · Part B

      A correct total does not by itself prove the ratio survived, and a correct ratio does not by itself prove the total is right. Run both checks on your three counts, then ask what would happen to the ratio if one count had come from a different multiplier than the other two.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    k=7k = 7k=7: the batch has 141414 cashews, 212121 almonds, and 282828 pretzels.

    Part B

    The counts 14+21+28=6314 + 21 + 28 = 6314+21+28=63 match the total, and 14:21:2814 : 21 : 2814:21:28 reduces to 2:3:42 : 3 : 42:3:4 by dividing every term by 777. The same kkk must scale every term because a ratio states how the quantities compare to EACH OTHER, not their separate sizes.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Write each count with the shared multiplier: cashews are 2k2k2k, almonds are 3k3k3k, and pretzels are 4k4k4k. The extra fact is the total, so add the three expressions and set the sum equal to 636363:

    2k+3k+4k=63.2k + 3k + 4k = 63.2k+3k+4k=63.

    Combine the like terms and solve:

    9k=63,k=7.9k = 63, \qquad k = 7.9k=63,k=7.

    Each part is worth 777 pieces. Multiply back through each term:

    2k=14,3k=21,4k=28.2k = 14, \qquad 3k = 21, \qquad 4k = 28.2k=14,3k=21,4k=28.

    So the batch has 141414 cashews, 212121 almonds, and 282828 pretzels.

    Part B

    Add the three counts to check the total:

    14+21+28=63,14 + 21 + 28 = 63,14+21+28=63,

    which matches. Divide every count by 777 to check the ratio:

    14:21:28=2:3:4.14 : 21 : 28 = 2 : 3 : 4.14:21:28=2:3:4.

    Both checks pass. The reason one shared kkk is required, rather than a separate multiplier for each term, is that the ratio is a statement about how the three quantities compare to one another. If cashews were scaled by one number and almonds by a different one, the new counts would no longer stand in the ratio 2:3:42 : 3 : 42:3:4 at all, so the very thing the problem asserted about them would stop being true.

    2. When three does not mean three . Foundational, 7 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Foundational 7 pts

    Claim: for a ratio a:ba : ba:b together with a stated total, the two actual amounts are simply the numbers aaa and bbb themselves, whatever the total happens to be.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Test the claim on a specific case. Two numbers are in the ratio 3:53 : 53:5 and their total is 404040. Use the common-multiplier method to find the two actual amounts.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Using the numbers from part A, refute the claim. State the specific counterexample plainly, and say exactly what it does, and does not, show.

      Carry your own answer forward Use the specific pair of numbers you computed in part A as your counterexample, whatever they came out to be.

      Construct a counterexample Give one specific case, and show it breaks the claim. 3 points

    Hints

    2. Hint 2 of 2 · Part B

      Line up what the claim predicted against what your own arithmetic in part A produced, number by number, and say which of the two, the ratio itself or the general claim about amounts, actually survives the comparison.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    k=5k = 5k=5: the two amounts are 151515 and 252525.

    Part B

    The pair 151515 and 252525 refutes the claim: with ratio 3:53 : 53:5 and total 404040, the actual amounts are 151515 and 252525, not 333 and 555. This shows the general claim is false; it does not show the ratio 3:53 : 53:5 is wrong, since 15:2515 : 2515:25 still reduces to it.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Write the two numbers as 3k3k3k and 5k5k5k, and set their sum equal to the total:

    3k+5k=40.3k + 5k = 40.3k+5k=40.

    Combine and solve:

    8k=40,k=5.8k = 40, \qquad k = 5.8k=40,k=5.

    Multiply back through each term:

    3k=15,5k=25.3k = 15, \qquad 5k = 25.3k=15,5k=25.

    So the two actual amounts are 151515 and 252525, and 15+25=4015 + 25 = 4015+25=40 checks.

    Part B

    One breaking case is enough to refute a claim about EVERY total. Part A supplies it: with ratio 3:53 : 53:5 and total 404040,

    3k=15,5k=25,3k = 15, \qquad 5k = 25,3k=15,5k=25,

    so the actual amounts are 151515 and 252525, while the claim asserted they would be 333 and 555. Since 15≠315 \neq 315=3 and 25≠525 \neq 525=5, the claim fails on this case, and one failing case is all a universal claim needs to be refuted.

    Be precise about the scope. The counterexample kills the claim that the ratio numbers ARE the amounts; it does not touch the ratio itself, since 15:2515 : 2515:25 still reduces to 3:53 : 53:5 by dividing both by 555. The only total for which the claim would hold is a+ba + ba+b itself, the one case where k=1k = 1k=1.

    In one line

    With ratio 3:53 : 53:5 and total 404040, the multiplier method gives k=5k = 5k=5 and actual amounts 151515 and 252525, not 333 and 555, so the pair 15,2515, 2515,25 refutes the claim. The ratio itself is untouched, since 15:2515 : 2515:25 still reduces to 3:53 : 53:5; only the claim that the ratio's own numbers ARE the amounts is false, and it fails for every total except a+ba + ba+b.

    3. Splitting a trivia prize by seniority . Application, 10 points. Question 3 of 5.

    A trivia contest team splits its winnings between its two members in the ratio 5:35 : 35:3, senior member to junior member. The senior member's share is 484848 dollars more than the junior member's.

    1. A

    The answer

    Part A

    Pop : rock : jazz =9:12:10= 9 : 12 : 10=9:12:10.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The shared quantity is rock, appearing as 444 in the first ratio and 666 in the second, so scale each ratio until rock reaches their least common multiple, 121212.

    Scale the first ratio, pop to rock, by 333:

    pop:rock=3:4=9:12.\text{pop} : \text{rock} = 3 : 4 = 9 : 12.pop:rock=3:4=9:12.

    rock:jazz=6:5=12:10.\text{rock} : \text{jazz} = 6 : 5 = 12 : 10.rock:jazz=6:5=12:10.

    Both now agree that rock is 121212, so line them up:

    pop:rock:jazz=9:12:10.\text{pop} : \text{rock} : \text{jazz} = 9 : 12 : 10.pop:rock:jazz=9:12:10.

    Part B

    31k=217,k=7.31k = 217, \qquad k = 7.31k=217,k=7.

    Multiply back through the jazz term:

    10k=70.10k = 70.10k=70.

    So there are 707070 jazz songs, alongside 9k=639k = 639k=63 pop songs and 12k=8412k = 8412k=84 rock songs, and 63+84+70=21763 + 84 + 70 = 21763+84+70=217 checks.

    Part C
