  1. Problem 1 The print run

    A printer finishes one print run in 404040 minutes at a steady rate. What is its rate in print runs per hour?

    1. Hint 1

      A rate is the fraction of a print run finished in one unit of time, and the unit asked for here is an hour.

    2. Hint 2

      Write 404040 minutes as a fraction of an hour, then take the reciprocal of that time.

    Answer

    32\frac3223​ print runs per hour, or 1.51.51.5 print runs per hour.

    Full solution

    Forty minutes is 4060=23\frac{40}{60}=\frac236040​=32​ of an hour, so the completion time is T=23T=\frac23T=32​ hour.

    A job finished in time TTT is done at the rate 1T\frac1TT1​ per unit of time.

    Here the rate is

    12/3=32\frac{1}{2/3}=\frac322/31​=23​

    print runs per hour.

    As a check, in one hour the printer finishes one run in the first 404040 minutes and half of another run in the remaining 202020 minutes, which is 1.51.51.5 runs.

    Answer

    32\frac3223​ print runs per hour, or 1.51.51.5 print runs per hour.

    Key idea

    Before turning a completion time into the rate 1T\frac1TT1​, write the time in the unit the rate is asked for.

  2. Problem 2 The road markers

    A cart moves at a steady speed from the 212121 mile marker to the 454545 mile marker in 1.51.51.5 hours. What is its speed in miles per hour?

    1. Hint 1

      The distance traveled is the change in marker reading.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide that distance by the elapsed time.

    Answer

    161616 miles per hour.

    Full solution

    The traveled distance is 45−21=2445-21=2445−21=24 miles.

    The speed is

    r=241.5r=\frac{24}{1.5}r=1.524​ r=16r=16r=16

    in miles per hour.

    At this speed, 16(1.5)=2416(1.5)=2416(1.5)=24 miles, matching the change in marker reading.

    Answer

    161616 miles per hour.

    Key idea

    For travel in one direction along a route, the distance covered is the difference between the position readings, not the final reading alone.

  3. Problem 3 The replaced helper

    Workers A and B complete 920\frac9{20}209​ of a job per hour together. A works at 15\frac1551​ of a job per hour. Worker C replaces A and works at 320\frac3{20}203​ of a job per hour. All rates are steady, with no duplicated work or interference. Find the combined rate of the new team, B and C.

    1. Hint 1

      First recover the contribution of B from the original team.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the rate of A from the original combined rate, then add the rate of C.

    Answer

    25\frac2552​ of a job per hour, or 0.40.40.4 of a job per hour.

    Full solution

    The rates of A and B add to the team rate, so the rate of B is what remains after removing A, whose rate is 15=420\frac15=\frac4{20}51​=204​.

    920−420=520\frac9{20}-\frac4{20}=\frac5{20}209​−204​=205​

    So B works at 520=14\frac5{20}=\frac14205​=41​ of a job per hour.

    The new team is B and C, so its rate is the sum of their rates.

    520+320=820\frac5{20}+\frac3{20}=\frac8{20}205​+203​=208​

    This is 25\frac2552​ of a job per hour.

    Adding B back to A gives 15+14=920\frac15+\frac14=\frac9{20}51​+41​=209​, confirming the recovered rate.

    Answer

    25\frac2552​ of a job per hour, or 0.40.40.4 of a job per hour.

    Key idea

    When every member's rate but one is known, a team rate reveals the missing rate, which can then be added into a new team.

  4. Problem 4 The delayed helper

    Worker A can finish a job alone in 999 hours and worker B in 333 hours. A works alone for 333 hours, then B joins until the job is complete. Both work steadily without repeating work or interfering. Find the total time from A starting to completion.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the completed fraction before the team starts.

    2. Hint 2

      The remaining fraction is divided by the combined rate after B joins.

    Answer

    92\frac9229​ hours, or 4.54.54.5 hours, which is 444 hours 303030 minutes.

    Full solution

    A finishes the job in 999 hours, so A works at 19\frac1991​ of the job per hour and completes 3⋅19=133\cdot\frac19=\frac133⋅91​=31​ of the job in the first three hours, leaving 23\frac2332​.

    B works at 13=39\frac13=\frac3931​=93​ of the job per hour, so once B joins the rate is

    19+39=49\frac19+\frac39=\frac4991​+93​=94​

    of the job per hour.

    Dividing by 49\frac4994​ is multiplying by 94\frac9449​, so the remainder takes

    23⋅94=32\frac23\cdot\frac94=\frac3232​⋅49​=23​

    hours.

    The total is

    3+32=923+\frac32=\frac923+23​=29​

    hours.

    A works for 92\frac9229​ hours and completes 12\frac1221​ of the job.

    B works for 32\frac3223​ hours and completes 12\frac1221​, so the whole job is completed.

    Answer

    92\frac9229​ hours, or 4.54.54.5 hours, which is 444 hours 303030 minutes.

    Key idea

    When a team changes during a job, separate the time intervals and track the remaining work.

  5. Problem 5 The partly filled tank

    A tank is initially one-quarter full. A pipe could fill it from empty in 444 hours, and a drain could empty it from full in 101010 hours. Assume both rates are constant while water is present. With both open, how long until the tank becomes full?

    1. Hint 1

      The tank needs only its missing fraction, not a whole tank of additional water.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the drain rate from the pipe rate before dividing the missing fraction by the net rate.

    Answer

    555 hours.

    Full solution

    The pipe fills at 14\frac1441​ of a tank per hour and the drain empties at 110\frac1{10}101​ of a tank per hour.

    Over the common denominator 202020 these rates are 520\frac5{20}205​ and 220\frac2{20}202​, so the net filling rate is

    520−220=320\frac5{20}-\frac2{20}=\frac3{20}205​−202​=203​

    tank per hour.

    This is positive, so the level rises.

    The missing fraction is 34\frac3443​, and dividing by 320\frac3{20}203​ is multiplying by 203\frac{20}3320​, so

    T=34⋅203T=\frac34\cdot\frac{20}3T=43​⋅320​ T=5T=5T=5

    hours.

    In five hours the pipe adds 54\frac5445​ of a tank and the drain removes 510=12\frac5{10}=\frac12105​=21​ of a tank.

    Writing 12\frac1221​ as 24\frac2442​, the net gain is 54−24=34\frac54-\frac24=\frac3445​−42​=43​, and adding it to the initial one-quarter gives a full tank.

    Answer

    555 hours.

    Key idea

    A positive net filling rate acts on the fraction still missing from a tank.

  6. Problem 6 The recorded stop

    A cyclist rides 181818 miles at 121212 miles per hour, stops for half an hour, then rides 101010 miles uphill at 555 miles per hour. Find the average speed for the whole outing, including the stop, and compare it with the average speed while moving.

    1. Hint 1

      The distance total is the same for both averages, but the time total differs.

    2. Hint 2

      Find each riding time from its distance and speed, then include or exclude the stop as requested.

    Answer

    Including the stop: 777 miles per hour. While moving: 888 miles per hour. The whole-outing average is 111 mile per hour lower.

    Full solution

    The riding times are 1812=1.5\frac{18}{12}=1.51218​=1.5 hours and 105=2\frac{10}{5}=2510​=2 hours.

    The total distance is 18+10=2818+10=2818+10=28 miles, and the full elapsed time is 1.5+0.5+2=41.5+0.5+2=41.5+0.5+2=4 hours.

    The average including the stop is

    284=7\frac{28}{4}=7428​=7

    miles per hour.

    Excluding the stop, the moving time is 3.53.53.5 hours, so the average is 283.5=8\frac{28}{3.5}=83.528​=8 miles per hour.

    The whole-outing average is 111 mile per hour lower because the same distance is divided by a larger time.

    The moving average is below the mean of the two speeds, 8.58.58.5, because the cyclist spent more riding time at 555 miles per hour than at 121212; the stop pulls the whole-outing average lower still.

    Answer

    Including the stop: 777 miles per hour. While moving: 888 miles per hour. The whole-outing average is 111 mile per hour lower.

    Key idea

    The time interval named in an average-speed question determines whether stopped time belongs in the denominator.

  7. Problem 7 The missing work record

    Workers A and B work together for 333 hours, completing three-fifths of a job. B then leaves, and A finishes the rest in 666 more hours. Assume steady rates with no duplication or interference. Find how long each worker would take to complete the whole job alone.

    1. Hint 1

      The solo interval directly reveals the rate of A.

    2. Hint 2

      The first interval reveals the combined rate, from which the rate of B can be recovered.

    Answer

    A: 151515 hours. B: 152\frac{15}{2}215​ hours, or 7.57.57.5 hours, which is 777 hours 303030 minutes.

    Full solution

    After the first three hours, 1−35=251-\frac35=\frac251−53​=52​ of the job is left.

    A completes that 25\frac2552​ alone in six hours, so its rate is 25÷6=115\frac25\div6=\frac1{15}52​÷6=151​ of a job per hour.

    Its whole-job time is therefore fifteen hours.

    The pair completes 35\frac3553​ in three hours, so the combined rate is 35÷3=15\frac35\div3=\frac1553​÷3=51​ of a job per hour.

    Writing 15\frac1551​ as 315\frac3{15}153​ and subtracting A gives

    315−115=215\frac3{15}-\frac1{15}=\frac2{15}153​−151​=152​

    for B, whose solo time is the reciprocal, 152=7.5\frac{15}{2}=7.5215​=7.5 hours.

    In the actual schedule A works nine hours and completes 915=35\frac9{15}=\frac35159​=53​ of the job, and B works three hours and completes 615=25\frac6{15}=\frac25156​=52​, checking the total.

    Answer

    A: 151515 hours. B: 152\frac{15}{2}215​ hours, or 7.57.57.5 hours, which is 777 hours 303030 minutes.

    Key idea

    Work completed during different team arrangements can reveal individual rates.

  8. Problem 8 The open outlets

    A half-full tank has one inlet that could fill an empty tank in 444 hours and two outlets that each could empty a full tank in 888 hours. Assume constant rates while water is present. Kai claims the tank will eventually fill because the inlet is faster than either outlet separately. Is Kai correct? Describe what happens with all three open.

    1. Hint 1

      Both outlets remove water at the same time, so their effects combine.

    2. Hint 2

      Add the two outlet rates, then compare that total with the inlet rate.

    Answer

    No; the level stays half full.

    Full solution

    The total outlet rate is

    18+18=14\frac18+\frac18=\frac1481​+81​=41​

    tank per hour, equal to the inlet rate.

    The net rate is

    14−14=0\frac14-\frac14=041​−41​=0

    Therefore the amount of water does not change.

    The tank stays half full rather than filling, so Kai is incorrect.

    Answer

    No; the level stays half full.

    Key idea

    An inlet must exceed the combined outlet rate for the water level to rise.

  9. Problem 9 The doubled schedule

    A trip consists of ttt hours at 202020 miles per hour and then 2t2t2t hours at 505050 miles per hour, without stops, where t>0t>0t>0. Dana claims that doubling both travel times, at the same two speeds, leaves the average speed unchanged. Is this true? Find the average before and after the change.

    1. Hint 1

      Doubling both times scales both the total distance and total time.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the average as a ratio involving ttt, then repeat with 2t2t2t and 4t4t4t.

    Answer

    Yes; both averages are 404040 miles per hour.

    Full solution

    The original distance is 20t+50(2t)=120t20t+50(2t)=120t20t+50(2t)=120t miles, and the original time is 3t3t3t hours.

    Since t>0t>0t>0,

    120t3t=40\frac{120t}{3t}=403t120t​=40

    miles per hour.

    After the change, the distance is 240t240t240t miles and the time is 6t6t6t hours, giving 240t/(6t)=40240t/(6t)=40240t/(6t)=40 miles per hour again.

    Both totals doubled, so the ratio stays the same.

    Answer

    Yes; both averages are 404040 miles per hour.

    Key idea

    Scaling every travel time by the same positive factor preserves the average speed when the speeds stay fixed.

  10. Problem 10 The two assignments

    Two workers each have positive constant rates and can divide a job freely without duplicating work or slowing each other down. Ari says that assigning each worker exactly half the job must give the fastest completion, even if their rates differ. Decide whether the claim is true. Use workers with solo times of 222 hours and 666 hours to justify your decision.

    1. Hint 1

      Find how long each worker takes to finish the share assigned to it; the job ends only when both are done.

    2. Hint 2

      With equal halves, one worker sits idle while the other finishes. Try a split that keeps both workers busy until the end.

    Answer

    False. Equal halves take 333 hours; giving the 222-hour worker 34\frac3443​ and the 666-hour worker 14\frac1441​ takes 1.51.51.5 hours.

    Full solution

    With equal halves, the faster worker takes one hour and the slower takes three hours, so completion waits until three hours.

    If the faster worker receives three-quarters and the slower one-quarter, their times are

    3/41/2=1.5\frac{3/4}{1/2}=1.51/23/4​=1.5 1/41/6=1.5\frac{1/4}{1/6}=1.51/61/4​=1.5

    hours.

    Both finish in 1.51.51.5 hours, less than three.

    The combined rate is 1/2+1/6=2/31/2+1/6=2/31/2+1/6=2/3 of a job per hour, so 1.51.51.5 hours also matches the time obtained when both remain productive throughout.

    The equal-share claim is therefore false.

    Answer

    False. Equal halves take 333 hours; giving the 222-hour worker 34\frac3443​ and the 666-hour worker 14\frac1441​ takes 1.51.51.5 hours.

    Key idea

    To finish together at unequal rates, workers generally need unequal shares of a divisible job.
