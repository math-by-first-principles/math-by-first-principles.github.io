Conversion Factors

Intuition

In the Rates lesson you turned gallons per second into gallons per minute by scaling both parts of the rate together. That works, but it asks you to invent the right multiplier each time and to keep the units straight in your head. There is a cleaner tool that never changes: the conversion factor. It rests on one plain observation. Since 111 foot and 121212 inches are the same length, the fraction 12 in1 ft\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft12 in​ has a top and a bottom that measure the same thing, so it equals 111. Multiply any length by it and the amount cannot change, because you multiplied by 111, even though the number and the units used to describe it both change, from feet to inches. Setting up these factors so that the units you do not want cancel is called dimensional analysis.

Learning goals

  • Build a factor from two equal amounts, so it equals one
  • Orient the factor so the unwanted unit cancels
  • Chain several factors when one hop is not enough
  • Convert a rate with two factors, one per unit
  • Raise the factor to a power for area and volume

A conversion factor is the number one in disguise

Here is the idea before its name. Since 111 foot is 121212 inches, multiplying by 12 in1 ft\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft12 in​ turns a length in feet into the same length in inches:

3 ft×12 in1 ft=3×12 in=36 in.3 \text{ ft} \times \frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}} = 3 \times 12 \text{ in} = 36 \text{ in}.3 ft×1 ft12 in​=3×12 in=36 in.

The length itself has not changed: 333 feet and 363636 inches are the same distance. What changed is the number and the unit used to describe it, together. A fraction like 12 in1 ft\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft12 in​, built from two equal amounts, is called a conversion factor.

Every conversion in this lesson rests on an equality between two amounts. A foot is twelve inches. A kilometer is a thousand meters. An hour is sixty minutes. Each such fact, read as an equation, lets you build a fraction that equals exactly 111, and that fraction is a conversion factor.

Take 1 ft=12 in1 \text{ ft} = 12 \text{ in}1 ft=12 in again. Because the two sides are equal, you may write the ratio of one to the other, and a ratio of equal quantities is 111:

12 in1 ft=1and1 ft12 in=1.\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}} = 1 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{1 \text{ ft}}{12 \text{ in}} = 1.1 ft12 in​=1and12 in1 ft​=1.

Both fractions equal 111; they are the same fact written two ways. Which one you reach for depends on the unit you want to cancel. First, though, here is why multiplying by such a factor is always allowed.

Why multiplying by a conversion factor keeps the amount the same#

The statement 12 in=1 ft12 \text{ in} = 1 \text{ ft}12 in=1 ft says that twelve inches and one foot are two names for the very same length. Divide both sides of that equation by 1 ft1 \text{ ft}1 ft, and the right side becomes 111:

12 in1 ft=1 ft1 ft=1.\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}} = \frac{1 \text{ ft}}{1 \text{ ft}} = 1.1 ft12 in​=1 ft1 ft​=1.

So the conversion factor is exactly 111, because its top and bottom name the same length. Multiplying any quantity by 111 leaves its actual size unchanged; the number and the unit you use to describe it change together, the way 333 feet and 363636 inches both describe the same distance, but the distance itself never moves.

The units cancel the same way numbers do. Just as 55=1\frac{5}{5} = 155​=1, a shared factor of ft\text{ft}ft cancels from a numerator and a denominator. In 3 ft×12 in1 ft3 \text{ ft} \times \frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}3 ft×1 ft12 in​ the ft\text{ft}ft on top of the first quantity meets the ft\text{ft}ft on the bottom of the factor. Those two form ftft=1\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{ft}} = 1ftft​=1 and disappear, leaving 3×12=363 \times 12 = 363×12=36 carrying the unit in\text{in}in. That is why lining up the factor so the unwanted unit sits opposite itself is the entire technique.

∎

Writing a factor both ways and choosing the orientation

A conversion factor comes in two orientations, and only one of them cancels the unit you are trying to remove. The rule is short: put the unwanted unit on the opposite side of the fraction from where it sits now. A plain measurement like 7 ft7 \text{ ft}7 ft carries its unit on top, so the factor needs that unit on the bottom; then the two line up as ftft\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{ft}}ftft​ and cancel. Pick the wrong orientation and nothing cancels, which is your signal to flip the factor over.

Worked example 1 Convert 777 feet to inches

Start with the quantity 7 ft7 \text{ ft}7 ft and the fact 1 ft=12 in1 \text{ ft} = 12 \text{ in}1 ft=12 in. You want feet to disappear and inches to appear, so choose the orientation with feet in the denominator, 12 in1 ft\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft12 in​. Multiply, and cancel the ft\text{ft}ft that shows up on top and bottom:

7 ft×12 in1 ft=7×12 in=84 in.7 \text{ ft} \times \frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}} = 7 \times 12 \text{ in} = 84 \text{ in}.7 ft×1 ft12 in​=7×12 in=84 in.

The foot cancels because ftft=1\frac{\text{ft}}{\text{ft}} = 1ftft​=1, leaving inches, so 777 feet is 848484 inches. Had you picked 1 ft12 in\frac{1 \text{ ft}}{12 \text{ in}}12 in1 ft​ instead, nothing would cancel and you would be left with ft2/in\text{ft}^2/\text{in}ft2/in, an unwanted unit that is not the inches you asked for, the built-in warning that the factor is upside down.

Chaining several factors

When no single factor connects your starting unit to your target unit, chain several together. Each factor still equals 111, so multiplying by all of them at once is still multiplying by 111, and the amount is preserved through the whole chain. Arrange the factors so that every intermediate unit appears once on top and once on the bottom; each intermediate unit then cancels, leaving the next unit in line.

Worked example 2 How many seconds are in one day?

No single conversion factor turns days straight into seconds, but a chain of familiar ones does the job. Use 1 day=24 h1 \text{ day} = 24 \text{ h}1 day=24 h, then 1 h=60 min1 \text{ h} = 60 \text{ min}1 h=60 min, then 1 min=60 s1 \text{ min} = 60 \text{ s}1 min=60 s, and line the factors up so each unwanted unit cancels the one before it:

1 day×24 h1 day×60 min1 h×60 s1 min.1 \text{ day} \times \frac{24 \text{ h}}{1 \text{ day}} \times \frac{60 \text{ min}}{1 \text{ h}} \times \frac{60 \text{ s}}{1 \text{ min}}.1 day×1 day24 h​×1 h60 min​×1 min60 s​.

Read the units down the line: day\text{day}day cancels the starting day, and h\text{h}h cancels between the first and second factors. Then min\text{min}min cancels between the second and third, leaving only s\text{s}s. Now multiply the numbers:

24×60×60=86,400 s.24 \times 60 \times 60 = 86{,}400 \text{ s}.24×60×60=86,400 s.

So a day holds 86,40086{,}40086,400 seconds. The chain never needed a day-to-second constant; each factor was one you already knew, and the intermediate units cancelled on their own.

Converting rates

A rate such as miles per hour is a fraction with a unit above and a unit below, so it has two units to convert, not one. Handle each with its own factor: one factor cancels the top unit and installs the new one, and a second factor does the same for the bottom unit. Because a bottom unit sits in the denominator, its factor goes in the orientation that cancels a denominator, which usually means flipping it relative to the top factor.

Worked example 3 Convert 303030 miles per hour to feet per second

A rate carries two units, one on top and one on the bottom. Converting a rate therefore takes two factors: one to fix the distance unit and one to fix the time unit. Write the speed as a fraction and attach both factors, using 1 mi=5280 ft1 \text{ mi} = 5280 \text{ ft}1 mi=5280 ft and 1 h=3600 s1 \text{ h} = 3600 \text{ s}1 h=3600 s:

30 mi1 h×5280 ft1 mi×1 h3600 s.\frac{30 \text{ mi}}{1 \text{ h}} \times \frac{5280 \text{ ft}}{1 \text{ mi}} \times \frac{1 \text{ h}}{3600 \text{ s}}.1 h30 mi​×1 mi5280 ft​×3600 s1 h​.

The mi\text{mi}mi on top cancels the mi\text{mi}mi in the second factor’s denominator, and the h\text{h}h in the first denominator cancels the h\text{h}h in the third numerator. What remains is ft\text{ft}ft over s\text{s}s, exactly the unit you want. Multiply the numbers, remembering the 360036003600 divides:

30×52803600=158,4003600=44 ft/s.\frac{30 \times 5280}{3600} = \frac{158{,}400}{3600} = 44 \text{ ft/s}.360030×5280​=3600158,400​=44 ft/s.

So 303030 miles per hour is 444444 feet per second. Notice the time factor is flipped compared with the distance factor: hours needed to leave the denominator, so hours went on top of its factor. Miles, in contrast, needed to leave the numerator, so miles went on the bottom of its factor.

Squared and cubed units

Area and volume carry powers of a unit, like square feet or cubic centimeters, and a power changes how many times you must convert. A squared unit hides two lengths and a cubed unit hides three. The conversion factor therefore has to be applied once for each hidden length, and that repetition raises the factor to that power.

Worked example 4 Convert 333 square feet to square inches

It is tempting to multiply by 12 in1 ft\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft12 in​ just once, but that leaves a stray unit and the wrong number. Square feet means feet multiplied by feet, 1 ft2=1 ft×1 ft1 \text{ ft}^2 = 1 \text{ ft} \times 1 \text{ ft}1 ft2=1 ft×1 ft, so there are two lengths to convert and the factor must appear twice:

3 ft2×12 in1 ft×12 in1 ft=3 ft2×(12 in1 ft)2.3 \text{ ft}^2 \times \frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}} \times \frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}} = 3 \text{ ft}^2 \times \left(\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}\right)^2.3 ft2×1 ft12 in​×1 ft12 in​=3 ft2×(1 ft12 in​)2.

Squaring the factor squares both the number and the unit, giving 144 in21 ft2\frac{144 \text{ in}^2}{1 \text{ ft}^2}1 ft2144 in2​, and the ft2\text{ft}^2ft2 now cancels the ft2\text{ft}^2ft2 you started with:

3 ft2×144 in21 ft2=3×144=432 in2.3 \text{ ft}^2 \times \frac{144 \text{ in}^2}{1 \text{ ft}^2} = 3 \times 144 = 432 \text{ in}^2.3 ft2×1 ft2144 in2​=3×144=432 in2.

So 333 square feet is 432432432 square inches. The factor is still equal to 111, since 12=11^2 = 112=1, so squaring it changes nothing about the amount, only how many times the unit gets converted.

Worked example 5 Convert 222 cubic yards to cubic feet

Volume raises the same idea one step higher. A cubic yard is three lengths multiplied together, 1 yd3=1 yd×1 yd×1 yd1 \text{ yd}^3 = 1 \text{ yd} \times 1 \text{ yd} \times 1 \text{ yd}1 yd3=1 yd×1 yd×1 yd, so the yard-to-foot factor must appear three times, which cubes it. With 1 yd=3 ft1 \text{ yd} = 3 \text{ ft}1 yd=3 ft,

2 yd3×(3 ft1 yd)3=2 yd3×27 ft31 yd3.2 \text{ yd}^3 \times \left(\frac{3 \text{ ft}}{1 \text{ yd}}\right)^3 = 2 \text{ yd}^3 \times \frac{27 \text{ ft}^3}{1 \text{ yd}^3}.2 yd3×(1 yd3 ft​)3=2 yd3×1 yd327 ft3​.

Cubing the factor turns 333 into 33=273^3 = 2733=27 and turns the unit into ft3\text{ft}^3ft3, which cancels the yd3\text{yd}^3yd3 you began with:

2×27=54 ft3.2 \times 27 = 54 \text{ ft}^3.2×27=54 ft3.

So 222 cubic yards is 545454 cubic feet. The rule generalizes cleanly: a squared unit needs the factor squared, and a cubed unit needs it cubed, because that is how many separate lengths are hidden inside the unit.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Writing the factor upside down. If the unwanted unit does not cancel, the factor is flipped. To convert 606060 inches to feet, inches must be in the denominator (1 ft12 in\frac{1 \text{ ft}}{12 \text{ in}}12 in1 ft​); using 12 in1 ft\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft12 in​ leaves in2/ft\text{in}^2/\text{ft}in2/ft and a wrong number. Let the units, not guesswork, choose the orientation.
  • Converting a squared or cubed unit only once. Square feet to square inches needs the factor twice (122=14412^2 = 144122=144), and cubic units need it three times (123=172812^3 = 1728123=1728). Multiplying by 121212 a single time is the most common area-and-volume error.
  • Putting a rate’s time factor in the wrong orientation. When the unit you are removing sits in the denominator, its factor needs that unit in the numerator to cancel it. Get this backward and you multiply where you should divide, or divide where you should multiply. Converting 303030 mph to ft/s needs 1 h3600 s\frac{1 \text{ h}}{3600 \text{ s}}3600 s1 h​, not 3600 s1 h\frac{3600 \text{ s}}{1 \text{ h}}1 h3600 s​, because hours sit in the denominator of the starting rate.
  • Dropping the units and trusting the numbers. The units are the check: keep them attached through every step. If a strange unit like ft2/in\text{ft}^2/\text{in}ft2/in survives, the setup is wrong. But reaching the target unit only shows the setup is consistent, not that the factor was true, so also confirm each factor came from a real equality.
  • Using a ratio whose two sides are not truly equal. 12 in1 ft\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft12 in​ works because a foot really is twelve inches. A made-up ratio like 10 in1 ft\frac{10 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft10 in​ is not 111, so it corrupts the amount instead of preserving it.

Key takeaways

  • A conversion factor is a fraction of two equal amounts (like 12 in1 ft\frac{12 \text{ in}}{1 \text{ ft}}1 ft12 in​), so it equals 111; multiplying by it changes a quantity’s units but not how much it is. Every factor has two orientations, and units cancel like numbers, so choose the orientation that puts the unwanted unit opposite itself.
  • Chain factors when one hop is not enough, and use two factors for a rate, one per unit; arrange each so every unwanted unit cancels, and the whole chain or pair is still a product of 111s.
  • Squared and cubed units need the factor raised to that power: 1 ft2=144 in21 \text{ ft}^2 = 144 \text{ in}^21 ft2=144 in2 and 1 ft3=1728 in31 \text{ ft}^3 = 1728 \text{ in}^31 ft3=1728 in3, because area hides two lengths and volume hides three.

A bit of history (optional)

A foot was once an actual foot, and towns disagreed about whose. A bolt of cloth measured in one market could come out a different length in the next, and traders carried thick books of local conversions to keep track.

In the 1790s, French scientists and lawmakers built a decimal system from a single base unit of length, the meter. Every larger or smaller length unit was placed a power of ten away from it: a thousand meters became a kilometer, a hundredth of a meter became a centimeter. The change spread gradually, but the goal was that a unit’s name would announce its size.

That is why converting between metric units, like kilometers and meters, usually costs only a shift of a decimal point, while miles still carry the 5280 they inherited from older units. The convenience is real, but it does not make a conversion factor any more true: a metric factor equals 111 for the same reason a customary one does, because its top and bottom name the same length.
