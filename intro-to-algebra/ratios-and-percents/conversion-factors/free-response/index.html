  1. Problem 1 The matching measures

    For a model kit, one rail is exactly four links long. A proposed conversion factor has 121212 links in its numerator. What number of rails belongs in its denominator so that the factor equals one?

    1. Hint 1

      The numerator and denominator must describe equal lengths.

    2. Hint 2

      Group the twelve links into groups matching one rail each.

    Answer

    333 rails.

    Full solution

    Each rail contains four links, so the length of twelve links in rails is

    12÷4=312\div4=312÷4=3

    Thus twelve links and three rails are equal lengths.

    The factor is twelve links divided by three rails.

    Its top and bottom name the same length, so the factor equals one.

    Answer

    333 rails.

    Key idea

    A conversion factor equals one when its numerator and denominator represent the same amount.

  2. Problem 2 The label multiplier

    A label shows how many 555-centimeter pieces make up a length of xxx meters. Write that number as a single multiple of xxx. Use 111 meter equal to 100100100 centimeters.

    1. Hint 1

      One piece and five centimeters are equal lengths, so they can form a conversion factor too.

    2. Hint 2

      Build a factor with centimeters on top, then one with pieces on top and centimeters below.

    Answer

    20x20x20x pieces.

    Full solution

    Meters must cancel, so the first factor has centimeters on top and meters below, and xxx meters becomes 100x100x100x centimeters.

    Centimeters must cancel next, so the second factor has one piece on top and five centimeters below.

    It divides the number of centimeters by five:

    100x5=20x\frac{100x}{5}=20x5100x​=20x

    Together the two factors multiply xxx by 100100100 times one fifth, which is 202020.

    For example, one meter is one hundred centimeters, which makes twenty pieces, matching 20x20x20x when x=1x=1x=1.

    Answer

    20x20x20x pieces.

    Key idea

    Converting and then regrouping is a chain of two factors, and the overall multiplier is their product.

  3. Problem 3 The wall panels

    Two wall panels each cover 2.52.52.5 square feet. The gap between them is a strip of 108108108 square inches. Find the total area of the two panels and the strip, in square feet. Use 111 foot equal to 121212 inches.

    1. Hint 1

      Areas can be added only once they are measured in the same square unit.

    2. Hint 2

      A square inch hides two lengths, so the inch-to-foot factor is used twice.

    3. Hint 3

      Square inches must cancel, so the length factor carries inches on the bottom.

    Answer

    5.755.755.75 square feet, or 5345\tfrac34543​ square feet.

    Full solution

    The strip must change from square inches to square feet.

    Inches must cancel, so the length factor is one foot per twelve inches, and a square unit needs it twice: one square foot is 12⋅12=14412\cdot12=14412⋅12=144 square inches.

    The strip is therefore

    108÷144=0.75108\div144=0.75108÷144=0.75

    square feet.

    Dividing by 121212 only once would give 999 square feet, the error of converting a square unit once.

    The total area is

    2(2.5)+0.75=5.752(2.5)+0.75=5.752(2.5)+0.75=5.75

    square feet.

    As a check in square inches, each panel is 2.5⋅144=3602.5\cdot144=3602.5⋅144=360 square inches, so the total is 2(360)+108=8282(360)+108=8282(360)+108=828 square inches, and 828÷144=5.75828\div144=5.75828÷144=5.75 square feet.

    Answer

    5.755.755.75 square feet, or 5345\tfrac34543​ square feet.

    Key idea

    Areas can be added once they share one square unit, and converting to a larger square unit divides the number by the square of the length ratio, here 121212 squared, or 144144144.

  4. Problem 4 The supply cartons

    A craft center receives 333 cartons of ribbon. Each carton holds 444 rolls, each roll is 222 yards long, one yard is 333 feet, and one foot is 121212 inches. It sets aside 484848 inches. How many inches remain? Explain the unit cancellations in your conversion chain.

    1. Hint 1

      Follow the quantities from cartons to rolls to yards to feet to inches.

    2. Hint 2

      Each conversion factor puts the current unit below and the next unit above.

    Answer

    816816816 inches.

    Full solution

    Use factors of four rolls per carton, two yards per roll, three feet per yard, and twelve inches per foot.

    Cartons, rolls, yards, and feet cancel successively, leaving inches.

    The numerical product is

    3⋅4⋅2⋅3⋅12=8643\cdot4\cdot2\cdot3\cdot12=8643⋅4⋅2⋅3⋅12=864

    After setting aside the ribbon,

    864−48=816864-48=816864−48=816

    So 816816816 inches remain.

    As a check, grouping the same product differently, the 333 cartons hold 121212 rolls, each roll is 2⋅3⋅12=722\cdot3\cdot12=722⋅3⋅12=72 inches, and 12⋅72=86412\cdot72=86412⋅72=864 inches, the same delivery total.

    Answer

    816816816 inches.

    Key idea

    A chain of true factors carries a quantity through its intermediate units, and amounts can be combined once they share a unit.

  5. Problem 5 The pair of dispensers

    Dispenser A releases 2.72.72.7 liters per hour. Dispenser B releases 0.80.80.8 milliliters per second. Use 111 liter equal to 100010001000 milliliters, 111 hour equal to 606060 minutes, and 111 minute equal to 606060 seconds. Which dispenser releases more, and by how many milliliters per minute?

    1. Hint 1

      Two rates can be compared directly only when they use the same volume unit and the same time unit.

    2. Hint 2

      Convert each rate to milliliters per minute. For A, change liters to milliliters as well as hours to minutes.

    3. Hint 3

      A time unit in the denominator of a rate cancels against a factor that carries that unit on top.

    Answer

    Dispenser B, by 333 milliliters per minute.

    Full solution

    For A, the factor of one thousand milliliters per liter cancels liters on top.

    Hours sit in the denominator, so the time factor is one hour per sixty minutes, with hours on top.

    The rate is

    2.7(1000)60=45\frac{2.7(1000)}{60}=45602.7(1000)​=45

    milliliters per minute.

    For B, seconds sit in the denominator, so the time factor is sixty seconds per minute, with seconds on top.

    The rate is

    0.8(60)=480.8(60)=480.8(60)=48

    milliliters per minute.

    So Dispenser B releases more, by 48−45=348-45=348−45=3 milliliters per minute.

    As a check in milliliters per second, A releases 45÷60=0.7545\div60=0.7545÷60=0.75, which is less than B's 0.80.80.8.

    Answer

    Dispenser B, by 333 milliliters per minute.

    Key idea

    Rates become directly comparable after both numerator and denominator units agree.

  6. Problem 6 The water bottles

    A tank holds 0.0480.0480.048 cubic meters of water. All of it is poured into bottles that each hold 1.51.51.5 liters. How many bottles does the water fill exactly? Use 111 meter equal to 100100100 centimeters and 111 liter equal to 100010001000 cubic centimeters.

    1. Hint 1

      A cubic meter is one meter long, one meter wide and one meter high, so it hides three lengths.

    2. Hint 2

      Convert the volume to cubic centimeters, then to liters, before comparing it with one bottle.

    Answer

    323232 bottles.

    Full solution

    A cubic meter hides three lengths, so the meter-to-centimeter factor is used three times, which cubes it.

    One cubic meter is 1003=1,000,000100^3=1{,}000{,}0001003=1,000,000 cubic centimeters, so the tank holds

    0.048(1,000,000)=48,0000.048(1{,}000{,}000)=48{,}0000.048(1,000,000)=48,000

    cubic centimeters.

    The factor of one liter per thousand cubic centimeters has cubic centimeters below, so they cancel, and the volume is

    48,000÷1000=4848{,}000\div1000=4848,000÷1000=48

    liters.

    The number of bottles is

    48÷1.5=3248\div1.5=3248÷1.5=32

    As a check, 323232 bottles of 1.51.51.5 liters hold 484848 liters, which is 48,00048{,}00048,000 cubic centimeters, the tank's volume.

    Answer

    323232 bottles.

    Key idea

    A cubed length factor converts the volume, and further true factors can then carry it to the unit the containers use.

  7. Problem 7 The board game units

    A board game measures lengths in two made-up units, the track and the step. Its rulebook says that 555 square tracks cover the same area as 320320320 square steps. How many steps long is one track?

    1. Hint 1

      A square track is one track long and one track wide, so it hides two lengths.

    2. Hint 2

      First find how many square steps make up one square track.

    3. Hint 3

      Then find the positive number whose square is that count of square steps.

    Answer

    888 steps per track: one track is 888 steps long.

    Full solution

    Five square tracks equal 320320320 square steps, so one square track equals

    3205=64\frac{320}{5}=645320​=64

    square steps.

    A square track is one track by one track, so converting it to square steps uses the length factor twice, which squares it.

    The number of steps in one track must therefore have a square of 646464.

    Since 82=648^2=6482=64 and a length is positive, one track is 888 steps long.

    As a check, one square track is 8⋅8=648\cdot8=648⋅8=64 square steps, so five square tracks are 5⋅64=3205\cdot64=3205⋅64=320 square steps, as the rulebook says.

    Answer

    888 steps per track: one track is 888 steps long.

    Key idea

    An area conversion uses the square of the length conversion, so a known area relationship reveals the length relationship.

  8. Problem 8 What canceling does not prove

    Nina converts a 3.53.53.5 kilogram bag of flour to grams. She multiplies by 100 g1 kg\frac{100\text{ g}}{1\text{ kg}}1 kg100 g​, the kilograms cancel, and her result of 350350350 carries grams, so she says the canceling proves her conversion is right. Explain what the canceling does show and what it does not, state the test a fraction must pass before it may be used as a conversion factor, and give the bag's mass in grams. Use 111 kilogram equal to 100010001000 grams.

    1. Hint 1

      Multiplying by a factor leaves an amount unchanged only when the factor itself equals one, and canceling alone does not arrange that.

    2. Hint 2

      Test her fraction the way the lesson tests any factor: do its top and bottom name the same amount?

    3. Hint 3

      Rebuild the factor from the given equality between kilograms and grams, then multiply the mass by it.

    Answer

    Canceling shows only that the factor is correctly oriented, not that it is true. A fraction may be used only when its top and bottom name equal amounts, and 100100100 grams is not 111 kilogram, so Nina's may not. The bag is 350035003500 grams.

    Full solution

    Canceling tracks the units alone.

    The kilograms attached to 3.53.53.5 kg meet the kilograms below Nina's fraction and disappear, so grams survive, which shows only that her fraction is correctly oriented.

    A conversion factor must also be true: its top and bottom must name the same amount, so that the fraction itself equals one.

    Hers do not, since 100100100 grams is a tenth of a kilogram, so her fraction equals one tenth.

    Multiplying by one tenth shrinks the mass instead of renaming it, which is why her 350350350 grams is a tenth of the true mass.

    Built from the given equality, the true factor is 1000 g1 kg\frac{1000\text{ g}}{1\text{ kg}}1 kg1000 g​, whose top and bottom are the same mass.

    It gives

    3.5(1000)=35003.5(1000)=35003.5(1000)=3500

    grams.

    As a check, 350035003500 grams is 3500÷1000=3.53500\div1000=3.53500÷1000=3.5 kilograms, the mass Nina started with, so only the unit changed.

    Answer

    Canceling shows only that the factor is correctly oriented, not that it is true. A fraction may be used only when its top and bottom name equal amounts, and 100100100 grams is not 111 kilogram, so Nina's may not. The bag is 350035003500 grams.

    Key idea

    Reaching the target unit shows only that a factor is correctly oriented; the amount survives only when the factor's top and bottom name equal amounts, so that it equals one.

  9. Problem 9 The printed capacity

    A label converts cubic yards to cubic feet by multiplying the numerical measure by 272727. Jo says converting cubic feet back to cubic yards also multiplies by 272727, since both conversions concern volume. Is Jo correct? Give a positive-volume example to justify your decision. Use 111 yard equal to 333 feet.

    1. Hint 1

      The direction of conversion matters as well as the power.

    2. Hint 2

      A reverse volume conversion uses the cube of the reciprocal length factor.

    Answer

    No; divide by 272727. For example, 818181 cubic feet is 333 cubic yards.

    Full solution

    One yard is three feet, so one cubic yard is 33=273^3=2733=27 cubic feet.

    Returning from cubic feet to cubic yards therefore uses numerical factor 1/271/271/27.

    For example,

    81⋅127=381\cdot\frac1{27}=381⋅271​=3

    Thus 818181 cubic feet is 333 cubic yards.

    Converting forward checks as 3(27)=813(27)=813(27)=81 cubic feet.

    Jo's rule would give 81⋅27=218781\cdot27=218781⋅27=2187 cubic yards, not 333, so the rule is false.

    Answer

    No; divide by 272727. For example, 818181 cubic feet is 333 cubic yards.

    Key idea

    Converting a cubed unit back uses the reciprocal of the whole cubed factor.

  10. Problem 10 The speed setup

    Dev converts 363636 kilometers per hour to meters per second. He multiplies the speed by the factor 1000 m1 km\frac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}}1 km1000 m​ and then by the factor 3600 s1 h\frac{3600\text{ s}}{1\text{ h}}1 h3600 s​. Explain what is wrong with his setup, and give the correct speed in meters per second. Use 111 kilometer equal to 100010001000 meters and 111 hour equal to 360036003600 seconds.

    1. Hint 1

      Follow each unit through the setup: an unwanted unit cancels only when it appears once on top and once on the bottom.

    2. Hint 2

      Check each factor in turn: which unit is it meant to remove, and where does that unit sit in the speed?

    3. Hint 3

      Once every unwanted unit cancels, multiply the numbers on top and divide by the numbers on the bottom.

    Answer

    His time factor 3600 s1 h\frac{3600\text{ s}}{1\text{ h}}1 h3600 s​ is upside down; it should be 1 h3600 s\frac{1\text{ h}}{3600\text{ s}}3600 s1 h​, and the correct speed is 101010 meters per second.

    Full solution

    Write the speed as 36 km1 h\frac{36\text{ km}}{1\text{ h}}1 h36 km​.

    Kilometers sit on its top and on the bottom of 1000 m1 km\frac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}}1 km1000 m​, so they cancel, and that factor is oriented correctly.

    Hours sit on the bottom of the speed and also on the bottom of 3600 s1 h\frac{3600\text{ s}}{1\text{ h}}1 h3600 s​, so they do not cancel.

    His product is left with meters times seconds on top and hours squared below, m⋅s/h2\text{m}\cdot\text{s}/\text{h}^2m⋅s/h2, which is not meters per second.

    So the time factor is upside down.

    Flipping it to 1 h3600 s\frac{1\text{ h}}{3600\text{ s}}3600 s1 h​ puts hours on top, so they cancel and leave meters over seconds.

    The correct speed is

    36(1000)3600=10\frac{36(1000)}{3600}=10360036(1000)​=10

    meters per second.

    As a check, 101010 meters per second for the 360036003600 seconds of one hour covers 36,00036{,}00036,000 meters, which is 363636 kilometers.

    Answer

    His time factor 3600 s1 h\frac{3600\text{ s}}{1\text{ h}}1 h3600 s​ is upside down; it should be 1 h3600 s\frac{1\text{ h}}{3600\text{ s}}3600 s1 h​, and the correct speed is 101010 meters per second.

    Key idea

    Following the units exposes an upside-down factor: the unit it should remove survives instead of canceling.
