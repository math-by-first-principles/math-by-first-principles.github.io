    1. The fraction that is secretly one . Foundational, 13 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 13 pts

    Throughout this question use the fact 1 gal=4 qt1 \text{ gal} = 4 \text{ qt}1 gal=4 qt. The last part steps back from that one fact to ask what makes ANY conversion factor, for any pair of units, work at all.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The starting quantity carries gal on top, so the factor needs gal on the bottom to cancel it, which fixes the orientation as 4 qt1 gal\dfrac{4 \text{ qt}}{1 \text{ gal}}1 gal4 qt​, not its flip.

    9 gal×4 qt1 gal=9×4 qt=36 qt.9 \text{ gal} \times \frac{4 \text{ qt}}{1 \text{ gal}} = 9 \times 4 \text{ qt} = 36 \text{ qt}.9 gal×1 gal4 qt​=9×4 qt=36 qt.

    The gal on top of the quantity and the gal on the bottom of the factor form galgal=1\frac{\text{gal}}{\text{gal}} = 1galgal​=1 and disappear, leaving qt.

    Part B

    This time the starting quantity carries qt on top, so the factor needs qt on the bottom, the OTHER orientation from part A: 1 gal4 qt\dfrac{1 \text{ gal}}{4 \text{ qt}}4 qt1 gal​.

    30 qt×1 gal4 qt=304 gal=7.5 gal.30 \text{ qt} \times \frac{1 \text{ gal}}{4 \text{ qt}} = \frac{30}{4} \text{ gal} = 7.5 \text{ gal}.30 qt×4 qt1 gal​=430​ gal=7.5 gal.

    A half gallon is no obstacle: gallons and quarts are measured, not counted, so a fractional amount is ordinary.

    Part C

    Work in letters, not the numbers 444 and 111 from parts A and B, since the claim covers every pair of units.

    Start from the given equality and divide both sides by a U1a\,U_1aU1​ (which is not zero):

    a U1=b U2⟹a U1a U1=b U2a U1⟹1=b U2a U1.a\,U_1 = b\,U_2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{a\,U_1}{a\,U_1} = \frac{b\,U_2}{a\,U_1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 1 = \frac{b\,U_2}{a\,U_1}.aU1​=bU2​⟹aU1​aU1​​=aU1​bU2​​⟹1=aU1​bU2​​.

    Nothing about aaa, bbb, U1U_1U1​, or U2U_2U2​ was special: the same division works for any true equality between two units of the same kind of quantity, so the fraction it produces is always exactly 111, never merely close to it.

    Now take any quantity c U1c\,U_1cU1​ and multiply by that fraction:

    c U1×b U2a U1=cba(U1U1)U2=cba U2,c\,U_1 \times \frac{b\,U_2}{a\,U_1} = \frac{cb}{a}\left(\frac{U_1}{U_1}\right)U_2 = \frac{cb}{a}\,U_2,cU1​×aU1​bU2​​=acb​(U1​U1​​)U2​=acb​U2​,

    using U1U1=1\frac{U_1}{U_1} = 1U1​U1​​=1, the same cancellation rule that removes a number from a fraction's top and bottom. The multiplicative identity says multiplying by 111 changes nothing, so the AMOUNT c U1c\,U_1cU1​ and the amount cba U2\frac{cb}{a}\,U_2acb​U2​ are the same amount, only carrying different labels; the number in front changed from ccc to cba\frac{cb}{a}acb​ precisely because the unit changed size, not because anything was added to or taken from the quantity itself.

    In one line

    999 gal is 363636 qt, using 4 qt1 gal\dfrac{4 \text{ qt}}{1 \text{ gal}}1 gal4 qt​; 303030 qt is 7.57.57.5 gal, using the flipped factor 1 gal4 qt\dfrac{1 \text{ gal}}{4 \text{ qt}}4 qt1 gal​.     Another way: Reach the identity by substitution instead of division

    Rather than dividing the equation, substitute directly. Since a U1=b U2a\,U_1 = b\,U_2aU1​=bU2​ names the same amount twice, the fraction b U2a U1\dfrac{b\,U_2}{a\,U_1}aU1​bU2​​ can be rewritten by replacing its numerator with the amount it equals: b U2a U1=a U1a U1=1\dfrac{b\,U_2}{a\,U_1} = \dfrac{a\,U_1}{a\,U_1} = 1aU1​bU2​​=aU1​aU1​​=1. The conclusion is identical; only the route there differs, and this route never performs a division step at all.

    When it is worth it When a division step feels like it is hiding what is really going on. Substitution shows the 111 appearing because the top and bottom are already, literally, the same amount, before any arithmetic happens.
