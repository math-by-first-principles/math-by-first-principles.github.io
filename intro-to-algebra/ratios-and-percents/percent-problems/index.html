Percent Problems

Intuition

You have solved percent problems before by setting up a proportion and cross-multiplying. Algebra offers something simpler and stronger. Notice that every percent question is the same single sentence, part = rate times whole, where the rate is the percent written as a decimal. Once you see that, you no longer need a separate recipe for each kind of question: name the quantity you do not know with a letter, write that one relationship, and solve for it. The same relationship finds a part, a whole, or a percent, and it also marks a price up or down, undoes a change, stacks several changes in a row, and computes interest.

Learning goals

  • Solve p=rwp = rwp=rw for whichever of the three is unknown
  • Multiply by 1+r1 + r1+r to raise and 1−r1 - r1−r to cut
  • Divide by the factor to reverse a percent change
  • Stack successive changes by multiplying their factors, since equal rises and falls do not cancel
  • Apply I=PrtI = PrtI=Prt, with the principal fixed

The percent relationship as an equation

A percent compares a part to a whole. For example, 30%30\%30% of 808080 dollars is 0.30×80=240.30 \times 80 = 240.30×80=24 dollars: here 242424 is the part, 0.300.300.30 is the rate, and 808080 is the whole. From the Percent lesson, a percent is a count out of 100100100, so a rate like 30%30\%30% is the decimal 30100=0.30\frac{30}{100} = 0.3010030​=0.30. Because “of” with a multiplier means multiply, taking rrr of a whole is exactly rrr times the whole. That gives the one relationship this whole lesson runs on:

part=rate×whole,p=r w.\text{part} = \text{rate} \times \text{whole}, \qquad p = r\,w.part=rate×whole,p=rw.

Written as the percent proportion you already know, the same fact is

pw=percent number100=r,\frac{p}{w} = \frac{\text{percent number}}{100} = r,wp​=100percent number​=r,

where the percent number is the plain number in front of the percent sign, 303030 for 30%30\%30%.

Dividing the part by the whole gives the rate; multiplying the rate by the whole gives the part. These are two views of a single equation. Three quantities live inside it, the part ppp, the rate rrr, and the whole www, and any percent problem hands you two of them and hides the third. Whichever quantity is missing, substitute the other two and use one multiplication or division to find it.

Solving for each of the three quantities

The three classic percent tasks are not three separate methods. They are the one equation p=r wp = r\,wp=rw solved for its three different letters.

Finding the part

When you know the rate and the whole, the part is a direct multiplication. Convert the percent to the decimal rate first, then multiply.

Worked example 1 Find a part: 15%15\%15% of 340340340

The rate and whole are given, and the part is unknown. Write the percent as a decimal, 15%=0.1515\% = 0.1515%=0.15, and substitute into p=r wp = r\,wp=rw:

p=0.15×340.p = 0.15 \times 340.p=0.15×340.

Carry out the multiplication:

p=51.p = 51.p=51.

So 15%15\%15% of 340340340 is 515151. As a check, 10%10\%10% of 340340340 is 343434 and 5%5\%5% is half of that, 171717, and 34+17=5134 + 17 = 5134+17=51.

Finding the whole

When you know the part and the rate, the whole sits multiplied by rrr. Solve r w=pr\,w = prw=p by dividing both sides by the rate, which gives w=prw = \dfrac{p}{r}w=rp​. That division needs the rate to be more than 000: a 0%0\%0% rate could not have produced any part other than 000, so there would be no single whole to divide back to.

Worked example 2 Find the whole from a part

A 454545 dollar deposit is 15%15\%15% of a bike’s price. What is the full price?

The part is 454545 and the rate is 15%=0.1515\% = 0.1515%=0.15; the whole price www is unknown. Substitute into p=r wp = r\,wp=rw:

45=0.15 w.45 = 0.15\,w.45=0.15w.

Divide both sides by the rate to isolate www:

w=450.15=300.w = \frac{45}{0.15} = 300.w=0.1545​=300.

The bike costs 300300300 dollars. Run it forward to check: 15%15\%15% of 300300300 is 0.15×300=450.15 \times 300 = 450.15×300=45, the deposit you were told. Notice the whole here is much larger than the part, which matches the rate: whenever the rate is below 100%100\%100%, the whole must be larger than the part.

Finding the rate

When you know the part and the whole, the rate is what multiplies www to reach ppp. Solve r w=pr\,w = prw=p by dividing by the whole, giving r=pwr = \dfrac{p}{w}r=wp​, then turn that decimal into a percent. That division needs the whole to be more than 000: a whole of 000 has nothing to take a part of.

Worked example 3 Find the percent one number is of another

A student answered 343434 of 404040 questions correctly. What percent is that?

The part is 343434 and the whole is 404040; the rate is unknown. From p=r wp = r\,wp=rw,

34=r×40.34 = r \times 40.34=r×40.

Divide both sides by the whole:

r=3440=0.85.r = \frac{34}{40} = 0.85.r=4034​=0.85.

Convert the decimal rate to a percent by multiplying by 100100100, so r=85%r = 85\%r=85%. The student scored 85%85\%85%. The whole is the number after “of,” here the 404040 questions, so it goes in the denominator.

Answer choices

0.30 w=45  ⟹  w=450.30=1500.30\,w = 45 \implies w = \frac{45}{0.30} = 1500.30w=45⟹w=0.3045​=150

Dividing recovers the whole. Multiplying 454545 by 0.300.300.30 answers a different question and gives the far-too-small 13.513.513.5.

Check your understanding

A basket has 999 red apples out of 363636 apples in total. What percent of the apples are red?

Answer choices

The part is 999 and the whole is 363636, so divide the part by the whole to find the rate.

r=936=0.25=25%r = \frac{9}{36} = 0.25 = 25\%r=369​=0.25=25%

Dividing the whole by the part instead, 36/9=436/9 = 436/9=4, gives an unrelated ratio, not a percent of the whole.

Percent change as a single multiplier

A percent change raises or lowers a quantity by a rate of itself. Writing that change as one multiplication is the key move that powers every discount, tax, markup, and interest problem. So that move is worth deriving carefully and then reusing everywhere.

Why a percent change is one multiplication#

Suppose a quantity starts at www and changes by a rate rrr, written as a decimal, so a 20%20\%20% rise is r=0.20r = 0.20r=0.20. Increasing www by that rate means adding r wr\,wrw to www:

new=w+r w.\text{new} = w + r\,w.new=w+rw.

Both terms on the right carry a factor of www, and the first is 1⋅w1 \cdot w1⋅w because the whole starting amount is 100%100\%100% of itself. Factor www out:

new=(1+r) w.\text{new} = (1 + r)\,w.new=(1+r)w.

A single number, the factor 1+r1 + r1+r, performs the entire increase. A decrease runs the identical argument with a minus sign, giving new=(1−r) w\text{new} = (1 - r)\,wnew=(1−r)w: you keep the whole and remove the rate, so the factor is 1−r1 - r1−r. A 20%20\%20% increase, for instance, is exactly this factor with the rate written as a decimal, 1+20100=1.201 + \frac{20}{100} = 1.201+10020​=1.20, now packaged as one decimal multiplier.

∎

With the factor in hand, a percent increase or decrease is one multiplication. To grow www by a rate rrr, multiply by 1+r1 + r1+r; to shrink it, multiply by 1−r1 - r1−r. The everyday money problems are exactly these factors:

  • A discount of rate rrr multiplies by 1−r1 - r1−r, because you pay the part that is left.
  • Sales tax or a tip at rate rrr multiplies by 1+r1 + r1+r, adding to the bill.
  • A markup of rate rrr on cost multiplies by 1+r1 + r1+r to set the selling price.
  • A commission is the part r⋅(sales)r \cdot (\text{sales})r⋅(sales), a straight use of p=r wp = r\,wp=rw.

Worked example 4 A markup as a single multiplier

A shop buys a bicycle for 180180180 dollars and marks it up 35%35\%35% to set the price. What does it sell for?

A markup is a percent increase on the cost, so the price is (1+r)(1 + r)(1+r) times the cost with r=0.35r = 0.35r=0.35. The factor is 1+0.35=1.351 + 0.35 = 1.351+0.35=1.35:

price=1.35×180=243.\text{price} = 1.35 \times 180 = 243.price=1.35×180=243.

The bicycle sells for 243243243 dollars. Reading the factor aloud helps: a 35%35\%35% markup leaves you paying 135%135\%135% of the cost, which is why the multiplier is 1.351.351.35. The two-step route agrees, since 35%35\%35% of 180180180 is 636363 and 180+63=243180 + 63 = 243180+63=243.

Finding a percent change from two amounts

Sometimes a problem gives you the amount before and after a change and asks for the rate, instead of giving you the rate. Find the size of the change, then divide by the original amount, never by the new amount. Keep the change positive by subtracting the smaller amount from the larger one: for an increase,

r=new−originaloriginal,r = \frac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}},r=originalnew−original​,

and for a decrease, subtract the other way, r=original−neworiginalr = \dfrac{\text{original} - \text{new}}{\text{original}}r=originaloriginal−new​, so rrr stays the same positive rate you use everywhere else in this lesson. Either way, this is the same r=pwr = \dfrac{p}{w}r=wp​ you already know, with the size of the change playing the part and the original amount playing the whole.

Worked example 5 Find a percent increase from two amounts

A grocery bill rises from 606060 dollars to 757575 dollars. What is the percent increase?

The change is the new amount minus the original amount, 75−60=1575 - 60 = 1575−60=15, and the original amount is 606060. Divide the change by the original amount:

r=1560=0.25=25%.r = \frac{15}{60} = 0.25 = 25\%.r=6015​=0.25=25%.

The bill rose 25%25\%25%. Dividing by the new amount, 757575, instead would wrongly give 20%20\%20%, because that treats the new amount as the whole instead of the original amount the change was measured against.

Reversing a percent change

Because a percent change is one multiplication, undoing it is one division. If new=(1+r) w\text{new} = (1 + r)\,wnew=(1+r)w, dividing both sides by the factor isolates the original amount:

w=new1+r.w = \frac{\text{new}}{1 + r}.w=1+rnew​.

That division is always safe for an increase, since a rate rrr is positive and 1+r1 + r1+r is never 000. A decrease undoes the same way, dividing by 1−r1 - r1−r instead, but only when 1−r1 - r1−r is not 000, which means the decrease has to be less than 100%100\%100%. A 100%100\%100% decrease cannot be reversed: it multiplies every original amount by 000, so it lands on the same new amount, 000, no matter what the original was, and there is no way to divide back to recover which original amount that 000 came from. This is exactly why you cannot recover the original by subtracting the percent back from the new value: the change multiplied the original, so undoing it must divide by the same factor. This is the single most common trap in percent work.

Worked example 6 Undo a tax to find the original price

A phone costs 929292 dollars including 15%15\%15% sales tax. What was the price before tax?

Tax is a percent increase, so the after-tax total is (1+r)(1 + r)(1+r) times the pre-tax price with r=0.15r = 0.15r=0.15. Let www be the pre-tax price:

1.15 w=92.1.15\,w = 92.1.15w=92.

The change multiplied by 1.151.151.15, so undo it by dividing by 1.151.151.15:

w=921.15=80.w = \frac{92}{1.15} = 80.w=1.1592​=80.

The pre-tax price was 808080 dollars. Check it forward: 15%15\%15% of 808080 is 121212, and 80+12=9280 + 12 = 9280+12=92. The tempting wrong move is to take 15%15\%15% of 929292 and subtract, but 15%15\%15% of 929292 is 13.8013.8013.80, giving 78.2078.2078.20, not 808080. That fails because the tax was 15%15\%15% of the smaller original, not 15%15\%15% of the larger total.

Check your understanding

After a 25%25\%25% discount, a shirt sells for 454545 dollars. What was the original price, in dollars?

Answer choices

A 25%25\%25% discount multiplies the original by 1−0.25=0.751 - 0.25 = 0.751−0.25=0.75, so the 454545 dollar sale price is 0.750.750.75 of the original. Divide by that factor to undo it.

0.75 w=45  ⟹  w=450.75=600.75\,w = 45 \implies w = \frac{45}{0.75} = 600.75w=45⟹w=0.7545​=60

Adding 25%25\%25% of 454545 back gives 56.2556.2556.25, which is wrong because the discount was taken of the larger original price.

Successive percent changes

Put two changes back to back: a change with factor f1f_1f1​ followed by a change with factor f2f_2f2​ multiplies the start by f1f_1f1​ and then by f2f_2f2​, so the overall factor is the product f1f2f_1 f_2f1​f2​. To apply several changes in a row, multiply the starting amount by each factor in turn, which is the same as multiplying by the product of the factors. That product is not 111 even when two rates are equal and opposite, because the second change acts on the amount the first change already produced, not on the original amount. Successive changes always combine by multiplying their factors, never by adding their rates.

Two 10%10\%10% raises in a row multiply by 1.10×1.10=1.211.10 \times 1.10 = 1.211.10×1.10=1.21, a 21%21\%21% raise, not the 20%20\%20% you would get by just adding the rates. The extra 1%1\%1% comes from the second raise acting on the already-raised amount, not on the original.

Expanding (1+r1)(1+r2)(1 + r_1)(1 + r_2)(1+r1​)(1+r2​) shows exactly where that extra percent comes from. You can read it in the Go deeper section at the end of this lesson.

Worked example 7 Two changes in a row

A 200200200 dollar item is marked up 20%20\%20% for the holidays, and then that higher price is cut 20%20\%20% in a sale. What is the final price, and is it back to 200200200?

Build a factor for each change and multiply in order. The markup is 1+0.20=1.201 + 0.20 = 1.201+0.20=1.20 and the sale is 1−0.20=0.801 - 0.20 = 0.801−0.20=0.80:

200×1.20×0.80.200 \times 1.20 \times 0.80.200×1.20×0.80.

Multiply the factors first to see the net effect:

1.20×0.80=0.96,200×0.96=192.1.20 \times 0.80 = 0.96, \qquad 200 \times 0.96 = 192.1.20×0.80=0.96,200×0.96=192.

The final price is 192192192 dollars, not 200200200. The equal rise and fall did not cancel: the 20%20\%20% cut was taken of the raised 240240240, so it removed more than the markup added. The net factor 0.960.960.96 is a 4%4\%4% decrease from the start.

Check your understanding

A price rises 10%10\%10% one week and falls 10%10\%10% the next. Compared with the start, the price is now:

Answer choices

Multiply the two factors rather than adding the rates. A 10%10\%10% rise is 1.101.101.10 and a 10%10\%10% fall is 0.900.900.90.

1.10×0.90=0.991.10 \times 0.90 = 0.991.10×0.90=0.99

The result is 0.990.990.99 of the start, a 1%1\%1% decrease, because the fall acts on the larger raised price.

Simple interest

Interest is a percent applied to money over time. In simple interest, the interest each year is the same rate of the original principal, and the principal itself never changes. Suppose you invest or borrow a principal PPP at an annual rate rrr (a decimal) for ttt years. Then the interest is the rate times the principal times the number of years:

I=P r t.I = P\,r\,t.I=Prt.

This is p=r wp = r\,wp=rw applied once per year and added up: each year earns r⋅Pr \cdot Pr⋅P, and over ttt years that totals PrtP r tPrt, because the principal stays fixed. The total amount is the principal plus the interest,

A=P+I=P+Prt=P(1+rt).A = P + I = P + P r t = P(1 + r t).A=P+I=P+Prt=P(1+rt).

The factor 1+rt1 + r t1+rt echoes the increase multiplier, except the rate is multiplied by the time first. Because the principal is fixed, simple interest grows in a straight line with ttt. (Letting each year’s interest join the principal and earn more is compound interest, a separate lesson.)

Worked example 8 Simple interest on a deposit

You deposit 800800800 dollars at 5%5\%5% simple annual interest for 333 years. How much interest do you earn, and what is the balance?

Identify the pieces: the principal is P=800P = 800P=800, the rate is r=5%=0.05r = 5\% = 0.05r=5%=0.05, and the time is t=3t = 3t=3 years. Substitute into I=PrtI = P r tI=Prt:

I=800×0.05×3=120.I = 800 \times 0.05 \times 3 = 120.I=800×0.05×3=120.

You earn 120120120 dollars of interest. Add it to the principal for the balance, or use the factor form A=P(1+rt)A = P(1 + r t)A=P(1+rt):

A=800+120=800(1+0.05×3)=800×1.15=920.A = 800 + 120 = 800(1 + 0.05 \times 3) = 800 \times 1.15 = 920.A=800+120=800(1+0.05×3)=800×1.15=920.

The balance is 920920920 dollars. Each year adds the same 0.05×800=400.05 \times 800 = 400.05×800=40 dollars, and three equal years give 120120120. That equal yearly interest is the mark of simple interest: no year earns interest on a previous year’s interest.

Just like p=r wp = r\,wp=rw, the equation I=P r tI = P\,r\,tI=Prt rearranges to find whichever quantity is missing: divide by the other two to isolate it, for instance r=IPtr = \dfrac{I}{Pt}r=PtI​ to find the rate or t=IPrt = \dfrac{I}{Pr}t=PrI​ to find the time.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Subtracting instead of dividing to reverse a change. Divide by the factor 1±r1 \pm r1±r to undo a change; adding or subtracting the percent from the new value gives the wrong answer.
  • Adding successive percents instead of multiplying factors. Two changes combine as a product of factors, f1f2f_1 f_2f1​f2​, never a sum of rates, because each change acts on the amount right before it.
  • Confusing “percent of” with “percent more than.” ”20%20\%20% of 505050” is 101010, the part; ”20%20\%20% more than 505050” is 606060, the whole plus the part.
  • Forgetting to convert the percent to a decimal, or building the multiplier from the wrong piece. In p=r wp = r\,wp=rw, the rate is r=0.30r = 0.30r=0.30, not 303030; and a 30%30\%30% decrease multiplies by 0.700.700.70, the part that remains, not by 0.300.300.30, the part removed.

Key takeaways

  • Every percent problem is the one relationship p=r wp = r\,wp=rw (part = rate times whole), with the rate rrr the percent written as a decimal. Name the unknown letter and solve: w=prw = \frac{p}{r}w=rp​ for the whole, r=pwr = \frac{p}{w}r=wp​ for the rate.
  • A percent increase multiplies the original by the factor 1+r1 + r1+r; a percent decrease multiplies by 1−r1 - r1−r, where rrr is that percent written as a decimal. Discounts use 1−r1 - r1−r; tax, tip, and markup use 1+r1 + r1+r.
  • Reverse a change by dividing by its factor: original=new1±r\text{original} = \dfrac{\text{new}}{1 \pm r}original=1±rnew​. Divide, do not subtract. A 100%100\%100% decrease (r=1r = 1r=1) cannot be reversed, since it sends every original amount to the same 000.
  • Stack successive changes by multiplying their factors, f1f2f_1 f_2f1​f2​. Equal rise and fall do not cancel, since (1.20)(0.80)=0.96(1.20)(0.80) = 0.96(1.20)(0.80)=0.96, and percents add only when taken of the same base.
  • Simple interest is I=PrtI = P r tI=Prt, with total A=P(1+rt)A = P(1 + r t)A=P(1+rt). The principal stays fixed, so the interest grows linearly with time.

Where the extra percent comes from

Expanding the product for two increases makes the gap explicit:

(1+r1)(1+r2)=1+r1+r2+r1r2.(1 + r_1)(1 + r_2) = 1 + r_1 + r_2 + r_1 r_2.(1+r1​)(1+r2​)=1+r1​+r2​+r1​r2​.

The single overall rate is r1+r2+r1r2r_1 + r_2 + r_1 r_2r1​+r2​+r1​r2​, which beats the naive r1+r2r_1 + r_2r1​+r2​ by the cross term r1r2r_1 r_2r1​r2​. That extra piece is the change-of-the-change: for two 10%10\%10% raises, r1=r2=0.10r_1 = r_2 = 0.10r1​=r2​=0.10, so the cross term r1r2=0.01r_1 r_2 = 0.01r1​r2​=0.01 adds the last percent, giving 21%21\%21% instead of 20%20\%20%.

A bit of history (optional)

A merchant’s son in medieval Italy was not sent to school to learn Latin. He was sent to learn markups.

From the thirteen hundreds on, the trading cities ran reckoning schools, the abbaco schools, named for the abacus but taught with pen and paper. A boy arrived at about eleven and stayed a couple of years. His master set problems all day, and the problems are the ones you have just done. What does a cloth sell for after a markup? What is the broker’s cut of the sale? How do two partners split a profit, and what interest has piled up on a loan?

None of it was written as an equation. Each kind of question arrived with its own remembered recipe, and a boy left school carrying a great many of them. Hundreds of their handwritten problem books have survived.

You have replaced that whole shelf with one line: a part is a rate times a whole. The three classic questions are not three methods but one equation, solved for whichever of its three letters has gone missing. Naming the unknown is what turned a feat of memory into a single step of algebra.
