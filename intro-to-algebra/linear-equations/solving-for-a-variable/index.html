Solving for a Variable

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

A rectangle has area 151515 square centimeters and length 555 centimeters. Its width is 15÷5=315 \div 5 = 315÷5=3 centimeters. You can write the same calculation for any rectangle with positive length: width equals area divided by length.

The formula A=lwA = lwA=lw gives area from length and width. Rearranging it to w=Alw = \dfrac{A}{l}w=lA​ gives width instead. The balancing moves are the ones you already know. This time, you will isolate one chosen letter while treating the others as fixed quantities, and sometimes factor your chosen letter out when it appears in several terms.

Learning goals

  • Isolate a chosen letter, treating the others as fixed numbers
  • Rearrange A=lwA = lwA=lw into w=Alw = \dfrac{A}{l}w=lA​
  • Factor the target out when it appears in several terms
  • State the nonzero condition that dividing by a letter assumes
  • Check a rearranged formula with simple numbers from the original

What it means to solve for a variable

A literal equation contains more than one letter, such as A=lwA = lwA=lw, d=rtd = rtd=rt, or P=2l+2wP = 2l + 2wP=2l+2w. Many geometry and science formulas have this form.

To solve for a variable means to get that variable alone on one side, with no copy of it on the other side. For a rectangle with positive lengths, solving A=lwA = lwA=lw for www gives w=Alw = \dfrac{A}{l}w=lA​; solving for lll gives l=Awl = \dfrac{A}{w}l=wA​.

Treat the other letters as fixed numbers while you work. For example, 12=4w12 = 4w12=4w gives w=3w = 3w=3 by division by 444. In A=lwA = lwA=lw, the fixed quantities AAA and lll play the roles of 121212 and 444. Their values need not be known yet to carry out the same division.

The same moves, now with letters

The properties of equality still apply. Add or subtract the same quantity on both sides, or multiply or divide both sides by a nonzero quantity. Undo the operations around the target variable in reverse order.

Why isolating a letter uses the very same moves#

For 18=6w18 = 6w18=6w, dividing both sides by 666 gives 186=w\dfrac{18}{6} = w618​=w, so w=3w = 3w=3. Multiplying by 666 reverses that step: 6⋅3=186 \cdot 3 = 186⋅3=18.

For A=lwA = lwA=lw, divide both sides by lll, provided l≠0l \neq 0l=0:

Al=lwl=w.\frac{A}{l} = \frac{lw}{l} = w.lA​=llw​=w.

Thus w=Alw = \dfrac{A}{l}w=lA​. Multiplying both sides by lll recovers A=lwA = lwA=lw, so the rearrangement keeps the same relationship. The reasoning did not depend on choosing 666 for the coefficient: any nonzero value of lll permits the division.

∎

Worked example 1 Solve d=rtd = rtd=rt for the rate rrr

The distance formula d=rtd = rtd=rt multiplies the rate rrr by the time ttt. To solve for rrr, undo that multiplication by dividing both sides by ttt, provided the elapsed time is nonzero:

dt=rtt=r,sor=dt.\frac{d}{t} = \frac{rt}{t} = r, \qquad\text{so}\qquad r = \frac{d}{t}.td​=trt​=r,sor=td​.

The rearranged formula reads rate as distance divided by time, which is exactly the average rate over the whole trip. Solving instead for ttt is the mirror image: dividing by rrr gives t=drt = \dfrac{d}{r}t=rd​, provided r≠0r \neq 0r=0. A zero rate does not let you recover time by division.

Rearranging a formula in more than one step

When several operations surround the target, undo the last one first. In 2w+2l2w + 2l2w+2l, the last operation is adding 2l2l2l. In 59(F−32)\dfrac{5}{9}(F - 32)95​(F−32), it is multiplying the whole group by 59\dfrac{5}{9}95​. The order depends on how the expression was built.

Worked example 2 Solve P=2l+2wP = 2l + 2wP=2l+2w for the width www

The perimeter of a rectangle is P=2l+2wP = 2l + 2wP=2l+2w. The target www sits inside the term 2w2w2w, with 2l2l2l added on, so undo in reverse. Subtract the added 2l2l2l from both sides first:

P−2l=2w.P - 2l = 2w.P−2l=2w.

Now divide both sides by the coefficient 222. The division must reach the whole left side, every term of it:

w=P−2l2.w = \frac{P - 2l}{2}.w=2P−2l​.

Watch that last step. Dividing only the PPP and forgetting the 2l2l2l gives the wrong P2−2l\dfrac{P}{2} - 2l2P​−2l; the correct result is P−2l2\dfrac{P - 2l}{2}2P−2l​, which equals P2−l\dfrac{P}{2} - l2P​−l once each term is divided.

Worked example 3 Solve C=59(F−32)C = \dfrac{5}{9}(F - 32)C=95​(F−32) for FFF

The formula C=59(F−32)C = \dfrac{5}{9}(F - 32)C=95​(F−32) turns a Fahrenheit temperature into Celsius. To aim it the other way and read Fahrenheit from Celsius, solve for FFF. The target is under two layers: it is inside the parentheses, and the whole parenthesis is multiplied by 59\frac{5}{9}95​. Undo the outer layer first by multiplying both sides by the reciprocal 95\frac{9}{5}59​:

95C=F−32.\frac{9}{5}C = F - 32.59​C=F−32.

Then add 323232 to both sides to free FFF:

F=95C+32.F = \frac{9}{5}C + 32.F=59​C+32.

This form gives the Fahrenheit temperature directly from the Celsius temperature. Rearrange once, then reuse the formula for each new reading.

Worked example 4 Solve y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b for xxx

In y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b, yyy already stands alone. To solve for xxx, treat yyy, mmm, and bbb as fixed numbers. Subtract bbb from both sides:

y−b=mx.y - b = mx.y−b=mx.

Then divide by the coefficient mmm, provided m≠0m \neq 0m=0:

x=y−bm.x = \frac{y - b}{m}.x=my−b​.

Keep the whole numerator y−by - by−b together over mmm.

Check your understanding

Which formula solves ax+b=cax + b = cax+b=c for xxx, where a≠0a \neq 0a=0 and aaa, bbb, ccc are fixed constants? Test your choice with a=2a = 2a=2, b=1b = 1b=1, c=7c = 7c=7 by substituting its value of xxx into 2x+1=72x + 1 = 72x+1=7.

Answer choices

Undo the operations in reverse: subtract bbb from both sides, then divide by aaa, which is nonzero.

ax=c−b,x=c−baax = c - b, \qquad x = \frac{c - b}{a}ax=c−b,x=ac−b​

For the test values, x=(7−1)/2=3x = (7 - 1)/2 = 3x=(7−1)/2=3. Substituting into the original gives 2(3)+1=72(3) + 1 = 72(3)+1=7, so it checks. The other formulas give 444, 2.52.52.5, and −3-3−3, none of which satisfies 2x+1=72x + 1 = 72x+1=7.

The successful check tests this instance; the reversible algebra justifies the general formula. In particular, ca−b\dfrac{c}{a} - bac​−b is wrong because division must reach the whole numerator c−bc - bc−b.

When the target appears in more than one term

When the target appears in several terms, combine those terms first. In 3x+2x=203x + 2x = 203x+2x=20, the left side is (3+2)x=5x(3 + 2)x = 5x(3+2)x=5x. Dividing by 555 then gives x=4x = 4x=4.

This is the distributive law in reverse, the same idea used for combining like terms and factoring expressions. It also works when the coefficients are letters, as in ax+bx=cax + bx = cax+bx=c. Dividing by aaa alone, even when a≠0a \neq 0a=0, leaves x+baxx + \dfrac{b}{a}xx+ab​x on the left. Factoring first gathers the target into one place.

Why a target in two terms must be factored out#

Start with 4x+3x=354x + 3x = 354x+3x=35. The distributive law gathers the two copies of xxx:

4x+3x=35,x(4+3)=35,7x=35.\begin{gathered} 4x + 3x = 35, \\ x(4 + 3) = 35, \\ 7x = 35. \end{gathered}4x+3x=35,x(4+3)=35,7x=35.​

Dividing by the nonzero combined coefficient 777 gives x=5x = 5x=5. Check: 4(5)+3(5)=20+15=354(5) + 3(5) = 20 + 15 = 354(5)+3(5)=20+15=35. Nothing required the particular coefficients 444 and 333; the same law applies to any fixed coefficients aaa and bbb:

ax+bx=x(a+b).ax + bx = x(a + b).ax+bx=x(a+b).

So ax+bx=cax + bx = cax+bx=c becomes x(a+b)=cx(a + b) = cx(a+b)=c. If a+b≠0a + b \neq 0a+b=0, divide both sides by a+ba + ba+b:

x=ca+b.x = \frac{c}{a + b}.x=a+bc​.

Factoring has made the combined coefficient visible. If a+b=0a + b = 0a+b=0 instead, the equation is 0=c0 = c0=c: it has no solution when c≠0c \neq 0c=0, and every real xxx works when c=0c = 0c=0.

∎

The same routine handles a target that starts on both sides of the equation. Bring every term containing the target to one side first, then factor it out.

Worked example 5 Solve ax=bx+cax = bx + cax=bx+c for xxx

Here the target xxx appears on both sides, in axaxax and in bxbxbx. First bring both xxx terms to the same side by subtracting bxbxbx from both sides:

ax−bx=c.ax - bx = c.ax−bx=c.

Now the two copies of xxx sit together, so factor xxx out by reading the distributive law backward:

x(a−b)=c.x(a - b) = c.x(a−b)=c.

With xxx in a single place, divide both sides by a−ba - ba−b, which requires a≠ba \neq ba=b so the divisor is not zero:

x=ca−b.x = \frac{c}{a - b}.x=a−bc​.

Collecting the target on one side and factoring it out is the routine whenever the variable you want appears more than once.

Worked example 6 Solve A=P+PrtA = P + PrtA=P+Prt for the principal PPP

The amount in a simple-interest account after a time ttt is A=P+PrtA = P + PrtA=P+Prt, the principal PPP plus the interest PrtPrtPrt it earns. To recover the principal from the final amount, solve for PPP. The target appears in both terms on the right, so factor it out. Both PPP and PrtPrtPrt share the factor PPP:

A=P(1+rt).A = P(1 + rt).A=P(1+rt).

Now PPP multiplies the single quantity 1+rt1 + rt1+rt, so divide both sides by it, provided 1+rt≠01 + rt \neq 01+rt=0:

P=A1+rt.P = \frac{A}{1 + rt}.P=1+rtA​.

Here rrr is the interest rate per time unit, written as a decimal, and ttt uses that same time unit. For nonnegative rrr and ttt, 1+rt1 + rt1+rt is at least 111, so division is allowed. The factor pulled out is PPP; the factor left in parentheses is 1+rt1 + rt1+rt. The lone PPP is P⋅1P \cdot 1P⋅1, so the 111 must remain. Expanding P(1+rt)P(1 + rt)P(1+rt) checks that both original terms return.

Check your understanding

Solve mx+nx=pmx + nx = pmx+nx=p for xxx, where m+n≠0m + n \neq 0m+n=0.

Answer choices

Both terms on the left contain xxx, so factor it out, then divide by the quantity left behind (assuming m+n≠0m + n \neq 0m+n=0).

x(m+n)=p,x=pm+nx(m + n) = p, \qquad x = \frac{p}{m + n}x(m+n)=p,x=m+np​

Dividing by mmm alone (assuming m≠0m \neq 0m=0) would rescale both terms rather than remove xxx from just one: mx+nx=pmx + nx = pmx+nx=p becomes x+nmx=pmx + \dfrac{n}{m}x = \dfrac{p}{m}x+mn​x=mp​, and xxx is still in two places. Factoring gathers both copies of xxx into one before the single division, and the factor is the sum m+nm + nm+n, not the product mnmnmn.

Dividing by a letter, and checking the result

Recall the nonzero-denominator rule from Algebraic Fractions: division is defined only when the divisor is not zero. For known numbers such as 222 and 2π2\pi2π, that is already settled. A symbolic divisor may be zero even when its separate letters are not. For example, a−ba - ba−b is zero whenever a=ba = ba=b. State the nonzero condition before dividing. Physical context may guarantee it, as positive side lengths do for a rectangle, but a letter by itself does not.

The formula x=ca−bx = \dfrac{c}{a - b}x=a−bc​ from Example 5 applies only when a≠ba \neq ba=b. When a=ba = ba=b, return to ax=bx+cax = bx + cax=bx+c: subtracting axaxax gives 0=c0 = c0=c. If c=0c = 0c=0, any real xxx works; if c≠0c \neq 0c=0, none does. The rearranged fraction cannot describe those cases.

Also keep restrictions already present in the original equation. Clearing a denominator does not make a previously excluded value allowed. For example, p=qrp = \dfrac{q}{r}p=rq​ requires r≠0r \neq 0r=0. Multiplying by rrr gives q=prq = prq=pr, but the original restriction r≠0r \neq 0r=0 still applies.

To isolate a letter that starts in the denominator, consider 4=28r4 = \dfrac{28}{r}4=r28​ with r≠0r \neq 0r=0. Multiplying by rrr gives 4r=284r = 284r=28, then dividing by 444 gives r=7r = 7r=7. Checking, 28/7=428/7 = 428/7=4.

For the general equation p=qrp = \dfrac{q}{r}p=rq​, start from q=prq = prq=pr. If p≠0p \neq 0p=0, dividing by ppp gives r=qpr = \dfrac{q}{p}r=pq​. This value is allowed only when q≠0q \neq 0q=0, since the original requires r≠0r \neq 0r=0. If ppp and qqq are both zero, every nonzero rrr works in the original equation. If exactly one of them is zero, no allowed rrr works. Thus an assumption of exactly one solution here requires both ppp and qqq to be nonzero.

Check your understanding

Solve C=2πrC = 2\pi rC=2πr for rrr. Which condition, if any, belongs with your answer?

Answer choices

Isolating rrr means dividing both sides by 2π2\pi2π.

r=C2πr = \frac{C}{2\pi}r=2πC​

The divisor 2π2\pi2π is a fixed number, about 6.286.286.28, so it is already known to be nonzero. No additional algebraic restriction is needed for this division. rrr is the target and CCC is the numerator; neither is the divisor, so neither needs a nonzero condition for this division. The fraction 2πC\dfrac{2\pi}{C}C2π​ reverses the needed division.

The area formula A=lwA = lwA=lw and the box-volume formula V=lwhV = lwhV=lwh use letter divisors when rearranged. Positive dimensions guarantee those divisors are nonzero. The algebraic conditions also tell us when these rearrangements work if the letters are unrestricted quantities.

Check your understanding

Isolate www in A=lwA = lwA=lw and hhh in V=lwhV = lwhV=lwh. Which pair gives the formulas and the algebraic divisor conditions that justify them?

Answer choices

Divide A=lwA = lwA=lw by lll to isolate the width.

w=Al,l≠0w = \frac{A}{l}, \qquad l \neq 0w=lA​,l=0

Divide V=lwhV = lwhV=lwh by the product lwlwlw to isolate the height.

h=Vlw,lw≠0h = \frac{V}{lw}, \qquad lw \neq 0h=lwV​,lw=0

Positive rectangle and box dimensions guarantee these conditions. For unrestricted quantities, the formulas still require them. AAA and VVV are numerators, so their being nonzero is not the divisor condition. If l=0l = 0l=0, both displayed fractions are undefined; return to the original equations for that case.

To check a rearrangement, choose simple numbers that satisfy the original formula and its restrictions. For a rectangle with l=5l = 5l=5 and w=3w = 3w=3, the area is A=15A = 15A=15. The rearranged formula gives Al=155=3\dfrac{A}{l} = \dfrac{15}{5} = 3lA​=515​=3, matching the original width.

A mismatch reveals an error. A successful numerical check is useful, but does not prove a formula for every input; the reversible algebra establishes that.

Keep the original restriction x≠0x \neq 0x=0.

y=mx−by = mx - by=mx−b keeps the wrong sign, subtracting bbb instead of adding it back. y=xm+by = \dfrac{x}{m} + by=mx​+b divides xxx by mmm instead of multiplying mmm by xxx, undoing the wrong operation. y=m(x+b)y = m(x + b)y=m(x+b) adds bbb to xxx before multiplying by mmm, placing bbb inside the parentheses instead of outside it.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Dividing only one term by the coefficient. After reaching 2w=P−2l2w = P - 2l2w=P−2l, dividing by 222 must hit the entire other side: w=P−2l2w = \dfrac{P - 2l}{2}w=2P−2l​, which equals P2−l\dfrac{P}{2} - l2P​−l, never P2−2l\dfrac{P}{2} - 2l2P​−2l. Every term crosses the division, just as every term does when you distribute.
  • Losing a sign when subtracting a term. Solving y=mx+by = mx + by=mx+b for xxx means subtracting bbb from both sides, which gives y−by - by−b, not y+by + by+b. Apply the same operation, with the same sign, to both sides rather than sliding a term across unchanged.
  • Forgetting to factor when the target is in two terms. If xxx appears in ax+bxax + bxax+bx, dividing by aaa (assuming a≠0a \neq 0a=0) rescales both terms but does not isolate xxx: it leaves x+baxx + \dfrac{b}{a}xx+ab​x, with xxx still in two places. Collect the xxx terms, factor xxx out to x(a+b)x(a + b)x(a+b), and only then divide.
  • Pulling out the wrong factor. Factoring P+PrtP + PrtP+Prt gives P(1+rt)P(1 + rt)P(1+rt), not P(rt)P(rt)P(rt): the lone PPP is P⋅1P \cdot 1P⋅1, so a 111 stays inside. Check any factoring by expanding it back.
  • Dividing by a letter without noting it must be nonzero. The answer x=ca−bx = \dfrac{c}{a - b}x=a−bc​ is valid only when a≠ba \neq ba=b. State the condition whenever you divide by an expression that contains a letter.
  • Solving for the wrong variable. “Solve for www” means www ends up alone on one side with no other www anywhere in the equation. Isolating lll instead, or leaving the target on both sides, does not answer the question that was asked.

Key takeaways

  • A literal equation has more than one letter. Solve for one letter by isolating it, treating the other letters as fixed numbers.
  • Undo operations in reverse order. For example, A=lwA = lwA=lw gives w=Alw = \dfrac{A}{l}w=lA​ when l≠0l \neq 0l=0.
  • If the target appears in several terms, collect them and factor it out. Thus ax+bx=cax + bx = cax+bx=c gives x=ca+bx = \dfrac{c}{a + b}x=a+bc​ when a+b≠0a + b \neq 0a+b=0.
  • State every nonzero divisor condition and retain original denominator restrictions. If a proposed divisor is zero, return to the original equation to decide that case.
  • Check the result with simple numbers that fit the original. A successful check supports your work; the reversible steps justify the general formula.

A bit of history (optional)

The letters in a formula have not always followed today’s conventions. In 1591, François Viète used letters for both known and unknown quantities. He distinguished them with consonants for known quantities and vowels for unknown ones. That let a calculation describe a pattern rather than just one numerical problem.

René Descartes used a different division of the alphabet in La Géométrie, published in 1637. Letters near the beginning, such as aaa, bbb, and ccc, represented known quantities; letters near the end, such as xxx, yyy, and zzz, represented unknowns. This convention became familiar enough that a reader can often guess the roles of letters before reading the explanation.

It is still a convention, not a rule that locks a letter into one job. A formula can be rearranged for aaa just as well as for xxx. What matters is which quantity the problem asks you to find.
