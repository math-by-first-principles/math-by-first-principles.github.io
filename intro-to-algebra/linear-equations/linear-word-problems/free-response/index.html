    1. Two names for the same pair . Foundational, 10 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 10 pts

    Two numbers have a sum of 606060, and the larger is 141414 more than the smaller. Which of the two you decide to call the unknown is your choice and not the problem's, and this question makes that choice twice.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Name the SMALLER number as your unknown. Write down what your letter stands for, write the larger number in terms of it, and write one equation saying that the sum is 606060.

      Model the situation Name your unknown first, then write every other quantity in terms of that one letter. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Now start the translation again from the beginning, this time naming the LARGER number as your unknown. Write the new equation, solve it, and report both numbers.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Your two equations do not have the same solution, and yet they describe the same two numbers. Explain why that is not a contradiction, and say what has to be written down beside a letter for it never to become one.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      The letter is a name you choose for one quantity, not something the problem hands you. Decide which quantity you are naming, and write that sentence down, before any equation appears.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      Read the relationship from the other end. If one number is 141414 more than a second, then that second one is 141414 less than the first, and it is that phrasing which becomes the expression this time.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask what each solved value is a value OF. Two numbers that measure different quantities are not in competition, however far apart they look.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    With nnn the smaller number, the larger number is n+14n + 14n+14, and the equation is n+(n+14)=60n + (n + 14) = 60n+(n+14)=60.

    • 2n+14=602n + 14 = 602n+14=60, the same equation with the left side already combined
    • any letter in place of nnn, provided the sentence saying what it stands for is written down

    Part B

    Naming the larger number mmm, the equation is m+(m−14)=60m + (m - 14) = 60m+(m−14)=60, which gives m=37m = 37m=37. The two numbers are 232323 and 373737.

    • 2m−14=602m - 14 = 602m−14=60 is the same equation with the left side combined

    Part C

    The two letters name different quantities, so their values were never meant to agree; each solved value is a statement about the number it names, and both routes report the same pair. What has to be written down is what the letter stands for, units included.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Say what the letter stands for before using it: let nnn be the smaller number.

    The words tie the other number to that one. "The larger is 141414 more than the smaller" is an instruction to add 141414, so the larger number is n+14n + 14n+14. Nothing else in the problem needs a letter of its own.

    The sentence that states an equality is the one about the sum, and it becomes

    n+(n+14)=60.n + (n + 14) = 60.n+(n+14)=60.

    Part B

    Let mmm be the larger number. The tie between the two numbers now runs the other way: the smaller is 141414 LESS than the larger, so it is m−14m - 14m−14. The sum is still 606060:

    m+(m−14)=60.m + (m - 14) = 60.m+(m−14)=60.

    Combine the like terms and solve:

    2m−14=60,2m=74,m=37.2m - 14 = 60, \qquad 2m = 74, \qquad m = 37.2m−14=60,2m=74,m=37.

    The larger number is 373737, so the smaller is m−14=23m - 14 = 23m−14=23. Check against the words: 23+37=6023 + 37 = 6023+37=60, and 373737 really is 141414 more than 232323.

    Part C

    A solution is not a bare number. It is a value OF something, and the two routes named different somethings.

    In the first route the letter was the smaller number, so its value belongs to the smaller number. In the second the letter was the larger, so its value belongs to the larger. Set the two solved values side by side:

    n=23,m=37.n = 23, \qquad m = 37.n=23,m=37.

    Those are not two answers to one question. They are one answer each to two different questions, and the pair of numbers, which is what the problem actually asks about, comes out the same either way.

    The contradiction appears only if the meanings are dropped and the two values are compared as though they measured the same thing. That is what step two of the method is for: write down what the letter stands for, with units wherever there are any. It costs one line, and it is the line that lets a solved value be read back into the words.

    In one line

    Naming the smaller number gives n+(n+14)=60n + (n + 14) = 60n+(n+14)=60 and n=23n = 23n=23; naming the larger gives m+(m−14)=60m + (m - 14) = 60m+(m−14)=60 and m=37m = 37m=37. Both routes describe the same pair, 232323 and 373737. The solved values differ because the letters name different numbers, which is exactly why the meaning of a letter has to be recorded alongside it.

    2. A model with its words missing . Application, 12 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Application 12 pts

    A 969696 cm board is cut into three pieces. The second piece is twice as long as the first, and the third is 888 cm longer than the first. Someone has already done the translating and left behind only this line:

    x+2x+(x+8)=96.x + 2x + (x + 8) = 96.x+2x+(x+8)=96.

    1. A
      Part A.

      State what xxx stands for, units included, and say which phrase of the problem each of the three terms on the left came from. Say also what the number on the right records.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Solve the equation, then give the length of the LONGEST piece, with units.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Only one letter appears in that line, though the board is in three pieces. Explain what it is about the wording that lets a single letter carry all three, and describe a change to the wording that would leave the same three pieces beyond the reach of any single equation in one unknown.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Every symbol in that line came from somewhere in the paragraph above it. Take the terms one at a time and find the phrase that produced each one before doing any algebra at all.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      The value that drops out of the equation is the length of one particular piece. Work out all three lengths, then look back at what was asked for.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Ask what each of the last two pieces is measured AGAINST. Then imagine measuring one of them against the other instead, and see whether the first piece is still fixed by anything.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    xxx is the length of the first piece in centimetres. The term xxx is that piece, 2x2x2x is the second (twice as long as the first) and x+8x + 8x+8 is the third (888 cm longer than the first); the 969696 is the whole board, so the line says the three pieces together make it up.

    Part B

    x=22x = 22x=22, so the three pieces are 222222 cm, 444444 cm and 303030 cm. The longest is the second piece, 444444 cm.

    Part C

    Both other pieces are described by their relation to the first, so each becomes an expression in the same letter. Tie the second and third only to each other instead (say the second is 888 cm longer than the third) and nothing fixes either of them to the named piece: more than one cut then fits every word.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Only one piece is described on its own terms, and the other two are described by their relation to it, so the letter has to be that one: let xxx be the length of the first piece in centimetres.

    Now read the left side term by term. "Twice as long as the first" doubles it, giving 2x2x2x. "888 cm longer than the first" adds 888, giving x+8x + 8x+8. So the three pieces are

    x,2x,x+8.x, \qquad 2x, \qquad x + 8.x,2x,x+8.

    The right side is the board those pieces were cut from. Cutting loses no length, so the pieces add up to the whole 969696 cm, and that is the only equality the problem states.

    Part B

    Combine the like terms, then undo the addition and the multiplication:

    4x+8=96,4x=88,x=22.4x + 8 = 96, \qquad 4x = 88, \qquad x = 22.4x+8=96,4x=88,x=22.

    The letter was the FIRST piece, so 222222 cm is that piece and not yet an answer to the question. Write the other two out before deciding anything: the second is 2x=442x = 442x=44 cm and the third is x+8=30x + 8 = 30x+8=30 cm.

    Check against the words: 22+44+30=9622 + 44 + 30 = 9622+44+30=96, the second is twice the first, and the third is 888 more than the first. Of the three, the second piece is the longest, at 444444 cm.

    Part C

    The wording never describes the second or third piece by itself. It describes each one BY the first: "twice as long as the first", "888 cm longer than the first". Each of those phrases is an instruction for turning the first piece's length into another piece's length, so once the first piece has a letter, the other two have expressions:

    x,2x,x+8.x, \qquad 2x, \qquad x + 8.x,2x,x+8.

    One letter, three lengths, one equation.

    Now break that chain. Suppose the problem said only that the board is 969696 cm long and that the second piece is 888 cm longer than the third, with nothing linking either of them to the first. Then a cut of 686868 cm, 181818 cm and 101010 cm fits every word, and so does a cut of 484848 cm, 282828 cm and 202020 cm:

    68+18+10=96,48+28+20=96.68 + 18 + 10 = 96, \qquad 48 + 28 + 20 = 96.68+18+10=96,48+28+20=96.

    In each the second piece is 888 cm longer than the third and the pieces make up the board, so the words do not pin the cut down, and there is nothing left for a single equation in one unknown to determine. That is the whole weight of step three: every other quantity has to be expressible in terms of the one you named.

    In one line

    Here xxx is the first piece in centimetres, 2x2x2x the second and x+8x + 8x+8 the third, and 969696 is the board they make up. Solving gives x=22x = 22x=22, so the pieces are 222222 cm, 444444 cm and 303030 cm and the longest is the second, at 444444 cm. One letter is enough because both other pieces are described in terms of the first; tie them only to each other and cuts such as 68,18,1068, 18, 1068,18,10 and 48,28,2048, 28, 2048,28,20 both fit, so no single equation in one unknown could choose between them.

    3. Two targets for a twenty pound blend . Application, 13 points. Question 3 of 5.

    Application 13 pts

    A coffee shop blends two beans: a house bean at 666 dollars a pound and a premium bean at 141414 dollars a pound. Every blend it sells weighs 202020 pounds, and the shop prices a blend by what the beans in it cost.

    1. A
      Part A.

      The owner wants a 202020 pound blend worth 111111 dollars a pound. Name one unknown, write the amount of each bean in terms of it, and write one equation setting the cost of the beans used equal to the cost of the finished blend.

      Model the situation Name your unknown first, then write every other quantity in terms of that one letter. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Solve your equation and say how many pounds of each bean that blend uses.

      Carry your own answer forward Solve the equation you wrote in part A, whatever it turned out to be, and read both amounts off your own value. The credit here is for solving correctly and for reporting both beans in pounds.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      The owner now asks for a 202020 pound blend worth 555 dollars a pound. Set that one up, solve it, decide whether the shop can make it, and name the quantity in your own model that settles the matter. Then say, without solving anything further, which blend prices are out of this shop's reach altogether, and why.

      Justify your claim State the claim, then give the reason it has to be true. 5 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Two quantities move here, the amount of each bean, but they are not free of one another: the finished blend has a fixed weight. Fix one of them with a letter and the other has nowhere to hide.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      Both sides of the equation are amounts of money. Price each bean by multiplying its pounds by its price a pound, and price the finished blend the same way.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      An answer can be arithmetically correct and describe nothing at all. Before judging the new target, write out every quantity in your model at that value, not only the letter.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    With ppp the pounds of house bean, the premium bean is 20−p20 - p20−p pounds, and the equation is 6p+14(20−p)=11(20)6p + 14(20 - p) = 11(20)6p+14(20−p)=11(20).

    • 6p+14(20−p)=2206p + 14(20 - p) = 2206p+14(20−p)=220, with the right side already worked out
    • naming the premium bean instead: 14q+6(20−q)=22014q + 6(20 - q) = 22014q+6(20−q)=220

    Part B

    p=7.5p = 7.5p=7.5, so the blend uses 7.57.57.5 pounds of the house bean and 12.512.512.5 pounds of the premium bean.

    Part C

    The shop cannot make it. The equation gives 22.522.522.5 pounds of house bean in a 202020 pound blend, leaving 20−22.5=−2.520 - 22.5 = -2.520−22.5=−2.5 pounds of premium, and no scoop holds a negative amount. Every pound of the blend costs between 666 and 141414 dollars, so no price outside that range is reachable.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Name the quantity that settles everything else: let ppp be the pounds of house bean in the blend. The blend weighs 202020 pounds, so whatever is not house bean is premium bean, which is 20−p20 - p20−p pounds. That subtraction is what keeps the problem to one letter.

    Both sides of the equation are amounts of money, each an amount times a price per pound. The beans used cost 6p6p6p dollars and 14(20−p)14(20 - p)14(20−p) dollars. The finished blend is to be worth 111111 dollars a pound for 202020 pounds, which is 11(20)=22011(20) = 22011(20)=220 dollars:

    6p+14(20−p)=220.6p + 14(20 - p) = 220.6p+14(20−p)=220.

    Part B

    Distribute, then collect the like terms:

    6p+280−14p=220,−8p=−60,p=7.5.6p + 280 - 14p = 220, \qquad -8p = -60, \qquad p = 7.5.6p+280−14p=220,−8p=−60,p=7.5.

    So the blend uses 7.57.57.5 pounds of house bean, and the rest of the 202020 pounds, 20−7.5=12.520 - 7.5 = 12.520−7.5=12.5 pounds, is premium bean.

    Check against the words, in money: the house bean costs 6(7.5)=456(7.5) = 456(7.5)=45 dollars and the premium costs 14(12.5)=17514(12.5) = 17514(12.5)=175 dollars, together 220220220 dollars for 202020 pounds, which is 111111 dollars a pound. A half pound is no obstacle here, because beans are weighed rather than counted, so a fractional amount is something the shop can actually scoop.

    Part C

    Only the target changes, so the setup is the one from part A with 5(20)=1005(20) = 1005(20)=100 dollars on the right:

    6p+14(20−p)=100,280−8p=100,p=22.5.6p + 14(20 - p) = 100, \qquad 280 - 8p = 100, \qquad p = 22.5.6p+14(20−p)=100,280−8p=100,p=22.5.

    The algebra is faultless and the answer is still impossible. Notice where the impossibility shows up: the unknown came out positive, so a student watching only for a negative value sees nothing wrong. But 22.522.522.5 pounds of one bean will not fit in a 202020 pound blend at all, and the expression written for the premium bean says the same thing more loudly:

    20−p=20−22.5=−2.5.20 - p = 20 - 22.5 = -2.5.20−p=20−22.5=−2.5.

    A blend cannot contain −2.5-2.5−2.5 pounds of anything, so no blend of these beans is worth 555 dollars a pound. Writing out every quantity in the model at the solved value, and not just the letter, is what exposes it.

    The general reason needs no algebra. Every pound of the blend is either a pound of house bean, costing 666 dollars, or a pound of premium bean, costing 141414 dollars, so every pound costs at least 666 dollars and the 202020 pounds cost at least 120120120 dollars. A blend worth 555 dollars a pound would have to cost

    20×5=100<120=20×6.20 \times 5 = 100 < 120 = 20 \times 6.20×5=100<120=20×6.

    Mixing can never take the price below the cheaper of the two things mixed, and the same argument the other way up says it can never take the price above the dearer.

    In one line

    With ppp the pounds of house bean, 6p+14(20−p)=2206p + 14(20 - p) = 2206p+14(20−p)=220 gives p=7.5p = 7.5p=7.5, so the 111111 dollar blend is 7.57.57.5 pounds of house bean and 12.512.512.5 pounds of premium. The 555 dollar blend cannot be made: the same setup gives 22.522.522.5 pounds of house bean and therefore −2.5-2.5−2.5 pounds of premium. No blend of these two beans can be worth less than 666 dollars a pound or more than 141414, since every pound in it costs between those two prices.

    Another way: Balance the savings against the excesses

    The distances from the blend's price to each bean's price decide the amounts, so the total cost never has to be worked out. Each pound of house bean is 11−6=511 - 6 = 511−6=5 dollars cheaper than the blend it goes into, and each pound of premium bean is 14−11=314 - 11 = 314−11=3 dollars dearer, and for the blend to come out at 111111 dollars a pound those savings and excesses must cancel:

    5p=3(20−p),5p=60−3p,8p=60,p=7.5.5p = 3(20 - p), \qquad 5p = 60 - 3p, \qquad 8p = 60, \qquad p = 7.5.5p=3(20−p),5p=60−3p,8p=60,p=7.5.

    When it is worth it When the prices are awkward numbers, since no total is ever computed, and when you want to see at a glance where a blend price sits between the two: it lands nearer the price of whichever bean there is more of, which is also why a target outside the two prices is hopeless.

    4. Three times, four times, five times . Reasoning, 12 points. Question 4 of 5.

    Reasoning 12 pts

    A father is 454545 today and his son is 999. Every year that passes adds one year to each of them, so how their ages compare depends on when you ask.

    1. A

    Part A

    Run the same reasoning forwards. As the son ages, the fixed gap of 363636 years is a smaller and smaller multiple of his age, so the comparison slides steadily towards one, and it never arrives, because the father stays 363636 years ahead forever.
