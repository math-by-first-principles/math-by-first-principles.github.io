Word Problems with Linear Equations

Intuition

Two numbers add to 525252, and one is 888 more than the other. If the smaller number is nnn, the larger is n+8n + 8n+8, so the sentence becomes n+(n+8)=52n + (n + 8) = 52n+(n+8)=52. Turning the words into that equation is the real work of a word problem: once it is written, the last two lessons already show you how to solve it. This lesson is a repeatable method for doing that translation, and a tour of the problem types you meet again and again, from ages and coins to distances and discounts.

Learning goals

  • Name one unknown with its units, and build everything from it
  • Turn the key relationship into a single equation
  • Write consecutive integers as nnn, n+1n + 1n+1, n+2n + 2n+2
  • Apply d=rtd = rtd=rt, total value, the perimeter formula, and percent relationships
  • Check against the words, and report what was actually asked

Reading a problem as an equation

You already know how to solve a linear equation once you have one. What a word problem asks of you first is to build that equation out of a paragraph, and that is a skill of its own. From the very first lesson of this subject you have been translating phrases into symbols. Here “more than” adds, “less than” subtracts, “times” multiplies, and the verb “is” becomes an equals sign. A word problem is that same translation carried out on a whole situation instead of a single phrase.

Every problem in this lesson can be worked with the same six steps, in this order.

  1. Read the problem and find the question. Decide exactly what quantity you are asked for before doing anything else.
  2. Choose one unknown to name with a variable, and write down what it stands for, units included, as in “let www be the width in centimeters.”
  3. Express every other quantity in the problem in terms of that one variable.
  4. Find the relationship that connects the quantities, and write it as an equation.
  5. Solve the equation.
  6. Check the answer against the words of the original problem, and state the answer to the actual question, with units.

Most of these steps are reading, not algebra. The two that carry the whole method are the third and the sixth. Expressing everything in terms of one variable is what keeps the problem to a single equation you can solve. Checking against the words is what catches a translation that came out backwards. Every problem in this lesson is built so that one quantity can be written in terms of another, which is why one variable is enough for all of them: even when a problem mentions two or three quantities, pin them all to one variable, and a single equation does the job instead of the system you have not learned to solve yet. Not every word problem is built this way; some genuinely need two unknowns and two equations, a tool for a later lesson.

One unknown, everything else in terms of it

The third step is the heart of the method, so it is worth a closer look. A problem often names several quantities, but they are not independent; the words tie them together, and those ties let you write them all with one letter. If a rope is cut into two pieces and one piece is 333 feet longer than the other, you do not need two variables. Call the short piece xxx feet, and the long piece is then x+3x + 3x+3 feet, because ”333 feet longer” means add 333. One name, and the relationship supplies the rest.

Consecutive integers are a clean case of this, and they come up often enough to state carefully.

Why consecutive integers are nnn, n+1n + 1n+1, n+2n + 2n+2#

Consecutive integers, such as 6,7,86, 7, 86,7,8, are defined by a single fact: each one is exactly 111 more than the one before it.

Name the first of them nnn. The next integer is ”111 more than nnn,” which is n+1n + 1n+1, and the one after that is ”111 more than n+1n + 1n+1,” which is n+2n + 2n+2. One unknown, nnn, names the whole list.

The same idea handles consecutive even integers and consecutive odd integers, with a single change: they step by 222 instead of 111, since one number of the other kind sits between each even number and the next. So consecutive even integers are n,n+2,n+4n, n + 2, n + 4n,n+2,n+4, and consecutive odd integers take the identical form n,n+2,n+4n, n + 2, n + 4n,n+2,n+4. The starting value nnn has to be even in the first case and odd in the second, but you do not assume which; you let the equation determine nnn.

∎

The picture behind a “sum” problem is a tape diagram: lay the parts end to end and their combined length is the whole.

Worked example 1 The sum of two numbers is 525252, and the larger is 888 more than the smaller

The question asks for the two numbers. Name the smaller one: let nnn be the smaller number. The larger is 888 more than the smaller, so it is n+8n + 8n+8, and that single relationship pins both numbers to one letter.

The sentence that states an equality is that the sum is 525252. Translate it:

n+(n+8)=52.n + (n + 8) = 52.n+(n+8)=52.

Combine like terms and solve:

2n+8=52,2n=44,n=22.2n + 8 = 52, \qquad 2n = 44, \qquad n = 22.2n+8=52,2n=44,n=22.

So the smaller number is 222222, and the larger is n+8=30n + 8 = 30n+8=30. Check against the words: the sum 22+30=5222 + 30 = 5222+30=52 is right, and 303030 really is 888 more than 222222. The two numbers are 222222 and 303030.

Worked example 2 Find three consecutive integers whose sum is 848484

The question asks for three integers. Name the smallest: let nnn be the smallest integer. The three consecutive integers are then nnn, n+1n + 1n+1, and n+2n + 2n+2. The equality in the problem is that they sum to 848484:

n+(n+1)+(n+2)=84.n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 84.n+(n+1)+(n+2)=84.

Combine and solve:

3n+3=84,3n=81,n=27.3n + 3 = 84, \qquad 3n = 81, \qquad n = 27.3n+3=84,3n=81,n=27.

The integers are 272727, 282828, and 292929. Checking, 27+28+29=8427 + 28 + 29 = 8427+28+29=84, and they are consecutive, so the answer is 27,28,2927, 28, 2927,28,29.

Check your understanding

The sum of three consecutive odd integers is 575757. What is the largest of the three?

Answer choices

Let nnn be the smallest. Consecutive odd integers step by 222, so they are nnn, n+2n + 2n+2, and n+4n + 4n+4.

n+(n+2)+(n+4)=57,3n+6=57,n=17n + (n + 2) + (n + 4) = 57, \qquad 3n + 6 = 57, \qquad n = 17n+(n+2)+(n+4)=57,3n+6=57,n=17

The integers are 171717, 191919, and 212121, so the largest is 212121. Check: 17+19+21=5717 + 19 + 21 = 5717+19+21=57.

Age problems

Age problems turn on one idea: everyone ages at the same rate. If you know someone’s age now, then in kkk years they will be kkk years older, and kkk years ago they were kkk years younger. So name one person’s present age, and use the given relationship to write everyone else’s present age in terms of it. Then shift the whole cast forward or backward together when the problem talks about another time.

Worked example 3 Maria is 444 times as old as her brother; in 666 years she will be twice as old as he is

The question asks for their present ages. Let bbb be the brother’s age now, in years. Maria is 444 times as old, so she is 4b4b4b now. In 666 years each is 666 years older, so the brother will be b+6b + 6b+6 and Maria will be 4b+64b + 64b+6. The equality is that at that time Maria’s age is twice her brother’s:

4b+6=2(b+6).4b + 6 = 2(b + 6).4b+6=2(b+6).

Distribute and solve:

4b+6=2b+12,2b=6,b=3.4b + 6 = 2b + 12, \qquad 2b = 6, \qquad b = 3.4b+6=2b+12,2b=6,b=3.

So the brother is 333 and Maria is 4b=124b = 124b=12. Check with the words: now Maria’s 121212 is 444 times the brother’s 333; in 666 years she is 181818 and he is 999, and 181818 is twice 999. Maria is 121212 and her brother is 333.

Money problems

Money problems lean on one formula: the total value of a pile of identical coins is the number of coins times the value of each. Ten dimes are worth 10×10=10010 \times 10 = 10010×10=100 cents, and in general ddd dimes are worth 10d10d10d cents. A nickel is worth 555 cents, a dime 101010 cents, and a quarter 252525 cents. To hold everything to one variable, count one kind of coin with the letter and get the other kind from the total. Working in cents is usually cleanest, because it keeps every value a whole number.

Worked example 4 303030 coins, all nickels and dimes, worth 245245245 cents

The question asks how many of each coin. Let nnn be the number of nickels. There are 303030 coins in all, so the number of dimes is whatever is left, 30−n30 - n30−n. Now write the total value in cents: each nickel is worth 555 cents and each dime 101010 cents, so

5n+10(30−n)=245.5n + 10(30 - n) = 245.5n+10(30−n)=245.

Distribute and solve:

5n+300−10n=245,−5n=−55,n=11.5n + 300 - 10n = 245, \qquad -5n = -55, \qquad n = 11.5n+300−10n=245,−5n=−55,n=11.

There are 111111 nickels and 30−11=1930 - 11 = 1930−11=19 dimes. Check: 111111 nickels are worth 555555 cents and 191919 dimes are worth 190190190 cents, and 55+190=24555 + 190 = 24555+190=245 cents, which is 222 dollars and 454545 cents. So there are 111111 nickels and 191919 dimes.

Check your understanding

A jar of 202020 coins holds only quarters and dimes and is worth 350350350 cents. If qqq is the number of quarters, which equation models the total value?

Answer choices

Let qqq be the number of quarters; the rest of the 202020 coins are dimes, so there are 20−q20 - q20−q dimes. Quarters are worth 252525 cents each and dimes 101010 cents each, so the total value in cents is

25q+10(20−q)=350.25q + 10(20 - q) = 350.25q+10(20−q)=350.

Counting the dimes as 20−q20 - q20−q is what keeps the problem in a single variable. The third choice would mean qqq counted dimes, which contradicts the wording.

Geometry problems

Geometry problems give you a figure whose measurements are linked by a formula you already know, most often a perimeter. That formula is the equality you translate. Name one length, then use the stated relationships to write the others in terms of it. Finally, set the perimeter equal to its given value.

Worked example 5 A rectangle's length is 333 cm more than twice its width, and its perimeter is 363636 cm

The question asks for the width and the length. Let www be the width in centimeters. The length is 333 more than twice the width, which is 2w+32w + 32w+3. The perimeter of a rectangle is twice the sum of its length and width, so

2((2w+3)+w)=36.2\big((2w + 3) + w\big) = 36.2((2w+3)+w)=36.

Simplify inside the parentheses, then solve:

2(3w+3)=36,6w+6=36,6w=30,w=5.2(3w + 3) = 36, \qquad 6w + 6 = 36, \qquad 6w = 30, \qquad w = 5.2(3w+3)=36,6w+6=36,6w=30,w=5.

The width is 555 cm and the length is 2(5)+3=132(5) + 3 = 132(5)+3=13 cm. Check: the perimeter is 2(13+5)=362(13 + 5) = 362(13+5)=36 cm, and 131313 is 333 more than twice 555. The rectangle is 555 cm by 131313 cm.

Percent problems

Percent problems are the ones you met in pre-algebra, now written as equations. Recall that a percent is a fraction out of 100100100, so 30%30\%30% of a quantity means 30100\frac{30}{100}10030​ of it, which is 0.30.30.3 times it. The word “of” tells you what the percent is taken of, and “is” tells you where the equals sign goes, so a sentence like ”181818 is 30%30\%30% of what number” becomes an equation directly.

A discount works the same way in reverse. Removing 20%20\%20% of a price leaves the other 80%80\%80% of it, so a 20%20\%20% discount on a 505050 dollar price leaves 0.80×50=400.80 \times 50 = 400.80×50=40 dollars. To find what is paid after a discount, multiply by the percent that remains, not the percent that was taken off.

Worked example 6 A student answered 85%85\%85% of the questions correctly, getting 343434 right

The question asks how many questions were on the test. Let ttt be the total number of questions. Getting 85%85\%85% of them right means

0.85 t=34.0.85\,t = 34.0.85t=34.

Divide both sides by 0.850.850.85:

t=340.85=40.t = \frac{34}{0.85} = 40.t=0.8534​=40.

So the test had 404040 questions. Check: 85%85\%85% of 404040 is 0.85×40=340.85 \times 40 = 340.85×40=34, which matches. A count of questions must be a whole number, and 404040 is one, so the answer makes sense in context: the test had 404040 questions.

Check your understanding

After a 20%20\%20% discount, a backpack sells for 363636 dollars. What was its original price, in dollars?

Answer choices

Let ppp be the original price in dollars. A 20%20\%20% discount leaves 80%80\%80% of the price, so

0.80 p=36,p=360.80=45.0.80\,p = 36, \qquad p = \frac{36}{0.80} = 45.0.80p=36,p=0.8036​=45.

Check: 20%20\%20% of 454545 is 999, and 45−9=3645 - 9 = 3645−9=36. The original price was 454545 dollars.

Distance problems

Distance problems rest on one relationship, distance equals rate times time, written d=rtd = rtd=rt. Pick the one unknown, usually the time, and write each distance as its rate times its time. Then translate the sentence that links the distances, such as “they are 707070 miles apart” or “they meet.”

Worked example 7 Two cyclists leave the same point at the same time, riding in opposite directions at 121212 and 161616 mph; when are they 707070 miles apart?

The question asks for the time. Let ttt be the number of hours since they started. Using d=rtd = rtd=rt, in ttt hours one cyclist covers 12t12t12t miles and the other covers 16t16t16t miles. Because they ride in opposite directions, the distance between them is the sum of the two distances, and the problem sets that sum to 707070:

12t+16t=70.12t + 16t = 70.12t+16t=70.

Combine and solve:

28t=70,t=7028=2.5.28t = 70, \qquad t = \frac{70}{28} = 2.5.28t=70,t=2870​=2.5.

After 2.52.52.5 hours they are 707070 miles apart. Check: in 2.52.52.5 hours one has gone 12×2.5=3012 \times 2.5 = 3012×2.5=30 miles and the other 16×2.5=4016 \times 2.5 = 4016×2.5=40 miles, and 30+40=7030 + 40 = 7030+40=70. The time is 2.52.52.5 hours.

Mixture problems (optional)

This last problem type is optional: it applies an idea you already have, so treat it as extra practice rather than new material. A mixture problem is the coin problem again, with pounds or liters standing in for coins: the value of each ingredient is still its amount times its unit value. Name the amount of one ingredient, get the other from the total, and set the combined value equal to the value of the whole batch.

Worked example 8 Mixing peanuts at 333 dollars a pound with cashews at 888 dollars a pound

Suppose you want 101010 pounds of a mix worth 555 dollars a pound. The blend uses peanuts at 333 dollars a pound and cashews at 888 dollars a pound, and the question asks how many pounds of each. Let ppp be the pounds of peanuts; the rest of the 101010 pounds is cashews, so there are 10−p10 - p10−p pounds of cashews. The value of the peanuts plus the value of the cashews equals the value of the mix:

3p+8(10−p)=5×10.3p + 8(10 - p) = 5 \times 10.3p+8(10−p)=5×10.

Solve:

3p+80−8p=50,−5p=−30,p=6.3p + 80 - 8p = 50, \qquad -5p = -30, \qquad p = 6.3p+80−8p=50,−5p=−30,p=6.

Use 666 pounds of peanuts and 10−6=410 - 6 = 410−6=4 pounds of cashews. Check: the peanuts are worth 3×6=183 \times 6 = 183×6=18 dollars and the cashews 8×4=328 \times 4 = 328×4=32 dollars, a total of 505050 dollars for 101010 pounds, which is 555 dollars a pound. So use 666 pounds of peanuts and 444 pounds of cashews.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Answering the wrong question. Solve for the variable, but then report what the problem actually asked for. If nnn is the smaller number and the question wants the larger, give n+8n + 8n+8, not nnn.
  • Translating “less than” and “more than” backwards. ”555 less than xxx” is x−5x - 5x−5, not 5−x5 - x5−x; the amount you take away comes second. In the same way ”333 less than twice a number” is 2n−32n - 32n−3.
  • Using more variables than the problem needs. Whenever the words tie one quantity to another, pin the second to the first instead of naming it separately. Writing the dimes as a new letter instead of 30−n30 - n30−n turns a solvable single equation into a system you are not yet set up to solve.
  • Mixing units. In a coin problem keep everything in cents (or everything in dollars), never a blend of the two. In a rate problem keep hours with hours and miles with miles.
  • Skipping the check against the words. Substituting your answer into your own equation only confirms the arithmetic, not the translation. Read the answer back into the original sentences, and make sure it makes sense: a count of people or coins cannot be negative or a fraction.

Key takeaways

  • Each problem in this lesson is solved by translation: name one unknown with its units, write every other quantity in terms of it, turn the key relationship into an equation, solve, and check against the words.
  • Consecutive integers are n,n+1,n+2n, n + 1, n + 2n,n+1,n+2; consecutive even or odd integers are n,n+2,n+4n, n + 2, n + 4n,n+2,n+4.
  • Ages: in kkk years everyone is kkk years older, and kkk years ago everyone was kkk years younger. Money and mixtures: total value is amount times unit value (work in cents for coins).
  • Geometry: the perimeter formula is your equation. Percent: “of” multiplies, p%p\%p% means p100\frac{p}{100}100p​, and a discount leaves the percent that remains. Distance: d=rtd = rtd=rt.
  • Always check the answer against the original words, report the quantity actually asked for with its units, and confirm it makes sense in context.

A bit of history (optional)

Isaac Newton spent years lecturing on algebra at his university, and William Whiston, his successor, published those lectures in 1707 as Arithmetica Universalis, or universal arithmetic. In it Newton tells the reader something blunt: a question posed in words has to be translated out of English into what he calls the algebraic language.

Then he shows what he means. He prints a page in two columns. Down the left runs a plain story about a merchant whose money grows year by year, cut into short phrases. Down the right, beside each phrase, stands the algebra that phrase turns into. One line of English, one line of signs, all the way down the page. It looks like nothing so much as a language exercise.

Calling it translation is more than a figure of speech. The work sits in the choosing: what shall the letter stand for, and how does every other quantity in the story get written with that same letter?

Newton’s two columns are this lesson’s method, set out side by side. Read for the sense, name one unknown, and write the rest in terms of it. Then read your answer back into the words that started it.
