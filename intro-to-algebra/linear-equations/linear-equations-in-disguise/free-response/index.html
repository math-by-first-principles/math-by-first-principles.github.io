    1. Clearing the disguise, and putting it back on . Foundational, 9 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 9 pts

    An equation cluttered with fractions or decimals is an ordinary linear equation under a layer of clutter, and one well-chosen multiplier removes the layer. This question strips two such layers, and then runs the whole process backwards to see what that multiplier did to each term on its way through.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Solve 2x3−x−42=56\dfrac{2x}{3} - \dfrac{x - 4}{2} = \dfrac{5}{6}32x​−2x−4​=65​, and check the value you find in the original equation.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Solve 0.25x+1.5=0.4x−0.90.25x + 1.5 = 0.4x - 0.90.25x+1.5=0.4x−0.9 by clearing the decimals, and say what a multiplier of 101010 would have left behind.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Here is a whole-number equation: 6x−4=96x - 4 = 96x−4=9. Write an equation containing fractions that becomes exactly this one when both sides are multiplied by 121212. Then explain why one whole-number equation can be the cleared form of many different fraction equations.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Nothing here needs a clever idea, only one number applied honestly. Ask what single multiplier would leave no denominator and no decimal point standing, then make sure it reaches every term on both sides, including the ones that look as though they do not need it.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      Count how many places the longest coefficient runs past the point. Each factor of ten shifts every digit one place to the left, so that count is exactly how many factors of ten you need.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Clearing multiplies every term by one number, so the way back divides every term by that same number. Carry out that division on the equation you were given, and look at what lands on each of its three terms.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    x=−7x = -7x=−7. Both sides of the original equation come to 56\dfrac{5}{6}65​ there.

    Part B

    x=16x = 16x=16. A multiplier of 101010 leaves 2.5x+15=4x−92.5x + 15 = 4x - 92.5x+15=4x−9, which still carries a decimal coefficient.

    Part C

    Dividing every term by 121212 gives x2−13=34\dfrac{x}{2} - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{3}{4}2x​−31​=43​. The multiplier is a choice, so dividing through by some other number produces another disguise of the same equation, and all of them share its solution.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The denominators are 333, 222 and 666, and the smallest number all three divide into is 666. Multiply every term of both sides by 666, keeping the numerator that is a difference inside a bracket as the multiplier lands on it:

    6⋅2x3−6⋅x−42=6⋅56,4x−3(x−4)=5.6 \cdot \frac{2x}{3} - 6 \cdot \frac{x-4}{2} = 6 \cdot \frac{5}{6}, \qquad 4x - 3(x - 4) = 5.6⋅32x​−6⋅2x−4​=6⋅65​,4x−3(x−4)=5.

    Distribute the −3-3−3 across both terms of the bracket, and watch the −4-4−4 turn into +12+12+12:

    4x−3x+12=5,x+12=5,x=−7.4x - 3x + 12 = 5, \qquad x + 12 = 5, \qquad x = -7.4x−3x+12=5,x+12=5,x=−7.

    Check in the original equation rather than in the cleared one, since it is the original that was asked about:

    2(−7)3−−7−42=−143+112=−28+336=56.\frac{2(-7)}{3} - \frac{-7-4}{2} = -\frac{14}{3} + \frac{11}{2} = \frac{-28 + 33}{6} = \frac{5}{6}.32(−7)​−2−7−4​=−314​+211​=6−28+33​=65​.

    Part B

    The coefficient reaching furthest to the right of the point is 0.250.250.25, which runs to the hundredths, so two factors of ten are needed and the multiplier is 100100100:

    100(0.25x+1.5)=100(0.4x−0.9),25x+150=40x−90.100(0.25x + 1.5) = 100(0.4x - 0.9), \qquad 25x + 150 = 40x - 90.100(0.25x+1.5)=100(0.4x−0.9),25x+150=40x−90.

    Gather the variable on one side and the constants on the other:

    240=15x,x=16.240 = 15x, \qquad x = 16.240=15x,x=16.

    Check in the original equation: the left side is 0.25(16)+1.5=4+1.5=5.50.25(16) + 1.5 = 4 + 1.5 = 5.50.25(16)+1.5=4+1.5=5.5, and the right side is 0.4(16)−0.9=6.4−0.9=5.50.4(16) - 0.9 = 6.4 - 0.9 = 5.50.4(16)−0.9=6.4−0.9=5.5.

    A single factor of ten moves every digit one place only, so multiplying by 101010 leaves

    2.5x+15=4x−9,2.5x + 15 = 4x - 9,2.5x+15=4x−9,

    which is tidier but not yet a whole-number equation. One factor of ten cleared the tenths; the hundredths needed the second.

    Part C

    Clearing multiplies every term by one number, so undoing it divides every term by that same number. Divide 6x−4=96x - 4 = 96x−4=9 through by 121212:

    6x12−412=912,x2−13=34.\frac{6x}{12} - \frac{4}{12} = \frac{9}{12}, \qquad \frac{x}{2} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{4}.126x​−124​=129​,2x​−31​=43​.

    Multiplying that back by 121212 returns 6x−4=96x - 4 = 96x−4=9 term for term, so it is a fraction equation with exactly the disguise asked for.

    Now the second half. Nothing forced the number 121212. Dividing the same whole-number equation through by 555 instead gives

    6x5−45=95,\frac{6x}{5} - \frac{4}{5} = \frac{9}{5},56x​−54​=59​,

    and that one clears with a multiplier of 555. Every choice of divisor produces a different-looking equation, and each clears back to 6x−4=96x - 4 = 96x−4=9, so a whole-number equation has as many disguises as there are numbers to divide it by.

    All of them have the same solution, x=136x = \dfrac{13}{6}x=613​, and that is the point worth carrying away: multiplying both sides by a number that is not zero changes how an equation looks and not which values satisfy it.

    In one line

    x=−7x = -7x=−7; x=16x = 16x=16, where a multiplier of 101010 would have left 2.5x+15=4x−92.5x + 15 = 4x - 92.5x+15=4x−9; and dividing 6x−4=96x - 4 = 96x−4=9 through by 121212 gives x2−13=34\dfrac{x}{2} - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{3}{4}2x​−31​=43​, one of many fraction equations that clear to it, because the number used to clear is a free choice.

    Foundational 12 pts

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Read the four lines as a chain and test each link on its own: does this line follow from the one directly above it? Exactly one link fails, and everything below the failure is a faithful consequence of a line that should never have been written.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    Line 2. Cross-multiplication is available only when a single fraction stands on each side, and the left side here is a sum of two terms. Lines 3 and 4 do follow from line 2, which is why the page looks sound all the way down.

    Part B

    x=−18x = -18x=−18, which makes both sides −4-4−4. Priya's −24-24−24 makes the left side −5.5-5.5−5.5 and the right side −6-6−6, so it does not solve the equation.

    Part C

    The left side is x+24\dfrac{x+2}{4}4x+2​, so the equation reads x+24=x+63\dfrac{x+2}{4} = \dfrac{x+6}{3}4x+2​=3x+6​, and cross-multiplying gives 3(x+2)=4(x+6)3(x+2) = 4(x+6)3(x+2)=4(x+6) and x=−18x = -18x=−18 once more. The rewriting had to be an identity, worth the same at every value, and the shortcut needs one single fraction on each side.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Test each line against the one directly above it, in order.

    Line 1 is the equation as given. Line 2 is the step to examine. It is a cross-multiplication, the move that turns ab=cd\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}ba​=dc​ into ad=bcad = bcad=bc, and that move is nothing more exotic than multiplying both sides by bdbdbd. What it needs is that each side IS one of those fractions. The left side here is x4+12\dfrac{x}{4} + \dfrac{1}{2}4x​+21​, a sum of two terms, so nothing licenses the step, and the 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ simply vanishes from the page.

    After that, the work is faithful to its own line 2. Distributing gives

    3x=4x+24,3x = 4x + 24,3x=4x+24,

    and subtracting 4x4x4x from both sides gives x=−24x = -24x=−24. So lines 3 and 4 are correct consequences of a line that should never have been written, which is exactly why nothing further down looks wrong.

    Part B

    The denominators are 444, 222 and 333, so the smallest number all three divide into is 121212. Multiply every term of both sides by it:

    12⋅x4+12⋅12=12⋅x+63,3x+6=4(x+6).12 \cdot \frac{x}{4} + 12 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 12 \cdot \frac{x+6}{3}, \qquad 3x + 6 = 4(x + 6).12⋅4x​+12⋅21​=12⋅3x+6​,3x+6=4(x+6).

    Distribute on the right and collect:

    3x+6=4x+24,−18=x.3x + 6 = 4x + 24, \qquad -18 = x.3x+6=4x+24,−18=x.

    Substitute x=−18x = -18x=−18 into the original equation. The left side is

    −184+12=−4.5+0.5=−4,\frac{-18}{4} + \frac{1}{2} = -4.5 + 0.5 = -4,4−18​+21​=−4.5+0.5=−4,

    and the right side is −18+63=−123=−4\dfrac{-18 + 6}{3} = \dfrac{-12}{3} = -43−18+6​=3−12​=−4, so the two agree.

    Substitute Priya's x=−24x = -24x=−24 into the same equation. The left side is

    −244+12=−6+0.5=−5.5,\frac{-24}{4} + \frac{1}{2} = -6 + 0.5 = -5.5,4−24​+21​=−6+0.5=−5.5,

    while the right side is −24+63=−6\dfrac{-24 + 6}{3} = -63−24+6​=−6. Those disagree, so −24-24−24 is not a solution. It solves line 2 faithfully, and line 2 is not the equation she was given.

    Part C

    Give the two terms on the left a common denominator and add them:

    x4+12=x4+24=x+24.\frac{x}{4} + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{x}{4} + \frac{2}{4} = \frac{x + 2}{4}.4x​+21​=4x​+42​=4x+2​.

    The equation is now a single fraction equal to a single fraction,

    x+24=x+63,\frac{x+2}{4} = \frac{x+6}{3},4x+2​=3x+6​,

    which is the shape cross-multiplication was made for. Multiplying both sides by 121212, the product of the two denominators, sends each numerator across to the opposite denominator:

    3(x+2)=4(x+6),3x+6=4x+24,x=−18.3(x + 2) = 4(x + 6), \qquad 3x + 6 = 4x + 24, \qquad x = -18.3(x+2)=4(x+6),3x+6=4x+24,x=−18.

    That is the value clearing the denominators produced, and the reason it had to be is worth stating. Rewriting x4+12\dfrac{x}{4} + \dfrac{1}{2}4x​+21​ as x+24\dfrac{x+2}{4}4x+2​ is an identity: the two expressions are worth the same at every value of xxx, so the equation before the rewriting and the equation after it are satisfied by exactly the same numbers. Priya's step was not of that kind. It deleted a term instead of absorbing it, and produced an equation with a different solution.

    The general condition is the one her line 2 walked past. Cross-multiplication applies when the equation is one ratio equal to one ratio. If a side is a sum, or carries an extra term, either combine that side into a single fraction first, as here, or clear every denominator with their least common denominator, as in part B. Both routes leave the solutions alone; the shortcut on its own does not.

    In one line

    Line 2 is the first that does not follow: cross-multiplication needs a single fraction on each side, and the left side is a sum, so the 12\dfrac{1}{2}21​ was dropped. Cleared with the least common denominator 121212, the equation gives x=−18x = -18x=−18, at which both sides are −4-4−4, while Priya's −24-24−24 leaves the sides at −5.5-5.5−5.5 and −6-6−6. Adding the left side into x+24\dfrac{x+2}{4}4x+2​ makes the equation a genuine proportion, and cross-multiplying that gives x=−18x = -18x=−18 as well.

    Another way: Clear only the denominator that is in the way

    The equation can also be walked to the same place one denominator at a time. Multiply both sides by 444 to clear the fraction that holds the variable on the left:

    x+2=4(x+6)3.x + 2 = \frac{4(x + 6)}{3}.x+2=34(x+6)​.

    Now multiply both sides by 333:

    3(x+2)=4(x+6),x=−18.3(x + 2) = 4(x + 6), \qquad x = -18.3(x+2)=4(x+6),x=−18.

    The two multiplications together are a multiplication by 121212, which is why this lands exactly where clearing with the least common denominator landed.

    When it is worth it When the least common denominator is awkward to spot, or when one fraction is clearly the obstacle. Doing it in stages is safer than guessing a multiplier, since each stage is a multiplication by a number and can be checked on its own.

    So the condition is plain. The claim as written is true whenever the multiplier cannot be zero, and a number that is not zero cannot: 121212 is not zero once and for all, whatever xxx turns out to be. That is why clearing the fractions in the earlier questions of this set needed no rejection step, while these two equations do.

    And it says what the comparison in parts A and B is for. It is not a ritual to be performed on every answer. It is the one place where a step you have already taken can hand you a value the original never had, and setting the candidate against the excluded values is what catches it.

    In one line

    Both equations exclude x=5x = 5x=5. The first gives x=132x = \dfrac{13}{2}x=213​, which is allowed and is a genuine solution; the second gives the candidate x=5x = 5x=5, which is the excluded value, so it is rejected and that equation has no solution. That second equation also refutes the claim, since multiplying by x−5x - 5x−5 produces an equation satisfied by x=5x = 5x=5 while the original is undefined there. The claim survives only for a multiplier that cannot be zero, which is what a nonzero number is, and that is why the excluded-value comparison belongs to any clearing whose multiplier holds the variable.

    Another way: Collect the fractions before clearing anything

    In both equations the two fractions already share a denominator, so they can be subtracted before anything is multiplied. For xx−5=5x−5+2\dfrac{x}{x-5} = \dfrac{5}{x-5} + 2x−5x​=x−55​+2, bring the fractions together:

    xx−5−5x−5=2,x−5x−5=2.\frac{x}{x-5} - \frac{5}{x-5} = 2, \qquad \frac{x - 5}{x - 5} = 2.x−5x​−x−55​=2,x−5x−5​=2.

    Every value the equation allows has x−5x - 5x−5 different from zero, so the left side is 111 at all of them and the equation says 1=21 = 21=2. Nothing can make that true, so there is no solution, and no candidate was ever manufactured to be thrown out.

    The same collection on the other equation gives x−2x−5=3\dfrac{x-2}{x-5} = 3x−5x−2​=3, which cross-multiplies to x−2=3(x−5)x - 2 = 3(x - 5)x−2=3(x−5) and leads to the same value as before.

    When it is worth it When the fractions already share a denominator. It reaches the verdict without producing a candidate at all, so there is nothing to reject. The excluded value still has to be named, since it is what licenses replacing x−5x−5\dfrac{x-5}{x-5}x−5x−5​ by 111.
