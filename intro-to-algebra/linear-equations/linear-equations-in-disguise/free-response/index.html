  1. Problem 1 Three denominators, one multiplier

    Solve 2x3−x−42=56\dfrac{2x}{3} - \dfrac{x - 4}{2} = \dfrac{5}{6}32x​−2x−4​=65​, and check your value in the original equation.

    1. Hint 1

      One multiplication can remove every denominator at once, as long as it reaches every term on both sides, including any term without a variable.

    2. Hint 2

      The smallest number that 333, 222 and 666 all divide into is 666. Keep x−4x - 4x−4 in parentheses as the 666 reaches it, because the minus sign in front of that fraction belongs to both of its terms.

    3. Hint 3

      After clearing, the −3-3−3 in front of the parentheses multiplies the −4-4−4 as well. To check, work out each fraction of the original separately at your value, then combine them over a common denominator.

    Answer

    x=−7x = -7x=−7; check: both sides of the original equation equal 56\tfrac{5}{6}65​.

    Full solution

    The denominators are 333, 222 and 666, and the smallest number all three divide into is 666.

    Multiply every term on both sides by 666.

    It is a nonzero number, so the new equation has exactly the same solutions as the original.

    Keep the numerator x−4x - 4x−4 in parentheses as the 666 reaches it:

    6⋅2x3−6⋅x−42=6⋅564x−3(x−4)=5.\begin{aligned} 6 \cdot \frac{2x}{3} - 6 \cdot \frac{x - 4}{2} &= 6 \cdot \frac{5}{6} \\ 4x - 3(x - 4) &= 5. \end{aligned}6⋅32x​−6⋅2x−4​4x−3(x−4)​=6⋅65​=5.​

    Distribute the −3-3−3 across both terms inside the parentheses, so the −4-4−4 becomes +12+12+12.

    Writing −3x−12-3x - 12−3x−12 there would lose that sign, because the minus in front of the fraction belongs to its whole numerator.

    Then collect the xxx terms and subtract 121212 from both sides:

    4x−3x+12=5x+12=5x=−7.\begin{aligned} 4x - 3x + 12 &= 5 \\ x + 12 &= 5 \\ x &= -7. \end{aligned}4x−3x+12x+12x​=5=5=−7.​

    Check in the original equation.

    At x=−7x = -7x=−7 the first fraction is 2(−7)3\dfrac{2(-7)}{3}32(−7)​, which is −143-\dfrac{14}{3}−314​, and the second is −7−42\dfrac{-7 - 4}{2}2−7−4​, which is −112-\dfrac{11}{2}−211​.

    Subtracting the second from the first over the common denominator 666 gives

    −143+112=−28+336=56,\begin{aligned} -\frac{14}{3} + \frac{11}{2} &= \frac{-28 + 33}{6} \\ &= \frac{5}{6}, \end{aligned}−314​+211​​=6−28+33​=65​,​

    which matches the right side.

    Answer

    x=−7x = -7x=−7; check: both sides of the original equation equal 56\tfrac{5}{6}65​.

    Key idea

    Multiply every term on both sides by the LCD, keeping each numerator in parentheses, so that a minus sign in front of a fraction reaches its whole numerator.

  2. Problem 2 Decimals that reach the hundredths

    Solve 0.25x+1.5=0.4x−0.90.25x + 1.5 = 0.4x - 0.90.25x+1.5=0.4x−0.9, and check your value in the original equation.

    1. Hint 1

      Every decimal here is a fraction with a power of ten underneath, so one multiplier that is a power of ten can clear all of them at once.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the number that runs furthest past the decimal point and count its places. Each factor of ten moves every digit one place, so that count is how many factors of ten you need.

    3. Hint 3

      Multiply every term on both sides, the constants included. Then collect the xxx terms on the side where their coefficient is larger, so that it stays positive.

    Answer

    x=16x = 16x=16; check: both sides of the original equation equal 5.55.55.5.

    Full solution

    The coefficient 0.250.250.25 runs to the hundredths place, so two factors of ten are needed and the multiplier is 100100100.

    A multiplier of 101010 alone would leave 2.5x+15=4x−92.5x + 15 = 4x - 92.5x+15=4x−9, which still has a decimal coefficient.

    Multiply every term on both sides by 100100100.

    It is a nonzero number, so the solutions do not change:

    25x+150=40x−90.25x + 150 = 40x - 90.25x+150=40x−90.

    The larger coefficient of xxx is on the right, so subtract 25x25x25x from both sides.

    Then add 909090 to both sides and divide both sides by 151515:

    150=15x−90240=15xx=16.\begin{aligned} 150 &= 15x - 90 \\ 240 &= 15x \\ x &= 16. \end{aligned}150240x​=15x−90=15x=16.​

    Check in the original equation.

    The left side is 0.25(16)+1.50.25(16) + 1.50.25(16)+1.5, which is 4+1.54 + 1.54+1.5, or 5.55.55.5.

    The right side is 0.4(16)−0.90.4(16) - 0.90.4(16)−0.9, which is 6.4−0.96.4 - 0.96.4−0.9, or 5.55.55.5.

    The two sides agree.

    Answer

    x=16x = 16x=16; check: both sides of the original equation equal 5.55.55.5.

    Key idea

    To clear decimals, multiply every term by the smallest power of ten that makes each number whole: 100100100 when any of them reaches the hundredths.

  3. Problem 3 Two ratios set equal

    Solve the proportion x−56=x+310\dfrac{x - 5}{6} = \dfrac{x + 3}{10}6x−5​=10x+3​, and check your value in the original equation.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

    3. Hint 3

    Answer

    x=17x = 17x=17; check: both ratios equal 222.

    Full solution

    Subtract 6x6x6x from both sides, add 505050 to both sides, and divide both sides by 444:

    10(x−5)=6(x+3)10x−50=6x+184x−50=184x=68x=17.\begin{aligned} 10(x - 5) &= 6(x + 3) \\ 10x - 50 &= 6x + 18 \\ 4x - 50 &= 18 \\ 4x &= 68 \\ x &= 17. \end{aligned}10(x−5)10x−504x−504xx​=6(x+3)=6x+18=18=68=17.​

    Check in the original proportion.

    At x=17x = 17x=17 the left ratio is 126\dfrac{12}{6}612​, which is 222, and the right ratio is 2010\dfrac{20}{10}1020​, which is also 222.

    The smaller multiplier 303030, the LCD, works too.

    It gives 5(x−5)=3(x+3)5(x - 5) = 3(x + 3)5(x−5)=3(x+3), each side half of the cross-multiplied one, and it leads to the same x=17x = 17x=17.

    Answer

    x=17x = 17x=17; check: both ratios equal 222.

    Key idea

  4. Problem 4 Two equations to unwrap

    For each equation below, find every value of xxx that makes it true.

    0.3(x+4)−0.5x=1.5−0.2x4x−(x−2(5−x))=x+10\begin{aligned} 0.3(x + 4) - 0.5x &= 1.5 - 0.2x \\ 4x - \big(x - 2(5 - x)\big) &= x + 10 \end{aligned}0.3(x+4)−0.5x4x−(x−2(5−x))​=1.5−0.2x=x+10​

    1. Hint 1

      Strip each disguise first, then gather the xxx terms on one side. Look at whether any xxx is left at all, and at what the remaining statement says.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiply every term of the first equation by 101010. In the second, simplify the inner group x−2(5−x)x - 2(5 - x)x−2(5−x) before removing the outer parentheses, and remember that the minus sign in front of them reaches every term inside.

    3. Hint 3

      If the xxx terms cancel, no choice of xxx can change the statement that is left. Neither equation has xxx in a denominator, so a false one means no number works, and a true one means every number does.

    Answer

    The first equation has no solution. Every number satisfies the second, so it has infinitely many solutions.

    Full solution

    In the first equation every number reaches the tenths place, so multiply every term on both sides by 101010, a nonzero number.

    The 101010 turns 0.3(x+4)0.3(x + 4)0.3(x+4) into 3(x+4)3(x + 4)3(x+4).

    Then expand and combine:

    3(x+4)−5x=15−2x3x+12−5x=15−2x12−2x=15−2x.\begin{aligned} 3(x + 4) - 5x &= 15 - 2x \\ 3x + 12 - 5x &= 15 - 2x \\ 12 - 2x &= 15 - 2x. \end{aligned}3(x+4)−5x3x+12−5x12−2x​=15−2x=15−2x=15−2x.​

    Adding 2x2x2x to both sides removes the variable from both sides at once and leaves 12=1512 = 1512=15, which is false.

    No value of xxx can change a statement with no xxx in it, so the first equation has no solution.

    Testing agrees: at x=0x = 0x=0 the sides are 1.21.21.2 and 1.51.51.5, and at x=10x = 10x=10 they are −0.8-0.8−0.8 and −0.5-0.5−0.5.

    In the second equation, work from the innermost parentheses outward.

    The product −2(5−x)-2(5 - x)−2(5−x) is −10+2x-10 + 2x−10+2x, so the inner group x−2(5−x)x - 2(5 - x)x−2(5−x) simplifies to 3x−103x - 103x−10.

    Subtracting that whole group from 4x4x4x flips both of its terms:

    4x−(3x−10)=x+10x+10=x+10.\begin{aligned} 4x - (3x - 10) &= x + 10 \\ x + 10 &= x + 10. \end{aligned}4x−(3x−10)x+10​=x+10=x+10.​

    The two sides are the same expression.

    Subtracting xxx from both sides leaves 10=1010 = 1010=10, which is true whatever xxx is, so every number satisfies the second equation.

    Testing agrees: at x=0x = 0x=0 both sides are 101010, and at x=1x = 1x=1 both sides are 111111.

    Answer

    The first equation has no solution. Every number satisfies the second, so it has infinitely many solutions.

    Key idea

    When the xxx terms cancel after clearing with nonzero numbers only, the statement left behind decides: false means no solution, and true means every number is a solution.

  5. Problem 5 Two equations over x+3x + 3x+3

    Each equation below rules out one value of xxx. For each, name that value before doing any algebra, then solve the equation and say what becomes of the value you find.

    1x+3+2=x+5x+32x+3+2=x+5x+3\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{x + 3} + 2 &= \frac{x + 5}{x + 3} \\ \frac{2}{x + 3} + 2 &= \frac{x + 5}{x + 3} \end{aligned}x+31​+2x+32​+2​=x+3x+5​=x+3x+5​​

    1. Hint 1

      A denominator can never be zero, so a value of xxx that makes x+3x + 3x+3 zero cannot be a solution. Find it first, and keep it in view until the end.

    2. Hint 2

      The left side is a sum, so cross-multiplication is not available. Multiply every term on both sides by x+3x + 3x+3 instead, the constant 222 included.

    3. Hint 3

      Each cleared equation is an ordinary linear equation. Compare the value it gives with the one you ruled out at the start, and ask whether the original equation says anything at that value.

    Answer

    Both rule out x=−3x = -3x=−3. The first has the solution x=−2x = -2x=−2. The second clears to the candidate x=−3x = -3x=−3, the ruled-out value, so the candidate is rejected and that equation has no solution.

    Full solution

    Both equations have the denominator x+3x + 3x+3, which is zero at x=−3x = -3x=−3.

    So x=−3x = -3x=−3 is excluded from both before any algebra starts.

    In the first equation the left side is a sum, so multiply every term on both sides by x+3x + 3x+3.

    The fractions lose their denominators and the 222 becomes 2(x+3)2(x + 3)2(x+3):

    1+2(x+3)=x+52x+7=x+5x+7=5x=−2.\begin{aligned} 1 + 2(x + 3) &= x + 5 \\ 2x + 7 &= x + 5 \\ x + 7 &= 5 \\ x &= -2. \end{aligned}1+2(x+3)2x+7x+7x​=x+5=x+5=5=−2.​

    The value −2-2−2 is not the excluded −3-3−3, so it stands.

    Checking in the original, x+3x + 3x+3 is 111 at x=−2x = -2x=−2, so the left side is 11+2\tfrac{1}{1} + 211​+2, which is 333, and the right side is 31\tfrac{3}{1}13​, which is also 333.

    In the second equation the same multiplication gives

    2+2(x+3)=x+52x+8=x+5x+8=5x=−3.\begin{aligned} 2 + 2(x + 3) &= x + 5 \\ 2x + 8 &= x + 5 \\ x + 8 &= 5 \\ x &= -3. \end{aligned}2+2(x+3)2x+8x+8x​=x+5=x+5=5=−3.​

    The only candidate is −3-3−3, exactly the value ruled out at the start.

    Putting it into the original asks for 20\tfrac{2}{0}02​, which names no number, so the candidate is rejected.

    Any genuine solution of the original would also satisfy the cleared equation, because x+3x + 3x+3 is a nonzero number at every allowed value.

    The cleared equation has no other solution, so the second equation has no solution at all.

    Answer

    Both rule out x=−3x = -3x=−3. The first has the solution x=−2x = -2x=−2. The second clears to the candidate x=−3x = -3x=−3, the ruled-out value, so the candidate is rejected and that equation has no solution.

    Key idea

    With a variable in a denominator, name the excluded value first; a candidate that lands on it is thrown away, and if no other candidate remains, the equation has no solution.

    A verdict read off a cleared equation is the original's too when every multiplier was a nonzero number; after clearing with an expression that holds the variable, remove the excluded values from it.

  10. Problem 10 Lena's claim

    Lena claims that multiplying both sides of an equation by the same expression always gives an equation with exactly the same solutions.

    Test her claim on 2x−5x−6=7x−6\dfrac{2x - 5}{x - 6} = \dfrac{7}{x - 6}x−62x−5​=x−67​: name the value of xxx that the original rules out, multiply both sides by x−6x - 6x−6 and solve the new equation, and compare the solutions of the two equations. Then state a condition on the multiplier under which Lena's claim is true.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the two equations value by value. They can disagree only at a value where the multiplier does something that a nonzero number cannot do.

    2. Hint 2

      The original equation says nothing at a value that makes its denominator zero. Solve the new equation, and ask whether its solution is such a value.

    3. Hint 3

      For the condition, think about which multipliers can be undone by dividing both sides by them, at every value of xxx.

    Answer

    The original rules out x=6x = 6x=6 and has no solution. The new equation 2x−5=72x - 5 = 72x−5=7 has the solution x=6x = 6x=6, so the solutions differ and the claim is false. It holds when the multiplier is nonzero at every value of xxx.

    Full solution

    The denominator x−6x - 6x−6 is zero at x=6x = 6x=6, so the original equation rules out x=6x = 6x=6.

    Multiplying both sides by x−6x - 6x−6 cancels both denominators and leaves the new equation, which solves as follows:

    2x−5=72x=12x=6.\begin{aligned} 2x - 5 &= 7 \\ 2x &= 12 \\ x &= 6. \end{aligned}2x−52xx​=7=12=6.​

    So x=6x = 6x=6 satisfies the new equation, since

    2(6)−5=7.2(6) - 5 = 7.2(6)−5=7.

    In the original, x=6x = 6x=6 makes both denominators zero, so each side asks for a division by zero and names no number.

    The value 666 is a solution of the new equation and not of the original, so the two equations have different solutions and Lena's claim is false.

    The original in fact has no solution, because its only candidate is the excluded value.

    The failure happened exactly where the multiplier is zero.

    Canceling x−6x - 6x−6 against each denominator is valid only where x−6x - 6x−6 is not zero.

    The new equation has no denominator, so it makes a statement at x=6x = 6x=6, where the original makes none, and there 2(6)−5=72(6) - 5 = 72(6)−5=7 is true.

    Undoing the step would mean dividing both sides by x−6x - 6x−6, which is impossible at x=6x = 6x=6, so there the step cannot be reversed.

    At any other value, x−6x - 6x−6 is a nonzero number, and dividing both sides of the new equation by it returns the original.

    The step also cannot lose a solution.

    A number that solves the original makes every denominator nonzero, so there x−6x - 6x−6 is a nonzero number, and multiplying both sides of a true equality by a nonzero number keeps it true.

    So the only change the step can make is to add a value where the multiplier is zero.

    Lena's claim is therefore true whenever the multiplier is nonzero at every value of xxx.

    A nonzero number, such as the LCD used to clear numerical fractions, meets that condition.

    A multiplier that holds the variable need not, which is why its excluded values have to be checked.

    Answer

    The original rules out x=6x = 6x=6 and has no solution. The new equation 2x−5=72x - 5 = 72x−5=7 has the solution x=6x = 6x=6, so the solutions differ and the claim is false. It holds when the multiplier is nonzero at every value of xxx.

    Key idea

    Clearing a denominator that holds the variable keeps every solution of the original but can add the value that makes the denominator zero, so each candidate must be checked against the excluded values.
