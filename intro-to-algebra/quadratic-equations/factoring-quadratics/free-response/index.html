    1. Reading the signs before the search . Foundational, 10 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 10 pts

    Every trinomial in this lesson gives away part of its answer before you search for a single number: the signs of bbb and ccc tell you what kind of pair you are hunting for. This question asks you to factor two trinomials completely, and then say what the signs told you in advance.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Factor x2−2x−24x^2 - 2x - 24x2−2x−24 completely.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Factor completely 3x2+27x+543x^2 + 27x + 543x2+27x+54.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Without finding either pair of numbers again, explain how the signs of bbb and ccc in each trinomial above told you, before you searched, whether the two numbers would share a sign or have opposite signs.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Before hunting for any pair of numbers, look only at the signs of bbb and ccc. The sign of ccc tells you whether the two numbers share a sign; the sign of bbb then tells you which sign, or which one is larger when the signs are opposite.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      For part A, ccc is negative, so list pairs of integers that multiply to −24-24−24 with one positive and one negative, and check which pair adds to −2-2−2.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part B

      For part B, factor out the greatest common numerical factor first. Once it is out front, the trinomial left inside behaves exactly like the ones from part A, just with different numbers.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    x2−2x−24=(x−6)(x+4)x^2 - 2x - 24 = (x - 6)(x + 4)x2−2x−24=(x−6)(x+4).

    Part B

    3x2+27x+54=3(x+3)(x+6)3x^2 + 27x + 54 = 3(x + 3)(x + 6)3x2+27x+54=3(x+3)(x+6).

    Part C

    In part A, c=−24c = -24c=−24 is negative, so the two numbers had to have opposite signs. In part B (after dividing by 333), c=18c = 18c=18 is positive, so the two numbers had to share a sign, and since b=9b = 9b=9 is positive that shared sign was positive.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The product c=−24c = -24c=−24 is negative, so the two numbers have opposite signs, and the sum b=−2b = -2b=−2 is negative, so the larger one (by size) is negative. List the pairs of integers that multiply to −24-24−24 and check their sums.

    (−6)(4)=−24,−6+4=−2.(-6)(4) = -24, \quad -6 + 4 = -2.(−6)(4)=−24,−6+4=−2.

    That pair fits, so

    x2−2x−24=(x−6)(x+4).x^2 - 2x - 24 = (x - 6)(x + 4).x2−2x−24=(x−6)(x+4).

    Expanding checks it: (x−6)(x+4)=x2+4x−6x−24=x2−2x−24(x - 6)(x + 4) = x^2 + 4x - 6x - 24 = x^2 - 2x - 24(x−6)(x+4)=x2+4x−6x−24=x2−2x−24.

    Part B

    Every coefficient is divisible by 333, so pull that factor out first.

    3x2+27x+54=3(x2+9x+18).3x^2 + 27x + 54 = 3\left(x^2 + 9x + 18\right).3x2+27x+54=3(x2+9x+18).

    Inside the parentheses, c=18c = 18c=18 is positive and b=9b = 9b=9 is positive, so both numbers are positive. Two positive numbers that multiply to 181818 and add to 999 are 333 and 666:

    x2+9x+18=(x+3)(x+6).x^2 + 9x + 18 = (x + 3)(x + 6).x2+9x+18=(x+3)(x+6).

    So the complete factorization is 3(x+3)(x+6)3(x + 3)(x + 6)3(x+3)(x+6); the 333 pulled out front is part of the answer, not a step you can drop.

    Part C

    The sign of ccc decides whether the two numbers share a sign, because ccc is their product.

    c=pq.c = pq.c=pq.

    A product of two numbers is negative only when the numbers have opposite signs, and positive only when they share a sign (both positive or both negative). In part A, c=−24<0c = -24 < 0c=−24<0 forced opposite signs. Inside part B's parentheses, c=18>0c = 18 > 0c=18>0 forced a shared sign. Once a shared sign is guaranteed, the sign of b=p+qb = p + qb=p+q decides which one: two numbers with the same sign add to a sum of that same sign, so b=9>0b = 9 > 0b=9>0 meant the shared sign was positive.

    In one line

    x2−2x−24=(x−6)(x+4)x^2 - 2x - 24 = (x - 6)(x + 4)x2−2x−24=(x−6)(x+4) and 3x2+27x+54=3(x+3)(x+6)3x^2 + 27x + 54 = 3(x + 3)(x + 6)3x2+27x+54=3(x+3)(x+6); the sign of ccc decided whether the two numbers shared a sign or were opposite, and the sign of bbb then decided which sign a shared pair took, or which number was larger when the signs were opposite.

    2. The pair that came out with the sign flipped . Reasoning, 11 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Reasoning 11 pts

    Reading a root straight off a factor, without flipping its sign, is the single most common slip in this method. This question asks you to catch that slip happening on one particular trinomial.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Factor x2+4x−21x^2 + 4x - 21x2+4x−21 completely, then use the zero-product property to solve x2+4x−21=0x^2 + 4x - 21 = 0x2+4x−21=0. Report both roots.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 4 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Reading the two numbers inside your factorization from part A, with their signs exactly as written in the factors (not flipped), gives a pair of values that is easy to mistake for the solution set of x2+4x−21=0x^2 + 4x - 21 = 0x2+4x−21=0. Explain precisely why that pair is wrong, and state the actual roots.

      Carry your own answer forward Use the two numbers exactly as they appear in your own factorization from part A, whatever they turned out to be.

      Find and correct the error Say which line first goes wrong, why it is wrong, and then do it correctly. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      In general, if a monic trinomial factors as (x+p)(x+q)(x + p)(x + q)(x+p)(x+q), explain in a sentence or two why the roots of the corresponding equation are −p-p−p and −q-q−q rather than ppp and qqq.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Two different numbers are hiding in this trinomial: the two numbers you search for when factoring, and the two roots of the equation. They are not the same numbers, and mixing them up is the trap this whole question is built around.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      Read the signs before you search: with ccc negative the two numbers you need have opposite signs, and the positive sign goes with whichever one is larger.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part B

      Look at exactly what the zero-product property does to a factor like x+7x + 7x+7: it does not report the number 777, it solves the equation x+7=0x + 7 = 0x+7=0.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    x2+4x−21=(x+7)(x−3)x^2 + 4x - 21 = (x + 7)(x - 3)x2+4x−21=(x+7)(x−3), so x=−7x = -7x=−7 or x=3x = 3x=3.

    Part B

    Reading the numbers inside the factors, with their signs unflipped, skips the zero-product step. Each factor (x+p)(x + p)(x+p) equals zero at x=−px = -px=−p, not x=px = px=p, so that pair is always the opposite-sign version of the true roots, which here are x=−7x = -7x=−7 or x=3x = 3x=3.

    Part C

    Because the zero-product property sets each FACTOR to zero, not each number inside it: x+p=0x + p = 0x+p=0 solves to x=−px = -px=−p, so the root is always the opposite of the number written in its factor, never the number itself.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The product c=−21c = -21c=−21 is negative, so the two numbers have opposite signs, and the sum b=4b = 4b=4 is positive, so the larger one is positive. Test integer pairs multiplying to −21-21−21:

    (7)(−3)=−21,7+(−3)=4.(7)(-3) = -21, \quad 7 + (-3) = 4.(7)(−3)=−21,7+(−3)=4.

    That pair fits, so

    x2+4x−21=(x+7)(x−3)=0.x^2 + 4x - 21 = (x + 7)(x - 3) = 0.x2+4x−21=(x+7)(x−3)=0.

    By the zero-product property, x+7=0x + 7 = 0x+7=0 or x−3=0x - 3 = 0x−3=0, so x=−7x = -7x=−7 or x=3x = 3x=3. Each root is the OPPOSITE sign of the number written in its factor.

    Part B

    The factorization from part A names two numbers, ppp and qqq, written inside the factors. Copying those two numbers directly, signs unchanged, is NOT the same step as solving the equation.

    The zero-product property sets each FACTOR equal to 000, not each number inside it:

    x+7=0orx−3=0,x + 7 = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x - 3 = 0,x+7=0orx−3=0,

    which gives x=−7x = -7x=−7 or x=3x = 3x=3, the opposite of each number written in its factor. Copying the factor numbers directly instead would report x=7x = 7x=7 and x=−3x = -3x=−3, a pair with both signs flipped from the true roots. That pair does not satisfy the equation; only x=−7x = -7x=−7 and x=3x = 3x=3 do.

    Part C

    The factorization (x+p)(x+q)=0(x + p)(x + q) = 0(x+p)(x+q)=0 names two numbers, ppp and qqq, but the equation is solved by asking what value of xxx makes each FACTOR equal to 000, not by reading off ppp and qqq themselves.

    x+p=0⟹x=−p,x + p = 0 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x = -p,x+p=0⟹x=−p,

    and the same reasoning gives x=−qx = -qx=−q from the other factor. The sign flip is not a memorized rule bolted onto the method; it falls straight out of solving x+p=0x + p = 0x+p=0 for xxx, and it happens for every value of ppp, not just the ones that appear in any one example.

    In one line

    x2+4x−21=(x+7)(x−3)=0x^2 + 4x - 21 = (x + 7)(x - 3) = 0x2+4x−21=(x+7)(x−3)=0 gives x=−7x = -7x=−7 or x=3x = 3x=3; reading the factor numbers directly instead gives the wrong, sign-flipped pair, because the zero-product property solves each factor for 000, not the number inside it; and in general (x+p)(x+q)=0(x+p)(x+q) = 0(x+p)(x+q)=0 always gives roots −p-p−p and −q-q−q, never ppp and qqq.

    3. Two trinomials, one digit apart . Foundational, 9 points. Question 3 of 5.

    Foundational 9 pts

    Two trinomials in this question look almost identical, changed by only one digit in the constant term. The search for two numbers behaves differently on each one, and this question asks you to carry out that search honestly on both and report exactly what you find.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Attempt to factor x2+6x+10x^2 + 6x + 10x2+6x+10 over the integers: list the pairs of integers whose product is 101010, check each pair's sum against 666, and state your conclusion about whether the trinomial factors over the integers.

      Solve and show your work Write each step out, and end with the value and its units. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Factor x2+6x+9x^2 + 6x + 9x2+6x+9 completely.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Use the factorization from part B to solve x2+6x+9=0x^2 + 6x + 9 = 0x2+6x+9=0 by the zero-product property, and say how many DIFFERENT solutions the equation has.

      Carry your own answer forward Solve using whichever factorization you found in part B, even if the two factors were not identical: set each factor equal to zero the same way you would for any factored equation.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Two very similar-looking trinomials are hiding two different lessons here: one shows what it looks like when the search genuinely fails, and the other shows that the two numbers you are searching for are allowed to be equal to each other.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      Since c=10c = 10c=10 is positive, only pairs of the SAME sign are worth checking. There are only two ways to write 101010 as a product of two positive whole numbers; try both before you decide.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      The zero-product property does not require the two factor equations to be different from each other. Solve each one honestly and see what happens when they turn out to say the same thing.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    No integer pair works: (1,10)(1,10)(1,10) sums to 111111, (2,5)(2,5)(2,5) sums to 777, and the negative pairs give negative sums. So x2+6x+10x^2 + 6x + 10x2+6x+10 does not factor over the integers.

    Part B

    x2+6x+9=(x+3)(x+3)x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)(x + 3)x2+6x+9=(x+3)(x+3).

    Part C

    Both factors give x=−3x = -3x=−3, so the equation has exactly one solution, x=−3x = -3x=−3, even though the zero-product property produces two factor equations. It is one root counted twice, not two different solutions.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The product c=10c = 10c=10 is positive, so any working pair would share a sign, matching the sign of b=6b = 6b=6: both positive. List the positive pairs that multiply to 101010 and check their sums.

    1×10=10(sum 11),2×5=10(sum 7).1 \times 10 = 10 \left(\text{sum } 11\right), \quad 2 \times 5 = 10 \left(\text{sum } 7\right).1×10=10(sum 11),2×5=10(sum 7).

    Those are the only two ways to write 101010 as a product of positive integers, and neither sums to 666. Since c>0c > 0c>0 rules out a mixed-sign pair entirely, no integer pair has sum 666 and product 101010, so x2+6x+10x^2 + 6x + 10x2+6x+10 does not factor over the integers.

    Part B

    Here c=9c = 9c=9 is positive and b=6b = 6b=6 is positive, so both numbers are positive. Two positive numbers with product 999 and sum 666 are 333 and 333:

    3×3=9,3+3=6.3 \times 3 = 9, \quad 3 + 3 = 6.3×3=9,3+3=6.

    So x2+6x+9=(x+3)(x+3)x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)(x + 3)x2+6x+9=(x+3)(x+3). Nothing in the method requires the two numbers to be different from each other.

    Part C

    Set each factor from part B equal to 000:

    x+3=0orx+3=0,x + 3 = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x + 3 = 0,x+3=0orx+3=0,

    and both give the same value, x=−3x = -3x=−3. The zero-product property does not promise two DIFFERENT solutions; it only promises that the product is zero when at least one factor is. Here both factors happen to be the same, so the method still runs correctly, it simply lands on one value instead of two. Saying the equation has a repeated root, one solution counted twice, is accurate; saying it has two different solutions is not.

    In one line

    x2+6x+10x^2 + 6x + 10x2+6x+10 has no integer pair with sum 666 and product 101010, so it does not factor over the integers; x2+6x+9=(x+3)(x+3)x^2 + 6x + 9 = (x+3)(x+3)x2+6x+9=(x+3)(x+3) factors with a repeated pair, and solving it gives the single solution x=−3x = -3x=−3, one root counted twice rather than two different solutions.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The claim is about every integer pair with a negative product, so the argument has to rule out every same-sign case, not check just one example.

    pq=c<0.pq = c < 0.pq=c<0.

    Since pq≠0pq \neq 0pq=0, neither ppp nor qqq is 000. That leaves the two same-sign cases to rule out, plus the mixed case.

    If p>0p > 0p>0 and q>0q > 0q>0, a positive times a positive is positive, so pq>0pq > 0pq>0, contradicting pq<0pq < 0pq<0.

    If p<0p < 0p<0 and q<0q < 0q<0, a negative times a negative is also positive, so again pq>0pq > 0pq>0, the same contradiction.

    Both same-sign cases are impossible, so the only possibility left is that one of p,qp, qp,q is positive and the other is negative: opposite signs.

    Part B

    By part A, ppp and qqq have opposite signs. Let the one with the larger absolute value have size mmm and the other have size nnn, with m>n>0m > n > 0m>n>0 (if their absolute values were equal the sum would be 000, which has no sign and leaves the claim with nothing to assert, so that case is set aside rather than ruled out: p=3p = 3p=3, q=−3q = -3q=−3 is a genuine pair of that kind).

    b=p+q=m−n.b = p + q = m - n.b=p+q=m−n.

    Since m>nm > nm>n, this is positive, matching the sign of the larger number.

    Case 2: the larger one is negative. Then p=−mp = -mp=−m and q=nq = nq=n, so

    b=p+q=−m+n=−(m−n),b = p + q = -m + n = -(m - n),b=p+q=−m+n=−(m−n),

    which is negative since m>nm > nm>n, again matching the sign of the larger number.

    Both cases give bbb the same sign as whichever of p,qp, qp,q has the larger absolute value, which is the claim.

    Part C

    From the coefficients, b=−5b = -5b=−5 and c=−36c = -36c=−36. Since c<0c < 0c<0, part A says the two numbers have opposite signs.
