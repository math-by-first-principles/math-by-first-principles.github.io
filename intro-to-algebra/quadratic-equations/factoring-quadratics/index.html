Factoring Quadratics

Intuition

In the last lesson you were handed a quadratic that was already factored, something like (x−2)(x+3)=0(x - 2)(x + 3) = 0(x−2)(x+3)=0, and the zero-product property finished it in a single line. Real quadratics rarely arrive so helpfully. They come as x2+bx+c=0x^2 + bx + c = 0x2+bx+c=0, with the roots buried inside a trinomial and no product in sight. Factoring is the move that digs them out. It rewrites the trinomial as a product of two simple factors, turning the equation back into the friendly form the zero-product property can crack. Because factoring simply undoes expanding, everything you need is already in your hands. This lesson turns that one idea into a reliable method for any trinomial whose squared term is a plain x2x^2x2.

Learning goals

  • Find two numbers with sum bbb and product ccc
  • Read the signs from ccc first, then from bbb
  • Solve by factoring and the zero-product property
  • Pull out a common factor, or a shared xxx, first
  • Check by expanding the factors back
  • Call a trinomial irreducible when no integer pair fits

Factoring reverses expanding

To factor a trinomial is to write it as a product of factors, the exact reverse of expanding a product into a trinomial. Since the two operations undo each other, the way to discover the factors is to look closely at what expanding produces. In the chapter on expanding you multiplied two simple factors of the form x+px + px+p and x+qx + qx+q and got

(x+p)(x+q)=x2+(p+q)x+pq.(x + p)(x + q) = x^2 + (p + q)x + pq.(x+p)(x+q)=x2+(p+q)x+pq.

Read that equation from right to left and it stops being a multiplication rule and becomes a factoring recipe.

Why factoring x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + cx2+bx+c means finding two numbers with sum bbb and product ccc#

Start by expanding a general product of the two factors x+px + px+p and x+qx + qx+q, multiplying every term of the first by every term of the second:

(x+p)(x+q)=x2+qx+px+pq=x2+(p+q)x+pq.(x + p)(x + q) = x^2 + qx + px + pq = x^2 + (p + q)x + pq.(x+p)(x+q)=x2+qx+px+pq=x2+(p+q)x+pq.

Now suppose this product is the trinomial x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + cx2+bx+c you are trying to factor. Two expressions in xxx are equal for every value of xxx only when they match term by term. So line up the coefficient of xxx and the constant term on each side:

p+q=bandpq=c.p + q = b \qquad \text{and} \qquad pq = c.p+q=bandpq=c.

That is the whole secret of the method. To factor x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + cx2+bx+c you need two numbers, ppp and qqq, that add up to bbb and multiply to ccc. Find that pair and the factorization is (x+p)(x+q)(x + p)(x + q)(x+p)(x+q), with nothing left to guess. Notice also that the squared term on the left is a plain x2x^2x2 with coefficient 111, and the expansion produces an x2x^2x2 with coefficient 111 to match. That match is exactly why this recipe fits a trinomial that begins with x2x^2x2 and no number in front.

∎

The picture behind the recipe is the same rectangle you drew for expanding, read the other way. Give a rectangle a width of x+px + px+p and a height of x+qx + qx+q. The two splits carve it into four pieces whose areas are x2x^2x2, pxpxpx, qxqxqx, and the small corner pqpqpq.

The two strips pxpxpx and qxqxqx together make the middle term, since they combine to (p+q)x(p + q)x(p+q)x, and the corner pqpqpq is the constant. So matching the middle term of a trinomial pins down p+qp + qp+q, and matching the constant pins down pqpqpq. Everything below is just carrying out that search.

Finding the two numbers

The recipe turns factoring into a small, orderly search. Run through the pairs of integers that multiply to ccc, and pick the pair that also adds to bbb. The signs do much of the work of narrowing that search, so read them before you start listing.

Because c=pqc = pqc=pq is the product of the two numbers, its sign tells you whether they share a sign. When ccc is positive, ppp and qqq must have the same sign, because only two positives or two negatives multiply to a positive. In that case the pair’s sum bbb tells you which sign they share: both positive when bbb is positive, and both negative when bbb is negative. When ccc is negative, the two numbers must have opposite signs, because a positive times a negative is negative. When the signs are opposite, the number with the larger size carries the sign of bbb. Reading the signs this way cuts the list of candidate pairs roughly in half before you add anything.

Worked example 1 Factor x2+7x+12x^2 + 7x + 12x2+7x+12

Here b=7b = 7b=7 and c=12c = 12c=12. The product 121212 is positive and the sum 777 is positive, so both numbers are positive. List the positive pairs that multiply to 121212 and check which one adds to 777:

1⋅12=12 (sum 13),2⋅6=12 (sum 8),3⋅4=12 (sum 7).1 \cdot 12 = 12\ (\text{sum } 13), \quad 2 \cdot 6 = 12\ (\text{sum } 8), \quad 3 \cdot 4 = 12\ (\text{sum } 7).1⋅12=12 (sum 13),2⋅6=12 (sum 8),3⋅4=12 (sum 7).

The pair 333 and 444 has the sum you want, so those are ppp and qqq:

x2+7x+12=(x+3)(x+4).x^2 + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4).x2+7x+12=(x+3)(x+4).

Check by expanding the answer back: (x+3)(x+4)=x2+4x+3x+12=x2+7x+12(x + 3)(x + 4) = x^2 + 4x + 3x + 12 = x^2 + 7x + 12(x+3)(x+4)=x2+4x+3x+12=x2+7x+12, the original.

Worked example 2 Factor x2−9x+20x^2 - 9x + 20x2−9x+20

Now b=−9b = -9b=−9 and c=20c = 20c=20. The product 202020 is positive, so the two numbers share a sign, and the sum −9-9−9 is negative, so both are negative. List the negative pairs multiplying to 202020:

(−1)(−20) (sum −21),(−2)(−10) (sum −12),(−4)(−5) (sum −9).(-1)(-20)\ (\text{sum } -21), \quad (-2)(-10)\ (\text{sum } -12), \quad (-4)(-5)\ (\text{sum } -9).(−1)(−20) (sum −21),(−2)(−10) (sum −12),(−4)(−5) (sum −9).

The pair −4-4−4 and −5-5−5 works, so

x2−9x+20=(x−4)(x−5).x^2 - 9x + 20 = (x - 4)(x - 5).x2−9x+20=(x−4)(x−5).

Expanding confirms it: (x−4)(x−5)=x2−5x−4x+20=x2−9x+20(x - 4)(x - 5) = x^2 - 5x - 4x + 20 = x^2 - 9x + 20(x−4)(x−5)=x2−5x−4x+20=x2−9x+20.

Worked example 3 Factor x2+2x−15x^2 + 2x - 15x2+2x−15, then x2−2x−15x^2 - 2x - 15x2−2x−15

Both trinomials have c=−15c = -15c=−15, which is negative, so in each the two numbers have opposite signs. The size pairs that multiply to 151515 are 111 and 151515 or 333 and 555. Of those two pairs, only 333 and 555 can be arranged to give a sum of size 222.

For x2+2x−15x^2 + 2x - 15x2+2x−15 the sum is +2+2+2, so the larger number is positive: take +5+5+5 and −3-3−3, since 5+(−3)=25 + (-3) = 25+(−3)=2 and 5⋅(−3)=−155 \cdot (-3) = -155⋅(−3)=−15:

x2+2x−15=(x+5)(x−3).x^2 + 2x - 15 = (x + 5)(x - 3).x2+2x−15=(x+5)(x−3).

For x2−2x−15x^2 - 2x - 15x2−2x−15 the sum is −2-2−2, so the larger number is negative: take −5-5−5 and +3+3+3, since −5+3=−2-5 + 3 = -2−5+3=−2 and (−5)(3)=−15(-5)(3) = -15(−5)(3)=−15:

x2−2x−15=(x−5)(x+3).x^2 - 2x - 15 = (x - 5)(x + 3).x2−2x−15=(x−5)(x+3).

The same size pair {3,5}\{3, 5\}{3,5} handles both trinomials; only the sign on the larger number flips to match the sign of bbb.

The method does not care if bbb happens to be 000. To factor x2−9x^2 - 9x2−9, read it as x2+0x−9x^2 + 0x - 9x2+0x−9 and look for two numbers with sum 000 and product −9-9−9. Opposite numbers of the same size add to 000, and 333 and −3-3−3 multiply to −9-9−9, so x2−9=(x+3)(x−3)x^2 - 9 = (x + 3)(x - 3)x2−9=(x+3)(x−3). A missing middle term simply means the two numbers are opposites.

Answer choices

The product c=15c = 15c=15 is positive and the sum b=8b = 8b=8 is positive, so both numbers are positive. Look for a positive pair that multiplies to 151515 and adds to 888.

3+5=8and3⋅5=153 + 5 = 8 \quad \text{and} \quad 3 \cdot 5 = 153+5=8and3⋅5=15

So x2+8x+15=(x+3)(x+5)x^2 + 8x + 15 = (x + 3)(x + 5)x2+8x+15=(x+3)(x+5). The pair 111 and 151515 multiplies to 151515 but adds to 161616, and the negative pairs give the wrong sign on the middle term.

Solving a quadratic by factoring

Factoring is worth the effort because it feeds straight into the zero-product property from the last lesson. To solve x2+bx+c=0x^2 + bx + c = 0x2+bx+c=0, factor the left side into (x+p)(x+q)(x + p)(x + q)(x+p)(x+q) and set the product to zero:

(x+p)(x+q)=0.(x + p)(x + q) = 0.(x+p)(x+q)=0.

A product is zero only when one of its factors is zero, so x+p=0x + p = 0x+p=0 or x+q=0x + q = 0x+q=0, which gives x=−px = -px=−p or x=−qx = -qx=−q. Watch the sign flip one more time: the number that sits inside a factor as +p+p+p becomes the root −p-p−p. A factor and the root it produces always have opposite signs, and that is where a careless solve most often goes wrong.

Worked example 4 Solve x2+x−6=0x^2 + x - 6 = 0x2+x−6=0

First factor the left side. With b=1b = 1b=1 and c=−6c = -6c=−6, the product is negative, so the two numbers have opposite signs, and they must add to 111. The pair 333 and −2-2−2 works, since 3+(−2)=13 + (-2) = 13+(−2)=1 and 3⋅(−2)=−63 \cdot (-2) = -63⋅(−2)=−6:

x2+x−6=(x+3)(x−2)=0.x^2 + x - 6 = (x + 3)(x - 2) = 0.x2+x−6=(x+3)(x−2)=0.

By the zero-product property, set each factor to 000:

x+3=0orx−2=0,x + 3 = 0 \qquad \text{or} \qquad x - 2 = 0,x+3=0orx−2=0,

so x=−3x = -3x=−3 or x=2x = 2x=2. Two checks are available, and it is worth using them. Substituting confirms the roots, since (−3)2+(−3)−6=9−3−6=0(-3)^2 + (-3) - 6 = 9 - 3 - 6 = 0(−3)2+(−3)−6=9−3−6=0 and 22+2−6=02^2 + 2 - 6 = 022+2−6=0. Expanding the factors also rebuilds the original, since (x+3)(x−2)=x2+x−6(x + 3)(x - 2) = x^2 + x - 6(x+3)(x−2)=x2+x−6. Either check catches a slip.

Check your understanding

Solve x2−3x−10=0x^2 - 3x - 10 = 0x2−3x−10=0 by factoring.

Answer choices

The product c=−10c = -10c=−10 is negative, so the two numbers have opposite signs, and their sum is −3-3−3. A product of −10-10−10 and a sum of −3-3−3 come from −5-5−5 and 222.

x2−3x−10=(x−5)(x+2)=0x^2 - 3x - 10 = (x - 5)(x + 2) = 0x2−3x−10=(x−5)(x+2)=0

Setting each factor to 000 gives x=5x = 5x=5 or x=−2x = -2x=−2. Mind the sign flip: the factor x−5x - 5x−5 yields the root +5+5+5, and x+2x + 2x+2 yields −2-2−2.

Pulling out a common factor first

Two setups look harder than they are, and both begin with a factor you pull out before the sum-and-product search even starts.

The first is a trinomial whose three coefficients share a common numerical factor, such as 2x2+6x+42x^2 + 6x + 42x2+6x+4. Its leading coefficient is not 111, so the recipe does not apply directly. But every term is even, so factor the 222 out front first:

2x2+6x+4=2(x2+3x+2).2x^2 + 6x + 4 = 2(x^2 + 3x + 2).2x2+6x+4=2(x2+3x+2).

Inside the parentheses the leading coefficient is back to 111. The sum-and-product method now finishes the job: two numbers with sum 333 and product 222 are 111 and 222, so

2(x2+3x+2)=2(x+1)(x+2).2(x^2 + 3x + 2) = 2(x + 1)(x + 2).2(x2+3x+2)=2(x+1)(x+2).

Always pull out a common numerical factor first, even when it will not leave a monic trinomial behind. It shrinks the numbers you have to work with, and here, because the factor pulled out equals the whole leading coefficient, it also restores a leading coefficient of 111, so the method above can finish the job. Check that leading coefficient after every pull: if it is back to 111, the method above applies; if a leading coefficient greater than 111 survives instead, a later lesson picks up from exactly that case.

The second setup is a trinomial with no constant term, like x2−5xx^2 - 5xx2−5x. Here c=0c = 0c=0, and instead of hunting for a pair you simply pull out the shared xxx:

x2−5x=x(x−5).x^2 - 5x = x(x - 5).x2−5x=x(x−5).

Every term contains an xxx, so xxx is the common factor, and a single linear factor is left. Solving x2−5x=0x^2 - 5x = 0x2−5x=0 then gives x=0x = 0x=0 or x=5x = 5x=5. The root x=0x = 0x=0 is the one most often lost, because dividing both sides by xxx quietly throws it away. Keep the factor rather than dividing, and both roots survive.

Worked example 5 Solve 3x2−6x−24=03x^2 - 6x - 24 = 03x2−6x−24=0

Every coefficient is a multiple of 333, so pull out the 333 first:

3x2−6x−24=3(x2−2x−8)=0.3x^2 - 6x - 24 = 3(x^2 - 2x - 8) = 0.3x2−6x−24=3(x2−2x−8)=0.

Now factor the monic trinomial inside. With sum −2-2−2 and product −8-8−8, the numbers are −4-4−4 and 222, since −4+2=−2-4 + 2 = -2−4+2=−2 and (−4)(2)=−8(-4)(2) = -8(−4)(2)=−8:

3(x−4)(x+2)=0.3(x - 4)(x + 2) = 0.3(x−4)(x+2)=0.

The constant 333 can never equal 000, so it contributes no root; the product is zero exactly when one of the variable factors is. Setting x−4=0x - 4 = 0x−4=0 or x+2=0x + 2 = 0x+2=0 gives

x=4orx=−2.x = 4 \qquad \text{or} \qquad x = -2.x=4orx=−2.

A nonzero number pulled out front is carried along for the ride and never changes the roots.

Check your understanding

Factor 2x2+10x+122x^2 + 10x + 122x2+10x+12 completely.

Answer choices

Every term is even, so pull out the common factor 222 first, which restores a leading coefficient of 111 inside.

2x2+10x+12=2(x2+5x+6)=2(x+2)(x+3)2x^2 + 10x + 12 = 2(x^2 + 5x + 6) = 2(x + 2)(x + 3)2x2+10x+12=2(x2+5x+6)=2(x+2)(x+3)

Inside, two numbers with sum 555 and product 666 are 222 and 333. The distractor (x+2)(x+6)(x + 2)(x + 6)(x+2)(x+6) expands to x2+8x+12x^2 + 8x + 12x2+8x+12, not the original trinomial.

When a trinomial will not factor

Not every trinomial can be factored this way. The method depends on finding two integers with the right sum and product, and sometimes no such pair exists. Take x2+x+1x^2 + x + 1x2+x+1. You would need two numbers that multiply to 111 and add to 111. The only integer pairs that multiply to 111 are 111 and 111, which add to 222, or −1-1−1 and −1-1−1, which add to −2-2−2. Neither adds to 111, so the search fails, and x2+x+1x^2 + x + 1x2+x+1 is irreducible over the integers: it cannot be written as a product of factors with integer coefficients.

Being irreducible over the integers is not the same as having no solutions at all. The equation x2+x−1=0x^2 + x - 1 = 0x2+x−1=0 is irreducible the same way, yet it still has two real solutions, just not integers. The equation x2+x+1=0x^2 + x + 1 = 0x2+x+1=0, though, has no real solution at all: later lessons on completing the square and the quadratic formula will show why. For now, an honest run through the factor pairs of ccc may turn up no pair that adds to bbb. When that happens, you can stop and report that the trinomial does not factor over the integers, rather than force an answer that is not there.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Losing the sign flip from factor to root. The factor x+3x + 3x+3 is zero at x=−3x = -3x=−3, not x=3x = 3x=3. A root is always the opposite sign of the number written in its factor, so (x+p)(x + p)(x+p) gives the root −p-p−p.
  • Putting the negative on the wrong number. For x2−2x−15x^2 - 2x - 15x2−2x−15 the pair is −5-5−5 and 333, giving (x−5)(x+3)(x - 5)(x + 3)(x−5)(x+3), while x2+2x−15x^2 + 2x - 15x2+2x−15 uses 555 and −3-3−3. The larger number carries the sign of the middle term; swap it and the middle term comes out wrong.
  • Not factoring completely. Writing 2x2+6x+4=(2x+2)(x+2)2x^2 + 6x + 4 = (2x + 2)(x + 2)2x2+6x+4=(2x+2)(x+2) still hides a common factor of 222. Pull the numerical factor out first, so the full answer is 2(x+1)(x+2)2(x + 1)(x + 2)2(x+1)(x+2).
  • Dividing by xxx and dropping a root. From x2=5xx^2 = 5xx2=5x, dividing both sides by xxx gives only x=5x = 5x=5 and loses x=0x = 0x=0. Move everything to one side and factor: x(x−5)=0x(x - 5) = 0x(x−5)=0 keeps both roots.
  • Using the zero-product property when the side is not zero. From (x+5)(x−3)=9(x + 5)(x - 3) = 9(x+5)(x−3)=9 you may not write x+5=9x + 5 = 9x+5=9. First expand and rearrange to standard form with 000 on one side, then factor.
  • Assuming every trinomial factors. If no integer pair has the right sum and product, as with x2+x+1x^2 + x + 1x2+x+1, the trinomial is irreducible over the integers. In that case, forcing a factorization only produces a wrong one.

Key takeaways

  • Factoring is expanding run backward. Since (x+p)(x+q)=x2+(p+q)x+pq(x + p)(x + q) = x^2 + (p + q)x + pq(x+p)(x+q)=x2+(p+q)x+pq, to factor x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + cx2+bx+c you find two numbers with sum bbb and product ccc, giving (x+p)(x+q)(x + p)(x + q)(x+p)(x+q).
  • Read the signs first. If c>0c > 0c>0 the numbers share a sign (the sign of bbb); if c<0c < 0c<0 they have opposite signs, with the larger carrying the sign of bbb.
  • Solve by factoring and the zero-product property: x2+bx+c=0x^2 + bx + c = 0x2+bx+c=0 becomes (x+p)(x+q)=0(x + p)(x + q) = 0(x+p)(x+q)=0, so x=−px = -px=−p or x=−qx = -qx=−q. The root is the opposite sign of the number in its factor.
  • Pull out a common numerical factor first (2x2+6x+4=2(x+1)(x+2)2x^2 + 6x + 4 = 2(x + 1)(x + 2)2x2+6x+4=2(x+1)(x+2)), and when c=0c = 0c=0 pull out the shared xxx (x2−5x=x(x−5)x^2 - 5x = x(x - 5)x2−5x=x(x−5)), keeping the root x=0x = 0x=0.
  • Check by expanding. Multiplying the factors back must return the original trinomial; if it does not, a number or a sign is off.
  • If no integer pair has sum bbb and product ccc, the trinomial is irreducible over the integers, and other tools come later.

A bit of history (optional)

Here is a puzzle roughly eighteen centuries old. Divide 202020 into two parts whose product is 969696.

It belongs to Diophantus, who worked in Alexandria, a Greek city in Egypt. He gathered a collection of problems of exactly this shape, and this one is the twenty-seventh in his opening book. You can already answer it, because two numbers with a given sum and a given product is precisely the search you ran all lesson. The pair is 121212 and 888, which is the same as writing x2−20x+96=(x−12)(x−8)x^2 - 20x + 96 = (x - 12)(x - 8)x2−20x+96=(x−12)(x−8).

The valuable part is the caution he attached. Before you begin the hunt, he says, check that a tidy answer exists. Halve the sum, square the result, and subtract the product. Whatever remains must itself be a square number. Here half of 202020 is 101010, and 100100100 minus 969696 leaves 444, a genuine square. The test is passed, so the search is worth starting.

He needed that caution for the reason you discovered at the end of this lesson. The search genuinely can fail. Confronted with x2+x+1x^2 + x + 1x2+x+1, you examined every factor pair of ccc and found nothing that added to bbb. His test and your run through the pairs deliver the same verdict in different words. No such pair exists, and reporting that honestly is a conclusion, not a defeat.
