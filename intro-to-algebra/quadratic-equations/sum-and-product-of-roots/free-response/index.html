    Part B

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The quadratic is monic, so b=−13b=-13b=−13 and c=40c=40c=40. The sum of the roots is −b-b−b and the product is ccc.

    r+s=−b=−(−13)=13,rs=c=40.r+s=-b=-(-13)=13, \qquad rs=c=40.r+s=−b=−(−13)=13,rs=c=40.

    Part B

    Here a=4a=4a=4, b=9b=9b=9, and c=−3c=-3c=−3, and this quadratic is not monic. Divide both totals by aaa, with the extra minus sign on the sum only.

    r+s=−ba=−94,rs=ca=−34=−34.r+s=-\frac{b}{a}=-\frac{9}{4}, \qquad rs=\frac{c}{a}=\frac{-3}{4}=-\frac{3}{4}.r+s=−ab​=−49​,rs=ac​=4−3​=−43​.

    The answer

    Part A

    Monic: x2+135x−65=0x^2+\tfrac{13}{5}x-\tfrac{6}{5}=0x2+513​x−56​=0. Scaled to integer coefficients: 5x2+13x−6=05x^2+13x-6=05x2+13x−6=0.

    Part C

    Both catch the same disagreement, but the sum-and-product route needs only two short totals, while expanding multiplies four terms and re-collects them; a sign slip is easier to isolate in one short total than buried inside a four-term expansion.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Find the sum and the product of the two given roots first.

    r+s=25+(−3)=25−155=−135,rs=25⋅(−3)=−65.r+s=\frac{2}{5}+(-3)=\frac{2}{5}-\frac{15}{5}=-\frac{13}{5}, \qquad rs=\frac{2}{5}\cdot(-3)=-\frac{6}{5}.r+s=52​+(−3)=52​−515​=−513​,rs=52​⋅(−3)=−56​.

    5(x2+135x−65)=5x2+13x−6=0.5\left(x^2+\frac{13}{5}x-\frac{6}{5}\right)=5x^2+13x-6=0.5(x2+513​x−56​)=5x2+13x−6=0.

    Part B

    The factors (5x+2)(5x+2)(5x+2) and (x−3)(x-3)(x−3) give roots x=−25x=-\tfrac{2}{5}x=−52​ and x=3x=3x=3: flip the sign of each factor's constant term, then divide by the coefficient of xxx.

    −25+3=135,(−25)(3)=−65.-\frac{2}{5}+3=\frac{13}{5}, \qquad \left(-\frac{2}{5}\right)(3)=-\frac{6}{5}.−52​+3=513​,(−52​)(3)=−56​.

    Part C

    Expanding directly gives

    (5x+2)(x−3)=5x2−15x+2x−6=5x2−13x−6,(5x+2)(x-3)=5x^2-15x+2x-6=5x^2-13x-6,(5x+2)(x−3)=5x2−15x+2x−6=5x2−13x−6,

    which disagrees with 5x2+13x−65x^2+13x-65x2+13x−6 on the middle term's sign, the same disagreement the sum-and-product check found. But reaching it took four products and a like-term collection.

    The sum-and-product route reaches the same verdict from two short totals. Because r+sr+sr+s and rsrsrs are each a single number, a sign error is easy to isolate; inside a full expansion, a sign error is just one term among several and easier to lose track of.

    Another way: Test one known root by direct substitution

    Instead of comparing totals, substitute a root you already trust, x=25x=\tfrac{2}{5}x=52​, directly into the proposed factors: 5(25)+2=4≠05\left(\tfrac{2}{5}\right)+2=4\ne05(52​)+2=4=0 and 25−3=−135≠0\tfrac{2}{5}-3=-\tfrac{13}{5}\ne052​−3=−513​=0, so neither factor vanishes there.
