Sums and Products of Roots

Intuition

Take x2−7x+10x^2 - 7x + 10x2−7x+10. It factors as (x−2)(x−5)(x - 2)(x - 5)(x−2)(x−5), so its roots are 222 and 555. Add them and you get 777, the opposite of the middle coefficient −7-7−7. Multiply them and you get 101010, the constant term itself. That is not a coincidence of this one example: whenever a quadratic has two real roots, their sum and product are already sitting inside the coefficients, readable without factoring or solving at all. This lesson shows why that pattern always holds for quadratics with real roots, and puts it to work three ways. You can build a quadratic from roots you want, recover a missing root from one you already have, or check a proposed pair of roots against the coefficients, all by matching a trinomial against the product of its factors.

Learning goals

  • Read r+s=−br + s = -br+s=−b and rs=crs = crs=c off a monic quadratic
  • Divide by aaa for the general sum and product
  • Build a quadratic from roots with x2−(r+s)x+rsx^2 - (r + s)x + rsx2−(r+s)x+rs
  • Recover a missing root from the sum, then check with the product
  • Compare the roots to the factoring pair, same product but opposite sum

Reading the sum and product off the coefficients

Look back at x2−7x+10=(x−2)(x−5)x^2 - 7x + 10 = (x - 2)(x - 5)x2−7x+10=(x−2)(x−5): the roots 222 and 555 summed to 777 and multiplied to 101010. Here is why that always happens. Start with a monic quadratic, one whose leading coefficient is 111, written x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + cx2+bx+c, and suppose it has roots rrr and sss. You already know from the last lessons that a quadratic with those roots can be written in factored form as (x−r)(x−s)(x - r)(x - s)(x−r)(x−s). That form works because setting xxx equal to either root makes one factor zero, and so makes the whole product zero. Expanding that product and comparing it with x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + cx2+bx+c is the whole idea, and it pins down the sum and the product exactly.

Why a monic quadratic's roots satisfy r+s=−br + s = -br+s=−b and rs=crs = crs=c#

Because rrr and sss are the roots of x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + cx2+bx+c, the quadratic is the product of the two factors they produce:

x2+bx+c=(x−r)(x−s).x^2 + bx + c = (x - r)(x - s).x2+bx+c=(x−r)(x−s).

Expand the right side, multiplying every term of the first factor by every term of the second:

(x−r)(x−s)=x2−sx−rx+rs=x2−(r+s)x+rs.(x - r)(x - s) = x^2 - sx - rx + rs = x^2 - (r + s)x + rs.(x−r)(x−s)=x2−sx−rx+rs=x2−(r+s)x+rs.

Two expressions in xxx are equal for every value of xxx only when they match term by term. So line up the coefficient of xxx and the constant on each side:

b=−(r+s)andc=rs.b = -(r + s) \qquad \text{and} \qquad c = rs.b=−(r+s)andc=rs.

Solving the first equation for the sum gives r+s=−br + s = -br+s=−b, and the second already reads rs=crs = crs=c. So for a monic quadratic the sum of the roots is the negative of the middle coefficient, and the product of the roots is the constant term. Both the sum and the product are read straight off the quadratic with no solving required. Watch the sign on the sum. The middle coefficient is −(r+s)-(r + s)−(r+s), so the roots add to −b-b−b, not bbb; that minus sign is the single most common slip with these formulas.

∎

Worked example 1 Find the sum and product of the roots of x2−7x+10x^2 - 7x + 10x2−7x+10

Read the coefficients off the quadratic: b=−7b = -7b=−7 and c=10c = 10c=10. The sum of the roots is the negative of the middle coefficient, and the product is the constant, so both totals come without solving:

r+s=−b=−(−7)=7andrs=c=10.r + s = -b = -(-7) = 7 \qquad \text{and} \qquad rs = c = 10.r+s=−b=−(−7)=7andrs=c=10.

As a check, this trinomial factors as (x−2)(x−5)(x - 2)(x - 5)(x−2)(x−5), whose roots 222 and 555 indeed add to 777 and multiply to 101010. The double negative on the sum is the piece to handle carefully: bbb is −7-7−7, so −b-b−b is +7+7+7.

Check your understanding

Without solving, find the sum and product of the roots of x2+4x−12x^2 + 4x - 12x2+4x−12.

Answer choices

Read the coefficients: b=4b = 4b=4 and c=−12c = -12c=−12. The sum of the roots is −b-b−b and the product is ccc.

r+s=−b=−4,rs=c=−12r + s = -b = -4, \qquad rs = c = -12r+s=−b=−4,rs=c=−12

So the sum is −4-4−4 and the product is −12-12−12. The choice with sum +4+4+4 forgets the minus sign that always sits in front of bbb.

When the leading coefficient is not 1

Everything so far assumed the quadratic started with a bare x2x^2x2. A general quadratic ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0 carries a leading coefficient aaa that need not be 111, with a≠0a \ne 0a=0, or it would not be a quadratic at all. Two short routes handle it, and they agree.

The quick route is to divide. Dividing the equation ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0 through by aaa does not move its roots, since scaling an equation by a nonzero number leaves the values that make it zero untouched. It produces the monic quadratic

x2+bax+ca=0,x^2 + \frac{b}{a}x + \frac{c}{a} = 0,x2+ab​x+ac​=0,

whose middle coefficient is ba\frac{b}{a}ab​ and whose constant is ca\frac{c}{a}ac​. Reading the monic formulas with those in place gives r+s=−bar + s = -\frac{b}{a}r+s=−ab​ and rs=cars = \frac{c}{a}rs=ac​ at once. The second route factors instead, and it is worth seeing because it explains where the aaa goes.

Why r+s=−bar + s = -\frac{b}{a}r+s=−ab​ and rs=cars = \frac{c}{a}rs=ac​ in general#

A quadratic with roots rrr and sss and leading coefficient aaa is aaa times the product of its factors:

ax2+bx+c=a(x−r)(x−s).ax^2 + bx + c = a(x - r)(x - s).ax2+bx+c=a(x−r)(x−s).

The factor aaa out front is needed so the squared terms match. The reason is that expanding (x−r)(x−s)(x - r)(x - s)(x−r)(x−s) produces a squared term of just x2x^2x2, and multiplying by aaa restores the ax2ax^2ax2 you started with. Carry out that expansion:

a(x−r)(x−s)=a(x2−(r+s)x+rs)=ax2−a(r+s)x+a rs.a(x - r)(x - s) = a\left(x^2 - (r + s)x + rs\right) = ax^2 - a(r + s)x + a\,rs.a(x−r)(x−s)=a(x2−(r+s)x+rs)=ax2−a(r+s)x+ars.

Match this term by term with ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c. The squared terms already agree, the middle terms give b=−a(r+s)b = -a(r + s)b=−a(r+s), and the constants give c=a rsc = a\,rsc=ars. Dividing each of those equations by aaa isolates the sum and the product:

r+s=−baandrs=ca.r + s = -\frac{b}{a} \qquad \text{and} \qquad rs = \frac{c}{a}.r+s=−ab​andrs=ac​.

When a=1a = 1a=1 these collapse back to r+s=−br + s = -br+s=−b and rs=crs = crs=c, so the monic case is just this general result with a=1a = 1a=1. Both routes divide by aaa, and only the sum picks up the extra minus sign.

∎

Both derivations above started from a real factorization a(x−r)(x−s)a(x - r)(x - s)a(x−r)(x−s), so they take rrr and sss to be two real roots, whether those two roots are different numbers or the same number repeated. In this lesson, and in every example and exercise it contains, rrr and sss always mean a real root pair.

One more thing has to be true before any of this works: the equation has to already be in standard form, ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0, with every term moved to one side. An equation like 3x2+12=11x3x^2 + 12 = 11x3x2+12=11x is not there yet. Move every term to one side first, 3x2−11x+12=03x^2 - 11x + 12 = 03x2−11x+12=0, and only then read off a=3a = 3a=3, b=−11b = -11b=−11, c=12c = 12c=12. Reading coefficients from the wrong side of the equation is a quiet way to flip a sign.

Worked example 2 Find the sum and product of the roots of 3x2+5x−23x^2 + 5x - 23x2+5x−2

Now a=3a = 3a=3, b=5b = 5b=5, and c=−2c = -2c=−2. Use the general formulas, dividing each of bbb and ccc by the leading coefficient and putting the minus sign on the sum:

r+s=−ba=−53andrs=ca=−23=−23.r + s = -\frac{b}{a} = -\frac{5}{3} \qquad \text{and} \qquad rs = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{-2}{3} = -\frac{2}{3}.r+s=−ab​=−35​andrs=ac​=3−2​=−32​.

Both totals are fractions because the leading 333 divides into them. Checking against the factorization 3x2+5x−2=(3x−1)(x+2)3x^2 + 5x - 2 = (3x - 1)(x + 2)3x2+5x−2=(3x−1)(x+2), the roots are 13\tfrac{1}{3}31​ and −2-2−2, and indeed 13+(−2)=−53\tfrac{1}{3} + (-2) = -\tfrac{5}{3}31​+(−2)=−35​ while 13⋅(−2)=−23\tfrac{1}{3} \cdot (-2) = -\tfrac{2}{3}31​⋅(−2)=−32​.

Check your understanding

Find the sum and product of the roots of 2x2−9x+42x^2 - 9x + 42x2−9x+4.

Answer choices

Here a=2a = 2a=2, b=−9b = -9b=−9, and c=4c = 4c=4. Divide each of bbb and ccc by aaa, with the minus sign on the sum.

r+s=−ba=92,rs=ca=2r + s = -\frac{b}{a} = \frac{9}{2}, \qquad rs = \frac{c}{a} = 2r+s=−ab​=29​,rs=ac​=2

So the sum is 92\tfrac{9}{2}29​ and the product is 222. Leaving the sum negative keeps the minus that has already been cancelled by b=−9b = -9b=−9.

Building a quadratic from its roots

The formulas run just as well in reverse. If you are told the roots and asked for a quadratic, you do not need to expand anything from scratch. The sum and the product of those roots are all a monic quadratic needs. Because (x−r)(x−s)=x2−(r+s)x+rs(x - r)(x - s) = x^2 - (r + s)x + rs(x−r)(x−s)=x2−(r+s)x+rs, a monic quadratic with roots rrr and sss is

x2−(r+s)x+rs=0.x^2 - (r + s)x + rs = 0.x2−(r+s)x+rs=0.

The coefficient of xxx is the negative of the sum, and the constant is the product with its own sign kept. That single template builds the quadratic from any pair of roots.

Worked example 3 Build a monic quadratic with roots 444 and −6-6−6

First find the sum and the product of the two roots:

r+s=4+(−6)=−2andrs=(4)(−6)=−24.r + s = 4 + (-6) = -2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad rs = (4)(-6) = -24.r+s=4+(−6)=−2andrs=(4)(−6)=−24.

Drop those into the template x2−(r+s)x+rsx^2 - (r + s)x + rsx2−(r+s)x+rs, taking care that the coefficient of xxx is the negative of the sum:

x2−(−2)x+(−24)=x2+2x−24.x^2 - (-2)x + (-24) = x^2 + 2x - 24.x2−(−2)x+(−24)=x2+2x−24.

So x2+2x−24=0x^2 + 2x - 24 = 0x2+2x−24=0 has the required roots, which you can confirm by factoring it back to (x−4)(x+6)(x - 4)(x + 6)(x−4)(x+6). Notice how the negative sum −2-2−2 turned into the positive middle coefficient +2+2+2; that is the sign flip working exactly as the formula promises.

Worked example 4 Build a quadratic with roots 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​ and 333 and whole-number coefficients

The monic template still works, but a fractional root leaves a fractional coefficient. Start there, with the sum and product of the two roots:

r+s=12+3=72andrs=12⋅3=32,r + s = \tfrac{1}{2} + 3 = \tfrac{7}{2} \qquad \text{and} \qquad rs = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3 = \tfrac{3}{2},r+s=21​+3=27​andrs=21​⋅3=23​,

so the monic quadratic is x2−72x+32=0x^2 - \tfrac{7}{2}x + \tfrac{3}{2} = 0x2−27​x+23​=0. To clear the fractions, multiply every term by 222, which scales the whole equation without changing its roots:

2(x2−72x+32)=2x2−7x+3=0.2\left(x^2 - \tfrac{7}{2}x + \tfrac{3}{2}\right) = 2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0.2(x2−27​x+23​)=2x2−7x+3=0.

Multiplying by 222 changes the coefficients and gives a different-looking equation, but it does not change which values of xxx make it zero: multiplying both sides of an equation by the same nonzero number always leaves its solutions untouched. So 2x2−7x+3=02x^2 - 7x + 3 = 02x2−7x+3=0 is an equation with a leading coefficient of 222, chosen to make every coefficient an integer, and it has exactly the same roots as the fractional one it came from. Factoring back gives (2x−1)(x−3)(2x - 1)(x - 3)(2x−1)(x−3), whose roots are 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​ and 333.

Answer choices

Find the sum and product of the two roots.

r+s=−3+5=2,rs=(−3)(5)=−15r + s = -3 + 5 = 2, \qquad rs = (-3)(5) = -15r+s=−3+5=2,rs=(−3)(5)=−15

The monic template is x2−(r+s)x+rsx^2 - (r + s)x + rsx2−(r+s)x+rs, so the quadratic is x2−2x−15=0x^2 - 2x - 15 = 0x2−2x−15=0. The coefficient of xxx is the negative of the sum, which is why +2+2+2 becomes −2-2−2; the choice x2+2x−15x^2 + 2x - 15x2+2x−15 misses that flip.

Recovering a missing root and checking a pair

The sum and product also work as shortcuts once you already know one root, or when someone hands you a proposed pair of roots to check. Both jobs use the same two totals.

To recover a missing root, the sum always works. Suppose x2−10x+21=0x^2 - 10x + 21 = 0x2−10x+21=0 and you have spotted that x=3x = 3x=3 is a root. The two roots must add to −b=10-b = 10−b=10, so the other root is whatever is left after removing 333 from that total:

s=(r+s)−r=10−3=7.s = (r + s) - r = 10 - 3 = 7.s=(r+s)−r=10−3=7.

The product gives the same answer as a check, since the roots multiply to c=21c = 21c=21 and 21÷3=721 \div 3 = 721÷3=7. That division worked because the known root, 333, was not zero. The product route breaks down exactly when the known root is 000, since dividing by 000 is never allowed; the sum route has no such gap, which is why it comes first.

Worked example 5 One root of 2x2−7x+3=02x^2 - 7x + 3 = 02x2−7x+3=0 is 333. Find the other root.

The roots of 2x2−7x+32x^2 - 7x + 32x2−7x+3 add to −ba=72-\frac{b}{a} = \frac{7}{2}−ab​=27​ and multiply to ca=32\frac{c}{a} = \frac{3}{2}ac​=23​. Using the sum, subtract the known root from the total:

s=−ba−r=72−3=12.s = -\frac{b}{a} - r = \frac{7}{2} - 3 = \frac{1}{2}.s=−ab​−r=27​−3=21​.

The product confirms it, since the roots multiply to 32\tfrac{3}{2}23​ and 32÷3=12\tfrac{3}{2} \div 3 = \tfrac{1}{2}23​÷3=21​. The missing root is 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​, a fraction because the leading 222 divides into it.

s=(r+s)−r=8−3=5s = (r + s) - r = 8 - 3 = 5s=(r+s)−r=8−3=5

Answer choices

Here b=−5b = -5b=−5 and c=0c = 0c=0, so the roots add to −b=5-b = 5−b=5 and multiply to c=0c = 0c=0. The known root is 000, so the product route would need c÷0c \div 0c÷0, which is undefined.

s=(r+s)−r=5−0=5s = (r + s) - r = 5 - 0 = 5s=(r+s)−r=5−0=5

The sum route still works and gives 555. When the known root is 000, the sum is the only option, since dividing by 000 is never allowed.

Checking a proposed pair works the same way, and it is quick because a correct pair has to pass both tests. To test whether 222 and 555 are the roots of x2−7x+10x^2 - 7x + 10x2−7x+10, compare their sum and product against the coefficients. The roots should sum to −b=7-b = 7−b=7 and multiply to c=10c = 10c=10, and 2+5=72 + 5 = 72+5=7 with 2⋅5=102 \cdot 5 = 102⋅5=10, so the pair passes both tests and is confirmed. A proposed pair like 111 and 666 would sum to 777 but multiply to only 666, failing the product test. That pair can therefore be ruled out at a glance, without any substitution.

The connection to factoring

To factor a monic trinomial x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + cx2+bx+c you searched for two numbers ppp and qqq with sum bbb and product ccc, and wrote (x+p)(x+q)(x + p)(x + q)(x+p)(x+q). Those two numbers are not the roots. The roots come from the sign flip in the zero-product step. Setting (x+p)(x+q)=0(x + p)(x + q) = 0(x+p)(x+q)=0 gives x=−px = -px=−p or x=−qx = -qx=−q, so the roots are −p-p−p and −q-q−q, the negatives of the factoring numbers.

Line the two searches up. The factoring numbers satisfy p+q=bp + q = bp+q=b and pq=cpq = cpq=c. The roots r=−pr = -pr=−p and s=−qs = -qs=−q then satisfy

r+s=−p−q=−(p+q)=−bandrs=(−p)(−q)=pq=c,r + s = -p - q = -(p + q) = -b \qquad \text{and} \qquad rs = (-p)(-q) = pq = c,r+s=−p−q=−(p+q)=−bandrs=(−p)(−q)=pq=c,

which are exactly the sum and product formulas from this lesson. Both jobs share the same product ccc; only the sum flips sign, from bbb for the factoring numbers to −b-b−b for the roots, and that one sign flip is the whole difference between a factor and a root.

Check your understanding

The factoring numbers for x2+5x−36x^2 + 5x - 36x2+5x−36 are p=−4p = -4p=−4 and q=9q = 9q=9, since (x−4)(x+9)(x - 4)(x + 9)(x−4)(x+9) expands back to it. What are the trinomial's two roots?

Answer choices

The roots are the negatives of the factoring numbers: r=−p=4r = -p = 4r=−p=4 and s=−q=−9s = -q = -9s=−q=−9. Check them against the coefficients, b=5b = 5b=5 and c=−36c = -36c=−36.

r+s=4+(−9)=−5=−b,rs=4⋅(−9)=−36=cr + s = 4 + (-9) = -5 = -b, \qquad rs = 4 \cdot (-9) = -36 = cr+s=4+(−9)=−5=−b,rs=4⋅(−9)=−36=c

Both totals match, confirming the roots. The choice −4-4−4 and 999 mistakes the factoring numbers themselves for the roots, forgetting the sign flip.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Dropping the minus sign on the sum. The roots add to −b-b−b, not bbb. For x2−7x+10x^2 - 7x + 10x2−7x+10 the middle coefficient is −7-7−7, so the roots sum to −(−7)=7-(-7) = 7−(−7)=7, not −7-7−7. The minus in front of bbb is never optional.
  • Forgetting to divide by aaa, or dividing only one coefficient. For ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c the sum is −ba-\frac{b}{a}−ab​ and the product is ca\frac{c}{a}ac​. Both totals are divided by aaa; the sum alone also takes the minus sign.
  • Flipping the wrong sign when building. From roots with sum SSS and product PPP, the monic quadratic is x2−Sx+Px^2 - Sx + Px2−Sx+P. The coefficient of xxx is the negative of the sum, while the constant keeps the product’s own sign. Writing x2+Sx+Px^2 + Sx + Px2+Sx+P reverses the middle sign.
  • Reading the product with a flipped sign. The product is ca\frac{c}{a}ac​ (just ccc when monic), and it keeps the constant’s own sign. Only the sum carries the extra minus, so do not attach one to the product as well.
  • Dividing by a zero root. Recovering a missing root with s=rsrs = \frac{rs}{r}s=rrs​ fails the moment the known root is 000, since dividing by 000 is never allowed. The sum route s=(r+s)−rs = (r + s) - rs=(r+s)−r has no such gap, so reach for it first whenever a known root is 000.
  • Forgetting to scale when integers are wanted. Roots 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​ and 333 give the monic x2−72x+32x^2 - \tfrac{7}{2}x + \tfrac{3}{2}x2−27​x+23​; multiply through by 222 to reach 2x2−7x+32x^2 - 7x + 32x2−7x+3. Both equations have the same roots.
  • Trusting a pair that matches only the sum. A proposed pair is right only when it matches both the sum and the product. The numbers 111 and 666 match the sum 777 of x2−7x+10x^2 - 7x + 10x2−7x+10 but fail its product 101010, so they are not the roots.

Key takeaways

  • For a monic quadratic x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + cx2+bx+c with roots rrr and sss: r+s=−br + s = -br+s=−b and rs=crs = crs=c, read straight from the coefficients with no solving.
  • In general, for ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c with a≠0a \ne 0a=0 and roots rrr and sss: r+s=−bar + s = -\frac{b}{a}r+s=−ab​ and rs=cars = \frac{c}{a}rs=ac​. Divide both by aaa, and only the sum takes the minus sign.
  • Build from roots with the monic template x2−(r+s)x+rs=0x^2 - (r + s)x + rs = 0x2−(r+s)x+rs=0, then multiply through by a common denominator to clear fractions or to set a chosen leading coefficient.
  • Recover a missing root as (r+s)−r(r + s) - r(r+s)−r using the sum. This always works. Check it with rsr\frac{rs}{r}rrs​ using the product when the known root r≠0r \ne 0r=0.
  • A proposed pair of roots is correct exactly when it matches both the sum −ba-\frac{b}{a}−ab​ and the product ca\frac{c}{a}ac​.
  • The two numbers you hunt for when factoring a monic trinomial have sum bbb and product ccc. Those numbers are the negatives of the roots, which have sum −b-b−b and product ccc: same product, opposite sum.

A bit of history (optional)

Before anyone could state the rule you proved today, somebody had to be able to say "bbb".

That sounds like nothing, and it was not. For centuries, most books on equations worked through one case at a time. The coefficients were fixed numbers, and every problem began again from nothing. A writer would solve x2−7x+10x^2 - 7x + 10x2−7x+10, and then solve x2+4x−12x^2 + 4x - 12x2+4x−12. The reader was left to notice what the two solutions had in common, case by case, with no single line to write down instead.

Francois Viete was a lawyer who did mathematics in whatever hours the courts left him. In the fifteen nineties he gave algebra the missing piece. He began using letters for the known numbers of a problem, not only for the unknown one. Once the knowns had names, a whole family of equations fitted into one line.

That is why these two relations carry his Latinized name, Vieta’s formulas. They are not a deeper truth than the worked examples they replaced. They are the same truth, at last sayable. For x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + cx2+bx+c with real roots rrr and sss, whatever bbb and ccc happen to be, those roots add to −b-b−b and multiply to ccc. A claim that covers a whole family at once needs letters that mean any number at once. Those letters are what you read the two totals off today.
