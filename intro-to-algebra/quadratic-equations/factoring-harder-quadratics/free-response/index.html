  1. Problem 1 A direct factorization

    Factor 9x2−9x+29x^2-9x+29x2−9x+2.

    1. Hint 1

      The leading coefficient no longer stays out of the way: it splits across the two factors, so both factor pairs of 999 and of 222 are in play.

    2. Hint 2

      Work out the product of the leading and constant coefficients, then look for two numbers with that product and with sum −9-9−9.

    3. Hint 3

      Both of those numbers are negative, since their product is positive and their sum is not. Build the candidate whose cross products are those two numbers, then expand to check.

    Answer

    (3x−1)(3x−2)(3x-1)(3x-2)(3x−1)(3x−2).

    Full solution

    The product of the leading and constant coefficients is

    9⋅2=18,9 \cdot 2 = 18,9⋅2=18,

    and the middle coefficient is −9-9−9.

    So the middle term splits into two numbers with product 181818 and sum −9-9−9.

    Both are negative, because a positive product with a negative sum forces two negative numbers.

    The pair is −3-3−3 and −6-6−6, since −3+(−6)=−9-3+(-6)=-9−3+(−6)=−9 and

    (−3)(−6)=18.(-3)(-6)=18.(−3)(−6)=18.

    Those two numbers are the cross products to aim for.

    Splitting 999 as 3⋅33 \cdot 33⋅3, pairing 3x3x3x with −1-1−1 gives a −3x-3x−3x and pairing 3x3x3x with −2-2−2 gives a −6x-6x−6x, which suggests (3x−1)(3x−2)(3x-1)(3x-2)(3x−1)(3x−2).

    Expanding checks it:

    (3x−1)(3x−2)=9x2−9x+2.(3x-1)(3x-2)=9x^2-9x+2.(3x−1)(3x−2)=9x2−9x+2.

    The cross products −3x-3x−3x and −6x-6x−6x add to −9x-9x−9x, so the factorization is correct.

    Answer

    (3x−1)(3x−2)(3x-1)(3x-2)(3x−1)(3x−2).

    Key idea

    The product of the leading and constant coefficients, together with the middle coefficient, tells you which two numbers the middle term splits into.

  7. Problem 7 An integer pair search

    Decide whether 7x2+5x+27x^2+5x+27x2+5x+2 factors over the integers, listing every integer pair you test.

    1. Hint 1

      An integer factorization would make the middle term split in a particular way, so decide what the two split numbers would have to satisfy.

    2. Hint 2

      Work out the product of the leading and constant coefficients, then list every integer pair with that product and compare each sum with 555.

    3. Hint 3

      A pair of negative numbers has a positive product but a negative sum, so decide whether any negative pair could reach 555.

    Answer

    It does not factor: no integer pair has product 141414 and sum 555. The pairs are 111 and 141414 (sum 151515), 222 and 777 (sum 999), −1-1−1 and −14-14−14 (sum −15-15−15), and −2-2−2 and −7-7−7 (sum −9-9−9), so 7x2+5x+27x^2+5x+27x2+5x+2 is irreducible over the integers.

    Full solution

    An integer factorization (px+r)(qx+s)(px+r)(qx+s)(px+r)(qx+s) would make the middle coefficient the sum of two cross products whose product is the leading coefficient times the constant.

    Here that product is

    7⋅2=14.7 \cdot 2 = 14.7⋅2=14.

    So the search is for two integers with product 141414 and sum 555.

    The positive pairs with product 141414 are 111 and 141414, of sum 151515, and 222 and 777, of sum 999.

    The only other integer pairs are the negatives of those two, −1-1−1 and −14-14−14, of sum −15-15−15, and −2-2−2 and −7-7−7, of sum −9-9−9.

    Every pair has been tested and none has sum 555.

    So no integer pair splits the middle term, and 7x2+5x+27x^2+5x+27x2+5x+2 is irreducible over the integers.

    Reporting that the search failed is the correct answer here; forcing a factorization would only produce a wrong one.

    Answer

    It does not factor: no integer pair has product 141414 and sum 555. The pairs are 111 and 141414 (sum 151515), 222 and 777 (sum 999), −1-1−1 and −14-14−14 (sum −15-15−15), and −2-2−2 and −7-7−7 (sum −9-9−9), so 7x2+5x+27x^2+5x+27x2+5x+2 is irreducible over the integers.

  10. Problem 10 An integer-root prediction

    Rae claims that if a quadratic has integer coefficients, every real root is an integer. Decide whether the claim is correct. If it is not, construct a quadratic with integer coefficients that factors over the integers and has a non-integer root, verify that root by substitution, and say why a factorization into monic factors could not have produced it.

    1. Hint 1

      The claim covers every quadratic, so a single quadratic that fails it settles the matter; look among the non-monic quadratics this lesson factors.

    2. Hint 2

      Think about what a factor has to look like for its own zero to be a fraction rather than a whole number.

    3. Hint 3

      Multiply two binomials with integer entries, expand to show the coefficients are integers, then substitute the fractional zero into the expanded form.

    Answer

    The claim is incorrect: 3x2+2x−1=(3x−1)(x+1)3x^2+2x-1=(3x-1)(x+1)3x2+2x−1=(3x−1)(x+1) has the non-integer root x=13x=\frac13x=31​. Monic factors x+rx+rx+r with integer rrr have the integer zero −r-r−r, so they cannot produce a fraction. Other counterexamples and valid reasons are accepted.

    Full solution

    A claim about every quadratic is refuted by one quadratic that fails it, so a single counterexample settles the question.

    Build one from factors whose entries are integers, so that the expanded coefficients are integers too.

    Take the product

    (3x−1)(x+1)=3x2+2x−1,(3x-1)(x+1)=3x^2+2x-1,(3x−1)(x+1)=3x2+2x−1,

    whose coefficients 333, 222 and −1-1−1 are all integers.

    3(13)2+23−1=0.3\left(\frac13\right)^2+\frac23-1=0.3(31​)2+32​−1=0.

    Since 13\frac1331​ is not an integer, Rae's claim is incorrect.
