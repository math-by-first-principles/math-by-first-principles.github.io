Factoring Harder Quadratics

Intuition

Last lesson every trinomial began with a lone x2x^2x2, and the whole method rested on one clean fact. The squared term of (x+p)(x+q)(x + p)(x + q)(x+p)(x+q) always comes out as x2x^2x2 with nothing in front. That let you ignore the leading coefficient and hunt for two numbers with the right sum and product. Now the squared term arrives with a number attached, as in 2x2+7x+32x^2 + 7x + 32x2+7x+3, and that number has to come from somewhere. It can only come from multiplying the xxx pieces of the two factors, so the leading coefficient splits across both binomials instead of staying out of the way. The good news is that factoring is still expanding run backward. You just have factor pairs of the leading coefficient to account for as well as factor pairs of the constant, and a short, orderly search handles both.

Learning goals

  • Split the leading coefficient across both factors
  • Try reverse FOIL, expanding each candidate to check
  • Use a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c to find the pair that splits the middle
  • Remove a common factor before factoring the rest
  • Recognize that a root can come out as a fraction when the leading coefficient exceeds one

The leading coefficient splits across both factors

A trinomial like 2x2+7x+32x^2 + 7x + 32x2+7x+3 is non-monic: its leading coefficient is 222, not 111. To factor a general non-monic trinomial ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c you are looking for two factors of the form px+rpx + rpx+r and qx+sqx + sqx+s. In those two factors the coefficients ppp and qqq sit in front of the xxx terms. The reason the old sum-and-product trick no longer applies directly is that aaa is now the product of ppp and qqq. So the leading coefficient is shared out between the two factors. To see exactly how every coefficient is built, expand the general product once and read it carefully.

Why the factors of ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c satisfy pq=apq = apq=a,  rs=c\ rs = c rs=c, and  ps+qr=b\ ps + qr = b ps+qr=b#

Multiply the two factors px+rpx + rpx+r and qx+sqx + sqx+s, taking every term of the first times every term of the second:

(px+r)(qx+s)=pq x2+ps x+qr x+rs=pq x2+(ps+qr) x+rs.(px + r)(qx + s) = pq\,x^2 + ps\,x + qr\,x + rs = pq\,x^2 + (ps + qr)\,x + rs.(px+r)(qx+s)=pqx2+psx+qrx+rs=pqx2+(ps+qr)x+rs.

Now suppose this equals the trinomial ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c you want to factor. Two expressions in xxx agree for every value of xxx only when they match term by term. So line up the squared term, the xxx term, and the constant on each side:

pq=a,ps+qr=b,rs=c.pq = a, \qquad ps + qr = b, \qquad rs = c.pq=a,ps+qr=b,rs=c.

This is the whole method in three equations. The constant ccc is still the product rsrsrs of the two plain numbers, exactly as before. What is new is the first equation: the leading coefficient aaa is the product pqpqpq of the two numbers that ride in front of the xxx terms. And the middle coefficient bbb is no longer a simple sum of two numbers, it is ps+qrps + qrps+qr. That expression is a sum of two cross products, each mixing a leading number from one factor with a constant from the other. So factoring a non-monic trinomial means choosing a factor pair for aaa and a factor pair for ccc that fit together so their cross products add to bbb.

∎

The same rectangle that pictured expanding shows where each coefficient lives. Give a rectangle a width of px+rpx + rpx+r and a height of qx+sqx + sqx+s. The two splits cut it into four pieces.

The two corner cells are the leading term pq x2pq\,x^2pqx2 and the constant rsrsrs. The two off-diagonal strips are ps xps\,xpsx and qr xqr\,xqrx, and together they make the middle term (ps+qr)x(ps + qr)x(ps+qr)x. That picture also hides a useful fact you will use in a moment: the two strips have areas whose coefficients multiply to (ps)(qr)=pq⋅rs=a⋅c(ps)(qr) = pq \cdot rs = a \cdot c(ps)(qr)=pq⋅rs=a⋅c.

Factoring by reverse FOIL

With no shortcut yet, the honest method is a guided trial. Choose a factor pair for aaa and a factor pair for ccc, write the candidate (px+r)(qx+s)(px + r)(qx + s)(px+r)(qx+s), and expand it to check the middle term. If the cross products ps+qrps + qrps+qr come out to bbb, you are done; if not, swap the numbers around or try the next pair. Because expanding is quick, a handful of tries settles most trinomials. Start with a>0a > 0a>0, b>0b > 0b>0, and c>0c > 0c>0, so every number in sight is positive.

Worked example 1 Factor 2x2+7x+32x^2 + 7x + 32x2+7x+3

Here a=2a = 2a=2, b=7b = 7b=7, and c=3c = 3c=3. Both 222 and 333 are prime, so there is only one factor pair to place: a=2a = 2a=2 splits as 2⋅12 \cdot 12⋅1, and c=3c = 3c=3 splits as 3⋅13 \cdot 13⋅1. That fixes the skeleton as (2x+ ‾)(x+ ‾)(2x + \underline{\ })(x + \underline{\ })(2x+ ​)(x+ ​) with 333 and 111 to drop into the blanks. Two arrangements are possible, so write each and expand to see which gives a middle term of 7x7x7x:

(2x+3)(x+1)=2x2+5x+3,(2x+1)(x+3)=2x2+7x+3.(2x + 3)(x + 1) = 2x^2 + 5x + 3, \qquad (2x + 1)(x + 3) = 2x^2 + 7x + 3.(2x+3)(x+1)=2x2+5x+3,(2x+1)(x+3)=2x2+7x+3.

The second arrangement lands the middle term on 7x7x7x, so

2x2+7x+3=(2x+1)(x+3).2x^2 + 7x + 3 = (2x + 1)(x + 3).2x2+7x+3=(2x+1)(x+3).

Which constant pairs with which xxx term matters here in a way it never did for monic trinomials. (Writing the two factors in the opposite order, (x+3)(2x+1)(x + 3)(2x + 1)(x+3)(2x+1), is still the same product; nothing new there.) Pairing the 333 with the 2x2x2x doubles it into a 666, while pairing the 111 there does not. That is the leading coefficient making itself felt inside the cross products.

Worked example 2 Factor 3x2+10x+83x^2 + 10x + 83x2+10x+8

Now a=3a = 3a=3, b=10b = 10b=10, and c=8c = 8c=8. Since 333 is prime the skeleton is (3x+ ‾)(x+ ‾)(3x + \underline{\ })(x + \underline{\ })(3x+ ​)(x+ ​), and the constant 888 offers the factor pairs 1⋅81 \cdot 81⋅8 and 2⋅42 \cdot 42⋅4. Each of those pairs can drop into the blanks in either order. Test them by expanding and watching only the middle term:

(3x+1)(x+8)→25x,(3x+8)(x+1)→11x,(3x+2)(x+4)→14x,(3x+4)(x+2)→10x.\begin{aligned} (3x + 1)(x + 8) &\to 25x, &\qquad (3x + 8)(x + 1) &\to 11x, \\ (3x + 2)(x + 4) &\to 14x, &\qquad (3x + 4)(x + 2) &\to 10x. \end{aligned}(3x+1)(x+8)(3x+2)(x+4)​→25x,→14x,​(3x+8)(x+1)(3x+4)(x+2)​→11x,→10x.​

The last pair hits 10x10x10x, so

3x2+10x+8=(3x+4)(x+2).3x^2 + 10x + 8 = (3x + 4)(x + 2).3x2+10x+8=(3x+4)(x+2).

Expanding confirms it fully: (3x+4)(x+2)=3x2+6x+4x+8=3x2+10x+8(3x + 4)(x + 2) = 3x^2 + 6x + 4x + 8 = 3x^2 + 10x + 8(3x+4)(x+2)=3x2+6x+4x+8=3x2+10x+8. Notice that the 333 in front makes the two arrangements (3x+4)(x+2)(3x + 4)(x + 2)(3x+4)(x+2) and (3x+2)(x+4)(3x + 2)(x + 4)(3x+2)(x+4) give different middle terms, so both really do need checking.

The cross products 2x2x2x and 5x5x5x add to 7x7x7x, so 2x2+7x+5=(2x+5)(x+1)2x^2 + 7x + 5 = (2x + 5)(x + 1)2x2+7x+5=(2x+5)(x+1). The other arrangement (2x+1)(x+5)(2x + 1)(x + 5)(2x+1)(x+5) gives 11x11x11x.

Let the product of a and c point the way

Trying every arrangement works, but you can aim the search instead of guessing. The middle coefficient is the sum of the two cross products, ps+qr=bps + qr = bps+qr=b, and from the rectangle those same cross products multiply to (ps)(qr)=a⋅c(ps)(qr) = a \cdot c(ps)(qr)=a⋅c. So the two pieces the middle term splits into are just a pair of numbers with a known product and a known sum:

(ps)+(qr)=band(ps)(qr)=a⋅c.(ps) + (qr) = b \qquad \text{and} \qquad (ps)(qr) = a \cdot c.(ps)+(qr)=band(ps)(qr)=a⋅c.

Finding that pair is exactly the sum-and-product search you already know, and it tells you what the two middle strips should be. Those strips point you straight at the factor pair to write down. The hint does not replace the trial; it tells you which candidate to build, and you still finish by expanding to be sure.

Worked example 3 Factor 6x2+7x−36x^2 + 7x - 36x2+7x−3

Here a=6a = 6a=6, b=7b = 7b=7, and c=−3c = -3c=−3, so the middle term should split into two numbers with sum 777 and product a⋅c=6⋅(−3)=−18a \cdot c = 6 \cdot (-3) = -18a⋅c=6⋅(−3)=−18. Because the product is negative the two numbers have opposite signs, and a short search gives them:

9+(−2)=7and9⋅(−2)=−18.9 + (-2) = 7 \qquad \text{and} \qquad 9 \cdot (-2) = -18.9+(−2)=7and9⋅(−2)=−18.

So the two middle strips are 9x9x9x and −2x-2x−2x. Now build a factor pair of 666 and of −3-3−3 whose cross products come out to 9x9x9x and −2x-2x−2x. Pairing 3x3x3x with 333 makes a 9x9x9x, and pairing 2x2x2x with −1-1−1 makes a −2x-2x−2x, which suggests (3x−1)(2x+3)(3x - 1)(2x + 3)(3x−1)(2x+3). Expand to confirm:

(3x−1)(2x+3)=6x2+9x−2x−3=6x2+7x−3.(3x - 1)(2x + 3) = 6x^2 + 9x - 2x - 3 = 6x^2 + 7x - 3.(3x−1)(2x+3)=6x2+9x−2x−3=6x2+7x−3.

The cross products are the 9x9x9x and −2x-2x−2x the hint promised, and the whole product rebuilds the original, so

6x2+7x−3=(3x−1)(2x+3).6x^2 + 7x - 3 = (3x - 1)(2x + 3).6x2+7x−3=(3x−1)(2x+3).

Pull out a common factor first

Before trying any factor pairs, check whether all three coefficients share a common factor. If they do, pull it out. It shrinks the leading coefficient you have to break apart and often turns a bristly trinomial into a gentler one.

Worked example 4 Factor 6x2+15x+66x^2 + 15x + 66x2+15x+6 completely

Every coefficient is a multiple of 333, so factor the 333 out front before anything else:

6x2+15x+6=3(2x2+5x+2).6x^2 + 15x + 6 = 3(2x^2 + 5x + 2).6x2+15x+6=3(2x2+5x+2).

Pulling out the 333 dropped the leading coefficient inside from 666 to 222, which is much easier to handle. Factor 2x2+5x+22x^2 + 5x + 22x2+5x+2 by the same trial as before. The middle should split into two numbers with sum 555 and product 2⋅2=42 \cdot 2 = 42⋅2=4, namely 444 and 111, so try (2x+1)(x+2)(2x + 1)(x + 2)(2x+1)(x+2):

(2x+1)(x+2)=2x2+4x+x+2=2x2+5x+2.(2x + 1)(x + 2) = 2x^2 + 4x + x + 2 = 2x^2 + 5x + 2.(2x+1)(x+2)=2x2+4x+x+2=2x2+5x+2.

Putting the common factor back gives the complete factorization:

6x2+15x+6=3(2x+1)(x+2).6x^2 + 15x + 6 = 3(2x + 1)(x + 2).6x2+15x+6=3(2x+1)(x+2).

Leaving off the 333, or stopping at something like (6x+3)(x+2)(6x + 3)(x + 2)(6x+3)(x+2) that still hides a factor of 333 inside, counts as factored incompletely. Pull the common factor all the way out and keep it out front.

Solving gives roots that can be fractions

Factoring pays off the same way it did before: once the trinomial is a product, the zero-product property from the earlier lesson turns it into roots. To solve ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0, factor the left side into (px+r)(qx+s)(px + r)(qx + s)(px+r)(qx+s) and set the product to zero. A product is zero only when one factor is zero, so

px+r=0orqx+s=0,px + r = 0 \qquad \text{or} \qquad qx + s = 0,px+r=0orqx+s=0,

which give x=−rpx = -\dfrac{r}{p}x=−pr​ and x=−sqx = -\dfrac{s}{q}x=−qs​. Here is the one genuinely new feature: because the leading numbers ppp and qqq can be bigger than 111, dividing by them can produce a fraction. Non-monic quadratics routinely have fractional roots, and that is expected, not a mistake.

Worked example 5 Solve 2x2+x−6=02x^2 + x - 6 = 02x2+x−6=0

First factor the left side. With a=2a = 2a=2, b=1b = 1b=1, and c=−6c = -6c=−6, the middle should split into two numbers with sum 111 and product 2⋅(−6)=−122 \cdot (-6) = -122⋅(−6)=−12. Those are 444 and −3-3−3, since 4+(−3)=14 + (-3) = 14+(−3)=1 and 4⋅(−3)=−124 \cdot (-3) = -124⋅(−3)=−12. Pairing 2x2x2x with 222 gives 4x4x4x, and pairing xxx with −3-3−3 gives −3x-3x−3x, which suggests (2x−3)(x+2)(2x - 3)(x + 2)(2x−3)(x+2), which expands to 2x2+4x−3x−6=2x2+x−62x^2 + 4x - 3x - 6 = 2x^2 + x - 62x2+4x−3x−6=2x2+x−6. So

2x2+x−6=(2x−3)(x+2)=0.2x^2 + x - 6 = (2x - 3)(x + 2) = 0.2x2+x−6=(2x−3)(x+2)=0.

By the zero-product property, set each factor to zero:

2x−3=0orx+2=0.2x - 3 = 0 \qquad \text{or} \qquad x + 2 = 0.2x−3=0orx+2=0.

The first factor gives x=32x = \dfrac{3}{2}x=23​, a fraction because the 222 in front had to be divided out; the second gives x=−2x = -2x=−2. When a harder trinomial will not factor

As with monic trinomials, not every non-monic trinomial factors over the integers. The search can simply fail: no factor pair of aaa and ccc produces cross products that add to bbb. Equivalently, no integer pair has product a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c and sum bbb. Take 2x2+2x−12x^2 + 2x - 12x2+2x−1. The middle would have to split into two integers with product 2⋅(−1)=−22 \cdot (-1) = -22⋅(−1)=−2 and sum 222. But the only integer pairs multiplying to −2-2−2 are 111 and −2-2−2 (sum −1-1−1) or −1-1−1 and 222 (sum 111). Neither adds to 222, so the trinomial is irreducible over the integers.

That does not say the equation 2x2+2x−1=02x^2 + 2x - 1 = 02x2+2x−1=0 has no solutions, only that integer factoring is the wrong tool for it. Later lessons build methods that solve any quadratic, whether it factors or not. For now, if an honest run through the pairs turns up nothing, you can stop and report that the trinomial does not factor over the integers. Reporting the failure is better than forcing a factorization that is not there.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting that pairing changes the middle term. Writing the two factors in the opposite order never changes the product, monic or not. What does change it is which constant is paired with which xxx term: (2x+3)(x+1)(2x + 3)(x + 1)(2x+3)(x+1) and (2x+1)(x+3)(2x + 1)(x + 3)(2x+1)(x+3) are different, because the leading 222 doubles the constant in the other binomial, the one it meets in the cross product, not the constant written beside it in the same binomial. Always expand to see which pairing lands the right middle term.
  • Ignoring the leading coefficient in the cross products. The middle term is ps+qrps + qrps+qr, not a plain sum of two numbers. You must account for how ppp and qqq multiply into the constants, which is exactly what the product a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c keeps track of.
  • Not factoring completely. Writing 6x2+15x+6=(6x+3)(x+2)6x^2 + 15x + 6 = (6x + 3)(x + 2)6x2+15x+6=(6x+3)(x+2) still hides a factor of 333 inside the first binomial. Pull every common factor out front first, so the answer is 3(2x+1)(x+2)3(2x + 1)(x + 2)3(2x+1)(x+2).
  • Treating a fractional root as an error. Solving 2x2+x−6=02x^2 + x - 6 = 02x2+x−6=0 gives x=32x = \tfrac{3}{2}x=23​ and x=−2x = -2x=−2. The fraction is correct: dividing by the leading number of a factor is what produces it.
  • Using the zero-product property when a side is not zero. From (2x−3)(x+2)=4(2x - 3)(x + 2) = 4(2x−3)(x+2)=4 you may not write 2x−3=42x - 3 = 42x−3=4. First expand and move everything to one side so a 000 stands alone, then factor.
  • Assuming every trinomial factors. If no integer pair has product a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c and sum bbb, as with 2x2+2x−12x^2 + 2x - 12x2+2x−1, the trinomial is irreducible over the integers, and forcing factors only produces a wrong answer.

Key takeaways

  • A non-monic trinomial ax2+bx+cax^2 + bx + cax2+bx+c factors as (px+r)(qx+s)(px + r)(qx + s)(px+r)(qx+s) where pq=apq = apq=a, rs=crs = crs=c, and the cross products satisfy ps+qr=bps + qr = bps+qr=b. The leading coefficient now splits across both factors.
  • Reverse FOIL: pick a factor pair of aaa and of ccc, write the candidate, and expand to check that the middle term is bxbxbx. Adjust the arrangement until it fits.
  • Let the product a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c guide you. The middle splits into two numbers with product a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c and sum bbb. Finding that pair tells you the two cross products and which factors to write, and then you confirm by expanding.
  • Pull out a common factor first. For 6x2+15x+6=3(2x2+5x+2)6x^2 + 15x + 6 = 3(2x^2 + 5x + 2)6x2+15x+6=3(2x2+5x+2), the 333 shrinks the leading coefficient before you factor the rest.
  • Solve by the zero-product property: (px+r)(qx+s)=0(px + r)(qx + s) = 0(px+r)(qx+s)=0 gives x=−rpx = -\tfrac{r}{p}x=−pr​ or x=−sqx = -\tfrac{s}{q}x=−qs​, and a root often comes out as a fraction when ppp or qqq is greater than 111.
  • If no integer pair has product a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c and sum bbb, the trinomial is irreducible over the integers, and other tools come later.

A bit of history (optional)

Suppose your run through the factor pairs turns up nothing. For a long stretch of history, that was where the mathematics stopped.

The rule then was that an answer had to be a whole number or a fraction. Anything else was a length you could draw but not a quantity you could calculate with. A problem that arrived at one was reported as having no answer at all. That rule cost real results, because plenty of ordinary questions land on such a value.

Around the year 900900900, in Egypt, a mathematician named Abu Kamil stopped obeying it. He wrote a book on equations in which those awkward roots appear as ordinary answers. He added them, he multiplied them, and he left them standing in the last line rather than apologizing for them. Later writers borrowed the habit, and within a few centuries it had become the plain meaning of the word number.

You met the same wall at the close of this lesson. When no integer pair has product a⋅ca \cdot ca⋅c and sum bbb, the trinomial is irreducible over the integers. The honest report is that integer factoring cannot reach its roots. Read that sentence narrowly. It says the tool has run out, not the equation. The roots are still there, waiting for a method willing to write them down.
