  1. Problem 1 One line for a list of facts

    Each of these facts is true:

    6⋅9=6⋅10−67⋅9=7⋅10−723⋅9=23⋅10−23\begin{aligned} 6 \cdot 9 &= 6 \cdot 10 - 6 \\ 7 \cdot 9 &= 7 \cdot 10 - 7 \\ 23 \cdot 9 &= 23 \cdot 10 - 23 \end{aligned}6⋅97⋅923⋅9​=6⋅10−6=7⋅10−7=23⋅10−23​

    Write one equation, using the letter nnn, that states this pattern for every number nnn, and check it at n=4n = 4n=4.

    1. Hint 1

      Look for what stays the same from one fact to the next and what changes. A letter takes the place of the part that changes.

    2. Hint 2

      In each fact, find every place where the changing number appears; there is more than one. Each of those places gets the letter, and the 999 and the 101010 stay as they are.

    Answer

    n⋅9=n⋅10−nn \cdot 9 = n \cdot 10 - nn⋅9=n⋅10−n (or 9n=10n−n9n = 10n - n9n=10n−n); at n=4n = 4n=4 both sides equal 363636.

    Full solution

    In each fact the numbers 999 and 101010 stay the same, while one number changes from fact to fact.

    That changing number appears three times: multiplied by 999, multiplied by 101010, and subtracted at the end.

    Replace each appearance of the changing number with nnn:

    n⋅9=n⋅10−n.n \cdot 9 = n \cdot 10 - n.n⋅9=n⋅10−n.

    The same line can be written

    9n=10n−n.9n = 10n - n.9n=10n−n.

    Check at n=4n = 4n=4.

    The left side is 4⋅94 \cdot 94⋅9, which is 363636, and the right side is 4⋅10−44 \cdot 10 - 44⋅10−4, which is 40−440 - 440−4, also 363636.

    The two sides agree.

    One check is evidence, not proof.

    The distributive law proves the line for every number: write 999 as 10+(−1)10 + (-1)10+(−1), and use the law with a=na = na=n, b=10b = 10b=10 and c=−1c = -1c=−1:

    n⋅9=n(10+(−1))=n⋅10+n⋅(−1)=n⋅10−n.\begin{aligned} n \cdot 9 &= n(10 + (-1)) \\ &= n \cdot 10 + n \cdot (-1) \\ &= n \cdot 10 - n. \end{aligned}n⋅9​=n(10+(−1))=n⋅10+n⋅(−1)=n⋅10−n.​

    The last step uses n⋅(−1)=−nn \cdot (-1) = -nn⋅(−1)=−n: multiplying a number by −1-1−1 gives its opposite, and adding the opposite of nnn is subtracting nnn.

    Answer

    n⋅9=n⋅10−nn \cdot 9 = n \cdot 10 - nn⋅9=n⋅10−n (or 9n=10n−n9n = 10n - n9n=10n−n); at n=4n = 4n=4 both sides equal 363636.

    Key idea

    One equation with a letter states a pattern for every number at once, where arithmetic would need an endless list of facts.

  2. Problem 2 A negative number in two places

    Evaluate 2a2−3(a−5)2a^2 - 3(a - 5)2a2−3(a−5) when a=−2a = -2a=−2.

    1. Hint 1

      Write the value in parentheses wherever aaa appears, so that its minus sign stays attached. Then the order of operations decides what to work out first.

    2. Hint 2

      The square comes before the multiplication by 222, and a−5a - 5a−5 is worked out inside its parentheses before the multiplication by 333. Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding its opposite.

    Answer

    292929

    Full solution

    Substitute −2-2−2 for aaa in both places, keeping it in parentheses:

    2(−2)2−3(−2−5).2(-2)^2 - 3(-2 - 5).2(−2)2−3(−2−5).

    Exponents come before multiplication, so square first.

    The square of −2-2−2 is (−2)(−2)(-2)(-2)(−2)(−2), which is 444, positive because a negative times a negative is positive.

    The first term is therefore 2⋅42 \cdot 42⋅4, which is 888.

    Parentheses come first in the second term: −2−5-2 - 5−2−5 is −7-7−7.

    The second term is therefore 3(−7)3(-7)3(−7), which is −21-21−21.

    Finally subtract the second term from the first.

    Taking away −21-21−21 is the same as adding 212121:

    8−(−21)=8+21=29.\begin{aligned} 8 - (-21) &= 8 + 21 \\ &= 29. \end{aligned}8−(−21)​=8+21=29.​

    Two slips to avoid: 2a22a^22a2 means 2⋅a22 \cdot a^22⋅a2, so it is not (2a)2(2a)^2(2a)2, which would give 161616; and writing −22-2^2−22 without parentheses would be read as −(22)-(2^2)−(22), which is −4-4−4.

    Answer

    292929

    Key idea

    Put a substituted value in parentheses, then let the order of operations decide the order of the arithmetic.

  3. Problem 3 Where do the two sides agree?

    Evaluate both sides of m2+6=5mm^2 + 6 = 5mm2+6=5m at m=1m = 1m=1, m=2m = 2m=2, m=3m = 3m=3 and m=4m = 4m=4, and say at which of these values the equation is true.

    1. Hint 1

      An equation is true at a value exactly when its two sides give the same number there, so work out each side on its own.

    2. Hint 2

      On the left, square mmm first and then add 666; on the right, multiply mmm by 555. At m=1m = 1m=1, for example, the left side is 1+61 + 61+6.

    Answer

    True at m=2m = 2m=2 (both sides 101010) and at m=3m = 3m=3 (both sides 151515); false at m=1m = 1m=1 (777 against 555) and at m=4m = 4m=4 (222222 against 202020).

    Full solution

    An equation is true at a value when its two sides come out equal there.

    Work out each side on its own, squaring before adding on the left.

    At m=1m = 1m=1 the left side is 1+61 + 61+6, which is 777, and the right side is 5⋅15 \cdot 15⋅1, which is 555.

    The sides differ, so the equation is false at m=1m = 1m=1.

    At m=2m = 2m=2 the left side is 4+64 + 64+6, which is 101010, and the right side is 5⋅25 \cdot 25⋅2, which is 101010.

    The sides agree, so the equation is true at m=2m = 2m=2.

    At m=3m = 3m=3 the left side is 9+69 + 69+6, which is 151515, and the right side is 5⋅35 \cdot 35⋅3, which is 151515.

    The sides agree, so the equation is true at m=3m = 3m=3.

    At m=4m = 4m=4 the left side is 16+616 + 616+6, which is 222222, and the right side is 5⋅45 \cdot 45⋅4, which is 202020.

    The sides differ, so the equation is false at m=4m = 4m=4.

    Of these four values, then, the equation is true at m=2m = 2m=2 and m=3m = 3m=3 and false at m=1m = 1m=1 and m=4m = 4m=4.

    Because it fails at some values, it is not an identity, even though it holds at two of them.

    Answer

    True at m=2m = 2m=2 (both sides 101010) and at m=3m = 3m=3 (both sides 151515); false at m=1m = 1m=1 (777 against 555) and at m=4m = 4m=4 (222222 against 202020).

    Key idea

    An equation with a letter can be true at some values and false at others, and each value is tested by working out both sides there.

  4. Problem 4 One law for each step

    Each line of this chain follows from the line before it by exactly one law of addition:

    (−6+y)+6=(y+(−6))+6=y+((−6)+6)=y+0=y.\begin{aligned} (-6 + y) + 6 &= (y + (-6)) + 6 \\ &= y + ((-6) + 6) \\ &= y + 0 \\ &= y. \end{aligned}(−6+y)+6​=(y+(−6))+6=y+((−6)+6)=y+0=y.​

    For each of the four steps, name the law, and say what each letter of that law stands for in the step.

    1. Hint 1

      A law holds for every number, so its letters can stand for a letter such as yyy or a negative number such as −6-6−6. For each step, ask what changed: the order, the grouping, or a part replaced by something simpler.

    2. Hint 2

      Write out the four laws of addition with letters: a+b=b+aa + b = b + aa+b=b+a, (a+b)+c=a+(b+c)(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)(a+b)+c=a+(b+c), a+0=aa + 0 = aa+0=a and a+(−a)=0a + (-a) = 0a+(−a)=0. Then match each step to the law whose two sides look like its before and after.

    3. Hint 3

      In the third step, (−6)+6(-6) + 6(−6)+6 has the shape a+(−a)a + (-a)a+(−a). Decide what aaa must be for −a-a−a to be 666.

    Answer

    Step 1: commutative law, a=−6a = -6a=−6, b=yb = yb=y. Step 2: associative law, a=ya = ya=y, b=−6b = -6b=−6, c=6c = 6c=6. Step 3: inverse law, a=−6a = -6a=−6 (so −a=6-a = 6−a=6). Step 4: identity law, a=ya = ya=y. All four are laws of addition.

    Full solution

    Each law is a claim about every number, so its letters may stand for any numbers, a letter such as yyy and a negative number such as −6-6−6 included.

    Matching a step to a law means finding what each letter of the law stands for.

    Step 1 swaps the order of the two numbers inside the parentheses and leaves the +6+ 6+6 outside alone.

    That is the commutative law a+b=b+aa + b = b + aa+b=b+a with a=−6a = -6a=−6 and b=yb = yb=y, which turns −6+y-6 + y−6+y into y+(−6)y + (-6)y+(−6).

    Step 2 keeps the order yyy, −6-6−6, 666 and moves the parentheses.

    That is the associative law (a+b)+c=a+(b+c)(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)(a+b)+c=a+(b+c) with a=ya = ya=y, b=−6b = -6b=−6 and c=6c = 6c=6.

    Step 3 replaces (−6)+6(-6) + 6(−6)+6 with 000.

    That is the inverse law a+(−a)=0a + (-a) = 0a+(−a)=0 with a=−6a = -6a=−6: the opposite of −6-6−6 is 666, so −a-a−a is 666.

    Step 4 replaces y+0y + 0y+0 with yyy.

    That is the identity law a+0=aa + 0 = aa+0=a with a=ya = ya=y.

    Read as a whole, the chain shows that adding 666 undoes adding −6-6−6.

    Every step used a law that holds for every number, so this is true whatever number yyy is.

    Answer

    Step 1: commutative law, a=−6a = -6a=−6, b=yb = yb=y. Step 2: associative law, a=ya = ya=y, b=−6b = -6b=−6, c=6c = 6c=6. Step 3: inverse law, a=−6a = -6a=−6 (so −a=6-a = 6−a=6). Step 4: identity law, a=ya = ya=y. All four are laws of addition.

    Key idea

    A law holds for every number, so a letter or a negative number can stand in for its letters, and that is what lets a law justify a step.

  5. Problem 5 Every value, one value, or none?

    For each equation below, decide whether it is true for every value of yyy, for exactly one value of yyy, or for no value of yyy, and say how you know. Where it is true for exactly one value, give that value.

    Equation A: 6(y+2)=6y+126(y + 2) = 6y + 126(y+2)=6y+12

    Equation B: y+11=4y + 11 = 4y+11=4

    Equation C: y−2=y+5y - 2 = y + 5y−2=y+5

    1. Hint 1

      An equation that is true for every value can be shown to be so with a law. One that is not an identity may still be true for exactly one value, or for none at all.

    2. Hint 2

      For A, think of the distributive law. For B, ask which number gives 444 when 111111 is added to it. For C, compare y−2y - 2y−2 and y+5y + 5y+5 for the same yyy: how far apart are they?

    Answer

    A: true for every value of yyy (an identity). B: true for exactly one value, y=−7y = -7y=−7. C: true for no value of yyy.

    Full solution

    Equation A: the distributive law a(b+c)=ab+aca(b + c) = ab + aca(b+c)=ab+ac, with a=6a = 6a=6, b=yb = yb=y and c=2c = 2c=2, says that 6(y+2)6(y + 2)6(y+2) equals 6y+126y + 126y+12 for every number yyy.

    So equation A is true for every value of yyy; it is an identity.

    Equation B: its left side is 111111 more than yyy.

    For that to be 444, yyy must be 111111 less than 444, which is −7-7−7.

    Check:

    −7+11=4.-7 + 11 = 4.−7+11=4.

    A different value of yyy gives a different sum, so equation B is true for exactly one value, y=−7y = -7y=−7.

    Equation C: for every number yyy, the right side y+5y + 5y+5 is 777 more than the left side y−2y - 2y−2, since climbing from y−2y - 2y−2 to yyy adds 222 and climbing on to y+5y + 5y+5 adds 555 more.

    Two numbers that differ by 777 are never equal, so equation C is true for no value of yyy.

    At y=0y = 0y=0, for instance, the sides are −2-2−2 and 555.

    So equation A holds whatever yyy is, equation B singles out one number to be found, and equation C is satisfied by no number at all.

    Answer

    A: true for every value of yyy (an identity). B: true for exactly one value, y=−7y = -7y=−7. C: true for no value of yyy.

    Key idea

    An identity holds for every value of its letter, while another equation may hold for one value, for several, or for none, so decide which kind an equation is before working on it.

  6. Problem 6 Equivalent or not?

    For each pair below, decide whether the two expressions are equivalent. If they are, name the law or laws that make them equal for every value of kkk; if they are not, give a value of kkk at which they differ.

    Pair A: 3(k⋅4)3(k \cdot 4)3(k⋅4) and 12k12k12k

    Pair B: 4(k+3)4(k + 3)4(k+3) and k+12k + 12k+12

    Pair C: −2(k−3)-2(k - 3)−2(k−3) and 6−2k6 - 2k6−2k

    1. Hint 1

      Equivalent means equal for every value of kkk. A law that holds for every number can guarantee that, and one value where the two expressions differ rules it out.

    2. Hint 2

      For a pair you suspect is not equivalent, substitute a simple value into both. Zero can make two different expressions agree, so do not rely on it alone.

    3. Hint 3

      For a pair you suspect is equivalent, look for laws that turn one expression into the other: reordering and regrouping factors, or spreading a factor over both terms inside parentheses.

    Answer

    A: equivalent (commutative and associative laws of multiplication). B: not equivalent; at k=1k = 1k=1 they give 161616 and 131313 (any k≠0k \neq 0k=0 works). C: equivalent (distributive law, then commutative law of addition).

    Full solution

    Equivalent means equal for every value of kkk.

    A law that holds for every number can guarantee that; a single value where the two expressions differ rules it out.

    Pair A: by the commutative law of multiplication, k⋅4k \cdot 4k⋅4 equals 4k4k4k, so 3(k⋅4)3(k \cdot 4)3(k⋅4) equals 3(4k)3(4k)3(4k).

    By the associative law of multiplication, 3(4k)3(4k)3(4k) equals (3⋅4)k(3 \cdot 4)k(3⋅4)k, which is 12k12k12k.

    Both laws hold for every number, so the pair is equivalent.

    Pair B: try k=1k = 1k=1.

    The first expression is 4(1+3)4(1 + 3)4(1+3), which is 161616, and the second is 1+121 + 121+12, which is 131313.

    They differ, so the pair is not equivalent.

    The distributive law makes the 444 multiply the kkk as well as the 333, giving 4k+124k + 124k+12; the second expression is what you get by multiplying only the 333.

    Testing k=0k = 0k=0 alone would have misled you in pair B, because both expressions give 121212 there.

    Every other value of kkk exposes the difference.

    Both 4k+124k + 124k+12 and k+12k + 12k+12 add 121212, so they agree only where 4k4k4k equals kkk, and four times a number equals the number only when the number is 000.

    Pair C: the distributive law, with a=−2a = -2a=−2, b=kb = kb=k and c=−3c = -3c=−3, gives −2k+6-2k + 6−2k+6, since (−2)(−3)(-2)(-3)(−2)(−3) is 666.

    The commutative law of addition turns −2k+6-2k + 6−2k+6 into 6+(−2k)6 + (-2k)6+(−2k), which is 6−2k6 - 2k6−2k.

    So the pair is equivalent.

    Answer

    A: equivalent (commutative and associative laws of multiplication). B: not equivalent; at k=1k = 1k=1 they give 161616 and 131313 (any k≠0k \neq 0k=0 works). C: equivalent (distributive law, then commutative law of addition).

    Key idea

    A law settles that two expressions are equivalent, and one value where they differ settles that they are not; a value where they agree, such as 000 in pair B, settles neither.

  7. Problem 7 Stickers in symbols

    Leo has nnn stickers. Ava has 666 fewer than three times as many stickers as Leo, and together they have 585858 stickers.

    Write Ava's number of stickers as an expression in nnn, and use it to write an equation that says they have 585858 stickers together. Then decide, by substitution, whether Leo can have 161616 stickers.

    1. Hint 1

      Build Ava's amount in the order the words set it up: first three times Leo's amount, then six fewer than that. A sentence saying that two amounts are equal becomes an equation.

    2. Hint 2

      Six fewer than a quantity takes 666 away from that quantity, so the 666 is written after it, not before. The total is Leo's amount added to Ava's.

    3. Hint 3

      To test whether Leo can have 161616, put 161616 in place of every nnn in your equation and see whether its two sides agree.

    Answer

    Ava has 3n−63n - 63n−6 stickers; the equation is n+(3n−6)=58n + (3n - 6) = 58n+(3n−6)=58 (or an equivalent form such as 4n−6=584n - 6 = 584n−6=58). Leo can have 161616: then Ava has 424242, and 16+42=5816 + 42 = 5816+42=58.

    Full solution

    Three times as many stickers as Leo is 3n3n3n.

    Six fewer than that takes 666 away from 3n3n3n, so Ava has 3n−63n - 63n−6 stickers.

    The order matters: 6−3n6 - 3n6−3n would take 3n3n3n away from 666, the reverse of what the words say.

    The phrase for Ava's stickers names an amount and claims nothing, so 3n−63n - 63n−6 is an expression.

    The sentence saying that together they have 585858 stickers claims that two amounts are equal, so it becomes an equation:

    n+(3n−6)=58.n + (3n - 6) = 58.n+(3n−6)=58.

    Substitute 161616 for nnn.

    Ava then has 3(16)−63(16) - 63(16)−6 stickers, which is 48−648 - 648−6, or 424242.

    Together they have 16+4216 + 4216+42, which is 585858, so the two sides of the equation agree and Leo can have 161616 stickers.

    Here the letter nnn names one particular number, the number of stickers Leo actually has, and the equation is a question about it.

    That is a different job from the one a letter does in a law such as a+b=b+aa + b = b + aa+b=b+a, where it stands for every number.

    Answer

    Ava has 3n−63n - 63n−6 stickers; the equation is n+(3n−6)=58n + (3n - 6) = 58n+(3n−6)=58 (or an equivalent form such as 4n−6=584n - 6 = 584n−6=58). Leo can have 161616: then Ava has 424242, and 16+42=5816 + 42 = 5816+42=58.

    Key idea

    A phrase becomes an expression and a sentence that says two amounts are equal becomes an equation, which a substitution can then test.

  8. Problem 8 A number trick

    A number trick goes like this: pick a number, double it, add 101010, halve the result, and then subtract the number you picked.

    Omar tries the trick with 111, 777 and 202020, and ends on the same number each time. Explain why his three tries do not settle whether every starting number ends on that number. Then settle it, by following the trick with a letter nnn for the number picked.

    1. Hint 1

      A try tells you what happens to the number tried and nothing about the others. A claim about every starting number needs an argument that works whatever the number is.

    2. Hint 2

      Run the trick on a letter: picking nnn and doubling gives 2n2n2n. Write each later instruction as an operation on the expression you have so far.

    3. Hint 3

      Halving a sum halves each part, by the distributive law with the factor 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​. Then see what subtracting nnn does to the result.

    Answer

    Three tries leave every other starting number untested. Every starting number ends on 555: the trick gives 12(2n+10)−n\tfrac{1}{2}(2n + 10) - n21​(2n+10)−n, which equals 555 for every nnn.

    Full solution

    Omar's tries: 111 becomes 222, then 121212, then 666, then 555; 777 becomes 141414, 242424, 121212, then 555; and 202020 becomes 404040, 505050, 252525, then 555.

    Each try ends on 555.

    Those tries show what happens to 111, 777 and 202020, and nothing more.

    There are infinitely many other starting numbers, and agreement on three of them cannot rule out one that ends somewhere else.

    Settling a claim about every starting number takes an argument that does not depend on which number was picked.

    Follow the trick with a letter.

    Pick nnn.

    Doubling gives 2n2n2n, adding 101010 gives 2n+102n + 102n+10, and halving gives 12(2n+10)\tfrac{1}{2}(2n + 10)21​(2n+10).

    Subtracting the number picked leaves

    12(2n+10)−n.\tfrac{1}{2}(2n + 10) - n.21​(2n+10)−n.

    Halve with the distributive law, multiplying each part of the sum by 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​:

    12(2n+10)=12⋅2n+12⋅10=n+5.\begin{aligned} \tfrac{1}{2}(2n + 10) &= \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2n + \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \\ &= n + 5. \end{aligned}21​(2n+10)​=21​⋅2n+21​⋅10=n+5.​

    Here 12⋅2n\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2n21​⋅2n is (12⋅2)n(\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 2)n(21​⋅2)n by the associative law, which is 1⋅n1 \cdot n1⋅n because 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​ and 222 multiply to 111 (the inverse law).

    That is n⋅1n \cdot 1n⋅1 by the commutative law, and nnn by the identity law.

    Now subtract the number picked, writing it as adding −n-n−n:

    (n+5)−n=(5+n)+(−n)=5+(n+(−n))=5+0=5.\begin{aligned} (n + 5) - n &= (5 + n) + (-n) \\ &= 5 + (n + (-n)) \\ &= 5 + 0 \\ &= 5. \end{aligned}(n+5)−n​=(5+n)+(−n)=5+(n+(−n))=5+0=5.​

    The four steps use the commutative, associative, inverse and identity laws of addition, in that order.

    Every step used a law that holds for every number, so every starting number ends on 555, the number Omar kept reaching.

    Answer

    Three tries leave every other starting number untested. Every starting number ends on 555: the trick gives 12(2n+10)−n\tfrac{1}{2}(2n + 10) - n21​(2n+10)−n, which equals 555 for every nnn.

    Key idea

    Tries check the numbers tried; following the steps with a letter, using laws that hold for every number, shows what happens to every starting number at once.

  9. Problem 9 Rui's check

    Rui wants to know whether 4(x−5)+20=4x4(x - 5) + 20 = 4x4(x−5)+20=4x is true for every value of xxx. He substitutes x=6x = 6x=6 and writes

    4⋅6−5+20=39.4 \cdot 6 - 5 + 20 = 39.4⋅6−5+20=39.

    Since 393939 is not 4⋅64 \cdot 64⋅6, which is 242424, he concludes that the equation is not an identity.

    Find the mistake in Rui's check and redo the check correctly. Then decide whether the equation is an identity, and justify your decision.

    1. Hint 1

      A counterexample settles a claim only when its arithmetic is right. Compare what Rui wrote with the left side of the equation he was testing.

    2. Hint 2

      Substitution puts 666 where xxx was and keeps the parentheses, so the 444 multiplies all of 6−56 - 56−5. Work out the parentheses first.

    3. Hint 3

      Agreement at one value does not prove an identity. Rewrite 4(x−5)4(x - 5)4(x−5) with the distributive law and see what adding 202020 then does.

    Answer

    Rui dropped the parentheses; correctly, 4(6−5)+20=244(6 - 5) + 20 = 244(6−5)+20=24, which matches 4⋅64 \cdot 64⋅6. The equation is an identity: its left side equals 4x4x4x for every xxx.

    Full solution

    Substituting replaces xxx with 666 and keeps everything else, the parentheses included.

    Rui wrote 4⋅6−54 \cdot 6 - 54⋅6−5, which multiplies only the 666 by 444.

    The correct substitution is 4(6−5)+204(6 - 5) + 204(6−5)+20, where the 444 multiplies the whole of 6−56 - 56−5.

    Redo the check, working out the parentheses first:

    4(6−5)+20=4(1)+20=24.\begin{aligned} 4(6 - 5) + 20 &= 4(1) + 20 \\ &= 24. \end{aligned}4(6−5)+20​=4(1)+20=24.​

    The right side is 4⋅64 \cdot 64⋅6, which is also 242424, so the two sides agree at x=6x = 6x=6.

    Rui's value was never a counterexample; it came from a slip in the substitution.
