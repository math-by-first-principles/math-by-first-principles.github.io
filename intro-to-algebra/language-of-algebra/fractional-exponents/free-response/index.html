    1. What the exponent laws leave no choice about . Foundational, 10 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 10 pts

    Nobody decided what a fractional exponent should mean. The exponent laws were taken at their word on a fraction, and they left exactly one value available. This question runs the arithmetic, then the notation, then the argument itself.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Evaluate 642/364^{2/3}642/3 and 2433/5243^{3/5}2433/5, taking the root before the power. For each one, say which number in the exponent chose the index and which chose the power.

    2. B

Prove that a1/5a^{1/5}a1/5 then has only one possible value for a real aaa, and name it. Then say what is different about the same argument when the index is 444 instead of 555.

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Nothing here is a new definition to memorize. Every claim comes from applying one exponent law to an exponent that happens to be a fraction, and then asking what value could possibly satisfy the line you are left with.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

      A minus sign and a fraction in an exponent do two different jobs. Do them one at a time: settle where the reciprocal puts the power, then read the fraction as an index and a power.

    The answer

    Part B

    • 1(t6)5\dfrac{1}{\left(\sqrt[6]{t}\right)^{5}}(6t​)51​ names the same number as 1t56\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[6]{t^{5}}}6t5​1​, since the root and the power may be taken in either order

    Part C

    Raising a1/5a^{1/5}a1/5 to the fifth power gives aaa, so it is a real fifth root of aaa, and an odd index leaves exactly one of those: a1/5=a5a^{1/5} = \sqrt[5]{a}a1/5=5a​. With index 444 the same line leaves two candidates when aaa is positive, one when aaa is zero, and none when aaa is negative.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The denominator of the exponent is the index of the root and the numerator is the power, so rooting first keeps the bases small.

    For 642/364^{2/3}642/3 the index is 333, and 43=644^{3} = 6443=64:

    642/3=(643)2=42=16.64^{2/3} = \left(\sqrt[3]{64}\right)^{2} = 4^{2} = 16.642/3=(364​)2=42=16.

    For 2433/5243^{3/5}2433/5 the index is 555, and 35=2433^{5} = 24335=243:

    2433/5=(2435)3=33=27.243^{3/5} = \left(\sqrt[5]{243}\right)^{3} = 3^{3} = 27.2433/5=(5243​)3=33=27.

    The other order agrees, as it has to: 642=409664^{2} = 4096642=4096 and 40963=16\sqrt[3]{4096} = 1634096​=16. It simply asks you to take the cube root of a four-digit number instead of a two-digit one.

    Part B

    Translate one feature at a time. The index becomes a denominator, and a reciprocal becomes a minus sign.

    The fourth root of m3m^{3}m3 has index 444 and inside power 333, so it is m3/4m^{3/4}m3/4, and the reciprocal negates that exponent:

    1m34=1m3/4=m−3/4.\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{m^{3}}} = \frac{1}{m^{3/4}} = m^{-3/4}.4m3​1​=m3/41​=m−3/4.

    Running the dictionary the other way, the minus sign in t−5/6t^{-5/6}t−5/6 asks for a reciprocal, and the 56\tfrac{5}{6}65​ that is left is a sixth root with the power 555:

    t−5/6=1t5/6=1t56.t^{-5/6} = \frac{1}{t^{5/6}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt[6]{t^{5}}}.t−5/6=t5/61​=6t5​1​.

    The minus sign moved the power downstairs. It did not make either value negative, and with mmm and ttt positive both results are positive.

    Part C

    The rule is all that has been assumed, so everything has to come out of it. Raise a1/5a^{1/5}a1/5 to the fifth power and multiply the exponents:

    (a1/5)5=a15⋅5=a1=a.\left(a^{1/5}\right)^{5} = a^{\frac{1}{5}\cdot 5} = a^{1} = a.(a1/5)5=a51​⋅5=a1=a.

    So whatever a1/5a^{1/5}a1/5 names, its fifth power is aaa. That is exactly the defining property of a fifth root of aaa, so the rule has narrowed the field to those.

    For an odd index the field holds one number. Raising to an odd power keeps the sign and keeps the order, so two different real numbers never share a fifth power, and every real number has a real fifth root. One candidate survives, and it is forced:

    a1/5=a5.a^{1/5} = \sqrt[5]{a}.a1/5=5a​.

    With index 444 the first half is untouched, since (a1/4)4=a\left(a^{1/4}\right)^{4} = a(a1/4)4=a by the same multiplication of exponents. The second half fails, and it fails differently depending on aaa. An even power destroys the sign, so for a>0a > 0a>0 both a positive and a negative number raise to aaa, and the field holds two. For a=0a = 0a=0 only 000 does, so the field holds one and the argument survives at that single value. For a<0a < 0a<0 nothing real raises to aaa at all, and the field is empty. Uniqueness therefore fails for every positive aaa and existence fails for every negative one, which is why an even index arrives with a convention (take the non-negative root) and a restriction (the radicand must not be negative), while an odd index needs neither.

    In one line

    642/3=1664^{2/3} = 16642/3=16 and 2433/5=27243^{3/5} = 272433/5=27; 1m34=m−3/4\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{m^{3}}} = m^{-3/4}4m3​1​=m−3/4 and t−5/6=1t56t^{-5/6} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[6]{t^{5}}}t−5/6=6t5​1​; and (a1/5)5=a\left(a^{1/5}\right)^{5} = a(a1/5)5=a forces a1/5a^{1/5}a1/5 to be a fifth root of aaa, of which an odd index offers exactly one, so a1/5=a5a^{1/5} = \sqrt[5]{a}a1/5=5a​.

    2. Simplify first, then see what matches . Foundational, 11 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Foundational 11 pts

    Two radicals that share nothing on the page can turn out to be multiples of one and the same root, and two that look closely related can stay stubbornly apart. Simplifying is what tells the cases apart, and the closing part asks what the test for combining really requires.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Simplify 147\sqrt{147}147​ and 1923\sqrt[3]{192}3192​, leaving no factor of either radicand that is a perfect power for that root's index.

    2. B
      Part B.

      Simplify 108+243−48\sqrt{108} + \sqrt{243} - \sqrt{48}108​+243​−48​ as far as it will go.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Two radical terms that are each already in simplest form combine into one only when they agree in two respects. Name both, explain why each one is needed, and say why 5+53\sqrt{5} + \sqrt[3]{5}5​+35​ stays as two terms however much simplifying is attempted.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Nothing here can be decided from the radicands as they are printed. Get every root into its simplest form first, and only then ask whether any two of them belong together.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      Run through the perfect squares for one of these roots and the perfect cubes for the other, and take the biggest divisor you can find rather than the first one you notice.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      Write a sum of two matching radicals with the radical pulled out front as a common factor. Then ask what would have to be true of two roots for such a factor to exist at all.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    147=73\sqrt{147} = 7\sqrt{3}147​=73​ and 1923=433\sqrt[3]{192} = 4\sqrt[3]{3}3192​=433​.

    Part B

    11311\sqrt{3}113​.

    Part C

    They must share the index and the radicand. Only then is the radical a single common factor the distributive law can pull out. 5\sqrt{5}5​ and 53\sqrt[3]{5}35​ share a radicand but not an index, so neither is the other times a coefficient, and 555 is prime, so neither simplifies at all.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Split each radicand into the largest factor that is a perfect power for its index, times whatever is left over.

    For 147\sqrt{147}147​ the index is 222, so hunt for a perfect square. Since 147=49⋅3147 = 49 \cdot 3147=49⋅3 and 49=7249 = 7^{2}49=72:

    147=49 3=73.\sqrt{147} = \sqrt{49}\,\sqrt{3} = 7\sqrt{3}.147​=49​3​=73​.

    For 1923\sqrt[3]{192}3192​ the index is 333, so hunt for a perfect cube instead. Since 192=64⋅3192 = 64 \cdot 3192=64⋅3 and 64=4364 = 4^{3}64=43:

    1923=643 33=433.\sqrt[3]{192} = \sqrt[3]{64}\,\sqrt[3]{3} = 4\sqrt[3]{3}.3192​=364​33​=433​.

    In both answers the leftover radicand is 333, which has no square factor and no cube factor beyond 111, so neither will come down any further.

    Part B

    None of the three radicands is a perfect square, so simplify each one and then look at what is left.

    108=36 3=63,243=81 3=93.\sqrt{108} = \sqrt{36}\,\sqrt{3} = 6\sqrt{3}, \qquad \sqrt{243} = \sqrt{81}\,\sqrt{3} = 9\sqrt{3}.108​=36​3​=63​,243​=81​3​=93​.

    48=16 3=43.\sqrt{48} = \sqrt{16}\,\sqrt{3} = 4\sqrt{3}.48​=16​3​=43​.

    All three now carry the same index and the same radicand, so they are like radicals and the shared 3\sqrt{3}3​ comes out as a common factor:

    63+93−43=(6+9−4)3=113.6\sqrt{3} + 9\sqrt{3} - 4\sqrt{3} = (6 + 9 - 4)\sqrt{3} = 11\sqrt{3}.63​+93​−43​=(6+9−4)3​=113​.

    The subtraction is handled entirely by the coefficients. Combining like radicals never changes the radicand, which is what makes the last line a single step rather than a calculation.

    Part C

    Adding like radicals is not a new rule. It is the distributive law applied to a common factor that happens to be a root:

    63+93=(6+9)3.6\sqrt{3} + 9\sqrt{3} = (6 + 9)\sqrt{3}.63​+93​=(6+9)3​.

    That line works only because the very same number 3\sqrt{3}3​ sits inside both terms, and two things decide whether it does.

    The radicand must match. 3\sqrt{3}3​ and 5\sqrt{5}5​ are different numbers, and neither is the other multiplied by anything you could write as a coefficient, so a sum of them has no shared factor to lift out.

    The index must match too. 5\sqrt{5}5​ and 53\sqrt[3]{5}35​ are also different numbers: the first squares to 555 and the second cubes to 555, so they cannot both be 555 raised to the same power. In exponent form the mismatch is plain:

    5+53=51/2+51/3.\sqrt{5} + \sqrt[3]{5} = 5^{1/2} + 5^{1/3}.5​+35​=51/2+51/3.

    Those are two different powers of 555, and no exponent law adds two powers of a base into one; the laws combine powers that are multiplied. Simplifying cannot rescue it either, because 555 is prime: it has no perfect-square factor and no perfect-cube factor beyond 111, so each radical is already in its simplest form.

    In one line

    147=73\sqrt{147} = 7\sqrt{3}147​=73​ and 1923=433\sqrt[3]{192} = 4\sqrt[3]{3}3192​=433​; 108+243−48=113\sqrt{108} + \sqrt{243} - \sqrt{48} = 11\sqrt{3}108​+243​−48​=113​; and two radicals that are each in simplest form combine only when the index and the radicand both match, since only then is there a single common factor to pull out, which is why 5+53\sqrt{5} + \sqrt[3]{5}5​+35​ stays as two terms. The order matters: as part B showed, radicands that differ as written can agree once both radicals are simplified.

    3. A cube described by its volume alone . Application, 12 points. Question 3 of 5.

    Application 12 pts

    A solid cube is described only by its volume, so everything else about it has to be recovered from that one number. A fractional exponent makes the recovery a single step, and it also settles what happens to the outside of the cube when the inside is scaled up.

    1. A
      Part A.

    2. B

    3. C

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

    The answer

    Part A

    777 centimetres.

    Part B

    S=6V2/3S = 6V^{2/3}S=6V2/3.

    • S=6(V3)2S = 6\left(\sqrt[3]{V}\right)^{2}S=6(3V​)2 and S=6V23S = 6\sqrt[3]{V^{2}}S=63V2​ are the same formula written with radicals

    Part C

    The surface area is 444 times as large, since 82/3=48^{2/3} = 482/3=4. The volume factor steps out of the base by the rule for a power of a product, and the fractional exponent then cube-roots it and squares the result.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The volume of a cube is its edge used as a factor three times, so the edge is whatever number cubes to the volume. That is the cube root, which is the one-third power:

    s=3431/3=3433.s = 343^{1/3} = \sqrt[3]{343}.s=3431/3=3343​.

    Since 73=3437^{3} = 34373=343,

    s=7 cm.s = 7 \text{ cm}.s=7 cm.

    Check it forwards: 7×7×7=3437 \times 7 \times 7 = 3437×7×7=343. The unit follows the same route, since a root of cubic centimetres returns plain centimetres, which is what a length has to be measured in.

    Part B

    The two quantities do not speak to each other directly, so go through the edge, which both of them are built from. A cube of edge sss has six square faces:

    S=6s2,V=s3.S = 6s^{2}, \qquad V = s^{3}.S=6s2,V=s3.

    The second equation says the edge is the cube root of the volume, that is s=V1/3s = V^{1/3}s=V1/3. Put that into the first and multiply the exponents:

    S=6(V1/3)2=6V2/3.S = 6\left(V^{1/3}\right)^{2} = 6V^{2/3}.S=6(V1/3)2=6V2/3.

    The exponent 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ carries the whole story. The denominator 333 undoes the cubing that produced the volume, and the numerator 222 redoes the squaring that produces a face.

    Test it on a cube of edge 777. Through the formula, 6⋅3432/3=6⋅72=2946 \cdot 343^{2/3} = 6 \cdot 7^{2} = 2946⋅3432/3=6⋅72=294; by counting faces, 6×49=2946 \times 49 = 2946×49=294 square centimetres. The two routes agree.

    Part C

    Put 8V8V8V into the formula in place of VVV, and split the power across the product, which is what the product rule for roots licenses:

    6(8V)2/3=6⋅82/3⋅V2/3.6(8V)^{2/3} = 6 \cdot 8^{2/3} \cdot V^{2/3}.6(8V)2/3=6⋅82/3⋅V2/3.

    The new factor is 82/38^{2/3}82/3, and the exponent says cube root first, then square:

    82/3=(83)2=22=4.8^{2/3} = \left(\sqrt[3]{8}\right)^{2} = 2^{2} = 4.82/3=(38​)2=22=4.

    So the second cube has surface area 4⋅6V2/34 \cdot 6V^{2/3}4⋅6V2/3, four times the first, and no particular volume ever had to be named for that to be true.

    It is worth seeing why the answer is 444 and not 888. The cube root turns the volume factor 888 into an edge factor of 222, since eight times the volume means twice the edge, and the square then turns that edge factor into an area factor of 444. Lengths scale once, areas twice, volumes three times, and the exponent 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ is exactly two of those against three.

    In one line

    A cube of volume 343343343 cubic centimetres has an edge of 777 centimetres; the surface area in terms of the volume is S=6V2/3S = 6V^{2/3}S=6V2/3; and multiplying the volume by 888 multiplies the surface area by 82/3=48^{2/3} = 482/3=4, because the cube root turns the volume factor into an edge factor of 222 and the square turns that into an area factor of 444.

    Another way: Scale the edge instead of the volume

    Part C can be settled without part B's formula at all, by asking what 888 times the volume does to the edge. Volume is the cube of the edge, so eight times the volume means the edge is multiplied by 83=2\sqrt[3]{8} = 238​=2. Surface area is 6s26s^{2}6s2, and multiplying sss by 222 multiplies s2s^{2}s2 by

    22=4.2^{2} = 4.22=4.

    Same factor, reached by scaling the edge rather than by feeding a product into a fractional power.

    When it is worth it When the volume factor is a perfect cube, so the edge factor comes out whole. It is also the version worth keeping in your head, because it says in words what the exponent 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ means: root the volume down to a length, then square that length up to an area.
