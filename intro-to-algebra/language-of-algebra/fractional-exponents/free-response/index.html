  1. Problem 1 A three-quarters power

    Evaluate 6253/4625^{3/4}6253/4 without a calculator.

    1. Hint 1

      The two numbers in a fractional exponent do different jobs: one names a root and the other names a power. Decide which does which here.

    2. Hint 2

      The denominator 444 is the index, so begin with the fourth root of 625625625, the number whose fourth power is 625625625. A fourth power is the square of a square, and 625625625 is 25225^2252.

    3. Hint 3

      Raise the fourth root to the power the numerator names. Taking the root first keeps every number in the work small.

    Answer

    6253/4=125625^{3/4} = 1256253/4=125.

    Full solution

    In 6253/4625^{3/4}6253/4 the denominator 444 is the index of a root and the numerator 333 is a power.

    Taking the root first keeps the numbers small, so read the power as

    6253/4=(6254)3.625^{3/4} = \left(\sqrt[4]{625}\right)^3.6253/4=(4625​)3.

    The fourth root of 625625625 is the non-negative number whose fourth power is 625625625.

    Since 52=255^2 = 2552=25 and 252=62525^2 = 625252=625, the fourth power of 555 is 625625625.

    So

    6254=5.\sqrt[4]{625} = 5.4625​=5.

    The number −5-5−5 also has fourth power 625625625, but an even root names the non-negative one.

    Now cube the root:

    (6254)3=53=125.\begin{aligned} \left(\sqrt[4]{625}\right)^3 &= 5^3 \\ &= 125. \end{aligned}(4625​)3​=53=125.​

    Check the root by raising it to the index: 54=6255^4 = 62554=625.

    Taking the power first gives the same value, but only by way of 6253625^36253, a nine-digit number whose fourth root is much harder to find.

    Answer

    6253/4=125625^{3/4} = 1256253/4=125.

    Key idea

    In am/na^{m/n}am/n the denominator is the index of the root and the numerator is the power; taking the root first kept every number here small.

  2. Problem 2 Simplifying a cube root

    Simplify 1923\sqrt[3]{192}3192​, leaving no perfect-cube factor other than 111 under the root.

    1. Hint 1

      A root splits across a product, so look for a way to write 192192192 as a product in which one factor comes out of the cube root cleanly.

    2. Hint 2

      Test the perfect cubes 888, 272727, 646464 and 125125125 as factors of 192192192. The largest one that divides it does the job in a single step.

    3. Hint 3

      If you pull out a smaller perfect cube first, check the radicand that is left: it may still hold another perfect-cube factor.

    Answer

    1923=433\sqrt[3]{192} = 4\sqrt[3]{3}3192​=433​.

    Full solution

    The product rule for roots, ab3=a3 b3\sqrt[3]{ab} = \sqrt[3]{a}\,\sqrt[3]{b}3ab​=3a​3b​, lets a perfect-cube factor come out of the root.

    So look for the largest perfect cube that divides 192192192.

    Among the perfect cubes up to 192192192, the number 888 divides it, 272727 does not, 646464 does, and 125125125 does not.

    The largest is 646464, since 192=64⋅3192 = 64 \cdot 3192=64⋅3 and 64=4364 = 4^364=43.

    Split the root and evaluate the part that comes out:

    1923=64⋅33=643 33=433.\begin{aligned} \sqrt[3]{192} &= \sqrt[3]{64 \cdot 3} \\ &= \sqrt[3]{64}\,\sqrt[3]{3} \\ &= 4\sqrt[3]{3}. \end{aligned}3192​​=364⋅3​=364​33​=433​.​

    The radicand left is 333, which has no perfect-cube factor other than 111, so the root is fully simplified.

    Pulling out 888 first also works but takes two rounds.

    It gives 22432\sqrt[3]{24}2324​, and since 24=8⋅324 = 8 \cdot 324=8⋅3 still holds the perfect cube 888, a second round gives 2⋅2332 \cdot 2\sqrt[3]{3}2⋅233​, the same 4334\sqrt[3]{3}433​.

    Check by cubing: (433)3=43⋅3\left(4\sqrt[3]{3}\right)^3 = 4^3 \cdot 3(433​)3=43⋅3, and

    64⋅3=192.64 \cdot 3 = 192.64⋅3=192.

    Answer

    1923=433\sqrt[3]{192} = 4\sqrt[3]{3}3192​=433​.

    Key idea

    Pulling out the largest perfect cube that divides the radicand simplifies a cube root in one step; a smaller one leaves a radicand to check again.

  3. Problem 3 Adding and subtracting square roots

    Simplify 54−24+96\sqrt{54} - \sqrt{24} + \sqrt{96}54​−24​+96​ as far as possible.

    1. Hint 1

      The three radicands are different, so the terms are not like radicals as written. Simplify each root before trying to combine anything.

    2. Hint 2

      Pull the largest perfect-square factor out of each radicand. All three radicands turn out to be a perfect square times the same number.

    3. Hint 3

      Once every term is a whole number times the same radical, combine them as you would like terms, keeping each sign with its term.

    Answer

    565\sqrt{6}56​.

    Full solution

    Unlike radicals do not combine, so first put each term in simplest form by pulling out its largest perfect-square factor.

    Each radicand is 666 times a perfect square: 54=9⋅654 = 9 \cdot 654=9⋅6, 24=4⋅624 = 4 \cdot 624=4⋅6 and

    96=16⋅6.96 = 16 \cdot 6.96=16⋅6.

    Splitting each root with the product rule and taking the square root of the perfect square gives

    54=36,24=26,96=46.\begin{aligned} \sqrt{54} &= 3\sqrt{6}, \\ \sqrt{24} &= 2\sqrt{6}, \\ \sqrt{96} &= 4\sqrt{6}. \end{aligned}54​24​96​​=36​,=26​,=46​.​

    All three terms now carry the same radical 6\sqrt{6}6​, so they are like radicals, and the distributive law combines them:

    36−26+46=(3−2+4)6=56.\begin{aligned} 3\sqrt{6} - 2\sqrt{6} + 4\sqrt{6} &= (3 - 2 + 4)\sqrt{6} \\ &= 5\sqrt{6}. \end{aligned}36​−26​+46​​=(3−2+4)6​=56​.​

    The radicand 666 has no perfect-square factor other than 111, so 565\sqrt{6}56​ is fully simplified.

    Answer

    565\sqrt{6}56​.

    Key idea

    Simplify every radical first, because terms that look unlike can turn out to share a radical and then combine as like terms.

  5. Problem 5 The faces of a cube-shaped box

    A cube-shaped box has volume VVV cubic centimeters. If each edge is sss centimeters long, then V=s3V = s^3V=s3, and each of the six faces is a square of side sss.

    Explain why the area of one face is V2/3V^{2/3}V2/3 square centimeters. Then find the total area of the six faces of a box whose volume is 512512512 cubic centimeters.

    1. Hint 1

      Go from the volume to a face in two stages: first find the edge from the volume, then find the area of a face from the edge.

    2. Hint 2

      The edge is the number whose cube is VVV, that is, its cube root. Write that cube root as a power of VVV.

    3. Hint 3

      A face's area is the edge squared, so square the power you just wrote and use the power-of-a-power rule. For a volume of 512512512, look for the whole number whose cube is 512512512.

    Answer

    Edge: V1/3V^{1/3}V1/3 centimeters; one face: its square, V2/3V^{2/3}V2/3 square centimeters. For V=512V = 512V=512: a total area of 384384384 square centimeters (edges of 888 centimeters, faces of 646464 square centimeters).

    Full solution

    The edge sss satisfies s3=Vs^3 = Vs3=V, so sss is the number whose cube is VVV, the cube root of VVV.

    As a fractional exponent, the edge is

    s=V1/3.s = V^{1/3}.s=V1/3.

    A face is a square of side sss, so its area is s2s^2s2.

    Replace sss with V1/3V^{1/3}V1/3 and multiply the exponents by the power-of-a-power rule:

    s2=(V1/3)2=V2/3.\begin{aligned} s^2 &= \left(V^{1/3}\right)^2 \\ &= V^{2/3}. \end{aligned}s2​=(V1/3)2=V2/3.​

    So one face has area V2/3V^{2/3}V2/3 square centimeters.

    The exponent records the two stages in the usual way.

    Its denominator 333 takes a cube root, which turns the volume into the edge, and its numerator 222 squares, which turns the edge into the area of a face.

    For V=512V = 512V=512, take the cube root first.

    Since 83=5128^3 = 51283=512, each edge is 888 centimeters.

    Then square it:

    5122/3=(5123)2=82=64.\begin{aligned} 512^{2/3} &= \left(\sqrt[3]{512}\right)^2 \\ &= 8^2 \\ &= 64. \end{aligned}5122/3​=(3512​)2=82=64.​

    Each face has area 646464 square centimeters.

    The six faces together have area 6⋅64=3846 \cdot 64 = 3846⋅64=384 square centimeters.

    As a check, a cube with edges of 888 centimeters has volume 83=5128^3 = 51283=512 cubic centimeters, as required.

    Answer

    Edge: V1/3V^{1/3}V1/3 centimeters; one face: its square, V2/3V^{2/3}V2/3 square centimeters. For V=512V = 512V=512: a total area of 384384384 square centimeters (edges of 888 centimeters, faces of 646464 square centimeters).

    Key idea

    The exponent 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ reads as two steps: a cube root that turns a cube's volume into its edge, and a square that turns the edge into the area of a face.

  6. Problem 6 Two square plots on one road

    Two square plots of land have areas of 444444 square meters and 176176176 square meters. They sit next to each other along a straight road, each with one full side on the road and no gap between them.

    Find the total length of road that the two plots touch, exactly and in simplest radical form. Sam adds the two areas first and says the length is 220\sqrt{220}220​ meters. Without a calculator, show that Sam's value is wrong.

    1. Hint 1

      The side of a square is the square root of its area, and the road length is the sum of two sides. Work out each side before adding.

    2. Hint 2

      Simplify 44\sqrt{44}44​ and 176\sqrt{176}176​ by pulling out perfect-square factors. If the results share a radical, they are like radicals and add.

    3. Hint 3

      To test Sam's value, trap each side and 220\sqrt{220}220​ between whole numbers, using the perfect squares next to 444444, 176176176 and 220220220.

    Answer

    The road length is 6116\sqrt{11}611​ meters. Sam's value, 220\sqrt{220}220​ meters, is wrong and too short.

    Full solution

    A square's side is the square root of its area, so the plots have sides of 44\sqrt{44}44​ meters and 176\sqrt{176}176​ meters.

    The road length is their sum, 44+176\sqrt{44} + \sqrt{176}44​+176​ meters.

    Simplify each side by pulling out its largest perfect-square factor, using 44=4⋅1144 = 4 \cdot 1144=4⋅11 and 176=16⋅11176 = 16 \cdot 11176=16⋅11:

    44=211,176=411.\begin{aligned} \sqrt{44} &= 2\sqrt{11}, \\ \sqrt{176} &= 4\sqrt{11}. \end{aligned}44​176​​=211​,=411​.​

    Both sides now carry 11\sqrt{11}11​, so they are like radicals and add by the distributive law:

    211+411=(2+4)11=611.\begin{aligned} 2\sqrt{11} + 4\sqrt{11} &= (2 + 4)\sqrt{11} \\ &= 6\sqrt{11}. \end{aligned}211​+411​​=(2+4)11​=611​.​

    The road length is 6116\sqrt{11}611​ meters.

    Now test Sam's value by size.

    Since 36<4436 < 4436<44, the side 44\sqrt{44}44​ is more than 666, and since 169<176169 < 176169<176, the side 176\sqrt{176}176​ is more than 131313.

    So the road length is more than 191919 meters.

    But 220<225220 < 225220<225, so 220\sqrt{220}220​ is less than 151515.

    Sam's value is too small to be the road length.

    Sam turned a sum of two roots into the root of a sum, and no rule allows that: a root splits across a product, not across a sum.

    Written as a single root, the correct length is 36 11\sqrt{36}\,\sqrt{11}36​11​, which the product rule makes 396\sqrt{396}396​ meters, not 220\sqrt{220}220​.

    Answer

    The road length is 6116\sqrt{11}611​ meters. Sam's value, 220\sqrt{220}220​ meters, is wrong and too short.

    Key idea

    Simplify square roots into like radicals before adding them; adding the numbers under the roots first, as Sam did, gives a different number.

  8. Problem 8 Which square root is 1211/2121^{1/2}1211/2?

  10. Problem 10 True for every positive number?

    Decide whether each statement below is true for every positive number aaa. Prove each true one, and show that each false one fails for a particular positive value of aaa.

    Statement A: a+a=4a\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{a} = \sqrt{4a}a​+a​=4a​

    Statement B: a+a3=2a\sqrt{a} + \sqrt[3]{a} = 2\sqrt{a}a​+3a​=2a​

    1. Hint 1

      A statement that is true for every positive aaa needs an argument that works for all of them at once; a false one needs just one value where the two sides differ.

    2. Hint 2

      Both statements add two roots. Two roots combine into one term when they are like radicals, with the same index and the same radicand. For statement A, split the right side 4a\sqrt{4a}4a​ with the product rule for roots.

    3. Hint 3

      To test a value, choose one where every root in the statement is a whole number, such as a perfect sixth power. A value where the two sides happen to agree proves nothing about the others.

    Answer

    Statement A is true. Statement B is false; for example, at a=64a = 64a=64 the left side is 121212 and the right side is 161616.

    Full solution

    A true statement needs an argument that covers every positive aaa at once, and a false one needs only one positive value of aaa where the two sides differ.

    Statement A is true.

    The two terms are like radicals, so the distributive law combines them, and the product rule for roots then brings the 222 under the root as 4\sqrt{4}4​:

    a+a=2a=4 a=4a.\begin{aligned} \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{a} &= 2\sqrt{a} \\ &= \sqrt{4}\,\sqrt{a} \\ &= \sqrt{4a}. \end{aligned}a​+a​​=2a​=4​a​=4a​.​

    Adding the radicands instead would give 2a\sqrt{2a}2a​, which is a different number.

    Statement B is false.

    The terms a\sqrt{a}a​ and a3\sqrt[3]{a}3a​ share a radicand but not an index, so they are not like radicals and do not combine into 2a2\sqrt{a}2a​.

    Take a=64a = 64a=64, whose square root and cube root are whole numbers.

    The left side is 8+48 + 48+4, which is 121212, and the right side is 2⋅82 \cdot 82⋅8, which is 161616.

    At a=1a = 1a=1 the two sides of statement B agree, since both equal 222.

    One agreeing value proves nothing: the statement fails at a=64a = 64a=64, so it is not true for every positive aaa.

    Answer

    Statement A is true. Statement B is false; for example, at a=64a = 64a=64 the left side is 121212 and the right side is 161616.

    Key idea

    Like radicals, with the same index and the same radicand, add by the distributive law, so a+a\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{a}a​+a​ is 2a2\sqrt{a}2a​, which is 4a\sqrt{4a}4a​; a shared radicand with different indices, as in a+a3\sqrt{a} + \sqrt[3]{a}a​+3a​, does not make two roots like radicals.
