    1. Reaching every term, signs included . Foundational, 11 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Foundational 11 pts

    A factor written outside a parenthesis multiplies each term inside, however many terms there are and whatever signs they carry. These parts push that in three directions at once: more than two terms inside, a factor carrying both a sign and a variable, and a factor that is not written as a factor at all.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Expand −4(3x−7)-4(3x - 7)−4(3x−7) and 6x(2x2−x+4)6x(2x^2 - x + 4)6x(2x2−x+4), showing the individual products before you write each final expression.

    2. B
      Part B.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 4 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Before computing anything, a student says the expansion of 6x(2x2−x+4)6x(2x^2 - x + 4)6x(2x2−x+4) has to come out with two terms, because 6x6x6x is two things multiplied together.     Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Nothing here calls for a new rule. One law does all three parts, and the only questions are what exactly is playing the part of the outside factor and how many terms it has to reach.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part B

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

    The answer

    Part A

    −4(3x−7)=−12x+28-4(3x - 7) = -12x + 28−4(3x−7)=−12x+28 and 6x(2x2−x+4)=12x3−6x2+24x6x(2x^2 - x + 4) = 12x^3 - 6x^2 + 24x6x(2x2−x+4)=12x3−6x2+24x.

    Part B

    The quantity multiplying the parenthesis is −2-2−2, and the expression simplifies to −2x2+10x−12-2x^2 + 10x - 12−2x2+10x−12.

    Part C

    The number of terms inside the parenthesis fixes it: the law pairs the outside factor with each term exactly once, and the shape of that outside factor never enters the count. So the first product of part A produces two and the second produces three.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Multiply the outside factor against each term inside, one term at a time, and keep the sign that each product earns.

    In the first, the factor is the negative number −4-4−4, so both products come out with the opposite sign to the terms they came from:

    −4(3x−7)=(−4)(3x)+(−4)(−7)=−12x+28.-4(3x - 7) = (-4)(3x) + (-4)(-7) = -12x + 28.−4(3x−7)=(−4)(3x)+(−4)(−7)=−12x+28.

    In the second, the factor carries a variable, so the exponents add wherever xxx meets a power of xxx. The three products are 6x⋅2x2=12x36x \cdot 2x^2 = 12x^36x⋅2x2=12x3, then 6x⋅(−x)=−6x26x \cdot (-x) = -6x^26x⋅(−x)=−6x2, then 6x⋅4=24x6x \cdot 4 = 24x6x⋅4=24x:

    6x(2x2−x+4)=12x3−6x2+24x.6x(2x^2 - x + 4) = 12x^3 - 6x^2 + 24x.6x(2x2−x+4)=12x3−6x2+24x.

    Three terms inside produced three products, and none of them was skipped.

    Part B

    The minus sign belongs to the factor. The expression subtracts twice the parenthesis, so the quantity multiplying it is −2-2−2, not 222, and every product it makes carries that sign:

    −2(x2−3x+6)=−2x2+6x−12.-2(x^2 - 3x + 6) = -2x^2 + 6x - 12.−2(x2−3x+6)=−2x2+6x−12.

    The middle product is (−2)(−3x)(-2)(-3x)(−2)(−3x), a negative times a negative, so it is positive; leaving it negative is the single most common slip here.

    Now restore the leading 4x4x4x and gather the two like terms:

    4x−2x2+6x−12=−2x2+10x−12.4x - 2x^2 + 6x - 12 = -2x^2 + 10x - 12.4x−2x2+6x−12=−2x2+10x−12.

    The x2x^2x2 term and the constant have nothing to pair with, so that is as far as the tidying goes.

    Part C

    Look at what the distributive law actually says. In a(b+c+d)=ab+ac+ada(b + c + d) = ab + ac + ada(b+c+d)=ab+ac+ad the letter aaa stands for the whole outside factor, however many symbols it happens to be written with, and the law hands back exactly one product for each term inside the parenthesis:

    a(b+c+d)=ab+ac+ad.a(b + c + d) = ab + ac + ad.a(b+c+d)=ab+ac+ad.

    So the count is decided by the parenthesis, and the outside factor has no say in it. The first product in part A has two terms inside, so it produces two products; the second has three terms inside, so it produces three:

    6x(2x2−x+4)=6x⋅2x2+6x⋅(−x)+6x⋅4.6x(2x^2 - x + 4) = 6x \cdot 2x^2 + 6x \cdot (-x) + 6x \cdot 4.6x(2x2−x+4)=6x⋅2x2+6x⋅(−x)+6x⋅4.

    The student's rule looks only at the factor, so applied to −4(3x−7)-4(3x - 7)−4(3x−7), whose outside factor is a single number, it would predict one product where there are plainly two. A rule that never inspects the parenthesis cannot be counting what the law produces.

    Gathering like terms afterwards can of course shorten a result, but that is a second step and a separate question; neither expansion in part A has like terms to gather.

    In one line

    −4(3x−7)=−12x+28-4(3x - 7) = -12x + 28−4(3x−7)=−12x+28 and 6x(2x2−x+4)=12x3−6x2+24x6x(2x^2 - x + 4) = 12x^3 - 6x^2 + 24x6x(2x2−x+4)=12x3−6x2+24x. Since the quantity multiplying the parenthesis is −2-2−2, 4x−2(x2−3x+6)=−2x2+10x−124x - 2(x^2 - 3x + 6) = -2x^2 + 10x - 124x−2(x2−3x+6)=−2x2+10x−12. And the number of products an expansion produces is fixed by the number of terms inside the parenthesis, two for the first product of part A and three for the second.

    The answer

    Part A

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Each cell is itself a rectangle, so its area is the product of its own two side lengths.

    The cell with sides 2x2x2x and xxx has area 2x22x^22x2. The cell with sides 2x2x2x and 333 has area 6x6x6x. The cell with sides 555 and xxx has area 5x5x5x. The cell with sides 555 and 333 has area 151515.

    Adding the four and gathering the two like terms gives the whole:

    2x2+6x+5x+15=2x2+11x+15.2x^2 + 6x + 5x + 15 = 2x^2 + 11x + 15.2x2+6x+5x+15=2x2+11x+15.

    The same rectangle has width 2x+52x + 52x+5 and height x+3x + 3x+3, so its area is also the single product (2x+5)(x+3)(2x + 5)(x + 3)(2x+5)(x+3). The four cells are exactly the four ways of pairing a term of one side with a term of the other:

    (2x+5)(x+3)=2x2+11x+15.(2x + 5)(x + 3) = 2x^2 + 11x + 15.(2x+5)(x+3)=2x2+11x+15.

    Part B

    Two terms in the first factor and three in the second give 2×3=62 \times 3 = 62×3=6 pairings, so six products appear before anything is gathered.

    Take the terms of the first factor one at a time, each with its own sign:

    3x(x2+4x−1)=3x3+12x2−3x,3x(x^2 + 4x - 1) = 3x^3 + 12x^2 - 3x,3x(x2+4x−1)=3x3+12x2−3x,

    −2(x2+4x−1)=−2x2−8x+2.-2(x^2 + 4x - 1) = -2x^2 - 8x + 2.−2(x2+4x−1)=−2x2−8x+2.

    Those are the six products. Adding them, the like x2x^2x2 terms give 12x2−2x2=10x212x^2 - 2x^2 = 10x^212x2−2x2=10x2 and the like xxx terms give −3x−8x=−11x-3x - 8x = -11x−3x−8x=−11x, while the x3x^3x3 term and the constant have nothing to pair with:

    3x3+10x2−11x+2.3x^3 + 10x^2 - 11x + 2.3x3+10x2−11x+2.

    The constant +2+2+2 is the product of the two negative terms, one from each factor.

    Part C

    Separate two things that are easy to run together: what makes the rule true, and what makes it easy to see.

    The derivation of the rule never mentions area. Treat the first factor as a single quantity QQQ, distribute it across the second factor, then distribute each term of the second factor back across QQQ:

    Q(c+d)=Qc+Qd,Q=a+b.Q(c + d) = Qc + Qd, \qquad Q = a + b.Q(c+d)=Qc+Qd,Q=a+b.

    Every step there is the distributive law, and the distributive law is an identity: it holds for every number, positive, negative or zero. So the products it predicts are the same whatever the terms stand for, and part B's −2-2−2 is no exception.

    What the negative term does cost is the drawing. A side of length −2-2−2 is not a length, so no rectangle can be cut the way part A's rectangle was cut. The area model is an illustration of the rule in the case where every term is positive, and an illustration does not carry the argument.

    This is worth being exact about, because the reverse move, treating a picture that works in the easy case as the reason a rule holds in every case, is how a false claim survives longest.

    In one line

    The four cells have areas 2x22x^22x2, 6x6x6x, 5x5x5x and 151515, so the rectangle's area is 2x2+11x+152x^2 + 11x + 152x2+11x+15, which is the single product (2x+5)(x+3)(2x + 5)(x + 3)(2x+5)(x+3). The rule predicts six products for (3x−2)(x2+4x−1)(3x - 2)(x^2 + 4x - 1)(3x−2)(x2+4x−1), and gathering them gives 3x3+10x2−11x+23x^3 + 10x^2 - 11x + 23x3+10x2−11x+2. The rule survives a negative term because it is derived from the distributive law, an identity for every number; it is only the rectangle that needs every term to be positive.

    Another way: Distribute in two stages instead of tracking six pairings

    Rather than listing every pairing at once, take the first factor one term at a time and expand two ordinary single-factor products:

    (3x−2)(x2+4x−1)=3x(x2+4x−1)−2(x2+4x−1).(3x - 2)(x^2 + 4x - 1) = 3x(x^2 + 4x - 1) - 2(x^2 + 4x - 1).(3x−2)(x2+4x−1)=3x(x2+4x−1)−2(x2+4x−1).

    Each of those is exactly the kind of expansion question 1 was about, and adding the two results gives the same six products. Nothing new happens; the pairings are produced in two organized batches instead of all at once.

    When it is worth it When a factor has three terms or more, where a single list of pairings is easy to leave one short. It also keeps the sign of the second term in view, since it is written into the factor −2-2−2 from the start.
