    Another way: Split the fraction into a number part and a variable part

    When the top and the bottom are each a single term, the fraction comes apart into two smaller fractions, and each can be reduced on its own:

    8x312x=812⋅x3x=23⋅x2=2x23.\frac{8x^3}{12x} = \frac{8}{12} \cdot \frac{x^3}{x} = \frac{2}{3} \cdot x^2 = \frac{2x^2}{3}.12x8x3​=128​⋅xx3​=32​⋅x2=32x2​.

    The variable part is then just the rule for dividing powers of the same base, and the numerical part is ordinary fraction reduction.

    When it is worth it When the numerator and the denominator are single terms, so the split is legitimate. It is no help at all on part B, where the numerator is a sum: splitting a sum away from its denominator is exactly the move the lesson forbids.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Multiplying fractions means multiplying the tops together and the bottoms together, with no common denominator anywhere in sight:

    4x7⋅218x3=84x56x3.\frac{4x}{7} \cdot \frac{21}{8x^3} = \frac{84x}{56x^3}.74x​⋅8x321​=56x384x​.

    Now reduce. The coefficients 848484 and 565656 share the factor 282828, and the single xxx on top matches one of the three below:

    84x56x3=28x⋅328x⋅2x2=32x2.\frac{84x}{56x^3} = \frac{28x \cdot 3}{28x \cdot 2x^2} = \frac{3}{2x^2}.56x384x​=28x⋅2x228x⋅3​=2x23​.

    Only one denominator in the original carries a variable, namely 8x38x^38x3, and it is zero exactly when x=0x = 0x=0. So x=0x = 0x=0 is the single excluded value.

    Part B

    Turn the division into a multiplication by the reciprocal of the divisor, and factor the first numerator on the way in, since 9x+12=3(3x+4)9x + 12 = 3(3x + 4)9x+12=3(3x+4):

    3(3x+4)2x⋅8x23x+4.\frac{3(3x + 4)}{2x} \cdot \frac{8x^2}{3x + 4}.2x3(3x+4)​⋅3x+48x2​.

    The bracket 3x+43x + 43x+4 is now a factor of a numerator and of a denominator, so it cancels, and 8x22x=4x\dfrac{8x^2}{2x} = 4x2x8x2​=4x takes care of the rest:

    3⋅8x22x=24x22x=12x.\frac{3 \cdot 8x^2}{2x} = \frac{24x^2}{2x} = 12x.2x3⋅8x2​=2x24x2​=12x.

    Three separate conditions produce the restrictions, and they have to be collected before any cancelling happens. The denominator 2x2x2x needs x≠0x \neq 0x=0. The denominator 8x28x^28x2 needs x≠0x \neq 0x=0 again. And dividing by the second fraction is only meaningful when that fraction is not itself zero, which fails when its numerator vanishes:

    3x+4=0  ⇒  x=−43.3x + 4 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = -\frac{4}{3}.3x+4=0⇒x=−34​.

    So the work is valid for x≠0x \neq 0x=0 and x≠−43x \neq -\dfrac{4}{3}x=−34​. Notice where the second condition came from: 3x+43x + 43x+4 is a NUMERATOR in the problem as it was posed, and only becomes a denominator once the divisor has been flipped. Reading restrictions off the denominators of the original alone would have missed it entirely.

    Part C

    Start from what division asks for. Writing A÷BA \div BA÷B asks for the number that multiplies BBB to give AAA, and the answer is well defined exactly when one such number exists and no other does. Set BBB to zero and the request collapses: every candidate multiplies 000 back to 000, so there is no answer at all when AAA is not zero, and no single answer when AAA is zero. Either way A÷0A \div 0A÷0 names nothing. That is a fact about division itself, not about how fractions are written.

    So a divisor must be nonzero, and when the divisor is an algebraic fraction, being nonzero becomes a condition on the variable. A fraction is zero exactly when its numerator is zero and its denominator is not, so the NUMERATOR of the divisor is the thing to inspect. That is what makes this restriction easy to miss: numerators are the one place nobody looks for a forbidden value.

    Now the example. Its first fraction has xxx underneath, so x≠0x \neq 0x=0. Its divisor is x−53\dfrac{x - 5}{3}3x−5​, which is zero when x−5=0x - 5 = 0x−5=0, so x≠5x \neq 5x=5. Its remaining denominator, 333, rules out nothing. Rewriting confirms both:

    1x⋅3x−5=3x(x−5).\frac{1}{x} \cdot \frac{3}{x - 5} = \frac{3}{x(x - 5)}.x1​⋅x−53​=x(x−5)3​.

    Both surviving conditions now show up as denominators, which is a useful check, though the reason for the second one was settled before any flipping took place.

    Part A
