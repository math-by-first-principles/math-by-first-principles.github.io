Algebraic Fractions

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

You simplify 69\tfrac{6}{9}96​ to 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ by removing a common factor. The same idea works when the numerator and denominator contain variables, as in 6x29x\tfrac{6x^2}{9x}9x6x2​. Multiplication, division, and addition still follow the fraction rules you know. There is one extra habit to build: record which values would make a denominator zero, because those values are not allowed.

Learning goals

  • Exclude every value that makes the denominator zero
  • Cancel a shared factor, never a term
  • Multiply straight across, canceling first where you can
  • Divide by multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor
  • Add over a common denominator, distributing a leading minus

What an algebraic fraction is

An algebraic fraction, also called a rational expression, is a ratio of two expressions, one written over the other, where each expression is built only by adding, subtracting, and multiplying numbers and variables (no variable appears in an exponent, and none sits under a root):

numeratordenominator,for examplex+1x−2.\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}, \qquad \text{for example} \qquad \frac{x + 1}{x - 2}.denominatornumerator​,for examplex−2x+1​.

The bar means divide the numerator by the denominator. At x=5x=5x=5, for example, x+1x−2\tfrac{x+1}{x-2}x−2x+1​ becomes 63=2\tfrac{6}{3}=236​=2.

Dividing asks which number, multiplied by the denominator, gives the numerator. A value that makes the denominator zero is an excluded value: division by zero is undefined. No number multiplied by zero gives a nonzero numerator. If the numerator is also zero, every number passes that multiplication check, so division cannot select a unique answer. For x+1x−2\tfrac{x+1}{x-2}x−2x+1​, the denominator is zero at x=2x=2x=2, so we write x≠2x\ne2x=2. To find excluded values, set the original denominator equal to zero. For 5x\tfrac{5}{x}x5​, exclude 000; for x2x−6\tfrac{x}{2x-6}2x−6x​, exclude 333. Depending on its denominator, a fraction may exclude several values, one value, or none.

Check your understanding

For what value of xxx is x+3x−4\dfrac{x + 3}{x - 4}x−4x+3​ undefined?

Answer choices

A fraction is undefined exactly where its denominator is zero. Set the denominator equal to zero.

x−4=0  ⇒  x=4x - 4 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 4x−4=0⇒x=4

So x=4x = 4x=4 is excluded; every other value of xxx is fine.

Simplifying by canceling a common factor

To simplify an algebraic fraction, factor its numerator and denominator using the GCF method from the previous lesson, then cancel their common nonzero factors. This preserves the value because a shared factor divided by itself is 111. For example, 6x2=3x⋅2x6x^2=3x\cdot2x6x2=3x⋅2x and 9x=3x⋅39x=3x\cdot39x=3x⋅3, so

6x29x=3x⋅2x3x⋅3=2x3,x≠0.\frac{6x^2}{9x}=\frac{3x\cdot2x}{3x\cdot3}=\frac{2x}{3},\qquad x\ne0.9x6x2​=3x⋅33x⋅2x​=32x​,x=0.

The restriction stays even though the simplified denominator is just 333. At x=0x=0x=0, the original fraction has a zero denominator; cancellation cannot give it a value.

The same reasoning works for any common factor, including an entire expression.

Why a factor shared by top and bottom can be canceled#

For a numerical example, separate the common factor 444 in 2028\tfrac{20}{28}2820​:

5⋅47⋅4=57⋅44=57⋅1=57.\frac{5\cdot4}{7\cdot4}=\frac57\cdot\frac44=\frac57\cdot1=\frac57.7⋅45⋅4​=75​⋅44​=75​⋅1=75​.

Now replace 444 by any nonzero factor ccc. If the remaining denominator bbb is also nonzero, the fraction multiplication rule gives

acbc=ab⋅cc=ab⋅1=ab.\frac{ac}{bc}=\frac ab\cdot\frac cc=\frac ab\cdot1=\frac ab.bcac​=ba​⋅cc​=ba​⋅1=ba​.

No step depends on the chosen numbers. Canceling removes a factor of 111, so it preserves the value wherever the original denominator is nonzero.

∎

The same idea handles a numerator that is a sum, once you factor it. In x2+3xx\tfrac{x^2 + 3x}{x}xx2+3x​ the top factors as x(x+3)x(x + 3)x(x+3), so the shared xxx cancels:

x2+3xx=x(x+3)x=x+3,x≠0.\frac{x^2 + 3x}{x} = \frac{x(x + 3)}{x} = x + 3, \qquad x \neq 0.xx2+3x​=xx(x+3)​=x+3,x=0.

A shared binomial factor (a factor with two terms) cancels in the same way. Since 3x+6=3(x+2)3x + 6 = 3(x + 2)3x+6=3(x+2), the factor x+2x + 2x+2 is common to top and bottom of 3x+6x+2\tfrac{3x + 6}{x + 2}x+23x+6​:

3x+6x+2=3(x+2)x+2=3,x≠−2.\frac{3x + 6}{x + 2} = \frac{3(x + 2)}{x + 2} = 3, \qquad x \neq -2.x+23x+6​=x+23(x+2)​=3,x=−2.

Cancel common factors, never individual terms. A factor is multiplied by the remaining factors to make the whole numerator or denominator; a term is added to other terms. In x+33\tfrac{x+3}{3}3x+3​, the 333 in the numerator is added to xxx, so it is not a factor of the whole numerator. Canceling just those 333‘s would give the wrong x+1x+1x+1. At x=3x=3x=3, the original equals 222, but x+1x+1x+1 equals 444.

A valid split divides both terms by 333:

x+33=x3+33=x3+1.\frac{x+3}{3}=\frac x3+\frac33=\frac x3+1.3x+3​=3x​+33​=3x​+1.

Likewise, for x≠0x\ne0x=0, x+2x=1+2x\tfrac{x+2}{x}=1+\tfrac{2}{x}xx+2​=1+x2​, not x+2x+2x+2. The denominator divides every term of the numerator.

Worked example 1 Simplify three algebraic fractions

In each, factor the top and bottom, then cancel only a shared factor.

For 9x26x\dfrac{9x^2}{6x}6x9x2​, the greatest common factor of the numerator and denominator is 3x3x3x, since 9x2=3x⋅3x9x^2 = 3x \cdot 3x9x2=3x⋅3x and 6x=3x⋅26x = 3x \cdot 26x=3x⋅2:

9x26x=3x⋅3x3x⋅2=3x2,x≠0.\frac{9x^2}{6x} = \frac{3x \cdot 3x}{3x \cdot 2} = \frac{3x}{2}, \qquad x \neq 0.6x9x2​=3x⋅23x⋅3x​=23x​,x=0.

For x2+5xx\dfrac{x^2 + 5x}{x}xx2+5x​, factor the numerator as x(x+5)x(x + 5)x(x+5) and cancel the shared xxx:

x2+5xx=x(x+5)x=x+5,x≠0.\begin{aligned}\frac{x^2+5x}{x}&=\frac{x(x+5)}{x}\\&=x+5,\end{aligned}\qquad x\ne0.xx2+5x​​=xx(x+5)​=x+5,​x=0.

For 4x+8x+2\dfrac{4x + 8}{x + 2}x+24x+8​, factor the numerator as 4(x+2)4(x + 2)4(x+2), which shares the factor x+2x + 2x+2 with the denominator:

4x+8x+2=4(x+2)x+2=4,x≠−2.\frac{4x + 8}{x + 2} = \frac{4(x + 2)}{x + 2} = 4, \qquad x \neq -2.x+24x+8​=x+24(x+2)​=4,x=−2.

Each answer is the original fraction in lowest terms, valid everywhere except the excluded value.

The 666 on the bottom is a factor, but the 666 on top is a term, added to xxx. You may cancel common factors, never terms, so nothing cancels here.

x+66=x6+66=x6+1\frac{x + 6}{6} = \frac{x}{6} + \frac{6}{6} = \frac{x}{6} + 16x+6​=6x​+66​=6x​+1

That is not x+1x + 1x+1: the xxx is divided by 666 as well. The fraction is already in simplest form.

Multiplying algebraic fractions

To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators:

ab⋅cd=acbd,b≠0, d≠0.\frac ab\cdot\frac cd=\frac{ac}{bd},\qquad b\ne0,\ d\ne0.ba​⋅dc​=bdac​,b=0, d=0.

Canceling common factors before multiplying can keep the work smaller. For 2x3⋅9x2\tfrac{2x}{3}\cdot\tfrac{9}{x^2}32x​⋅x29​, write 9=3⋅39=3\cdot39=3⋅3 and x2=x⋅xx^2=x\cdot xx2=x⋅x. One factor of 333 and one of xxx cancel:

2x3⋅9x2=2⋅x⋅3⋅33⋅x⋅x=6x,x≠0.\frac{2x}{3}\cdot\frac{9}{x^2} =\frac{2\cdot x\cdot3\cdot3}{3\cdot x\cdot x} =\frac6x,\qquad x\ne0.32x​⋅x29​=3⋅x⋅x2⋅x⋅3⋅3​=x6​,x=0.

When a numerator or denominator is a sum, factor it first so any shared binomial can cancel.

Worked example 2 Multiply and simplify two products

Cancel any shared factor first, then multiply what remains.

For x+25⋅10x+2\dfrac{x + 2}{5} \cdot \dfrac{10}{x + 2}5x+2​⋅x+210​, the binomial x+2x + 2x+2 is a factor of one top and the other bottom, so it cancels before anything is multiplied:

x+25⋅10x+2=15⋅101=105=2,x≠−2.\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}&\frac{x+2}{5}\cdot\frac{10}{x+2}\\&=\frac15\cdot\frac{10}{1}\\&=\frac{10}{5}=2,\end{aligned}\\x\ne-2.\end{gathered}​5x+2​⋅x+210​=51​⋅110​=510​=2,​x=−2.​

For 3xx+2⋅x+26\dfrac{3x}{x + 2} \cdot \dfrac{x + 2}{6}x+23x​⋅6x+2​, the shared x+2x + 2x+2 cancels the same way, leaving only 3x6\tfrac{3x}{6}63x​ to multiply out:

3xx+2⋅x+26=3x1⋅16=3x6=x2,x≠−2.\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}&\frac{3x}{x+2}\cdot\frac{x+2}{6}\\&=\frac{3x}{1}\cdot\frac16\\&=\frac{3x}{6}=\frac{x}{2},\end{aligned}\\x\ne-2.\end{gathered}​x+23x​⋅6x+2​=13x​⋅61​=63x​=2x​,​x=−2.​

Canceling the binomial before multiplying kept both products short.

Dividing algebraic fractions

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal, with its numerator and denominator swapped. The divisor (the fraction you divide by) must be defined and nonzero. Thus both its denominator and its numerator must be nonzero:

ab÷cd=ab⋅dc,b≠0, c≠0, d≠0.\frac ab\div\frac cd=\frac ab\cdot\frac dc, \qquad b\ne0,\ c\ne0,\ d\ne0.ba​÷dc​=ba​⋅cd​,b=0, c=0, d=0.

These are two separate checks: a zero denominator makes the divisor undefined; a zero numerator makes a defined divisor equal to zero, which you still cannot divide by.

Why dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal#

A quotient is the number that, multiplied by the divisor, gives the dividend. The reciprocal of 45\tfrac{4}{5}54​ is 54\tfrac{5}{4}45​, because 45⋅54=1\tfrac{4}{5}\cdot\tfrac{5}{4}=154​⋅45​=1. So dividing 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ by 45\tfrac{4}{5}54​ means multiplying 23\tfrac{2}{3}32​ by 54\tfrac{5}{4}45​, giving 56\tfrac{5}{6}65​. We can check by multiplying back: 56⋅45=23\tfrac{5}{6}\cdot\tfrac{4}{5}=\tfrac{2}{3}65​⋅54​=32​.

For nonzero c,dc,dc,d, the same calculation gives

cd⋅dc=cddc=1.\frac cd\cdot\frac dc=\frac{cd}{dc}=1.dc​⋅cd​=dccd​=1.

Thus dc\tfrac{d}{c}cd​ is the multiplicative inverse of cd\tfrac{c}{d}dc​, the number that multiplies it to give 111. For b≠0b\ne0b=0, multiplying ab⋅dc\tfrac{a}{b}\cdot\tfrac{d}{c}ba​⋅cd​ by the divisor cd\tfrac{c}{d}dc​ returns ab\tfrac{a}{b}ba​. That is why

ab÷cd=ab⋅dc.\frac ab\div\frac cd=\frac ab\cdot\frac dc.ba​÷dc​=ba​⋅cd​.

The reasoning still holds when the letters are expressions, provided these nonzero conditions hold.

∎

After replacing division with multiplication by the reciprocal, factor and cancel as before. Keep every exclusion from the original fractions and from the divisor being nonzero.

Worked example 3 Divide two algebraic fractions

Flip the second fraction to turn division into multiplication, then cancel before multiplying what remains.

For x4÷x28\dfrac{x}{4} \div \dfrac{x^2}{8}4x​÷8x2​, the reciprocal of x28\tfrac{x^2}{8}8x2​ is 8x2\tfrac{8}{x^2}x28​. Write 8=4⋅28 = 4 \cdot 28=4⋅2 and x2=x⋅xx^2 = x \cdot xx2=x⋅x to expose the shared factors 444 and xxx:

x4÷x28=x4⋅8x2=x4⋅4⋅2x⋅x=2x,x≠0.\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}\frac{x}{4}\div\frac{x^2}{8}&=\frac{x}{4}\cdot\frac8{x^2}\\&=\frac{x}{4}\cdot\frac{4\cdot2}{x\cdot x}\\&=\frac2x,\end{aligned}\\x\ne0.\end{gathered}4x​÷8x2​​=4x​⋅x28​=4x​⋅x⋅x4⋅2​=x2​,​x=0.​

For 3x+6x÷x+24x\dfrac{3x + 6}{x} \div \dfrac{x + 2}{4x}x3x+6​÷4xx+2​, first factor 3x+6=3(x+2)3x + 6 = 3(x + 2)3x+6=3(x+2), then flip and multiply. Writing 4x4x4x as 4⋅x4 \cdot x4⋅x exposes the shared x+2x + 2x+2 and the shared xxx:

3x+6x÷x+24x=3(x+2)x⋅4xx+2=3(x+2)x⋅4⋅xx+2=3⋅4=12,x≠0,x≠−2.\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}&\frac{3x+6}{x}\div\frac{x+2}{4x}\\&=\frac{3(x+2)}{x}\cdot\frac{4x}{x+2}\\&=\frac{3(x+2)}{x}\cdot\frac{4\cdot x}{x+2}\\&=3\cdot4=12,\end{aligned}\\x\ne0,\quad x\ne-2.\end{gathered}​x3x+6​÷4xx+2​=x3(x+2)​⋅x+24x​=x3(x+2)​⋅x+24⋅x​=3⋅4=12,​x=0,x=−2.​

The binomial x+2x + 2x+2 and the single xxx both cancel, leaving the plain number 121212. The restriction x≠−2x \neq -2x=−2 comes from the divisor x+24x\tfrac{x + 2}{4x}4xx+2​ itself: its numerator x+2x + 2x+2 must be nonzero for its reciprocal to exist.

Adding and subtracting algebraic fractions

Addition and subtraction need a common denominator: the same denominator after rewriting each fraction without changing its value. For example, adding 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​ and 13\tfrac{1}{3}31​ means rewriting both in sixths. Algebraic fractions follow the same rule.

With a shared nonzero denominator ddd, combine the numerators and keep that denominator:

ad+bd=a+bd,ad−bd=a−bd.\begin{aligned} \frac ad+\frac bd&=\frac{a+b}{d},\\ \frac ad-\frac bd&=\frac{a-b}{d}. \end{aligned}da​+db​da​−db​​=da+b​,=da−b​.​

For example, 2x7+3x7=5x7\tfrac{2x}{7}+\tfrac{3x}{7}=\tfrac{5x}{7}72x​+73x​=75x​. When subtracting a whole numerator, put it in parentheses so the minus reaches every term. For x≠0x\ne0x=0,

4x+1x−x−2x=(4x+1)−(x−2)x=4x+1−x+2x=3x+3x.\begin{aligned}&\frac{4x+1}{x}-\frac{x-2}{x}\\&=\frac{(4x+1)-(x-2)}{x}\\&=\frac{4x+1-x+2}{x}\\&=\frac{3x+3}{x}.\end{aligned}​x4x+1​−xx−2​=x(4x+1)−(x−2)​=x4x+1−x+2​=x3x+3​.​

The −2-2−2 becomes +2+2+2, just as when subtracting expressions without fraction bars.

When denominators differ, multiply each fraction’s numerator and denominator by the same nonzero factor to make them match. First factor the denominators: split positive whole-number coefficients into prime factors and use the algebraic factoring you know. For example, 4=2⋅24=2\cdot24=2⋅2 and 6=2⋅36=2\cdot36=2⋅3, so their least common denominator is 2⋅2⋅3=122\cdot2\cdot3=122⋅2⋅3=12.

The least common denominator (LCD) uses each needed prime or algebraic factor as many times as it appears in the denominator that uses it most. Every denominator then divides it without a remainder, and no extra copies of a factor are needed. This can keep the arithmetic smaller than multiplying all denominators together.

Worked example 4 Combine fractions that already share a denominator

Keep the common denominator and combine the numerators, watching the sign on any subtraction.

Adding over the shared x+1x + 1x+1, the numerators are like terms:

5xx+1+2xx+1=5x+2xx+1=7xx+1,x≠−1.\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}&\frac{5x}{x+1}+\frac{2x}{x+1}\\&=\frac{5x+2x}{x+1}\\&=\frac{7x}{x+1},\end{aligned}\\x\ne-1.\end{gathered}​x+15x​+x+12x​=x+15x+2x​=x+17x​,​x=−1.​

Subtracting, the minus sign reaches both terms of the second numerator, so distribute it:

6x+5x+2−2x−4x+2=(6x+5)−(2x−4)x+2=6x+5−2x+4x+2=4x+9x+2,x≠−2.\begin{gathered}\begin{aligned}&\frac{6x+5}{x+2}-\frac{2x-4}{x+2}\\&=\frac{(6x+5)-(2x-4)}{x+2}\\&=\frac{6x+5-2x+4}{x+2}\\&=\frac{4x+9}{x+2},\end{aligned}\\x\ne-2.\end{gathered}​x+26x+5​−x+22x−4​=x+2(6x+5)−(2x−4)​=x+26x+5−2x+4​=x+24x+9​,​x=−2.​

The −4-4−4 turned into +4+4+4, giving the constant 5+4=95 + 4 = 95+4=9; treating the subtraction as reaching only the 2x2x2x would have produced the wrong 4x+14x + 14x+1.

Rewrite each fraction over the least common denominator, then add the numerators.

For x4+x6\dfrac{x}{4} + \dfrac{x}{6}4x​+6x​, the LCD of 444 and 666 is 121212. Multiply the first fraction by 33\tfrac{3}{3}33​ and the second by 22\tfrac{2}{2}22​:

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Canceling a term instead of a factor. You may cancel only a factor shared by the whole top and the whole bottom. So x+33\tfrac{x + 3}{3}3x+3​ is not x+1x + 1x+1, and x+2x\tfrac{x + 2}{x}xx+2​ is not x+2x + 2x+2. Factor first; if there is no shared factor, nothing cancels.
  • Forgetting the denominator cannot be zero. Every algebraic fraction excludes the values that make its denominator zero. In x+1x−2\tfrac{x + 1}{x - 2}x−2x+1​ the value x=2x = 2x=2 is off limits, and a simplified form like 3x+6x+2=3\tfrac{3x + 6}{x + 2} = 3x+23x+6​=3 still carries the restriction x≠−2x \neq -2x=−2.
  • Dropping a sign when subtracting a sum. A minus sign in front of a fraction reaches every term of its numerator. So 4x+1x−x−2x=3x+3x\tfrac{4x + 1}{x} - \tfrac{x - 2}{x} = \tfrac{3x + 3}{x}x4x+1​−xx−2​=x3x+3​, not 3x−1x\tfrac{3x - 1}{x}x3x−1​; the −2-2−2 becomes +2+2+2.
  • Adding without a common denominator. 3x+12\tfrac{3}{x} + \tfrac{1}{2}x3​+21​ is not 4x+2\tfrac{4}{x + 2}x+24​ or 42x\tfrac{4}{2x}2x4​. Rewrite each fraction over the LCD 2x2x2x first, giving 6+x2x\tfrac{6 + x}{2x}2x6+x​.
  • Treating multiplication like addition. To multiply, multiply straight across: ab⋅cd=acbd\tfrac{a}{b} \cdot \tfrac{c}{d} = \tfrac{ac}{bd}ba​⋅dc​=bdac​. Multiply the denominators rather than adding them; you do not need to make them equal first.
  • Flipping the wrong fraction when dividing. Reciprocate the divisor (the fraction after the division sign), never the first one: ab÷cd=ab⋅dc\tfrac{a}{b} \div \tfrac{c}{d} = \tfrac{a}{b} \cdot \tfrac{d}{c}ba​÷dc​=ba​⋅cd​.

Key takeaways

  • An algebraic fraction is a ratio of two expressions built from numbers and variables using addition, subtraction, and multiplication. Keep every excluded value from the original denominator, even after cancellation.
  • Cancellation removes common nonzero factors because cc=1\tfrac{c}{c}=1cc​=1 for c≠0c\ne0c=0. Thus acbc=ab\tfrac{ac}{bc}=\tfrac{a}{b}bcac​=ba​ when b,c≠0b,c\ne0b,c=0. Individual terms of a sum do not cancel.
  • Multiplication gives ab⋅cd=acbd\tfrac{a}{b}\cdot\tfrac{c}{d}=\tfrac{ac}{bd}ba​⋅dc​=bdac​ for b,d≠0b,d\ne0b,d=0. Factoring and canceling first can make the work easier; retain every original exclusion.
  • Division gives ab÷cd=ab⋅dc\tfrac{a}{b}\div\tfrac{c}{d}=\tfrac{a}{b}\cdot\tfrac{d}{c}ba​÷dc​=ba​⋅cd​ for b,c,d≠0b,c,d\ne0b,c,d=0, because cd⋅dc=1\tfrac{c}{d}\cdot\tfrac{d}{c}=1dc​⋅cd​=1. Exclude zeros of the divisor’s numerator as well as zeros of all original denominators.
  • Addition and subtraction use a common nonzero denominator. For d≠0d\ne0d=0, addition gives ad+bd=a+bd\tfrac{a}{d}+\tfrac{b}{d}=\tfrac{a+b}{d}da​+db​=da+b​, and subtraction gives ad−bd=a−bd\tfrac{a}{d}-\tfrac{b}{d}=\tfrac{a-b}{d}da​−db​=da−b​. Distribute a leading minus across every numerator term, and keep the original exclusions.

A bit of history (optional)

The word fraction comes from Latin for a breaking. In early calculus, a different kind of break in the reasoning started a long argument.

Some calculations divided by a small changing quantity, then treated that quantity as zero after simplifying. The methods produced useful answers, but explaining why they worked was harder. In 1734, George Berkeley, an Irish philosopher and bishop who had also written on mathematics, challenged these arguments in The Analyst. His pointed question was whether a quantity could be nonzero when you divided by it, yet zero when it became inconvenient. He famously asked whether these vanishing quantities were “ghosts of departed quantities.”

Berkeley’s pamphlet was also part of a dispute about religion and certainty. Mathematicians answered it with arguments of their own, and the foundations of calculus continued to develop. During the 1800s, precise definitions of limits gave a way to describe a quantity approaching zero without dividing by zero. The debate was about the reasoning behind successful methods, not merely whether their answers looked right.

Sources: Berkeley’s 1734 text; Berkeley’s life and the controversy.
