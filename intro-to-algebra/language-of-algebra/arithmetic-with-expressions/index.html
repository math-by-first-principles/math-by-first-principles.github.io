Arithmetic with Expressions

Intuition

In arithmetic, an expression like 3+4×23 + 4 \times 23+4×2 works out to a single number, 111111, and there is nothing left to say. Once a variable is present, such as in 3x+2+5x3x + 2 + 5x3x+2+5x, you usually cannot reach a single number, so instead you tidy it into the shorter 8x+28x + 28x+2. Both forms give the same value for every xxx, but the shorter one is easier to read and use. This lesson has two tools for that kind of tidying: gathering terms of the same kind, and handling the plus and minus signs correctly when whole expressions are added or subtracted. Both tools come from laws of arithmetic you already know; here you apply them to expressions that contain a letter.

Learning goals

  • Name a term's coefficient and variable part, and tell like terms from unlike
  • Combine like terms by adding coefficients, the distributive law reversed
  • Add and subtract expressions by dropping parentheses and flipping signs
  • Scale an expression by distributing across every term, including nested grouping
  • Check a simplification by substitution, and say what agreement and disagreement each show

The parts of an expression

An expression is any meaningful combination of numbers, variables, and operations that names a value, such as 3x2−5x+73x^2 - 5x + 73x2−5x+7. Its building blocks have names worth fixing now, because the rest of the lesson is about moving these blocks around without breaking anything.

A term is a piece of the expression that is added to the others. The expression 3x2−5x+73x^2 - 5x + 73x2−5x+7 has three terms: 3x23x^23x2, −5x-5x−5x, and 777. Because subtraction is adding the opposite, the sign in front of a term belongs to it, so the middle term is −5x-5x−5x, not 5x5x5x. Keeping the sign attached to its term is what makes the arithmetic below reliable.

The coefficient of a term is its numerical factor, and the variable part is what is left. In −5x-5x−5x the coefficient is −5-5−5 and the variable part is xxx; in 3x23x^23x2 the coefficient is 333 and the variable part is x2x^2x2. A term like xxx or x2x^2x2 that shows no number has an invisible coefficient of 111, since x=1xx = 1xx=1x. A constant term is a term with no variable at all, just a number, like the 777 above.

Finally, like terms are terms with the same variable part, meaning the same variables raised to the same powers. So 3x3x3x and −5x-5x−5x are like terms, and so are 4xy4xy4xy and 7yx7yx7yx, because yx=xyyx = xyyx=xy makes their variable parts identical. But 3x23x^23x2 and 5x5x5x are unlike, because x2x^2x2 and xxx are different variable parts, and 3x3x3x and 3y3y3y are unlike because they use different letters. Two constant terms, such as 777 and −2-2−2, are like terms too, since neither one has a variable part to differ on. Telling like from unlike is the whole game, so read the variable part carefully every time.

The coefficient is the term's numerical factor, sign included, and the variable part is everything left over.

In −6x3-6x^3−6x3 the numerical factor is −6-6−6, so the coefficient is −6-6−6, and what remains is x3x^3x3, so that is the variable part. Dropping the sign gives 666 instead of −6-6−6; reading only the base without its exponent gives xxx instead of x3x^3x3; and splitting off the xxx with the coefficient gives −6x-6x−6x and 333, which does not separate a number from a variable at all.

Combining like terms

To simplify an expression is to rewrite it as an equivalent expression that has no more like terms left to combine, and the main tool is combining like terms. The rule is short: like terms combine into one term by adding their coefficients; terms with different variable parts cannot combine that way.

It helps to picture terms as tiles. Let a long tile stand for one xxx and a small square for a 111. Then combining like terms is nothing more than sorting the tiles into piles of the same shape and counting each pile.

The sorting in the picture is exactly what the distributive law says in reverse. Here is that same idea written with algebra, so you can see why the tile trick always works.

Why like terms combine, and unlike terms do not#

The distributive law says a(b+c)=ab+aca(b + c) = ab + aca(b+c)=ab+ac. Read it from right to left and it becomes a rule for pulling out a shared factor: ab+ac=a(b+c)ab + ac = a(b + c)ab+ac=a(b+c). That reverse reading is the entire engine behind combining like terms.

Two like terms share the same variable part. Write that shared part as vvv, so the two terms are mvmvmv and nvnvnv for some numbers mmm and nnn. For 3x3x3x and 5x5x5x the shared part is v=xv = xv=x with m=3m = 3m=3 and n=5n = 5n=5; for 4x24x^24x2 and x2x^2x2 it is v=x2v = x^2v=x2. Because multiplication is commutative, mv=vmmv = vmmv=vm and nv=vnnv = vnnv=vn, so

mv+nv=vm+vn=v(m+n)=(m+n)v.mv + nv = vm + vn = v(m + n) = (m + n)v.mv+nv=vm+vn=v(m+n)=(m+n)v.

The two terms collapse into a single term whose coefficient is the sum m+nm + nm+n and whose variable part is unchanged. This is why 3x+5x=(3+5)x=8x3x + 5x = (3 + 5)x = 8x3x+5x=(3+5)x=8x, and why x+x=1x+1x=(1+1)x=2xx + x = 1x + 1x = (1 + 1)x = 2xx+x=1x+1x=(1+1)x=2x, since a bare xxx carries the invisible coefficient 111.

Unlike terms do not share a complete variable part, so there is no single vvv whose coefficient the two terms could combine around, and the step above never starts. In 3x+53x + 53x+5 one term has variable part xxx and the other is a constant; in 3x+3y3x + 3y3x+3y the parts are xxx and yyy. In 3x+3x23x + 3x^23x+3x2 the variable parts are xxx and x2x^2x2, even though both terms do carry a factor of xxx. None of these pairs shares a complete variable part, so none of them can be merged into one term by adding coefficients. A pair like 3x+3x23x + 3x^23x+3x2 can still be factored a different way later on, but that is a different move from combining like terms.

Adding and subtracting whole expressions

Often you need to add or subtract entire expressions, each wrapped in parentheses, such as (4x+3)+(2x+5)(4x + 3) + (2x + 5)(4x+3)+(2x+5). Adding is the easy case. The associative and commutative laws of addition, which hold for every number, let you erase the parentheses and reorder the terms however you like. So when you are adding, you simply drop the parentheses and combine like terms:

(4x+3)+(2x+5)=4x+3+2x+5=(4x+2x)+(3+5)=6x+8.(4x + 3) + (2x + 5) = 4x + 3 + 2x + 5 = (4x + 2x) + (3 + 5) = 6x + 8.(4x+3)+(2x+5)=4x+3+2x+5=(4x+2x)+(3+5)=6x+8.

Subtraction takes one extra moment of care, because subtracting an expression is not the same as subtracting only its first term. Subtracting a whole expression means adding its opposite, and the opposite of a sum flips the sign of every term inside.

Why a minus sign in front reverses every term#

To subtract an expression is to add its opposite, and the opposite of any quantity QQQ is (−1)⋅Q(-1) \cdot Q(−1)⋅Q. So a minus sign written in front of a parenthesis is really the factor −1-1−1 waiting to be distributed.

Take −(3x−5)-(3x - 5)−(3x−5). Rewrite the subtraction inside as an addition, 3x−5=3x+(−5)3x - 5 = 3x + (-5)3x−5=3x+(−5), then distribute the −1-1−1 across both terms:

−(3x−5)=(−1)(3x+(−5))=(−1)(3x)+(−1)(−5)=−3x+5.-(3x - 5) = (-1)\big(3x + (-5)\big) = (-1)(3x) + (-1)(-5) = -3x + 5.−(3x−5)=(−1)(3x+(−5))=(−1)(3x)+(−1)(−5)=−3x+5.

Every term inside came out with its sign reversed: 3x3x3x became −3x-3x−3x, and −5-5−5 became +5+5+5. Nothing here depends on the particular numbers, so the same reasoning gives the general rule −(a+b)=−a−b-(a + b) = -a - b−(a+b)=−a−b. Distributing a leading minus sign changes the sign of each term it reaches. Forgetting to flip the second term is the single most common error in this whole lesson.

∎

Worked example 2 Subtract (3x−5)(3x - 5)(3x−5) from (7x+2)(7x + 2)(7x+2)

Subtraction adds the opposite of the second expression, so distribute the minus sign across both of its terms:

(7x+2)−(3x−5)=7x+2−3x+5.(7x + 2) - (3x - 5) = 7x + 2 - 3x + 5.(7x+2)−(3x−5)=7x+2−3x+5.

The −5-5−5 inside became +5+5+5; missing that flip is the classic slip. Now reorder and combine like terms:

7x−3x+2+5=4x+7.7x - 3x + 2 + 5 = 4x + 7.7x−3x+2+5=4x+7.

To check, substitute a value into both the original and the result and confirm they agree. At x=3x = 3x=3 the original is (7(3)+2)−(3(3)−5)=23−4=19(7(3) + 2) - (3(3) - 5) = 23 - 4 = 19(7(3)+2)−(3(3)−5)=23−4=19, and the simplified form is 4(3)+7=194(3) + 7 = 194(3)+7=19. They match, which is a good sign the simplification is correct.

Check your understanding

Simplify (5x−3)−(2x−8)(5x - 3) - (2x - 8)(5x−3)−(2x−8).

Answer choices

Subtracting the second group flips the sign of each of its terms.

(5x−3)−(2x−8)=5x−3−2x+8=3x+5(5x - 3) - (2x - 8) = 5x - 3 - 2x + 8 = 3x + 5(5x−3)−(2x−8)=5x−3−2x+8=3x+5

The −8-8−8 becomes +8+8+8, which is the step most often missed; the answer is 3x+53x + 53x+5, not 3x−113x - 113x−11.

Scaling an expression by a number or a term

You already know the distributive law, a(b+c)=ab+aca(b + c) = ab + aca(b+c)=ab+ac. It lets you scale an entire expression by a single factor: multiply every term inside the parentheses by the factor outside. Scaling 3x+13x + 13x+1 by 222 gives

2(3x+1)=2(3x)+2(1)=6x+2.2(3x + 1) = 2(3x) + 2(1) = 6x + 2.2(3x+1)=2(3x)+2(1)=6x+2.

The factor can carry a sign, and the sign travels to every term it reaches. Scaling 3x+13x + 13x+1 by −2-2−2 instead gives

−2(3x+1)=(−2)(3x)+(−2)(1)=−6x−2.-2(3x + 1) = (-2)(3x) + (-2)(1) = -6x - 2.−2(3x+1)=(−2)(3x)+(−2)(1)=−6x−2.

The factor outside can even be a single term rather than a bare number. Distributing xxx across 2x+32x + 32x+3 still reaches every term, multiplying powers as it goes:

x(2x+3)=x(2x)+x(3)=2x2+3x,x(2x + 3) = x(2x) + x(3) = 2x^2 + 3x,x(2x+3)=x(2x)+x(3)=2x2+3x,

since x⋅2x=2(x⋅x)=2x2x \cdot 2x = 2(x \cdot x) = 2x^2x⋅2x=2(x⋅x)=2x2. Multiplying two multi-term expressions together, where both factors have more than one term, is a bigger job saved for the next lesson.

When a simplification nests grouping symbols inside one another, distribute one layer at a time and clear them from the inside out: finish everything inside the innermost parentheses before touching the symbol around it. Most simplifications from here combine scaling with the earlier skills, so distribute every product first and only then collect like terms.

Worked example 3 Distribute, then combine 2(3x+1)+3(x−4)2(3x + 1) + 3(x - 4)2(3x+1)+3(x−4)

Use the distributive law on each product first, keeping every sign:

2(3x+1)=6x+2,3(x−4)=3x−12.2(3x + 1) = 6x + 2, \qquad 3(x - 4) = 3x - 12.2(3x+1)=6x+2,3(x−4)=3x−12.

Now the expression is a plain sum, so gather like terms:

6x+2+3x−12=(6x+3x)+(2−12)=9x−10.6x + 2 + 3x - 12 = (6x + 3x) + (2 - 12) = 9x - 10.6x+2+3x−12=(6x+3x)+(2−12)=9x−10.

The two steps never blur together: distribute every product before you start collecting like terms.

Worked example 4 Simplify the nested expression 8x−2[3x−(x−4)]8x - 2[3x - (x - 4)]8x−2[3x−(x−4)]

Nested grouping symbols are cleared from the inside out. Start with the innermost parentheses, where a minus sign flips both terms:

3x−(x−4)=3x−x+4=2x+4.3x - (x - 4) = 3x - x + 4 = 2x + 4.3x−(x−4)=3x−x+4=2x+4.

Multiply by the 222 outside the brackets:

2[2x+4]=4x+8.2[2x + 4] = 4x + 8.2[2x+4]=4x+8.

Finally subtract that whole quantity from 8x8x8x, flipping its signs:

8x−(4x+8)=8x−4x−8=4x−8.8x - (4x + 8) = 8x - 4x - 8 = 4x - 8.8x−(4x+8)=8x−4x−8=4x−8.

Reaching outward before an inner layer is finished is what produces sign mistakes, so resolve one bracket completely before moving out to the next.

Checking a simplification by substitution

Simplifying is supposed to produce an equivalent expression, one that gives the same value for every input. So a quick substitution is the natural way to catch an error. Pick a convenient value, evaluate both the original and your simplified form, and compare. Agreement at one chosen value is evidence the two are equivalent, not proof of it. But a disagreement at any value instantly reveals a slip, almost always a dropped sign. Values like x=2x = 2x=2 or x=−1x = -1x=−1 tend to expose mistakes that x=0x = 0x=0 or x=1x = 1x=1 quietly hide.

Worked example 5 Simplify and check 2(x2+3x)−(x2−x)2(x^2 + 3x) - (x^2 - x)2(x2+3x)−(x2−x)

Distribute the 222, then subtract every term of the second group:

2(x2+3x)−(x2−x)=2x2+6x−x2+x.2(x^2 + 3x) - (x^2 - x) = 2x^2 + 6x - x^2 + x.2(x2+3x)−(x2−x)=2x2+6x−x2+x.

Combine the x2x^2x2 terms and the xxx terms:

(2x2−x2)+(6x+x)=x2+7x.(2x^2 - x^2) + (6x + x) = x^2 + 7x.(2x2−x2)+(6x+x)=x2+7x.

Now verify with a substitution, which a correct simplification will survive. At x=2x = 2x=2 the original is 2(4+6)−(4−2)=20−2=182(4 + 6) - (4 - 2) = 20 - 2 = 182(4+6)−(4−2)=20−2=18, and x2+7xx^2 + 7xx2+7x gives 4+14=184 + 14 = 184+14=18. The agreement is good evidence that the simplified form is equivalent; a mismatch, not a match, is what would have settled the question for certain.

3(2+4)−(2−1)=18−1=17,2(2)+13=173(2 + 4) - (2 - 1) = 18 - 1 = 17, \qquad 2(2) + 13 = 173(2+4)−(2−1)=18−1=17,2(2)+13=17

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Adding unlike terms. 3x+53x + 53x+5 is already fully simplified; it is not 8x8x8x. Only terms with the same variable part combine, so a variable term and a constant stay apart.
  • Forgetting to flip every sign after a minus. (7x+2)−(3x−5)=4x+7(7x + 2) - (3x - 5) = 4x + 7(7x+2)−(3x−5)=4x+7, not 4x−34x - 34x−3. The −5-5−5 becomes +5+5+5, because subtracting an expression negates all of its terms.
  • Distributing to only the first term. 2(3x+1)=6x+22(3x + 1) = 6x + 22(3x+1)=6x+2, not 6x+16x + 16x+1. The factor must reach every term inside the parentheses.
  • Losing the coefficient of 111. A lone variable counts as one copy, so x+x=2xx + x = 2xx+x=2x and 7a−a=6a7a - a = 6a7a−a=6a, never xxx or 7a7a7a.
  • Merging different powers. x2x^2x2 and xxx are unlike terms, so 2x2+3x2x^2 + 3x2x2+3x does not become 5x25x^25x2 or 5x35x^35x3; leave the two powers separate.
  • Changing the value while “simplifying.” Combining like terms must leave an equivalent expression. If a substitution check disagrees, some step changed the expression, most often a dropped term or a flipped sign.

Key takeaways

  • A term is a signed piece of an expression; its coefficient is the number factor and its variable part is the rest. A constant term has no variable, and a lone variable has coefficient 111.
  • Like terms share the same variable part (same letters, same powers). They combine by adding coefficients, 3x+5x=(3+5)x=8x3x + 5x = (3 + 5)x = 8x3x+5x=(3+5)x=8x, which is the distributive law read in reverse. Unlike terms such as 3x+53x + 53x+5, 3x+3y3x + 3y3x+3y, and 3x+3x23x + 3x^23x+3x2 cannot be merged into one term this way.
  • To add expressions, drop the parentheses and collect like terms. To subtract an expression, add its opposite, so a leading minus sign flips the sign of every term inside: (7x+2)−(3x−5)=4x+7(7x + 2) - (3x - 5) = 4x + 7(7x+2)−(3x−5)=4x+7. The commutative and associative laws make the reordering legal.
  • To scale by a number or a single term, distribute it across every term: 2(3x+1)=6x+22(3x + 1) = 6x + 22(3x+1)=6x+2. Clear nested grouping symbols from the inside out.
  • A substitution check guards your work: an equivalent expression agrees with the original at every value, so any disagreement flags a mistake. A single agreement is good evidence, not proof, since it tests only one value out of infinitely many.

A bit of history (optional)

Look at 4x2−x+7−2x2+3x4x^2 - x + 7 - 2x^2 + 3x4x2−x+7−2x2+3x. Five terms, and every one of them carries its own sign. That convenience is more recent than you might imagine. For most of the history of arithmetic there were no signs at all.

The plus and minus marks first reached print in 1489. They appear in a German textbook by Johannes Widmann, and there they perform no arithmetic whatever. Widmann was writing about barrels. A crate holding more than its official weight received one mark. A crate falling short of it received the opposite mark. They recorded a surplus and a shortfall, never an operation to carry out.

Within a few decades other writers had promoted them. The symbols stopped describing a crate and started commanding an addition or a subtraction. Every term in an expression could then wear its own sign in public.

That promotion is what makes this lesson possible. When the sign belongs to the term, you can shuffle the terms freely and gather the ones that match without a minus going missing. Rewriting 4x2−x+7−2x2+3x4x^2 - x + 7 - 2x^2 + 3x4x2−x+7−2x2+3x as 2x2+2x+72x^2 + 2x + 72x2+2x+7 is a few seconds of sorting, something no one could have done with Widmann’s marks alone.
