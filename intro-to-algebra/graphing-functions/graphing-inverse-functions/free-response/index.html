    1. Hint 1

      The inverse’s horizontal spread comes from the original graph’s vertical spread.

    2. Hint 2

      Read the highest and lowest original outputs and subtract them.

    Answer

    666 coordinate units.

    Full solution

    The original outputs run from −2-2−2 to 444.

    After reflecting across y=xy=xy=x, those become the horizontal endpoints of the inverse domain.

    Its span is therefore

    4−(−2)=6.4-(-2)=6.4−(−2)=6.

    The original horizontal span is a different measurement and becomes the inverse’s vertical span.

    Answer

    666 coordinate units.

    Key idea

    Reflecting a graph across y=xy=xy=x trades its horizontal and vertical extents, so the inverse’s horizontal span is the original’s vertical span.

  6. Problem 6 The level segment

    The graph shows all of fff on −3≤x≤4-3\le x\le4−3≤x≤4. Restrict the domain to c≤x≤4c\le x\le4c≤x≤4. Find the smallest ccc for which the restricted graph has an inverse function, and describe the inverse graph with its endpoints.

    Text description of this figure

    A square coordinate grid. The horizontal x axis and the vertical y axis each run from negative 4 to 6, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number, equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0. Three joined straight segments carry filled dots at the points (negative 3, negative 2), (0, 2), (2, 2) and (4, 5): the first segment rises, the second is level at height 2, and the third rises again. A dashed diagonal line labeled y = x runs from the lower left corner of the grid to the upper right corner. No boundary, retained piece or reflected curve is marked.

    1. Hint 1

      A level segment repeats its height at different inputs, and reflection turns that into a vertical segment.

    2. Hint 2

      Keep the right endpoint as required and move the left boundary until every remaining height is used once.

    Answer

    c=2c=2c=2; inverse is the segment from (2,2)(2,2)(2,2) to (5,4)(5,4)(5,4), with both endpoints included.

    Full solution

    Between inputs zero and two, the original graph remains at height two.

    Any c<2c<2c<2 retains at least two distinct inputs with that height, so the reflected graph fails the vertical line test.

    At c=2c=2c=2, only the final strictly rising segment survives, from (2,2)(2,2)(2,2) to (4,5)(4,5)(4,5).

    Each height now occurs once.

    Its reflection is the segment from (2,2)(2,2)(2,2) to (5,4)(5,4)(5,4), including both endpoints.

    Thus c=2c=2c=2 is both sufficient and the smallest possible boundary.

    Answer

    c=2c=2c=2; inverse is the segment from (2,2)(2,2)(2,2) to (5,4)(5,4)(5,4), with both endpoints included.

    Key idea

    Removing a level stretch can repair repeated outputs before the graph is reflected into an inverse.

  10. Problem 10 The three plotted points

    The complete graph of fff consists of the three plotted points. Kai says reflecting the graph across y=xy=xy=x leaves the whole graph unchanged, even though some individual points move. Is Kai correct? Explain and identify every point that stays fixed.

    Text description of this figure

    A square coordinate grid. The horizontal x axis and the vertical y axis each run from negative 3 to 4, with tick marks, number labels and gridlines at every whole number, equal unit lengths on both axes, and the origin labeled 0.     1. Hint 1

    Full solution

    The three input values and three output values are all distinct, so the graph represents a one-to-one function.

    Reflection exchanges (−2,3)(-2,3)(−2,3) with (3,−2)(3,-2)(3,−2) and leaves (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) unchanged.

    Thus the set of three plotted points is exactly the same after reflection, confirming Kai’s claim.

    The two off-diagonal points move to one another’s positions.

    Only (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) has equal coordinates and remains individually fixed.

    Answer

    Yes; (−2,3)(-2,3)(−2,3) and (3,−2)(3,-2)(3,−2) exchange places, while (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) is the only fixed point.

    An inverse graph may equal its original graph as a set while individual off-diagonal points exchange positions.
