    The answer

    Part A

    Part A

    Swapping coordinates reflects each point across y=xy=xy=x, so (a,b)(a,b)(a,b) becomes (b,a)(b,a)(b,a) for every point on hhh.

    Part B

    domain of h−1=range of h=[−1,6],range of h−1=domain of h=[−3,5].\text{domain of } h^{-1} = \text{range of } h = [-1,6], \qquad \text{range of } h^{-1} = \text{domain of } h = [-3,5].domain of h−1=range of h=[−1,6],range of h−1=domain of h=[−3,5].

    Part C

    The reflection across y=xy=xy=x is a swap of the plane's two axes: the horizontal axis (all points (t,0)(t,0)(t,0)) lands on the vertical axis (all points (0,t)(0,t)(0,t)), and the reverse.

    domain of f=horizontal spread,range of f=vertical spread.\text{domain of } f = \text{horizontal spread}, \qquad \text{range of } f = \text{vertical spread}.domain of f=horizontal spread,range of f=vertical spread.

    After the reflection, whatever used to be a horizontal spread becomes a vertical spread and whatever used to be vertical becomes horizontal. Since domain is defined as the horizontal spread and range as the vertical one, the reflected graph's domain must be the original's vertical spread (its range), and its range must be the original's horizontal spread (its domain). No other outcome is possible once the axes themselves are what moved.
