  1. Problem 1 Signed counters

    A collection contains three counters worth 111, seven worth −1-1−1, four worth iii, and two worth −i-i−i. Two counters worth 111 and three worth iii are then removed. Write the value of the remaining collection in the form a+bia+bia+bi.

    1. Hint 1

      Counters worth real values and counters worth imaginary values never mix, so keep a running total of each kind on its own.

    2. Hint 2

      Removing counters subtracts their combined value, and opposite counters cancel within each kind.

    Answer

    −6−i-6-i−6−i.

    Full solution

    Before the removal the real counters total

    3(1)+7(−1)=−43(1)+7(-1)=-43(1)+7(−1)=−4

    The imaginary counters total

    4i+2(−i)=2i4i+2(-i)=2i4i+2(−i)=2i

    So the collection starts at −4+2i-4+2i−4+2i, and the counters taken away are worth 2+3i2+3i2+3i together.

    Subtract part by part.

    (−4+2i)−(2+3i)=−6−i(-4+2i)-(2+3i)=-6-i(−4+2i)−(2+3i)=−6−i

    Answer

    −6−i-6-i−6−i.

    Key idea

    Complex addition and subtraction combine the real and imaginary parts separately.

    Answer

    34−13i34-13i34−13i.

  3. Problem 3 A quotient in standard form

    Write 12−14i5−3i\dfrac{12-14i}{5-3i}5−3i12−14i​ in standard form, then check the result by multiplying it by 5−3i5-3i5−3i.

    1. Hint 1

      A denominator with an imaginary part can be made real by multiplying above and below by one well chosen number.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the conjugate 5+3i5+3i5+3i, then divide each part of the new numerator by the real denominator.

    Answer

    3−i3-i3−i.

    Full solution

    The conjugate of the denominator is 5+3i5+3i5+3i, and it makes the denominator real.

    (5−3i)(5+3i)=34(5-3i)(5+3i)=34(5−3i)(5+3i)=34

    Multiply the numerator by 5+3i5+3i5+3i as well.

    (12−14i)(5+3i)=102−34i(12-14i)(5+3i)=102-34i(12−14i)(5+3i)=102−34i

    Divide each part by 343434.

    102−34i34=3−i\frac{102-34i}{34}=3-i34102−34i​=3−i

    The check multiplies back.

    (3−i)(5−3i)=12−14i(3-i)(5-3i)=12-14i(3−i)(5−3i)=12−14i

    Answer

    3−i3-i3−i.

    Key idea

    Multiplying above and below by the denominator's conjugate turns a quotient into standard form.

  4. Problem 4 Two-stage calculation

    A machine first adds 1−2i1-2i1−2i to its input, then multiplies the result by 2+i2+i2+i. Its output is 5+5i5+5i5+5i. Find the input in standard form.

    1. Hint 1

      Undo the operations in reverse order.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide by the nonzero number 2+i2+i2+i using its conjugate, then undo the addition.

    Answer

    2+3i2+3i2+3i.

    Full solution

    Let the input be zzz.

    Before the multiplication, its value was

    z+1−2i=5+5i2+iz+1-2i=\frac{5+5i}{2+i}z+1−2i=2+i5+5i​

    The denominator is nonzero.

    Multiply numerator and denominator by 2−i2-i2−i.

    5+5i2+i=15+5i5\frac{5+5i}{2+i}=\frac{15+5i}{5}2+i5+5i​=515+5i​

    This is 3+i3+i3+i.

    Undo the first stage.

    z=(3+i)−(1−2i)z=(3+i)-(1-2i)z=(3+i)−(1−2i)

    Therefore z=2+3iz=2+3iz=2+3i.

    Checking forward gives 3+i3+i3+i after the addition and 5+5i5+5i5+5i after multiplication.

    Answer

    2+3i2+3i2+3i.

    Key idea

    Reverse a sequence of complex operations in the opposite order to recover its input.

    1. Hint 1

      Read each arrow as a change, not as its endpoint alone.

    Full solution

    −6+11i−4i2-6+11i-4i^2−6+11i−4i2

    Replace i2i^2i2 with −1-1−1.

    (−2+i)(3−4i)=−2+11i(-2+i)(3-4i)=-2+11i(−2+i)(3−4i)=−2+11i

    A complex arrow represents its change in position, even when it does not start at the origin.

  6. Problem 6 Adjacent inputs

    Let z=4−5iz=4-5iz=4−5i. Find (z+1)2−z2(z+1)^2-z^2(z+1)2−z2 in standard form, and check it using an expansion with zzz left as a symbol.

    1. Hint 1

      One route evaluates both squares; another expands before substituting.

    2. Hint 2

      In the symbolic expansion the z2z^2z2 terms cancel.

    Answer

    9−10i9-10i9−10i.

    Full solution

    Here z+1=5−5iz+1=5-5iz+1=5−5i.

    (5−5i)2=−50i(5-5i)^2=-50i(5−5i)2=−50i

  8. Problem 8 A student's division

    To write 8+11i6−i\dfrac{8+11i}{6-i}6−i8+11i​ in standard form, a student multiplies the numerator and the denominator by 6−i6-i6−i. Explain why that choice leaves the quotient no nearer standard form, and give the correct standard form.

  10. Problem 10 A negative real square

    Let z=a+biz=a+biz=a+bi with real aaa and bbb. A student says that if z2z^2z2 is a negative real number, then a=0a=0a=0 and b≠0b\ne0b=0. Is this necessarily true? Explain.

    If b=0b=0b=0, its real part would be a2a^2a2, which is not negative.

    Therefore b≠0b\ne0b=0 and a=0a=0a=0.

    With these conditions, the square is −b2-b^2−b2, which is indeed negative.

    Answer

    Yes; a=0a=0a=0 and b≠0b\ne0b=0.

    Key idea

    A negative real square of a complex number forces the original number to be nonzero and pure imaginary.
