Complex Numbers and the Quadratic Formula: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

The imaginary unit iii
The number defined by i2=−1i^2 = -1i2=−1, equivalently i=−1i = \sqrt{-1}i=−1​.
Pure imaginary number
A real multiple of iii, such as 3i3i3i or −12i-\tfrac{1}{2}i−21​i.
Complex number, standard form a+bia + bia+bi
A real part aaa plus an imaginary part bbb. Every answer should end in this form; a quotient with iii still in the denominator is unfinished.
Real and imaginary parts
For z=a+biz = a + biz=a+bi: Re⁡(z)=a\operatorname{Re}(z) = aRe(z)=a and Im⁡(z)=b\operatorname{Im}(z) = bIm(z)=b, a real number, not bibibi.
Equality of complex numbers
a+bi=c+dia + bi = c + dia+bi=c+di exactly when a=ca = ca=c and b=db = db=d: the parts match separately.
Complex conjugate z‾\overline{z}z
For z=a+biz = a + biz=a+bi, the conjugate is a−bia - bia−bi: only the imaginary part flips sign.
Completed-square form (x−h)2+k(x - h)^2 + k(x−h)2+k
For a quadratic with leading coefficient 111, the quadratic rewritten as a perfect square plus a constant, solvable by one square root. Divide the whole equation by the leading coefficient first when it is not 111.
Standard form ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0
A quadratic set equal to zero, with a≠0a \neq 0a=0. Read aaa, bbb, ccc, with their signs, only from this form.
The discriminant D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4ac
The quantity under the root in the quadratic formula. Its sign alone decides the kind of roots.
Complex conjugate pair
The two roots p+qip + qip+qi and p−qip - qip−qi that a quadratic with real coefficients has when D<0D < 0D<0: same real part, opposite imaginary parts.

Formulas and theorems

  • Definition of iii

    i2=−1i^2 = -1i2=−1

    Use when Always; it is the definition. Every simplification of a power of iii or a root of a negative traces back to it.

    e.g. (3i)2=9i2=−9(3i)^2 = 9i^2 = -9(3i)2=9i2=−9.

  • Powers of iii

    i1=i,i2=−1i3=−i,i4=1\begin{gathered} i^1 = i, \quad i^2 = -1 \\ i^3 = -i, \quad i^4 = 1 \end{gathered}i1=i,i2=−1i3=−i,i4=1​
    Text description

    The values 1, i, minus 1 and minus i sit a quarter turn apart around the origin, and each multiplication by i steps counterclockwise to the next, so four steps return to 1.

    Use when The cycle repeats every four: reduce ini^nin to the power given by the remainder of nnn on division by 444 (remainder 000 gives 111).

  • Square root of a negative

    −n=in\sqrt{-n} = i\sqrt{n}−n​=in​

    Use when n>0n > 0n>0. Convert BEFORE multiplying roots: a⋅b=ab\sqrt{a}\cdot\sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}a​⋅b​=ab​ fails when both radicands are negative.

    e.g. −12=i12=2i3\sqrt{-12} = i\sqrt{12} = 2i\sqrt{3}−12​=i12​=2i3​.

  • Addition and subtraction

    (a+bi)±(c+di)=(a±c)+(b±d)i\begin{gathered} (a + bi) \pm (c + di) \\ = (a \pm c) + (b \pm d)i \end{gathered}(a+bi)±(c+di)=(a±c)+(b±d)i​
    Text description

    Arrows from the origin to 3 plus i and to 1 plus 2i are two sides of a parallelogram whose far corner is their sum, 4 plus 3i.

    Use when Any two complex numbers: real parts combine with real parts, iii parts with iii parts.

    e.g. (6−2i)−(3+4i)=3−6i(6 - 2i) - (3 + 4i) = 3 - 6i(6−2i)−(3+4i)=3−6i: the minus reaches both parts.

  • Multiplication

    (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i\begin{gathered} (a + bi)(c + di) \\ = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i \end{gathered}(a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i​

    Use when Any two complex numbers. In practice: expand as binomials, then replace i2i^2i2 with −1-1−1 before collecting.

    e.g. (1+2i)(3−i)=3−i+6i−2i2=5+5i(1 + 2i)(3 - i) = 3 - i + 6i - 2i^2 = 5 + 5i(1+2i)(3−i)=3−i+6i−2i2=5+5i.

  • Squaring a complex number

    (a+bi)2=(a2−b2)+2abi(a + bi)^2 = (a^2 - b^2) + 2abi(a+bi)2=(a2−b2)+2abi

    Use when Expand with the square-of-a-binomial pattern, then replace i2i^2i2 with −1-1−1. A higher power is repeated multiplication: square, then multiply by the original number again.

    e.g. (2+3i)2=4+12i+9i2=−5+12i(2 + 3i)^2 = 4 + 12i + 9i^2 = -5 + 12i(2+3i)2=4+12i+9i2=−5+12i.

  • Conjugate product

    (a+bi)(a−bi)=a2+b2(a + bi)(a - bi) = a^2 + b^2(a+bi)(a−bi)=a2+b2
    Text description

    The points for 5 plus 2i and 5 minus 2i sit the same distance directly above and below the real axis, so a conjugate is the mirror image across that axis.

    Use when Any complex number; the result is real and nonnegative. A sum, not the difference of squares.

    e.g. (5+2i)(5−2i)=25+4=29(5 + 2i)(5 - 2i) = 25 + 4 = 29(5+2i)(5−2i)=25+4=29.

  • Division

    a+bic+di=(a+bi)(c−di)c2+d2\frac{a + bi}{c + di} = \frac{(a + bi)(c - di)}{c^2 + d^2}c+dia+bi​=c2+d2(a+bi)(c−di)​

    Use when c+di≠0c + di \neq 0c+di=0. Multiply top and bottom by the denominator's conjugate, then finish in standard form.

  • Completing the square

    x2+bx+(b2)2=(x+b2)2x^2 + bx + \left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 = \left(x + \tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2x2+bx+(2b​)2=(x+2b​)2

    Use when Leading coefficient 111: divide the equation by aaa first if a≠1a \neq 1a=1, then use the resulting coefficient of xxx as bbb below. In an equation, add (b2)2\left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2(2b​)2 to both sides; in an expression, add and subtract it in the same line.

    e.g. x2+8x+16=(x+4)2x^2 + 8x + 16 = (x + 4)^2x2+8x+16=(x+4)2.

  • The quadratic formula

    x=−b±b2−4ac2ax = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}x=2a−b±b2−4ac​​

    Use when a≠0a \neq 0a=0, and the equation must be in standard form ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0 before reading the coefficients. The whole numerator sits over 2a2a2a.

  • The discriminant

    D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4ac

    Use when Coefficients read from standard form, signs included.

  • Root classification by DDD

    D>0D > 0D>0: two distinct real roots. D=0D = 0D=0: one repeated real root, x=−b2ax = -\tfrac{b}{2a}x=−2ab​. D<0D < 0D<0: a complex conjugate pair.

    Use when Standard form with real coefficients. Answers how many and what kind of roots without solving.

  • Projectile height model

    h=−16t2+v0t+h0h = -16t^2 + v_0 t + h_0h=−16t2+v0​t+h0​

    Use when Height in feet, ttt in seconds, v0v_0v0​ the initial upward speed, h0h_0h0​ the launch height; in metres the first term is −4.9t2-4.9t^2−4.9t2. Set h=0h = 0h=0 for landing, h=Hh = Hh=H for a target height.

    e.g. Thrown up at 323232 ft/s from 484848 ft: h=−16t2+32t+48h = -16t^2 + 32t + 48h=−16t2+32t+48.

Problem types, step by step

Simplify a power of iii

  1. Divide the exponent by 444 and keep the remainder.
  2. Replace: remainder 0→10 \to 10→1, 1→i1 \to i1→i, 2→−12 \to -12→−1, 3→−i3 \to -i3→−i. Never multiply the exponent out.

e.g. i23i^{23}i23: 23=4⋅5+323 = 4 \cdot 5 + 323=4⋅5+3, so i23=i3=−ii^{23} = i^3 = -ii23=i3=−i.

Simplify roots of negatives, alone or in products

  1. Rewrite every −n\sqrt{-n}−n​ as ini\sqrt{n}in​ first, before any other move.
  2. Simplify the remaining real radical: 12=23\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}12​=23​.
  3. Multiply or combine, replacing i2i^2i2 with −1-1−1.

e.g. −4⋅−9=(2i)(3i)=6i2=−6\sqrt{-4}\cdot\sqrt{-9} = (2i)(3i) = 6i^2 = -6−4​⋅−9​=(2i)(3i)=6i2=−6.

Add, subtract, or multiply complex numbers

  1. Distribute: a subtraction's minus reaches both parts; a product expands like binomials.
  2. Replace every i2i^2i2 with −1-1−1.
  3. Combine real parts together and iii parts together.
  4. Report the result as a+bia + bia+bi.

e.g. (2−i)(3+2i)=6+4i−3i−2i2=8+i(2 - i)(3 + 2i) = 6 + 4i - 3i - 2i^2 = 8 + i(2−i)(3+2i)=6+4i−3i−2i2=8+i.

Divide complex numbers (write a quotient in standard form)

  1. Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator.
  2. Expand: the denominator becomes the real number c2+d2c^2 + d^2c2+d2.
  3. Replace i2i^2i2 with −1-1−1 in the numerator and collect.
  4. Split into realc2+d2+imagc2+d2i\tfrac{\text{real}}{c^2 + d^2} + \tfrac{\text{imag}}{c^2 + d^2}ic2+d2real​+c2+d2imag​i.

e.g. 2+i1−2i=(2+i)(1+2i)1+4=5i5=i\frac{2 + i}{1 - 2i} = \frac{(2 + i)(1 + 2i)}{1 + 4} = \frac{5i}{5} = i1−2i2+i​=1+4(2+i)(1+2i)​=55i​=i.

Solve a quadratic by completing the square

  1. If a≠1a \neq 1a=1, divide the whole equation by aaa; call the resulting coefficient of xxx still bbb.
  2. Move the constant to the right side.
  3. Add (b2)2\left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2(2b​)2 to both sides.
  4. Write the left side as (x+b2)2\left(x + \tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2(x+2b​)2.
  5. Square-root both sides with ±\pm±; a negative right side gives ±ix\pm i\sqrt{\phantom{x}}±ix​.
  6. Solve for xxx.

e.g. x2+6x−7=0x^2 + 6x - 7 = 0x2+6x−7=0: (x+3)2=16(x + 3)^2 = 16(x+3)2=16, so x=1x = 1x=1 or x=−7x = -7x=−7.

Solve a quadratic with the quadratic formula

  1. Rearrange to ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0ax2+bx+c=0 and read aaa, bbb, ccc with their signs.
  2. Compute D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4ac first, on its own line.
  3. Substitute into x=−b±D2ax = \tfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{D}}{2a}x=2a−b±D​​.
  4. Simplify the radical; if D<0D < 0D<0, write D=i−D\sqrt{D} = i\sqrt{-D}D​=i−D​.
  5. Reduce the fraction, dividing the whole numerator by 2a2a2a.

e.g. x2−3x−10=0x^2 - 3x - 10 = 0x2−3x−10=0: D=9+40=49D = 9 + 40 = 49D=9+40=49, x=3±72=5x = \frac{3 \pm 7}{2} = 5x=23±7​=5 or −2-2−2.

Classify roots without solving

  1. Rearrange to standard form.
  2. Compute D=b2−4acD = b^2 - 4acD=b2−4ac.
  3. Read off: D>0D > 0D>0 two distinct real, D=0D = 0D=0 one repeated real, D<0D < 0D<0 a complex conjugate pair. Stop; the full formula is not needed.

e.g. 2x2+3x+5=02x^2 + 3x + 5 = 02x2+3x+5=0: D=9−40=−31<0D = 9 - 40 = -31 < 0D=9−40=−31<0, a complex conjugate pair.

Solve a quadratic word problem

  1. Name one variable and write every other quantity in terms of it (www and w+3w + 3w+3, not www and lll).
  2. Translate the situation into an equation and rearrange to standard form.
  3. Solve by the fastest method that fits: factoring if it factors, otherwise the formula.
  4. Test each root against the situation: reject a negative length, time, or count, but keep both roots when both fit (a thrown object passes a height going up and coming down).
  5. Answer the quantity actually asked for, in a sentence, with units.

e.g. Width www metres, length w+3w + 3w+3, area 404040: w2+3w−40=0w^2 + 3w - 40 = 0w2+3w−40=0, w=5w = 5w=5 or −8-8−8; reject −8-8−8, so the width is 555 metres.

Exam traps

  • Trap Multiplying two negative radicands with the product rule: −4⋅−9=36=6\sqrt{-4}\cdot\sqrt{-9} = \sqrt{36} = 6−4​⋅−9​=36​=6.

    Fix The rule fails for two negatives. Convert first: (2i)(3i)=6i2=−6(2i)(3i) = 6i^2 = -6(2i)(3i)=6i2=−6.

  • Trap Leaving an i2i^2i2 term in a product, or treating it as 000 or 111.

    Fix Every i2i^2i2 becomes −1-1−1 and merges into the real part: in (2−i)(3+2i)(2 - i)(3 + 2i)(2−i)(3+2i), −2i2-2i^2−2i2 becomes +2+2+2.

  • Trap Conjugate product by the difference-of-squares reflex: (a+bi)(a−bi)=a2−b2(a + bi)(a - bi) = a^2 - b^2(a+bi)(a−bi)=a2−b2.

    Fix −(bi)2=+b2-(bi)^2 = +b^2−(bi)2=+b2, so the product is a2+b2a^2 + b^2a2+b2: a sum, always real.

  • Trap Adding (b2)2\left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2(2b​)2 to one side of an equation, or forgetting the matching subtraction in an expression.

    Fix Equation: add it to both sides. Expression: add and subtract it in the same line.

  • Trap Reading aaa, bbb, ccc straight off x2=3x+10x^2 = 3x + 10x2=3x+10.

    Fix Coefficients are valid only from standard form: rearrange to x2−3x−10=0x^2 - 3x - 10 = 0x2−3x−10=0, then read the signs.

  • Trap Sign slips inside the formula: b=−7b = -7b=−7 giving b2=−49b^2 = -49b2=−49, −b-b−b kept as bbb, or −4ac-4ac−4ac mis-signed when c<0c < 0c<0.

    Fix b2b^2b2 is never negative; −b-b−b is the opposite of bbb; when aaa and ccc have opposite signs, −4ac-4ac−4ac is positive, so it adds.

  • Trap Calling a negative discriminant "no solution".

    Fix D<0D < 0D<0 gives a complex conjugate pair; every quadratic has roots, real or complex.

  • Trap Growing each dimension by xxx when a border or frame of width xxx surrounds it: (5+x)(7+x)(5 + x)(7 + x)(5+x)(7+x).

    Fix A border sits on both ends of each dimension, so each grows by 2x2x2x: (5+2x)(7+2x)(5 + 2x)(7 + 2x)(5+2x)(7+2x).

  • Trap Auto-rejecting the negative root, or auto-keeping exactly one, in a word problem.

    Fix Test each root against the situation and say why the rejected ones go; two positive times can both be valid.
