  1. Problem 1 A rewrite and a check

    Rewrite x2−14x+30x^2-14x+30x2−14x+30 in completed-square form, then expand your form to check that it returns the original expression.

    1. Hint 1

      The constant that builds a perfect square is the square of half the linear coefficient.

    2. Hint 2

      An expression has no second side to balance against, so add that constant and subtract it again in the same line, then collect the loose constants.

    Answer

    (x−7)2−19(x-7)^2-19(x−7)2−19.

    Full solution

    Half of the linear coefficient −14-14−14 is −7-7−7, and squaring that half gives the constant 494949.

    Add 494949 and subtract it again, which leaves the expression unchanged.

    x2−14x+49−49+30x^2-14x+49-49+30x2−14x+49−49+30

    The first three terms are the perfect square (x−7)2(x-7)^2(x−7)2, and the loose constants combine to −19-19−19.

    x2−14x+30=(x−7)2−19x^2-14x+30=(x-7)^2-19x2−14x+30=(x−7)2−19

    Expanding the answer gives x2−14x+49−19x^2-14x+49-19x2−14x+49−19, which is x2−14x+30x^2-14x+30x2−14x+30, the original expression.

    Answer

    (x−7)2−19(x-7)^2-19(x−7)2−19.

    Key idea

    Adding and subtracting the square of half the linear coefficient rewrites a quadratic with leading coefficient 111 so its variable appears only once.

  2. Problem 2 Matching values

    The expression (x+5)2−3(x+5)^2-3(x+5)2−3 takes a certain value when x=1x=1x=1. Find every real xxx at which it takes that same value.

    1. Hint 1

      Work out the value at x=1x=1x=1 first, then find every xxx that produces it.

    2. Hint 2

      Isolate the squared expression and take both square roots.

    Answer

    x=1x=1x=1 or x=−11x=-11x=−11.

    Full solution

    At x=1x=1x=1 the expression equals (1+5)2−3(1+5)^2-3(1+5)2−3, which is 333333.

    A value of xxx giving 333333 satisfies

    (x+5)2=36(x+5)^2=36(x+5)2=36

    Thus x+5=±6x+5=\pm6x+5=±6, giving x=1x=1x=1 or x=−11x=-11x=−11.

    Both values put either 666 or −6-6−6 inside the square, so both give 333333.

    Answer

    x=1x=1x=1 or x=−11x=-11x=−11.

    Key idea

    Equal squared values can come from opposite values inside a binomial.

  3. Problem 3 An identity condition

    For some real number bbb, the identity x2+bx+10=(x+s)2−6x^2+bx+10=(x+s)^2-6x2+bx+10=(x+s)2−6 holds for every xxx, and sss is negative. Find sss.

    1. Hint 1

      An identity has matching constant terms on both sides.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand the right side, then apply the sign condition to its constant equation.

    Answer

    s=−4s=-4s=−4.

    Full solution

    The right side expands to x2+2sx+s2−6x^2+2sx+s^2-6x2+2sx+s2−6.

    Matching the constants gives

    s2−6=10s^2-6=10s2−6=10

    Therefore s2=16s^2=16s2=16.

    Since sss is negative, s=−4s=-4s=−4.

    The corresponding middle coefficient would be −8-8−8, so such an identity exists.

    Answer

    s=−4s=-4s=−4.

    Key idea

    Matching constants in a completed-square identity can recover the binomial shift.

  4. Problem 4 Two sides of an equation

    Find all solutions of 2x(x+3)=5−x22x(x+3)=5-x^22x(x+3)=5−x2.

    1. Hint 1

      Expand and gather all squared terms before making a squared binomial.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide by the leading coefficient, then add the square of half the linear coefficient to both sides.

    Answer

    x=−1±263x=-1\pm\dfrac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}x=−1±326​​, or equivalently x=−3±263x=\dfrac{-3\pm2\sqrt{6}}{3}x=3−3±26​​.

    Full solution

    Expand and collect the terms.

    3x2+6x−5=03x^2+6x-5=03x2+6x−5=0

    Divide by 333 and move the constant.

    x2+2x=53x^2+2x=\frac53x2+2x=35​

    Add 111 to both sides.

    (x+1)2=83(x+1)^2=\frac83(x+1)2=38​

    Take both square roots and simplify.

    x=−1±263x=-1\pm\frac{2\sqrt6}{3}x=−1±326​​

    For either value, (x+1)2=8/3(x+1)^2=8/3(x+1)2=8/3, so 3x2+6x−5=03x^2+6x-5=03x2+6x−5=0 and the original two expressions agree.

    Answer

    x=−1±263x=-1\pm\dfrac{2\sqrt{6}}{3}x=−1±326​​, or equivalently x=−3±263x=\dfrac{-3\pm2\sqrt{6}}{3}x=3−3±26​​.

    Key idea

    Normalize the squared term before completing a square in an equation assembled from two sides.

  5. Problem 5 A specified value

    The quadratic x2+8x+qx^2+8x+qx2+8x+q equals 111111 when x=−4x=-4x=−4. Find qqq, then find every complex xxx at which the quadratic equals zero.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the given value at x=−4x=-4x=−4 to recover the missing constant first.

    2. Hint 2

      Rewrite the recovered quadratic as a square centered at −4-4−4 plus a constant.

    Answer

    q=27q=27q=27; x=−4±i11x=-4\pm i\sqrt{11}x=−4±i11​.

    Full solution

    Substitute x=−4x=-4x=−4 and set the result equal to the given value.

    16−32+q=1116-32+q=1116−32+q=11

    This gives q=27q=27q=27.

    Write the resulting quadratic as a square plus a constant.

    x2+8x+27=(x+4)2+11x^2+8x+27=(x+4)^2+11x2+8x+27=(x+4)2+11

    For the value zero,

    (x+4)2=−11.(x+4)^2=-11.(x+4)2=−11.

    Therefore

    x=−4±i11x=-4\pm i\sqrt{11}x=−4±i11​

    The squared term at either value of xxx is −11-11−11, giving the required zero.

    Answer

    q=27q=27q=27; x=−4±i11x=-4\pm i\sqrt{11}x=−4±i11​.

    Key idea

    A known value can supply a missing constant before a quadratic is solved.

  6. Problem 6 Two squared terms

    Write (x−1)2+(x+3)2(x-1)^2+(x+3)^2(x−1)2+(x+3)2 in the form 2(x+h)2+k2(x+h)^2+k2(x+h)2+k, and use that form to find every real input for which the original expression equals 202020.

    1. Hint 1

      Expand both squares and collect their terms.

    2. Hint 2

      Factor the leading coefficient out of the variable terms, complete the square inside the parentheses, then solve the resulting equation.

    Answer

    2(x+1)2+82(x+1)^2+82(x+1)2+8; x=−1±6x=-1\pm\sqrt6x=−1±6​.

    Full solution

    Expanding each square gives

    x2−2x+1+x2+6x+9x^2-2x+1+x^2+6x+9x2−2x+1+x2+6x+9

    Combining like terms gives

    2x2+4x+102x^2+4x+102x2+4x+10

    This is an expression, not an equation, so there is no other side to divide; factor 222 from the variable terms instead.

    2x2+4x+10=2(x2+2x)+102x^2+4x+10=2(x^2+2x)+102x2+4x+10=2(x2+2x)+10

    Complete the square inside the parentheses by adding and subtracting 111.

    x2+2x=(x+1)2−1x^2+2x=(x+1)^2-1x2+2x=(x+1)2−1

    Substituting that back gives

    2((x+1)2−1)+102\left((x+1)^2-1\right)+102((x+1)2−1)+10

    Distributing the 222 and combining the constants gives

    2(x+1)2+82(x+1)^2+82(x+1)2+8

    Set this equal to 202020, subtract 888, and divide by 222.

    (x+1)2=6(x+1)^2=6(x+1)2=6

    The inputs are

    x=−1±6.x=-1\pm\sqrt6.x=−1±6​.

    Substitution into the completed form gives 2(6)+8=202(6)+8=202(6)+8=20 for both.

    Answer

    2(x+1)2+82(x+1)^2+82(x+1)2+8; x=−1±6x=-1\pm\sqrt6x=−1±6​.

    Key idea

    Combining several squares may produce one completed square that is easier to solve.

  7. Problem 7 A product condition

    Find all complex numbers xxx for which (x−1)(x+6)=−16(x-1)(x+6)=-16(x−1)(x+6)=−16, and explain whether either solution is real.

    1. Hint 1

      Expand the product so its linear term is visible.

    2. Hint 2

      Half of an odd linear coefficient is a fraction; complete the square anyway, then inspect the sign of the number the square must equal.

    Answer

    x=−5±i152x=\dfrac{-5\pm i\sqrt{15}}{2}x=2−5±i15​​; neither solution is real.

    Full solution

    Expand and gather every term on the left.

    x2+5x+10=0x^2+5x+10=0x2+5x+10=0

    Half of 555 is 52\frac{5}{2}25​, whose square is 254\frac{25}{4}425​.

    Move 101010 across and add that square to both sides.

    (x+52)2=254−10\left(x+\frac{5}{2}\right)^2=\frac{25}{4}-10(x+25​)2=425​−10

    The right side is negative.

    (x+52)2=−154\left(x+\frac{5}{2}\right)^2=-\frac{15}{4}(x+25​)2=−415​

    No real square is negative, so neither solution is real.

    Since −15=i15\sqrt{-15}=i\sqrt{15}−15​=i15​, taking both roots gives

    x+52=±i152x+\frac{5}{2}=\pm\frac{i\sqrt{15}}{2}x+25​=±2i15​​

    Subtract 52\frac{5}{2}25​ and write the pair over one denominator.

    x=−5±i152x=\frac{-5\pm i\sqrt{15}}{2}x=2−5±i15​​

    Each value makes x2+5x+10=0x^2+5x+10=0x2+5x+10=0, so the original product equals −16-16−16.

    Answer

    x=−5±i152x=\dfrac{-5\pm i\sqrt{15}}{2}x=2−5±i15​​; neither solution is real.

    Key idea

    The sign on the other side of a completed square distinguishes real from nonreal solutions.

  8. Problem 8 A family of equations

    For a real number ppp, consider x2+2px+p2+7=0x^2+2px+p^2+7=0x2+2px+p2+7=0. A student says that no choice of ppp gives a real solution for xxx. Is the claim correct? Explain.

    How many distinct real solutions would the equation have if its final constant 777 were replaced by 000?

    1. Hint 1

      The first three terms form a square.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the value required of that square with the possible values of a real square.

    3. Hint 3

      Replacing the final constant changes the number on the other side of the completed square.

    Answer

    The claim is correct. With 777 replaced by 000, there is one distinct real solution, x=−px=-px=−p.

    Full solution

    The equation is equivalent to

    (x+p)2=−7(x+p)^2=-7(x+p)2=−7

    For real xxx and real ppp, the square on the left is zero or positive.

    It cannot equal −7-7−7, regardless of the chosen real ppp.

    After replacing 777 by 000, the equation becomes

    (x+p)2=0(x+p)^2=0(x+p)2=0

    A square equals zero exactly when its base is zero.

    Thus x=−px=-px=−p is the only distinct real solution, a repeated root.

    Answer

    The claim is correct. With 777 replaced by 000, there is one distinct real solution, x=−px=-px=−p.

    Key idea

    The sign of the value required of a completed square determines the number of distinct real roots.

  9. Problem 9 A replacement of the input

    A student says that x2+6x+10=0x^2+6x+10=0x2+6x+10=0 and (2x)2+6(2x)+10=0(2x)^2+6(2x)+10=0(2x)2+6(2x)+10=0 have the same solutions. Decide whether the claim is correct, and give the solution set of each equation.

    1. Hint 1

      Complete the square in the expression that plays the role of the input.

    2. Hint 2

      In the second equation, isolate 2x2x2x before dividing to obtain xxx.

    Answer

    False. First: x=−3±ix=-3\pm ix=−3±i. Second: x=−3±i2x=\dfrac{-3\pm i}{2}x=2−3±i​.

    Full solution

    The first equation becomes

    (x+3)2=−1(x+3)^2=-1(x+3)2=−1

    Its solutions are x=−3±ix=-3\pm ix=−3±i.

    In the second equation, the same square is built from 2x2x2x.

    (2x+3)2=−1(2x+3)^2=-1(2x+3)2=−1

    Thus 2x=−3±i2x=-3\pm i2x=−3±i, so

    x=−3±i2x=\frac{-3\pm i}{2}x=2−3±i​

    These are half the first pair, not the same pair.

    Substitution gives squared binomial value −1-1−1 in each respective equation.

    Answer

    False. First: x=−3±ix=-3\pm ix=−3±i. Second: x=−3±i2x=\dfrac{-3\pm i}{2}x=2−3±i​.

    Key idea

    Replacing xxx by 2x2x2x in an equation halves each of its solutions.

  10. Problem 10 Two pairs of roots

    The equations x2+6x+1=0x^2+6x+1=0x2+6x+1=0 and x2+6x−3=0x^2+6x-3=0x2+6x−3=0 each have two real roots. A student says that the average of the two roots is the same for both equations. Decide whether the claim is correct, and find that average.

    1. Hint 1

      Complete the square in each equation.

    2. Hint 2

      The plus and minus square-root terms cancel when each pair of roots is added.

    Answer

    The claim is correct; the common average is −3-3−3.

    Full solution

    The first equation becomes

    (x+3)2=8(x+3)^2=8(x+3)2=8

    Its roots are −3±22-3\pm2\sqrt2−3±22​, whose sum is −6-6−6.

    The second equation becomes

    (x+3)2=12(x+3)^2=12(x+3)2=12

    Its roots are −3±23-3\pm2\sqrt3−3±23​, whose sum is also −6-6−6.

    Each average is

    −62=−3\frac{-6}{2}=-32−6​=−3

    The different distances from −3-3−3 do not change the shared average.

    Answer

    The claim is correct; the common average is −3-3−3.

    Key idea

    A pair of roots written as a fixed number plus or minus a real amount has that fixed number as its average.
