Adding and Subtracting Polynomials

Intuition

Try adding (3x2+5x)+(x2−2x)(3x^2 + 5x) + (x^2 - 2x)(3x2+5x)+(x2−2x). You can combine the two x2x^2x2 pieces with each other, and separately combine the two xxx pieces with each other, but an x2x^2x2 piece can never join an xxx piece. That one idea, you may only ever combine terms that carry the same power of xxx, is the whole engine behind adding and subtracting expressions like the ones you have been building all along, things like x2−5x+6x^2 - 5x + 6x2−5x+6. An expression of that shape, a sum of terms where each term is a number times a whole-number power of xxx, is called a polynomial. Before you can multiply or factor polynomials fluently, you need these two simplest operations on them. Everything in this lesson follows from the same-power rule above and from the distributive property you already trust.

Learning goals

  • Name a polynomial's terms, degree and leading coefficient
  • Write standard form in descending order of degree
  • Combine only like terms, those sharing a power
  • Distribute the minus across every term when subtracting
  • Predict the degree of a sum, allowing for cancellation

What a polynomial is

Take the expression

3x4−5x2+7x−2.3x^4 - 5x^2 + 7x - 2.3x4−5x2+7x−2.

Every piece of it follows the same pattern, a number multiplied by a whole-number power of xxx. Reading it piece by piece pins down the vocabulary you will use for the rest of the chapter.

TermCoefficientDegree
3x43x^43x4333444
−5x2-5x^2−5x2−5-5−5222
7x7x7x777111
−2-2−2−2-2−2000

The degree of a term is the power of xxx in it. The term 7x7x7x is really 7x17x^17x1, so its degree is 111, and the constant −2-2−2 is really −2x0-2x^0−2x0 (since x0=1x^0 = 1x0=1), so its degree is 000. The degree of the polynomial is the largest degree among its terms, which is 444 here. The highest-degree term, 3x43x^43x4, is the leading term, and its coefficient 333 is the leading coefficient. The constant term is the term with no variable, the x0x^0x0 term, which is −2-2−2. Notice that this polynomial has no x3x^3x3 term. That simply means its x3x^3x3 coefficient is 000, and a term with a zero coefficient contributes nothing and is left out.

In general, a polynomial in xxx is any finite sum of terms built the same way, each one a real number (its coefficient) times a whole-number power of xxx. Every exponent must be a nonnegative integer (0,1,2,…0, 1, 2, \ldots0,1,2,…). That requirement is exactly what separates a polynomial from other expressions. No variable sits in a denominator, no variable hides under a root, and no power is negative or fractional.

Which of these is a polynomial in xxx?

Answer choices

A polynomial is a finite sum of terms, each a number times a nonnegative-integer power of xxx, exponents 0,1,2,…0, 1, 2, \ldots0,1,2,… only, never negative, never fractional, and never a variable stuck under a root or in a denominator.

2x3−x+72x^3 - x + 72x3−x+7 fits every part of that test: its exponents are 333, 111, and 000, all nonnegative integers. The other three fail it. x2+3xx^2 + \frac{3}{x}x2+x3​ has xxx in a denominator, which is really x−1x^{-1}x−1, a negative exponent. x−4\sqrt{x} - 4x​−4 has xxx under a root, which is really x1/2x^{1/2}x1/2, a fractional exponent. And 5x−2+15x^{-2} + 15x−2+1 writes a negative exponent directly. None of the three qualifies as a polynomial.

Short polynomials get their own names by their number of terms. A one-term polynomial such as 7x7x7x or 4x34x^34x3 or 999 is a monomial. A two-term polynomial such as x+1x + 1x+1 is a binomial. A three-term polynomial such as x2−5x+6x^2 - 5x + 6x2−5x+6 is a trinomial. Past three terms there is no special word, and you simply call it a polynomial.

Standard form

A polynomial is in standard form when its terms are written in descending order of degree, the highest power first and the constant last. The polynomial 3x4−5x2+7x−23x^4 - 5x^2 + 7x - 23x4−5x2+7x−2 above is already in standard form. You could rearrange it as 7x−2+3x4−5x27x - 2 + 3x^4 - 5x^27x−2+3x4−5x2, and it would equal the same number for every value of xxx, but that scrambled order hides everything useful about it.

Descending order earns its place as the default for three reasons. The degree and the leading term sit right at the front, so you can read a polynomial’s most important features at a glance. Two polynomials written in standard form can be compared term by term, the way you compare two whole numbers digit by digit. And when you add or subtract, lining terms up by descending power drops like terms into the same column automatically. That alignment is exactly the setup the rest of this lesson uses.

Rewriting in standard form as −x5+6x3−2x+4-x^5 + 6x^3 - 2x + 4−x5+6x3−2x+4 makes the leading term obvious. The 666 is only the coefficient of x3x^3x3.

Standard form lists the terms in descending order of degree: the degree-444 term first, then degree 222, then degree 111, then the constant.

5−3x4+2x2−x  =  −3x4+2x2−x+55 - 3x^4 + 2x^2 - x \;=\; -3x^4 + 2x^2 - x + 55−3x4+2x2−x=−3x4+2x2−x+5

The original order goes constant, degree 444, degree 222, degree 111, which is not sorted by degree at all. And flipping the sign on 2x22x^22x2 changes the polynomial itself, not just the order its terms are written in.

Why only like terms combine

Like terms are terms with the same variable part, meaning the same power of xxx. In a single variable that just means the same exponent. So 3x23x^23x2 and −8x2-8x^2−8x2 are like terms, and 5x5x5x and xxx are like terms, but x2x^2x2 and x3x^3x3 are not, and neither are 4x4x4x and 444. Only like terms can be merged into a single term, and the reason is the distributive property, run backward.

Why like terms combine and unlike terms do not#

The distributive property says that for any numbers and quantities, ba+ca=(b+c)aba + ca = (b + c)aba+ca=(b+c)a. Let the shared quantity aaa be a single power of xxx, say x2x^2x2. Two terms that both carry that power are bbb copies and ccc copies of the very same thing, so

3x2+5x2=(3+5)x2=8x2.3x^2 + 5x^2 = (3 + 5)x^2 = 8x^2.3x2+5x2=(3+5)x2=8x2.

The common factor x2x^2x2 pulls out to the right, the coefficients 333 and 555 add, and the power itself is untouched. That is the whole mechanism: combining like terms is the distributive property read from left to right. That combination works only because both terms are copies of the identical power. The exponent stays 222, so the answer is 8x28x^28x2, never 8x48x^48x4.

Now try two different powers, x2x^2x2 and x3x^3x3. Factoring the smaller power out is the most you can do,

x2+x3=x2(1+x),x^2 + x^3 = x^2(1 + x),x2+x3=x2(1+x),

and 1+x1 + x1+x is not a constant, since it still contains an xxx, so the result is not a single term c xkc\,x^kcxk for any constant ccc and power kkk. The two terms cannot merge into one, because they are not copies of the same shared power. Like terms carry the same power and merge into one; unlike terms carry different powers and stay side by side.

∎

The rule to carry forward is short: to combine like terms, add their coefficients and keep the shared power unchanged.

Adding polynomials

Adding two polynomials is now almost mechanical. A plus sign in front of a parenthesis changes nothing inside it, so you can erase the parentheses, gather the like terms, and add their coefficients.

Worked example 1 Add (3x2+5x−2)+(x2−4x+6)(3x^2 + 5x - 2) + (x^2 - 4x + 6)(3x2+5x−2)+(x2−4x+6)

The leading plus sign lets the parentheses drop with every sign kept as it stands:

(3x2+5x−2)+(x2−4x+6)=3x2+5x−2+x2−4x+6.(3x^2 + 5x - 2) + (x^2 - 4x + 6) = 3x^2 + 5x - 2 + x^2 - 4x + 6.(3x2+5x−2)+(x2−4x+6)=3x2+5x−2+x2−4x+6.

Group the like terms by power, then add the coefficients in each group:

(3x2+x2)+(5x−4x)+(−2+6)=4x2+x+4.(3x^2 + x^2) + (5x - 4x) + (-2 + 6) = 4x^2 + x + 4.(3x2+x2)+(5x−4x)+(−2+6)=4x2+x+4.

The x2x^2x2 terms give 3+1=43 + 1 = 43+1=4, the xxx terms give 5+(−4)=15 + (-4) = 15+(−4)=1, and the constants give −2+6=4-2 + 6 = 4−2+6=4. The sum 4x2+x+44x^2 + x + 44x2+x+4 is already in standard form.

Lining the terms up in columns, one column per power, makes the bookkeeping visible. It is ordinary column addition, with powers of xxx playing the role that place values play for whole numbers.

When one polynomial is missing a power the other one has, leave that column empty rather than forcing an entry. The gap just means the coefficient there is 000.

Worked example 2 Add (2x3+x−5)+(4x2−3x+1)(2x^3 + x - 5) + (4x^2 - 3x + 1)(2x3+x−5)+(4x2−3x+1)

The first polynomial has no x2x^2x2 term and the second has no x3x^3x3 term, so those powers each appear in only one of the two. Drop the parentheses and group by power:

2x3+x−5+4x2−3x+1=2x3+4x2+(x−3x)+(−5+1).2x^3 + x - 5 + 4x^2 - 3x + 1 = 2x^3 + 4x^2 + (x - 3x) + (-5 + 1).2x3+x−5+4x2−3x+1=2x3+4x2+(x−3x)+(−5+1).

Only the xxx terms and the constants have partners to combine:

2x3+4x2−2x−4.2x^3 + 4x^2 - 2x - 4.2x3+4x2−2x−4.

The 2x32x^32x3 and the 4x24x^24x2 carry straight down, because nothing is there to add to them. Writing the result in descending order gives 2x3+4x2−2x−42x^3 + 4x^2 - 2x - 42x3+4x2−2x−4.

Add (3x3−2x+4)+(−x3+5x2−7)(3x^3 - 2x + 4) + (-x^3 + 5x^2 - 7)(3x3−2x+4)+(−x3+5x2−7).

Answer choices

Drop the parentheses and group by power. The x3x^3x3 terms combine, the x2x^2x2 term has no partner and carries straight down, the xxx term has no partner either, and the constants combine.

(3x3−x3)+5x2+(−2x)+(4−7)=2x3+5x2−2x−3(3x^3 - x^3) + 5x^2 + (-2x) + (4 - 7) = 2x^3 + 5x^2 - 2x - 3(3x3−x3)+5x2+(−2x)+(4−7)=2x3+5x2−2x−3

Adding 3x33x^33x3 and −x3-x^3−x3 gives 2x32x^32x3, not 4x34x^34x3: read the coefficient's sign, do not just add its size. And the lone 5x25x^25x2 term still has to carry down; it is easy to drop a term that has no partner.

Subtracting polynomials

Subtraction is where nearly every mistake happens, and it has a single cause. Subtracting a polynomial means subtracting every term in it, not just the first. A minus sign in front of a parenthesis is a factor of −1-1−1 multiplying the whole group. By the distributive property, that factor flips the sign of each term inside:

−(2x2+6x−7)=−2x2−6x+7.-(2x^2 + 6x - 7) = -2x^2 - 6x + 7.−(2x2+6x−7)=−2x2−6x+7.

The 2x22x^22x2 becomes −2x2-2x^2−2x2, the +6x+6x+6x becomes −6x-6x−6x, and the −7-7−7 becomes +7+7+7. Every sign turns over. Once the parentheses are gone, you combine like terms exactly as with addition.

Worked example 3 Subtract (5x2−3x+4)−(2x2+6x−7)(5x^2 - 3x + 4) - (2x^2 + 6x - 7)(5x2−3x+4)−(2x2+6x−7)

Distribute the minus sign across all three terms of the second polynomial, flipping each sign:

(5x2−3x+4)−(2x2+6x−7)=5x2−3x+4−2x2−6x+7.(5x^2 - 3x + 4) - (2x^2 + 6x - 7) = 5x^2 - 3x + 4 - 2x^2 - 6x + 7.(5x2−3x+4)−(2x2+6x−7)=5x2−3x+4−2x2−6x+7.

Watch the two signs that changed: +6x+6x+6x became −6x-6x−6x, and −7-7−7 became +7+7+7. Now group and combine:

(5x2−2x2)+(−3x−6x)+(4+7)=3x2−9x+11.(5x^2 - 2x^2) + (-3x - 6x) + (4 + 7) = 3x^2 - 9x + 11.(5x2−2x2)+(−3x−6x)+(4+7)=3x2−9x+11.

Had you flipped only the first term and written 5x2−3x+4−2x2+6x−75x^2 - 3x + 4 - 2x^2 + 6x - 75x2−3x+4−2x2+6x−7, the xxx term and the constant would both come out wrong. The sign change has to reach every term.

Worked example 4 Subtract (4x3−2x2+x)−(x3−5x2−3x+2)(4x^3 - 2x^2 + x) - (x^3 - 5x^2 - 3x + 2)(4x3−2x2+x)−(x3−5x2−3x+2)

The second polynomial has four terms, so four signs flip. In particular the −5x2-5x^2−5x2 becomes +5x2+5x^2+5x2, and the +2+2+2 becomes −2-2−2:

4x3−2x2+x−x3+5x2+3x−2.4x^3 - 2x^2 + x - x^3 + 5x^2 + 3x - 2.4x3−2x2+x−x3+5x2+3x−2.

Group by power and add the coefficients in each group:

(4x3−x3)+(−2x2+5x2)+(x+3x)−2=3x3+3x2+4x−2.(4x^3 - x^3) + (-2x^2 + 5x^2) + (x + 3x) - 2 = 3x^3 + 3x^2 + 4x - 2.(4x3−x3)+(−2x2+5x2)+(x+3x)−2=3x3+3x2+4x−2.

The middle group is the one to double-check: −2x2-2x^2−2x2 plus the flipped +5x2+5x^2+5x2 gives +3x2+3x^2+3x2, not −7x2-7x^2−7x2. Subtracting a negative term adds it back, which is precisely what the distributed minus sign takes care of.

Check your understanding

Simplify (4x2−x+3)−(x2+2x−5)(4x^2 - x + 3) - (x^2 + 2x - 5)(4x2−x+3)−(x2+2x−5).

Answer choices

Distribute the minus sign to every term of the second polynomial, not just the first. Each of x2x^2x2, 2x2x2x, and −5-5−5 flips its sign.

4x2−x+3−x2−2x+5=3x2−3x+84x^2 - x + 3 - x^2 - 2x + 5 = 3x^2 - 3x + 84x2−x+3−x2−2x+5=3x2−3x+8

Stopping after the first flip gives 3x2+x−23x^2 + x - 23x2+x−2, the most common error. The +2x+2x+2x and the −5-5−5 have to flip too, which turns the xxx term into −3x-3x−3x and the constant into +8+8+8.

Closure and the degree of a sum

Add or subtract two polynomials and you always get another polynomial. Adding or subtracting real coefficients gives real coefficients, and the powers involved were already nonnegative integers, so the result still fits the definition. Mathematicians describe this by saying the polynomials are closed under addition and subtraction, the same way the whole numbers are closed under addition.

The degree of the result is usually easy to predict. When you add two polynomials, the highest power present in either one is still present in the sum. Because that power is still present, the degree of the sum is the larger of the two degrees. There is one exception worth knowing, and it happens only when the two polynomials share the same degree and their leading terms cancel. In the rare case where every term cancels, the result is 000 itself, which has no degree at all, since there is no term left to point to.

Worked example 5 Compare the degree of (x2+x)+(x2−1)(x^2 + x) + (x^2 - 1)(x2+x)+(x2−1) with (x2+x)−(x2−1)(x^2 + x) - (x^2 - 1)(x2+x)−(x2−1)

Both polynomials have degree 222, with the same leading term x2x^2x2. Add them first:

(x2+x)+(x2−1)=2x2+x−1.(x^2 + x) + (x^2 - 1) = 2x^2 + x - 1.(x2+x)+(x2−1)=2x2+x−1.

The leading coefficients are 111 and 111, and 1+1=21 + 1 = 21+1=2 is not zero, so the x2x^2x2 term survives and the sum keeps degree 222. Now subtract instead:

(x2+x)−(x2−1)=x2+x−x2+1=x+1.(x^2 + x) - (x^2 - 1) = x^2 + x - x^2 + 1 = x + 1.(x2+x)−(x2−1)=x2+x−x2+1=x+1.

This time the x2x^2x2 terms cancel, since 1−1=01 - 1 = 01−1=0, and the highest power left is x1x^1x1. The difference has degree 111, lower than either polynomial you started with. The same two polynomials give a degree-222 sum and a degree-111 difference. The difference comes out lower because subtraction lined up equal leading terms with opposite signs, and they wiped each other out.

Check your understanding

What is the degree of (2x3−4x+1)+(−2x3+5x2−3)(2x^3 - 4x + 1) + (-2x^3 + 5x^2 - 3)(2x3−4x+1)+(−2x3+5x2−3)?

Answer choices

Both polynomials have degree 333, but their leading coefficients, 222 and −2-2−2, are opposites, so adding cancels the x3x^3x3 term.

2x3−4x+1−2x3+5x2−3=5x2−4x−22x^3 - 4x + 1 - 2x^3 + 5x^2 - 3 = 5x^2 - 4x - 22x3−4x+1−2x3+5x2−3=5x2−4x−2

With the x3x^3x3 terms gone, the highest power left is x2x^2x2, so the degree is 222, lower than either original polynomial. Notice this cancellation happened in a sum: the leading coefficients were opposites (222 and −2-2−2), not equal, the way they were in Worked Example 5's subtraction.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Dropping the negative on the later terms of a subtraction. A minus sign in front of a parenthesis multiplies every term inside by −1-1−1, not just the first term. Flipping only the first sign is the single most common error in the whole chapter.
  • Combining unlike terms. Only terms with the same power merge. 3x2+4x3x^2 + 4x3x2+4x stays 3x2+4x3x^2 + 4x3x2+4x; it is not 7x27x^27x2 and not 7x37x^37x3. If the powers differ, leave the terms side by side.
  • Changing the exponent when combining like terms. Adding like terms adds the coefficients and keeps the power: 3x2+5x2=8x23x^2 + 5x^2 = 8x^23x2+5x2=8x2, never 8x48x^48x4. You are counting copies of one power, not multiplying powers.
  • Losing the constant term. The constant is the x0x^0x0 term, and it combines only with other constants. It is easy to carry down a row of variable terms and forget the plain number at the end. The mistake is especially easy in a subtraction, where the constant’s sign has just flipped.
  • Mislabeling the leading coefficient. The leading coefficient belongs to the highest-degree term, which is not always the first one written. In 6x3−x5+46x^3 - x^5 + 46x3−x5+4 the leading term is −x5-x^5−x5, so the leading coefficient is −1-1−1. Put the polynomial in standard form first.

Key takeaways

  • A polynomial is a finite sum of terms, each a real coefficient times a nonnegative-integer power of xxx. Key words: term, coefficient, degree of a term and of the polynomial, leading term and leading coefficient, and constant term. The words monomial / binomial / trinomial name polynomials of one, two, and three terms.
  • Standard form writes the terms in descending order of degree, so the degree and leading term are visible up front and like terms line up when you add.
  • Like terms carry the same power. Combine them by adding coefficients and keeping the power unchanged, because 3x2+5x2=(3+5)x2=8x23x^2 + 5x^2 = (3 + 5)x^2 = 8x^23x2+5x2=(3+5)x2=8x2 is the distributive property run backward. Unlike terms cannot merge.
  • To add, drop the parentheses, group like terms, and add coefficients. To subtract, distribute the minus sign to every term of the second polynomial, then combine like terms.
  • A sum or difference of polynomials is again a polynomial (closure). Its degree is at most the larger of the two starting degrees, and it drops below that only when both polynomials share that degree and the operation cancels their leading coefficients to zero, as in (x2+x)−(x2−1)=x+1(x^2 + x) - (x^2 - 1) = x + 1(x2+x)−(x2−1)=x+1.

A bit of history (optional)

For most of the history of algebra there was no plus sign. Writers set the terms of a sum beside each other and left it at that.

Diophantus worked in Alexandria, a Greek city in Egypt, around the year 250. He wrote the earliest surviving book that handles unknowns with symbols rather than sentences. He had short marks for the unknown, for its square, and for its cube. Addition needed no mark at all. Two terms written next to each other were simply added together.

Subtraction was another matter. Diophantus had one symbol for it, and he used that symbol once. Every term he meant to take away was gathered behind it, at the end of the expression. A reader met the added terms first, then the single mark, then the entire subtracted group in a bundle.

That looks like a strange habit until you notice what you did in this lesson. Write −(2x2+6x−7)-(2x^2 + 6x - 7)−(2x2+6x−7) and the minus sign in front is standing guard over three terms at once, exactly as his did. Clearing the parentheses means letting that one sign reach every term inside, so all three signs turn over. Diophantus bundled the subtracted terms together. Your first move is to unbundle them.
