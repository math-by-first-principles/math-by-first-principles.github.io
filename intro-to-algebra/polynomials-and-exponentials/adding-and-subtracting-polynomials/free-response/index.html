    2. What survives an addition, and why . Foundational, 10 points. Question 2 of 5.

    Foundational 10 pts

    Add (4x3−6x+9)+(2x2+5x−3)(4x^3 - 6x + 9) + (2x^2 + 5x - 3)(4x3−6x+9)+(2x2+5x−3).

    2. B

    3. C
      Part C.

      However the coefficients had come out, the sum of these two polynomials was guaranteed to be a polynomial itself. Explain why, referring to what addition does to each term's exponent and to each term's coefficient.

      Explain why it works A sentence or two. Reasons, not steps. 3 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 3

      Work through the two polynomials degree by degree: for each power of xxx that appears, ask whether it shows up in the first polynomial, the second, or both.

    2. Hint 2 of 3 · Part A

      A plus sign in front of a parenthesis leaves everything inside it unchanged; erase the parentheses and gather the powers that match each other.

    3. Hint 3 of 3 · Part C

      A polynomial's definition asks only two things of every term: a whole-number exponent that is not negative, and a real coefficient. Check whether adding two polynomials can ever break either one.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    4x3+2x2−x+64x^3 + 2x^2 - x + 64x3+2x2−x+6.

    Part B

    The degree-333 term appears only in the first polynomial and the degree-222 term only in the second; the degree-111 terms and the two constant terms each appear in both and merge.

    Part C

    Adding two terms never changes an exponent, it only merges terms that already share one, and adding two real numbers always gives another real number. Since a polynomial's definition demands only nonnegative whole-number exponents and real coefficients, and addition disturbs neither, the sum must again be a polynomial.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    A leading plus sign changes nothing inside the parentheses, so drop them and group by power:

    4x3−6x+9+2x2+5x−3.4x^3 - 6x + 9 + 2x^2 + 5x - 3.4x3−6x+9+2x2+5x−3.

    Only the xxx-terms and the constants have a partner to combine:

    4x3+2x2+(−6x+5x)+(9−3)=4x3+2x2−x+6.4x^3 + 2x^2 + (-6x+5x) + (9-3) = 4x^3+2x^2-x+6.4x3+2x2+(−6x+5x)+(9−3)=4x3+2x2−x+6.

    The x3x^3x3 term and the x2x^2x2 term each came from only one of the two polynomials, so they carry straight down unchanged.

    Part B

    Compare the two polynomials term by term. The first, 4x3−6x+94x^3-6x+94x3−6x+9, has degrees 333, 111, 000. The second, 2x2+5x−32x^2+5x-32x2+5x−3, has degrees 222, 111, 000.

    Degree 333 shows up only in the first polynomial, and degree 222 shows up only in the second, so each of those two terms carries straight down into the sum with no partner:

    4x3  (no partner),2x2  (no partner).4x^3 \;\text{(no partner)}, \qquad 2x^2 \;\text{(no partner)}.4x3(no partner),2x2(no partner).

    Degree 111 and degree 000 each show up in BOTH polynomials, which is exactly why the xxx-terms and the constants were the ones that combined in part A.

    Part C

    A polynomial's definition places two requirements on every term: its exponent must be a nonnegative whole number, and its coefficient must be a real number. Check what addition does to each.

    Exponents: combining like terms never changes the shared power, it only merges terms that already have it,

    (−6x)+(5x)=(−6+5)x=−x;(-6x)+(5x) = (-6+5)x = -x;(−6x)+(5x)=(−6+5)x=−x;

    a term with no partner, like 4x34x^34x3 or 2x22x^22x2 above, simply carries down with its own exponent untouched. Every exponent in the sum is therefore one that was already present, still a nonnegative whole number.

    Coefficients: adding two real numbers, whether they are matching coefficients such as −6-6−6 and 555, or any other pair, always produces another real number.

    Since neither requirement is ever broken, the sum of 4x3−6x+94x^3-6x+94x3−6x+9 and 2x2+5x−32x^2+5x-32x2+5x−3, or of any two polynomials at all, satisfies the definition of a polynomial. That is what it means to say the polynomials are closed under addition.

    In one line

    (4x3−6x+9)+(2x2+5x−3)=4x3+2x2−x+6(4x^3-6x+9)+(2x^2+5x-3) = 4x^3+2x^2-x+6(4x3−6x+9)+(2x2+5x−3)=4x3+2x2−x+6; the degree-333 term came only from the first polynomial and the degree-222 term only from the second, while the degree-111 terms and the constants each combined; and the sum is guaranteed to be a polynomial because addition never changes an exponent and never turns two real coefficients into anything but another real number.

    The answer

    Part A

    Line 3. Line 2 already shows the x3x^3x3 terms have cancelled, so the difference has degree 222, not 333: the rule that a difference keeps the larger degree holds only when the leading terms do not cancel, and here they do.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Test each line against the one before it.

    Line 1 distributes the minus sign to every term of the second polynomial, correctly: sound.

    Line 2 combines like terms from Line 1. The two x3x^3x3 terms cancel (5x3−5x3=05x^3-5x^3=05x3−5x3=0), the x2x^2x2 terms give −2x2−3x2=−5x2-2x^2-3x^2=-5x^2−2x2−3x2=−5x2, and the constants give 0+4=40+4=40+4=4:

    Line 3 is where the chain breaks.     Part B

    Line 2 already gives the fully simplified difference; nothing further needs combining. It is in standard form,

    −5x2+x+4,-5x^2+x+4,−5x2+x+4,

    with degree 222. Its leading term is −5x2-5x^2−5x2, so its leading coefficient is −5-5−5, not the 555 that Line 4 reports.

    Part C

    The rule that survives every case is guarded, not absolute: the degree of a difference is at most the larger of the two degrees, and drops below it only when the two polynomials share that same degree AND their leading terms cancel.

    Check the condition here. Both (5x3−2x2+x)(5x^3-2x^2+x)(5x3−2x2+x) and (5x3+3x2−4)(5x^3+3x^2-4)(5x3+3x2−4) have degree 333, and the leading term of each is 5x35x^35x3. Subtracting lines them up with opposite signs,

    5x3−5x3=0,5x^3 - 5x^3 = 0,5x3−5x3=0,

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Drop the parentheses, since the connecting sign is a plus, and group by power:

    (2x4−3x2+x−5)+(−2x4+5x2−4)=(2x4−2x4)+(−3x2+5x2)+x+(−5−4).\left(2x^4-3x^2+x-5\right)+\left(-2x^4+5x^2-4\right) = (2x^4-2x^4)+(-3x^2+5x^2)+x+(-5-4).(2x4−3x2+x−5)+(−2x4+5x2−4)=(2x4−2x4)+(−3x2+5x2)+x+(−5−4).

    The x4x^4x4 terms cancel completely, since 2+(−2)=02+(-2)=02+(−2)=0:

    f(x)+g(x)=2x2+x−9.f(x)+g(x) = 2x^2+x-9.f(x)+g(x)=2x2+x−9.

    Part B

    Subtract h(x)=3x3−x+2h(x)=3x^3-x+2h(x)=3x3−x+2 from the sum found in part A, flipping the sign of every one of its three terms:

    (2x2+x−9)−(3x3−x+2)=2x2+x−9−3x3+x−2.\left(2x^2+x-9\right) - \left(3x^3-x+2\right) = 2x^2+x-9-3x^3+x-2.(2x2+x−9)−(3x3−x+2)=2x2+x−9−3x3+x−2.

    Combine like terms, in standard form:

    k(x)=−3x3+2x2+(x+x)+(−9−2)=−3x3+2x2+2x−11.k(x) = -3x^3+2x^2+(x+x)+(-9-2) = -3x^3+2x^2+2x-11.k(x)=−3x3+2x2+(x+x)+(−9−2)=−3x3+2x2+2x−11.

    Part C

    Read off each polynomial's degree from its own leading term: fff and ggg both have degree 444 (leading terms 2x42x^42x4 and −2x4-2x^4−2x4), and hhh has degree 333 (leading term 3x33x^33x3). A first guess might say kkk inherits the largest of these, degree 444.

    But the guarded rule for a sum applies at the FIRST step: f(x)+g(x)f(x)+g(x)f(x)+g(x) has the same degree, 444, in both polynomials, and their leading terms 2x42x^42x4 and −2x4-2x^4−2x4 are opposite, so

    2x4+(−2x4)=0,2x^4 + (-2x^4) = 0,2x4+(−2x4)=0,

    and the sum drops to degree 222, as part A found. The degree of the whole expression is decided step by step, not by looking at the three original degrees 444, 444, 333 and taking the largest.
