    The answer

    Part A

    240.88240.88240.88 dollars.

    Part B

    Year 666.

    Part C

    The unknown year sits in the exponent, and this course has not yet introduced the operation that isolates an exponent directly; testing candidate whole-number years and comparing each result to the target is a way to locate the crossing point without that operation.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Apply A=P(1+r)tA = P(1+r)^tA=P(1+r)t with P=180P = 180P=180 and the given growth factor.

    A=180 (1.06)5=180×1.338226A = 180\,(1.06)^{5} = 180 \times 1.338226A=180(1.06)5=180×1.338226

    A=240.88.A = 240.88.A=240.88.

    The predicted price after 555 years is 240.88240.88240.88 dollars.

    Part B

    Evaluate the price at both candidate years and compare each to the budget.

    Year 555:

    180 (1.06)5=180×1.338226=240.88,180\,(1.06)^{5} = 180 \times 1.338226 = 240.88,180(1.06)5=180×1.338226=240.88,

    still under 250250250 dollars.

    Year 666:

    180 (1.06)6=180×1.418519=255.33,180\,(1.06)^{6} = 180 \times 1.418519 = 255.33,180(1.06)6=180×1.418519=255.33,

    which is over 250250250 dollars. Since year 555 has not yet crossed the budget and year 666 has, the price first exceeds 250250250 dollars in year 666.

    Part C

    In the equation

    180(1.06)t=250,180(1.06)^t = 250,180(1.06)t=250,

    the unknown ttt is stuck in the exponent, not sitting as a plain factor. Every quantity solved for so far in this lesson, whether a balance or a principal, has been found by running the formula forward or by dividing, and neither of those operations can pull an exponent down on its own.

    Without a tool for that, the honest way to answer a question like "when does this cross a target" is to evaluate the formula at whole-number candidates and watch for the value to cross the target, exactly what part B does with years 555 and 666. That bracket is not a shortcut standing in for a real solution; it is the correct method available at this point, and it pins down the answer for a whole-year question just as precisely as an exact method would. A tool for isolating the exponent directly is introduced in the next lesson.

    In one line

    The ticket is predicted to cost 240.88240.88240.88 dollars after 555 years, and it first crosses a 250250250 dollar budget in year 666, since year 555's price of 240.88240.88240.88 dollars is still under budget while year 666's price of 255.33255.33255.33 dollars is over; bracketing candidate years this way stands in for solving the exponent directly, which this course has not yet introduced a tool for.
