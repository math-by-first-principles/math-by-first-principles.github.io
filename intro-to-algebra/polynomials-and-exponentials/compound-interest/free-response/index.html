  1. Problem 1 The semiannual balance

    An account receives a single deposit of 140014001400 dollars and pays 6%6\%6% compounded semiannually for 333 years, with no further deposits or withdrawals. Find the balance at the end of the 333 years, to the nearest cent.

    1. Hint 1

      Each period pays only a share of the yearly rate, and the exponent counts periods rather than years.

    2. Hint 2

      Semiannual compounding means n=2n=2n=2, so the rate per period is 0.06÷20.06\div20.06÷2 and the number of periods is 2(3)2(3)2(3).

    Answer

    1671.671671.671671.67 dollars.

    Full solution

    Semiannual compounding gives n=2n=2n=2, so each period pays

    0.06÷2=0.030.06\div2=0.030.06÷2=0.03

    and over 333 years there are

    nt=2(3)=6\begin{aligned} nt&=2(3)\\ &=6 \end{aligned}nt​=2(3)=6​

    periods.

    Substituting into the formula gives

    A=1400(1.03)6A=1400(1.03)^6A=1400(1.03)6

    The sixth power is 1.036≈1.1940521.03^6\approx1.1940521.036≈1.194052, so the balance is

    A≈1671.67A\approx1671.67A≈1671.67

    dollars.

    Using the exponent 333 instead of 666 would count years rather than periods and understate the balance.

    Answer

    1671.671671.671671.67 dollars.

    Key idea

    Compounding nnn times a year divides the rate by nnn and multiplies the exponent by nnn, so the exponent is ntntnt.

  2. Problem 2 The extra deposit

    An account already contains 160160160 dollars. A single extra deposit, a whole number of dollars, is made now, before any interest is earned. The account then pays 10%10\%10% compounded annually for 222 years with no further changes, and the final balance must be at least 300300300 dollars. What is the smallest extra deposit that works?

    2. Hint 2

    888888 dollars, which makes the principal 248248248 dollars.

    Full solution

    Two years multiply the principal by

    1.102=1.211.10^2=1.211.102=1.21

    so a principal of PPP dollars must satisfy

    1.21P≥3001.21P\ge3001.21P≥300

    P≥247.93…P\ge247.93\ldotsP≥247.93…

    The extra deposit is then

    248−160=88248-160=88248−160=88

    dollars.

    Checking,

    248(1.21)=300.08248(1.21)=300.08248(1.21)=300.08

    dollars clears the target, while a principal of 247247247 dollars gives only

    247(1.21)=298.87247(1.21)=298.87247(1.21)=298.87

  3. Problem 3 The retained interest

    An account earns 363636 dollars of interest during its first year at 8%8\%8% compounded annually. All interest stays in the account, and there are no other deposits or withdrawals. How many more dollars of interest does the account earn during its second year than during its first?

    1. Hint 1

      The second interest payment is figured on the balance that already includes the first payment.

    2. Hint 2

      Recover the principal from the first payment and the decimal rate, then add the retained interest before applying the rate again.

    Answer

    2.882.882.88 dollars more; the second payment is 38.8838.8838.88 dollars.

    Full solution

    The first payment is 0.08P=360.08P=360.08P=36, so the principal is

    P=36÷0.08=450\begin{aligned} P&=36\div0.08\\ &=450 \end{aligned}P​=36÷0.08=450​

    dollars.

    Retaining that interest makes the balance

    450+36=486450+36=486450+36=486

    dollars.

    The second payment is

    0.08(486)=38.880.08(486)=38.880.08(486)=38.88

    dollars, which exceeds the first by

    38.88−36=2.8838.88-36=2.8838.88−36=2.88

    dollars.

    That excess is exactly 8%8\%8% of the retained 363636 dollars, the interest the first payment earns for itself.

    Answer

    2.882.882.88 dollars more; the second payment is 38.8838.8838.88 dollars.

    Key idea

    Retained interest joins the balance, so each payment exceeds the one before it by the rate applied to that earlier payment.

  4. Problem 4 The two deposits

  5. Problem 5 The withdrawal record

  6. Problem 6 The balance gap

    Two accounts earn 8%8\%8% compounded annually for 333 years. Their initial principals differ by 808080 dollars. There are no deposits, withdrawals, or fees.     Full solution

    80(1.259712)≈100.7880(1.259712)\approx100.7880(1.259712)≈100.78

    dollars.

    The gap has increased by

    100.78−80=20.78100.78-80=20.78100.78−80=20.78

    dollars.

    The difference behaves exactly like an additional 808080 dollars earning the same interest.

    Answer

    Final gap: 100.78100.78100.78 dollars. Increase in gap: 20.7820.7820.78 dollars.

  8. Problem 8 The two-year claim

    A principal P>0P>0P>0 earns a positive annual decimal rate rrr, with no money added or removed. Lena claims that after exactly 222 years, annual compounding exceeds simple interest by Pr2Pr^2Pr2. Decide whether she is correct, justifying algebraically, and then state, in terms of PPP and rrr, by how much annual compounding exceeds simple interest after exactly 333 years.

    1. Hint 1

      Write the two balances over the same period before comparing them.

    2. Hint 2

      Expand the annual factor raised to the number of years, then subtract the simple-interest balance.

    3. Hint 3

      Over three years every term of the expanded cube matters, not only the squared one.

    Answer

    Yes, the two-year excess is Pr2Pr^2Pr2; after 333 years the excess is 3Pr2+Pr33Pr^2+Pr^33Pr2+Pr3.

    Full solution

    The compound balance is P(1+r)2P(1+r)^2P(1+r)2 and the simple balance is P(1+2r)P(1+2r)P(1+2r).

    The squared factor expands to

    P(1+r)2=P+2Pr+Pr2P(1+r)^2=P+2Pr+Pr^2P(1+r)2=P+2Pr+Pr2

    Subtracting P+2PrP+2PrP+2Pr leaves

    P(1+r)2−P(1+2r)=Pr2P(1+r)^2-P(1+2r)=Pr^2P(1+r)2−P(1+2r)=Pr2

    so Lena is correct.

    Over 333 years the compound balance expands to the four terms PPP, 3Pr3Pr3Pr, 3Pr23Pr^23Pr2 and Pr3Pr^3Pr3, while the simple balance is only P+3PrP+3PrP+3Pr.

    Subtracting that simple balance leaves an excess of

    3Pr2+Pr33Pr^2+Pr^33Pr2+Pr3

    Since P>0P>0P>0 and r>0r>0r>0, both excesses are positive, matching interest earned on earlier interest.

    Answer

    Yes, the two-year excess is Pr2Pr^2Pr2; after 333 years the excess is 3Pr2+Pr33Pr^2+Pr^33Pr2+Pr3.

    Key idea

    Compounding beats simple interest by the interest earned on earlier interest, Pr2Pr^2Pr2 after two years and 3Pr2+Pr33Pr^2+Pr^33Pr2+Pr3 after three.
