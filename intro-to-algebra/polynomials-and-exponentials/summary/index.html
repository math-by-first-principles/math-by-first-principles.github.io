Polynomials, Exponentials, and Logarithms: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Polynomial
A finite sum of terms, each a real coefficient times a nonnegative-integer power of xxx. No variable in a denominator or under a root, and no negative or fractional exponent.
Term and coefficient
A term is one coefficient times one power of xxx; the coefficient is its numerical factor with its sign, as −5-5−5 is in −5x2-5x^2−5x2.
Degree
Of a term, its power of xxx (7x7x7x is degree 111, a constant degree 000); of a polynomial, the largest degree among its terms.
Leading term, leading coefficient, constant term
The highest-degree term, its coefficient (never 000), and the x0x^0x0 term: in 6x3−x5+46x^3 - x^5 + 46x3−x5+4, −x5-x^5−x5, −1-1−1, and 444.
Standard form
Terms in descending order of degree, highest power first and the constant last.
Monomial, binomial, trinomial
Polynomials of one, two, and three terms.
Like terms
Terms carrying the same power of xxx. Only these merge into one term.
Closure
Adding, subtracting, or multiplying polynomials always gives another polynomial.
Exponential function
The variable in the exponent over a fixed base b>0b > 0b>0 with b≠1b \ne 1b=1: f(x)=a⋅bxf(x) = a \cdot b^{x}f(x)=a⋅bx.
Power function
Variable in the base under a fixed exponent, as in x2x^2x2. The opposite arrangement, not exponential.
Principal PPP, rate rrr, frequency nnn
The amount deposited, the yearly rate as a decimal (5%5\%5% is r=0.05r = 0.05r=0.05), and the compounding periods per year.
The number eee
The constant e≈2.71828e \approx 2.71828e≈2.71828 that (1+1m)m\left(1 + \tfrac{1}{m}\right)^{m}(1+m1​)m climbs toward as mmm grows.
Logarithm
An exponent. Read log⁡b(y)\log_{b}(y)logb​(y) as "the exponent that turns bbb into yyy": bx=yb^{x} = ybx=y is exponential form, log⁡b(y)=x\log_{b}(y) = xlogb​(y)=x logarithmic form.
Common log and natural log
log⁡(y)\log(y)log(y) with no base written means log⁡10(y)\log_{10}(y)log10​(y); ln⁡(y)\ln(y)ln(y) means log⁡e(y)\log_{e}(y)loge​(y).

Formulas and theorems

  • Combining like terms

    c1xk+c2xk=(c1+c2)xkc_1 x^{k} + c_2 x^{k} = (c_1 + c_2)x^{k}c1​xk+c2​xk=(c1​+c2​)xk

    Use when The same power kkk on both. Add the coefficients and keep the power; different powers never merge.

  • Subtracting a polynomial

    −(A+B+C)=−A−B−C-(A + B + C) = -A - B - C−(A+B+C)=−A−B−C

    Use when A factor of −1-1−1 on EVERY term inside, however many there are.

  • Degree of a sum or difference

    deg⁡(p±q)≤max⁡(deg⁡p,deg⁡q)\deg(p \pm q) \le \max(\deg p, \deg q)deg(p±q)≤max(degp,degq)

    Use when Equality unless ppp and qqq share a degree AND their leading coefficients cancel, the only way a degree can drop; then it falls to the next surviving power, or the result is 000, whose degree is left undefined.

    e.g. (5x3+x)−(5x3−2)=x+2(5x^3 + x) - (5x^3 - 2) = x + 2(5x3+x)−(5x3−2)=x+2: degree 111 from two degree-333 polynomials.

  • One term times one term

    (axm)(cxn)=ac xm+n(a x^{m})(c x^{n}) = ac\,x^{m+n}(axm)(cxn)=acxm+n

    Use when Multiply the coefficients, ADD the exponents. A negative sign folds into the coefficient.

    e.g. (−4x5)(3x2)=−12x7(-4x^5)(3x^2) = -12x^7(−4x5)(3x2)=−12x7.

  • Two binomials (FOIL)

    (a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd(a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd(a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd

    Use when Four products (First, Outer, Inner, Last), and only for two terms times two terms. The rule that always works: every term of the first times every term of the second, mnmnmn products in all. A lone term distributes the same way.

    e.g. (x−5)(x+2)=x2−3x−10(x - 5)(x + 2) = x^2 - 3x - 10(x−5)(x+2)=x2−3x−10.

  • Degree of a product

    deg⁡(pq)=deg⁡p+deg⁡q\deg(pq) = \deg p + \deg qdeg(pq)=degp+degq

    Use when Neither factor the zero polynomial. No exception: leading terms multiply, and nonzero times nonzero cannot cancel.

  • Exponential y=a bxy = a\,b^{x}y=abx, and its graph

    y=a bxy = a\,b^{x}y=abx
    Text description

    Two exponential curves meeting at the point (0, a): the growth curve flattens toward the asymptote y = 0 on the left and climbs on the right, and the decay curve is its mirror image.

    Use when a>0a > 0a>0, b>0b > 0b>0, b≠1b \ne 1b=1: a negative base breaks at fractional exponents, b=1b = 1b=1 is a constant. Growth when b>1b > 1b>1, decay when 0<b<10 < b < 10<b<1. Through (0,a)(0, a)(0,a), horizontal asymptote y=0y = 0y=0 (flat side left for growth, right for decay), domain all real numbers, range y>0y > 0y>0, one-to-one, which is why bxb^{x}bx has an inverse, the logarithm.

    e.g. y=3⋅2xy = 3 \cdot 2^{x}y=3⋅2x grows from 333; y=5(13)xy = 5\left(\tfrac{1}{3}\right)^{x}y=5(31​)x decays from 555.

  • Adding inputs multiplies outputs

    bx+y=bx⋅byb^{x + y} = b^{x} \cdot b^{y}bx+y=bx⋅by

    Use when b>0b > 0b>0, any real xxx and yyy. With y=1y = 1y=1: each unit step right multiplies the output by bbb, the signature of an exponential.

    e.g. 32⋅34=36=7293^{2} \cdot 3^{4} = 3^{6} = 72932⋅34=36=729.

  • Simple interest

    A=P(1+rt)A = P(1 + rt)A=P(1+rt)

    Use when rrr a decimal, ttt in years. Linear: the same PrPrPr is added every year, never interest on interest.

    e.g. 100010001000 dollars at 5%5\%5% for 333 years: 1000(1+0.05⋅3)=11501000(1 + 0.05 \cdot 3) = 11501000(1+0.05⋅3)=1150 dollars.

  • Compound interest

    A=P(1+rn)ntP=A(1+rn)nt\begin{gathered} A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt} \\ P = \frac{A}{\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}} \end{gathered}A=P(1+nr​)ntP=(1+nr​)ntA​​

    Use when nnn is compounding periods per YEAR (222 semiannual, 444 quarterly, 121212 monthly), rrr a decimal, ttt in years. The rate is divided by nnn AND the exponent multiplied by nnn. Annual is n=1n = 1n=1: A=P(1+r)tA = P(1 + r)^{t}A=P(1+r)t. Solving for PPP needs no logarithm: the unknown is a plain factor, so division finishes it.

    e.g. 150015001500 dollars at 6%6\%6% semiannually for 222 years: 1500(1.03)4≈1688.261500(1.03)^{4} \approx 1688.261500(1.03)4≈1688.26 dollars.

  • Continuous compounding

    A=PertA = P e^{rt}A=Pert

    Use when The limit as nnn grows without bound, and a CEILING: with r>0r > 0r>0, compounding more often earns a little more each time, by shrinking amounts, but no finite schedule reaches PertPe^{rt}Pert.

    e.g. 200020002000 dollars at 3%3\%3% for 111 year: 2000e0.03≈2060.912000e^{0.03} \approx 2060.912000e0.03≈2060.91 dollars.

  • Definition of the logarithm

    log⁡b(y)=x  ⟺  bx=y\log_{b}(y) = x \iff b^{x} = ylogb​(y)=x⟺bx=y

    Use when Base b>0b > 0b>0 with b≠1b \ne 1b=1, input y>0y > 0y>0; log⁡b(0)\log_{b}(0)logb​(0) and the log of a negative are undefined, a positive base to a real power being always positive. Read off log⁡b(1)=0\log_{b}(1) = 0logb​(1)=0, log⁡b(b)=1\log_{b}(b) = 1logb​(b)=1.

  • The logarithm and the exponential undo each other

    blog⁡b(y)=y,log⁡b(bx)=xb^{\log_{b}(y)} = y, \qquad \log_{b}(b^{x}) = xblogb​(y)=y,logb​(bx)=x

    Use when b>0b > 0b>0 with b≠1b \ne 1b=1. The first needs y>0y > 0y>0; the second holds for every real xxx.

  • The three laws of logarithms

    log⁡b(MN)=log⁡bM+log⁡bNlog⁡b ⁣(MN)=log⁡bM−log⁡bNlog⁡b(Mk)=klog⁡bM\begin{gathered} \log_{b}(MN) = \log_{b} M + \log_{b} N \\ \log_{b}\!\left(\frac{M}{N}\right) = \log_{b} M - \log_{b} N \\ \log_{b}(M^{k}) = k\log_{b} M \end{gathered}logb​(MN)=logb​M+logb​Nlogb​(NM​)=logb​M−logb​Nlogb​(Mk)=klogb​M​

    Use when One base throughout, b>0b > 0b>0, b≠1b \ne 1b=1, M>0M > 0M>0, N>0N > 0N>0, kkk any real. Products become sums, quotients differences, powers coefficients. The power law moves an exponent on the INPUT out front, so log⁡b(Mk)\log_{b}(M^{k})logb​(Mk) is not (log⁡bM)k\left(\log_{b} M\right)^{k}(logb​M)k. There is NO law for the log of a sum.

  • Change of base, and solving for an exponent

    log⁡b(y)=log⁡(y)log⁡(b)\log_{b}(y) = \frac{\log(y)}{\log(b)}logb​(y)=log(b)log(y)​

    Use when y>0y > 0y>0, b>0b > 0b>0, b≠1b \ne 1b=1. One base top and bottom, ln⁡\lnln as readily as log⁡\loglog. The answer is a QUOTIENT.

    e.g. log⁡2(10)=log⁡10log⁡2≈10.3010≈3.32\log_{2}(10) = \frac{\log 10}{\log 2} \approx \frac{1}{0.3010} \approx 3.32log2​(10)=log2log10​≈0.30101​≈3.32.

  • Graph of y=log⁡b(x)y = \log_{b}(x)y=logb​(x)

    The reflection of y=bxy = b^{x}y=bx across y=xy = xy=x: through (1,0)(1, 0)(1,0) and (b,1)(b, 1)(b,1), vertical asymptote x=0x = 0x=0, domain x>0x > 0x>0, range all real numbers.

    Text description

    The exponential and the logarithm are mirror images across the line y = x, so their points, intercepts, and asymptotes all swap coordinates.

    Use when b>0b > 0b>0 with b≠1b \ne 1b=1; increasing when b>1b > 1b>1, decreasing when 0<b<10 < b < 10<b<1. Domain and range are the exponential's, swapped.

Problem types, step by step

Add or subtract two polynomials

  1. For a subtraction, distribute the minus across every term of the second polynomial first.
  2. Drop the parentheses and group the terms by power.
  3. Add the coefficients in each group, leave the power unchanged, and write descending order.

e.g. (6x2−x)−(2x2+4x−3)=6x2−x−2x2−4x+3=4x2−5x+3(6x^2 - x) - (2x^2 + 4x - 3) = 6x^2 - x - 2x^2 - 4x + 3 = 4x^2 - 5x + 3(6x2−x)−(2x2+4x−3)=6x2−x−2x2−4x+3=4x2−5x+3.

Expand a product of two polynomials

  1. Count the products you owe: mmm terms times nnn terms is mnmnmn of them.
  2. Multiply every term of the first by every term of the second, carrying signs, coefficients multiplying and exponents adding.
  3. Use a grid past four products, so none is dropped.
  4. Combine like terms and write standard form.

e.g. (x+2)(x2−3x+1)=x3−x2−5x+2(x + 2)(x^2 - 3x + 1) = x^3 - x^2 - 5x + 2(x+2)(x2−3x+1)=x3−x2−5x+2, from six products.

Decide whether a rule or a table is exponential

  1. In a rule, locate the variable: in the exponent is exponential, in the base is a power function.
  2. In a table with equally spaced inputs, test the differences first; a constant difference is linear.
  3. Then test the ratios: a constant, positive ratio other than 111 means the data fits an exponential rule, and when the inputs step by 111 that ratio is the base bbb.
  4. Read aaa as the output at x=0x = 0x=0 and write f(x)=a⋅bxf(x) = a \cdot b^{x}f(x)=a⋅bx.

e.g. Outputs 2,6,18,542, 6, 18, 542,6,18,54 at x=0,1,2,3x = 0, 1, 2, 3x=0,1,2,3: ratio 333, so f(x)=2⋅3xf(x) = 2 \cdot 3^{x}f(x)=2⋅3x.

Describe and evaluate f(x)=a bxf(x) = a\,b^{x}f(x)=abx

  1. With a>0a > 0a>0, read the base for growth (b>1b > 1b>1) or decay (0<b<10 < b < 10<b<1), and aaa as the value at x=0x = 0x=0.
  2. To evaluate, substitute: a zero exponent gives 111, a negative exponent a reciprocal, a fractional exponent a root.
  3. To describe, add the asymptote y=0y = 0y=0 and, for a>0a > 0a>0, the range y>0y > 0y>0.

e.g. f(x)=24(12)xf(x) = 24\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{x}f(x)=24(21​)x decays from 242424, and f(3)=24⋅18=3f(3) = 24 \cdot \tfrac{1}{8} = 3f(3)=24⋅81​=3.

Find a compound-interest balance

  1. Convert the percent to a decimal and read off PPP, rrr, ttt, and nnn.
  2. Substitute, dividing the rate by nnn and multiplying the exponent by nnn.
  3. Evaluate the growth factor in full, multiply by PPP, and round only the final balance to the nearest cent.

e.g. 200020002000 dollars at 4%4\%4% compounded annually for 222 years: 2000(1.04)2=2163.202000(1.04)^{2} = 2163.202000(1.04)2=2163.20 dollars.

Evaluate a logarithm by hand

  1. Ask what the definition asks: the base to what power gives the input?
  2. Rewrite the input as a power of the base, using a negative exponent for a reciprocal.
  3. Report that exponent. If the input is 000 or negative, stop: the logarithm is undefined.

e.g. log⁡2 ⁣(116)\log_{2}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{16}\right)log2​(161​): since 2−4=1162^{-4} = \tfrac{1}{16}2−4=161​, the answer is −4-4−4.

Expand and combine known logarithms with the laws

  1. Factor the input into pieces whose logarithms you know or were given.
  2. Split products into sums and quotients into differences, one base throughout.
  3. Bring every exponent on an input down to a coefficient in front.
  4. The same laws run the other way too: a sum of two logs with the same base combines into the logarithm of a product, and a difference combines into the logarithm of a quotient.

e.g. Given log⁡b(2)≈0.30\log_{b}(2) \approx 0.30logb​(2)≈0.30 and log⁡b(3)≈0.48\log_{b}(3) \approx 0.48logb​(3)≈0.48: log⁡b(12)≈2(0.30)+0.48=1.08\log_{b}(12) \approx 2(0.30) + 0.48 = 1.08logb​(12)≈2(0.30)+0.48=1.08.

Solve for an unknown exponent, including a doubling time

  1. Isolate the power so the equation reads bx=yb^{x} = ybx=y with y>0y > 0y>0; doubling gives (1+r)t=2(1 + r)^{t} = 2(1+r)t=2.
  2. Take the same logarithm of both sides and use the power law: xlog⁡(b)=log⁡(y)x\log(b) = \log(y)xlog(b)=log(y).
  3. Divide by log⁡(b)\log(b)log(b) and evaluate.

e.g. Doubling at 5%5\%5% compounded annually: t=log⁡2log⁡1.05≈0.30100.0212≈14.2t = \frac{\log 2}{\log 1.05} \approx \frac{0.3010}{0.0212} \approx 14.2t=log1.05log2​≈0.02120.3010​≈14.2 years. Interest is only credited once a year, so the balance has not yet doubled at year 141414; the first completed year it reaches double is year 151515.

Exam traps

  • Trap Splitting the logarithm of a SUM: log⁡2(4+4)=log⁡2(4)+log⁡2(4)=4\log_{2}(4 + 4) = \log_{2}(4) + \log_{2}(4) = 4log2​(4+4)=log2​(4)+log2​(4)=4.

    Fix No law breaks up the log of a sum. Add inside first: log⁡2(4+4)=log⁡2(8)=3\log_{2}(4 + 4) = \log_{2}(8) = 3log2​(4+4)=log2​(8)=3. The product law needs a PRODUCT.

  • Trap Flipping only the first term of a subtraction: (5x2−3x+4)−(2x2+6x−7)=3x2+3x−3(5x^2 - 3x + 4) - (2x^2 + 6x - 7) = 3x^2 + 3x - 3(5x2−3x+4)−(2x2+6x−7)=3x2+3x−3.

    Fix All three signs flip: 5x2−3x+4−2x2−6x+7=3x2−9x+115x^2 - 3x + 4 - 2x^2 - 6x + 7 = 3x^2 - 9x + 115x2−3x+4−2x2−6x+7=3x2−9x+11.

  • Trap Squaring a sum term by term: (x+4)2=x2+16(x + 4)^2 = x^2 + 16(x+4)2=x2+16.

    Fix (x+4)2(x + 4)^2(x+4)2 is (x+4)(x+4)(x + 4)(x + 4)(x+4)(x+4), four products with two cross terms: x2+8x+16x^2 + 8x + 16x2+8x+16.

  • Trap Doing the wrong thing to exponents: 3x2+5x2=8x43x^2 + 5x^2 = 8x^43x2+5x2=8x4 when adding, x2⋅x3=x6x^2 \cdot x^3 = x^6x2⋅x3=x6 when multiplying.

    Fix Adding like terms adds the COEFFICIENTS and leaves the power alone, giving 8x28x^28x2. Multiplying powers ADDS the exponents, giving x5x^5x5.

  • Trap Reading b0b^{0}b0 as 000, or a negative exponent as a negative output, so 2−32^{-3}2−3 looks like −8-8−8.

    Fix b0=1b^{0} = 1b0=1 for every allowed base, and a negative exponent gives a reciprocal: 2−3=182^{-3} = \tfrac{1}{8}2−3=81​, positive like every exponential output.

  • Trap Treating bxb^{x}bx and xbx^{b}xb as the same species.

    Fix 2x2^{x}2x is exponential, x2x^{2}x2 a power function: at x=3x = 3x=3 they give 888 and 999, and past x=4x = 4x=4 the exponential outruns it for good.

  • Trap Putting the percent itself into an interest formula: A=1000(1+5)tA = 1000(1 + 5)^{t}A=1000(1+5)t for 5%5\%5%.

    Fix The rate is the decimal, so A=1000(1.05)tA = 1000(1.05)^{t}A=1000(1.05)t. Writing 555 multiplies the balance by 666 every year.

  • Trap Solving bx=yb^{x} = ybx=y as x=log⁡(y)−log⁡(b)x = \log(y) - \log(b)x=log(y)−log(b), or as x=log⁡ ⁣(yb)x = \log\!\left(\tfrac{y}{b}\right)x=log(by​).

    Fix Both are the quotient law, a different situation. Dividing xlog⁡(b)=log⁡(y)x\log(b) = \log(y)xlog(b)=log(y) by log⁡(b)\log(b)log(b) leaves x=log⁡(y)log⁡(b)x = \dfrac{\log(y)}{\log(b)}x=log(b)log(y)​, a QUOTIENT.
