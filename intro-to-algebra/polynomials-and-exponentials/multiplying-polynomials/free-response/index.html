    1. A garden bed and its enlargement . Application, 10 points. Question 1 of 5.

    Application 10 pts

    A rectangular garden bed measures (x+8)(x + 8)(x+8) feet by (x+3)(x + 3)(x+3) feet. The gardener plans to enlarge it by extending both the length and the width by 222 feet, keeping the shape rectangular, so the enlarged bed measures (x+10)(x + 10)(x+10) feet by (x+5)(x + 5)(x+5) feet.

    1. A
      Part A.

      Write the ORIGINAL garden bed's area as a single polynomial in standard form, showing the four products that produce it.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 3 points

    2. B
      Part B.

      Write the ENLARGED bed's area as a single polynomial in standard form, the same way.

      Write the expression An equation or an expression is enough here. Show how you built it. 3 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      It is tempting to expect the enlargement to add exactly 2×2=42 \times 2 = 42×2=4 square feet, on the reasoning that only the corner where the two 222-foot extensions overlap really changes. Using your two areas from parts A and B, find how much area the enlargement actually adds, and explain, in terms of the strips and corner the extensions create, how the actual increase compares to that expectation.

      Carry your own answer forward Use the two areas you found in parts A and B; the point here is explaining the size of the increase, not reproducing a particular pair of expressions.

      Explain what it means Words, not just symbols. Say what the number is telling you about the situation. 4 points

    Hints

    One at a time, each one a step further than the last. Take only as many as you need.

    1. Hint 1 of 4

      Every area here is a product of two side lengths, so this whole question is the distributive property used twice: each side length is itself a sum of two parts.

    2. Hint 2 of 4 · Part A

      Before multiplying, notice the original bed's dimensions are (x+8)(x + 8)(x+8) and (x+3)(x + 3)(x+3): form the four products these two binomials produce.

    3. Hint 3 of 4 · Part B

      The enlarged bed's dimensions add 222 feet to EACH of the original two side lengths; write those two new binomials before you multiply them.

    4. Hint 4 of 4 · Part C

      Subtract the two areas you already have to get the actual increase, then split that increase into the pieces the box model predicts: two new strips and one new corner square.

    That is every hint for this question.

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    x2+11x+24x^2 + 11x + 24x2+11x+24 square feet.

    Part B

    x2+15x+50x^2 + 15x + 50x2+15x+50 square feet.

    Part C

    The increase is 4x+264x + 264x+26 square feet: extending by 222 feet on two adjoining sides adds two long strips, of lengths x+8x + 8x+8 and x+3x + 3x+3, each 222 feet wide, plus the one 2×22\times22×2 corner square, not just the corner alone.

    Not what you got? Look for the slip on your own paper before you open the solution. Finding it yourself is worth more than reading it.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Multiply each term of the first factor by each term of the second, four products in all.

    (x+8)(x+3)=x⋅x+x⋅3+8⋅x+8⋅3.(x + 8)(x + 3) = x\cdot x + x\cdot 3 + 8\cdot x + 8\cdot 3.(x+8)(x+3)=x⋅x+x⋅3+8⋅x+8⋅3.

    Combine the two middle products, 3x+8x=11x3x + 8x = 11x3x+8x=11x:

    (x+8)(x+3)=x2+11x+24.(x + 8)(x + 3) = x^2 + 11x + 24.(x+8)(x+3)=x2+11x+24.

    Part B

    (x+10)(x+5)=x⋅x+x⋅5+10⋅x+10⋅5=x2+5x+10x+50=x2+15x+50.(x + 10)(x + 5) = x\cdot x + x\cdot 5 + 10\cdot x + 10\cdot 5 = x^2 + 5x + 10x + 50 = x^2 + 15x + 50.(x+10)(x+5)=x⋅x+x⋅5+10⋅x+10⋅5=x2+5x+10x+50=x2+15x+50.

    Part C

    Subtract the original area from the enlarged area:

    (x2+15x+50)−(x2+11x+24)=4x+26.(x^2 + 15x + 50) - (x^2 + 11x + 24) = 4x + 26.(x2+15x+50)−(x2+11x+24)=4x+26.

    The extension is 222 feet along the length and 222 feet along the width, so the box model gains three new pieces: a strip 222 feet wide running the old width x+3x + 3x+3 feet, a strip 222 feet wide running the old length x+8x + 8x+8 feet, and a 2×22\times22×2 corner square where the two strips meet.

    2(x+3)+2(x+8)+2⋅2=(2x+6)+(2x+16)+4=4x+26.2(x + 3) + 2(x + 8) + 2\cdot2 = (2x + 6) + (2x + 16) + 4 = 4x + 26.2(x+3)+2(x+8)+2⋅2=(2x+6)+(2x+16)+4=4x+26.

    The corner alone is only 444 square feet, so most of the increase comes from the two strips, not the overlap alone.

    In one line

    The original bed has area x2+11x+24x^2 + 11x + 24x2+11x+24 square feet, the enlarged bed has area x2+15x+50x^2 + 15x + 50x2+15x+50 square feet, and the enlargement actually adds 4x+264x + 264x+26 square feet, far more than the 444 square feet a single corner would give, because it also adds two strips running the old length and the old width.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents of the like base in each case.

    (4x3)(−5x2)=(4⋅(−5)) x3+2=−20x5.(4x^3)(-5x^2) = (4\cdot(-5))\,x^{3+2} = -20x^5.(4x3)(−5x2)=(4⋅(−5))x3+2=−20x5.

    Both factors of the second product are negative, so their product is positive:

    (−2x)(−3x4)=((−2)⋅(−3)) x1+4=6x5.(-2x)(-3x^4) = ((-2)\cdot(-3))\,x^{1+4} = 6x^5.(−2x)(−3x4)=((−2)⋅(−3))x1+4=6x5.

    Part B

    The lone factor 3x23x^23x2 reaches every term inside the parentheses.

    3x2(2x3−5x+4)=(3x2)(2x3)+(3x2)(−5x)+(3x2)(4).3x^2(2x^3 - 5x + 4) = (3x^2)(2x^3) + (3x^2)(-5x) + (3x^2)(4).3x2(2x3−5x+4)=(3x2)(2x3)+(3x2)(−5x)+(3x2)(4).

    Each small product multiplies coefficients and adds exponents:

    3x2(2x3−5x+4)=6x5−15x3+12x2.3x^2(2x^3 - 5x + 4) = 6x^5 - 15x^3 + 12x^2.3x2(2x3−5x+4)=6x5−15x3+12x2.

    Part C

    Write each power out as a repeated product. x2x^2x2 is x⋅xx\cdot xx⋅x and x3x^3x3 is x⋅x⋅xx\cdot x\cdot xx⋅x⋅x, so their product strings all five factors of xxx together:

    x2⋅x3=(x⋅x)(x⋅x⋅x)=x5.x^2\cdot x^3 = (x\cdot x)(x\cdot x\cdot x) = x^5.x2⋅x3=(x⋅x)(x⋅x⋅x)=x5.

    There are five xxx's multiplied, not six, because lining up two strings of factors end to end only concatenates them; it does not change how many factors are in each string. That is why the rule adds the exponents rather than multiplying them.

    The same reasoning applies to any pair of powers of xxx. For x4⋅x4x^4\cdot x^4x4⋅x4, four factors meet four more factors, eight in total:

    x4⋅x4=x4+4=x8.x^4\cdot x^4 = x^{4+4} = x^8.x4⋅x4=x4+4=x8.

    In one line

    (4x3)(−5x2)=−20x5(4x^3)(-5x^2) = -20x^5(4x3)(−5x2)=−20x5 and (−2x)(−3x4)=6x5(-2x)(-3x^4) = 6x^5(−2x)(−3x4)=6x5; distributing 3x23x^23x2 across (2x3−5x+4)(2x^3-5x+4)(2x3−5x+4) gives 6x5−15x3+12x26x^5-15x^3+12x^26x5−15x3+12x2; and exponents add rather than multiply because x2⋅x3x^2\cdot x^3x2⋅x3 strings five factors of xxx together, the same reasoning that gives x4⋅x4=x8x^4\cdot x^4 = x^8x4⋅x4=x8.

    4. Comparing the degree of a sum and the degree of a product . Reasoning, 13 points. Question 4 of 5.

    Reasoning 13 pts

    Here is a claim: 'Whenever you combine two polynomials, the degree of the result follows the same pattern whether you add or multiply: add the two degrees for a product, or take the larger of the two degrees for a sum.' This question checks each half of that claim in turn.

    1. A
      Part A.

    2. B
      Part B.

      Construct a counterexample Give one specific case, and show it breaks the claim. 5 points

    3. C
      Part C.

      Contrast what you found in part A with what you found in part B. State, in GUARDED language, what is always true about the degree of a sum or difference of two polynomials, and explain why the analogous statement for a product needs no such guard.

      Carry your own answer forward Draw on the pair you built in part B and the leading-term argument from part A; the point here is the contrast between the two rules, not reproducing either computation.

    Hints

    Answer and solution

    Check your answer first. If it is wrong, go back to your paper: the worked solution will still be here.

    The answer

    Part A

    Degree 555; leading term −8x5-8x^5−8x5.

    Part B

    S(x)=x2+4x−1S(x) = x^2 + 4x - 1S(x)=x2+4x−1 and T(x)=−x2+3x+5T(x) = -x^2 + 3x + 5T(x)=−x2+3x+5 both have degree 222, but S(x)+T(x)=7x+4S(x) + T(x) = 7x + 4S(x)+T(x)=7x+4, degree 111. So the claim that a sum's degree always matches the larger of the two degrees is false as stated.

    Part C

    A sum or difference has degree at most the larger of the two, equalling it unless the two share that degree and their leading terms cancel. A product needs no guard: leading coefficients multiply, and nonzero times nonzero is never zero, so its top term can never cancel.

    Worked solution

    Part A

    The leading term of a product comes from multiplying the two leading terms, since every other pairing involves at least one factor of smaller degree.

    PPP has leading term 4x34x^34x3 and QQQ has leading term −2x2-2x^2−2x2, so

    (4x3)(−2x2)=−8x3+2=−8x5.(4x^3)(-2x^2) = -8x^{3+2} = -8x^5.(4x3)(−2x2)=−8x3+2=−8x5.

    No other product in the full expansion can reach degree 555, and the coefficient is not zero, so the product genuinely has degree 555 with that leading term.

    Part B

    Pick two degree-222 polynomials whose leading terms are opposites, so they are guaranteed to cancel.

    S(x)=x2+4x−1,T(x)=−x2+3x+5.S(x) = x^2 + 4x - 1, \qquad T(x) = -x^2 + 3x + 5.S(x)=x2+4x−1,T(x)=−x2+3x+5.

    Both have degree 222. Add them:

    S(x)+T(x)=(x2−x2)+(4x+3x)+(−1+5)=7x+4.S(x) + T(x) = (x^2 - x^2) + (4x + 3x) + (-1 + 5) = 7x + 4.S(x)+T(x)=(x2−x2)+(4x+3x)+(−1+5)=7x+4.

    The x2x^2x2 terms cancel completely, leaving a degree-111 result. Since 111 is smaller than the 222 the unguarded claim demands, this single pair refutes it.

    Part C

    Part B shows the guard is necessary: two degree-222 polynomials with opposite leading coefficients summed to something of degree 111, so 'the degree of a sum is the larger of the two' fails whenever the top terms are equal and opposite. The correct statement carries that exception: the degree of a sum or difference is at most the larger of the two degrees, and equals it unless the two polynomials share that degree and their leading terms cancel.

    Part A shows why a product never needs the same guard. Adding two leading coefficients can hit 000, but multiplying them cannot:

    and a leading coefficient is never 000 by definition. So the top term of a product,

    ab xm+n,ab\,x^{m+n},abxm+n,

    always survives, with nothing else of that degree to cancel it against. Sums have a genuine escape hatch that products structurally cannot.
