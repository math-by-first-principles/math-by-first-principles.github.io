  1. Problem 1 The new total

    The sum of the first 777 terms of an arithmetic sequence is 464646. The sum of its first 888 terms is 555555. Find its eighth term.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the terms included in the two totals.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the shorter total from the longer total.

    Answer

    a8=9a_8=9a8​=9.

    Full solution

    The longer sum contains exactly one extra term, a8a_8a8​.

    Therefore

    a8=55−46.a_8=55-46.a8​=55−46.

    This gives

    a8=9.a_8=9.a8​=9.

    The check is 46+9=5546+9=5546+9=55.

    Answer

    a8=9a_8=9a8​=9.

    Key idea

    The difference between consecutive partial sums is the newly included term.

  2. Problem 2 The late start

    Evaluate ∑j=620(43−3j)\sum_{j=6}^{20}(43-3j)∑j=620​(43−3j).

    1. Hint 1

      Each step of the index changes the summand by the same amount, so the terms are evenly spaced.

    2. Hint 2

      Both limits are included, so count the index values from the lower limit through the upper limit.

    3. Hint 3

      Substitute each limit to get the first and last terms, then use the arithmetic sum formula.

    Answer

    606060.

    Full solution

    Raising the index by 111 lowers the summand by 333, so the terms are evenly spaced.

    Substituting the lower limit gives

    43−3⋅6=25.43-3\cdot6=25.43−3⋅6=25.

    Substituting the upper limit gives

    43−3⋅20=−17.43-3\cdot20=-17.43−3⋅20=−17.

    Both limits are included, so the number of terms is 20−6+120-6+120−6+1, which is 151515.

    The first and last terms add to 888, so

    S=15⋅82.S=\frac{15\cdot8}{2}.S=215⋅8​.

    Therefore S=60S=60S=60.

    Answer

    606060.

    Key idea

    A sigma whose summand changes by a fixed amount per index step is an arithmetic series, so its own first term, last term and count give the total.

  3. Problem 3 The starting value

    The first nine terms of an arithmetic sequence total 117117117, and its common difference is 222. Find the first term.

    1. Hint 1

      The ninth term is eight steps after the first.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the arithmetic sum formula with a9=a1+16a_9=a_1+16a9​=a1​+16.

    Answer

    a1=5a_1=5a1​=5.

    Full solution

    There are eight increases of 222, so

    a9=a1+16.a_9=a_1+16.a9​=a1​+16.

    The sum formula gives

    117=9(2a1+16)2.117=\frac{9(2a_1+16)}{2}.117=29(2a1​+16)​.

    Dividing by 999 and multiplying by 222 gives

    26=2a1+16.26=2a_1+16.26=2a1​+16.

    Therefore

    a1=5.a_1=5.a1​=5.

    The last term is 212121, and

    9(5+21)/2=117.9(5+21)/2=117.9(5+21)/2=117.

    Answer

    a1=5a_1=5a1​=5.

    Key idea

    A known sum and common difference can determine the first term of an arithmetic sequence.

  4. Problem 4 The omitted entries

    A list has 888 arithmetic terms, first term 555, and common difference 333. A report omits the third and sixth entries and includes each of the remaining six entries once. Find the report total.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

    3. Hint 3

    939393.

    The omitted entries are

    a3=5+2⋅3a_3=5+2\cdot3a3​=5+2⋅3

    and

    a6=5+5⋅3.a_6=5+5\cdot3.a6​=5+5⋅3.

    They equal 111111 and 202020.

    The report total is

    124−11−20=93.124-11-20=93.124−11−20=93.

    Answer

    939393.

    Key idea

    Find a complete arithmetic total first, then remove any entries that were excluded.

  5. Problem 5 The overlapping reports

    A record has entries ak=2k+1a_k=2k+1ak​=2k+1. One report totals positions 333 through 111111, inclusive; another totals positions 888 through 151515, inclusive. Find the amount counted twice when the reports are added, and the total for positions 333 through 151515 counted once each.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify the positions common to both reports.

    2. Hint 2

      The overlap runs from the later of the two starting positions to the earlier of the two ending positions.

    3. Hint 3

      For the complete range, count both endpoint positions.

    Answer

    Counted twice: 808080; total counted once: 247247247.

    Full solution

    The overlap consists of positions 888 through 111111, whose values run from 171717 to 232323.

    Its total is

    4(17+23)2=80.\frac{4(17+23)}{2}=80.24(17+23)​=80.

    The union consists of 15−3+1=1315-3+1=1315−3+1=13 positions, from value 777 to value 313131.

    Its total is

    13(7+31)2=247.\frac{13(7+31)}{2}=247.213(7+31)​=247.

    Adding the two report totals would give 247+80=327247+80=327247+80=327, because the overlap is included twice.

    Answer

    Counted twice: 808080; total counted once: 247247247.

    Key idea

    Overlapping index ranges require counting the shared entries only once in the final total.

  6. Problem 6 The distances from zero

    Eight points on a number line have coordinates forming an arithmetic sequence with first term −5-5−5 and common difference 222. Find the sum of their distances from zero.

    1. Hint 1

      A distance from zero is the absolute value of a coordinate.

    2. Hint 2

      Separate the negative coordinates from the positive coordinates.

    3. Hint 3

      Add each group of positive distances.

    Answer

    343434 units.

    Full solution

    The coordinates are −5,−3,−1,1,3,5,7,9-5,-3,-1,1,3,5,7,9−5,−3,−1,1,3,5,7,9.

    Their distances form the groups 5,3,15,3,15,3,1 and 1,3,5,7,91,3,5,7,91,3,5,7,9.

    The first group has sum

    3(5+1)2=9.\frac{3(5+1)}{2}=9.23(5+1)​=9.

    The second group has sum

    5(1+9)2=25.\frac{5(1+9)}{2}=25.25(1+9)​=25.

    The total distance is

    9+25=34.9+25=34.9+25=34.

    Adding the signed coordinates instead would measure a different quantity.

    Answer

    343434 units.

    Key idea

    When summing distances, convert signed coordinates to nonnegative distances before adding.

  7. Problem 7 The two totals

  8. Problem 8 The shifted second row

    An arithmetic row a1,…,ana_1,\ldots,a_na1​,…,an​ has sum SSS. A second row reverses it and adds ccc to every entry. A student says that adding the rows proves 2S=n(a1+an+c)2S=n(a_1+a_n+c)2S=n(a1​+an​+c). Is this correct for every ccc? Use the two rows to derive the correct formula for SSS.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the sum of the second row with SSS.

    2. Hint 2

      Each column still has the same total.

    3. Hint 3

      Account for the added ccc in all nnn entries before solving for SSS.

    Answer

    No (the equality holds only when c=0c=0c=0); S=n(a1+an)2S=\frac{n(a_1+a_n)}{2}S=2n(a1​+an​)​.

    Full solution

    Reversal does not change a sum, but adding ccc to every entry makes the second row total S+ncS+ncS+nc.

    Equal distances from the ends of an arithmetic list give

    aj+an+1−j=a1+an.a_j+a_{n+1-j}=a_1+a_n.aj​+an+1−j​=a1​+an​.

    Set E=a1+anE=a_1+a_nE=a1​+an​.

    Every column therefore totals E+cE+cE+c, and adding all columns gives

    2S+nc=n(E+c).2S+nc=n(E+c).2S+nc=n(E+c).

    Expanding and canceling ncncnc gives

    2S=nE.2S=nE.2S=nE.

    Substituting for EEE yields

    S=n(a1+an)2.S=\frac{n(a_1+a_n)}{2}.S=2n(a1​+an​)​.

    The proposed equality holds when c=0c=0c=0, but omits the change in the second row when c≠0c\ne0c=0.

    Answer

    No (the equality holds only when c=0c=0c=0); S=n(a1+an)2S=\frac{n(a_1+a_n)}{2}S=2n(a1​+an​)​.

    Key idea

    Reversing and adding an arithmetic list pairs equal endpoint sums, but any adjustment to the copied row must also change its total.

  9. Problem 9 The shaded square

    The figure shows a 444 by 444 array with 1,2,3,41,2,3,41,2,3,4 cells shaded in successive rows. In an nnn by nnn version, row kkk has its first kkk cells shaded. Use reflection across the main diagonal to derive a formula for 1+2+⋯+n1+2+\cdots+n1+2+⋯+n.

    Text description of this figure

    A square array of four rows and four columns of equal cells, with the rows numbered 1 to 4 from top to bottom. In row 1 the first cell from the left is shaded. In row 2 the first two cells are shaded, in row 3 the first three, and in row 4 all four cells are shaded. The four cells running from the top left corner to the bottom right corner, the main diagonal, carry a heavier outline. Every shaded cell uses the same light shade, and no count, total or formula is printed.

    1. Hint 1

      The nnn diagonal cells are all shaded.

    2. Hint 2

      Reflection pairs each cell below the diagonal with one above it.

    3. Hint 3

      Count half the cells outside the diagonal, then include the diagonal cells.

    Answer

    1+2+⋯+n=n(n+1)21+2+\cdots+n=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}1+2+⋯+n=2n(n+1)​.

    Full solution

    There are n2n^2n2 cells altogether and nnn on the main diagonal.

    The cell in row iii and column jjj is shaded exactly when j≤ij\le ij≤i, so of any off-diagonal cell and its mirror image, exactly one is shaded.

    Reflection pairs the other n2−nn^2-nn2−n cells, with one shaded cell in each pair.

    If TTT is the number of shaded cells, then

    T=n2−n2+n.T=\frac{n^2-n}{2}+n.T=2n2−n​+n.

    Combining the terms gives

    T=n2+n2.T=\frac{n^2+n}{2}.T=2n2+n​.

    Factoring gives

    T=n(n+1)2.T=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}.T=2n(n+1)​.

    The rows contain 1,2,…,n1,2,\ldots,n1,2,…,n shaded cells, so this is their sum.

    Answer

    1+2+⋯+n=n(n+1)21+2+\cdots+n=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}1+2+⋯+n=2n(n+1)​.

    Key idea

    Counting the same shaded region by rows and by diagonal symmetry proves the formula for the first nnn positive integers.

  10. Problem 10 The zero total

    A list has an odd positive number of arithmetic terms and total 000. Theo says its middle term must be 000. Is he correct? Justify your answer, and say whether your argument depends on the sign of the common difference.

    1. Hint 1

      Write the term count as 2m+12m+12m+1, where m≥0m\ge0m≥0.

    2. Hint 2

      Terms equally far from the middle have average equal to the middle term.

    3. Hint 3

      Express the whole sum using the middle term and the term count.

    Answer

    Yes; the middle term is 000, and the argument does not depend on the sign of the common difference.

    Full solution

    Let the middle term be bbb and the count be 2m+12m+12m+1.

    Terms jjj positions from the middle are b−jdb-jdb−jd and b+jdb+jdb+jd, whose sum is 2b2b2b.

    There are mmm such pairs and the middle term itself, so

    S=2mb+b.S=2mb+b.S=2mb+b.

    Since S=0S=0S=0,

    0=(2m+1)b.0=(2m+1)b.0=(2m+1)b.

    The count 2m+12m+12m+1 is positive, so b=0b=0b=0.

    The argument also covers a one-term list and does not depend on the sign of ddd.

    Answer

    Yes; the middle term is 000, and the argument does not depend on the sign of the common difference.

    Key idea

    The average of an odd-length arithmetic list equals its middle term.
