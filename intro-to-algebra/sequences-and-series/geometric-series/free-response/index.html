  1. Problem 1 The alternating terms

    A geometric sequence begins 2,−8,32,…2,-8,32,\ldots2,−8,32,…, each term being −4-4−4 times the one before it. Find the sum of its first five terms.

    1. Hint 1

      A finite geometric sum needs only the first term, the ratio, and the number of terms.

    2. Hint 2

      Substitute rrr with its minus sign attached, both in the power and in the denominator.

    3. Hint 3

      An odd power of a negative ratio is negative, so subtracting it adds to the numerator.

    Answer

    410410410.

    Full solution

    Read off the three inputs: the first term is a1=2a_1=2a1​=2, the ratio is r=−4r=-4r=−4, and five terms are being added.

    Since r≠1r\ne1r=1, the finite-sum formula applies:

    S5=2(1−(−4)5)1−(−4).S_5=\frac{2\left(1-(-4)^{5}\right)}{1-(-4)}.S5​=1−(−4)2(1−(−4)5)​.

    The fifth power of a negative number is negative, so (−4)5=−1024(-4)^{5}=-1024(−4)5=−1024 and the numerator is

    2(1+1024)=2050.2(1+1024)=2050.2(1+1024)=2050.

    The denominator is 1−(−4)=51-(-4)=51−(−4)=5, so

    S5=20505=410.S_5=\frac{2050}{5}=410.S5​=52050​=410.

    Adding the five terms directly, 2−8+32−128+512=4102-8+32-128+512=4102−8+32−128+512=410, confirms it.

    Answer

    410410410.

    Key idea

    The finite geometric sum formula handles a negative ratio as long as the sign travels into every power.

  2. Problem 2 The sigma total

    Decide whether the infinite series ∑k=1∞8(−14)k−1\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}8\left(-\frac14\right)^{k-1}∑k=1∞​8(−41​)k−1 has a finite sum, and if it does, find it.

    1. Hint 1

      Read the first term and the ratio straight off the summand before deciding anything.

    2. Hint 2

      An infinite geometric series has a finite sum exactly when the ratio satisfies ∣r∣<1|r|<1∣r∣<1.

    3. Hint 3

      Carry the minus sign into S=a11−rS=\frac{a_1}{1-r}S=1−ra1​​, so the denominator grows rather than shrinks.

    Answer

    It does have a finite sum: S=325S=\frac{32}{5}S=532​, or 6.46.46.4.

    Full solution

    At k=1k=1k=1 the summand is 8(−14)08\left(-\tfrac14\right)^{0}8(−41​)0, which is 888, so the first term is a1=8a_1=8a1​=8.

    Each step raises the exponent by one, so every term is −14-\tfrac14−41​ times the one before it, giving

    r=−14.r=-\tfrac14.r=−41​.

    Because ∣−14∣=14<1\left|-\tfrac14\right|=\tfrac14<1​−41​​=41​<1, the terms shrink toward zero and the series does have a finite sum.

    Put the ratio into the infinite-sum formula with its sign attached:

    S=81−(−14)=854=325\begin{aligned} S &= \frac{8}{1-\left(-\tfrac14\right)} \\ &= \frac{8}{\tfrac54} \\ &= \frac{32}{5} \end{aligned}S​=1−(−41​)8​=45​8​=532​​

    As a decimal that is 6.46.46.4.

    The running totals 888, 666, 6.56.56.5, 6.3756.3756.375 swing above and below it by ever smaller amounts, as a negative ratio should.

    Answer

    It does have a finite sum: S=325S=\frac{32}{5}S=532​, or 6.46.46.4.

  3. Problem 3 The assembly charges

    An assembly has four layers. The material charge for the first layer is 7 dollars, and each later layer has three times the preceding material charge. Each layer also has a fixed handling charge of 2.50 dollars. Find the total charge for all four layers.

    1. Hint 1

      Separate material charges from handling charges.

    2. Hint 2

      The material charges form a four-term geometric series.

    3. Hint 3

      Add the handling charge once for each layer.

    Answer

    290 dollars.

    Full solution

    The material charges have first term 777, ratio 333, and count 444.

    Their sum is

    M=7(34−1)3−1.M=\frac{7(3^4-1)}{3-1}.M=3−17(34−1)​.

    Since 34=813^4=8134=81, this gives M=280M=280M=280.

    The handling total is

    4⋅2.50=10.4\cdot2.50=10.4⋅2.50=10.

    Therefore the complete charge is

    280+10=290.280+10=290.280+10=290.

    Directly, the material charges 7,21,63,1897,21,63,1897,21,63,189 total 280280280, and all four handling charges are included.

    Answer

    290 dollars.

    Key idea

    An added fixed charge must be totaled separately from charges that grow geometrically.

  4. Problem 4 The decimal prefix

    Write 0.214‾=0.2141414…0.2\overline{14}=0.2141414\ldots0.214=0.2141414… as a fraction in lowest terms.

    Full solution

    15+7495=106495.\frac15+\frac{7}{495}=\frac{106}{495}.51​+4957​=495106​.

    Answer

  5. Problem 5 The running display

    A display adds the positive entries 12,3,34,…12,3,\frac34,\ldots12,3,43​,…, each a quarter of the preceding entry. Find the least number of entries needed for its running total to be within 15\frac1551​ of the infinite total, meaning that the remaining difference is at most 15\frac1551​.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the infinite total from the first term and ratio.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the finite sum from the infinite total.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare the remaining differences at neighboring term counts.

    Answer

    444 entries.

    Full solution

    The infinite total is

    12/(1−1/4)=16.12/(1-1/4)=16.12/(1−1/4)=16.

    The finite total is

    Sn=16(1−4−n).S_n=16\left(1-4^{-n}\right).Sn​=16(1−4−n).

    Thus the remaining difference is 16⋅4−n16\cdot4^{-n}16⋅4−n.

    At three entries it is

    16⋅4−3=14,16\cdot4^{-3}=\frac14,16⋅4−3=41​,

    which is greater than 1/51/51/5.

    At four entries it is

    16⋅4−4=116,16\cdot4^{-4}=\frac{1}{16},16⋅4−4=161​,

    which is less than 1/51/51/5.

    The positive difference is divided by four at every step, so four is the least possible count.

    Directly, the first four entries total 25516\frac{255}{16}16255​, which falls 116\frac{1}{16}161​ short of 161616.

    Answer

    444 entries.

    Key idea

    The difference between a geometric infinite sum and its partial sum gives the exact amount still missing.

  6. Problem 6 The paired totals

    1. Hint 1

      Use the infinite total to express the first term in terms of the ratio.

    2. Hint 2

      The first two terms have sum a1(1+r)a_1(1+r)a1​(1+r).

    3. Hint 3

    Full solution

    a1=3(1−r).a_1=3(1-r).a1​=3(1−r).

    3(1−r)(1+r)=94.3(1-r)(1+r)=\frac94.3(1−r)(1+r)=49​.

    Dividing by 333 and using the difference of squares gives 1−r2=3/41-r^2=3/41−r2=3/4, so

    r2=14.r^2=\frac14.r2=41​.

    Thus r=1/2r=1/2r=1/2 or r=−1/2r=-1/2r=−1/2, both allowed.

    They give first terms 3/23/23/2 and 9/29/29/2, respectively.

    Their two-term sums are 3/2+3/4=9/43/2+3/4=9/43/2+3/4=9/4 and 9/2−9/4=9/49/2-9/4=9/49/2−9/4=9/4, and their infinite sums both equal 333.

    Answer

    (a1,r)=(32,12)(a_1,r)=(\frac32,\frac12)(a1​,r)=(23​,21​) or (92,−12)(\frac92,-\frac12)(29​,−21​).

    Key idea

    A partial total and an infinite total can leave a sign choice for a geometric ratio.

  7. Problem 7 The signed adjustments

    Six adjustments are 10,−5,52,…10,-5,\frac52,\ldots10,−5,25​,…, each −12-\frac12−21​ times the preceding adjustment. Find their net total and the total of their absolute values.

    1. Hint 1

  8. Problem 8 The middle block

  9. Problem 9 The ratio built from xxx

    For a real number xxx, an infinite series begins 1+x4+(x4)2+⋯1+\frac{x}{4}+\left(\frac{x}{4}\right)^{2}+\cdots1+4x​+(4x​)2+⋯, each term being x4\frac{x}{4}4x​ times the one before it. Find every value of xxx for which this series has a finite sum, and give that sum in terms of xxx.

    1. Hint 1

      An infinite geometric series settles on a finite total only for ratios of a certain size.

    2. Hint 2

      Here the ratio is x4\frac{x}{4}4x​, so that size condition turns into an inequality in xxx.

    3. Hint 3

      The first term is 111; put the ratio into S=a11−rS=\frac{a_1}{1-r}S=1−ra1​​, then clear the inner fraction.

    Answer

    ∣x∣<4|x|<4∣x∣<4, that is −4<x<4-4<x<4−4<x<4; the sum is S=44−xS=\frac{4}{4-x}S=4−x4​, equivalently 11−x/4\frac{1}{1-x/4}1−x/41​.

    Full solution

    The first term is 111 and the ratio is

    r=x4.r=\frac{x}{4}.r=4x​.

    An infinite geometric series has a finite sum exactly when ∣r∣<1\left|r\right|<1∣r∣<1, so the condition here is

    ∣x4∣<1.\left|\frac{x}{4}\right|<1.​4x​​<1.

    Multiplying that inequality by the positive number 444 leaves ∣x∣<4|x|<4∣x∣<4, which says the same as −4<x<4-4<x<4−4<x<4.

    For those values the infinite-sum formula applies with a1=1a_1=1a1​=1:

    S=11−x4.S=\frac{1}{1-\frac{x}{4}}.S=1−4x​1​.

    Multiplying the top and the bottom by 444 clears the fraction inside the denominator:

    S=44−x.S=\frac{4}{4-x}.S=4−x4​.

    The denominator 4−x4-x4−x is never zero on −4<x<4-4<x<4−4<x<4, so the sum is defined everywhere the series converges.

    Check one value.

    At x=2x=2x=2 the series is 1+12+14+⋯1+\tfrac12+\tfrac14+\cdots1+21​+41​+⋯, whose total is 222, and the formula gives

    44−2=2.\frac{4}{4-2}=2.4−24​=2.

    The two ends are excluded for good reason: at x=4x=4x=4 every term equals 111, and at x=−4x=-4x=−4 the terms flip between 111 and −1-1−1, so neither running total settles.

    Answer

    ∣x∣<4|x|<4∣x∣<4, that is −4<x<4-4<x<4−4<x<4; the sum is S=44−xS=\frac{4}{4-x}S=4−x4​, equivalently 11−x/4\frac{1}{1-x/4}1−x/41​.

  10. Problem 10 Lina's running totals

    A report adds the first nnn terms of a geometric sequence whose first term a1a_1a1​ is not zero. It gives total na1na_1na1​ for every positive integer nnn. Lina says the ratio must be 111, so the infinite series has no finite sum. Is she correct? Explain.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

    3. Hint 3

      Then describe what adding arbitrarily many equal nonzero terms does.

    Answer

    Yes; r=1r=1r=1, and the infinite series diverges.

    Full solution

    At n=2n=2n=2, the stated total gives

    a1+a1r=2a1.a_1+a_1r=2a_1.a1​+a1​r=2a1​.

    Since a1≠0a_1\ne0a1​=0, division by a1a_1a1​ gives 1+r=21+r=21+r=2, hence r=1r=1r=1.

    Every term is therefore a1a_1a1​.

    The partial sum na1na_1na1​ grows in magnitude without bound because a1a_1a1​ is nonzero, so it cannot approach a finite total.

    Answer

    Yes; r=1r=1r=1, and the infinite series diverges.

    Key idea

    The formula for a constant geometric sum also identifies the ratio and explains its divergent infinite total.
