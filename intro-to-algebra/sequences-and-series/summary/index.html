Sequences and Series: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Sequence
An ordered list of terms. Changing their order can change the sequence: reversing 2, 5, 8 gives 8, 5, 2.
Position and the subscript ana_nan​
ana_nan​ means the term in position nnn, not aaa multiplied by nnn. Positions in this chapter are positive integers starting at 111.
Series, partial sum SnS_nSn​
A series adds sequence terms. The partial sum SnS_nSn​ adds the first nnn terms; an infinite sum is defined by what the partial sums approach.
Arithmetic sequence, common difference ddd
Each term is the one before it plus a fixed ddd, which may be positive, negative, fractional, or zero (a constant sequence).
Geometric sequence, common ratio rrr
Each term is the preceding term times a fixed rrr. This chapter uses a1≠0a_1\ne0a1​=0 and r≠0r\ne0r=0, so dividing by the preceding term is always allowed.
Explicit form and recursive form
An explicit rule gives a term from its position. A recursive rule gives the next term from the preceding one and needs a starting value.
Summation (sigma) notation
In ∑k=1nak\sum_{k=1}^n a_k∑k=1n​ak​, kkk is the index, 111 and nnn are the lower and upper limits, and aka_kak​ is the summand. Changing only the index letter does not change the sum.
Converge, diverge
An infinite series converges when its partial sums SnS_nSn​ approach one fixed value as nnn grows, which becomes the infinite sum; otherwise it diverges and has no finite sum.
Telescoping sum (Advanced)
A sum rewritten as shifted differences. Opposite interior pieces cancel, leaving a fixed number of boundary values to combine.

  • Telescoping collapse (Advanced)

    ∑k=1n(bk−bk+1)=b1−bn+1∑k=1n(bk+1−bk)=bn+1−b1\begin{gathered} \sum_{k=1}^{n} (b_k - b_{k+1}) = b_1 - b_{n+1} \\ \sum_{k=1}^{n} (b_{k+1} - b_k) = b_{n+1} - b_1 \end{gathered}k=1∑n​(bk​−bk+1​)=b1​−bn+1​k=1∑n​(bk+1​−bk​)=bn+1​−b1​​
    Text description

    The four brackets of a telescoping sum are stacked in a staircase so that each subtracted term sits directly above the identical added term in the row below; every one of those interior pairs is struck through, leaving only the first term and the last subtracted term, whose difference is the whole sum.

    Use when These formulas use consecutive differences. A wider gap leaves more boundary pieces. For an infinite sum, combine all surviving pieces and ask whether that complete expression approaches a finite value; individual pieces need not approach zero.

Problem types, step by step

Find a chosen term of a sequence

  1. Read a1a_1a1​, then get ddd by subtracting consecutive terms or rrr by dividing them.
  2. Substitute into an=a1+(n−1)da_n = a_1 + (n-1)dan​=a1​+(n−1)d or an=a1r n−1a_n = a_1 r^{\,n-1}an​=a1​rn−1, writing the count as n−1n - 1n−1, and evaluate the product or power first.

e.g. 2,9,16,…2, 9, 16, \ldots2,9,16,… has d=7d = 7d=7, so a20=2+19×7=135a_{20} = 2 + 19 \times 7 = 135a20​=2+19×7=135.

Recover ddd or rrr, and a1a_1a1​, from two known terms

  1. Count the positions apart, k−mk - mk−m, and substitute into ak=am+(k−m)da_k = a_m + (k-m)dak​=am​+(k−m)d or ak=amr k−ma_k = a_m r^{\,k-m}ak​=am​rk−m.
  2. Solve: divide out the coefficient for ddd, or isolate the power and take the root for rrr.
  3. An even gap can allow both signs of a real ratio; use additional information to choose a sign when needed.
  4. Step back to a1a_1a1​ by subtracting ddd or dividing by rrr the right number of times.

e.g. a2=6a_2 = 6a2​=6 and a5=48a_5 = 48a5​=48 give 6r3=486r^3 = 486r3=48, so r=2r = 2r=2 and a1=3a_1 = 3a1​=3.

Decide whether a value is a term, and which one

  1. Set the term formula equal to the target. For a nonconstant arithmetic sequence solve the linear equation. For a geometric sequence isolate the power, then match powers or use logarithms if r>0r>0r>0, r≠1r\ne1r=1, and V/a1>0V/a_1>0V/a1​>0.
  2. Keep only positive integer positions. A calculator gives an approximate candidate; check nearby integer powers in the original formula. For d=0d=0d=0 or r=1r=1r=1, compare the target with the constant term directly.

e.g. For 5,8,11,…5, 8, 11, \ldots5,8,11,…: 5+3(n−1)=985 + 3(n-1) = 985+3(n−1)=98 gives n=32n = 32n=32, while 100100100 gives n−1=953n - 1 = \tfrac{95}{3}n−1=395​, so 100100100 never appears.

Total the first nnn terms of an arithmetic series

  1. Read a1a_1a1​, ddd, and how many terms the question wants.
  2. Knowing the last term, use Sn=n(a1+an)2S_n = \frac{n(a_1 + a_n)}{2}Sn​=2n(a1​+an​)​; knowing only a1a_1a1​ and ddd, use Sn=n2(2a1+(n−1)d)S_n = \frac{n}{2}\big(2a_1 + (n-1)d\big)Sn​=2n​(2a1​+(n−1)d).
  3. Simplify inside the bracket, then divide by 222.
  4. If the count is unknown instead, set the formula equal to the given total. Keep all positive integer roots: a decreasing sequence can give the same total at two different counts.

e.g. 3,7,11,…3, 7, 11, \ldots3,7,11,… with n=25n = 25n=25: S25=252(6+96)=1275S_{25} = \tfrac{25}{2}(6 + 96) = 1275S25​=225​(6+96)=1275.

Total a range of terms, the mmmth through the nnnth

  1. Compute the end terms ama_mam​ and ana_nan​, and count n−m+1n - m + 1n−m+1 terms, both ends included.
  2. Apply the series formula to that run with its own first term, last term, and count.
  3. Check with Sn−Sm−1S_n - S_{m-1}Sn​−Sm−1​, which strips everything before position mmm.

e.g. With a1=4a_1 = 4a1​=4, d=3d = 3d=3, terms 101010 to 303030 run 313131 to 919191 over 212121 terms: 21(31+91)2=1281\frac{21(31 + 91)}{2} = 1281221(31+91)​=1281.

Total a geometric series, finite or infinite

  1. Read a1a_1a1​ as the first term written, rrr by dividing consecutive terms.
  2. Finite: confirm r≠1r \neq 1r=1 (if r=1r = 1r=1 the total is n a1n\,a_1na1​), then substitute a1a_1a1​, rrr, and nnn, evaluating r nr^{\,n}rn first.
  3. Infinite with a1≠0a_1\ne0a1​=0: first test ∣r∣<1|r|<1∣r∣<1. If it passes, use S=a1/(1−r)S=a_1/(1-r)S=a1​/(1−r); otherwise there is no finite sum.

e.g. 8+4+2+⋯=81−12=168 + 4 + 2 + \cdots = \frac{8}{1 - \tfrac{1}{2}} = 168+4+2+⋯=1−21​8​=16, while the 646464-square chessboard totals 264−12^{64} - 1264−1.

Turn a repeating decimal into an exact fraction

  1. Identify the repeating block BBB, its length bbb in digits, and the number ppp of nonrepeating digits after the decimal point before it begins.
  2. The first repeating block contributes a1=B/10p+ba_1=B/10^{p+b}a1​=B/10p+b; each following block is 1/10b1/10^b1/10b of the previous one, so r=1/10br=1/10^br=1/10b. Use p=0p=0p=0 if the repetition starts immediately.
  3. Add the nonrepeating prefix to S=a1/(1−r)S=a_1/(1-r)S=a1​/(1−r) and reduce. Collapse a telescoping sum (Advanced)

  1. Rewrite each term as a shifted difference by splitting fractions or rationalizing.
  2. Recombine to check the split and any required constant factor.
  3. Write enough terms to identify every unmatched boundary piece; a wider gap can leave several at each end.
  4. For a gap of 111, keep b1−bn+1b_1-b_{n+1}b1​−bn+1​ or its negative, according to the direction. For an infinite sum, examine the complete boundary expression. It must approach a finite value, not necessarily zero.

e.g. ∑k=1991k+k+1=100−1=9\sum_{k=1}^{99} \frac{1}{\sqrt{k} + \sqrt{k+1}} = \sqrt{100} - \sqrt{1} = 9∑k=199​k​+k+1​1​=100​−1​=9.

Set up a word problem about a repeating pattern

  1. A fixed amount added gives an arithmetic pattern; a fixed multiplier gives a geometric pattern. A nonzero constant sequence is both.
  2. Name a1a_1a1​ as the value at position 111, and decide whether the question wants one term or a total.
  3. Apply the matching formula and answer what was actually asked, with units.

e.g. 121212 rows from 181818 seats with d=2d = 2d=2 end at a12=40a_{12} = 40a12​=40, so S12=12(18+40)2=348S_{12} = \frac{12(18 + 40)}{2} = 348S12​=212(18+40)​=348 seats.

Exam traps

  • Trap Confusing a term with a total.

    Fix In 2,5,8,112,5,8,112,5,8,11, the fourth term is a4=11a_4=11a4​=11, while the first four terms total S4=26S_4=26S4​=26.

  • Trap Taking nnn steps from the first term, or forgetting an inclusive endpoint.

    Fix Term nnn takes n−1n-1n−1 steps. Positions 101010 through 303030 contain 30−10+1=2130-10+1=2130−10+1=21 terms. In an Advanced gap-1 telescope, the final boundary index is n+1n+1n+1, not nnn.

  • Trap Reaching for n(a1+an)2\frac{n(a_1 + a_n)}{2}2n(a1​+an​)​ to total a geometric series.

    Fix The arithmetic rule relies on equal differences. A geometric list does not generally have those; for r≠1r\ne1r=1, use a1(1−rn)/(1−r)a_1(1-r^n)/(1-r)a1​(1−rn)/(1−r). The constant case r=1r=1r=1 is both families and totals na1na_1na1​.
