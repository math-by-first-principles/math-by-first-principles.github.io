    A 9 m long floor has a 2 m long end strip removed across the full width www. The width ranges from 4.3 to 4.7 m, inclusive. What is the least remaining area?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The original floor and removed strip have the same width. Subtract the strip length before multiplying. The least area uses the least width.

    A=9w−2w=7wAmin⁡=7(4.3)=30.1 m2.\begin{aligned}A&=9w-2w\\&=7w\\A_{\min}&=7(4.3)\\&=30.1\text{ m}^2.\end{aligned}AAmin​​=9w−2w=7w=7(4.3)=30.1 m2.​
  2. 2

    A cover model subtracts a rectangular vent from a fixed 22-square-meter top. The vent length is fixed at 2 m. Its width is reported as 1.8 m to the nearest tenth, with ties going to the larger report. Which describes the least end of the remaining area range?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    A wider vent leaves less cover. The vent width approaches 1.85 m from below.

    22−2(1.85)=18.3 m2.22-2(1.85)=18.3\text{ m}^2.22−2(1.85)=18.3 m2.

    The remaining area approaches this bound from above. It cannot equal the bound.

  3. 3

    A mat model has a fixed length of 4.5 m and width from 1.6 to 1.8 m, inclusive. A second method reports 8.6 square meters for the same mat. Which next step is supported?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The model’s largest area uses the widest allowed mat.

    4.5(1.8)=8.1 m2.4.5(1.8)=8.1\text{ m}^2.4.5(1.8)=8.1 m2.

    The separate observation is larger, so check how each estimate was made.

  4. 4

    A curb cut has a lower model area of 5.6 square meters and upper model area of 6.4 square meters, both attained. A revised model adds a fixed 0.35-square-meter side patch. What is the new model interval?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Add the same fixed area to both endpoints.

    5.6+0.35=5.95 m26.4+0.35=6.75 m2.\begin{gathered}5.6+0.35=5.95\text{ m}^2\\6.4+0.35=6.75\text{ m}^2.\end{gathered}5.6+0.35=5.95 m26.4+0.35=6.75 m2.​

    Both endpoints are still attained.

  5. 5

    An outer rectangle has fixed length 8 m. Its width is at least 4.05 m and less than 4.15 m. A rectangular recess of fixed length 3 m has width at least 0.65 m and less than 0.75 m. The widths vary independently and the recess always fits. What are the bounds on the remaining area in square meters?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    For the lower bound, take the smallest outer width and approach the largest recess width. For the upper bound, approach the largest outer width and take the smallest recess width.

    8(4.05)−3(0.75)=30.158(4.15)−3(0.65)=31.25.\begin{gathered}8(4.05)-3(0.75)=30.15\\8(4.15)-3(0.65)=31.25.\end{gathered}8(4.05)−3(0.75)=30.158(4.15)−3(0.65)=31.25.​

    Both limiting choices use an excluded width, so the area satisfies 30.15<A<31.2530.15<A<31.2530.15<A<31.25.

  6. 6

    A footbridge model uses a rectangle to predict the deck area. It closely matches a separate area survey. The builder wants to use the same model to locate holes along the deck's curved railing. What is supported by the survey?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Different boundary shapes can have nearly the same area. The survey checked area, so it gives no hole positions.

    A 1.7 m long, 65 cm wide, 30 cm tall block is modeled as a rectangular prism. Which computation gives its volume in cubic meters?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Convert the width and height to meters before multiplying three lengths.

    V=1.7(0.65)(0.30) m3.V=1.7(0.65)(0.30)\text{ m}^3.V=1.7(0.65)(0.30) m3.

    A bench seat model has fixed depth 38 cm. The length report of 1.2 m is rounded to the nearest tenth of a meter, with ties going to the larger report. Which describes model area in square meters?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The unrounded length satisfies 1.15≤L<1.251.15\le L<1.251.15≤L<1.25 meters. Convert the depth to 0.38 m and multiply both endpoints.

    0.38(1.15)=0.4370.38(1.25)=0.475.\begin{gathered}0.38(1.15)=0.437\\0.38(1.25)=0.475.\end{gathered}0.38(1.15)=0.4370.38(1.25)=0.475.​

    The area has the same inclusion and exclusion at the two endpoints.

  9. 9

    A team measures a path width with a damaged tape and rounds its reading to the nearest tenth. What does the interval from that rounding rule bound?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The rounding rule describes which unrounded readings could produce the report. Information about tape error would be needed to relate the interval to the true width.

    The revised interval overlaps the survey. The survey’s lower endpoint is below the revised lower bound.

  11. 11

    A greenhouse model treats its side wall as a rectangle plus a triangle. Both parts use the same base width. A second team measures the total wall area directly and falls within the predicted bounds. Which would give useful new evidence about the triangular part of the model?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    A separate measurement of the upper part tests a component whose error could have been hidden by an opposite error in the rectangle area.

  12. 12

    A technician reports that a rectangular intake has area 0.1749 square meters from two rounded length readings. A calculation with convenient nearby numbers gave about 0.18 square meters. What does the rough estimate support?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The estimate and the calculation use the same input information. Their agreement checks the size of the arithmetic result.
