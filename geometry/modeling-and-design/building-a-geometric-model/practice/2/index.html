    A canvas cover is modeled as a rectangle 2.2 m by 90 cm plus a nonoverlapping triangle. The triangle shares the full 2.2 m side, and its perpendicular height is 40 cm. What is the model area?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The two perpendicular dimensions are 0.90 m and 0.40 m after conversion. Add the rectangular and triangular areas.

    Arectangle=2.2(0.90)=1.98Atriangle=12(2.2)(0.40)=0.44A=1.98+0.44=2.42 m2.\begin{aligned}A_{\text{rectangle}}&=2.2(0.90)\\&=1.98\\A_{\text{triangle}}&=\tfrac12(2.2)(0.40)\\&=0.44\\A&=1.98+0.44\\&=2.42\text{ m}^2.\end{aligned}Arectangle​Atriangle​A​=2.2(0.90)=1.98=21​(2.2)(0.40)=0.44=1.98+0.44=2.42 m2.​

    Solution

    Two rectangular panels are disjoint. They share a common width www. Their fixed lengths are 4 m and 1.25 m. If www is at least 2.1 m and less than 2.3 m, which is the upper bound on their total area, and can it occur?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Factor the common width out of the combined area. Substitute the excluded upper width to find the limiting bound.

    A=(4+1.25)w=5.25w5.25(2.3)=12.075 m2.\begin{aligned}A&=(4+1.25)w\\&=5.25w\\5.25(2.3)&=12.075\text{ m}^2.\end{aligned}A5.25(2.3)​=(4+1.25)w=5.25w=12.075 m2.​

    The bound is excluded because the width cannot equal 2.3 m.

  12. 12

    A hall plan treats its measured length as lying in an interval and stipulates an exact width of 4 m for the model. What assumption allows you to multiply both length endpoints by 4 to find the area bounds?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The rectangle model makes the area four times the length. The same width applies at every allowed length, so the length order gives the area order.
