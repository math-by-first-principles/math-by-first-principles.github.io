  1. Problem 1 Check three sign proposals

    A notice board fits a rectangular sign at most 115115115 cm wide and 170170170 cm high. The club has at most 16,00016{,}00016,000 square centimeters of usable card. Treat the sign as a single rectangle and assume no card is lost in cutting.

    Plan A is 969696 cm wide and 155155155 cm high.

    Plan B is 110110110 cm wide and 149149149 cm high.

    Plan C is 120120120 cm wide and 126126126 cm high.

    Which plans are feasible? If the club wants the most display area among these plans, which should it choose?

    1. Hint 1

      Check each limit separately before ranking the plans.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the area of each plan and compare its width, height, and area with their respective caps.

    Answer

    Only A is feasible. Choose A for its 14,88014{,}88014,880 square centimeters of display area.

    Full solution

    Plan A fits both dimension caps.

    Its area is 96(155)96(155)96(155) square centimeters, or 14,88014{,}88014,880 square centimeters, within the card limit.

    Plan B fits the dimension caps, but its area is 110(149)110(149)110(149) square centimeters, or 16,39016{,}39016,390 square centimeters, above the card limit.

    Plan C uses 120(126)120(126)120(126) square centimeters of card, or 15,12015{,}12015,120 square centimeters, but it is wider than the space permits.

    A is the only feasible proposal, so it gives the most display area among these choices.

    Answer

    Only A is feasible. Choose A for its 14,88014{,}88014,880 square centimeters of display area.

    Key idea

    A plan must meet every cap before its objective value can earn it a place in the comparison.

  2. Problem 2 Make room for a gate

    A rectangular outdoor waiting area is 111111 m by 777 m. It needs a 222 m gate on one of the 111111 m sides and new fencing on the rest of the perimeter. Fencing costs 888 dollars per meter, and the gate costs 393939 dollars. Assume the only costs are the gate and the exact length of fence used.

    Find the total cost. If the budget is 320320320 dollars, say whether the design is feasible and give the difference between cost and budget.

    Text description of this figure

    An 11 meter by 7 meter rectangular enclosure. A 2 meter gap centered in the top 11 meter side holds the gate.

    1. Hint 1

      The gate and fencing cover different parts of the boundary.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the perimeter, then take out the length supplied by the gate before pricing the fence.

    Answer

    The total is 311311311 dollars. The design is feasible with 999 dollars left.

    Full solution

    The boundary length is 2(11+7)2(11+7)2(11+7) meters, or 363636 meters.

    The gate fills 222 m of that boundary, leaving 343434 m for fence.

    Pay for that fencing and the gate separately.

    34(8)+39=311.34(8)+39=311.34(8)+39=311.

    The total is within budget.

    The difference between 320320320 dollars and 311311311 dollars is 999 dollars.

    Answer

    The total is 311311311 dollars. The design is feasible with 999 dollars left.

    Key idea

    Charge each part of the perimeter at the rate that applies to it.

  3. Problem 3 Clearance between pots

    A shelf is 747474 cm long. Each flowerpot takes 111111 cm of shelf length. Neighboring pots must have a gap of at least 333 cm between them, but there is no required gap at either end of the shelf.

    Find the largest number of pots in one row. How much shelf length is left over when all gaps in that row are exactly 333 cm?

    Text description of this figure

    A sample row with three pots. Each pot uses 11 centimeters of shelf width. The gaps between neighboring pots are 3 centimeters. No gap is required at the ends.

    1. Hint 1

      Gaps occur between neighbors, so count pots and gaps separately.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the length used by several consecutive pot counts, with one fewer gap than pots.

    Answer

    At most 555 pots fit, with 777 cm left at minimum spacing.

    Full solution

    Five pots have four gaps between them.

    5(11)+4(3)=67.5(11)+4(3)=67.5(11)+4(3)=67.

    Six pots would have five gaps.

    6(11)+5(3)=81.6(11)+5(3)=81.6(11)+5(3)=81.

    Six pots exceed the shelf length even with minimum gaps.

    Adding more pots only increases the required length.

    Five fit and leave 74−6774-6774−67 cm, or 777 cm.

    Answer

    At most 555 pots fit, with 777 cm left at minimum spacing.

    Key idea

    Use the minimum gap required between neighbors when testing how many items can fit.

  4. Problem 4 Pay for whole sheets

  5. Problem 5 More storage with the same coating supply

    A craft studio can choose one of three closed display boxes. It can coat at most 550 cm2550\text{ cm}^2550 cm2 and wants the largest inside volume. The coating covers all six exterior faces.     SA=2(126+84+54).S_A=2(126+84+54).SA​=2(126+84+54).

    The area is 528528528 square centimeters.

    Multiplying 121212, 121212, and 555 gives 720720720 cubic centimeters.

  6. Problem 6 Challenge a partial table

    A music studio can install a rectangular acoustic mat. The side parallel to the window must be at most 444 m and the perpendicular side at most 888 m. Both lengths must be positive whole meters. Edging costs 666 dollars per meter on each parallel side and 222 dollars per meter on each perpendicular side. The budget is 565656 dollars. The objective is to maximize mat area.

    Tested layout A is 222 m parallel by 666 m perpendicular, with area 121212 square meters.

    Tested layout B is 333 m parallel by 444 m perpendicular, with area 121212 square meters.

    Tested layout C is 444 m parallel by 222 m perpendicular, with area 888 square meters.

    A studio member says the table proves a maximum of 121212 square meters. Give one feasible counterexample and explain what it proves.

    1. Hint 1

      A single untested feasible layout that improves the area is enough to refute the claim.

    2. Hint 2

      For one permitted parallel length, work out how much money remains to edge the two perpendicular sides.

    Answer

    One counterexample is 222 m parallel by 888 m perpendicular. It costs 565656 dollars and covers 161616 square meters, so 121212 square meters is not the maximum.

    Full solution

    Layout A fits both dimension caps and costs 2(2)(6)+2(6)(2)2(2)(6)+2(6)(2)2(2)(6)+2(6)(2) dollars, or 484848 dollars.

    It has 888 dollars of budget left.

    Keep A's parallel length and extend each perpendicular side by 222 m.

    The 444 extra meters of edging cost 888 dollars.

    The revised layout is 222 m by 888 m, within both dimension caps.

    Its area is 161616 square meters and its cost is 565656 dollars.

    That is one feasible design with area above the tested best.

    A 333 m by 555 m layout also works, with cost 565656 dollars and area 151515 square meters.

    Answer

    One counterexample is 222 m parallel by 888 m perpendicular. It costs 565656 dollars and covers 161616 square meters, so 121212 square meters is not the maximum.

    Key idea

    A counterexample can disprove an optimum without finding the true optimum.

  8. Problem 8 What the row count leaves open

    A shelf is 282828 cm wide and 212121 cm deep. Its rectangular trays are 888 cm by 555 cm, and they can touch without overlapping. An exhaustive check of parallel full-width rows, with one orientation per row, finds a maximum of 131313 trays.

    First verify that 131313 trays actually fit using two rows that are each 888 cm deep and one row that is 555 cm deep.

    The owner now permits any placement of the trays in a single layer without overlaps. Use the areas of the shelf and tray to give an upper bound for the number that fit.

    Find the true maximum under the new rule and justify it.

    1. Hint 1

      Separate an arrangement that fits from an upper bound based on area.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide the shelf area by the area of one tray. Ask whether the next whole number can fit by area alone.

    3. Hint 3

      Try splitting the shelf into two side-by-side regions that use different layouts.

    Answer

    The full-width row layout fits 13. The area bound is 14. An 8 cm column holds 4 trays, and three rows in the remaining width hold 4, 4, and 2, so the true maximum is 14.

    Full solution

    An 888 cm deep row fits five trays across, each 555 cm wide.

    A 555 cm deep row fits three trays across, each 888 cm wide.

    The two deep rows and one shallow row use 212121 cm of depth and fit 5+5+35+5+35+5+3 trays, or 131313.

    This arrangement is still allowed after the rule changes.

    The shelf area is 588588588 square centimeters and each tray covers 404040 square centimeters.

    Fifteen trays cover 600600600 square centimeters, so they cannot fit.

    Fourteen trays need 560560560 square centimeters.

    That passes the area test, which by itself cannot certify a placement.

    The true maximum is at least 131313 and at most 141414.

    Showing a placement of 141414 or ruling out all such placements would close the gap.

    One placement closes the gap.

    In a column 888 cm wide, four trays lie across the column and use 202020 cm of depth.

    In the remaining 202020 cm of width, two 888 cm deep rows hold four trays each.

    A 555 cm deep row holds two more.

    This side uses the full 212121 cm of depth.

    The placement fits 4+8+2=144+8+2=144+8+2=14 trays.

    The area bound now proves that 141414 is the maximum.

    Answer

    The full-width row layout fits 13. The area bound is 14. An 8 cm column holds 4 trays, and three rows in the remaining width hold 4, 4, and 2, so the true maximum is 14.

    Key idea

    An upper bound and an arrangement attaining it prove a maximum.

  9. Problem 9 A stencil inside a triangular banner

    A right triangular banner has a horizontal leg of 181818 dm and a vertical leg of 121212 dm. A rectangular stencil shares the banner's right-angle corner and has its sides along those two legs. The corner opposite the shared corner lies on the hypotenuse. Both stencil dimensions may be any positive real lengths satisfying these conditions.

    Find the stencil dimensions with the largest area. Prove that your area is greatest over all permitted widths.

    Text description of this figure

    The banner is a right triangle with an 18 decimeter horizontal leg and a 12 decimeter vertical leg. The rectangular stencil shares the lower-left right-angle corner. Its upper-right corner lies on the sloping edge. Its width w and height h are variable.

    1. Hint 1

      The sloping edge makes the allowed height fall as the width grows.

    2. Hint 2

      Use similarity of the triangle to the right of the stencil and the whole banner to express the height in terms of the width.

    3. Hint 3

      Once you have an area expression, look for a squared difference that gives its distance below an upper bound.

    Answer

    Width 999 dm and height 666 dm give the greatest area, 54 dm254\text{ dm}^254 dm2.

    Full solution

    Let www and hhh be the width and height in decimeters.

    To the right of the stencil is a right triangle with base 18−w18-w18−w and height hhh.

    Its acute angle at the banner's lower-right vertex is shared with the banner, so the two triangles are similar.

    h18−w=1218.\frac{h}{18-w}=\frac{12}{18}.18−wh​=1812​.

    The height is 23(18−w)\tfrac23(18-w)32​(18−w), so the area is

    A=23w(18−w).A=\tfrac23w(18-w).A=32​w(18−w).

    The feasible widths satisfy 0<w<180<w<180<w<18.

    Every such width gives a positive height.

    The two factors www and 18−w18-w18−w sum to 181818.

    Rewrite their product using w=9+(w−9)w=9+(w-9)w=9+(w−9) and 18−w=9−(w−9)18-w=9-(w-9)18−w=9−(w−9).

    w(18−w)=81−(w−9)2.w(18-w)=81-(w-9)^2.w(18−w)=81−(w−9)2.

    The square is nonnegative, so the product is at most 818181.

    Multiplying by two-thirds gives an area of at most 545454 square decimeters.

    At width 999 dm, the square vanishes.

    The height is 666 dm and the area reaches the bound.

    Width 999 dm belongs to the feasible interval, so this proves the maximum.

    Answer

    Width 999 dm and height 666 dm give the greatest area, 54 dm254\text{ dm}^254 dm2.

    Key idea

    An upper bound plus a feasible design that reaches it proves a maximum over the modeled dimensions.

  10. Problem 10 Move an order to a shallower shelf
