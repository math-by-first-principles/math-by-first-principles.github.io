The Exterior Angle Theorem

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Continue one side of a triangle past a corner. The new opening is outside the triangle, yet its size is controlled by the two corners far from it. Following the boundary creates three such openings, each measuring a change of direction. The same triangle sum will explain both connections.

Learning goals

  • Identify an exterior angle and its two remote interior angles from a side extension
  • Derive the exterior angle theorem using triangle sums and linear pairs
  • Solve angle equations while checking the resulting triangle measures
  • Compare an exterior angle with each remote interior angle using positivity
  • Justify the total of three consistently oriented exterior turns around a triangle

Which angles belong together?

Throughout this lesson a triangle has three noncollinear vertices, so each interior angle is greater than 0∘0^\circ0∘ and less than 180∘180^\circ180∘. All angles named here are non-reflex unless a turn is described explicitly.

Take triangle ABCABCABC and extend BCBCBC past CCC to a distinct point DDD. Thus B,C,DB,C,DB,C,D lie on one line in that order. An exterior angle is the angle between a triangle side and the continuation of an adjacent side beyond their common vertex. Here it is ∠ACD\angle ACD∠ACD.

The remote interior angles for a chosen exterior angle are the triangle’s two interior angles at the other vertices. For ∠ACD\angle ACD∠ACD they are ∠BAC\angle BAC∠BAC and ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC. The angle ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB shares vertex CCC with the exterior angle, so it is adjacent, not remote.

The rays CB→\overrightarrow{CB}CB and CD→\overrightarrow{CD}CD are opposite. With the common side CA→\overrightarrow{CA}CA, they make ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB and ∠ACD\angle ACD∠ACD a linear pair. A point placed somewhere outside a triangle does not create an exterior angle unless the required side extension is present.

Check your understanding

Triangle ABC has AB extended beyond B to E, with A, B, E in that order. Which pair contains the remote interior angles for exterior angle CBE?

Answer choices

The exterior angle has vertex B. Its remote interior angles are the triangle's interior angles at the other two vertices, A and C. The interior angle at B is adjacent to this exterior angle.

Why the two remote angles add to the exterior angle

The exterior opening equals the combined openings at the two remote corners. For instance, suppose the interior angles at AAA and BBB measure 37∘37^\circ37∘ and 64∘64^\circ64∘. The triangle sum gives the angle at CCC:

m∠ACB=180∘−37∘−64∘=79∘.m\angle ACB=180^\circ-37^\circ-64^\circ=79^\circ.m∠ACB=180∘−37∘−64∘=79∘.

The exterior angle completes a linear pair with that 79∘79^\circ79∘ angle:

m∠ACD=180∘−79∘=101∘.m\angle ACD=180^\circ-79^\circ=101^\circ.m∠ACD=180∘−79∘=101∘.

The remote angles also total 37∘+64∘=101∘37^\circ+64^\circ=101^\circ37∘+64∘=101∘. The triangle-sum equation and the linear-pair equation both have a total of 180∘180^\circ180∘. Removing the same interior measure at CCC leaves equal remainders.

The exterior angle theorem states that when one side of a triangle is extended beyond a vertex, the resulting non-reflex exterior angle has measure equal to the sum of the two remote interior angle measures. In the stated B,C,DB,C,DB,C,D order,

m∠ACD=m∠BAC+m∠ABC.m\angle ACD=m\angle BAC+m\angle ABC.m∠ACD=m∠BAC+m∠ABC.

The exterior angle is the sum of the two remote interior angles#

First run the argument with another triangle. If the remote angles measure 42∘42^\circ42∘ and 73∘73^\circ73∘, their sum is 115∘115^\circ115∘. The triangle sum makes the adjacent interior angle 65∘65^\circ65∘, and the linear pair makes the exterior angle 180∘−65∘=115∘180^\circ-65^\circ=115^\circ180∘−65∘=115∘.

Now let a,b,ca,b,ca,b,c be the interior angle measures at A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C, and let e=m∠ACDe=m\angle ACDe=m∠ACD. The triangle sum, proved in the previous lesson, gives

a+b+c=180∘.a+b+c=180^\circ.a+b+c=180∘.

The side extension gives opposite rays CBCBCB and CDCDCD, so the linear pair gives

c+e=180∘.c+e=180^\circ.c+e=180∘.

Both a+b+ca+b+ca+b+c and c+ec+ec+e equal 180∘180^\circ180∘. Set them equal and subtract the common measure ccc:

a+b+c=c+e,a+b=e.\begin{aligned} a+b+c&=c+e,\\ a+b&=e. \end{aligned}a+b+ca+b​=c+e,=e.​

The numerical choices played no part in the subtraction. The argument works for every triangle with this side extension.

∎

Worked example 1 Recover a remote angle and its neighbor

Problem. In triangle PQRPQRPQR, extend QRQRQR beyond RRR to SSS, with Q,R,SQ,R,SQ,R,S in that order. Given m∠PRS=128∘m\angle PRS=128^\circm∠PRS=128∘ and m∠QPR=57∘m\angle QPR=57^\circm∠QPR=57∘, find m∠PQRm\angle PQRm∠PQR and m∠PRQm\angle PRQm∠PRQ. Give a reason for each calculation.

Solution. The remote interior angles for ∠PRS\angle PRS∠PRS are at PPP and QQQ. The exterior angle theorem gives

128∘=57∘+m∠PQR,m∠PQR=71∘.\begin{aligned} 128^\circ&=57^\circ+m\angle PQR,\\ m\angle PQR&=71^\circ. \end{aligned}128∘m∠PQR​=57∘+m∠PQR,=71∘.​

At RRR, the adjacent interior angle and the exterior angle form a linear pair:

m∠PRQ=180∘−128∘=52∘.m\angle PRQ=180^\circ-128^\circ=52^\circ.m∠PRQ=180∘−128∘=52∘.

The interior measures check: 57∘+71∘+52∘=180∘57^\circ+71^\circ+52^\circ=180^\circ57∘+71∘+52∘=180∘.

Check your understanding

A triangle has interior measures a,b,ca,b,ca,b,c, and an exterior angle adjacent to ccc has measure eee. The triangle sum gives a+b+c=180∘a+b+c=180^\circa+b+c=180∘, and the linear pair gives c+e=180∘c+e=180^\circc+e=180∘. Which justified move derives e=a+be=a+be=a+b?

Answer choices

The expressions a+b+ca+b+ca+b+c and c+ec+ec+e equal the same total, so they are equal to each other.

a+b+c=c+ea+b+c=c+ea+b+c=c+e

Subtracting the common measure ccc from both sides gives a+b=ea+b=ea+b=e, or e=a+be=a+be=a+b. The triangle sum and linear pair supplied the two equations; equality and subtraction connect them.

Turn the geometry into an equation

Identify the adjacent and remote angles before doing algebra. The remote measures add to the exterior measure. The adjacent interior and exterior pair totals 180∘180^\circ180∘; adding the two remote measures to the exterior measure does not generally give that total.

Worked example 2 Solve and check the geometric values

Problem. Triangle ABCABCABC has BCBCBC extended past CCC to DDD, with B,C,DB,C,DB,C,D in that order. The measures in degrees are m∠BAC=4x+5m\angle BAC=4x+5m∠BAC=4x+5, m∠ABC=3x+6m\angle ABC=3x+6m∠ABC=3x+6, and m∠ACD=9x−11m\angle ACD=9x-11m∠ACD=9x−11. Find xxx, the exterior measure, and all three interior measures.

Solution. The angles at AAA and BBB are remote. The exterior angle theorem gives

(4x+5)+(3x+6)=9x−11,7x+11=9x−11,22=2x,x=11.\begin{aligned} (4x+5)+(3x+6)&=9x-11,\\ 7x+11&=9x-11,\\ 22&=2x,\\ x&=11. \end{aligned}(4x+5)+(3x+6)7x+1122x​=9x−11,=9x−11,=2x,=11.​

Combine like terms first. Subtracting 7x7x7x and adding 111111 to both sides then isolates 2x2x2x; division by 222 gives xxx.

Substitution gives m∠BAC=49∘m\angle BAC=49^\circm∠BAC=49∘, m∠ABC=39∘m\angle ABC=39^\circm∠ABC=39∘, and m∠ACD=88∘m\angle ACD=88^\circm∠ACD=88∘. The adjacent interior angle is

m∠ACB=180∘−88∘=92∘.m\angle ACB=180^\circ-88^\circ=92^\circ.m∠ACB=180∘−88∘=92∘.

All three interior measures are positive and below 180∘180^\circ180∘, and 49∘+39∘+92∘=180∘49^\circ+39^\circ+92^\circ=180^\circ49∘+39∘+92∘=180∘. The exterior check is 49∘+39∘=88∘49^\circ+39^\circ=88^\circ49∘+39∘=88∘.

In a triangle, two remote interior angles measure (4x+13)∘(4x+13)^\circ(4x+13)∘ and (x+24)∘(x+24)^\circ(x+24)∘, and the corresponding exterior angle measures (8x−2)∘(8x-2)^\circ(8x−2)∘. What is xxx?

(4x+13)+(x+24)=8x−2(4x+13)+(x+24)=8x-2(4x+13)+(x+24)=8x−2

Combining terms gives 5x+37=8x−25x+37=8x-25x+37=8x−2, so 39=3x39=3x39=3x and x=13x=13x=13. The measures are 65, 37, and 102 degrees. The adjacent interior angle is 78 degrees, so the triangle measures are valid.

Why an exterior angle exceeds either remote angle

Adding a positive angle makes a larger measure. For example, remote measures 31∘31^\circ31∘ and 55∘55^\circ55∘ give an exterior measure of 86∘86^\circ86∘. That is greater than 31∘31^\circ31∘ and greater than 55∘55^\circ55∘.

The exterior angle inequality#

Take another triangle whose remote interior angles measure 28∘28^\circ28∘ and 59∘59^\circ59∘. The exterior angle theorem gives

28∘+59∘=87∘.28^\circ+59^\circ=87^\circ.28∘+59∘=87∘.

Subtract each remote measure from the exterior measure:

87∘−28∘=59∘,87∘−59∘=28∘.\begin{aligned} 87^\circ-28^\circ&=59^\circ,\\ 87^\circ-59^\circ&=28^\circ. \end{aligned}87∘−28∘87∘−59∘​=59∘,=28∘.​

Both differences are positive, so 87∘>28∘87^\circ>28^\circ87∘>28∘ and 87∘>59∘87^\circ>59^\circ87∘>59∘.

In general, use the theorem e=a+be=a+be=a+b with a>0∘a>0^\circa>0∘ and b>0∘b>0^\circb>0∘, since the triangle’s vertices are noncollinear. Then e−a=b>0∘e-a=b>0^\circe−a=b>0∘, so e>ae>ae>a. Also e−b=a>0∘e-b=a>0^\circe−b=a>0∘, so e>be>be>b.

∎

This is the exterior angle inequality: a triangle’s exterior angle has greater measure than either of its remote interior angles. It compares each remote angle separately. It does not claim the exterior angle exceeds the adjacent interior angle. For the earlier remote measures 31∘31^\circ31∘ and 55∘55^\circ55∘, the adjacent angle is 180∘−86∘=94∘180^\circ-86^\circ=94^\circ180∘−86∘=94∘, which is larger than the exterior angle.

Check your understanding

An exterior angle has measure eee, and its remote interior angles have measures aaa and bbb. Which explanation establishes e>ae>ae>a for every triangle?

Answer choices

The exterior angle theorem gives e=a+be=a+be=a+b. Because the other remote interior angle has positive measure, adding it makes eee greater than aaa. A remote interior angle is not the adjacent angle in the linear pair.

Three corners, one full turn

At each corner of a triangular path, continue the incoming side straight ahead. The non-reflex angle from that direction to the outgoing side is an exterior turn. Traverse the three sides in order, turning the same way each time, either all left or all right.

For a triangle with interior measures 46∘46^\circ46∘, 63∘63^\circ63∘, and 71∘71^\circ71∘, the exterior turns are their supplements:

180∘−46∘=134∘,180∘−63∘=117∘,180∘−71∘=109∘.\begin{aligned} 180^\circ-46^\circ&=134^\circ,\\ 180^\circ-63^\circ&=117^\circ,\\ 180^\circ-71^\circ&=109^\circ. \end{aligned}180∘−46∘180∘−63∘180∘−71∘​=134∘,=117∘,=109∘.​

Their total is 134∘+117∘+109∘=360∘134^\circ+117^\circ+109^\circ=360^\circ134∘+117∘+109∘=360∘. The completed path also returns you to your starting direction after one full turn.

Three consistently oriented exterior turns total a full turn#

Take another triangle with interior measures 24∘24^\circ24∘, 73.5∘73.5^\circ73.5∘, and 82.5∘82.5^\circ82.5∘ at A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C, respectively. Their sum is 180∘180^\circ180∘. Each exterior turn forms a linear pair with its adjacent interior angle, so the three turns measure

180∘−24∘=156∘,180∘−73.5∘=106.5∘,180∘−82.5∘=97.5∘.\begin{aligned} 180^\circ-24^\circ&=156^\circ,\\ 180^\circ-73.5^\circ&=106.5^\circ,\\ 180^\circ-82.5^\circ&=97.5^\circ. \end{aligned}180∘−24∘180∘−73.5∘180∘−82.5∘​=156∘,=106.5∘,=97.5∘.​

Adding the turns gives

156∘+106.5∘=262.5∘,262.5∘+97.5∘=360∘.\begin{aligned} 156^\circ+106.5^\circ&=262.5^\circ,\\ 262.5^\circ+97.5^\circ&=360^\circ. \end{aligned}156∘+106.5∘262.5∘+97.5∘​=262.5∘,=360∘.​

This calculation starts with three measures of 180∘180^\circ180∘ and removes the interior-angle total of 180∘180^\circ180∘:

540∘−180∘=360∘.540^\circ-180^\circ=360^\circ.540∘−180∘=360∘.

For arbitrary interior measures a,b,ca,b,ca,b,c, let the exterior turns be eA,eB,eCe_A,e_B,e_CeA​,eB​,eC​. At each vertex the continuation and the interior angle form a linear pair, so eA=180∘−ae_A=180^\circ-aeA​=180∘−a, eB=180∘−be_B=180^\circ-beB​=180∘−b, and eC=180∘−ce_C=180^\circ-ceC​=180∘−c. Adding gives

eA+eB+eC=540∘−(a+b+c),=540∘−180∘,=360∘.\begin{aligned} e_A+e_B+e_C&=540^\circ-(a+b+c),\\ &=540^\circ-180^\circ,\\ &=360^\circ. \end{aligned}eA​+eB​+eC​​=540∘−(a+b+c),=540∘−180∘,=360∘.​

The linear-pair relationships and the triangle sum give this total, independent of the particular interior measures. The three positive exterior measures describe the turns along the path when their directions are consistent.

∎

You can extend the other side at a vertex and obtain a second exterior angle with the same measure. But swapping just that angle into a turning record reverses its direction. Its positive measure still supplements the interior angle; it no longer describes the chosen traversal’s turn. A list of three measures and a record of three directed turns are different pieces of information.

Worked example 3 Complete a triangular turning record

Problem. A path follows the three sides of a triangle once in order, turning right at every corner. Two exterior turns measure 112∘112^\circ112∘ and 137∘137^\circ137∘. Find the third turn and the interior angle at that third corner. Explain why the sum rule applies.

Solution. The path takes one non-reflex exterior turn at each vertex, all in the same direction. Their measures therefore total 360∘360^\circ360∘:

e=360∘−112∘−137∘=111∘.e=360^\circ-112^\circ-137^\circ=111^\circ.e=360∘−112∘−137∘=111∘.

The third corner’s interior angle and this exterior turn form a linear pair, so the interior measure is 180∘−111∘=69∘180^\circ-111^\circ=69^\circ180∘−111∘=69∘.

The other interior measures would be 68∘68^\circ68∘ and 43∘43^\circ43∘. Together with 69∘69^\circ69∘ they total 180∘180^\circ180∘, confirming a valid triangle. The nearby turning figure shows the left-turn version; reversing the entire traversal gives three right turns with the same measures.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Using the adjacent angle as a remote angle. Name the exterior vertex first; the two remote angles are at the other vertices.
  • Adding an exterior angle to the remote sum to get 180∘180^\circ180∘. The exterior measure equals the remote sum. Only its adjacent interior angle forms the linear pair.
  • Comparing with the wrong interior angle. The exterior angle inequality concerns either remote angle, not the adjacent interior angle.
  • Mixing turn directions. Three supplements still total 360∘360^\circ360∘ numerically, but a turning path requires the exterior choices to follow one traversal consistently.

Key takeaways

  • Exterior angle: form it by continuing a triangle side beyond a vertex; its remote interior angles are at the other two vertices.
  • Exterior angle theorem: its measure equals the sum of the two remote interior angle measures.
  • Exterior angle inequality: each remote interior angle is smaller, since the other remote measure is positive.
  • Triangle turning: one non-reflex exterior turn at each vertex totals 360∘360^\circ360∘ when the turns follow the boundary in one consistent direction.
  • Check algebraic results against positivity, the triangle sum, and the stated side extension.

A bit of history (optional)

Euclid’s Elements presents the exterior-angle inequality earlier than the exact sum rule. Book I, Proposition 16 compares the exterior angle with each opposite interior angle. Proposition 32 later relates the exterior angle to both opposite interior angles together and also establishes the triangle’s interior-angle sum. The order shows that a comparison can be known before an exact measure relationship is available.

Read Proposition 16 and Proposition 32 in David Joyce’s presentation of the Elements.
