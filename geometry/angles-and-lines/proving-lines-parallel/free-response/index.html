    Distinct coplanar lines ℓ,m\ell,mℓ,m are cut by transversal ttt at distinct points. The diagram numbers the upper sectors northwest, northeast, southeast, southwest as 1, 2, 3, 4 and the lower sectors in the same order as 5, 6, 7, 8.

    Case A. m∠2=m∠6=35∘m\angle2=m\angle6=35^\circm∠2=m∠6=35∘.

    Case B. m∠4=75∘m\angle4=75^\circm∠4=75∘ and m∠5=105∘m\angle5=105^\circm∠5=105∘.

    For each independent case, decide whether ℓ∥m\ell\parallel mℓ∥m follows and name the converse that justifies your decision.

    Text description of this figure

    The transversal rises from lower left to upper right. At the upper crossing, sectors 1, 2, 3, 4 occupy northwest, northeast, southeast, southwest positions. The lower crossing has 5, 6, 7, 8 in the same order. No parallel markings or derived measures are shown.

    1. Hint 1

      Locate each pair relative to the two lines and transversal.

    2. Hint 2

      A corresponding pair needs equal measures; a same-side interior pair needs a total of 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Answer

    Case A: ℓ∥m\ell\parallel mℓ∥m, corresponding-angle converse. Case B: ℓ∥m\ell\parallel mℓ∥m, same-side interior converse.

    Full solution

    Case A.

    Angles 2 and 6 are corresponding.

    Their stated equal measures make them congruent, so the corresponding-angle converse gives ℓ∥m\ell\parallel mℓ∥m.

    Case B.

    Angles 4 and 5 are same-side interior angles.

    Their measures add to 75∘+105∘=180∘75^\circ+105^\circ=180^\circ75∘+105∘=180∘, so the same-side interior converse gives ℓ∥m\ell\parallel mℓ∥m.

    Answer

    Case A: ℓ∥m\ell\parallel mℓ∥m, corresponding-angle converse. Case B: ℓ∥m\ell\parallel mℓ∥m, same-side interior converse.

    Key idea

    A parallelism test needs both the right positions and the required measure relationship.

  3. Problem 3 Supplement an exterior pair

    Distinct coplanar lines ℓ,m\ell,mℓ,m are cut by transversal ttt at distinct points. The diagram numbers the upper sectors northwest, northeast, southeast, southwest as 1, 2, 3, 4 and the lower sectors in the same order as 5, 6, 7, 8.

  4. Problem 4 The second crossing

    Distinct coplanar lines aaa and bbb are parallel. Transversal ttt is perpendicular to aaa. At the crossing of ttt and bbb, one angle measures (11w+2)∘(11w+2)^\circ(11w+2)∘. Find www and state the relationship between ttt and bbb, with a reason.

    1. Hint 1

      Relate the crossing at bbb to the known right angle at aaa.

    2. Hint 2

      The perpendicular transversal theorem supplies the angle measure needed for the equation.

    Answer

    w=8w=8w=8 and t⊥bt\perp bt⊥b, by the perpendicular transversal theorem.

    Full solution

    Because a∥ba\parallel ba∥b and t⊥at\perp at⊥a in one plane, the perpendicular transversal theorem gives t⊥bt\perp bt⊥b.

    The stated angle is therefore right.

    11w+2=9011w+2=9011w+2=90

    Subtract 2 to get 11w=8811w=8811w=88, then divide by 11 to obtain w=8w=8w=8.

    Substitution checks

    11(8)+2=90.11(8)+2=90.11(8)+2=90.

    Answer

    w=8w=8w=8 and t⊥bt\perp bt⊥b, by the perpendicular transversal theorem.

    Key idea

    A perpendicular transversal to one of two parallel lines is perpendicular to both.

  6. Problem 6 Repair a parallelism claim

    In one plane, distinct lines ℓ\ellℓ and ttt cross at PPP. Angles 2 and 4 are vertical angles there, each measuring 39∘39^\circ39∘. A different line mmm crosses ttt at another point.

    Inez writes, “The congruent angles show that ℓ∥m\ell\parallel mℓ∥m.” Explain why the information does not justify that conclusion. Sketch one possible choice of mmm that preserves the stated angles and is not parallel to ℓ\ellℓ.

    1. Hint 1

      Ask which intersections the given angles describe.

    2. Hint 2

      Could the direction of mmm change while the crossing at PPP stays fixed?

    Answer

    The conclusion is unjustified. One valid sketch chooses SSS on ℓ\ellℓ and RRR on ttt, both different from PPP, and draws m=RS↔m=\overleftrightarrow{RS}m=RS.

    Full solution

    The given congruent angles are vertical angles at PPP.

    The vertical-angle theorem supplies their equality without using mmm, so the equality does not restrict the direction of mmm.

    Choose SSS on ℓ\ellℓ different from PPP and RRR on ttt different from PPP.

    Draw

    m=RS↔.m=\overleftrightarrow{RS}.m=RS.

    This line crosses ttt at RRR and meets ℓ\ellℓ at SSS, so it is not parallel to ℓ\ellℓ.

    The crossing at PPP and both 39∘39^\circ39∘ vertical-angle givens remain unchanged.

    Answer

    The conclusion is unjustified. One valid sketch chooses SSS on ℓ\ellℓ and RRR on ttt, both different from PPP, and draws m=RS↔m=\overleftrightarrow{RS}m=RS.

  7. Problem 7 Perform and annotate the construction

  8. Problem 8 Use uniqueness at an assumed crossing

    Three pairwise distinct coplanar lines satisfy a∥ca\parallel ca∥c and b∥cb\parallel cb∥c. An indirect proof temporarily assumes that aaa and bbb meet at RRR.

    Explain why RRR is outside ccc, identify the contradiction, and classify aaa and bbb.

    1. Hint 1

      A point on aaa cannot also lie on ccc under the given parallelism.

    2. Hint 2

      Count the claimed parallels to ccc through the assumed point RRR.

    Answer

    RRR would be outside ccc; two distinct parallels to ccc through RRR contradict uniqueness. Therefore a∥ba\parallel ba∥b.

    Full solution

    Because a∥ca\parallel ca∥c, no point of aaa lies on ccc.

    The assumed common point RRR lies on aaa, so it is outside ccc.

    Both aaa and bbb would pass through RRR and be parallel to ccc.

    They are given distinct, contradicting the accepted uniqueness of a parallel through an external point.

    Reject the assumed crossing.

    The distinct lines aaa and bbb lie in one plane and do not meet, so they are parallel.

    Answer

    RRR would be outside ccc; two distinct parallels to ccc through RRR contradict uniqueness. Therefore a∥ba\parallel ba∥b.

    Key idea

    An indirect proof must name the exact accepted fact contradicted by its temporary assumption.

  10. Problem 10 Choose and complete sufficient givens
