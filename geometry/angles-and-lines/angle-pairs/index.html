Angle Pairs

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Two lines cross, making four angles. Measuring all four would work, but one measurement is enough to determine the others. The useful information is how the rays fit together. We will turn that information into reasons, so that an equation follows from the geometry rather than from a guess about the drawing.

Learning goals

  • Classify angle pairs using their positions and the sums of their measures
  • Justify linear-pair sums and prove that vertical angles are congruent
  • Explain why complements or supplements of congruent angles are congruent
  • Solve angle equations using justified relationships and complete turns around a point

Position and measure describe different things

Two angles can touch without adding to a special total. Two other angles can add to a special total while sitting far apart. Keep these two kinds of information separate.

Adjacent angles share a vertex and a side, and their interiors do not overlap. For example, if a ray inside an 85∘85^\circ85∘ opening splits it into 32∘32^\circ32∘ and 53∘53^\circ53∘ parts, those parts are adjacent. Angle addition gives 32∘+53∘=85∘32^\circ+53^\circ=85^\circ32∘+53∘=85∘. Sharing just a vertex is not enough, and one angle lying inside another is not adjacency.

Two angles are complementary when their measures add to 90∘90^\circ90∘. Angles measuring 37∘37^\circ37∘ and 53∘53^\circ53∘ are complementary because 37∘+53∘=90∘37^\circ+53^\circ=90^\circ37∘+53∘=90∘. The definition concerns the sum; they do not have to be adjacent.

Two angles are supplementary when their measures add to 180∘180^\circ180∘. For example, 62∘62^\circ62∘ and 118∘118^\circ118∘ are supplementary. Their positions do not enter that test.

Unless a reflex opening is specified, named angles here are non-reflex. When we split an angle into parts, the parts have positive measures and nonoverlapping interiors. Write m∠AOBm\angle AOBm∠AOB for the measure and ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB for the angle itself. Angles are congruent when their measures are equal.

Check your understanding

Two angles have different vertices and measures 24∘24^\circ24∘ and 66∘66^\circ66∘. Which description must hold?

Answer choices

The measures add to 24∘+66∘=90∘24^\circ+66^\circ=90^\circ24∘+66∘=90∘, so the angles are complementary. Different vertices rule out adjacency and a linear pair; the sum is not 180∘180^\circ180∘.

A linear pair earns a straight-angle sum

When adjacent angles have opposite noncommon rays, they fill a straight angle. Imagine a ray standing inside a straight 180∘180^\circ180∘ opening. If one part measures 46∘46^\circ46∘, the other measures 180∘−46∘=134∘180^\circ-46^\circ=134^\circ180∘−46∘=134∘. Their total comes from the straight opening, not from their sizes in the sketch.

A linear pair is a pair of adjacent angles whose noncommon sides are opposite rays. Every linear pair is supplementary. Angle addition proves the claim: the two parts exactly fill a straight angle, whose measure is 180∘180^\circ180∘.

The reverse statement without a position condition is false. The separate 62∘62^\circ62∘ and 118∘118^\circ118∘ angles above are supplementary but are not a linear pair. If supplementary angles are also adjacent, however, their combined opening is 180∘180^\circ180∘, so their outer rays are opposite.

Worked example 1 Use the rays to justify a subtraction

Problem. Points A,O,CA,O,CA,O,C lie on one line with OOO between AAA and CCC. Ray OB→\overrightarrow{OB}OB is not on that line. Given m∠AOB=129∘m\angle AOB=129^\circm∠AOB=129∘, find m∠BOCm\angle BOCm∠BOC and explain the relationship used.

Solution. The rays OA→\overrightarrow{OA}OA and OC→\overrightarrow{OC}OC are opposite because OOO is between AAA and CCC. The angles share ray OB→\overrightarrow{OB}OB and have no overlapping interiors, so they form a linear pair. Their measures add to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

m∠BOC=180∘−129∘=51∘.m\angle BOC=180^\circ-129^\circ=51^\circ.m∠BOC=180∘−129∘=51∘.

The check is 129∘+51∘=180∘129^\circ+51^\circ=180^\circ129∘+51∘=180∘. The betweenness and shared-ray information justify that sum even if the sketch is inaccurate.

Check your understanding

Which information guarantees that two angles form a linear pair?

Answer choices

A linear pair requires both adjacency and opposite noncommon rays. A sum of 180∘180^\circ180∘ alone gives supplementary angles, which can be in separate places.

Why vertical angles are congruent

Opposite angles at the crossing of two distinct lines have the same measure. Here is a numerical reason before the general proof. Suppose one angle at a crossing is 71∘71^\circ71∘. A neighboring angle forms a linear pair with it, so it measures 109∘109^\circ109∘. The angle opposite the first also forms a linear pair with that same 109∘109^\circ109∘ angle. It therefore measures 180∘−109∘=71∘180^\circ-109^\circ=71^\circ180∘−109∘=71∘.

Vertical angles are the opposite angles formed by two distinct intersecting lines. Their sides come in pairs of opposite rays. The word refers to a shared vertex; the lines need not point up and down.

Vertical angles are congruent#

Let a,b,c,da,b,c,da,b,c,d be the measures of the sectors labeled in the figure. The line through A,O,CA,O,CA,O,C makes aaa and bbb a linear pair, so a+b=180∘a+b=180^\circa+b=180∘. The line through B,O,DB,O,DB,O,D makes bbb and ccc a linear pair, so b+c=180∘b+c=180^\circb+c=180∘.

Both sums equal 180∘180^\circ180∘, so a+b=b+ca+b=b+ca+b=b+c. Subtract the shared measure bbb from both sides to obtain a=ca=ca=c. Thus ∠AOB≅∠COD\angle AOB\cong\angle COD∠AOB≅∠COD.

For the other pair, b+c=180∘b+c=180^\circb+c=180∘ and c+d=180∘c+d=180^\circc+d=180∘. Equating the sums and subtracting ccc gives b=db=db=d. Thus ∠BOC≅∠DOA\angle BOC\cong\angle DOA∠BOC≅∠DOA.

∎

The particular 71∘71^\circ71∘ measurement was not used in the proof. Each opposite angle is what remains after removing the same neighboring angle from 180∘180^\circ180∘.

Worked example 2 Translate a position into an equation

Problem. Two distinct lines intersect at PPP. One pair of vertical angles has measures (5x+7)∘(5x+7)^\circ(5x+7)∘ and (8x−35)∘(8x-35)^\circ(8x−35)∘. Find xxx, the measure of each of these angles, and the measure of each remaining angle at PPP.

Solution. Vertical angles are congruent, so their measures are equal.

5x+7=8x−35,42=3x,x=14.\begin{aligned} 5x+7&=8x-35,\\ 42&=3x,\\ x&=14. \end{aligned}5x+742x​=8x−35,=3x,=14.​

Subtracting 5x5x5x and adding 353535 gives the second line; dividing by 333 gives the third. Substitution gives 5(14)+7=775(14)+7=775(14)+7=77 and 8(14)−35=778(14)-35=778(14)−35=77. Each angle in the named vertical pair is 77∘77^\circ77∘.

Each remaining angle forms a linear pair with a 77∘77^\circ77∘ angle, so each measures 180∘−77∘=103∘180^\circ-77^\circ=103^\circ180∘−77∘=103∘. Opposite angles agree, and each neighboring pair sums to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

Check your understanding

In the vertical-angle proof, a+b=180∘a+b=180^\circa+b=180∘ and b+c=180∘b+c=180^\circb+c=180∘. Which step proves a=ca=ca=c?

Answer choices

Both sums equal the same number, so a+b=b+ca+b=b+ca+b=b+c. Removing the same measure bbb from both sides leaves a=ca=ca=c. The three sectors do not together fill a straight angle.

Equal angles leave equal complements and supplements

Equal amounts removed from equal totals leave equal remainders. For example, two angles each measuring 34∘34^\circ34∘ have complements measuring 90∘−34∘=56∘90^\circ-34^\circ=56^\circ90∘−34∘=56∘. Their supplements each measure 180∘−34∘=146∘180^\circ-34^\circ=146^\circ180∘−34∘=146∘. The partners are congruent because both calculations use the same total and remove the same amount.

Congruent complements and supplements#

Suppose angles UUU and VVV are congruent, so m∠U=m∠Vm\angle U=m\angle Vm∠U=m∠V. If RRR complements UUU and SSS complements VVV, then m∠R=90∘−m∠Um\angle R=90^\circ-m\angle Um∠R=90∘−m∠U and m∠S=90∘−m∠Vm\angle S=90^\circ-m\angle Vm∠S=90∘−m∠V. Substitution makes the two right sides equal, so ∠R≅∠S\angle R\cong\angle S∠R≅∠S.

If RRR and SSS instead supplement UUU and VVV, replace 90∘90^\circ90∘ in both equations with 180∘180^\circ180∘. The same substitution proves ∠R≅∠S\angle R\cong\angle S∠R≅∠S. This includes two complements, or two supplements, of the very same angle.

∎

Use the same kind of partner on both sides. A complement and a supplement of one angle differ by 90∘90^\circ90∘, so this rule does not make them congruent.

Check your understanding

Angles UUU and VVV are congruent acute angles. Angle RRR complements UUU, and angle SSS supplements VVV. Which statement is justified?

Answer choices

Let each given acute angle measure uuu. Then m∠R=90∘−um\angle R=90^\circ-um∠R=90∘−u and m∠S=180∘−um\angle S=180^\circ-um∠S=180∘−u. Subtracting cancels uuu and leaves 90∘90^\circ90∘. Different partner totals do not give congruent partners.

Account for a complete turn

Consecutive sectors that fill one turn around a point have measures adding to 360∘360^\circ360∘. For example, sectors measuring 88∘88^\circ88∘, 137∘137^\circ137∘, and 135∘135^\circ135∘ fill a turn because 88+137+135=36088+137+135=36088+137+135=360. This is the angle-addition foundation from lesson 1.3 applied to a full turn. Check that every sector is counted once, with no gap or overlap.

Worked example 4 Keep track of every sector

Problem. Four distinct rays from OOO occur in the cyclic order OA,OB,OC,ODOA,OB,OC,ODOA,OB,OC,OD. The consecutive sectors AOBAOBAOB, BOCBOCBOC, CODCODCOD, and DOADOADOA fill one complete turn and measure 82∘82^\circ82∘, 96∘96^\circ96∘, (2x)∘(2x)^\circ(2x)∘, and (x+20)∘(x+20)^\circ(x+20)∘, respectively. Three consecutive nonoverlapping sectors fill one turn around OOO. Two measure 113∘113^\circ113∘ and 149∘149^\circ149∘. What is the third measure?

Answer choices

The total is a full turn, so the missing sector measures 360∘−113∘−149∘=98∘360^\circ-113^\circ-149^\circ=98^\circ360∘−113∘−149∘=98∘. The two given sectors already total more than a straight angle, so 180∘180^\circ180∘ is not the appropriate whole.

The working order is to identify the geometric relationship, write its equation, solve, and then check the measures against the stated configuration. Algebra can produce a number even when an incorrect geometric equation was used.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Supplementary versus linear pair. A sum of 180∘180^\circ180∘ does not supply adjacency or opposite rays.
  • Adjacent versus vertical. Adjacent angles share a side; vertical angles are opposite sectors at two distinct intersecting lines.
  • Congruent versus supplementary. Vertical angles have equal measures. Neighboring angles at a crossing have measures adding to 180∘180^\circ180∘.
  • A missing position condition. Use stated collinearity, betweenness, and ray order rather than the apparent shape of a sketch.
  • An incomplete turn. The 360∘360^\circ360∘ sum requires sectors that fill the complete turn exactly once.

Key takeaways

  • Adjacent angles share a vertex and side with no overlapping interiors.
  • Complementary and supplementary angles have measure sums of 90∘90^\circ90∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘, respectively.
  • Linear pairs are supplementary because their noncommon rays are opposite; vertical angles are congruent because they share a supplementary neighbor.
  • Congruent partners have congruent complements and congruent supplements, with the same type of partner used on each side.
  • Justify the relationship before solving an equation, and count a complete turn only when every sector is included once.

A bit of history (optional)

In Euclid’s Elements, the crossing-lines result appears as Proposition 15 of Book I. Its proof borrows a result just two propositions earlier: a ray meeting a straight line makes two angles whose sum is two right angles. Euclid then removes the same angle from two equal sums, leaving the opposite angles equal. A small geometric fact becomes the next fact’s tool.

The translated proposition and accompanying diagram preserve that chain of reasons. Euclid described the total as two right angles rather than writing it as 180∘180^\circ180∘.
