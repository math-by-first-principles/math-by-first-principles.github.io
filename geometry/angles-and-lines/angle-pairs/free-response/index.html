  1. Problem 1 The shared side

    Ray OBOBOB lies inside the non-reflex angle AOCAOCAOC. The consecutive angles AOBAOBAOB and BOCBOCBOC measure 28∘28^\circ28∘ and 62∘62^\circ62∘. Describe the pair as adjacent or nonadjacent, and as complementary, supplementary, or neither. Find and classify ∠AOC\angle AOC∠AOC.

    Text description of this figure

    Rays OA, OB, and OC have common vertex O. Ray OB is inside angle AOC. The two consecutive sectors are labeled 28 degrees and 62 degrees.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the shared ray and the stated interior position to test adjacency.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the sum of the two given measures with the special angle totals.

    Answer

    The pair is adjacent and complementary; m∠AOC=90∘m\angle AOC=90^\circm∠AOC=90∘, so ∠AOC\angle AOC∠AOC is right.

    Full solution

    The angles share vertex O and side OB.

    Since OB is inside the selected opening, their interiors do not overlap, so they are adjacent.

    Angle addition gives m∠AOC=28∘+62∘m\angle AOC=28^\circ+62^\circm∠AOC=28∘+62∘, hence

    m∠AOC=90∘.m\angle AOC=90^\circ.m∠AOC=90∘.

    The pair is complementary because its measure sum is 90∘90^\circ90∘, and the whole angle is right.

    Answer

    The pair is adjacent and complementary; m∠AOC=90∘m\angle AOC=90^\circm∠AOC=90∘, so ∠AOC\angle AOC∠AOC is right.

    Key idea

    Adjacency concerns the rays' positions; complementarity concerns the sum of the measures.

  3. Problem 3 All four sectors

    Two distinct lines cross at OOO. The northwest (NW) sector measures 97∘97^\circ97∘. Find the other three sector measures, listing them clockwise from NW as northeast (NE), southeast (SE), and southwest (SW). Give a reason for each measure.

    Text description of this figure

    1. Hint 1

      Separate neighboring sectors from opposite sectors.

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    NE 83∘83^\circ83∘; SE 97∘97^\circ97∘; SW 83∘83^\circ83∘.

    Full solution

    180∘−97∘=83∘.180^\circ-97^\circ=83^\circ.180∘−97∘=83∘.

    The SE sector is vertical to the NW sector, so its measure is 97∘97^\circ97∘.

    The SW sector is vertical to the NE sector, so its measure is 83∘83^\circ83∘.

    Checking the turn gives 97+83+97+83=36097+83+97+83=36097+83+97+83=360 degrees.

    Each neighboring pair also totals 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Answer

    NE 83∘83^\circ83∘; SE 97∘97^\circ97∘; SW 83∘83^\circ83∘.

    Key idea

    At two distinct intersecting lines, opposite sectors agree and neighboring sectors are supplementary.

  8. Problem 8 Test the position claim

    Consider the claim that every pair of supplementary angles is a linear pair. Draw a counterexample using angles with different vertices and measures 98∘98^\circ98∘ and 82∘82^\circ82∘. Explain why your drawing meets the hypothesis but fails the conclusion.

    1. Hint 1

      Check the measure requirement separately from the position requirement.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the different vertices to test a necessary condition for a linear pair.

    Answer

    The claim is false; separate angles of 98 degrees and 82 degrees are supplementary and do not form a linear pair.

    Full solution

    Draw a 98∘98^\circ98∘ angle at P and an 82∘82^\circ82∘ angle at a different point Q.

    Their measure sum is 98∘+82∘=180∘98^\circ+82^\circ=180^\circ98∘+82∘=180∘, so they satisfy the supplementary hypothesis.

    The angles have different vertices, so they are not adjacent.

    A linear pair must be adjacent with opposite noncommon rays.

    The conclusion therefore fails for this pair, which refutes the universal claim.

    Answer

    The claim is false; separate angles of 98 degrees and 82 degrees are supplementary and do not form a linear pair.

    Key idea

    A single counterexample can separate a measure condition from a stronger geometric configuration.

  10. Problem 10 Design a crossing under two conditions

    Case A: Draw two distinct intersecting lines whose one pair of vertical angles is also complementary. Label all four sector measures and justify that your configuration satisfies both requirements.

    Case B: Could two adjacent sectors at the crossing of two distinct lines be complementary? Either construct such a crossing or justify why it is impossible.

    1. Hint 1

      Case A: impose both the position rule for vertical angles and the measure rule for complementary angles.

    2. Hint 2

      Case A: once that pair is fixed, use a neighboring sector to complete the crossing.

    3. Hint 3

      Case B: determine the sum already forced by adjacency at a crossing of two full lines.

    Answer

    Case A: opposite sectors 45∘45^\circ45∘ and 45∘45^\circ45∘, with the other opposite pair 135∘135^\circ135∘ and 135∘135^\circ135∘. Case B: impossible.

    Full solution

    Case A: vertical angles are congruent, and the requested pair must total 90∘90^\circ90∘.

    Each therefore measures 45∘45^\circ45∘.

    Draw a line through O, draw a second line making a 45∘45^\circ45∘ opening with it, and extend both lines in both directions.

    Case A: the vertical opposite of the 45∘45^\circ45∘ sector is also 45∘45^\circ45∘.

    Each neighboring sector measures

    180∘−45∘=135∘.180^\circ-45^\circ=135^\circ.180∘−45∘=135∘.

    The selected opposite pair is vertical and complementary, and all four sectors sum to 360∘360^\circ360∘.

    Case B: two adjacent sectors at two distinct intersecting lines have opposite noncommon rays.

    They form a linear pair, so their measure sum is 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Complementarity would require the same sum to be 90∘90^\circ90∘, which is impossible.

    Answer
