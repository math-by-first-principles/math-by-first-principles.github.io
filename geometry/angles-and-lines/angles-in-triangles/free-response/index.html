  1. Problem 1 Two classification records

    Classify each nondegenerate triangle using only the supplied information.

    Case A. A triangle has side lengths 12,12,1212,12,1212,12,12 centimeters. Give every applicable side classification.

    Case B. A triangle has interior angle measures 41∘,52∘,87∘41^\circ,52^\circ,87^\circ41∘,52∘,87∘. Give its angle classification.

    1. Hint 1

      For Case A, compare the equal side lengths with both the isosceles and equilateral definitions.

    2. Hint 2

      For Case B, compare each angle measure with 90 degrees.

    Answer

    Case A: equilateral and isosceles. Case B: acute.

    Full solution

    Case A.

    All three sides have equal lengths, so they are congruent and the triangle is equilateral.

    It also has at least two congruent sides, so it is isosceles under the inclusive definition.

    Case B.

    All three angle measures are positive and less than 90∘90^\circ90∘, so the triangle is acute.

    They also have the required total, since

    41+52+87=180.41+52+87=180.41+52+87=180.

    Answer

    Case A: equilateral and isosceles. Case B: acute.

    Key idea

    Choose a side classification from side information and an angle classification from angle information.

    Text description of this figure

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

    3. Hint 3

    Answer

    r=16r=16r=16; m∠LMN=50∘m\angle LMN=50^\circm∠LMN=50∘ and m∠LNM=40∘m\angle LNM=40^\circm∠LNM=40∘.

  5. Problem 5 Prove the sum from another vertex

    Let ABCABCABC be a nondegenerate Euclidean triangle with ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB obtuse. Orient your paper sketch with BBB to the left of CCC on a horizontal base and AAA above and to the right of CCC, as shown. Your proof must apply to every triangle with this stated arrangement, without measuring the drawing.

    Draw an auxiliary line through AAA parallel to BCBCBC. Choose points D,ED,ED,E on it with DDD left of AAA and EEE right of AAA. Mark the two alternate interior angle pairs and write a paragraph proof that the three interior angle measures of ABCABCABC sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘. Name the accepted foundation that allows the new parallel. Use straight-angle addition rather than the triangle angle-sum theorem as a premise.

    Text description of this figure

    Triangle ABC has B to the left of C on a horizontal base, and A above and to the right of C. Angle ACB is given to be obtuse. No auxiliary line, angle equality, or derived measure is drawn.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the three noncollinear vertices to check that A is outside line BC before choosing a parallel.

    2. Hint 2

      Treat AB and AC as separate transversals. In the stated orientation, follow the angles below the new line from its leftward ray to its rightward ray.

    3. Hint 3

      Write angle addition for the straight angle at A, then replace the two outside pieces with their congruent triangle angles.

    Answer

    Accepted foundation: a unique parallel through a point outside a line in the same plane. Alternate interior pairs: ∠DAB≅∠ABC\angle DAB\cong\angle ABC∠DAB≅∠ABC and ∠CAE≅∠ACB\angle CAE\cong\angle ACB∠CAE≅∠ACB. The interior measures total 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Full solution

    Because A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C are noncollinear, AAA lies outside line BCBCBC.

    The accepted parallel foundation supplies a line through AAA parallel to BCBCBC.

    Choose DDD and EEE on its opposite rays in the stated left-to-right order D,A,ED,A,ED,A,E.

    With ABABAB as a transversal of the parallel lines DEDEDE and BCBCBC, ∠DAB\angle DAB∠DAB and ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC are alternate interior angles.

    Therefore ∠DAB≅∠ABC\angle DAB\cong\angle ABC∠DAB≅∠ABC.

    With ACACAC as the transversal, ∠CAE\angle CAE∠CAE and ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB are alternate interior angles.

    Therefore ∠CAE≅∠ACB\angle CAE\cong\angle ACB∠CAE≅∠ACB.

    This pair is obtuse in the chosen arrangement; alternate interior congruence applies to obtuse angles as well.

    Both BBB and CCC are below DEDEDE.

    Since BBB is left of CCC and AAA is above and right of CCC, the rays occur below DEDEDE in the order AD,AB,AC,AEAD,AB,AC,AEAD,AB,AC,AE.

    Thus ∠DAB\angle DAB∠DAB, ∠BAC\angle BAC∠BAC, and ∠CAE\angle CAE∠CAE are adjacent parts of the straight angle ∠DAE\angle DAE∠DAE.

    Write p=m∠DABp=m\angle DABp=m∠DAB, q=m∠BACq=m\angle BACq=m∠BAC, and

    r=m∠CAE.r=m\angle CAE.r=m∠CAE.

    Straight-angle addition gives

    p+q+r=180∘.p+q+r=180^\circ.p+q+r=180∘.

    The two alternate interior congruences give p=m∠ABCp=m\angle ABCp=m∠ABC and

    r=m∠ACB.r=m\angle ACB.r=m∠ACB.

    Substituting those equal measures shows that the three original interior angle measures of ABCABCABC sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    No triangle-sum or right-angle restriction was assumed in this argument.

    Answer

    Accepted foundation: a unique parallel through a point outside a line in the same plane. Alternate interior pairs: ∠DAB≅∠ABC\angle DAB\cong\angle ABC∠DAB≅∠ABC and ∠CAE≅∠ACB\angle CAE\cong\angle ACB∠CAE≅∠ACB. The interior measures total 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Key idea

    An auxiliary parallel can place matching copies of two triangle angles beside the third at a different vertex.

  6. Problem 6 Two right triangles inside one

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at BBB. Point DDD lies strictly between AAA and CCC, and BD⊥ACBD\perp ACBD⊥AC. Given m∠BAC=32∘m\angle BAC=32^\circm∠BAC=32∘, find m∠ACBm\angle ACBm∠ACB, m∠ABDm\angle ABDm∠ABD, and m∠DBCm\angle DBCm∠DBC. Justify the transfer of any angle from the large triangle to a smaller one.

    Text description of this figure

    Triangle ABC is right at B. D lies inside AC and BD is perpendicular to AC, with right-angle squares at B and D. The angle at A is labeled 32 degrees. No other measure is displayed.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify the right angle in each triangle you use.

    2. Hint 2

      First use the whole triangle to find its angle at C. In triangle ABD, the angle at A is the original 32-degree angle.

    3. Hint 3

      Use the right angle at D in each smaller triangle; no side-length relationship is needed.

    Answer

    m∠ACB=58∘m\angle ACB=58^\circm∠ACB=58∘, m∠ABD=58∘m\angle ABD=58^\circm∠ABD=58∘, and m∠DBC=32∘m\angle DBC=32^\circm∠DBC=32∘.

    Full solution

    In the whole right triangle, the acute angles are complementary, so

    m∠ACB=90∘−32∘=58∘.m\angle ACB=90^\circ-32^\circ=58^\circ.m∠ACB=90∘−32∘=58∘.

    Since DDD lies on ACACAC, rays ADADAD and ACACAC agree.

    Therefore the angle at AAA in triangle ABDABDABD is the given 32∘32^\circ32∘ angle.

    The perpendicularity gives a right angle at DDD.

    180−90−32=58.180-90-32=58.180−90−32=58.

    Thus

    m∠ABD=58∘.m\angle ABD=58^\circ.m∠ABD=58∘.

    Similarly, rays CDCDCD and CACACA agree, so the angle at CCC in triangle BDCBDCBDC is 58∘58^\circ58∘.

    Its angle at DDD is right.

    The remaining measure in degrees is

    180−90−58=32.180-90-58=32.180−90−58=32.

    Hence

    m∠DBC=32∘.m\angle DBC=32^\circ.m∠DBC=32∘.

    As a local check, the adjacent parts of the right angle at BBB total

    58∘+32∘=90∘.58^\circ+32^\circ=90^\circ.58∘+32∘=90∘.

    Answer

    m∠ACB=58∘m\angle ACB=58^\circm∠ACB=58∘, m∠ABD=58∘m\angle ABD=58^\circm∠ABD=58∘, and m∠DBC=32∘m\angle DBC=32^\circm∠DBC=32∘.

    Key idea

    A smaller triangle inherits a larger triangle angle when its two defining rays agree with the original rays.

  7. Problem 7 A shared acute measure

    Triangle PQRPQRPQR has 118∘118^\circ118∘ at PPP and 22∘22^\circ22∘ at QQQ. Triangle UVWUVWUVW has ∠U≅∠R\angle U\cong\angle R∠U≅∠R and 35∘35^\circ35∘ at VVV. Find m∠Rm\angle Rm∠R, m∠Wm\angle Wm∠W, and the angle classification of each triangle. Explain why triangle UVW cannot also have a right angle, using the angle sum and the positivity of interior angles.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the remaining angle of the first triangle before transferring a measure.

    2. Hint 2

      Congruent angles have equal measures; that gives one measure in the second triangle.

    3. Hint 3

      Use a separate angle-sum equation in the second triangle.

    4. Hint 4

      Compare the obtuse measure with a hypothetical 90-degree angle; the third interior angle would still have to be positive.

    Answer

    m∠R=40∘m\angle R=40^\circm∠R=40∘ and m∠W=105∘m\angle W=105^\circm∠W=105∘; both triangles are obtuse, and UVWUVWUVW cannot also have a right angle.

    Full solution

    The first triangle has

    m∠R=180∘−118∘−22∘=40∘.m\angle R=180^\circ-118^\circ-22^\circ=40^\circ.m∠R=180∘−118∘−22∘=40∘.

    The given congruence gives

    m∠U=40∘.m\angle U=40^\circ.m∠U=40∘.

    In the second triangle,

    m∠W=180∘−40∘−35∘=105∘.m\angle W=180^\circ-40^\circ-35^\circ=105^\circ.m∠W=180∘−40∘−35∘=105∘.

    Triangle PQRPQRPQR is obtuse because of its 118∘118^\circ118∘ angle.

    Triangle UVWUVWUVW is obtuse because of its 105∘105^\circ105∘ angle.

    If another interior angle of UVWUVWUVW were right, those two measures would already total

    105∘+90∘=195∘.105^\circ+90^\circ=195^\circ.105∘+90∘=195∘.

    The third interior angle must be positive, so the total would be greater still and could not equal 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Answer

    m∠R=40∘m\angle R=40^\circm∠R=40∘ and m∠W=105∘m\angle W=105^\circm∠W=105∘; both triangles are obtuse, and UVWUVWUVW cannot also have a right angle.

    Any obtuse angle measures more than 90 degrees, so adding a right angle already exceeds the triangle total before the positive third angle is included.

  8. Problem 8 A three-point arrangement

    A claim says that joining any three distinct points always forms a triangle, so the triangle angle-sum theorem always applies. Consider distinct collinear points R,S,TR,S,TR,S,T, with SSS between RRR and TTT. Decide whether the theorem applies to this arrangement, and identify the condition that the claim omitted.

    1. Hint 1

      Check the definition of a triangle before adding angle measures.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the positions of these three points with the hypothesis required for the triangle theorem.

    Answer

    The theorem does not apply; a triangle requires three noncollinear vertices.

    Full solution

    The points R,S,TR,S,TR,S,T all lie on one line.

    Joining the pairs gives overlapping straight segments rather than three sides enclosing a nondegenerate triangle.

    The claim omitted noncollinearity.

    Distinctness alone does not make three points triangle vertices, so the triangle angle-sum theorem does not apply to this arrangement.

    Answer

    The theorem does not apply; a triangle requires three noncollinear vertices.

    Key idea

    Check that an object satisfies a theorem’s hypotheses before applying its conclusion.

  9. Problem 9 Combine a bisector with a linear pair

    In triangle ABCABCABC, DDD is strictly between AAA and BBB, and CDCDCD bisects ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB. Given m∠CAB=48∘m\angle CAB=48^\circm∠CAB=48∘ and m∠CDB=96∘m\angle CDB=96^\circm∠CDB=96∘, find m∠ABCm\angle ABCm∠ABC and m∠ACBm\angle ACBm∠ACB. Justify each intermediate angle.

    The bisector gives

    m∠DCB=48∘.m\angle DCB=48^\circ.m∠DCB=48∘.
