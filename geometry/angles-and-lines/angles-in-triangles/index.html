Angles in a Triangle

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Make one triangle tall and another almost flat. Their individual angles can look very different, yet their total stays fixed. A drawing suggests that pattern. To explain why every triangle obeys it, add a parallel line that brings the three angles together along one straight line.

Learning goals

  • Classify triangles by their stated side lengths and interior angle measures
  • Prove the triangle angle sum using an auxiliary parallel and angle relationships
  • Calculate missing angles and justify the third-angle theorem from the angle sum
  • Explain restrictions on right and obtuse angles within a triangle
  • Plan an angle chase using triangle sums and previously proved angle relationships

Two ways to classify a triangle

A triangle joins three noncollinear points with three segments. Its three interior angles are the openings inside those segments, one at each vertex. Throughout this lesson, triangles lie in the Euclidean plane and have three noncollinear vertices. A flattened arrangement of three collinear points is not a triangle.

You can classify a triangle using either its side lengths or its angles. For example, side lengths 7,7,107,7,107,7,10 give two congruent sides, so the triangle is isosceles. Separately, interior angles 47∘,68∘,65∘47^\circ,68^\circ,65^\circ47∘,68∘,65∘ are all acute, so they describe an acute triangle.

Classify byNameDefinition
SidesScaleneNo two sides are congruent
SidesIsoscelesAt least two sides are congruent
SidesEquilateralAll three sides are congruent
AnglesAcuteAll three interior angles are acute
AnglesRightOne interior angle is right
AnglesObtuseOne interior angle is obtuse

Our isosceles definition is inclusive: every equilateral triangle is also isosceles. A side classification uses side information. An angle classification uses angle information. We have not yet proved a relationship between equal side lengths and equal triangle angles, so do not add one from the sketch.

Worked example 1 Use the information each classification needs

Problem. Classify each triangle using the information supplied. Do not infer additional equalities from appearance.

Case A. A triangle has side lengths 8,8,88,8,88,8,8 centimeters. Give every applicable side classification.

Case B. A triangle has interior angles 37∘,64∘,79∘37^\circ,64^\circ,79^\circ37∘,64∘,79∘. Give its angle classification.

Solution. Case A. All three sides have equal lengths, so the triangle is equilateral. It also has at least two congruent sides, so it is isosceles.

Case B. Each angle is less than 90∘90^\circ90∘, so the triangle is acute. None of the given facts states which side lengths are equal.

Check your understanding

A triangle has three congruent sides. Which side classifications apply under our definitions?

Answer choices

Three congruent sides meet the equilateral definition. They also meet the requirement of at least two congruent sides, so the triangle is isosceles as well.

Why the three angles make a straight angle

The three interior angles of a triangle together have the measure of a straight angle. The earlier acute example has total

47∘+68∘+65∘=180∘.47^\circ+68^\circ+65^\circ=180^\circ.47∘+68∘+65∘=180∘.

Checking more drawings would give more evidence, but it would not establish every case. We need the parallel-line results proved earlier in this chapter. An auxiliary line is an extra line added to reveal a useful relationship.

The triangle interior-angle sum#

First run the argument on a triangle whose angles at A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C measure 52∘,73∘,55∘52^\circ,73^\circ,55^\circ52∘,73∘,55∘. Draw a line through CCC parallel to ABABAB. Choose DDD and EEE on it with CCC between them, as shown. The angles below that line, from left to right, are ∠DCA\angle DCA∠DCA, ∠ACB\angle ACB∠ACB, and ∠BCE\angle BCE∠BCE.

Using ACACAC as a transversal gives m∠DCA=52∘m\angle DCA=52^\circm∠DCA=52∘ by alternate interior angles. Using BCBCBC as a transversal gives m∠BCE=73∘m\angle BCE=73^\circm∠BCE=73∘ for the same reason. The three adjacent angles at CCC fill the straight angle ∠DCE\angle DCE∠DCE, so angle addition gives

52∘+55∘+73∘=180∘.52^\circ+55^\circ+73^\circ=180^\circ.52∘+55∘+73∘=180∘.

Now take any triangle ABCABCABC. Since CCC is outside line ABABAB, the accepted parallel foundation supplies a line through CCC parallel to ABABAB. Choose D,ED,ED,E on opposite sides of CCC so that the rays CA,CBCA,CBCA,CB divide the straight angle below DEDEDE in the order shown.

The parallel-line theorem gives ∠DCA≅∠CAB\angle DCA\cong\angle CAB∠DCA≅∠CAB and ∠BCE≅∠ABC\angle BCE\cong\angle ABC∠BCE≅∠ABC. Angle addition along the straight line gives

m∠DCA+m∠ACB+m∠BCE=180∘.m\angle DCA+m\angle ACB+m\angle BCE=180^\circ.m∠DCA+m∠ACB+m∠BCE=180∘.

Substitute the equal measures of the matching angles:

m∠CAB+m∠ACB+m∠ABC=180∘.m\angle CAB+m\angle ACB+m\angle ABC=180^\circ.m∠CAB+m∠ACB+m∠ABC=180∘.

No step depended on the particular numerical measures. Each triangle has the same parallel construction and the same two alternate interior pairs.

∎

This is the triangle angle-sum theorem. It applies to every nondegenerate Euclidean triangle, whether its sketch is acute, right, obtuse, rotated, or very narrow. The auxiliary line creates the proof; a measured picture alone does not.

Check your understanding

In the general proof, what justifies replacing the measure of angle DCA with that of angle CAB?

Answer choices

AC crosses the parallel lines DE and AB. The selected angles lie between those lines on opposite sides of AC, so alternate interior angle congruence applies.

Collinearity supplies the straight-angle total at C; it does not make its three parts equal.

Missing angles and the third-angle theorem

Knowing two triangle angles fixes the third. If two angles are 58∘58^\circ58∘ and 76∘76^\circ76∘, the third has measure

180∘−58∘−76∘=46∘.180^\circ-58^\circ-76^\circ=46^\circ.180∘−58∘−76∘=46∘.

The sum proof tells you why subtraction is justified: the two known angles and the unknown together fill exactly 180∘180^\circ180∘. In general, subtract the two known measures from 180∘180^\circ180∘. Check that all three measures are positive and their total is 180∘180^\circ180∘.

Worked example 2 Translate all three angle expressions

Problem. In triangle PQRPQRPQR, m∠QPR=(2x+9)∘m\angle QPR=(2x+9)^\circm∠QPR=(2x+9)∘, m∠PQR=(3x−4)∘m\angle PQR=(3x-4)^\circm∠PQR=(3x−4)∘, and m∠PRQ=(x+13)∘m\angle PRQ=(x+13)^\circm∠PRQ=(x+13)∘. Find xxx and all three angle measures. Then classify the triangle by angles.

Solution. The three expressions measure the three interior angles of one triangle, so the angle-sum theorem gives

(2x+9)+(3x−4)+(x+13)=180,6x+18=180,6x=162,x=27.\begin{aligned} (2x+9)+(3x-4)+(x+13)&=180,\\ 6x+18&=180,\\ 6x&=162,\\ x&=27. \end{aligned}(2x+9)+(3x−4)+(x+13)6x+186xx​=180,=180,=162,=27.​

Substitute 272727 into each expression separately.

At PPP, 2(27)+9=632(27)+9=632(27)+9=63, so m∠QPR=63∘m\angle QPR=63^\circm∠QPR=63∘.

At QQQ, 3(27)−4=773(27)-4=773(27)−4=77, so m∠PQR=77∘m\angle PQR=77^\circm∠PQR=77∘.

At RRR, 27+13=4027+13=4027+13=40, so m∠PRQ=40∘m\angle PRQ=40^\circm∠PRQ=40∘.

The measures are positive and 63+77+40=18063+77+40=18063+77+40=180. All three are less than 90∘90^\circ90∘, so the triangle is acute.

Two triangles with matching measures for two pairs of angles also have matching third angles. For example, if each has one angle of 69∘69^\circ69∘ and another of 46∘46^\circ46∘, each remaining angle measures 180∘−69∘−46∘=65∘180^\circ-69^\circ-46^\circ=65^\circ180∘−69∘−46∘=65∘. Both subtract the same two numbers from the same total.

The same reasoning works for any two matching pairs. This is the third-angle theorem: if two angles of one triangle are congruent to two angles of another, then the remaining angles are congruent. It is a claim about angles. It makes no claim that the triangles’ side lengths match.

Check your understanding

Triangle ABC has angle measures 43 degrees at A and 81 degrees at B. Triangle DEF has angle measures 81 degrees at D and 43 degrees at E. Which conclusion follows?

Answer choices

Each remaining angle is what is left after removing 43 degrees and 81 degrees from 180 degrees.

180∘−43∘−81∘=56∘.180^\circ-43^\circ-81^\circ=56^\circ.180∘−43∘−81∘=56∘.

Thus the remaining angles are congruent. Reversing the order of the two known measures does not change their sum.

What right and obtuse angles leave over

A triangle cannot have two right angles, two obtuse angles, or one of each. For a numerical check, proposed angles of 92∘92^\circ92∘ and 91∘91^\circ91∘ already total 183∘183^\circ183∘. There is no room for the third positive angle.

More generally, any two angles each at least 90∘90^\circ90∘ total at least 180∘180^\circ180∘. A nondegenerate triangle’s third angle is positive, which would make the total greater than 180∘180^\circ180∘. Therefore a triangle has at most one angle that is right or obtuse.

In a right triangle, the right angle uses 90∘90^\circ90∘ of the 180∘180^\circ180∘ total. The other two positive measures total 90∘90^\circ90∘, so both are acute and they are complementary. For instance, if one is 34∘34^\circ34∘, the other is 90∘−34∘=56∘90^\circ-34^\circ=56^\circ90∘−34∘=56∘.

Worked example 3 Use the right angle before solving

Problem. Triangle LMNLMNLMN is right at LLL. Its acute angle measures are m∠LMN=(4y+3)∘m\angle LMN=(4y+3)^\circm∠LMN=(4y+3)∘ and m∠LNM=(2y+9)∘m\angle LNM=(2y+9)^\circm∠LNM=(2y+9)∘. Find yyy and those two angle measures.

Solution. The two acute angles of a right triangle are complementary, so

(4y+3)+(2y+9)=90,6y+12=90,y=13.\begin{aligned} (4y+3)+(2y+9)&=90,\\ 6y+12&=90,\\ y&=13. \end{aligned}(4y+3)+(2y+9)6y+12y​=90,=90,=13.​

At MMM, 4(13)+3=554(13)+3=554(13)+3=55, so m∠LMN=55∘m\angle LMN=55^\circm∠LMN=55∘.

At NNN, 2(13)+9=352(13)+9=352(13)+9=35, so m∠LNM=35∘m\angle LNM=35^\circm∠LNM=35∘.

Their sum is 90∘90^\circ90∘, and adding the given right angle gives 180∘180^\circ180∘.

Two interior angles of a triangle measure 24 degrees and 31 degrees. What is the third angle, and which angle classification follows?

Answer choices

Use the triangle total, not the right-triangle total: no right angle was given.

180∘−24∘−31∘=125∘.180^\circ-24^\circ-31^\circ=125^\circ.180∘−24∘−31∘=125∘.

One interior angle is obtuse, so the triangle is obtuse. The other two remain acute.

Plan a justified angle chase

An angle chase finds unknown angles through a sequence of justified relationships. Work from a triangle whose angles you can determine toward the requested angle. Name a reason whenever you move from one angle to another.

Worked example 4 Use a smaller triangle to reach the target

Problem. In triangle ABCABCABC, DDD lies strictly between AAA and BBB, and segment CDCDCD is drawn. You are given m∠CAB=44∘m\angle CAB=44^\circm∠CAB=44∘, m∠ABC=63∘m\angle ABC=63^\circm∠ABC=63∘, and m∠ACD=29∘m\angle ACD=29^\circm∠ACD=29∘. Find m∠CDBm\angle CDBm∠CDB and m∠DCBm\angle DCBm∠DCB, with a reason for each step.

Solution. First use triangle ACDACDACD. Since rays ADADAD and ABABAB agree, m∠CAD=44∘m\angle CAD=44^\circm∠CAD=44∘. The triangle sum gives

m∠ADC=180∘−44∘−29∘=107∘.m\angle ADC=180^\circ-44^\circ-29^\circ=107^\circ.m∠ADC=180∘−44∘−29∘=107∘.

Next use the line at DDD. Rays DADADA and DBDBDB are opposite, so ∠ADC\angle ADC∠ADC and ∠CDB\angle CDB∠CDB form a linear pair. Thus

m∠CDB=180∘−107∘=73∘.m\angle CDB=180^\circ-107^\circ=73^\circ.m∠CDB=180∘−107∘=73∘.

Finally use triangle CDBCDBCDB. Rays BDBDBD and BABABA agree, so its angle at BBB is 63∘63^\circ63∘. Its angle sum gives

m∠DCB=180∘−73∘−63∘=44∘.m\angle DCB=180^\circ-73^\circ-63^\circ=44^\circ.m∠DCB=180∘−73∘−63∘=44∘.

As a check, the whole top angle is 29∘+44∘=73∘29^\circ+44^\circ=73^\circ29∘+44∘=73∘, and the three angles of ABCABCABC total 44∘+63∘+73∘=180∘44^\circ+63^\circ+73^\circ=180^\circ44∘+63∘+73∘=180∘. The equal numerical measures at AAA and in ∠DCB\angle DCB∠DCB came from the equations, not from an assumed side equality.

Check your understanding

Two distinct lines intersect at O. Points A,O,C lie in that order on one line, and B,O,D lie in that order on the other. Triangle AOB has angles 38 degrees at A and 57 degrees at B. What is the measure of angle COD?

Answer choices

First use the triangle sum in AOB.

m∠AOB=180∘−38∘−57∘=85∘.m\angle AOB=180^\circ-38^\circ-57^\circ=85^\circ.m∠AOB=180∘−38∘−57∘=85∘.

Angles AOB and COD are vertical angles of the two distinct intersecting lines, so they are congruent. Thus angle COD also measures 85 degrees.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • A flattened drawing: Three collinear points do not form a triangle. Its interior angles must be positive.
  • The wrong total: All three triangle angles total 180∘180^\circ180∘. Only the two acute angles of a right triangle total 90∘90^\circ90∘.
  • A side inference: Equal-looking sides give no information without a stated equality or a proved reason.
  • An inclusive definition: Equilateral triangles also meet our isosceles definition of at least two congruent sides.
  • An unexplained chase: Identify the triangle, linear pair, vertical pair, or parallel-line relationship that earns each equation.

Key takeaways

  • Classify sides from stated length or congruence information, and angles from their measures.
  • An auxiliary parallel through a vertex places matching copies of the other two angles beside the angle at that vertex, making a straight angle.
  • A nondegenerate Euclidean triangle has interior-angle sum 180∘180^\circ180∘; two matching angle pairs force the remaining pair to match.
  • A triangle has at most one right or obtuse angle. In a right triangle, the other two angles are acute and complementary.
  • Plan angle chases from known facts, justifying each intermediate angle before using it.

A bit of history (optional)

In Euclid’s Elements, the triangle angle-sum result arrives only after a construction for drawing a parallel through an outside point. His argument adds a parallel line at a triangle vertex and uses the earlier parallel-line angle result to bring the triangle’s angles together. The order of the book leaves a trail a reader can follow: first establish the angle relationships, then construct the parallel, then use both in the triangle argument. David Joyce’s edition preserves the cross-references beside the steps, so the dependencies remain visible rather than disappearing behind a finished formula.
