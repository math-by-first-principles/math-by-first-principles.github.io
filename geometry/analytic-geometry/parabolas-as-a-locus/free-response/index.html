  1. Problem 1 Two perpendicular distances

    Case A: Find the perpendicular foot and distance from PPP at (8,−2)(8,-2)(8,−2) to y=4y=4y=4.

    Case B: Find the perpendicular foot and distance from QQQ at (−5,−7)(-5,-7)(−5,−7) to x=−1x=-1x=−1.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify whether each directrix is horizontal or vertical.

    2. Hint 2

      Keep the coordinate parallel to the line and change the other to reach it.

    Answer

    Case A: foot (8,4)(8,4)(8,4), distance 666. Case B: foot (−1,−7)(-1,-7)(−1,−7), distance 444.

    Full solution

    Case A: The perpendicular is vertical, so the foot is (8,4)(8,4)(8,4).

    The vertical gap has length

    ∣−2−4∣=6|{-2}-4|=6∣−2−4∣=6

    Case B: The perpendicular is horizontal, so the foot is (−1,−7)(-1,-7)(−1,−7).

    The horizontal gap has length

    ∣−5−(−1)∣=4|{-5}-(-1)|=4∣−5−(−1)∣=4

    Answer

    Case A: foot (8,4)(8,4)(8,4), distance 666. Case B: foot (−1,−7)(-1,-7)(−1,−7), distance 444.

    Key idea

    Distance to a coordinate-parallel line uses one perpendicular coordinate gap.

  2. Problem 2 Locate the middle of the spacing

    A parabola has focus (5,6)(5,6)(5,6) and directrix y=−2y=-2y=−2. Find its vertex, axis, and vertex-focus distance.

    Text description of this figure

    Equal horizontal and vertical scales. Focus F at (5, 6). Directrix y = -2. The focus and directrix are shown without a parabola curve.

    1. Hint 1

      The axis passes through the focus and is perpendicular to the directrix.

    2. Hint 2

      The vertex is halfway from the focus to its foot on the directrix.

    Answer

    Vertex (5,2)(5,2)(5,2); axis x=5x=5x=5; vertex-focus distance 444.

    Full solution

    The foot from (5,6)(5,6)(5,6) to the directrix is (5,−2)(5,-2)(5,−2).

    Their midpoint has height

    (6+(−2))/2=2(6+(-2))/2=2(6+(−2))/2=2

    The vertex is (5,2)(5,2)(5,2) and the vertical axis is x=5x=5x=5.

    The focus is four units above the vertex.

    Answer

    Vertex (5,2)(5,2)(5,2); axis x=5x=5x=5; vertex-focus distance 444.

    Key idea

    The vertex bisects the perpendicular spacing between the focus and directrix.

  3. Problem 3 Check two proposed locus points

    A parabola has focus FFF at (−2,1)(-2,1)(−2,1) and directrix x=4x=4x=4. Decide whether each point is on the locus. Give both distances in each case.

    Point A: (0,1)(0,1)(0,1).

    Point B: (−2,7)(-2,7)(−2,7).

    1. Hint 1

      A point is on the locus exactly when its focus and directrix distances agree.

    2. Hint 2

      The line distance is horizontal. For the focus distance, check whether one coordinate already agrees.

    Answer

    A: focus distance 222, line distance 444, off the locus. B: both distances 666, on the locus.

    Full solution

    Point A: Its height agrees with the focus, giving focus distance

    ∣0−(−2)∣=2|0-(-2)|=2∣0−(−2)∣=2

    Point A: Its perpendicular distance to the directrix is

    ∣0−4∣=4|0-4|=4∣0−4∣=4

    Point A: The distances differ, so A is off the locus.

    Point B: Its x-coordinate agrees with the focus, giving focus distance

    ∣7−1∣=6|7-1|=6∣7−1∣=6

    Point B: Its perpendicular distance to the directrix is

    ∣−2−4∣=6|-2-4|=6∣−2−4∣=6

    Point B: They agree, so B is on the locus.

    Answer

    A: focus distance 222, line distance 444, off the locus. B: both distances 666, on the locus.

    Key idea

    Membership depends on the two actual distances, not the visual closeness of a point to a curve.

  4. Problem 4 Write the locus from its geometry

    The focus of a parabola is (6,−3)(6,-3)(6,−3) and its directrix is x=2x=2x=2. Find its equation, showing the distance equality from which it follows.

    Text description of this figure

    Equal horizontal and vertical scales. Focus F at (6, -3). Directrix x = 2. The focus and directrix are shown without a parabola curve.

    1. Hint 1

      A possible point must have the same distance from the focus as from the line.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the focus distance using both coordinate gaps and the line distance using its perpendicular gap.

    3. Hint 3

      Square the equality and cancel matching terms.

    Answer

    (y+3)2=8(x−4)(y+3)^2=8(x-4)(y+3)2=8(x−4); equivalent equation forms are accepted.

    Full solution

    For a possible point (x,y)(x,y)(x,y), the distance equality is

    (x−6)2+(y+3)2=∣x−2∣\sqrt{(x-6)^2+(y+3)^2}=|x-2|(x−6)2+(y+3)2​=∣x−2∣

    Both sides are nonnegative.

    Squaring and canceling the x-squared terms gives

    (x−6)2+(y+3)2=(x−2)2,(y+3)2=8x−32,(y+3)2=8(x−4).\begin{aligned}(x-6)^2+(y+3)^2&=(x-2)^2,\\(y+3)^2&=8x-32,\\(y+3)^2&=8(x-4).\end{aligned}(x−6)2+(y+3)2(y+3)2(y+3)2​=(x−2)2,=8x−32,=8(x−4).​

    The vertex is (4,−3)(4,-3)(4,−3) and the signed parameter is 222.

    Reversing the squared equality and taking nonnegative square roots recovers the defining distances.

    Answer

    (y+3)2=8(x−4)(y+3)^2=8(x-4)(y+3)2=8(x−4); equivalent equation forms are accepted.

    Key idea

    An equation is the distance condition written for every possible point.

  5. Problem 5 Sketch from the defining geometry

    For (x+6)2=−4(y−1)(x+6)^2=-4(y-1)(x+6)2=−4(y−1), find the vertex, focus, and directrix.

    Sketch the curve using the vertex and its two points at height y=0y=0y=0. Give those two points.

    1. Hint 1

      The x-square indicates a vertical axis. Read the vertex and signed parameter.

    2. Hint 2

      At the requested height, the squared gap determines two horizontal positions.

    Answer

    Vertex (−6,1)(-6,1)(−6,1); focus (−6,0)(-6,0)(−6,0); directrix y=2y=2y=2; points (−8,0)(-8,0)(−8,0) and (−4,0)(-4,0)(−4,0); opens down.

    Full solution

    The vertex is (−6,1)(-6,1)(−6,1).

    The signed parameter is −1-1−1, so the focus is (−6,0)(-6,0)(−6,0) and directrix is y=2y=2y=2.

    At y=0y=0y=0, the equation becomes

    (x+6)2=4(x+6)^2=4(x+6)2=4

    The gaps from x=−6x=-6x=−6 are −2-2−2 and 222.

    The points are (−8,0)(-8,0)(−8,0) and (−4,0)(-4,0)(−4,0).

    Each is 2 units from the focus and 2 units from the directrix.

    Plot these points and the vertex, then draw a smooth downward curve symmetric about x=−6x=-6x=−6.

    Answer

    Vertex (−6,1)(-6,1)(−6,1); focus (−6,0)(-6,0)(−6,0); directrix y=2y=2y=2; points (−8,0)(-8,0)(−8,0) and (−4,0)(-4,0)(−4,0); opens down.

    Key idea

    Exact equidistant points support a sketch without treating the drawing as proof.

  6. Problem 6 Translate the whole defining configuration

    A parabola has equation (y−4)2=20(x+1)(y-4)^2=20(x+1)(y−4)2=20(x+1). Translate it 333 units left and 666 units down. Give the new equation, focus, and directrix.

    1. Hint 1

      A translation moves the vertex, focus, and directrix together.

    2. Hint 2

      The signed vertex-to-focus step stays the same.

    3. Hint 3

      Apply the horizontal move to the directrix as well as the vertex.

    Answer

    Focus (1,−2)(1,-2)(1,−2); equation

    (y+2)2=20(x+4)(y+2)^2=20(x+4)(y+2)2=20(x+4)

    Directrix

    x=−9x=-9x=−9

    Full solution

    The original vertex is (−1,4)(-1,4)(−1,4) and the signed parameter is 555.

    The original focus is (4,4)(4,4)(4,4) and directrix is x=−6x=-6x=−6.

    The vertex moves to (−4,−2)(-4,-2)(−4,−2), while the focus moves to (1,−2)(1,-2)(1,−2).

    Moving the vertical directrix left three units gives x=−9x=-9x=−9.

    The parameter remains 555, so the translated equation is

    (y+2)2=20(x+4)(y+2)^2=20(x+4)(y+2)2=20(x+4)

    The new focus is 5 units right of the new vertex and the directrix is 5 units left, checking the translated geometry.

    Answer

    Focus (1,−2)(1,-2)(1,−2); equation

    (y+2)2=20(x+4)(y+2)^2=20(x+4)(y+2)2=20(x+4)

    Directrix

    x=−9x=-9x=−9

    Key idea

    A translation preserves the defining distances while changing the location.

  7. Problem 7 Recover geometry from an expanded equation

    A parabola has equation x2+2x+16y+49=0x^2+2x+16y+49=0x2+2x+16y+49=0. Find its vertex, axis, focus, directrix, and opening direction.

    1. Hint 1

      A completed square identifies the vertex before you locate the focus.

    2. Hint 2

      Complete the x-square and factor the remaining linear expression.

    3. Hint 3

      The multiplier of the unsquared gap is four times the signed parameter.

    Answer

    Vertex (−1,−3)(-1,-3)(−1,−3); focus (−1,−7)(-1,-7)(−1,−7); opens down. Axis

    x=−1x=-1x=−1

    Directrix

    y=1y=1y=1

    Full solution

    Complete the x-square by adding 1 on both sides.

    x2+2x=−16y−49,(x+1)2=−16y−48,(x+1)2=−16(y+3).\begin{aligned}x^2+2x&=-16y-49,\\(x+1)^2&=-16y-48,\\(x+1)^2&=-16(y+3).\end{aligned}x2+2x(x+1)2(x+1)2​=−16y−49,=−16y−48,=−16(y+3).​

    The vertex is (−1,−3)(-1,-3)(−1,−3) and the axis is x=−1x=-1x=−1.

    The multiplier is −16-16−16, so the signed parameter is −4-4−4.

    The focus is four units below the vertex at (−1,−7)(-1,-7)(−1,−7).

    The directrix is four units above the vertex, so its equation is y=1y=1y=1.

    The curve opens down.

    Answer

    Vertex (−1,−3)(-1,-3)(−1,−3); focus (−1,−7)(-1,-7)(−1,−7); opens down. Axis

    x=−1x=-1x=−1

    Directrix

    y=1y=1y=1

    Key idea

    Completing the square exposes the coordinate gaps that locate the parabola.

  8. Problem 8 Recover focus data from one point

    A parabola has directrix y=−3y=-3y=−3 and focus (2,f)(2,f)(2,f), with the focus off the directrix. It passes through (6,2)(6,2)(6,2). Find every possible fff and the corresponding parabola equation.

    1. Hint 1

      The known point has a fixed distance from the directrix.

    2. Hint 2

      Its horizontal gap from the focus is 4. Use that and the fixed distance to find possible vertical gaps.

    3. Hint 3

      For each focus, locate the vertex before writing the equation.

    Answer

    Focus height −1-1−1:

    (x−2)2=4(y+2)(x-2)^2=4(y+2)(x−2)2=4(y+2)

    Focus height 555:

    (x−2)2=16(y−1)(x-2)^2=16(y-1)(x−2)2=16(y−1)

    Full solution

    The line distance from (6,2)(6,2)(6,2) is ∣2+3∣=5|2+3|=5∣2+3∣=5.

    Its squared focus distance must therefore be 25.

    16+(2−f)2=2516+(2-f)^2=2516+(2−f)2=25

    Thus (2−f)2=9(2-f)^2=9(2−f)2=9, giving focus heights −1-1−1 or 555.

    Both differ from −3-3−3, so both positions are allowed.

    For f=−1f=-1f=−1, the vertex is (2,−2)(2,-2)(2,−2) and the signed parameter is 1.

    The equation is

    (x−2)2=4(y+2)(x-2)^2=4(y+2)(x−2)2=4(y+2)

    For f=5f=5f=5, the vertex is (2,1)(2,1)(2,1) and the signed parameter is 4.

    The equation is

    (x−2)2=16(y−1)(x-2)^2=16(y-1)(x−2)2=16(y−1)

    At (6,2)(6,2)(6,2), the first equation gives 16 on both sides, as does the second.

    In either configuration the focus distance is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs 4 and 3, so it is 5.

    Answer

    Focus height −1-1−1:

    (x−2)2=4(y+2)(x-2)^2=4(y+2)(x−2)2=4(y+2)

    Focus height 555:

    (x−2)2=16(y−1)(x-2)^2=16(y-1)(x−2)2=16(y−1)

    Key idea

    One point and a directrix can leave two possible focus positions.

  9. Problem 9 Use spacing and direction together

    A parabola has vertex (3,−4)(3,-4)(3,−4), a horizontal axis, and focus-directrix spacing 777. Its focus is left of its vertex. Find the equation, focus, and directrix.

    1. Hint 1

      The vertex bisects the focus-to-directrix spacing along the axis.

    2. Hint 2

      The direction from vertex to focus determines the sign of the parameter.

    Answer

    Focus (−1/2,−4)(-1/2,-4)(−1/2,−4); equation

    (y+4)2=−14(x−3)(y+4)^2=-14(x-3)(y+4)2=−14(x−3)

    Directrix

    x=13/2x=13/2x=13/2

    Full solution

    The vertex-focus distance is half of 7, or 7/27/27/2.

    The focus is left, so p=−7/2p=-7/2p=−7/2.

    The coefficient 4p4p4p is −14-14−14.

    The horizontal-axis equation is

    (y+4)2=−14(x−3)(y+4)^2=-14(x-3)(y+4)2=−14(x−3)

    The focus x-coordinate is −1/2-1/2−1/2.

    The directrix x-coordinate is 13/213/213/2.

    Their difference is 7, checking the given spacing.

    Answer

    Focus (−1/2,−4)(-1/2,-4)(−1/2,−4); equation

    (y+4)2=−14(x−3)(y+4)^2=-14(x-3)(y+4)2=−14(x−3)

    Directrix

    x=13/2x=13/2x=13/2

    Key idea

    Spacing determines the size of the parameter while opening direction determines its sign.

  10. Problem 10 Link the equation back to the distances

    A parabola has equation (y+1)2=c(x−2)(y+1)^2=c(x-2)(y+1)2=c(x−2) with nonzero ccc.

    Its directrix is x=5x=5x=5. Find ccc, the vertex, and the focus.

    Check whether PPP at (−1,5)(-1,5)(−1,5) belongs to this parabola using both its equation and the two defining distances.

    1. Hint 1

      Read the vertex first, then use the directrix to recover the signed parameter.

    2. Hint 2

      The directrix is three units right of the vertex, so the focus is equally far to its left.

    3. Hint 3

      Evaluate the equation and the perpendicular line distance separately.

    Answer

    c=−12c=-12c=−12; vertex (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1); focus (−1,−1)(-1,-1)(−1,−1). PPP is on the locus; both distances are 666 and both equation sides are 363636.

    Full solution

    The coordinate gaps give vertex (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1).

    The directrix is three units right of the vertex, so the focus is three units left.

    The signed parameter is −3-3−3.

    The coefficient is four times the signed parameter.

    c=−12c=-12c=−12

    The focus is (−1,−1)(-1,-1)(−1,−1).

    For PPP at (−1,5)(-1,5)(−1,5), the left side of the equation is

    (5+1)2=36(5+1)^2=36(5+1)2=36

    The right side is

    −12(−1−2)=36-12(-1-2)=36−12(−1−2)=36

    Thus P satisfies the equation.

    The focus and P share x-coordinate −1-1−1, so their distance is

    ∣5−(−1)∣=6|5-(-1)|=6∣5−(−1)∣=6

    The perpendicular foot is (5,5)(5,5)(5,5) and the line distance is

    ∣−1−5∣=6|-1-5|=6∣−1−5∣=6

    The equal distances verify that the recovered focus and directrix define the same locus as the recovered equation.

    Answer

    c=−12c=-12c=−12; vertex (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1); focus (−1,−1)(-1,-1)(−1,−1). PPP is on the locus; both distances are 666 and both equation sides are 363636.

    Key idea

    Recover the geometry and then check a point in both representations.
