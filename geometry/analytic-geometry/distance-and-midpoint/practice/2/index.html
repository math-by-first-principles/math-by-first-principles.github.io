    For AAA at (−6,1)(-6,1)(−6,1) and BBB at (2,7)(2,7)(2,7), find the distance from AAA to the midpoint of ABABAB.

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    First find the whole length.

    AB2=82+62,AB=10,AM=10/2,AM=5.\begin{aligned}AB^2&=8^2+6^2,\\AB&=10,\\AM&=10/2,\\AM&=5.\end{aligned}AB2ABAMAM​=82+62,=10,=10/2,=5.​

    The midpoint of ABABAB is (−3/2,2)(-3/2,2)(−3/2,2), and B=(4,−3)B=(4,-3)B=(4,−3). Find AAA.

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The coordinate sums are twice the midpoint coordinates.

    xA=2(−3/2)−4,xA=−7,yA=2(2)−(−3),yA=7.\begin{aligned}x_A&=2(-3/2)-4,\\x_A&=-7,\\y_A&=2(2)-(-3),\\y_A&=7.\end{aligned}xA​xA​yA​yA​​=2(−3/2)−4,=−7,=2(2)−(−3),=7.​

    For AAA at (−5,2)(-5,2)(−5,2) and BBB at (1,−2)(1,-2)(1,−2), what is the exact length ABABAB in simplest radical form?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The signed changes are 6 and −4-4−4.

    AB2=62+(−4)2,AB2=52,AB=213.\begin{aligned}AB^2&=6^2+(-4)^2,\\AB^2&=52,\\AB&=2\sqrt{13}.\end{aligned}AB2AB2AB​=62+(−4)2,=52,=213​.​
  4. 4

    A is at (−2,1)(-2,1)(−2,1). Point B is at (t,4)(t,4)(t,4), and ABABAB is 5 units. Which list gives all possible values of ttt?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The vertical gap is 3.

    (t+2)2+32=25,(t+2)2=16.\begin{aligned}(t+2)^2+3^2&=25,\\(t+2)^2&=16.\end{aligned}(t+2)2+32(t+2)2​=25,=16.​

    The horizontal change is either −4-4−4 or 4. Subtracting 2 gives −6-6−6 or 2.

    The midpoint of ABABAB is (2,3)(2,3)(2,3). Both endpoints have x-coordinate 2, and AAA has y-coordinate −4-4−4. What is ABABAB?

  6. 6

    Segments ABABAB and CDCDCD have endpoints AAA at (−4,−2)(-4,-2)(−4,−2), BBB at (2,4)(2,4)(2,4), CCC at (−2,4)(-2,4)(−2,4), and DDD at (4,−2)(4,-2)(4,−2). How are their midpoints positioned?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Average each pair of endpoints.

    MAB=(−1,1),MCD=(1,1).\begin{aligned}M_{AB}&=(-1,1),\\M_{CD}&=(1,1).\end{aligned}MAB​MCD​​=(−1,1),=(1,1).​

    The heights agree, and the x-coordinate increases from −1-1−1 to 1. Thus the second midpoint is 2 units right of the first.

    For AAA at (−1,0)(-1,0)(−1,0), BBB at (3,2)(3,2)(3,2), and PPP at (1,1)(1,1)(1,1), which statement confirms that P is the midpoint of AB?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The average of the x-coordinates is 1, and the average of the y-coordinates is 1.

    P=((−1+3)/2,(0+2)/2).\begin{aligned}P&=(( -1+3)/2,(0+2)/2).\end{aligned}P​=((−1+3)/2,(0+2)/2).​

    Halving both changes places P on AB at half its length.

  8. 8

    Triangle ABC has squared side lengths 20 for AB, 45 for AC, and 29 for BC. Under the order ABC to DEF, which data establish congruence?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The order pairs A with D, B with E, and C with F. Thus AB pairs with DE, AC with DF, and BC with EF.

    DE2=20,DF2=45,EF2=29.\begin{aligned}DE^2&=20,\\DF^2&=45,\\EF^2&=29.\end{aligned}DE2DF2EF2​=20,=45,=29.​

    All three must match these particular pairs.

    Solution

  10. 10

    A segment has endpoints (−5,3)(-5,3)(−5,3) and (1,−1)(1,-1)(1,−1). The second endpoint moves to (5,−3)(5,-3)(5,−3) while the first stays fixed. What is the new midpoint?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Average the fixed endpoint and the new endpoint, not the old endpoint.

    xM=(−5+5)/2,xM=0,yM=(3−3)/2,yM=0.\begin{aligned}x_M&=(-5+5)/2,\\x_M&=0,\\y_M&=(3-3)/2,\\y_M&=0.\end{aligned}xM​xM​yM​yM​​=(−5+5)/2,=0,=(3−3)/2,=0.​
  11. 11

    For AAA at (−3,2)(-3,2)(−3,2), BBB at (4,6)(4,6)(4,6), and CCC at (5,−1)(5,-1)(5,−1), which segment is longer, AB or AC?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Compare the squared lengths, since both lengths are nonnegative.

    AB2=72+42,AB2=65,AC2=82+(−3)2,AC2=73.\begin{aligned}AB^2&=7^2+4^2,\\AB^2&=65,\\AC^2&=8^2+(-3)^2,\\AC^2&=73.\end{aligned}AB2AB2AC2AC2​=72+42,=65,=82+(−3)2,=73.​

    The larger square belongs to AC.

    With AAA at (1,−3)(1,-3)(1,−3) and BBB at (t,2)(t,2)(t,2), for which prescribed length is there exactly one possible endpoint B?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The vertical gap is fixed at 5.

    (t−1)2+25=d2.\begin{aligned}(t-1)^2+25&=d^2.\end{aligned}(t−1)2+25​=d2.​

    At length 5, the horizontal square is zero, so t=1t=1t=1 only. A shorter length is impossible, and a longer length gives two horizontal positions.
