Lines, Slopes, and Perpendicularity

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

A midpoint tells you where a segment is balanced. A slope tells you which way a line points. Together, location and direction can turn a construction into an equation. You can then find an exact intersection even when the drawing is too small to measure. The geometric reasons behind the slope rules matter: they tell you when a calculation actually establishes parallelism or a right angle.

Learning goals

  • Justify constant slope and the parallel criterion using similar right triangles
  • Prove the perpendicular slope criterion and handle horizontal and vertical cases
  • Find perpendicular bisectors from midpoint and direction information
  • Distinguish median and altitude equations using their geometric definitions
  • Locate intersections of geometric lines and check their defining conditions

A short line-equation refresher

From Algebra, the slope of a nonvertical line is its signed rise divided by its signed run. For distinct points

A=(xA,yA),B=(xB,yB)\begin{aligned}A&=(x_A,y_A),\\B&=(x_B,y_B)\end{aligned}AB​=(xA​,yA​),=(xB​,yB​)​

with different first coordinates,

m=yB−yAxB−xA.m=\frac{y_B-y_A}{x_B-x_A}.m=xB​−xA​yB​−yA​​.

Keep the same subtraction order. A nonvertical line through AAA has point-slope equation

y−yA=m(x−xA).y-y_A=m(x-x_A).y−yA​=m(x−xA​).

Solving for yyy gives slope-intercept form. A vertical line instead fixes its first coordinate. Its slope is undefined, never infinity.

Worked example 1 Turn two points into a line equation

Problem. Find an equation for the line through

U=(−5,7),V=(3,3).\begin{aligned}U&=(-5,7),\\V&=(3,3).\end{aligned}UV​=(−5,7),=(3,3).​

Check both points.

Solution. The first coordinates differ, so the slope calculation is valid.

m=3−73−(−5),m=−12.\begin{aligned} m&=\frac{3-7}{3-(-5)},\\ m&=-\frac12. \end{aligned}mm​=3−(−5)3−7​,=−21​.​

Use UUU in point-slope form, then clear the fraction.

y−7=−12(x+5),x+2y=9.\begin{aligned} y-7&=-\frac12(x+5),\\ x+2y&=9. \end{aligned}y−7x+2y​=−21​(x+5),=9.​

At UUU, the left side is −5+2(7)-5+2(7)−5+2(7), which is 999. At VVV, it is 3+2(3)3+2(3)3+2(3), also 999.

Why slope stays the same

Changing the size of a slope triangle changes both legs in the same proportion.

In the figure, the small triangle has rise 222 and run 444. The larger one has rise 555 and run 101010. Both report slope 1/21/21/2. Their right angles agree, and their acute angles with the horizontal agree because their slanted sides lie on one line. AA similarity makes the matching legs proportional.

Similarity makes slope independent of the chosen points#

Take two slope triangles on a falling line, with runs 888 and 202020. If the first signed rise is −6-6−6, AA similarity multiplies its vertical leg by 20/820/820/8, giving signed rise −15-15−15 for the second. Both left-to-right trips fall, so

−68=−34,−1520=−34.\begin{aligned} \frac{-6}{8}&=-\frac34,\\ \frac{-15}{20}&=-\frac34. \end{aligned}8−6​20−15​​=−43​,=−43​.​

For any nonhorizontal, nonvertical line, choose any two pairs of distinct points and travel left to right. Complete each pair to a right triangle with horizontal and vertical legs. The horizontal legs are parallel. The line makes equal corresponding acute angles with them, so AA makes the triangles similar. Hence the ratios of vertical leg length to horizontal leg length agree.

The positive runs give both signed rises the same sign: positive for a rising line and negative for a falling line. Thus the signed rise-to-run ratios also agree. Reversing either pair changes both signs and preserves its ratio. For a horizontal line, every rise is zero and every nonzero run gives slope zero. The particular leg lengths did not matter; the matching angles did.

∎

The triangles have a right angle and the same acute angle with a horizontal line, so they are similar. The run triples, so the vertical leg length triples too.

h=3(−2)h=3(-2)h=3(−2) h=−6h=-6h=−6

Both left-to-right trips fall, so both rises are negative.

Parallel is a statement about distinct lines

Parallel nonvertical lines have the same slope. For example, slope triangles with runs 444 and 666 on two parallel lines have rises 222 and 333. Corresponding angles make them similar, giving slope 1/21/21/2 on both.

The parallel slope criterion and its converse#

Take distinct parallel rising lines. A slope triangle on the first has run 666 and rise 555; choose run 181818 on the second. Equal corresponding acute angles and right angles give AA similarity, so its rise is 151515. Thus

56=1518.\begin{aligned} \frac56&=\frac{15}{18}. \end{aligned}65​​=1815​.​

For the forward direction in general, parallel slanted sides make equal angles with parallel horizontal legs. Together with the right angles, this gives AA similarity and equal rise-to-run ratios. This reasoning applies to any parallel slanted lines. If one parallel is horizontal, the other is horizontal too, so both slopes are zero.

For the converse, first compare two lines of slope 2/32/32/3, one through (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) and the other through (0,−2)(0,-2)(0,−2). A shared point would require

23x+1=23x−2,1=−2.\begin{aligned} \frac23x+1&=\frac23x-2,\\ 1&=-2. \end{aligned}32​x+11​=32​x−2,=−2.​

The impossible equality shows that they never meet. In general, two distinct nonvertical lines of the same slope have different intercepts. At a shared point, subtracting their equal slope terms would force the intercepts to agree. Hence they cannot meet and are parallel. The values of the intercepts were irrelevant; their difference mattered.

∎

Thus two distinct nonvertical lines are parallel exactly when their slopes agree. Equal slopes and a shared point instead give one line, called coincident lines when described by two equations. Distinct vertical lines are parallel to each other; they have no slope numbers to compare.

Worked example 2 Check distinctness before concluding parallelism

Problem. Compare each line with ℓ\ellℓ, whose equation is

3x−2y=8.3x-2y=8.3x−2y=8.

Case A. 6x−4y=166x-4y=166x−4y=16.

Case B. 3x−2y=−23x-2y=-23x−2y=−2.

Solution. Case A divides by 222 to give precisely the equation of ℓ\ellℓ. The equations describe the same line.

Case B has the same slope, 3/23/23/2, but a different intercept. Its intercept is 111, whereas ℓ\ellℓ has intercept −4-4−4. These lines are distinct, so they are parallel.

Check your understanding

Two nonvertical lines both have slope −3/4-3/4−3/4 and contain (2,5)(2,5)(2,5). Which conclusion follows?

Answer choices

The same point and slope determine one line. Both have the equation

y−5=−34(x−2).y-5=-\frac34(x-2).y−5=−43​(x−2).

They coincide, so they are not a pair of distinct parallels.

A quarter-turn gives the perpendicular criterion

A right-angle turn exchanges the jobs of the horizontal and vertical legs, and one direction changes sign.

Start with run 333 right and rise 222 up. A 90∘90^\circ90∘ counterclockwise rotation sends the rightward part upward and the upward part leftward. The rotated triangle has run −2-2−2 and rise 333, so its slope is −3/2-3/2−3/2. The original slope is 2/32/32/3.

Perpendicular slopes from rotated right triangles#

Use a fresh triangle with run 555 right and signed rise −8-8−8. A counterclockwise quarter-turn sends the rightward part upward and the downward part rightward. The new signed run is 888 and rise is 555. The slopes multiply to

(−85)58=−1.\left(-\frac85\right)\frac58=-1.(−58​)85​=−1.

Now take a line that is neither horizontal nor vertical. Choose a positive run rrr and a nonzero signed rise sss between two of its points. Its slope is

m=sr.m=\frac{s}{r}.m=rs​.

Rotate the triangle 90∘90^\circ90∘ counterclockwise about its starting point. The run becomes a rise of rrr, and the signed rise becomes a run of −s-s−s. This also covers a falling line: a downward step turns right, giving a positive run. The perpendicular direction has slope

n=r−s,mn=−1.\begin{aligned} n&=\frac{r}{-s},\\ mn&=-1. \end{aligned}nmn​=−sr​,=−1.​

The rotation preserves the right triangle and turns its hypotenuse line through a right angle. The earlier uniqueness of a perpendicular through a point, and the perpendicular transversal theorem for parallels, show that any perpendicular line has this direction. Nothing depended on the chosen rrr and sss.

Conversely, suppose two nonvertical, nonhorizontal lines have slopes whose product is −1-1−1. Rotate the first through 90∘90^\circ90∘ about one of its points. The image has the same slope as the second, so the image and second line coincide or are parallel. The original line is perpendicular to the image. In the parallel case the perpendicular transversal theorem transfers that right angle to the second line; in the coincident case it is already the same right angle. This proves the converse too.

∎

For two nonvertical, nonhorizontal lines, perpendicularity is equivalent to slopes whose product is negative one. The slopes are negative reciprocals. Check both the sign and the reciprocal, rather than merely changing the sign.

A slope triangle has run 7 and signed rise −4-4−4. After a counterclockwise quarter-turn, what are its signed run and rise?

Answer choices

The rightward run becomes an upward rise of 7. The downward rise of 4 becomes a rightward run of 4. The new slope is

n=74.n=\frac74.n=47​.

Its product with the original slope −4/7-4/7−4/7 is −1-1−1.

Horizontal and vertical lines need their own case

A horizontal line and a vertical line meet at a right angle because their directions are parallel to the perpendicular coordinate axes. For example, the lines in the figure meet at (−2,3)(-2,3)(−2,3).

The horizontal slope is zero. The vertical slope is undefined, so there is no slope product to calculate. The negative reciprocal of zero also has no value. Through a specified point, the perpendicular to a horizontal line fixes the point’s first coordinate; the perpendicular to a vertical line fixes its second coordinate.

Perpendicular bisectors combine a point and a direction

The perpendicular bisector of a segment is the line through its midpoint perpendicular to its full line. For distinct endpoints, it passes through neither endpoint. From the earlier construction theorem, its points are exactly the points equidistant from the two endpoints.

Worked example 3 Find a perpendicular bisector

Problem. Find the perpendicular bisector of the segment with endpoints

A=(−4,−1),B=(2,3).\begin{aligned}A&=(-4,-1),\\B&=(2,3).\end{aligned}AB​=(−4,−1),=(2,3).​

Solution. Midpoint and slope give different pieces of information.

M=(−1,1),mAB=23.\begin{aligned} M&=(-1,1),\\ m_{AB}&=\frac23. \end{aligned}MmAB​​=(−1,1),=32​.​

The required line goes through MMM with slope −3/2-3/2−3/2.

y−1=−32(x+1),3x+2y=−1.\begin{aligned} y-1&=-\frac32(x+1),\\ 3x+2y&=-1. \end{aligned}y−13x+2y​=−23​(x+1),=−1.​

Substitution of MMM gives −1-1−1 on the left of the final equation. The product of the two slopes is −1-1−1, so the line meets both defining conditions.

Check your understanding

A segment has endpoints (−6,4)(-6,4)(−6,4) and (2,4)(2,4)(2,4). Which equation is its perpendicular bisector?

Answer choices

The segment is horizontal and its midpoint is (−2,4)(-2,4)(−2,4). Its perpendicular bisector is vertical through that midpoint.

x=−2x=-2x=−2

A slope-product calculation would be invalid because this perpendicular is vertical.

Medians, altitudes, and intersections

A median joins a triangle’s vertex to the opposite side’s midpoint. Its line needs those two points. An altitude passes through a vertex perpendicular to the opposite side’s full line. Its line needs the vertex and the perpendicular direction. The definitions do not normally select the same line.

Worked example 4 Separate a median from an altitude

Problem. Triangle ABCABCABC has

A=(0,6),B=(−4,0),C=(8,0).\begin{aligned}A&=(0,6),\\B&=(-4,0),\\C&=(8,0).\end{aligned}ABC​=(0,6),=(−4,0),=(8,0).​

Find the median line and the altitude line from AAA.

Solution. For the median, the midpoint of BCBCBC is M=(2,0)M=(2,0)M=(2,0). The slope from AAA to MMM is −3-3−3, giving

y=−3x+6.y=-3x+6.y=−3x+6.

For the altitude, BCBCBC is horizontal. The perpendicular through AAA is vertical, so its equation is

x=0.x=0.x=0.

The median passes through MMM. The altitude meets BCBCBC at (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), which is not its midpoint. A perpendicular line alone does not make a median.

An intersection must satisfy both line equations. Solve them together, then check the point in each. Parallel distinct lines have no intersection; coincident lines have infinitely many common points.

Worked example 5 Locate a median-altitude intersection

Problem. Triangle ABCABCABC has

A=(−2,5),B=(0,−1),C=(6,1).\begin{aligned}A&=(-2,5),\\B&=(0,-1),\\C&=(6,1).\end{aligned}ABC​=(−2,5),=(0,−1),=(6,1).​

Find the intersection PPP of the median line from AAA and the altitude line from BBB.

Solution. The midpoint of BCBCBC is M=(3,0)M=(3,0)M=(3,0). The median through AAA and MMM has slope −1-1−1:

y=−x+3.y=-x+3.y=−x+3.

The slope of ACACAC is −1/2-1/2−1/2. Its perpendicular through BBB has slope 222:

y=2x−1.y=2x-1.y=2x−1.

Set the heights equal and solve.

−x+3=2x−1,3x=4,x=43,y=53.\begin{aligned} -x+3&=2x-1,\\ 3x&=4,\\ x&=\frac43,\\ y&=\frac53. \end{aligned}−x+33xxy​=2x−1,=4,=34​,=35​.​

Thus P=(4/3,5/3)P=(4/3,5/3)P=(4/3,5/3). Substitution gives height 5/35/35/3 in both equations. The median uses the correct midpoint, and the altitude slopes multiply to −1-1−1, so the equations represent the requested geometric lines.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Equal slopes: check that the lines are distinct before calling them parallel. A shared point and slope give one line.
  • Perpendicular slopes: changing a sign alone usually fails. For two nonvertical, nonhorizontal lines, use negative reciprocals.
  • Vertical lines: undefined slope is not infinity. Handle a horizontal-vertical right angle directly.
  • Named lines: a median uses an opposite midpoint; an altitude uses an opposite perpendicular direction; a perpendicular bisector uses both midpoint and perpendicular direction.

Key takeaways

  • Similar right triangles explain why any two points on a nonvertical line give the same slope.
  • Distinct nonvertical lines are parallel exactly when their slopes agree. Distinct vertical lines are parallel too.
  • For nonvertical, nonhorizontal lines, perpendicular slopes multiply to −1-1−1. A horizontal line is perpendicular to a vertical line.
  • Translate each geometric definition into a required point and direction before writing an equation.
  • Check an intersection in both equations, including any vertical or coincident case.

A bit of history (optional)

A few pieces of palm leaf and some limestone weights once sat unrecognized among material excavated in Egypt’s Fayum region. The excavation was in 1899; the Science Museum’s account says that the instrument was identified in 1925. Missing parts were later reconstructed.

The object was an early form of the groma, a surveyor’s tool. Two ribs were tied at right angles, with weighted cords hanging from the ends. Sighting along one opposite pair set a straight direction; the other pair supplied a perpendicular direction. Roman surveyors used an improved form to lay out land and roads.

The Science Museum’s object record tells the story of this small tool’s rediscovery. What looked like ordinary plant material held a practical way to carry right angles across open ground.
