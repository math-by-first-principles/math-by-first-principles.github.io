  1. Problem 1 A different slope triangle

    On a falling nonvertical line, one slope triangle has horizontal run 4 and signed rise −3-3−3. A second slope triangle on that line has run 12. Find its signed rise and justify why the slope triangles give the same ratio.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the angles made by the line and the horizontal legs.

    2. Hint 2

      AA similarity scales corresponding legs by the same positive factor. Compare the two runs.

    Answer

    Signed rise −9-9−9; AA similarity preserves the leg ratio, and both rises are negative.

    Full solution

    Both triangles have a right angle and the same acute angle with a horizontal line.

    Thus they are similar by AA.

    The horizontal leg length changes by a factor of 3, so the vertical leg length changes by that factor too.

    Both left-to-right trips fall, so the larger signed rise is

    h=3(−3)h=3(-3)h=3(−3) h=−9.h=-9.h=−9.

    Its rise-to-run ratio is −9/12-9/12−9/12, the same as −3/4-3/4−3/4.

    Answer

    Signed rise −9-9−9; AA similarity preserves the leg ratio, and both rises are negative.

    Key idea

    Similarity fixes the ratio while the line's direction fixes the sign.
