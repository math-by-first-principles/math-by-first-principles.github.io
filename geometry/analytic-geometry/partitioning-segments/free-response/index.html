  1. Problem 1 Turn a ratio into a coordinate point

    Let A(−11,2)A(-11,2)A(−11,2) and B(9,12)B(9,12)B(9,12). Find internal P with AP:PB=1:4AP:PB=1:4AP:PB=1:4. State the fraction of AB that AP occupies.

    1. Hint 1

      Count the total number of ratio parts.

    2. Hint 2

      Apply the first-part fraction to both signed coordinate changes.

    Answer

    P=(−7,4),AP/AB=1/5.\begin{aligned}P&=(-7,4),\\AP/AB&=1/5.\end{aligned}PAP/AB​=(−7,4),=1/5.​

    Full solution

    The ratio has five parts in total, so AP/AB=1/5AP/AB=1/5AP/AB=1/5.

    The changes from A to B are 20 and 10.

    xP=−11+15(20)=−7,yP=2+15(10)=4.\begin{aligned}x_P&=-11+\tfrac15(20)\\&=-7,\\y_P&=2+\tfrac15(10)\\&=4.\end{aligned}xP​yP​​=−11+51​(20)=−7,=2+51​(10)=4.​

    Both coordinates use the same fraction, and 0<1/5<10<1/5<10<1/5<1, so P is internal.

    Answer

    P=(−7,4),AP/AB=1/5.\begin{aligned}P&=(-7,4),\\AP/AB&=1/5.\end{aligned}PAP/AB​=(−7,4),=1/5.​

    Key idea

    The first ratio part is a fraction of the sum of both parts.

  2. Problem 2 Preserve decreasing coordinate changes

    Let A(14,9)A(14,9)A(14,9) and B(−6,−11)B(-6,-11)B(−6,−11). Find internal P with AP:PB=3:2AP:PB=3:2AP:PB=3:2 and explain why both coordinates decrease.

    1. Hint 1

      Read the ratio from A toward B.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the signed whole changes before taking three fifths.

    Answer

    P=(2,−3)P=(2,-3)P=(2,−3); both endpoint coordinates decrease from A to B.

    Full solution

    The first part is three fifths of AB.

    Both whole coordinate changes are -20.

    Take three fifths of each change.

    xP=14+35(−20)=2,yP=9+35(−20)=−3.\begin{aligned}x_P&=14+\tfrac35(-20)\\&=2,\\y_P&=9+\tfrac35(-20)\\&=-3.\end{aligned}xP​yP​​=14+53​(−20)=2,=9+53​(−20)=−3.​

    Both changes are negative because B is left of and below A.

    The fraction 3/53/53/5 is internal.

    Answer

    P=(2,−3)P=(2,-3)P=(2,−3); both endpoint coordinates decrease from A to B.

    Key idea

    Ratios of lengths are positive while coordinate changes retain their direction.

    ▾

  3. Problem 3 Read an ordered ratio from a horizontal segment

    The points A(−9,6)A(-9,6)A(−9,6), P(3,6)P(3,6)P(3,6), and B(11,6)B(11,6)B(11,6) are on one horizontal segment. Find AP:PBAP:PBAP:PB and BP:PABP:PABP:PA. Explain the change in order.

    Text description of this figure

    A schematic internal division with endpoint order and positive ratio parts. Use the written coordinates, not a measurement of the picture.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the horizontal gaps, since the second coordinate is fixed.

    2. Hint 2

      Reduce the two positive lengths in each stated order.

    Answer

    AP:PB=3:2,BP:PA=2:3.\begin{aligned}AP:PB&=3:2,\\BP:PA&=2:3.\end{aligned}AP:PBBP:PA​=3:2,=2:3.​

    Full solution

    The horizontal lengths are

    AP=3−(−9)=12,PB=11−3=8.\begin{aligned}AP&=3-(-9)\\&=12,\\PB&=11-3\\&=8.\end{aligned}APPB​=3−(−9)=12,=11−3=8.​

    The second coordinate agrees throughout, and −9<3<11-9<3<11−9<3<11, so P is internal.

    The ordered ratio 12:812:812:8 reduces to 3:23:23:2.

    Starting from B exchanges the first and last parts, giving BP:PA=8:12BP:PA=8:12BP:PA=8:12, or 2:32:32:3.

    Answer

    AP:PB=3:2,BP:PA=2:3.\begin{aligned}AP:PB&=3:2,\\BP:PA&=2:3.\end{aligned}AP:PBBP:PA​=3:2,=2:3.​

    Key idea

    Reversing the endpoints reverses the ratio for the same point.

  4. Problem 4 Partition a vertical segment

    Let A(7,−15)A(7,-15)A(7,−15) and B(7,20)B(7,20)B(7,20). Find internal P satisfying AP:PB=4:3AP:PB=4:3AP:PB=4:3. Explain how you handle the coordinate that does not change.

    1. Hint 1

      The first coordinates agree, so every point of AB has that first coordinate.

    2. Hint 2

      Four of the seven ratio parts lie before P.

    Answer

    P=(7,5)P=(7,5)P=(7,5); its first coordinate stays 7.

    Full solution

    The first coordinate remains 7 because the entire segment is vertical.

    Its horizontal change is zero.

    The vertical change is 35.

    yP=−15+47(35)=5.\begin{aligned}y_P&=-15+\tfrac47(35)\\&=5.\end{aligned}yP​​=−15+74​(35)=5.​

    The second-coordinate gaps are 20 and 15, whose ratio is 4:34:34:3.

    Also −15<5<20-15<5<20−15<5<20, checking internality.

    Answer

    P=(7,5)P=(7,5)P=(7,5); its first coordinate stays 7.

    Key idea

    A zero change stays zero; use the changing coordinate to verify the ratio.

  5. Problem 5 Compare two endpoint orders

    Let A(−2,−9)A(-2,-9)A(−2,−9) and B(13,6)B(13,6)B(13,6).

    (a) Find internal P satisfying AP:PB=2:3AP:PB=2:3AP:PB=2:3.

    (b) Find internal Q satisfying BQ:QA=2:3BQ:QA=2:3BQ:QA=2:3. Are P and Q the same point?

    1. Hint 1

      Part (b) starts at B rather than A.

    2. Hint 2

      Use two fifths of the change from the named starting endpoint in each part.

    Answer

    (a) P=(4,−3)P=(4,-3)P=(4,−3). (b) Q=(7,0)Q=(7,0)Q=(7,0). They are different points.

    Full solution

    (a) Starting from A, two fifths of each change 15 gives a change of 6.

    Thus P=(4,−3)P=(4,-3)P=(4,−3).

    (b) Starting from B, two fifths of each change -15 gives a change of -6.

    Thus Q=(7,0)Q=(7,0)Q=(7,0).

    For the original point P, the ratio from B would be BP:PA=3:2BP:PA=3:2BP:PA=3:2.

    Retaining the numerical ratio 2:32:32:3 while reversing the endpoints specifies a different point.

    Answer

    (a) P=(4,−3)P=(4,-3)P=(4,−3). (b) Q=(7,0)Q=(7,0)Q=(7,0). They are different points.

    Key idea

    A ratio identifies a point only after its endpoint order is fixed.

  6. Problem 6 Recover the first endpoint

    The endpoint is B(17,−4)B(17,-4)B(17,−4) and the internal point is P(5,8)P(5,8)P(5,8). Given AP:PB=3:2AP:PB=3:2AP:PB=3:2, find A and check the ratio in both coordinates.

    1. Hint 1

      The change from P to B is two ratio parts.

    2. Hint 2

      Find one part, then move three such parts backward from P.

    Answer

    A=(−13,26)A=(-13,26)A=(−13,26); both coordinates give the internal fraction 3/53/53/5.

    Full solution

    From P to B the changes are 12 and -12, representing two parts.

    One part therefore changes the coordinates by 6 and -6.

    Move backward three parts from P.

    xA=5−3(6)=−13,yA=8−3(−6)=26.\begin{aligned}x_A&=5-3(6)\\&=-13,\\y_A&=8-3(-6)\\&=26.\end{aligned}xA​yA​​=5−3(6)=−13,=8−3(−6)=26.​

    The full changes from A to B are 30 and -30.

    The changes to P are 18 and -18, so both fractions are 3/53/53/5.

    This lies between zero and one, and its leftover is 2/52/52/5, checking the ordered ratio 3:23:23:2.

    Answer

    A=(−13,26)A=(-13,26)A=(−13,26); both coordinates give the internal fraction 3/53/53/5.

    Key idea

    When the known endpoint is B, the known gap PB represents the second ratio part.

  7. Problem 7 Place an anchor on a straight cable

    A straight cable runs from A(−10,4)A(-10,4)A(−10,4) to B(18,25)B(18,25)B(18,25), with coordinates measured in meters. An internal anchor P must satisfy AP:PB=5:2AP:PB=5:2AP:PB=5:2. Find its coordinates and the two cable lengths AP and PB.

    Text description of this figure

    A schematic internal division with endpoint order and positive ratio parts. Use the written coordinates, not a measurement of the picture.

    1. Hint 1

      Locate P by the fraction of the whole cable before it.

    2. Hint 2

      Find AB from the endpoint differences, then divide that length in the same ratio.

    Answer

    P=(10,19),AP=25 m,PB=10 m.\begin{aligned}P&=(10,19),\\AP&=25\text{ m},\\PB&=10\text{ m}.\end{aligned}PAPPB​=(10,19),=25 m,=10 m.​

    Full solution

    The fraction from A is 5/75/75/7.

    The coordinate changes are 28 and 21.

    xP=−10+57(28)=10,yP=4+57(21)=19.\begin{aligned}x_P&=-10+\tfrac57(28)\\&=10,\\y_P&=4+\tfrac57(21)\\&=19.\end{aligned}xP​yP​​=−10+75​(28)=10,=4+75​(21)=19.​

    The cable length comes from the earlier distance formula.

    AB=282+212=35 m.\begin{aligned}AB&=\sqrt{28^2+21^2}\\&=35\text{ m}.\end{aligned}AB​=282+212​=35 m.​

    The two parts are

    AP=57(35)=25 m,PB=27(35)=10 m.\begin{aligned}AP&=\tfrac57(35)\\&=25\text{ m},\\PB&=\tfrac27(35)\\&=10\text{ m}.\end{aligned}APPB​=75​(35)=25 m,=72​(35)=10 m.​

    Their sum is 35 m and their ratio is 5:25:25:2.

    Answer

    P=(10,19),AP=25 m,PB=10 m.\begin{aligned}P&=(10,19),\\AP&=25\text{ m},\\PB&=10\text{ m}.\end{aligned}PAPPB​=(10,19),=25 m,=10 m.​

    Key idea

    A straight segment has the same partition fraction in coordinates and in length.

  8. Problem 8 Use one coordinate to recover the other

    Let A(1,10)A(1,10)A(1,10) and B(16,−5)B(16,-5)B(16,−5). An internal point P has coordinates (k,4)(k,4)(k,4). Find k and the ordered ratio AP:PBAP:PBAP:PB. Check both coordinates.

    1. Hint 1

      Recover the journey fraction from the second coordinates first.

    2. Hint 2

      Apply that fraction to the horizontal change, then compare used and leftover parts.

    Answer

    k=7,AP:PB=2:3.\begin{aligned}k&=7,\\AP:PB&=2:3.\end{aligned}kAP:PB​=7,=2:3.​

    Full solution

    The vertical fraction is

    4−10−5−10=25.\frac{4-10}{-5-10}=\frac25.−5−104−10​=52​.

    Use the same fraction horizontally.

    k=1+25(16−1)=7.\begin{aligned}k&=1+\tfrac25(16-1)\\&=7.\end{aligned}k​=1+52​(16−1)=7.​

    The horizontal fraction is 6/15=2/56/15=2/56/15=2/5, agreeing with the vertical fraction.

    The common fraction is strictly between zero and one, so P is internal and AP:PB=2:3AP:PB=2:3AP:PB=2:3.

    Answer

    k=7,AP:PB=2:3.\begin{aligned}k&=7,\\AP:PB&=2:3.\end{aligned}kAP:PB​=7,=2:3.​

    Key idea

    A known changing coordinate determines the fraction for every other coordinate.

  9. Problem 9 Decide whether endpoint data can coexist

    A record lists A(−7,−6)A(-7,-6)A(−7,−6), P(2,3)P(2,3)P(2,3), and B(b,14)B(b,14)B(b,14). It requires P to be internal and AP:PB=3:2AP:PB=3:2AP:PB=3:2. Is there a real value of b satisfying all the data? Justify your decision using both coordinate requirements.

    1. Hint 1

      The ratio fixes the fraction from A before b is determined.

    2. Hint 2

      Check the second coordinate, which does not contain b.

    Answer

    No real value of b satisfies all the data.

    Full solution

    The required fraction is 3/53/53/5.

    The first-coordinate equation alone gives

    2=−7+35(b+7),b=8.\begin{aligned}2&=-7+\tfrac35(b+7),\\b&=8.\end{aligned}2b​=−7+53​(b+7),=8.​

    The second coordinate required by the same fraction is

    yP=−6+35(14+6)=6.\begin{aligned}y_P&=-6+\tfrac35(14+6)\\&=6.\end{aligned}yP​​=−6+53​(14+6)=6.​

    The record instead gives 3.

    Its actual vertical fraction is 9/209/209/20, not 3/53/53/5.

    Since b cannot change that vertical fraction, no real b satisfies the complete record.

    Answer

    No real value of b satisfies all the data.

    Key idea

    An endpoint coordinate cannot repair an inconsistency in the other coordinate.

  10. Problem 10 Recover a segment from two internal points

    The points A, P, Q, B lie in that order on one straight segment. Their coordinates are A(−12,9)A(-12,9)A(−12,9), P(−4,3)P(-4,3)P(−4,3), Q(4,−3)Q(4,-3)Q(4,−3), and B(b,−12)B(b,-12)B(b,−12).

    Find b, AP:PBAP:PBAP:PB, and AQ:QBAQ:QBAQ:QB. Verify that both P and Q have the required common fraction in the two coordinates and are internal.

    Text description of this figure

    The points occur in the order A, P, Q, B on one segment. A has second coordinate nine, P three, Q negative three and B negative twelve. The first coordinate of B is unknown.

    1. Hint 1

      The second coordinates determine the fractions from A for P and Q.

    2. Hint 2

      Use either fraction to recover b, and then check the other point independently.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare each used fraction with its own leftover fraction.

    Answer

    b=16,AP:PB=2:5,AQ:QB=4:3.\begin{aligned}b&=16,\\AP:PB&=2:5,\\AQ:QB&=4:3.\end{aligned}bAP:PBAQ:QB​=16,=2:5,=4:3.​

    Both points satisfy both coordinate fractions and are internal.

    Full solution

    The full vertical change is -21.

    The changes to P and Q are -6 and -12, respectively, so their fractions are 2/72/72/7 and 4/74/74/7.

    P has horizontal change 8.

    Its required fraction determines the endpoint.

    8=27(b+12),b+12=28,b=16.\begin{aligned}8&=\tfrac27(b+12),\\b+12&=28,\\b&=16.\end{aligned}8b+12b​=72​(b+12),=28,=16.​

    Now the full horizontal change is 28.

    For P, 8/28=2/78/28=2/78/28=2/7 matches the vertical fraction.

    For Q, 16/28=4/716/28=4/716/28=4/7 also matches its vertical fraction.

    Thus both points lie on the recovered segment.

    The two fractions satisfy 0<2/7<4/7<10<2/7<4/7<10<2/7<4/7<1, so both are internal and the stated order is correct.

    Their leftover fractions are 5/75/75/7 and 3/73/73/7, giving AP:PB=2:5AP:PB=2:5AP:PB=2:5 and AQ:QB=4:3AQ:QB=4:3AQ:QB=4:3.

    Answer

    b=16,AP:PB=2:5,AQ:QB=4:3.\begin{aligned}b&=16,\\AP:PB&=2:5,\\AQ:QB&=4:3.\end{aligned}bAP:PBAQ:QB​=16,=2:5,=4:3.​

    Both points satisfy both coordinate fractions and are internal.

    Key idea

    Recover the data, then verify every point with both coordinates and an internal fraction.
