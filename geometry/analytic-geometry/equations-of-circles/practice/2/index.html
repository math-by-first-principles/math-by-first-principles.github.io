    Complete the squares in x2+y2−8x−10y+32=0x^2+y^2-8x-10y+32=0x2+y2−8x−10y+32=0. What are the center and radius?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Move 32 and add 16 and 25 to complete both squares.

    (x−4)2+(y−5)2=9.(x-4)^2+(y-5)^2=9.(x−4)2+(y−5)2=9.

    The center is (4,5) and the positive radius is 3. The other choices reverse the center signs or read an incorrect radius.

  2. 2

    A diameter has endpoints (−7,−2)(-7,-2)(−7,−2) and (1,4)(1,4)(1,4). Which circle equation fits it?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The midpoint of the diameter is (-3,1), so it is the center. From that center to either endpoint the coordinate gaps have magnitudes 4 and 3.

    r2=42+32.r^2=4^2+3^2.r2=42+32.

    The squared radius is 25. Using 100 would square the full diameter instead, while 5 is the radius rather than its square.

  3. 3

    Find every common point of the circle and line.

    (x−1)2+(y+2)2=20,y=2x−4.\begin{aligned}(x-1)^2+(y+2)^2&=20,\\y&=2x-4.\end{aligned}(x−1)2+(y+2)2y​=20,=2x−4.​
    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    After substituting, the vertical gap is twice the horizontal gap.

    (x−1)2+(2x−2)2=20,5(x−1)2=20,(x−1)2=4.\begin{aligned}(x-1)^2+(2x-2)^2&=20,\\5(x-1)^2&=20,\\(x-1)^2&=4.\end{aligned}(x−1)2+(2x−2)25(x−1)2(x−1)2​=20,=20,=4.​

    The first coordinates are negative 1 and 3; the line then gives heights negative 6 and 2. Each makes the circle left side 20. The other proposed pairs omit a root or contain points failing one equation.

  4. 4

    Which description fits the common points of these graphs?

    x2+y2+2x−6y+10=0,x=−1.\begin{aligned}x^2+y^2+2x-6y+10&=0,\\x&=-1.\end{aligned}x2+y2+2x−6y+10x​=0,=−1.​
    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Complete both squares.

    (x+1)2+(y−3)2=0.(x+1)^2+(y-3)^2=0.(x+1)2+(y−3)2=0.

    The graph contains only (-1,3), which satisfies the line x equals negative 1. There is one common point, but no nonzero radius and no positive-radius circle to which the tangent criterion applies.

  5. 5

    A circle has center (−2,1)(-2,1)(−2,1) and radius 5. Which line is tangent at T=(1,5)T=(1,5)T=(1,5)?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The point T is on the circle because its coordinate gaps are 3 and 4, whose squared sum is 25. The radius slope is 4/3, so the tangent slope is negative 3/4.

    y−5=−34(x−1),3x+4y=23.\begin{aligned}y-5&=-\frac34(x-1),\\3x+4y&=23.\end{aligned}y−53x+4y​=−43​(x−1),=23.​

    The equation with right side 17 misses T. The other two pass through T but have slopes 4/3 and negative 4/3, neither perpendicular to the radius.

  6. 6

    Classify P=(7,1)P=(7,1)P=(7,1) relative to the graph x2+y2−6x+4y−7=0x^2+y^2-6x+4y-7=0x2+y2−6x+4y−7=0.

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Completing the squares gives

    (x−3)2+(y+2)2=20.(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=20.(x−3)2+(y+2)2=20.

    The squared center distance of P is

    42+32=25.4^2+3^2=25.42+32=25.

    The right side 20 is positive, so a real circle exists; 25 exceeds 20, putting P outside.

  7. 7

    The line cuts a chord from the circle below. What is the chord length?

    (x−4)2+(y+4)2=13,y=−2.\begin{aligned}(x-4)^2+(y+4)^2&=13,\\y&=-2.\end{aligned}(x−4)2+(y+4)2y​=13,=−2.​
    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Substitute the line height.

    (x−4)2+4=13,(x−4)2=9.\begin{aligned}(x-4)^2+4&=13,\\(x-4)^2&=9.\end{aligned}(x−4)2+4(x−4)2​=13,=9.​

    The two first coordinates are 1 and 7. The chord is horizontal, so its length is

    7−1=6.7-1=6.7−1=6.

    Three is only half the chord, the diameter has length twice the square root of 13, and 9 is a squared half-length.

  8. 8

    For which real values of b is the line tangent to this circle?

    (x+1)2+(y−3)2=9,y=b.\begin{aligned}(x+1)^2+(y-3)^2&=9,\\y&=b.\end{aligned}(x+1)2+(y−3)2y​=9,=b.​
    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    A horizontal tangent has its contact directly above or below the center (-1,3), at one radius distance. The radius is 3.

    (b−3)2=9.(b-3)^2=9.(b−3)2=9.

    The two heights are 0 and 6. At either height the only first coordinate is negative 1, and the vertical contact radius is perpendicular to the horizontal line. Height 3 cuts through the center; height negative 3 misses the circle.

  9. 9

    What is the radius of 2x2+2y2−20x−8y+48=02x^2+2y^2-20x-8y+48=02x2+2y2−20x−8y+48=0?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Divide the whole equation by 2 before completing the squares.

    x2+y2−10x−4y+24=0,(x−5)2+(y−2)2=5.\begin{aligned}x^2+y^2-10x-4y+24&=0,\\(x-5)^2+(y-2)^2&=5.\end{aligned}x2+y2−10x−4y+24(x−5)2+(y−2)2​=0,=5.​

    The positive radius is the square root of 5. None of the other proposed radii has square 5.

  10. 10

    The circle x2+y2−2x+4y+c=0x^2+y^2-2x+4y+c=0x2+y2−2x+4y+c=0 passes through (4,2)(4,2)(4,2). Find c.

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Substitute the known boundary point into the original equation.

    16+4−8+8+c=0,20+c=0.\begin{aligned}16+4-8+8+c&=0,\\20+c&=0.\end{aligned}16+4−8+8+c20+c​=0,=0.​

    Thus c is negative 20. Completing the squares would then give squared radius 25, confirming that a positive-radius circle results. The other constants fail the boundary-point substitution.

  11. 11

    The line passes through T at (0,−3)(0,-3)(0,−3) on the circle below. Which description is correct?

    (x+4)2+(y+3)2=16,y=−3.\begin{aligned}(x+4)^2+(y+3)^2&=16,\\y&=-3.\end{aligned}(x+4)2+(y+3)2y​=16,=−3.​
    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The center is (-4,-3), so the given horizontal line passes through the center as well as T. Substitution gives

    (x+4)2=16.(x+4)^2=16.(x+4)2=16.

    There are two intersections, (0,-3) and (-8,-3). A tangent at T would be vertical, perpendicular to the horizontal contact radius.

  12. 12

    For this circle and line, what does substitution show?

    (x−4)2+(y+1)2=3,y=2.\begin{aligned}(x-4)^2+(y+1)^2&=3,\\y&=2.\end{aligned}(x−4)2+(y+1)2y​=3,=2.​
    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The line fixes the vertical gap from the center at 3. Substitute it.

    (x−4)2+9=3,(x−4)2=−6.\begin{aligned}(x-4)^2+9&=3,\\(x-4)^2&=-6.\end{aligned}(x−4)2+9(x−4)2​=3,=−6.​

    A real square cannot equal negative 6, so there is no intersection. Each other option uses a different squared equation from the actual substitution.
