  1. Problem 1 A radius fixes the locus

    A circle has center CCC at (−5,−2)(-5,-2)(−5,−2) and passes through RRR at (−3,4)(-3,4)(−3,4). Write its equation and check R in that equation.

    1. Hint 1

      Use squared distance from the center to the given point.

    2. Hint 2

      Substitute the given boundary point into the equation to check it.

    Answer

    (x+5)2+(y+2)2=40(x+5)^2+(y+2)^2=40(x+5)2+(y+2)2=40; R satisfies the equation.

    Full solution

    The coordinate gaps from C to R are 2 and 6.

    The squared radius is

    r2=22+62.r^2=2^2+6^2.r2=22+62.

    This is 40.

    The required equation is

    (x+5)2+(y+2)2=40.(x+5)^2+(y+2)^2=40.(x+5)2+(y+2)2=40.

    Its left side is the squared distance to C.

    Substitute R to check the boundary condition.

    (−3+5)2+(4+2)2=40.(-3+5)^2+(4+2)^2=40.(−3+5)2+(4+2)2=40.

    It gives the required squared radius, so R lies on the circle.

    Answer

    (x+5)2+(y+2)2=40(x+5)^2+(y+2)^2=40(x+5)2+(y+2)2=40; R satisfies the equation.

    Key idea

    A circle equation imposes a distance condition on every solution point.

  2. Problem 2 Read the geometry and sketch it

    Find the center and radius of the graph below. Sketch it and list four circle points horizontally or vertically aligned with its center.

    x2+y2+10x−8y+37=0.x^2+y^2+10x-8y+37=0.x2+y2+10x−8y+37=0.

    1. Hint 1

      Rewrite the equation so that each variable appears in a completed square.

    2. Hint 2

      Step one radius in each coordinate direction from the center.

    Answer

    Center (−5,4)(-5,4)(−5,4); radius 2; four points (−7,4)(-7,4)(−7,4), (−3,4)(-3,4)(−3,4), (−5,2)(-5,2)(−5,2), (−5,6)(-5,6)(−5,6).

    Full solution

    Move 37 to the other side and add 25 and 16.

    (x+5)2+(y−4)2=4.(x+5)^2+(y-4)^2=4.(x+5)2+(y−4)2=4.

    The center is (−5,4)(-5,4)(−5,4) and radius 2.

    Stepping left, right, down, and up gives (−7,4)(-7,4)(−7,4), (−3,4)(-3,4)(−3,4), (−5,2)(-5,2)(−5,2), and (−5,6)(-5,6)(−5,6).

    Draw a round curve through these four points on equal-scale axes.

    For each listed point, one coordinate gap is zero and the other has magnitude 2.

    Its squared distance is therefore 4, verifying the sketch.

    Answer

    Center (−5,4)(-5,4)(−5,4); radius 2; four points (−7,4)(-7,4)(−7,4), (−3,4)(-3,4)(−3,4), (−5,2)(-5,2)(−5,2), (−5,6)(-5,6)(−5,6).

    Key idea

    A completed-square equation supplies both the center and the scale of a graph.

  3. Problem 3 Which point can be a contact?

    A circle has equation

    (x−1)2+(y+6)2=17.(x-1)^2+(y+6)^2=17.(x−1)2+(y+6)2=17.

    Classify each point as inside, on, or outside. Which point could be the contact point of a tangent to this circle?

    A is (2,−4)(2,-4)(2,−4).

    B is (5,−5)(5,-5)(5,−5).

    D is (−3,−4)(-3,-4)(−3,−4).

    1. Hint 1

      Compare squared distances from the center with the squared radius.

    2. Hint 2

      A tangent contact must lie on the circle itself.

    Answer

    A is inside; B is on the circle; D is outside. Only B can be a tangent contact.

    Full solution

    For A, the squared center distance is

    12+22=5.1^2+2^2=5.12+22=5.

    Since 5 is less than 17, A is inside.

    For B, the squared center distance is

    42+12=17.4^2+1^2=17.42+12=17.

    Thus B is on the circle.

    For D, the squared center distance is

    (−4)2+22=20.(-4)^2+2^2=20.(−4)2+22=20.

    Since 20 exceeds 17, D is outside.

    A contact point belongs to the circle, so only B qualifies.

    The line perpendicular to the radius at B gives such a tangent.

    Answer

    A is inside; B is on the circle; D is outside. Only B can be a tangent contact.

    Key idea

    A contact point passes the circle equation before any line direction is considered.

  4. Problem 4 A vertical chord

    Find all common points of the circle and line below. Then find the length and midpoint of the chord between the intersections.

    (x+2)2+(y−1)2=18,x=1.\begin{aligned}(x+2)^2+(y-1)^2&=18,\\x&=1.\end{aligned}(x+2)2+(y−1)2x​=18,=1.​
    Text description of this figure

    A vertical line cuts the circle. Both coordinate axes have the same unit scale. C is at (-2, 1). Solid blue circle boundaries and dashed red lines have different line styles.

    1. Hint 1

      A vertical line fixes the first coordinate.

    2. Hint 2

      The remaining squared equation gives two heights; their difference gives the chord length.

    Answer

    Intersections (1,−2)(1,-2)(1,−2) and (1,4)(1,4)(1,4); chord length 6 units; midpoint (1,1)(1,1)(1,1).

    Full solution

    Substituting the line condition gives

    9+(y−1)2=18,(y−1)2=9.\begin{aligned}9+(y-1)^2&=18,\\(y-1)^2&=9.\end{aligned}9+(y−1)2(y−1)2​=18,=9.​

    The possible heights are negative 2 and 4.

    Both points have first coordinate 1, and each makes the circle left side 18.

    The chord is vertical, so its length is

    4−(−2)=6.4-(-2)=6.4−(−2)=6.

    Its midpoint is (1,1)(1,1)(1,1), obtained by averaging the two heights.

    Answer

    Intersections (1,−2)(1,-2)(1,−2) and (1,4)(1,4)(1,4); chord length 6 units; midpoint (1,1)(1,1)(1,1).

    Key idea

    A vertical line-circle intersection is solved by substituting x first.

  5. Problem 5 A path through a watered region

    On a level plan, each coordinate unit represents one meter. An ideal sprinkler waters every point at distance at most 5 meters from (3,2)(3,2)(3,2). A straight path follows the line below. Find the two boundary crossings and the length of the part of the path inside or on the watered region.

    y=6.y=6.y=6.

    1. Hint 1

      The boundary is a circle; points on the path must also satisfy its line equation.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the two boundary crossings as the endpoints of the watered path segment.

    Answer

    Boundary crossings (0,6)(0,6)(0,6) and (6,6)(6,6)(6,6); watered path length 6 meters.

    Full solution

    The ideal circular boundary is

    (x−3)2+(y−2)2=25.(x-3)^2+(y-2)^2=25.(x−3)2+(y−2)2=25.

    Substitute the path height to obtain

    (x−3)2+16=25,(x−3)2=9.\begin{aligned}(x-3)^2+16&=25,\\(x-3)^2&=9.\end{aligned}(x−3)2+16(x−3)2​=25,=9.​

    The first coordinates are 0 and 6, giving crossings (0,6)(0,6)(0,6) and (6,6)(6,6)(6,6).

    Along the path, the watered condition is

    (x−3)2≤9.(x-3)^2\le9.(x−3)2≤9.

    This holds from first coordinate 0 through 6.

    The horizontal length is

    6−0=6.6-0=6.6−0=6.

    The scale is one meter per unit, so the requested length is 6 meters.

    Answer

    Boundary crossings (0,6)(0,6)(0,6) and (6,6)(6,6)(6,6); watered path length 6 meters.

    Key idea

    A line condition locates the part of a modeled circular region reached by a path.

  6. Problem 6 One family and one vertical line

    For each value of k below, describe the real graph as a positive-radius circle, a single point, or empty. Find all of its common points with x=6x=6x=6. If there is only one common point, explain whether this is tangency to a positive-radius circle.

    x2+y2−12x+2y+k=0.x^2+y^2-12x+2y+k=0.x2+y2−12x+2y+k=0.

    Case A: k=21k=21k=21.

    Case B: k=37k=37k=37.

    Case C: k=40k=40k=40.

    1. Hint 1

      Complete the two squares before applying the line condition.

    2. Hint 2

      A single graph point may lie on a line without forming a positive-radius circle.

    Answer

    A: circle; (6,−5)(6,-5)(6,−5) and (6,3)(6,3)(6,3). B: single point (6,−1)(6,-1)(6,−1), not positive-radius circle tangency. C: empty; no common points.

    Full solution

    Completing the squares gives

    (x−6)2+(y+1)2=37−k.(x-6)^2+(y+1)^2=37-k.(x−6)2+(y+1)2=37−k.

    At first coordinate 6, the first square is zero.

    Case A: the right side is 16.

    The graph is a circle with center (6,−1)(6,-1)(6,−1) and radius 4.

    The two common points are (6,−5)(6,-5)(6,−5) and (6,3)(6,3)(6,3), each 4 units vertically from the center.

    Case B: the right side is zero.

    Both squares vanish only at (6,−1)(6,-1)(6,−1), and that point lies on the line.

    The graph is a single point with no nonzero contact radius, so this is not tangency to a positive-radius circle.

    Case C: the right side is negative 3.

    A sum of real squares cannot equal it, so the graph is empty and there are no common points.

    Answer

    A: circle; (6,−5)(6,-5)(6,−5) and (6,3)(6,3)(6,3). B: single point (6,−1)(6,-1)(6,−1), not positive-radius circle tangency. C: empty; no common points.

    Key idea

    One common point implies circle tangency only when the graph is a positive-radius circle.

  7. Problem 7 Check three tangency conditions

    Decide whether the line below is tangent to the circle at T=(1,0)T=(1,0)T=(1,0). Check that T is on both objects and check the contact-radius direction.

    (x+3)2+(y+2)2=20,2x+y=2.\begin{aligned}(x+3)^2+(y+2)^2&=20,\\2x+y&=2.\end{aligned}(x+3)2+(y+2)22x+y​=20,=2.​

    1. Hint 1

      A tangent verification needs incidence as well as perpendicularity.

    2. Hint 2

      Read the center and compare the slope from it to T with the line slope.

    Answer

    Yes. T lies on both; the contact-radius slope is 1/21/21/2 and the line slope is −2-2−2, so they are perpendicular.

    Full solution

    At T, the circle left side is

    42+22=20.4^2+2^2=20.42+22=20.

    Thus T is on the circle.

    The line left side at T is

    2(1)+0=2.2(1)+0=2.2(1)+0=2.

    Thus T is also on the line.

    The center is C=(−3,−2)C=(-3,-2)C=(−3,−2).

    The radius slope is

    mCT=24.m_{CT}=\frac24.mCT​=42​.

    It is 1/21/21/2, while the line slope is −2-2−2.

    Both slopes are defined and nonzero.

    Their product is −1-1−1, so the line is perpendicular to the radius at its circle endpoint T.

    The tangent-radius converse proves tangency.

    Answer

    Yes. T lies on both; the contact-radius slope is 1/21/21/2 and the line slope is −2-2−2, so they are perpendicular.

    Key idea

    Tangency verification combines a shared point with a perpendicular contact radius.

  8. Problem 8 Compare a line and a circle

    Find the perpendicular foot H from the center of the circle to the line below. Use its distance to decide how many common points the line and circle have. Confirm the count by substitution.

    x2+y2=10,y=x+6.\begin{aligned}x^2+y^2&=10,\\y&=x+6.\end{aligned}x2+y2y​=10,=x+6.​

    1. Hint 1

      The center is the origin; its perpendicular to the line has slope negative 1.

    2. Hint 2

      Find H from the two line equations, then compare its squared center distance with 10.

    Answer

    H=(−3,3)H=(-3,3)H=(−3,3); no common points.

    Full solution

    The center is O=(0,0)O=(0,0)O=(0,0).

    The perpendicular through it is

    y=−x.y=-x.y=−x.

    Its intersection with the given line satisfies

    −x=x+6.-x=x+6.−x=x+6.

    Thus H=(−3,3)H=(-3,3)H=(−3,3).

    The squared perpendicular distance is

    OH2=18.OH^2=18.OH2=18.

    This exceeds the squared radius 10.

    H is the nearest line point to the center, so the entire line is outside.

    Substitution confirms the count:

    x2+(x+6)2=10,2(x+3)2+18=10,(x+3)2=−4.\begin{aligned}x^2+(x+6)^2&=10,\\2(x+3)^2+18&=10,\\(x+3)^2&=-4.\end{aligned}x2+(x+6)22(x+3)2+18(x+3)2​=10,=10,=−4.​

    There is no real root, so there are no common points.

    Answer

    H=(−3,3)H=(-3,3)H=(−3,3); no common points.

  10. Problem 10 Combine the circle tests

    Use the graph below for all parts. Recover its center and radius. Find every intersection with each of the three lines. For any tangent, verify the perpendicular contact radius. Finally classify P=(−5,3)P=(-5,3)P=(−5,3) as inside, on, or outside.
