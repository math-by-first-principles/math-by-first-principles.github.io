    A convex quadrilateral has vertices A(-2,-1), B(4,2), C(2,6), D(-4,3) in boundary order. What is its most specific classification among these choices?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Both diagonal midpoints are (0,5/2), so it is a parallelogram.

    mAB=1/2mBC=−2mABmBC=−1.\begin{aligned}m_{AB}&=1/2\\m_{BC}&=-2\\m_{AB}m_{BC}&=-1.\end{aligned}mAB​mBC​mAB​mBC​​=1/2=−2=−1.​

    An adjacent right angle makes it a rectangle. Adjacent squared lengths are 45 and 20, so it is not a square.

    A convex quadrilateral has A(-5,1), B(1,5), C(7,1), D(1,-3). Which exact calculation supports a rhombus classification after its diagonals have a common midpoint?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The adjacent changes are (6,4) and (6,-4).

    AB2=36+16AB2=52BC2=36+16BC2=52.\begin{aligned}AB^2&=36+16\\AB^2&=52\\BC^2&=36+16\\BC^2&=52.\end{aligned}AB2AB2BC2BC2​=36+16=52=36+16=52.​

    Equal adjacent sides in the established parallelogram prove a rhombus. Its diagonals are 12 and 8, and the slope product is -4/9.

    A triangle has A(-6,4), B(3,-5), and centroid G(1,2). What is its third vertex C?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The three vertex coordinates sum to three times the centroid coordinates.

    −6+3+xC=3xC=64−5+yC=6yC=7.\begin{aligned}-6+3+x_C&=3\\x_C&=6\\4-5+y_C&=6\\y_C&=7.\end{aligned}−6+3+xC​xC​4−5+yC​yC​​=3=6=6=7.​

    The side AB has slope -1, whereas AC has slope 1/4, so the recovered triangle is nondegenerate.

    For a rectangle with A(0,0), B(a,0), C(a,b), D(0,b), a,b positive, which observation proves diagonal bisection?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Averaging A with C gives (a/2,b/2). Averaging B with D gives the same point.

    Thus each diagonal has the same midpoint; this is precisely diagonal bisection. Their slopes instead have opposite signs.

    MAC=(a/2,b/2)M_{AC}=(a/2,b/2)MAC​=(a/2,b/2) MBD=(a/2,b/2).M_{BD}=(a/2,b/2).MBD​=(a/2,b/2).

    A nondegenerate triangle has A(0,0), B(2a,0), C(2b,2c), with a,c positive. Midpoints M of AC and N of BC are joined. Which condition on b is needed for the midsegment proof?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    MN=(a+b)−bMN=(a+b)-bMN=(a+b)−b MN=a.MN=a.MN=a.

    Their common positive height ensures a line distinct from AB, whatever the real value of b.

    A proof aims to show that every parallelogram has diagonals that bisect each other. Which starting coordinate model includes all nondegenerate parallelograms after choosing axes?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    A horizontal side can have arbitrary positive length a. The adjacent side can have any horizontal change b and any positive vertical change c after orienting the axes.

    The other vertices keep the same side changes. Forcing b to zero would restrict the proof to rectangles.

    C=(a+b,c).C=(a+b,c).C=(a+b,c).
