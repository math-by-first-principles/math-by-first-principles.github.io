Medians and the Centroid

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Watch the video explainer

Intuition

Join each corner of a triangle to the middle of the opposite side. Two of these segments cross, but the third also passes through that crossing. The meeting point has a fixed position on every one of the three segments, even when the triangle is stretched into a very different shape.

Learning goals

  • Construct a median by locating the midpoint of the opposite side
  • Prove that three medians meet using midsegments, similarity, and a unique division point
  • Calculate median lengths while distinguishing the whole segment from its two parts
  • Compare the balance interpretation for a uniform sheet with other material distributions

Join a vertex to the opposite midpoint

A median of a triangle is the segment from a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. For example, if a side is 14 cm long, its midpoint is 7 cm from each endpoint. Joining that midpoint to the opposite vertex makes a median. There is one median from each of the three vertices.

The word midpoint describes where the median ends. It does not say that the median meets the side at a right angle or divides the vertex angle equally. Those are separate conditions.

Worked example 1 Construct the median from one vertex

Problem. Given a nondegenerate triangle ABCABCABC, construct its median from AAA using a compass and unmarked straightedge. Explain why the constructed segment is a median.

Solution. Construct the perpendicular bisector of BCBCBC, using the construction from the first lesson of this chapter. Set the compass to the same radius from BBB and from CCC, greater than half of BCBCBC. The two pairs of arcs meet at distinct points on opposite sides of BCBCBC.

Draw the line through those arc intersections. It meets BCBCBC at its midpoint DDD, because the perpendicular-bisector construction bisects the segment.

Draw segment ADADAD. Its endpoints are the vertex AAA and the midpoint of the opposite side, so it is the required median. The perpendicular bisector was a tool for finding DDD; it is not generally the median ADADAD.

Check your understanding

In triangle PQRPQRPQR, point MMM lies on side QRQRQR. Which fact makes segment PMPMPM a median?

Answer choices

The endpoint M must be the midpoint of the opposite side. Since M is on QR, equal lengths QM and MR establish exactly that condition.

Two medians give a fixed division

The crossing of two medians divides each into a longer vertex part and a shorter midpoint part. For a median of length 12 cm, the parts will be 8 cm from the vertex and 4 cm to the side midpoint. The longer part contains two copies of the shorter part, so the whole contains three. We will prove why the same split occurs in every triangle.

Let DDD be the midpoint of BCBCBC and EEE the midpoint of CACACA. Draw medians ADADAD and BEBEBE, meeting at GGG. Join DDD to EEE.

The midsegment theorem says that the segment joining two side midpoints is parallel to the remaining side and half as long. Thus DEDEDE is parallel to ABABAB. That parallel gives equal angles in the two small triangles, and their angles at GGG are vertical angles. Similarity transfers the factor of two from ABABAB and DEDEDE to the median parts.

Why two medians divide each other in a two-to-one ratio#

First follow the argument with lengths. Suppose ABABAB is 18 cm. The midsegment DEDEDE is then 9 cm. Parallel angles and the vertical angles at GGG give similar triangles AGBAGBAGB and DGEDGEDGE. The large-to-small factor is 18/918/918/9, or 2, so the vertex parts of both medians are twice their midpoint parts. If ADADAD is 15 cm, its three ratio parts are each 5 cm, giving a 10 cm vertex part. Nothing in that reasoning depends on 18 or 15.

For the general proof, assume A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C are not collinear. Because DDD is strictly between B,CB,CB,C, the line ADADAD puts BBB and CCC on opposite sides. The interior point EEE of ACACAC is on the same side of ADADAD as CCC. Thus segment BEBEBE crosses ADADAD. Both segments lie in the triangle, and they cannot meet at a boundary endpoint, so their crossing GGG is strictly inside both medians.

The midsegment theorem gives

DE∥AB.DE\parallel AB.DE∥AB.

It also gives

AB=2DE.AB=2DE.AB=2DE.

The parallel lines give congruent alternate interior angles GABGABGAB and GDEGDEGDE. The angles AGBAGBAGB and DGEDGEDGE are vertical angles. Therefore AA gives

△AGB∼△DGE.\triangle AGB\sim\triangle DGE.△AGB∼△DGE.

These are genuine triangles. The interior crossing GGG is not on ABABAB, and the line DEDEDE meets ADADAD only at DDD, not at GGG. Their corresponding sides have the same factor, so

AGGD=2.\frac{AG}{GD}=2.GDAG​=2.

Similarly,

BGGE=2.\frac{BG}{GE}=2.GEBG​=2.

All four lengths are positive because GGG is strictly inside the two medians. On ADADAD, segment addition now gives

AD=AG+GDAD=3GD.\begin{aligned} AD&=AG+GD\\ AD&=3GD. \end{aligned}ADAD​=AG+GD=3GD.​

The vertex part is twice the shorter part, not twice the whole median.

∎

Check your understanding

In the proof configuration, why are triangles AGB and DGE similar?

Answer choices

DE is parallel to AB by the midsegment theorem. This supplies a matching angle pair, and the angles at G are vertical angles. These two pairs establish AA similarity.

Why the third median meets them

Three or more lines are concurrent when they pass through one common point. The midpoint conditions force concurrence here; it is not an extra fact you must assume from the drawing.

Why the third median passes through the same point#

In the numerical proof above, a median of length 15 cm has just one interior point 10 cm from its vertex. Two different crossings cannot both occupy that position.

In general, let FFF be the midpoint of ABABAB. Draw the third median CFCFCF, and call its crossing with ADADAD point HHH. The same opposite-side argument used for the first pair puts HHH strictly inside both segments.

Apply the preceding proof to medians ADADAD and CFCFCF, using midsegment DFDFDF parallel to ACACAC. It gives

AH=2HD.AH=2HD.AH=2HD.

Segment addition gives

HD=13AD.HD=\frac13 AD.HD=31​AD.

But the first crossing also satisfies

GD=13AD.GD=\frac13 AD.GD=31​AD.

Both GGG and HHH lie on the same segment from DDD toward AAA, at the same distance from DDD. There is only one such point, so HHH is GGG. The third median therefore passes through GGG, and the repeated two-median proof also gives

CG=2GF.CG=2GF.CG=2GF.

∎

The centroid of a nondegenerate triangle is this common point of its three medians. It is always inside the triangle, including when the triangle has a right or obtuse angle. On every median, the order is vertex, centroid, side midpoint, and the ratio in that order is 2:12:12:1.

Use Back and Next to construct each median. Drag A or change the base angles; BC stays fixed at 6 units. Compare AG with GD before comparing the other two vertex-to-centroid and centroid-to-midpoint pairs. The lengths change while the ordered ratios illustrate the proof above.

Worked example 2 Use uniqueness rather than the appearance of a crossing

Problem. In triangle ABCABCABC, D,E,FD,E,FD,E,F are the midpoints of BC,CA,ABBC,CA,ABBC,CA,AB. The median ADADAD has length 21 cm. Let GGG be the crossing of ADADAD and BEBEBE, and HHH the crossing of ADADAD and CFCFCF. Use the two-median result to explain why GGG and HHH are the same point.

Solution. Applied to the first pair, the result gives

DG=7 cm.DG=7\text{ cm}.DG=7 cm.

Applied to the second pair, it gives

DH=7 cm.DH=7\text{ cm}.DH=7 cm.

Both points are on segment DADADA. The point 7 cm along that segment from DDD is unique, so the names GGG and HHH identify the same point. The preceding three-median figure shows the resulting configuration.

Check your understanding

Why is the uniqueness step needed after proving the two-median ratio?

Answer choices

The pairwise proof gives each crossing the same distance from the same endpoint on AD. Uniqueness then identifies the crossings, bringing the third median through the first crossing.

Keep the parts and the whole separate

If the shorter part of median ADADAD is ttt, its vertex part is 2t2t2t and its whole length is 3t3t3t. Thus

AG=23AD.AG=\frac23 AD.AG=32​AD.

The other part is

GD=13AD.GD=\frac13 AD.GD=31​AD.

Worked example 3 Recover a whole median from its vertex part

Problem. Triangle ABCABCABC has centroid GGG, and DDD is the midpoint of BCBCBC. If AGAGAG is 14 cm, find GDGDGD and ADADAD.

Solution. The given 14 cm is two ratio parts, so one part is

GD=14÷2GD=7 cm.\begin{aligned} GD&=14\div2\\ GD&=7\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}GDGD​=14÷2=7 cm.​

Add the two portions to recover the whole.

AD=14+7AD=21 cm.\begin{aligned} AD&=14+7\\ AD&=21\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}ADAD​=14+7=21 cm.​

Worked example 4 Check whether two given expressions are compatible

Problem. On median PMPMPM, point GGG is the centroid. The lengths, in centimeters, are

PG=3x+2.PG=3x+2.PG=3x+2.

The other portion is

GM=x+4.GM=x+4.GM=x+4.

Find xxx and the whole median PMPMPM. Check that both portions are positive.

Solution. The vertex portion is twice the midpoint portion.

3x+2=2(x+4)x=6.\begin{aligned} 3x+2&=2(x+4)\\ x&=6. \end{aligned}3x+2x​=2(x+4)=6.​

Substitute into each given expression.

PG=20 cm.PG=20\text{ cm}.PG=20 cm.

Also,

GM=10 cm.GM=10\text{ cm}.GM=10 cm.

Both are positive, and 20 is twice 10. Adding them gives

PM=30 cm.PM=30\text{ cm}.PM=30 cm.

Check your understanding

On median AD, G is the centroid. If GD is 5 cm, what is the length of AD?

Answer choices

GD is one of the three ratio parts in the whole median. Therefore AD is 3 times 5 cm, or 15 cm.

A balance point depends on the material

A uniform triangular sheet has its balance point at the centroid. Here uniform means the same mass in equally sized pieces everywhere in the thin sheet, with no extra weight attached. This describes material spread across the whole triangular region.

Imagine the sheet made from many narrow strips parallel to BCBCBC. Each strip has its middle on median ADADAD: a parallel cross-section has endpoints at the same fraction along the two sides, so their midpoint follows that median. A uniform strip balances at its middle. This explains physically why the sheet’s balance point lies on ADADAD. The same reasoning puts it on the other medians, so it is their intersection.

This is an explanation of the ideal physical model, not a new proof from area formulas. A real balancing experiment supplies evidence and may be affected by thickness, uneven material, or the support. The earlier similarity proof established the exact geometric ratio independently.

Worked example 5 Decide which balance claim the model supports

Problem. A uniform thin triangular sheet and a frame made only from uniform wire have the same scalene triangular outline. For which object does the sheet-balance interpretation guarantee balance at the intersection of the medians? Explain the difference.

Solution. The guarantee applies to the uniform sheet. Its material fills the whole triangular region with the same mass per equally sized piece.

The wire frame places material only along the boundary. The parallel-strip explanation for a filled sheet does not describe that object. Its balance point can differ, so the outline alone does not justify using the centroid as its center of mass. A sheet with an added weight also falls outside the uniform-sheet assumption.

Check your understanding

What additional information makes the centroid balance interpretation apply to a triangular cutout?

Answer choices

The balance statement concerns a uniform thin sheet filling the triangle. Orientation does not supply uniformity, and a wire boundary has a different mass distribution.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Median endpoint: find the opposite side midpoint. A right-angle mark or an angle-bisector mark alone does not establish a median.
  • Ratio direction: the part touching the vertex is twice the part touching the side midpoint.
  • Whole versus part: the whole median contains three ratio parts, not two.
  • Balance model: the centroid balance claim requires a uniform triangular sheet. A wire frame or added weight changes the material distribution.

Key takeaways

  • A median joins a vertex to the midpoint of its opposite side.
  • Midsegments and AA similarity prove the two-to-one division. A unique division point proves concurrence.
  • The centroid lies inside every nondegenerate triangle.
  • The centroid is two thirds of each median from the vertex and one third from the side midpoint.
  • A uniform thin triangular sheet balances at the centroid.

A bit of history (optional)

Some of Archimedes’ work survived inside a book that had been turned into something else. Medieval parchment was valuable, so older writing could be removed and the pages reused. In the manuscript now called the Archimedes Palimpsest, mathematical writing lay underneath a prayer book.

In 1906, the scholar Johan Ludvig Heiberg examined it and recognized works by Archimedes. He photographed pages and used them in a new edition of Archimedes’ writings. The surviving works included material from Equilibrium of Planes, a treatise concerned with balance.

The manuscript later suffered from missing pages and mold. In 1999 it was deposited at the Walters Art Museum for conservation and study. Researchers combined careful preservation with imaging to recover writing that ordinary light could barely reveal. The project’s history tells how this reused book became an important mathematical source.
