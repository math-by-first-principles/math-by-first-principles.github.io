Angle Bisectors and the Incenter: Practice

12 multiple-choice questions, progressively harder.

Level 3 · Challenge 0 / 12 answered
Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    Q lies outside angle UOV and is equally far from its two supporting side lines. What may be concluded from the interior-point converse?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The converse requires an interior point. Equal distances for an exterior point can put it on an external bisector instead.

  2. 2

    Point P has equal distances to the full side lines of an angle, but its foot on one line lies behind the vertex. Why must line distance be distinguished from distance to the side ray?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The whole line contains a backward extension that the chosen ray does not. If the foot is there, its perpendicular length is a line distance, while the closest ray point is the vertex.

  3. 3

    P is strictly inside triangle ABC and its perpendicular distances to all three side lines are equal. Which argument proves P is the incenter?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The equal distances put P on the internal bisectors at A and B, because P is inside both angles. Their unique intersection is the incenter.

  4. 4

    P is strictly inside triangle ABC and lies on the internal bisector at A. Which additional condition is sufficient to make P the incenter?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The additional equal distances put P on the internal bisector at B. Together with the bisector at A, that identifies the incenter. The first bisector also supplies equality with the distance to AC.

  5. 5

    Why do two different internal angle-bisector lines of a nondegenerate triangle determine at most one incenter?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The lines are distinct: a line containing both vertex A and vertex B would be AB itself, which cannot internally bisect their nonzero angles. Distinct intersecting lines have only one common point.

  6. 6

    A circle has center I and radius IF, where IF is perpendicular to line AB at F. Why can no other point T on line AB lie on that circle?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    For T≠FT\ne FT=F, the nonzero leg FT gives

    IT2=IF2+FT2.IT^2=IF^2+FT^2.IT2=IF2+FT2.

    Thus IT is longer than the radius. Only the perpendicular foot can be on both the line and the circle.

  7. 7

    U and V are distinct first-arc points. Two later circles centered at U and V use equal radius k, where k is less than half of UV. What happens?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    If X lay on both circles, the two-segment path from U through X to V would have total length 2k. That is smaller than the direct distance UV here. A broken path cannot be shorter than the direct segment, so there is no intersection.

  8. 8

    A circle centered at an interior point P of a nondegenerate triangle ABC lies inside the triangle and touches AB and AC. A circle with the same center is drawn to touch AB and BC while staying inside the triangle. What must follow?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Each circle lies inside the triangle and touches AB, so each radius equals the perpendicular distance from P to AB. The circles therefore have the same center and radius and coincide.

    The first circle also reaches AC and the second reaches BC. Thus P has equal perpendicular distances to all three side lines.

    Because P is inside the triangle, the interior-point converse at A and B places it on both internal bisectors. Their intersection is the incenter.

  10. 10

    In the converse proof, an interior P has a perpendicular foot at the vertex O on one side line. Why cannot its two perpendicular distances be equal?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The second foot cannot also be O in an angle strictly below 180 degrees. Its right triangle has hypotenuse OP, so its perpendicular leg is shorter than the first distance OP.

  11. 11

    A point inside triangle ABC is equally far from AB and AC but is closer to BC. Which conclusion is justified?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The converse at A applies to the equal pair. An incenter would have all three perpendicular distances equal, so the unequal third distance rules it out.

  12. 12

    What supplies the geometric reason that one compass setting reaches all three sides in the incircle construction?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Two internal bisectors link the three perpendicular side distances. The converse puts their intersection on the third bisector, and the shared distance becomes the radius.
