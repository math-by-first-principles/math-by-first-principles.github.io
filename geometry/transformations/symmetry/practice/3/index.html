  1. 1

    A figure has finite rotational order 14 about O, counting identity. Must it have point symmetry about O?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The order is even, so half of the 14 equally spaced steps reach half of a full turn.

    14÷2=714\div2=714÷2=7

    That matching 180∘180^\circ180∘ rotation gives point symmetry.

  2. 2

    A figure has finite rotational order 15 about O. Which candidate angle must match?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Its least turn is found from the full turn.

    360∘÷15=24∘360^\circ\div15=24^\circ360∘÷15=24∘

    The candidate 72∘72^\circ72∘ is three least turns. None of the other listed angles is an integer multiple of 24∘24^\circ24∘.

  3. 3

    A report says a figure has finite rotational order and least positive matching angle 100∘100^\circ100∘. Which conclusion follows?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A least matching angle must divide a full turn exactly.

    360÷100=3.6360\div100=3.6360÷100=3.6

    Order counts distinct rotations and must be an integer. Rounding would change the claimed match.

  4. 4

    A circle has a single marked point on its boundary, and the mark is part of the figure. Which statement about rotations around the circle center is true?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The unmarked circle matches any center rotation, but a nonidentity turn moves the unique mark to an unmarked position. The whole decorated figure therefore has order 1.

  5. 5

    The boundary of a square is symmetric under a half-turn. A short segment is then added from its center to the midpoint of its top side only. Does the new figure keep point symmetry about the center?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A symmetry must include the added segment. Its image extends from the center toward the bottom side, where the figure contains no such segment. Matching the outer boundary is insufficient.

  6. 6

    For a finite-order figure, matching angles include 60∘60^\circ60∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘. No turn strictly between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 60∘60^\circ60∘ matches. What is its order and point-symmetry status?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The least positive angle is 60∘60^\circ60∘.

    360÷60=6360\div60=6360÷60=6

    The given matching half-turn also directly establishes point symmetry.

  7. 7

    A triangular outline joins (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), (6,0)(6,0)(6,0), and (0,2)(0,2)(0,2). A half-turn about (3,0)(3,0)(3,0) swaps the first two vertices. Is that enough to establish point symmetry?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The point (0,2) is three units left and two above the center, so its image is (6,-2). That point lies below the outline. The first two matches do not rescue the failed third one.

  8. 8

    An exact figure has finite order 9 about O, including identity. Which statement explains why it has no point symmetry about O?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The least angle is 40∘40^\circ40∘.

    180÷40=4.5180\div40=4.5180÷40=4.5

    All matching angles are integer multiples of the least angle. A half-turn is not among them.

  9. 9

    A candidate line divides a figure into equal areas. What extra fact would establish that it is a line of symmetry?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Area equality does not describe where the points or boundaries go. The required additional fact is exact whole-figure matching under reflection in that line.

  10. 10

    For a circle centered at O with positive radius, why does the finite-order least-angle formula fail?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Every rotation about O preserves the circle, including arbitrarily small positive angles. There is no finite order to put into the formula and no least positive angle to find.

  11. 11

    A square boundary has letter labels at its corners used only as names. A quarter-turn moves each label name to a different named corner. Does that alone reject the square symmetry?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The task explicitly excludes the labels from the figure. A symmetry may permute named corners while matching the boundary. If actual printed decorations were included, they too would need to match.

  12. 12

    A zero translation and a full-turn rotation are both recorded as symmetries of the same figure. How many distinct plane mappings do these two records describe?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Both send every point to itself. They are two descriptions of the same identity mapping, so they contribute only one distinct symmetry.
