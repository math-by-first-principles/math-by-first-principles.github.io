Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    What must a symmetry of a figure do?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    A symmetry may exchange points, but every part of the image must cover the original figure exactly. A single matching corner leaves the rest untested.

  2. 2

    An unmarked rectangle has four right angles, width 10, and height 4. How many lines of symmetry does it have?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The horizontal and vertical center folds each match the whole rectangle. A diagonal would pair a length-10 side with a length-4 side, which reflection cannot do. No other mirror pairs its corners and sides.

  3. 3

    An unmarked square has four right angles and four equal sides. What is its rotational order about its center, including identity once?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The matching angles are 0∘0^\circ0∘, 90∘90^\circ90∘, 180∘180^\circ180∘, and 270∘270^\circ270∘. These are four distinct rotations; 360∘360^\circ360∘ repeats identity.

  4. 4

    Which turn defines point symmetry about O?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Point symmetry means a half-turn about the named point matches the whole figure. A quarter-turn or reflection is a different test.

  5. 5

    Why are 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 360∘360^\circ360∘ counted only once when finding rotational order?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Both leave every point at its starting position. Order counts distinct mappings in a full turn, so the same identity mapping is counted once.

  6. 6

    A figure has rotational order 1 about C, counting identity. What does this tell you?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Order 1 means identity is the sole matching rotation about C. It does not describe a nontrivial rotational symmetry or determine the reflection symmetries.

  7. 7

    A figure has finite rotational order 5 about O, including identity. Find its least positive matching angle.

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Five equal least turns complete one full turn.

    360∘÷5=72∘360^\circ\div5=72^\circ360∘÷5=72∘

  8. 8

    Why does a quarter-turn about the center fail for an unmarked rectangle of width 10 and height 4?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The quarter-turn produces a rectangle of width 4 and height 10 in the same plane. The long sides occupy different positions, so the whole boundary does not match.

  9. 9

    How many lines of symmetry does an unmarked circle of positive radius have?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Every line through the center reflects the fixed-radius condition onto itself. There are infinitely many such directions. An offset line moves the circle to a different center.

  10. 10

    Which describes the least positive matching rotation of an unmarked circle about its center?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Every positive turn matches the circle. Halving any proposed least positive angle gives a smaller positive matching angle. Zero is not positive.

  11. 11

    For the exact block T outline shown, which candidate reflection matches the whole figure?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The vertical fold pairs the left and right parts of the top bar and the two sides of the stem. The horizontal fold sends the wide top bar to a wide bottom bar that is absent.

  12. 12

    A half-turn exchanges opposite corners of a rectangle and maps every side onto an original side. Which conclusion is correct?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Symmetry concerns the set of positions occupied by the entire figure. Points can exchange places, so a matching figure need not have all of its points fixed.
