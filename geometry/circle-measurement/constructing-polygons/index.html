Inscribed Regular-Polygon Constructions

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Watch the video explainer

Intuition

The circle is already drawn. Your job is to place a polygon’s corners exactly on its boundary. A compass can carry one distance from corner to corner, and each retained arc records why a new point belongs there. The drawing becomes convincing when those distance facts explain why the corners close up and the polygon is regular.

Learning goals

  • Construct a regular hexagon in a given circle by retaining a fixed compass opening
  • Justify closure and regularity using equal chords and central angles
  • Derive an inscribed equilateral triangle from alternating hexagon vertices
  • Build an inscribed square using compass-constructed perpendicular diameters
  • Calculate exact lengths and areas from the justified constructions

Start with the given circle

An inscribed polygon has all its vertices on a circle. Its sides are straight chords; the curved arcs between the vertices are not polygon sides. A regular polygon has equal sides and equal interior angles.

Throughout this lesson, the given circle has a known center OOO and positive radius rrr. Choose any point AAA on its boundary. The construction tools let us retain a compass opening and join distinct points with an unmarked straightedge. A measured angle or a shape that merely looks symmetric does not supply a compass construction.

Step the radius to construct a hexagon

Set the compass opening to OAOAOA. Keep that opening throughout the following steps, and keep the compass arcs visible.

First crossing. With center AAA and opening OAOAOA, draw arcs cutting the given circle at BBB and FFF. Choose BBB on one side of line OAOAOA to begin traveling around the circle. The two circles have equal positive radius rrr and their centers are rrr apart, so they intersect in two distinct points, one on each side of OAOAOA.

Continue the same way. With center BBB and the same opening OAOAOA, mark CCC at the intersection with the given circle other than AAA.

With center CCC and opening OAOAOA, mark DDD at the intersection other than BBB.

With center DDD and opening OAOAOA, mark EEE at the intersection other than CCC.

With center EEE and opening OAOAOA, select the intersection other than DDD. It is the point FFF already found from AAA.

Finally, an arc centered at FFF with opening OAOAOA returns to AAA at its intersection other than EEE. Join consecutive vertices A,B,C,D,E,FA,B,C,D,E,FA,B,C,D,E,F, then join FFF to AAA.

The claims that the fifth step reaches the original FFF and the sixth returns to AAA need reasons. Six marks that happen to look evenly spaced are not enough.

Why the steps close and produce a regular hexagon#

For a circle of radius 444 cm, the first compass crossing gives AB=4AB=4AB=4 cm. Also OA=4OA=4OA=4 cm and OB=4OB=4OB=4 cm, so triangle OABOABOAB is equilateral. Its angle at OOO is 60∘60^\circ60∘.

The same argument works for every r>0r>0r>0. The given circle supplies the two radii, and the compass arc supplies the new chord:

OA=rOB=rAB=r.\begin{gathered} OA=r\\ OB=r\\ AB=r. \end{gathered}OA=rOB=rAB=r.​

Thus every successive center triangle is equilateral and each step advances 60∘60^\circ60∘ around OOO. At each vertex the two possible intersections lie 60∘60^\circ60∘ in opposite directions from its radius. Excluding the previous vertex selects the forward intersection.

The first five forward turns are less than a full turn, so their points are distinct. After five turns, the radius points toward the other first crossing FFF. After six turns,

6⋅60∘=360∘.6\cdot60^\circ=360^\circ.6⋅60∘=360∘.

The ray has returned to OAOAOA, which meets the given circle at the unique point AAA. This proves closure. The six equal chords have length rrr. At each vertex the two adjacent equilateral center triangles contribute two 60∘60^\circ60∘ angles, giving a 120∘120^\circ120∘ interior angle. The vertices occur in order around the circle, so the joined polygon is convex and has six equal sides and equal angles. It is a regular hexagon.

Worked example 1 Find the perimeter of the constructed hexagon

Problem. A given circle has radius 5.55.55.5 cm. Construct its inscribed regular hexagon with a compass and unmarked straightedge. State the opening used for every forward step and find the hexagon’s perimeter.

Solution. Choose AAA on the circle and set the opening to OAOAOA, or 5.55.55.5 cm. Move the compass point successively to A,B,C,D,E,FA,B,C,D,E,FA,B,C,D,E,F, each time choosing the forward circle intersection as described above. Join the six consecutive vertices. The closure proof makes this a regular hexagon, and each side has the retained opening as its length.

s=5.5 cmP=6(5.5)P=33 cm.\begin{aligned} s&=5.5\text{ cm}\\ P&=6(5.5)\\ P&=33\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}sPP​=5.5 cm=6(5.5)=33 cm.​

A compass has advanced four successive radius-length chords around a circle in one direction. What total turn have their four center angles made?

Answer choices

Each radius-length chord forms an equilateral triangle with the center. Four forward steps therefore give

4(60∘)=240∘.4(60^\circ)=240^\circ.4(60∘)=240∘.

This is the accumulated turn, not the smaller angle between the first and last radii.

Use alternate hexagon vertices for a triangle

Keep the justified hexagon ABCDEFABCDEFABCDEF. Join AAA to CCC, CCC to EEE, and EEE to AAA. Every new side skips exactly one hexagon vertex. No new compass opening is needed. The retained hexagon arcs show how all three selected vertices were located on the given circle.

Why alternate vertices give an equilateral triangle#

Two neighboring 60∘60^\circ60∘ center angles combine to give

60∘+60∘=120∘.60^\circ+60^\circ=120^\circ.60∘+60∘=120∘.

The three center angles AOCAOCAOC, COECOECOE, and EOAEOAEOA are therefore equal. In a circle of radius 666 cm, each of these center triangles has two sides of 666 cm and an included 120∘120^\circ120∘ angle, so SAS makes them congruent.

For any positive circle radius, the triangles AOCAOCAOC, COECOECOE, and EOAEOAEOA likewise have equal pairs of radii and the same included 120∘120^\circ120∘ angle. SAS makes them congruent, so their opposite chords ACACAC, CECECE, and EAEAEA are equal. These three distinct vertices lie on the given circle, making ACEACEACE an inscribed equilateral triangle. Its three equal angles are 60∘60^\circ60∘ by the triangle angle sum.

Worked example 2 Recover the triangle side from the circle

Problem. Construct an equilateral triangle inside a given circle of radius 777 cm by using alternate hexagon vertices. Find its exact side length and perimeter.

Solution. Step the 777 cm radius around the given circle to make ABCDEFABCDEFABCDEF, then join ACACAC, CECECE, and EAEAEA. The proof above establishes the triangle’s equal sides.

Let MMM be the midpoint of ACACAC. In isosceles triangle AOCAOCAOC, the segment OMOMOM bisects the 120∘120^\circ120∘ center angle and is perpendicular to ACACAC. Each right half is a 303030-606060-909090 triangle with hypotenuse 777 cm. Its shorter leg is OMOMOM, while its longer leg is half of ACACAC.

OM=72 cmAM=732 cmAC=73 cmP=213 cm.\begin{aligned} OM&=\frac72\text{ cm}\\ AM&=\frac{7\sqrt3}{2}\text{ cm}\\ AC&=7\sqrt3\text{ cm}\\ P&=21\sqrt3\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}OMAMACP​=27​ cm=273​​ cm=73​ cm=213​ cm.​

Check your understanding

Triangle ACE uses alternate vertices of constructed hexagon ABCDEF. Which fact establishes that AC and CE have equal lengths?

Answer choices

Each chord spans two hexagon steps, giving equal included angles:

2(60∘)=120∘.2(60^\circ)=120^\circ.2(60∘)=120∘.

The two pairs of radii also match, so SAS makes center triangles AOC and COE congruent. Their corresponding chords are equal.

Construct a square using perpendicular diameters

Begin a fresh construction in the given circle. Draw a line through OOO and call its circle intersections AAA and CCC. Thus ACACAC is a diameter, with OOO its midpoint.

Set the compass opening to the full length ACACAC. Draw arcs centered at AAA above and below ACACAC. With center CCC and the same opening ACACAC, draw arcs crossing them at UUU and VVV. These are genuine compass-circle intersections; UUU and VVV are not points estimated above and below the center.

The opening ACACAC is greater than half of ACACAC, so the two equal circles intersect in two distinct points. Draw line UVUVUV. Call its intersections with the given circle BBB and DDD. Finally join ABABAB, BCBCBC, CDCDCD, and DADADA.

This uses the earlier perpendicular-bisector construction. Retaining its arcs makes the reason for the second diameter visible.

Why the constructed quadrilateral is a square#

If the given circle has radius 333 cm, then AC=6AC=6AC=6 cm. Each helper-circle intersection is 666 cm from both AAA and CCC, so both lie on the perpendicular bisector of ACACAC.

For any positive radius the same equal-distance argument applies. Points UUU and VVV each lie on both helper circles, giving

UA=UCVA=VC.\begin{gathered} UA=UC\\ VA=VC. \end{gathered}UA=UCVA=VC.​

The perpendicular-bisector converse puts both points on that bisector. Since UUU and VVV are distinct, their line is the bisector. It passes through the known midpoint OOO, so BDBDBD is a diameter perpendicular to ACACAC.

The four center angles are 90∘90^\circ90∘. Their center triangles have equal radii and equal included angles, so SAS gives four equal chords. Each interior angle of ABCDABCDABCD subtends a diameter: for example, angle ABCABCABC subtends ACACAC. The angle in a semicircle is a right angle, so all four interior angles are right angles. The four vertices occur in order around the circle. Thus ABCDABCDABCD is an inscribed square.

Worked example 3 Construct a square from a given diameter

Problem. A circle has diameter 151515 cm and known center OOO. Construct its inscribed square using a compass and unmarked straightedge, and find its exact side length and perimeter.

Solution. Draw diameter ACACAC. Use opening ACACAC, or 151515 cm, at AAA and then CCC to obtain the two helper intersections U,VU,VU,V. Draw UVUVUV, mark B,DB,DB,D on the given circle, and join consecutive vertices. The equal-distance proof establishes perpendicular diameters and hence a square.

Its diagonal ACACAC is the hypotenuse of right isosceles triangle ABCABCABC. For positive square side sss,

2s2=152s=1522 cmP=302 cm.\begin{aligned} 2s^2&=15^2\\ s&=\frac{15\sqrt2}{2}\text{ cm}\\ P&=30\sqrt2\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}2s2sP​=152=2152​​ cm=302​ cm.​

The 151515 cm compass opening locates the helper intersections. It is the diameter, not the square’s side length.

Equal compass arcs centered at the endpoints of a diameter meet at U and V. What establishes that UV passes through the circle center?

Answer choices

Both intersections have equal distances to the diameter endpoints. Their line is the perpendicular bisector, and the circle center O is the diameter midpoint. Thus

O∈UV.O\in UV.O∈UV.

The endpoints and the two distinct crossings supply the geometric reason.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

Keep the compass opening fixed while stepping the hexagon; equal-looking gaps are not evidence of equal chords. At each new vertex choose the circle intersection other than the preceding vertex so the construction continues forward.

For the triangle, join alternate hexagon vertices. Three consecutive hexagon vertices do not create three equal central angles. For the square, retain the equal-radius helper arcs and draw their intersection line; a sketched right angle is not the required perpendicular construction.

Use the polygon’s straight sides for perimeter and its perpendicular apothem for 12Pa\tfrac12 Pa21​Pa. Neither a curved arc nor a slanted radius can replace them.

Key takeaways

The compass creates equal distances. Equilateral center triangles make six radius steps close into a regular hexagon; alternate vertices then give an equilateral triangle. Equal-distance helper crossings construct perpendicular diameters and hence a square. Once each shape is justified, right triangles connect the given circle radius to its side lengths, apothem, and area.

A bit of history (optional)

Euclid’s Elements, Book IV, Proposition 15 asks for an equilateral and equiangular hexagon inside a given circle. Its construction uses a different economy of marks from our six successive compass steps. After drawing a diameter, Euclid draws one additional circle centered at a diameter endpoint, with opening equal to the original radius. Lines through the original center and the new circle intersections locate the remaining vertices.

The proof then returns to those same marks. Equal radii make an equilateral center triangle, and angle relationships spread its one-third-of-a-straight-angle turn around the circle. Equal arcs give equal sides. This is a useful feature of a construction diagram: its helper marks record facts that the later proof can use, rather than decoration that must be erased once the shape appears.
