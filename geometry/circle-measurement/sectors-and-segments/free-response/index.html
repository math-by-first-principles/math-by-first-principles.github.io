    Text description of this figure

    A sector is shaded. A circle has center O, endpoints A and B, radius 7 cm, and the indicated central angle 120°. The circular incidence and indicated angle are drawn accurately.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the selected angle with a full turn.

    2. Hint 2

      Apply that fraction separately to the disk area and the circumference.

    Answer

    A=49π3 cm2s=14π3 cm\begin{gathered}A=\frac{49\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^2\\s=\frac{14\pi}{3}\text{ cm}\end{gathered}A=349π​ cm2s=314π​ cm​

    Full solution

    The selected angle is one third of a full turn, so the sector receives one third of the disk area.

    A=13π(7)2A=49π3 cm2\begin{gathered}A=\frac13\pi(7)^2\\A=\frac{49\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^2\end{gathered}A=31​π(7)2A=349π​ cm2​

    s=13(2π⋅7)s=14π3 cm\begin{gathered}s=\frac13(2\pi\cdot7)\\s=\frac{14\pi}{3}\text{ cm}\end{gathered}s=31​(2π⋅7)s=314π​ cm​

    These givens describe radii 11 cm and 5 cm, so a positive inner circle fits inside the outer circle.

  4. Problem 4 A small cap

    Two radii of a radius-999 cm circle meet at 60∘60^\circ60∘. Find the area and complete perimeter of the minor circular segment cut off by the chord between their endpoints.

    Text description of this figure

    The minor circular segment is shaded. A circle has center O, endpoints A and B, radius 9 cm, and the indicated central angle 60°. The circular incidence and indicated angle are drawn accurately.

    1. Hint 1

      Separate the curved cap from the sector that contains it.

    2. Hint 2

      The two radii and the chord form an equilateral triangle.

    3. Hint 3

      Its altitude comes from a special right triangle.

    Answer

    A=(27π2−8134) cm2P=(9+3π) cm\begin{gathered}A=\left(\frac{27\pi}{2}-\frac{81\sqrt3}{4}\right)\text{ cm}^2\\P=(9+3\pi)\text{ cm}\end{gathered}A=(227π​−4813​​) cm2P=(9+3π) cm​

    Full solution

    The central triangle is equilateral, so its side is 9 cm and its altitude is half that side times the square root of three.

    h=932 cmA△=8134 cm2\begin{gathered}h=\frac{9\sqrt3}{2}\text{ cm}\\A_{\triangle}=\frac{81\sqrt3}{4}\text{ cm}^2\end{gathered}h=293​​ cmA△​=4813​​ cm2​

    The sector is one sixth of the disk.

    Subtract the triangle.

    Asector=27π2 cm2A=(27π2−8134) cm2\begin{gathered}A_{\text{sector}}=\frac{27\pi}{2}\text{ cm}^2\\A=\left(\frac{27\pi}{2}-\frac{81\sqrt3}{4}\right)\text{ cm}^2\end{gathered}Asector​=227π​ cm2A=(227π​−4813​​) cm2​

    The chord is one side of the equilateral triangle, and the selected arc is one sixth of the circumference.

    s=3π cmP=(9+3π) cm\begin{gathered}s=3\pi\text{ cm}\\P=(9+3\pi)\text{ cm}\end{gathered}s=3π cmP=(9+3π) cm​

    Answer

    A=(27π2−8134) cm2P=(9+3π) cm\begin{gathered}A=\left(\frac{27\pi}{2}-\frac{81\sqrt3}{4}\right)\text{ cm}^2\\P=(9+3\pi)\text{ cm}\end{gathered}A=(227π​−4813​​) cm2P=(9+3π) cm​

    The triangle removed for area still supplies the chord used in the segment perimeter.

  5. Problem 5 Keep the larger side of the chord

    A circle has radius 888 cm. The smaller central angle between the radii to two chord endpoints is 90∘90^\circ90∘.

    Find the area and complete perimeter of the major circular segment.

    Text description of this figure

    The major circular segment is shaded. A circle has center O, endpoints A and B, radius 8 cm, and the indicated central angle 90°. The circular incidence and indicated angle are drawn accurately.

    1. Hint 1

      Decide which of the two regions on either side of the chord is requested.

    2. Hint 2

      You can find the minor cap first and subtract it from the disk.

    3. Hint 3

      The major arc and minor arc share the same chord.

    Answer

    A=(48π+32) cm2P=(12π+82) cm\begin{gathered}A=(48\pi+32)\text{ cm}^2\\P=(12\pi+8\sqrt2)\text{ cm}\end{gathered}A=(48π+32) cm2P=(12π+82​) cm​

    Full solution

    The minor sector is a quarter disk and its central triangle is right.

    Aminor=14π(8)2−12(8)(8)Aminor=(16π−32) cm2\begin{gathered}A_{\text{minor}}=\frac14\pi(8)^2-\frac12(8)(8)\\A_{\text{minor}}=(16\pi-32)\text{ cm}^2\end{gathered}Aminor​=41​π(8)2−21​(8)(8)Aminor​=(16π−32) cm2​

    Subtract the minor cap from the disk to keep the major region.

    A=64π−(16π−32)A=(48π+32) cm2\begin{gathered}A=64\pi-(16\pi-32)\\A=(48\pi+32)\text{ cm}^2\end{gathered}A=64π−(16π−32)A=(48π+32) cm2​

    The major arc is three quarters of the circumference.

    The chord follows from the right triangle.

    s=12π cmc=82 cmP=(12π+82) cm\begin{gathered}s=12\pi\text{ cm}\\c=8\sqrt2\text{ cm}\\P=(12\pi+8\sqrt2)\text{ cm}\end{gathered}s=12π cmc=82​ cmP=(12π+82​) cm​

    Answer

    A=(48π+32) cm2P=(12π+82) cm\begin{gathered}A=(48\pi+32)\text{ cm}^2\\P=(12\pi+8\sqrt2)\text{ cm}\end{gathered}A=(48π+32) cm2P=(12π+82​) cm​

    Key idea

    Changing the chosen arc changes the segment area and curved boundary, but not the chord.

  6. Problem 6 Three lines of work

    A proper sector has radius 131313 cm and angle 2/52/52/5 radian. The following work attempts to find its complete perimeter.

    Line 1: s=13⋅25s=13\cdot\frac25s=13⋅52​ cm.

    Line 2: P=s+13P=s+13P=s+13 cm.

    Line 3: P=915P=\frac{91}{5}P=591​ cm.

    Identify the first invalid line, explain why it fails, and give the correct perimeter.

    Text description of this figure

    A sector is shaded. A circle has center O, endpoints A and B, radius 13 cm, and the indicated central angle 2/5 rad. The circular incidence and indicated angle are drawn accurately.

    1. Hint 1

      Trace a closed trip around the sector boundary.

    2. Hint 2

      Check how many straight radius-length edges that trip includes.

    Answer

    Line 2 is the first invalid line.

    P=1565 cm\begin{gathered}P=\frac{156}{5}\text{ cm}\end{gathered}P=5156​ cm​

    Full solution

    Line 1 correctly multiplies the radius by the radian angle.

    s=265 cm\begin{gathered}s=\frac{26}{5}\text{ cm}\end{gathered}s=526​ cm​

    Line 2 includes only one radius.

    The sector boundary has two radii, so this is the first invalid line.

    Line 3 is consistent with that mistaken setup, but it is not the complete perimeter.

    Add both radii to the arc length.

    P=26+265P=1565 cm\begin{gathered}P=26+\frac{26}{5}\\P=\frac{156}{5}\text{ cm}\end{gathered}P=26+526​P=5156​ cm​

    Answer

    Line 2 is the first invalid line.

    P=1565 cm\begin{gathered}P=\frac{156}{5}\text{ cm}\end{gathered}P=5156​ cm​

  7. Problem 7 Four corner cutouts

    A rectangular plate is 181818 cm by 141414 cm. At each corner, a quarter disk of radius 333 cm, centered at that corner, is removed. The four removed regions do not overlap.

    Find the remaining area and the complete perimeter after all four cuts. Give exact answers.

    Text description of this figure

    An 18 cm by 14 cm rectangle has a quarter disk of radius 3 cm removed at each corner. The four removed quarter disks do not overlap. The remaining region is shaded.

    1. Hint 1

      Account for all four removed regions when finding area.

    2. Hint 2

      For the perimeter, determine how much straight edge remains on each side.

    3. Hint 3

      The four new quarter-circle arcs together have the length of one full circumference.

    Answer

    A=(252−9π) cm2P=(40+6π) cm\begin{gathered}A=(252-9\pi)\text{ cm}^2\\P=(40+6\pi)\text{ cm}\end{gathered}A=(252−9π) cm2P=(40+6π) cm​

    Full solution

    The rectangle area is 252 square centimeters.

    The four removed quarter disks together have the area of a radius-3 disk.

    A=18⋅14−π(3)2A=(252−9π) cm2\begin{gathered}A=18\cdot14-\pi(3)^2\\A=(252-9\pi)\text{ cm}^2\end{gathered}A=18⋅14−π(3)2A=(252−9π) cm2​

    Each original side loses 3 cm at both ends.

    The remaining straight pieces total

    L=2(18−6)+2(14−6)L=40 cm\begin{gathered}L=2(18-6)+2(14-6)\\L=40\text{ cm}\end{gathered}L=2(18−6)+2(14−6)L=40 cm​

    The new arcs total one radius-3 circumference.

    s=6π cmP=(40+6π) cm\begin{gathered}s=6\pi\text{ cm}\\P=(40+6\pi)\text{ cm}\end{gathered}s=6π cmP=(40+6π) cm​

    Answer

    A=(252−9π) cm2P=(40+6π) cm\begin{gathered}A=(252-9\pi)\text{ cm}^2\\P=(40+6\pi)\text{ cm}\end{gathered}A=(252−9π) cm2P=(40+6π) cm​

    Several curved cuts can simplify in area while still requiring a new boundary inventory.

  8. Problem 8 A larger radius and a smaller turn

    A proper sector has positive radius rrr and angle θ\thetaθ radians, strictly between zero and 2π2\pi2π.

    A second sector has radius 2r2r2r and angle θ/2\theta/2θ/2.

    Compare their areas, selected arc lengths, and complete perimeters. Justify your conclusions for every allowed radius and angle.

    1. Hint 1

      Track the radius factor and angle factor separately.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the products in the area and arc-length formulas.

    3. Hint 3

      The perimeter also contains the two radius-length edges.

    Answer

    A2=2A1s2=s1P2−P1=2r\begin{gathered}A_2=2A_1\\s_2=s_1\\P_2-P_1=2r\end{gathered}A2​=2A1​s2​=s1​P2​−P1​=2r​

    Full solution

    For a concrete instance, take a radius of 6 units and an angle of 1 radian.

    The second sector then has radius 12 units and angle one half radian.

    A1=18A2=36s1=6s2=6P1=18P2=30\begin{gathered}A_1=18\\A_2=36\\s_1=6\\s_2=6\\P_1=18\\P_2=30\end{gathered}A1​=18A2​=36s1​=6s2​=6P1​=18P2​=30​

    For general positive radius and the stated radian angle, the squared-radius factor grows by four while the angle factor halves.

    A2=12(2r)2(θ2)A2=r2θA2=2A1\begin{gathered}A_2=\frac12(2r)^2\left(\frac\theta2\right)\\A_2=r^2\theta\\A_2=2A_1\end{gathered}A2​=21​(2r)2(2θ​)A2​=r2θA2​=2A1​​

    Arc length uses only the first power of radius, so its two scale factors cancel.

    s2=(2r)(θ2)s2=rθs2=s1\begin{gathered}s_2=(2r)\left(\frac\theta2\right)\\s_2=r\theta\\s_2=s_1\end{gathered}s2​=(2r)(2θ​)s2​=rθs2​=s1​​

    Both angles remain proper.

    Each sector keeps the same arc length, but the second has longer straight edges.

    P1=2r+s1P2=4r+s1P2−P1=2r\begin{gathered}P_1=2r+s_1\\P_2=4r+s_1\\P_2-P_1=2r\end{gathered}P1​=2r+s1​P2​=4r+s1​P2​−P1​=2r​

    Answer

    A2=2A1s2=s1P2−P1=2r\begin{gathered}A_2=2A_1\\s_2=s_1\\P_2-P_1=2r\end{gathered}A2​=2A1​s2​=s1​P2​−P1​=2r​

  9. Problem 9 Material around two holes

    A disk has radius 151515 cm. Two equal circular holes lie entirely inside it, do not touch each other, and have common positive radius rrr.

    The material left after removing both holes has area 207π cm2207\pi\text{ cm}^2207π cm2. Find rrr and the total length of every boundary of the material.

    Text description of this figure

    A disk of radius 15 cm contains two equal circular holes, entirely inside the disk and not touching each other. Their common radius r is unknown. The material outside the holes is shaded. Schematic; use the stated measurements.

    1. Hint 1

      The removed area belongs to two disks, not one.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the material area to find the common hole radius.

    3. Hint 3

      Count the outside circle and each hole circle separately.

    Answer

    r=3 cmP=42π cm\begin{gathered}r=3\text{ cm}\\P=42\pi\text{ cm}\end{gathered}r=3 cmP=42π cm​

    Full solution

    Subtract both equal hole areas from the outer disk area.

    225π−2πr2=207π2r2=18r=3 cm\begin{gathered}225\pi-2\pi r^2=207\pi\\2r^2=18\\r=3\text{ cm}\end{gathered}225π−2πr2=207π2r2=18r=3 cm​

    The positive root is required.

    Two holes of this radius can fit strictly inside the radius-15 disk without touching, as the statement requires.

    The total boundary consists of three circles.

    P=2π(15)+2(2π⋅3)P=42π cm\begin{gathered}P=2\pi(15)+2(2\pi\cdot3)\\P=42\pi\text{ cm}\end{gathered}P=2π(15)+2(2π⋅3)P=42π cm​

    Answer

    r=3 cmP=42π cm\begin{gathered}r=3\text{ cm}\\P=42\pi\text{ cm}\end{gathered}r=3 cmP=42π cm​

    Key idea

    Separate holes contribute separate boundary circles even when their areas were combined in one subtraction.

  10. Problem 10 Recover the inside of a quarter ring
