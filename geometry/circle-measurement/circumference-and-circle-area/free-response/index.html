  1. Problem 1 Three measurements from a diameter

    A circle has diameter 17/317/317/3 cm. Give exact results for each case.

    (a) Its radius.

  2. Problem 2 Recover a circle from its area

    d=203 cmd=20\sqrt3\text{ cm}d=203​ cm

    Multiply the radius by twice pi.

    C=20π3 cmC=20\pi\sqrt3\text{ cm}C=20π3​ cm

    Answer

  3. Problem 3 Two square enclosures

    A circle has radius 323\sqrt232​ cm. One square is inscribed in it. Another square has parallel sides tangent to the circle.

    (a) Find each square's perimeter and area.

    (b) Give strict lower and upper bounds for the circle circumference and disk area from those squares.

    1. Hint 1

      The inner square diagonal is a diameter; the outer square apothem is the radius.

    2. Hint 2

      A square diagonal is its side multiplied by square root 2.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare the circle separately with the straight perimeters and the polygon areas.

    Answer

    Inner: perimeter 24 cm, area 36 square cm. Outer: perimeter 24224\sqrt2242​ cm, area 72 square cm.

    Full solution

    (a) The diameter is 626\sqrt262​ cm, so the inner square has side 6 cm.

    Its perimeter is 24 cm and its area is 36 square cm.

    The outer square has apothem 323\sqrt232​ cm and side 626\sqrt262​ cm.

    Its perimeter is 24224\sqrt2242​ cm and its area is 72 square cm.

    (b) The circle lies between the two polygons.

    Its circumference is greater than the inner perimeter and less than the outer perimeter.

    24<C<24224<C<24\sqrt224<C<242​

    Its disk area is greater than 36 and less than 72 square cm.

    These are finite bounds, not an exact area calculation.

    Answer

    A polygon enclosure supplies separate bounds for curved length and enclosed area.

  5. Problem 5 A length factor and an area change

    Find the second disk's exact area and the percentage increase in area from the first disk.

    The increase is 72π72\pi72π square cm.

    Relative to 75π75\pi75π, it is

    72π75π=0.96\frac{72\pi}{75\pi}=0.9675π72π​=0.96

    This is a 96 percent increase.

    Answer

    Area 147π147\pi147π square cm; increase 96 percent.

    Key idea

    A fractional circumference factor must be squared before it is applied to disk area.

  6. Problem 6 Recover two radii

    Two circles have positive radii. The larger circumference exceeds the smaller by 4π4\pi4π cm. The larger disk area exceeds the smaller by 52π52\pi52π square cm. Find both radii.

    1. Hint 1

      Translate the two differences into equations involving the radii.

    2. Hint 2

      The difference of two squares factors as the difference times the sum.

    3. Hint 3

      Use the known difference and sum to recover the two positive lengths.

    Answer

    Larger radius 14 cm; smaller radius 12 cm.

    Full solution

    Let the larger and smaller radii, in centimeters, be R and r.

    The circumference difference gives

    2π(R−r)=4π2\pi(R-r)=4\pi2π(R−r)=4π

    Thus R−r=2R-r=2R−r=2.

    The area difference gives

    R2−r2=52R^2-r^2=52R2−r2=52

    Factor it and use the difference already found.

    2(R+r)=522(R+r)=522(R+r)=52

    Thus R+r=26R+r=26R+r=26.

    Adding the sum and difference gives 2R=282R=282R=28, so R is 14 cm.

    Then r is 12 cm.

    Their circumference difference is 4π4\pi4π cm, and their area difference is 52π52\pi52π square cm.

    Answer

    Larger radius 14 cm; smaller radius 12 cm.

    Key idea

    The two measurement differences reveal the difference and the sum of the radii.

    First: 6.28<x<6.296.28<x<6.296.28<x<6.29.

    Second: 6.283<x<6.2846.283<x<6.2846.283<x<6.284.

    Third: 6.2831<x<6.28326.2831<x<6.28326.2831<x<6.2832.

    Do these three rows alone force the exact equality x=6.28318x=6.28318x=6.28318? Give two different numerical possibilities that the rows do not distinguish, and explain what an exact exhaustion conclusion requires.

    1. Hint 1

      Test whether more than one number fits even the narrowest interval.

    2. Hint 2

      Choose two different numbers strictly between 6.2831 and 6.2832.

    3. Hint 3

      Distinguish an arbitrarily refinable enclosure from a fixed finite list.

    Answer

    No. For example, 6.28315 and 6.28319 both satisfy every row. Exact exhaustion requires valid bounds with arbitrarily small gaps around the quantity and the proposed exact value.

    Full solution

    The values 6.28315 and 6.28319 both lie strictly between 6.2831 and 6.2832, and therefore lie in the two wider intervals too.

    The three rows cannot distinguish them.

    Thus the table does not force the particular decimal 6.28318.

    It gives a finite precision enclosure.

    An exact exhaustion argument needs valid bounds that can be tightened past any prescribed positive error, with both the actual quantity and the proposed exact value inside.

    A fixed positive difference would then be ruled out by a narrower enclosure.

  8. Problem 8 An exact area from shrinking bounds

    A circle has positive radius rrr and circumference CCC. Regular inscribed polygons have perimeters PPP and apothems hhh. Parallel-sided tangent polygons have perimeters QQQ.

    As their arcs are repeatedly bisected, the perimeter gap can be made arbitrarily small, both perimeters approach C, and the apothems h approach r.

    Derive an exact expression for the disk area A using only C and r. Explain why a nonzero difference from your expression is impossible.

    1. Hint 1

      The disk area lies between the two polygon areas.

    2. Hint 2

      Express each polygon area through its perimeter and its perpendicular center-to-side distance.

    3. Hint 3

      Show that both polygon areas approach one value, and use the shrinking gap.

    Answer

    A=Cr/2A=Cr/2A=Cr/2.

    Full solution

    The inner polygon area is I=Ph/2I=Ph/2I=Ph/2, and the outer polygon area is U=Qr/2U=Qr/2U=Qr/2.

    The outer apothem is exactly r because its sides are tangent.

    The disk area lies between I and U.

    The inequalities P<C<QP<C<QP<C<Q and h<rh<rh<r also place the value Cr/2Cr/2Cr/2 between I and U.

    As refinement continues, both polygon areas approach Cr/2Cr/2Cr/2.

    Their gap can be made as small as desired, since

    Ep=r(Q−P)2,Eh=P(r−h)2,U−I=Ep+Eh.\begin{aligned}E_p&=\frac{r(Q-P)}2,\\E_h&=\frac{P(r-h)}2,\\U-I&=E_p+E_h.\end{aligned}Ep​Eh​U−I​=2r(Q−P)​,=2P(r−h)​,=Ep​+Eh​.​

    Here P remains bounded, and both differences shrink as far as required.

    If A differed from Cr/2Cr/2Cr/2 by a fixed positive amount, choose an enclosure narrower than that amount.

    Both numbers remain inside it, which is impossible for numbers that far apart.

    Therefore A=Cr/2A=Cr/2A=Cr/2 exactly.

    Answer

    A=Cr/2A=Cr/2A=Cr/2.

    Key idea

    Exact equality follows when every possible positive discrepancy is ruled out by a valid shrinking enclosure.

  9. Problem 9 A range of panel sizes

    A circular panel must cover at least 18π18\pi18π square meters. Its edge can use at most 10π10\pi10π meters of trim.

    Find all positive radii that meet both requirements. Give the range exactly and approximate its lower endpoint to the nearest hundredth of a meter.

    1. Hint 1

      Translate the area and boundary requirements separately.

    2. Hint 2

      For a positive radius, the area lower bound becomes a lower bound on r.

    3. Hint 3

      Use the intersection of the two allowed ranges.

    Answer

    32≤r≤53\sqrt2\le r\le532​≤r≤5 meters; the lower endpoint is about 4.24 meters.

    Full solution

    The area condition is

    πr2≥18π\pi r^2\ge18\piπr2≥18π

    Since r is positive, this requires

    r≥32.r\ge3\sqrt2.r≥32​.

    The trim condition is

    2πr≤10π2\pi r\le10\pi2πr≤10π

    It requires r≤5r\le5r≤5.

    Both conditions hold exactly when 32≤r≤53\sqrt2\le r\le532​≤r≤5, in meters.

    The lower endpoint rounds to 4.24 meters; the exact endpoint remains 323\sqrt232​, so 4.24 itself is not substituted for that exact bound.

    Answer
