Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    Two vertical objects are measured simultaneously, but their shadows lie on surfaces with different unknown slopes. Is the usual height-over-shadow proportion guaranteed?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The usual AA argument needs matching base and ray-to-ground angles. Different unknown slopes remove that justification. A special setup might still give the same numerical result, so the precise conclusion is not guaranteed.

  2. 2

    In a valid mirror setup, EF is eye height, TB object height, FM and MB are ground legs, and EM is a slanted sight line. Which proportion is supported?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    AA pairs TB with EF and MB with FM. The sight line EM is not interchangeable with a ground leg, and FB is the whole ground separation.

  3. 3

    In the crossed survey, A,B,C and T,B,D are collinear in order, while AT and CD are perpendicular to AC. Which ordered similarity states the correspondence correctly?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The inaccessible target T matches D, the right-angle vertex A matches C, and the crossing vertex B matches itself. Thus the order is TAB to DCB.

  4. 4

    In a valid crossed survey, the ratio of bank segments AB:BC is 7:3 and accessible perpendicular CD is 4.20 m. Find inaccessible perpendicular AT.

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    AA matches AT with CD and AB with CB. The ratio of inaccessible length AT to accessible length CD is seven thirds.

    AT=4.20⋅73,=9.80 m.\begin{aligned}AT&=4.20\cdot\frac 73,\\&=9.80\text{ m}.\end{aligned}AT​=4.20⋅37​,=9.80 m.​

  5. 5

    A print changes a 4.0 cm horizontal length to 5.0 cm and a 9.0 cm vertical length to 10.8 cm. Can one new similarity scale describe the whole print?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Compare corresponding lengths in both directions.

    khorizontal=5.0/4.0,=1.25,kvertical=10.8/9.0,=1.2.\begin{aligned}k_{\rm horizontal}&=5.0/4.0,\\&=1.25,\\k_{\rm vertical}&=10.8/9.0,\\&=1.2.\end{aligned}khorizontal​kvertical​​=5.0/4.0,=1.25,=10.8/9.0,=1.2.​

    A single similarity requires one common factor.

  6. 6

    Two similar triangular frames have original and image perimeters 72 and 108 cm. Corresponding sides are 2x+4 and 5x-1 cm. Find x.

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The known similarity makes the perimeter ratio the common length factor.

    k=108/72,=1.5,5x−1=1.5(2x+4),2x=7,x=3.5.\begin{aligned}k&=108/72,\\&=1.5,\\5x-1&=1.5(2x+4),\\2x&=7,\\x&=3.5.\end{aligned}k5x−12xx​=108/72,=1.5,=1.5(2x+4),=7,=3.5.​

    The corresponding side lengths are 11 and 16.5 cm, both positive.

  7. 7

    A measurement calculation gives 17.8625 m. The task asks for an estimate to the nearest tenth of a meter. Which report is appropriate?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Round the hundredths digit 6 upward into the tenths place. The result is approximately 17.9 m; a measured model does not gain exactness from a long decimal.

  8. 8

    A 5 cm model edge corresponds to a 12 m real edge. What is the factor mapping real lengths to model lengths in common units?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Express the real edge in centimeters before forming model divided by real.

    k=51200,=1240.\begin{aligned}k&=\frac 5{1200},\\&=\frac 1{240}.\end{aligned}k​=12005​,=2401​.​

  9. 9

    A mirror is horizontal and the modeled triangles have right angles, but no reflection-angle relation is supplied. What further premise does the usual AA argument need?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The right angles give only one pair. The second pair comes from the equal-angle reflection model, expressed with the mirror perpendicular or its complementary ground angles.

  10. 10

    An original triangular boundary has sides a,b,c. A similar image has corresponding sides ka,kb,kc for positive k. Which perimeter equation is justified?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Add the three image sides. Factoring the same multiplier from ka+kb+kc gives k(a+b+c).

  11. 11

    An original 1:6000 map is reprinted uniformly. The new print has scale 1:4000. What is new drawing length divided by old drawing length?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The real length stays fixed. Compare the new drawing-to-real ratio with the old one.

    LnewLold=1/40001/6000,=1.5.\begin{aligned}\frac{L_{\rm new}}{L_{\rm old}}&=\frac{1/4000}{1/6000},\\&=1.5.\end{aligned}Lold​Lnew​​​=1/60001/4000​,=1.5.​

  12. 12

    A simultaneous shadow check gives height/shadow ratios 2.4/1.8 and 4.0/3.0 for two calibration objects. What does their agreement show?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Both ratios agree.

    2.41.8=4.03.0,=43.\begin{aligned}\frac{2.4}{1.8}&=\frac{4.0}{3.0},\\&=\frac 43.\end{aligned}1.82.4​​=3.04.0​,=34​.​
