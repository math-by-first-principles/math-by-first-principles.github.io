  1. Problem 1 Calibrate a shadow with its own triangle

    A vertical calibration pole is 1.801.801.80 m tall and casts its own 1.401.401.40 m shadow. A vertical tree simultaneously casts its own 12.612.612.6 m shadow on the same level ground. Treat the sunrays as parallel.

    Estimate the tree height to the nearest tenth of a meter. Justify the angle correspondence before writing a proportion.

    Text description of this figure

    Separate schematic triangles show the calibration pole and tree, each with its own base-to-tip shadow. Only given measurements are labeled.

    1. Hint 1

      Which angles follow from vertical objects and level ground?

    2. Hint 2

      Use the parallel ray directions for a second angle pair, then match each height with its own shadow.

    Answer

    The tree is approximately 16.216.216.2 m tall.

    Full solution

    Both base angles are right angles.

    The parallel sunrays and level ground give equal acute angles at the shadow tips, so AA makes the triangles similar.

    The tree height matches the pole height, and the tree's own shadow matches the pole's own shadow.

    Use height over own shadow in both triangles.

    H12.6=1.801.40,H=16.2 m.\begin{aligned}\frac{H}{12.6}&=\frac{1.80}{1.40},\\H&=16.2\text{ m}.\end{aligned}12.6H​H​=1.401.80​,=16.2 m.​

    The model estimate is approximately 16.216.216.2 m to the requested tenth.

    No distance between the two object bases enters the proportion.

    Answer

    The tree is approximately 16.216.216.2 m tall.

    Key idea

    AA must justify the paired height and own-shadow measurements.

  2. Problem 2 Ground legs in a mirror measurement

    A small horizontal mirror MMM lies on level ground between observer foot FFF and vertical mast base BBB. The observer sees the mast top TTT through MMM, with eye EEE vertically above FFF. Assume equal-angle reflection and a common vertical sight plane.

    The eye height is

    EF=1.56 mFM=2.4 mMB=17.5 m.\begin{aligned}EF&=1.56\text{ m}\\FM&=2.4\text{ m}\\MB&=17.5\text{ m}.\end{aligned}EFFMMB​=1.56 m=2.4 m=17.5 m.​

    Estimate mast height TBTBTB to the nearest tenth of a meter. Name the two matching side pairs used.

    Text description of this figure

    The horizontal mirror M lies between observer foot F and object base B. Given labels mark eye height 1.56 and the two ground legs 2.4, 17.5; no sight-line length is supplied.

    1. Hint 1

      Right angles at F,B provide one pair; the reflection model provides the mirror-angle pair.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare object height with eye height and object ground leg with observer ground leg.

    Answer

    TB≈11.4TB\approx 11.4TB≈11.4 m. The pairs are TBTBTB with EFEFEF, and MBMBMB with FMFMFM.

    Full solution

    The right angles at F,BF,BF,B and the equal acute mirror angles establish △EFM∼△TBM\triangle EFM\sim\triangle TBM△EFM∼△TBM by AA.

    The matching is EEE to TTT, FFF to BBB, and MMM to MMM.

    Use the object triangle over the observer triangle.

    TB1.56=17.52.4,TB=11.375 m.\begin{aligned}\frac{TB}{1.56}&=\frac{17.5}{2.4},\\TB&=11.375\text{ m}.\end{aligned}1.56TB​TB​=2.417.5​,=11.375 m.​

  3. Problem 3 Measure across the river from the accessible bank

    In a top-view survey, A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C are collinear in that order on an accessible straight bank. Target TTT is across the river, with AT⊥ACAT\perp ACAT⊥AC established. A dry-land perpendicular CD⊥ACCD\perp ACCD⊥AC meets sight line TBTBTB extended, with T,B,DT,B,DT,B,D in order.

    Given

    AB=38.0 mBC=16.0 mCD=12.0 m\begin{aligned}AB&=38.0\text{ m}\\BC&=16.0\text{ m}\\CD&=12.0\text{ m}\end{aligned}ABBCCD​=38.0 m=16.0 m=12.0 m​

    , estimate perpendicular crossing ATATAT to the nearest tenth. Give the ordered similarity and explain why none of the measured inputs requires reaching TTT.

    Text description of this figure

    The schematic top view supplies both collinearities and the bank perpendiculars. Blue TAB and red dashed DCB encode the stated AA correspondence without showing a calculated crossing length.

    1. Hint 1

      The two bank perpendiculars give right angles; identify the crossing-angle pair at B.

    2. Hint 2

      AT matches CD, not the slanted line from T to B.

    Answer

    △TAB∼△DCB\triangle TAB\sim\triangle DCB△TAB∼△DCB, and AT≈28.5AT\approx 28.5AT≈28.5 m.

    Full solution

    The right angles at A,CA,CA,C and the vertical angles at BBB give △TAB∼△DCB\triangle TAB\sim\triangle DCB△TAB∼△DCB by AA.

    Thus ATATAT matches DCDCDC and ABABAB matches CBCBCB.

    The corresponding ratio gives

    All three inputs AB,BC,CDAB,BC,CDAB,BC,CD lie on the accessible bank or dry land.

    Sight-line alignment locates DDD without measuring ATATAT or TBTBTB.

    The requested crossing is the perpendicular ATATAT, not slanted TBTBTB.

    Answer

    △TAB∼△DCB\triangle TAB\sim\triangle DCB△TAB∼△DCB, and AT≈28.5AT\approx 28.5AT≈28.5 m.

  4. Problem 4 Follow a map route out and back

    A uniform map uses scale 1:240001:240001:24000. A three-leg walking route has map lengths 2.02.02.0, 3.253.253.25, and 3.53.53.5 cm in order. The return trip follows the same route.

    Find the one-way route length and total out-and-back length in kilometers. Explain why a straight line from the start to the end is not the requested measurement.

    Text description of this figure

    A three-leg schematic route has lengths 2.0, 3.25, 3.5 cm on a uniform 1:24000 map. The drawn endpoint gap is not a route segment.

    1. Hint 1

      Add all three mapped route legs before applying the common scale.

    2. Hint 2

      Convert the real centimeter total into kilometers, then account for the return trip.

    Answer

    The one-way route is 2.12.12.1 km; the out-and-back total is 4.24.24.2 km.

    Full solution

    The route follows all three legs.

    Lmap=2.0+3.25+3.5,=8.75 cm.\begin{aligned}L_{\rm map}&=2.0+3.25+3.5,\\&=8.75\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}Lmap​​=2.0+3.25+3.5,=8.75 cm.​

    Let LLL be the real one-way route length.

    Use the uniform real-per-drawing factor in common units.

    L=8.75⋅24000,=210000 cm,=2.1 km.\begin{aligned}L&=8.75\cdot 24000,\\&=210000\text{ cm},\\&=2.1\text{ km}.\end{aligned}L​=8.75⋅24000,=210000 cm,=2.1 km.​

    The return covers the same route, so the total is 2(2.1)=4.22(2.1)=4.22(2.1)=4.2 km.

    The direct endpoint gap would omit the bends and measure a different path.

    Answer

    The one-way route is 2.12.12.1 km; the out-and-back total is 4.24.24.2 km.

    Key idea

    Scale the length of the requested route, including every leg actually traveled.

  5. Problem 5 Recover a real boundary perimeter from a model

    A triangular model and a real triangular boundary are similar with a fixed correspondence. A 7.27.27.2 cm model edge matches a 2.42.42.4 m real edge. The model perimeter is 545454 cm.

    Find the factor mapping model lengths to real lengths in common units, then find the real perimeter in meters. Explain why the factor applies to the whole boundary sum.

    Text description of this figure

    Similar schematic triangular boundaries have a model perimeter 54 cm and matched edges 7.2 cm,2.4 m. The displayed size ratio is not measurement evidence.

    1. Hint 1

      Convert the real edge into centimeters before comparing it with the model edge.

    2. Hint 2

      Every corresponding boundary edge uses the same factor; add those scaled edges.

    Answer

    The model-to-real factor is 100/3100/3100/3; the real perimeter is 181818 m.

    Full solution

    Express both matched edges in centimeters.

    2.4 m=240 cm,k=2407.2,=1003.\begin{aligned}2.4\text{ m}&=240\text{ cm},\\k&=\frac{240}{7.2},\\&=\frac{100}{3}.\end{aligned}2.4 mk​=240 cm,=7.2240​,=3100​.​

    Each real boundary side is kkk times its matching model side.

    Adding those edges therefore multiplies the model perimeter by kkk.

    Preal=54⋅1003,=1800 cm,Preal=18 m.\begin{aligned}P_{\rm real}&=54\cdot\frac{100}{3},\\&=1800\text{ cm},\\P_{\rm real}&=18\text{ m}.\end{aligned}Preal​Preal​​=54⋅3100​,=1800 cm,=18 m.​

    Answer

    The model-to-real factor is 100/3100/3100/3; the real perimeter is 181818 m.

    Key idea

    A common length factor multiplies perimeter because all corresponding sides are added.

    1. Hint 1

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the observer ground leg before comparing the object and observer triangles.

    ; the corrected height is approximately 12.812.812.8 m.

    The whole separation FBFBFB crosses both triangles and is not the tower's ground leg.

    Since F,M,BF,M,BF,M,B are in order,

    MB=18.0−2.0,=16.0 m.\begin{aligned}MB&=18.0-2.0,\\&=16.0\text{ m}.\end{aligned}MB​=18.0−2.0,=16.0 m.​

    The model assumptions are supplied; the error lies in which measured ground length was assigned to the tower triangle.

    Answer

    MB=16.0 mFM=2.0 m\begin{aligned}MB&=16.0\text{ m}\\FM&=2.0\text{ m}\end{aligned}MBFM​=16.0 m=2.0 m​

  7. Problem 7 Compare two shadow records

    A vertical staff of height 2.002.002.00 m casts a 1.601.601.60 m shadow at noon. A vertical tree's own shadow is measured as 18.018.018.0 m later that afternoon. Both sites are level, but no evidence says the sunray angle remained unchanged.

    (a) Is the estimate 22.522.522.5 m from these two records guaranteed? Explain and propose a repair.

    (b) When the tree shadow is measured, the staff's simultaneous shadow is actually 2.402.402.40 m. Under the parallel-ray model at that time, estimate the tree height.

    1. Hint 1

      The usual AA argument needs the same ray-to-ground angle for the two records.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the calibration shadow recorded at the tree measurement time.

    Answer

    (a) The estimate is not guaranteed; measure the calibration shadow at the same time as the tree's. (b) The corrected estimate is approximately 15.015.015.0 m.

    Full solution

    Noon and afternoon need not have the same sunray angle.

    The original records therefore do not establish the acute-angle pair for AA.

    Their arithmetic value 22.522.522.5 m is not guaranteed, though missing evidence alone does not make every possible coincidence impossible.

    Repair the procedure by measuring both own shadows simultaneously on level ground with vertical objects.

    The staff's current shadow supplies the correct calibration.

    The repaired model has right base angles and equal ray-to-ground angles, hence AA.

    H18.0=2.002.40,H=15.0 m.\begin{aligned}\frac{H}{18.0}&=\frac{2.00}{2.40},\\H&=15.0\text{ m}.\end{aligned}18.0H​H​=2.402.00​,=15.0 m.​

    Answer

    (a) The estimate is not guaranteed; measure the calibration shadow at the same time as the tree's. (b) The corrected estimate is approximately 15.015.015.0 m.

    Key idea

    A changed physical angle requires a new simultaneous calibration.

  8. Problem 8 Restore the missing survey angle

    In a river survey, A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C lie on the straight bank in order and AT⊥ACAT\perp ACAT⊥AC is verified. A point DDD is sighted with T,B,DT,B,DT,B,D in order. The lengths

    AB=32.0 mBC=14.0 mCD=9.0 m\begin{aligned}AB&=32.0\text{ m}\\BC&=14.0\text{ m}\\CD&=9.0\text{ m}\end{aligned}ABBCCD​=32.0 m=14.0 m=9.0 m​

    are recorded, but nobody establishes CD⊥ACCD\perp ACCD⊥AC or CD∥ATCD\parallel ATCD∥AT.

    (a) Explain why AT=CD⋅AB/BCAT=CD\cdot AB/BCAT=CD⋅AB/BC is not yet justified, and describe a dry-land repair.

    (b) After constructing the perpendicular through CCC and finding its new sight-line intersection, the corrected land leg is 10.510.510.5 m. Find the supported estimate of ATATAT.

    1. Hint 1

      The crossing angle at B gives just one pair; identify which second pair is missing.

    2. Hint 2

      Construct the bank perpendicular through C, then use its intersection with the extended sight line and remeasure that leg.

    Answer

    (a) The second equal-angle pair is missing; construct the bank perpendicular through CCC and remeasure to its sight-line intersection. (b) AT≈24.0AT\approx 24.0AT≈24.0 m.

    Full solution

    The collinearities establish vertical angles at BBB, but there is no verified right angle at CCC.

    A single equal-angle pair does not justify AA, so inserting the recorded CD=9.0CD=9.0CD=9.0 into the usual formula is unsupported.

    On dry land, construct a perpendicular to the bank through CCC.

    Let its intersection with sight line TBTBTB extended be the corrected point DDD.

    Now the right angles at A,CA,CA,C and vertical angles at BBB justify AA.

    Use the remeasured corrected leg.

    AT10.5=32.014.0,AT=24.0 m.\begin{aligned}\frac{AT}{10.5}&=\frac{32.0}{14.0},\\AT&=24.0\text{ m}.\end{aligned}10.5AT​AT​=14.032.0​,=24.0 m.​

    (a) The second equal-angle pair is missing; construct the bank perpendicular through CCC and remeasure to its sight-line intersection. (b) AT≈24.0AT\approx 24.0AT≈24.0 m.

    A sight-line alignment needs a second established angle pair for similarity.

  10. Problem 10 Compare two justified estimates of the same mast

    Two methods measure one vertical mast on level ground. For the shadow method, a vertical 1.601.601.60 m staff and the mast are measured simultaneously under parallel sunrays; their own shadows are 1.251.251.25 m and 18.618.618.6 m respectively.

    For the mirror method, a small horizontal mirror lies between the observer foot and mast base, a common vertical sight plane and equal-angle reflection apply, eye height is 1.681.681.68 m, and the observer and mast ground legs are 1.81.81.8 m and 25.525.525.5 m respectively.

    Find each height estimate to the nearest tenth of a meter. Do the estimates agree at that precision, and what does their agreement say about the measurement check?

    1. Hint 1

      Write a separate justified height-to-ground-leg proportion for each model.

    2. Hint 2

      Round after each complete calculation, then compare the reported estimates without treating agreement as proof of every assumption.

    Answer

    The shadow and mirror estimates are both approximately 23.823.823.8 m to the nearest tenth, so they agree at that precision.

    Full solution

    The shadow triangles have right base angles and equal parallel-ray tip angles, so AA pairs the two heights and their own shadows.

    Hshadow=18.6⋅1.601.25,=23.808 m.\begin{aligned}H_{\rm shadow}&=18.6\cdot\frac{1.60}{1.25},\\&=23.808\text{ m}.\end{aligned}Hshadow​​=18.6⋅1.251.60​,=23.808 m.​

    The mirror triangles have right base angles and equal reflection-derived mirror angles.

    AA pairs heights and ground legs.

    Hmirror=1.68⋅25.51.8,=23.8 m.\begin{aligned}H_{\rm mirror}&=1.68\cdot\frac{25.5}{1.8},\\&=23.8\text{ m}.\end{aligned}Hmirror​​=1.68⋅1.825.5​,=23.8 m.​

    Both report approximately 23.823.823.8 m to the nearest tenth.

    The agreement is a useful consistency check between the two supplied models and records.

    It supports the estimate without turning approximate measurements into an exact height or independently proving every physical premise.

    Answer

    The shadow and mirror estimates are both approximately 23.823.823.8 m to the nearest tenth, so they agree at that precision.

    Key idea

    Independent model calculations can check consistency at the stated reporting precision.
