  1. Problem 1 Two denominators, two different comparisons

    In nondegenerate triangle ABCABCABC, DDD lies between A,BA,BA,B, EEE between A,CA,CA,C, and DE∥BCDE\parallel BCDE∥BC. The lengths are

    AD=9DB=16AC=40.\begin{aligned}AD&=9\\DB&=16\\AC&=40.\end{aligned}ADDBAC​=9=16=40.​

    (a) Find AEAEAE and ECECEC.

    (b) Give AD/ABAD/ABAD/AB and AD/DBAD/DBAD/DB, and explain why they are different.

    Text description of this figure

    The parallel segment DE cuts interior points on AB and AC. Given lengths AD9, DB16 and AC40 are labeled; the drawing is schematic.

    1. Hint 1

      Name the whole side before choosing a proportion.

    2. Hint 2

      The whole-side fraction transfers to the other whole side. Subtract only after finding its first part.

    Answer

    (a)

    AE=14.4EC=25.6.\begin{aligned}AE&=14.4\\EC&=25.6.\end{aligned}AEEC​=14.4=25.6.​

    (b)

    AD/AB=9/25AD/DB=9/16.\begin{aligned}AD/AB&=9/25\\AD/DB&=9/16.\end{aligned}AD/ABAD/DB​=9/25=9/16.​

    Full solution

    The whole side is

    AB=9+16,=25.\begin{aligned}AB&=9+16,\\&=25.\end{aligned}AB​=9+16,=25.​

    AA gives the same part-to-whole fraction on both sides.

    AE40=925,AE=14.4.\begin{aligned}\frac{AE}{40}&=\frac 9{25},\\AE&=14.4.\end{aligned}40AE​AE​=259​,=14.4.​

    The remaining part is

    EC=40−14.4,=25.6.\begin{aligned}EC&=40-14.4,\\&=25.6.\end{aligned}EC​=40−14.4,=25.6.​

    The two comparisons have different denominators.

    ADAB=925,ADDB=916.\begin{aligned}\frac{AD}{AB}&=\frac 9{25},\\\frac{AD}{DB}&=\frac 9{16}.\end{aligned}ABAD​DBAD​​=259​,=169​.​

    The first compares the first part with the whole; the second compares it with the leftover part.

    Answer

    (a)

    AE=14.4EC=25.6.\begin{aligned}AE&=14.4\\EC&=25.6.\end{aligned}AEEC​=14.4=25.6.​

    (b)

    AD/AB=9/25AD/DB=9/16.\begin{aligned}AD/AB&=9/25\\AD/DB&=9/16.\end{aligned}AD/ABAD/DB​=9/25=9/16.​

    Key idea

    A ratio is determined by both its numerator and its denominator.

  3. Problem 3 A parallel through one midpoint

    In nondegenerate triangle ABCABCABC, DDD is the midpoint of ABABAB and AC=30AC=30AC=30. A parallel to BCBCBC through DDD meets ACACAC at an interior point EEE.

    (a) Find AEAEAE and ECECEC and identify the position of EEE on ACACAC.

    (b) If

    BC=5u+4DE=3u−2\begin{aligned}BC&=5u+4\\DE&=3u-2\end{aligned}BCDE​=5u+4=3u−2​

    , find uuu, BCBCBC, and DEDEDE.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the direct side-splitter theorem with the known equal parts of AB.

    2. Hint 2

      Once both endpoints are midpoints, use both conclusions of the midsegment theorem.

    Answer

    (a)

    AE=EC,=15\begin{aligned}AE&=EC,\\&=15\end{aligned}AE​=EC,=15​

    , so EEE is the midpoint of ACACAC. (b)

    u=8BC=44DE=22.\begin{aligned}u&=8\\BC&=44\\DE&=22.\end{aligned}uBCDE​=8=44=22.​

    Full solution

    Since DDD is a midpoint, AD/DB=1AD/DB=1AD/DB=1.

    Side-splitter gives AE/EC=1AE/EC=1AE/EC=1, so the two parts of the side of length 303030 are equal.

    Hence

    AE=EC,=15\begin{aligned}AE&=EC,\\&=15\end{aligned}AE​=EC,=15​

    , and the interior point EEE is its midpoint.

    Now DEDEDE is a midsegment, so its length is half of BCBCBC.

    2(3u−2)=5u+4,6u−4=5u+4,u=8.\begin{aligned}2(3u-2)&=5u+4,\\6u-4&=5u+4,\\u&=8.\end{aligned}2(3u−2)6u−4u​=5u+4,=5u+4,=8.​

    Substitute to find the positive lengths.

    BC=5(8)+4,=44,DE=3(8)−2,=22.\begin{aligned}BC&=5(8)+4,\\&=44,\\DE&=3(8)-2,\\&=22.\end{aligned}BCDE​=5(8)+4,=44,=3(8)−2,=22.​

    Answer

    (a)

    AE=EC,=15\begin{aligned}AE&=EC,\\&=15\end{aligned}AE​=EC,=15​

    , so EEE is the midpoint of ACACAC. (b)

    u=8BC=44DE=22.\begin{aligned}u&=8\\BC&=44\\DE&=22.\end{aligned}uBCDE​=8=44=22.​

    Key idea

    A midpoint plus a parallel supplies the second midpoint.

  4. Problem 4 Match the central triangle by its sides

    A triangle has

    AB=22AC=28BC=36.\begin{aligned}AB&=22\\AC&=28\\BC&=36.\end{aligned}ABACBC​=22=28=36.​

    The points D,E,FD,E,FD,E,F are the midpoints of AB,AC,BCAB,AC,BCAB,AC,BC respectively.

    (a) Find DEDEDE, EFEFEF, and FDFDFD.

    (b) Write the natural ordered similarity from DEFDEFDEF to the outer triangle and state its factor in that direction.

    1. Hint 1

      For each central side, name the outer side to which it is parallel.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the vertex corresponding to D from the two outer sides matching DE and DF.

    Answer

    (a)

    DE=18EF=11FD=14.\begin{aligned}DE&=18\\EF&=11\\FD&=14.\end{aligned}DEEFFD​=18=11=14.​

    (b) △DEF∼△CBA\triangle DEF\sim\triangle CBA△DEF∼△CBA, with central-to-outer factor 222.

    Full solution

    Apply the midsegment theorem to each pair of midpoints.

    DE=12BC,=18,EF=12AB,=11,FD=12AC,=14.\begin{aligned}DE&=\tfrac 12 BC,\\&=18,\\EF&=\tfrac 12 AB,\\&=11,\\FD&=\tfrac 12 AC,\\&=14.\end{aligned}DEEFFD​=21​BC,=18,=21​AB,=11,=21​AC,=14.​

    The side pairs are DEDEDE with CBCBCB, EFEFEF with BABABA, and FDFDFD with ACACAC.

    Their shared endpoints give DDD to CCC, EEE to BBB, and FFF to AAA.

    Thus △DEF∼△CBA\triangle DEF\sim\triangle CBA△DEF∼△CBA by SSS similarity.

    The requested factor goes from the central triangle to the outer one:

    CB/DE=36/18,=2.\begin{aligned}CB/DE&=36/18,\\&=2.\end{aligned}CB/DE​=36/18,=2.​

    The reverse direction has factor 1/21/21/2.

    Answer

    (a)

    DE=18EF=11FD=14.\begin{aligned}DE&=18\\EF&=11\\FD&=14.\end{aligned}DEEFFD​=18=11=14.​

    (b) △DEF∼△CBA\triangle DEF\sim\triangle CBA△DEF∼△CBA, with central-to-outer factor 222.

    Key idea

    The central triangle reverses the natural outer vertex order.

  5. Problem 5 Transversals with no common vertex

    Three parallel cross-lines meet one transversal at A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C and another at P,Q,RP,Q,RP,Q,R, in corresponding order. The two transversals are themselves parallel. Given

    AB=9BC=14\begin{aligned}AB&=9\\BC&=14\end{aligned}ABBC​=9=14​

    , find PQPQPQ and QRQRQR.

    Justify equality of the corresponding intervals using earlier ASA congruence. Do not assume a property of quadrilaterals.

    1. Hint 1

      Draw AQ across the first strip. The opposite interval endpoints determine two triangles.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the two parallel families to match the angles around AQ, which is shared.

    Answer

    PQ=9QR=14.\begin{aligned}PQ&=9\\QR&=14.\end{aligned}PQQR​=9=14.​

    Full solution

    In the first strip,

    AB∥PQAP∥BQ.\begin{aligned}AB\parallel PQ\\AP\parallel BQ.\end{aligned}AB∥PQAP∥BQ.​

    The diagonal AQAQAQ gives equal angles

    ∠BAQ=∠PQA∠BQA=∠PAQ.\begin{aligned}\angle BAQ&=\angle PQA\\\angle BQA&=\angle PAQ.\end{aligned}∠BAQ∠BQA​=∠PQA=∠PAQ.​

    Side AQAQAQ is common and lies between those angles.

    Hence △ABQ≅△QPA\triangle ABQ\cong\triangle QPA△ABQ≅△QPA by ASA, so

    AB=QP,=9.\begin{aligned}AB&=QP,\\&=9.\end{aligned}AB​=QP,=9.​

    Apply the same argument in the next strip, using diagonal BRBRBR, to obtain

    BC=QR,=14.\begin{aligned}BC&=QR,\\&=14.\end{aligned}BC​=QR,=14.​

    No intersection of the transversals or quadrilateral theorem is needed.

    Answer

    PQ=9QR=14.\begin{aligned}PQ&=9\\QR&=14.\end{aligned}PQQR​=9=14.​

    Key idea

    When parallel transversals never meet, a strip diagonal provides an ASA argument.

  6. Problem 6 An interval that crosses the vertex

    Two transversals meet at OOO. Their points occur in order A,O,B,CA,O,B,CA,O,B,C and P,O,Q,RP,O,Q,RP,O,Q,R, with AP∥BQ∥CRAP\parallel BQ\parallel CRAP∥BQ∥CR. The lengths are

    OA=2.8OB=4.2OP=4OQ=6BC=7.\begin{aligned}OA&=2.8\\OB&=4.2\\OP&=4\\OQ&=6\\BC&=7.\end{aligned}OAOBOPOQBC​=2.8=4.2=4=6=7.​

    (a) Find ABABAB and PQPQPQ.

    (b) Find QRQRQR and explain why subtraction alone would not give the first pair of intervals.

    1. Hint 1

      Check whether O lies inside each intercepted interval.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the second-to-first vertex-distance factor, then apply it to a corresponding interval.

    Answer

    (a)

    AB=7PQ=10.\begin{aligned}AB&=7\\PQ&=10.\end{aligned}ABPQ​=7=10.​

    (b) QR=10QR=10QR=10; intervals crossing OOO require addition.

    Full solution

    The point OOO lies inside both first intervals.

    Add their two endpoint distances.

    AB=2.8+4.2,=7,PQ=4+6,=10.\begin{aligned}AB&=2.8+4.2,\\&=7,\\PQ&=4+6,\\&=10.\end{aligned}ABPQ​=2.8+4.2,=7,=4+6,=10.​

    AA between the triangles from OOO gives one common second-to-first factor.

    OPOA=42.8,=107,OQOB=64.2,=107.\begin{aligned}\frac{OP}{OA}&=\frac 4{2.8},\\&=\frac{10}{7},\\\frac{OQ}{OB}&=\frac 6{4.2},\\&=\frac{10}{7}.\end{aligned}OAOP​OBOQ​​=2.84​,=710​,=4.26​,=710​.​

    That factor also applies to corresponding intervals between the next cross-lines, so

    QR=(10/7)⋅7,=10.\begin{aligned}QR&=(10/7)\cdot 7,\\&=10.\end{aligned}QR​=(10/7)⋅7,=10.​

    A difference gives the gap only for two endpoints on the same ray from OOO.

    Here each first interval crosses OOO, so its length is a sum; multiplying both summands by one factor preserves that factor.

    Answer

    (a)

    AB=7PQ=10.\begin{aligned}AB&=7\\PQ&=10.\end{aligned}ABPQ​=7=10.​

    (b) QR=10QR=10QR=10; intervals crossing OOO require addition.

    Key idea

    Use addition across the vertex and subtraction along one ray.

  7. Problem 7 Seven equal parts without ruler division

  10. Problem 10 Does one midpoint force every joining segment?

    Refute the claim: a segment from the midpoint of one triangle side to any interior point of another side must be a midsegment.

    Give a concrete nondegenerate triangle and interior points that satisfy the stated midpoint condition but fail the conclusion. Explain the failure using this lesson's ratios.

    1. Hint 1

      Keep the first endpoint at a midpoint but divide the second side into unequal positive parts.

    2. Hint 2

      Choose outer side lengths satisfying the triangle inequality, then compare the two part-to-part ratios.

    Answer

    One counterexample has

    AB=14AC=20BC=24\begin{aligned}AB&=14\\AC&=20\\BC&=24\end{aligned}ABACBC​=14=20=24​

    , with

    AD=DB,=7AE=8EC=12.\begin{aligned}AD&=DB,\\&=7\\AE&=8\\EC&=12.\end{aligned}ADAEEC​=DB,=7=8=12.​

    Then DEDEDE is not a midsegment and is not parallel to BCBCBC.

    Full solution

    Take a triangle with

    AB=14AC=20BC=24.\begin{aligned}AB&=14\\AC&=20\\BC&=24.\end{aligned}ABACBC​=14=20=24.​

    All lengths are positive and satisfy the triangle inequality, so this is a possible nondegenerate triangle.

    Place DDD halfway along ABABAB and EEE eight units from AAA along ACACAC.

    Then

    AD=DB,=7AE=8EC=12.\begin{aligned}AD&=DB,\\&=7\\AE&=8\\EC&=12.\end{aligned}ADAEEC​=DB,=7=8=12.​

    The first endpoint is a midpoint; the second is interior but is not a midpoint.

    ADDB=1,AEEC=23.\begin{aligned}\frac{AD}{DB}&=1,\\\frac{AE}{EC}&=\frac 23.\end{aligned}DBAD​ECAE​​=1,=32​.​

    If DEDEDE were parallel to BCBCBC, side-splitter would make the ratios equal, a contradiction.

    Thus this segment fails both the second-midpoint requirement and the claimed parallelism.

    Answer

    AB=14AC=20BC=24\begin{aligned}AB&=14\\AC&=20\\BC&=24\end{aligned}ABACBC​=14=20=24​
