Dilations and Scale Factors

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Choose a point O on paper and mark two points on different rays from it. Now mark the halfway point from O to each one. Both points move toward the same center by the same factor, even though they travel in different directions. What happens to the segment joining them?

Learning goals

  • Locate image points from a center and a positive scale factor
  • Calculate scaled lengths and coordinates, including centers away from the origin
  • Describe fixed points and the images of lines under a dilation
  • Distinguish a uniform dilation from a stretch in one direction

Resize from a center

A dilation tells every point how far to sit from one chosen point. Suppose a point PPP is 444 units from OOO. To scale from OOO by a factor of 32\frac 3223​, place P′P'P′ on ray OPOPOP, 666 units from OOO.

OP′=32⋅4,=6.\begin{aligned}OP'&=\frac32\cdot4,\\&=6.\end{aligned}OP′​=23​⋅4,=6.​

The mark P′P'P′ is read “PPP prime.” It names the image, the point after the transformation; PPP is the original point, or preimage. The ray fixes the direction, so the distance does not leave us choosing between two opposite sides of OOO.

A dilation with center OOO and positive scale factor kkk maps each point P≠OP\ne OP=O to the point P′P'P′ on ray OPOPOP satisfying the rule below. The center itself maps to OOO.

OP′=k⋅OP.OP'=k\cdot OP.OP′=k⋅OP.

The same center and factor apply to every point of a figure, not just its vertices. Join the images of the vertices to draw the image polygon.

  • An enlargement has k>1k>1k>1. Every point other than OOO moves farther from OOO.
  • A reduction has 0<k<10<k<10<k<1. Every point other than OOO moves closer to OOO.
  • The identity case has k=1k=1k=1. Every point stays where it is.

Zero and negative scale factors are outside this lesson. A fraction need not mean reduction; 54\frac 5445​ is greater than 111.

Check your understanding

A point is 181818 units from the center. Its image is on the same ray, 121212 units from the center. What is the scale factor?

Answer choices

Divide image distance by original distance.

k=1218,=23.\begin{aligned}k&=\frac{12}{18},\\&=\frac 23.\end{aligned}k​=1812​,=32​.​

The image is closer to the center, so this is a reduction.

Measurements support these patterns, but a few drawings cannot prove them for every figure. We explicitly accept the following dilation properties as foundations for the geometry that follows. We will use them to establish later results, rather than use those later results to prove them here.

  • Lengths. For any distinct points A,BA,BA,B, their images satisfy A′B′=k⋅ABA'B'=k\cdot ABA′B′=k⋅AB.
  • Angles. The angle between two rays equals the angle between their image rays.
  • Lines. A line through OOO maps onto the same line as a set. If k≠1k\ne1k=1, a line away from OOO maps onto a distinct parallel line. If k=1k=1k=1, every line maps onto itself.

These accepted properties will be the foundation for similarity in the next lesson.

Preserving an angle means preserving its measure, even when its vertex moves. Scaling a side means multiplying the distance between its endpoints, even when neither endpoint is the center.

Worked example 1 Read one factor in three ways

Problem. A dilation with center OOO sends PPP to P′P'P′. The distances OPOPOP and OP′OP'OP′ are 888 cm and 666 cm. A segment QRQRQR has length 141414 cm, and an angle at QQQ measures 42∘42^\circ42∘. Find the scale factor, the image length Q′R′Q'R'Q′R′, and the image angle at Q′Q'Q′.

Solution. Put image distance over original distance.

k=68,=34.\begin{aligned}k&=\frac68,\\&=\frac34.\end{aligned}k​=86​,=43​.​

This is a reduction. Apply the same factor to the segment, although OOO need not be one of its endpoints.

Q′R′=34⋅14,=10.5 cm.\begin{aligned}Q'R'&=\frac34\cdot14,\\&=10.5\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}Q′R′​=43​⋅14,=10.5 cm.​

The image angle measures 42∘42^\circ42∘. Angles are preserved; their measures are not multiplied by 34\frac 3443​.

Check your understanding

A dilation has scale factor 111. A line does not pass through the center. What is its image?

Answer choices

At scale factor 111, every point maps to itself. That includes every point on a line away from the center, so the image is the same line.

Coordinates measure from the center

With the origin as center, multiply both coordinates by the scale factor. For example, a factor of 333 sends (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1) to (6,−3)(6,-3)(6,−3). The horizontal displacement and the vertical displacement from the origin both triple, so the point stays in the same direction from the origin.

For a dilation centered at (0,0)(0, 0)(0,0), the rule is

(x,y)⟼(kx,ky).(x,y)\longmapsto(kx,ky).(x,y)⟼(kx,ky).

Worked example 2 Use the origin rule

Problem. Dilate T(−4,3)T(-4, 3)T(−4,3) about the origin by a scale factor of 1.51.51.5. Find T′T'T′ and classify the dilation.

Solution. Multiply each coordinate by 1.51.51.5, keeping the sign of each displacement.

x′=1.5(−4),=−6,y′=1.5(3),=4.5.\begin{aligned}x'&=1.5(-4),\\&=-6,\\y'&=1.5(3),\\&=4.5.\end{aligned}x′y′​=1.5(−4),=−6,=1.5(3),=4.5.​

The image is T′(−6,4.5)T'(-6, 4.5)T′(−6,4.5). Since 1.5>11.5>11.5>1, this is an enlargement.

When the center is away from the origin, measure the displacements from that center first. Multiplying the raw coordinates would resize from the wrong point.

Worked example 3 Move halfway from a different center

Problem. A dilation has center O(2,−1)O(2,-1)O(2,−1) and scale factor 12\frac 1221​. Find the image of P(6,3)P(6, 3)P(6,3).

Solution. From OOO to PPP, move 444 units right and 444 units up. Halving those displacements gives 222 units right and 222 units up. Start those new moves at OOO, not at the origin.

x′=2+12(6−2),=4,y′=−1+12(3−(−1)),=1.\begin{aligned}x'&=2+\frac12(6-2),\\&=4,\\y'&=-1+\frac12(3-(-1)),\\&=1.\end{aligned}x′y′​=2+21​(6−2),=4,=−1+21​(3−(−1)),=1.​

Thus P′=(4,1)P'=(4, 1)P′=(4,1). It lies halfway along the segment from OOO to PPP.

The same three moves work for any center O(a,b)O(a,b)O(a,b). Subtract a,ba,ba,b to measure from OOO, multiply both displacements by kkk, then add a,ba,ba,b to return to the original coordinate system.

x′=a+k(x−a),y′=b+k(y−b).\begin{aligned}x'&=a+k(x-a),\\y'&=b+k(y-b).\end{aligned}x′y′​=a+k(x−a),=b+k(y−b).​

The coordinates a,ba,ba,b locate the center; kkk is the scale factor. The origin rule is the special case where both center coordinates are zero.

Check your understanding

A dilation has center (1,2)(1, 2)(1,2) and scale factor 222. Where does (4,5)(4, 5)(4,5) map?

Answer choices

The point is 333 units right and 333 units above the center. Double each displacement, then start at (1,2)(1, 2)(1,2).

x′=1+2(4−1),=7,y′=2+2(5−2),=8.\begin{aligned}x'&=1+2(4-1),\\&=7,\\y'&=2+2(5-2),\\&=8.\end{aligned}x′y′​=1+2(4−1),=7,=2+2(5−2),=8.​

The image is (7,8)(7, 8)(7,8).

Fixed points and unchanged lines

A fixed point has its own location as its image. The center is always fixed because its distance from itself is zero. At scale factor 111, all points are fixed.

For a different scale factor, a point away from the center cannot stay put. A point 555 units from OOO, for example, moves to distance 101010 when k=2k=2k=2. In general, staying put would require both

OP′=OPOP′=k⋅OP.\begin{aligned}OP'&=OP\\OP'&=k\cdot OP.\end{aligned}OP′OP′​=OP=k⋅OP.​

Since OPOPOP is positive away from the center, dividing by it forces k=1k=1k=1. Thus when k≠1k\ne 1k=1, the center is the only fixed point.

A line can stay the same as a set while its points move. On a line through OOO, scaling sends points along its two opposite rays, and every location on that line is the image of some point. With k=2k=2k=2, a point 333 units from OOO moves to the location 666 units from OOO; the original 333-unit location is now occupied by the image of the point 1.51.51.5 units from OOO. The entire line remains, but its individual points are not all fixed.

Worked example 4 Recover the center from a point and its image

Problem. A dilation with scale factor 222 sends P(1,3)P(1, 3)P(1,3) to P′(5,7)P'(5, 7)P′(5,7). Find its center O(a,b)O(a,b)O(a,b).

Solution. The move from PPP to P′P'P′ is 444 right and 444 up. When the distance from the center doubles, that move equals the move from OOO to PPP. Go 444 left and 444 down from PPP to locate O(−3,−1)O(-3,-1)O(−3,−1).

Check with the coordinate rule.

5=a+2(1−a),a=−3,7=b+2(3−b),b=−1.\begin{aligned}5&=a+2(1-a),\\a&=-3,\\7&=b+2(3-b),\\b&=-1.\end{aligned}5a7b​=a+2(1−a),=−3,=b+2(3−b),=−1.​

The center is (−3,−1)(-3,-1)(−3,−1). Both coordinate equations give the same dilation, and O,P,P′O,P,P'O,P,P′ lie on the required ray in that order.

A stretch changes the shape

Making every horizontal distance twice as large while keeping vertical distances unchanged is a stretch in one direction. It uses different factors for different directions, so it is not a dilation of the plane.

Worked example 5 Test whether one factor works

Problem. A transformation sends (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) to (2x,y)(2x,y)(2x,y). Consider O(0,0)O(0, 0)O(0,0), A(2,0)A(2, 0)A(2,0) and B(0,2)B(0, 2)B(0,2). Decide whether their images could come from one dilation, and justify your decision using lengths.

Solution. The images are O′(0,0)O'(0, 0)O′(0,0), A′(4,0)A'(4, 0)A′(4,0) and B′(0,2)B'(0, 2)B′(0,2). The horizontal segment OAOAOA, originally 222 units long, becomes 444 units long. It requires a scale factor of 222.

The vertical segment OBOBOB, also 222 units long, stays 222 units long. It requires a scale factor of 111.

One dilation cannot give two different length factors. These three images therefore do not come from a single dilation, whatever center we try.

Some angles can survive a stretch, such as the right angle between these horizontal and vertical legs. One unchanged angle does not establish that the whole transformation is a dilation. Check one common center, the ray condition, and one common factor for all points.

Check your understanding

A transformation doubles every horizontal distance but leaves every vertical distance unchanged. Which observation rules out a dilation?

Answer choices

A dilation uses one positive length factor for every segment. Requiring 222 in one direction and 111 in another contradicts that property. Keeping a particular angle, point, or straight side is not enough.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Reversing the factor. Use image length divided by original length, in that order.
  • Multiplying angle measures. A dilation scales lengths and preserves angle measures.
  • Forgetting the center. Multiply raw coordinates only when the center is the origin.
  • Confusing a line with its points. A through-center line stays the same as a set; when k≠1k\ne1k=1, only its center point is fixed.
  • Treating any resize as a dilation. One factor must work in every direction.

Key takeaways

  • A dilation uses a center and a positive scale factor; each image stays on the same ray from that center.
  • Lengths multiply by kkk, while angle measures stay the same.
  • Coordinate rules scale displacements from the center, then add the center back.
  • At k=1k=1k=1 all points are fixed; otherwise only the center is fixed.
  • A through-center line maps onto itself; an away-center line has a distinct parallel image unless k=1k=1k=1.

A bit of history (optional)

Before a digital image could be resized with a slider, drawing instruments could make enlarged or reduced copies. A pantograph linked movable bars to a tracing point and a drawing point. Following the original with one point guided the other across the paper.

The Jesuit astronomer and mathematician Christoph Scheiner devised a wooden pantograph in 1603 and described it in a book published in 1631. Other makers later built brass and ebony versions. These were practical copying tools, not just mathematical demonstrations. The Smithsonian’s collection of pantographs includes instruments from the nineteenth and twentieth centuries and describes how the same copying principle reached printing and textile design.
