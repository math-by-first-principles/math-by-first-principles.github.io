Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    A proposed AA proof uses the angle sum to find equal third angles and immediately concludes that the sides are proportional. What step is missing?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The angle sum establishes equal angles, but it does not itself establish a common side ratio. A dilation matching one side followed by ASA or AAS congruence supplies the missing rigid-motion match.

  2. 2

    Triangle ABCABCABC has angles 48∘48^\circ48∘, 67∘67^\circ67∘, 65∘65^\circ65∘ at A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C. Triangle PQRPQRPQR has angles 65∘65^\circ65∘, 48∘48^\circ48∘, 67∘67^\circ67∘ at P,Q,RP,Q,RP,Q,R. Which ordered statement is valid?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Match equal measures: C to P, A to Q, and B to R. In the order PQR, the corresponding first-triangle order is CAB.

  5. 5

    Triangle ABCABCABC has angles 46∘46^\circ46∘, 67∘67^\circ67∘, 67∘67^\circ67∘ at A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C. Triangle DEFDEFDEF has angles 67∘67^\circ67∘, 46∘46^\circ46∘, 67∘67^\circ67∘ at D,E,FD,E,FD,E,F. Which correspondence is forced in every valid similarity statement?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The 46-degree angle occurs at only A and E, so those vertices must match. The two 67-degree vertices in each triangle can be paired in either order; neither B to D nor C to D is forced.

    Two rectangles have all four corresponding angles equal. Their corresponding side lengths are 2 and 3 in the first rectangle, and 2 and 5 in the second. What follows?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    AA is a triangle criterion. Equal angles alone do not force a common length factor for rectangles.

    22=1,53≠1.\begin{aligned}\frac 22&=1,\\\frac 53&\ne 1.\end{aligned}22​35​​=1,=1.​

    The two corresponding length factors disagree.

  7. 7

    In an AA proof, a dilation has made the side between two preserved angles equal to the corresponding side of the target. Why is it valid to use ASA at this point?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The dilation changes the original side length to the target side length while preserving the two angles. ASA congruence is already available and gives a rigid-motion match; no similarity criterion is being used to prove itself.

    Two right triangles have their right angles at BBB and EEE. The first has A=28∘A=28^\circA=28∘; the second has D=62∘D=62^\circD=62∘. Which ordered similarity statement follows?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The first triangle has C = 62 degrees, and the second has F = 28 degrees. Pair the equal angles.

    m∠C=180∘−90∘−28∘,=62∘,m∠F=180∘−90∘−62∘,=28∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle C&=180^\circ-90^\circ-28^\circ,\\&=62^\circ,\\m\angle F&=180^\circ-90^\circ-62^\circ,\\&=28^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠Cm∠F​=180∘−90∘−28∘,=62∘,=180∘−90∘−62∘,=28∘.​

    Thus A matches F, B matches E, and C matches D; AA proves the ordered statement.

    Known corresponding angles satisfy

    m∠A=39∘m∠D=(x+19)∘m∠B=67∘m∠E=(2x+27)∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle A&=39^\circ\\m\angle D&=(x+19)^\circ\\m\angle B&=67^\circ\\m\angle E&=(2x+27)^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠Am∠Dm∠Bm∠E​=39∘=(x+19)∘=67∘=(2x+27)∘.​

    Which value makes both angle pairs equal for AA?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Both pairs must be equal for the same value of x.

    x+19=39,x=20,2(20)+27=67.\begin{aligned}x+19&=39,\\x&=20,\\2(20)+27&=67.\end{aligned}x+19x2(20)+27​=39,=20,=67.​

    It is given that

    △ABC∼△XYZAB=XY.\begin{aligned}\triangle ABC\sim\triangle XYZ\\AB&=XY.\end{aligned}△ABC∼△XYZAB​=XY.​

    Which conclusion follows for the stated correspondence?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The known corresponding side equality makes the common length factor one.

    k=XYAB,=1.\begin{aligned}k&=\frac{XY}{AB},\\&=1.\end{aligned}k​=ABXY​,=1.​

    Every corresponding length therefore stays equal, and the similarity is a rigid-motion congruence.
