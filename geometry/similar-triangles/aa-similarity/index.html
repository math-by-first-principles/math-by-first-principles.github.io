Similarity and AA

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Two triangular signs can have different sizes and face different directions, yet have matching shapes. Sliding or turning a sign changes its position; resizing changes its lengths. Similarity puts those changes together. For triangles, two matching angles will turn out to be enough to guarantee the whole shape matches.

Learning goals

  • Identify corresponding vertices, angles, and sides in an ordered similarity statement
  • Explain AA similarity using a dilation followed by an earlier congruence criterion
  • Calculate missing lengths using consistently ordered ratios of corresponding sides
  • Justify angle correspondences in nested triangles and triangles across intersecting lines

Matching shape through transformations

A triangle can change size and position while keeping its shape. Start with a triangle whose base is 444 units. Dilate it by 1.51.51.5, then turn and slide the copy. Its base becomes 666 units, while the angles keep their measures. A reflection may also be needed to match a copy facing the other way.

Two figures are similar if a positive dilation, together with a sequence of rigid motions, maps one figure exactly onto the other. A rigid motion is a translation, rotation, or reflection. The dilation can have factor 111, so congruent figures are included among similar figures.

The dilation properties from the previous lesson tell us that lengths scale by one positive factor and angle measures stay unchanged. Rigid motions preserve both lengths and angle measures.

Change the scale factor while keeping the other controls still. The displayed angles stay fixed and every corresponding side uses the same factor.

To align the triangles, set the scale to 111, the turn to 0∘0^\circ0∘, the slide to 000, and the reflection to “retained.” Then vary just the turn or slide. The triangles remain congruent even while their positions differ. Reset restores the initial rotated enlargement. Without scripting, the diagram and readout display that initial state.

Put the correspondence in the names

A correspondence specifies which point of the first figure matches each point of the second. It follows the transformation, not the page position. If AAA matches PPP, BBB matches QQQ, and CCC matches RRR, write

△ABC∼△PQR.\triangle ABC\sim\triangle PQR.△ABC∼△PQR.

The symbol ∼\sim∼ means “is similar to.” Read the names in order. For these triangles, the angle measures and length ratios satisfy

m∠A=m∠P,m∠B=m∠Q,m∠C=m∠R.\begin{aligned}m\angle A&=m\angle P,\\m\angle B&=m\angle Q,\\m\angle C&=m\angle R.\end{aligned}m∠Am∠Bm∠C​=m∠P,=m∠Q,=m∠R.​ PQAB=k,QRBC=k,PRAC=k.\begin{aligned}\frac{PQ}{AB}&=k,\\\frac{QR}{BC}&=k,\\\frac{PR}{AC}&=k.\end{aligned}ABPQ​BCQR​ACPR​​=k,=k,=k.​

Here kkk is the scale factor from ABCABCABC to PQRPQRPQR: image length divided by original length. These side lengths are proportional, meaning that their matching ratios are equal. Reversing the mapping replaces kkk by 1k\frac 1kk1​. At k=1k=1k=1, corresponding lengths are equal, so the triangles are congruent.

Worked example 1 Follow the order instead of the picture

Problem. It is given that △ABC∼△QRP\triangle ABC\sim\triangle QRP△ABC∼△QRP. The lengths ABABAB, QRQRQR, and BCBCBC are 666 cm, 999 cm, and 888 cm. Identify the side matching BCBCBC, find its length, and give the scale factor in each direction.

Solution. The statement pairs AAA with QQQ, BBB with RRR, and CCC with PPP. Thus BCBCBC matches RPRPRP.

Use the direction from ABCABCABC to QRPQRPQRP throughout the calculation.

k=QRAB,k=96,=1.5,RP=1.5⋅8,=12 cm.\begin{aligned}k&=\frac{QR}{AB},\\k&=\frac96,\\&=1.5,\\RP&=1.5\cdot8,\\&=12\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}kkRP​=ABQR​,=69​,=1.5,=1.5⋅8,=12 cm.​

The reverse factor, from QRPQRPQRP to ABCABCABC, is 23\frac 2332​. A larger second triangle must give a factor above 111 in the forward direction and below 111 in reverse.

Check your understanding

Given △JKL∼△VUW\triangle JKL\sim\triangle VUW△JKL∼△VUW, which side corresponds to JLJLJL?

Answer choices

The ordered matching is J to V, K to U, and L to W. The endpoints of JL therefore match V and W, so JL corresponds to VW.

Why two angles are enough for triangles

Matching two triangle angles guarantees similarity, even when no side lengths have been given. This is the angle-angle criterion, or AA similarity: if two angles of one nondegenerate triangle equal two corresponding angles of another, the triangles are similar.

The triangle-angle sum makes the third angles equal too. That observation alone does not explain why side lengths must be proportional. The missing step is a dilation followed by congruence.

Why AA establishes similarity#

First take triangles with corresponding angles 36∘36^\circ36∘ and 79∘79^\circ79∘. Let the side between those angles be 555 units in ABCABCABC and 888 units in PQRPQRPQR.

Dilate ABCABCABC by 85\frac 8558​. The side between the two selected angles becomes 888 units long, and the two angles stay 36∘36^\circ36∘ and 79∘79^\circ79∘. The dilated triangle and PQRPQRPQR now have two equal angles and the equal included side. By the earlier ASA congruence criterion, rigid motions can make them coincide. The resizing and rigid motions together give similarity.

Nothing depended on the particular numbers. In general, suppose

∠A≅∠P∠B≅∠Q.\begin{aligned}\angle A\cong\angle P\\\angle B\cong\angle Q.\end{aligned}∠A≅∠P∠B≅∠Q.​

All triangle sides are positive. Choose

k=PQAB.k=\frac{PQ}{AB}.k=ABPQ​.

Dilate ABCABCABC by this factor about AAA, calling the image A′B′C′A'B'C'A′B′C′. Length scaling gives

A′B′=PQAB⋅AB,=PQ.\begin{aligned}A'B'&=\frac{PQ}{AB}\cdot AB,\\&=PQ.\end{aligned}A′B′​=ABPQ​⋅AB,=PQ.​

Dilation preserves the two angles, so

∠A′≅∠P∠B′≅∠Q.\begin{aligned}\angle A'\cong\angle P\\\angle B'\cong\angle Q.\end{aligned}∠A′≅∠P∠B′≅∠Q.​

The matching side A′B′A'B'A′B′ lies between these angles. Thus △A′B′C′≅△PQR\triangle A'B'C'\cong\triangle PQR△A′B′C′≅△PQR by ASA. There is a sequence of rigid motions mapping the dilated triangle onto PQRPQRPQR, which proves similarity by its definition.

Those rigid motions preserve the scaled side lengths. Therefore every side of PQRPQRPQR is kkk times its matching side of ABCABCABC, which also justifies using proportional sides after AA has established the similarity.

∎

Worked example 2 Find the angle matching before the length ratio

Problem. Triangle ABCABCABC has angles

A=38∘B=64∘.\begin{aligned}A&=38^\circ\\B&=64^\circ.\end{aligned}AB​=38∘=64∘.​

Triangle DEFDEFDEF has angles

The side lengths ABABAB and EFEFEF are 777 cm and 10.510.510.5 cm. Prove the triangles similar, write a correct ordered statement, and find the factor from the first triangle to the second.

Solution. Use the angle sum to find the remaining angles.

m∠C=180∘−38∘−64∘,=78∘,m∠F=180∘−78∘−38∘,=64∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle C&=180^\circ-38^\circ-64^\circ,\\&=78^\circ,\\m\angle F&=180^\circ-78^\circ-38^\circ,\\&=64^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠Cm∠F​=180∘−38∘−64∘,=78∘,=180∘−78∘−38∘,=64∘.​

The matching is AAA to EEE, BBB to FFF, and CCC to DDD. Two of these angle pairs establish AA, so

△ABC∼△EFD.\triangle ABC\sim\triangle EFD.△ABC∼△EFD.

Now the ratio uses the side EFEFEF corresponding to ABABAB.

k=10.57,=1.5.\begin{aligned}k&=\frac{10.5}{7},\\&=1.5.\end{aligned}k​=710.5​,=1.5.​

Check your understanding

Two nondegenerate triangles have two pairs of equal corresponding angles. After dilating one triangle to match the side between those angles, which earlier result finishes the similarity justification?

Answer choices

The dilation preserves both angles and makes their included sides equal. ASA congruence then supplies a rigid-motion match. Combined with the dilation, that match proves similarity.

A small triangle inside a larger one

Overlapping triangles can still have a fixed correspondence. Name the complete small triangle and complete large triangle before forming a ratio.

Worked example 3 Use parallels to establish the two angle pairs

Problem. In △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC, DDD lies between AAA and BBB, and EEE lies between AAA and CCC. It is given that

DE∥BCAD=9 cmAB=15 cmAE=7.2 cm.\begin{aligned}DE\parallel BC\\AD&=9\text{ cm}\\AB&=15\text{ cm}\\AE&=7.2\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}DE∥BCADABAE​=9 cm=15 cm=7.2 cm.​

Prove △ADE∼△ABC\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ABC△ADE∼△ABC and find ACACAC.

Solution. Rays ADADAD and ABABAB are the same ray, as are AEAEAE and ACACAC. Thus the small and large triangles share the angle at AAA.

With ABABAB as a transversal of DEDEDE and BCBCBC, corresponding angles give ∠ADE≅∠ABC\angle ADE\cong\angle ABC∠ADE≅∠ABC. These two angle matches prove △ADE∼△ABC\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ABC△ADE∼△ABC by AA, with AAA to AAA, DDD to BBB, and EEE to CCC.

Keep small-triangle lengths over large-triangle lengths.

AEAC=ADAB,7.2AC=915,AC=12 cm.\begin{aligned}\frac{AE}{AC}&=\frac{AD}{AB},\\\frac{7.2}{AC}&=\frac9{15},\\AC&=12\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}ACAE​AC7.2​AC​=ABAD​,=159​,=12 cm.​

The known 7.27.27.2 cm is the small side AEAEAE, not the leftover segment ECECEC.

Check your understanding

In the nested configuration above, which ratio has the same small-to-large direction as AD/ABAD/ABAD/AB?

Answer choices

The ordered matching ADE to ABC pairs AD with AB, AE with AC, and DE with BC. Thus DE/BC uses the same direction. The leftover segments DB and EC are not sides of the small triangle.

Triangles across crossing lines

Two triangles can face opposite ways and still have matching angles. At an intersection, vertical angles give one pair; another pair must come from a stated fact.

Worked example 4 Keep the shared intersection in the middle

Problem. Points A,O,CA,O,CA,O,C are collinear in that order, and B,O,DB,O,DB,O,D are collinear in that order. The segments ABABAB and CDCDCD are parallel. The lengths AOAOAO, COCOCO, and BOBOBO are 999 cm, 151515 cm, and 6.66.66.6 cm. Prove △AOB∼△COD\triangle AOB\sim\triangle COD△AOB∼△COD and find DODODO.

Solution. Angles AOBAOBAOB and CODCODCOD are vertical angles, so they are equal. Since AB∥CDAB\parallel CDAB∥CD and ACACAC is a transversal, ∠BAO≅∠DCO\angle BAO\cong\angle DCO∠BAO≅∠DCO by alternate interior angles.

AA now gives the stated order: AAA matches CCC, OOO matches OOO, and BBB matches DDD. Use second-triangle lengths over first-triangle lengths.

DOBO=COAO,DO6.6=159,DO=11 cm.\begin{aligned}\frac{DO}{BO}&=\frac{CO}{AO},\\\frac{DO}{6.6}&=\frac{15}{9},\\DO&=11\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}BODO​6.6DO​DO​=AOCO​,=915​,=11 cm.​

The left-hand position of BBB does not make it correspond to whichever right-hand point looks closest. The angle matches determine the correspondence.

Worked example 5 Recognize when the evidence stops short

Problem. Two triangles each have a 55∘55^\circ55∘ angle. Is that fact enough to prove them similar? Justify your answer.

Solution. No. One triangle can have angles 55∘55^\circ55∘, 70∘70^\circ70∘, and 55∘55^\circ55∘. Another can have angles 55∘55^\circ55∘, 60∘60^\circ60∘, and 65∘65^\circ65∘. Both sets are positive and sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘, so they describe possible triangles, but the full angle sets do not match.

AA needs equal angles at two distinct vertices in each triangle. A single angle, even one marked in both pictures, supplies only one pair.

Check your understanding

In a crossing-lines configuration, only the vertical angles at the intersection are known to be equal. What can be concluded about the two triangles?

Answer choices

Vertical angles give one pair of equal angles. A second corresponding pair is still needed. The triangles might be similar, but the stated evidence does not establish it.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Matching by position. Similarity statements follow named angle matches, including after a turn or reflection.
  • Reversing one ratio. Put the same triangle’s corresponding lengths on top throughout an equation.
  • Using AA for one angle. Two pairs at distinct triangle vertices are required.
  • Assuming parallelism. Crossing lines give vertical angles; a second angle match needs a stated reason.
  • Skipping the congruence step. Equal third angles follow from the angle sum; proportional sides follow from the dilation and congruence argument.

Key takeaways

  • Similar figures can be matched by a positive dilation and rigid motions; the factor can be 111.
  • The order in a similarity statement fixes corresponding vertices, angles, and sides.
  • AA proves triangle similarity by scaling one side to match and then using congruence.
  • After similarity is established, corresponding side ratios have one common positive value.
  • In nested or crossing triangles, prove the angle matches before using a length ratio.

A bit of history (optional)

Euclid gave similarity its own place in Book VI of the Elements. The opening definition paired equal angles with proportional sides. Later, Proposition 4 established that triangles with matching angles have proportional corresponding sides.

There is a small language trap in that old statement. Its “equiangular triangles” means two triangles whose angles match each other, not two triangles that each have three equal angles. The translated proposition and its guide preserve the wording and explain that distinction. A reader who interpreted it the other way would turn a result about all matching triangle shapes into one about equilateral triangles alone.
