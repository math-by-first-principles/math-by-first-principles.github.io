  1. Problem 1 Read the matching in order

    It is given that △ABC∼△FDE\triangle ABC\sim\triangle FDE△ABC∼△FDE.

    (a) Name the vertex corresponding to BBB and the side corresponding to ACACAC.

    (b) Complete the ratio statement using the matching side of BCBCBC:

    FDAB=□BC.\frac{FD}{AB}=\frac{\square}{BC}.ABFD​=BC□​.

    1. Hint 1

      Read matching positions in the two triangle names.

    2. Hint 2

      A side matches the side joining the images of its two endpoints.

    Answer

    Part (a). BBB matches DDD; ACACAC matches FEFEFE. Part (b).

    FDAB=DEBC.\frac{FD}{AB}=\frac{DE}{BC}.ABFD​=BCDE​.

    Full solution

    (a) The matching is AAA to FFF, BBB to DDD, and CCC to EEE.

    Therefore the matching side of ACACAC joins FFF and EEE.

    (b) The endpoints of BCBCBC match DDD and EEE.

    The numerator must be DEDEDE, keeping second-triangle lengths over first-triangle lengths.

    Answer

    Part (a). BBB matches DDD; ACACAC matches FEFEFE. Part (b).

    FDAB=DEBC.\frac{FD}{AB}=\frac{DE}{BC}.ABFD​=BCDE​.

    Key idea

    The vertex order determines every side ratio, regardless of page position.

  4. Problem 4 Finish AA with a nonincluded side

    Nondegenerate triangles ABCABCABC and PQRPQRPQR satisfy

    ∠A≅∠P∠B≅∠Q.\begin{aligned}\angle A\cong\angle P\\\angle B\cong\angle Q.\end{aligned}∠A≅∠P∠B≅∠Q.​

    Call the image A′B′C′A'B'C'A′B′C′.

    The side A′C′A'C'A′C′ has length 151515, matching PRPRPR.

    The angle pairs A′A'A′ to PPP and B′B'B′ to QQQ remain equal because dilation preserves angles.

    The side A′C′A'C'A′C′ is not between the selected vertices A′A'A′ and B′B'B′.

  5. Problem 5 Find the leftover segment

    In triangle ABCABCABC, DDD lies between AAA and BBB, EEE lies between AAA and CCC, and DE∥BCDE\parallel BCDE∥BC. The lengths are

    AD=7DB=5AE=8.4.\begin{aligned}AD&=7\\DB&=5\\AE&=8.4.\end{aligned}ADDBAE​=7=5=8.4.​

    Prove a similarity statement, then find ECECEC.

    Text description of this figure

    D lies between A and B, E lies between A and C, and DE is parallel to BC. AD is 7, DB is 5, and AE is 8.4; EC is the requested unknown. The drawing is schematic.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify an angle shared by the whole triangle and the small triangle.

    2. Hint 2

      Use a corresponding-angle pair from the parallel segments for AA.

    3. Hint 3

      Find the whole side AC before subtracting AE.

    Answer

    Full solution

    The small and large triangles share angle AAA.

    Since DE∥BCDE\parallel BCDE∥BC, corresponding angles give ∠ADE≅∠ABC\angle ADE\cong\angle ABC∠ADE≅∠ABC.

    Therefore △ADE∼△ABC\triangle ADE\sim\triangle ABC△ADE∼△ABC by AA.

    First add the two parts of the left side.

    AB=7+5,=12.\begin{aligned}AB&=7+5,\\&=12.\end{aligned}AB​=7+5,=12.​

    Use small-side lengths over whole-triangle lengths.

    8.4AC=712,AC=14.4.\begin{aligned}\frac{8.4}{AC}&=\frac 7{12},\\AC&=14.4.\end{aligned}AC8.4​AC​=127​,=14.4.​

    The requested segment is the leftover part of ACACAC.

    EC=14.4−8.4,=6.\begin{aligned}EC&=14.4-8.4,\\&=6.\end{aligned}EC​=14.4−8.4,=6.​

  9. Problem 9 Test a claim about isosceles triangles

    Consider the claim “Any two nondegenerate isosceles triangles are similar.” Decide whether the claim is true. If it is false, give two possible angle sets that refute it and explain why they work.

    1. Hint 1

      An isosceles triangle has two equal base angles, but its vertex angle can vary.

    2. Hint 2

      Choose different vertex angles and use the triangle-angle sum to determine the base angles.

    Answer

    The claim is false. One counterexample uses angle sets 52°, 64°, 64° and 86°, 47°, 47°; each is isosceles, but their angle sets do not match.

    Full solution

    Take one triangle with vertex angle 52∘52^\circ52∘.

    Its two equal base angles each measure 64∘64^\circ64∘.

    180∘−52∘2=64∘.\frac{180^\circ-52^\circ}{2}=64^\circ.2180∘−52∘​=64∘.

    Take another with vertex angle 86∘86^\circ86∘.

    Its two base angles each measure 47∘47^\circ47∘.

    180∘−86∘2=47∘.\frac{180^\circ-86^\circ}{2}=47^\circ.2180∘−86∘​=47∘.

    Each angle set is positive, sums to 180∘180^\circ180∘, and has equal base angles, so each describes a nondegenerate isosceles triangle.

    No complete angle matching is possible between the sets.

    Similarity would preserve all angle measures, so these triangles refute the claim.

    Answer

    The claim is false. One counterexample uses angle sets 52°, 64°, 64° and 86°, 47°, 47°; each is isosceles, but their angle sets do not match.

    Key idea

    A shared triangle type does not guarantee matching angle measures.
