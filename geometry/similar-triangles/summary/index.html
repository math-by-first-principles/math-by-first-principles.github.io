Similarity and Proportion: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Preimage and image
The preimage is the original figure; the image is the transformed figure. A prime, as in P′P'P′, names an image point.
Dilation and positive scale factor
A dilation resizes from one center using one positive factor kkk in every direction. Enlargement: factor greater than one; reduction: between zero and one; identity: one.
Fixed point
A point whose image is its own location. A line remaining unchanged as a set does not mean every point on it is fixed.
Similarity
A positive dilation together with translations, rotations, or reflections maps one whole figure exactly onto the other. Congruence is included at factor 111.
Correspondence
The ordered matching of vertices, angles, and side endpoints. In △ABC∼△PQR\triangle ABC\sim\triangle PQR△ABC∼△PQR, the pairs are AAA with PPP, BBB with QQQ, and CCC with RRR.
Proportional lengths
Matched length ratios agree in one consistent direction. A ratio of two sides within one triangle need not be the factor between two triangles.
Included angle
The angle formed by the two selected sides, at their common endpoint. For sides ABABAB and ACACAC, it is ∠BAC\angle BAC∠BAC.
Midsegment
A segment joining the midpoints of two triangle sides. The three midsegments enclose the central, half-size triangle.
Internal angle bisector
A segment from a triangle vertex to the opposite side that divides the interior angle into two equal angles. Its endpoint lies strictly inside that side.
Uniform map scale
One length ratio applies in every direction. A scale 1:n1:n1:n compares drawing length with real length in common units.

    • Identity factor: at k=1k=1k=1, every point is fixed and every line maps onto itself.

  • Corresponding sides and reverse factors

    PQAB=kQRBC=kPRAC=kkreverse=1k\begin{gathered} \frac{PQ}{AB}=k\\ \frac{QR}{BC}=k\\ \frac{PR}{AC}=k\\ k_{\rm reverse}=\frac1k \end{gathered}ABPQ​=kBCQR​=kACPR​=kkreverse​=k1​​

    The blue triangle has A at its lower left corner, B to the right, and C above A. The larger red dashed triangle has P at its upper right corner, Q to the left, and R below P. The matching is A to P, B to Q, and C to R despite their different orientations.

    Use when Similarity △ABC∼△PQR\triangle ABC\sim\triangle PQR△ABC∼△PQR is already established. The positive factor maps the first triangle to the second; corresponding angles are equal. Factor one means congruence.

  • AA similarity

    Two equal corresponding angle pairs at distinct vertices establish triangle similarity.

    Use when Both triangles are nondegenerate. The angle sum supplies a third angle match; proportional sides follow from similarity, not from the angle sum alone.

  • SSS similarity

    Three corresponding side ratios equal to one positive factor establish triangle similarity.

    Use when Both side triples must form nondegenerate triangles. Preserve one vertex matching and the same ratio direction for all three pairs.

    e.g. Test side triples 7,11,157,11,157,11,15 and 9.8,15.4,219.8,15.4,219.8,15.4,21: similar by SSS, with first-to-second factor 1.41.41.4.

  • SAS similarity

    Two equal positive corresponding side ratios and equal included angles establish triangle similarity.

    Use when Both triangles are nondegenerate. The equal angles must lie between the two sides actually compared in each triangle.

Problem types, step by step

Find an image point or recover a center

  1. Subtract the center coordinates, scale both displacements, then add the center coordinates back. Successive dilations about the same center multiply their factors.
  2. If the factor and a point-image pair are given, solve the two coordinate equations for the center. For k=1k=1k=1, unchanged points allow any center; a moved point rules out identity.
  3. Check every stated point mapping against the same center, rays, and factor.

Establish triangle similarity and find a length

  1. Find angle matches from stated facts, or compare all three side pairs; use shortest-to-shortest order when no correspondence is given.
  2. Choose AA, SSS, or included-angle SAS from the actual evidence, and write an ordered similarity statement.
  3. Form a ratio with corresponding sides in one direction. Solve, then check positive lengths and triangle feasibility.

Read nested or crossing triangles

  1. Name each complete triangle. A shared angle or a vertical-angle pair supplies only one pair; establish another pair from givens such as parallel lines.
  2. Write the correspondence before comparing lengths. For crossing triangles, retain the intersection's matching position.
  3. Distinguish a triangle side from a leftover part or a whole crossing segment; add or subtract only after finding the needed side.

Prove or disprove an interior parallel cut

  1. Check nondegeneracy and both interior point conditions; compute the two first-part-to-leftover ratios.
  2. Equal ratios establish parallelism by the side-splitter converse. Unequal ratios rule it out by the direct theorem.

Calculate midsegments and repeated halves

  1. Identify which outer side each midpoint pair faces, then halve that side; retain the parallel conclusion too.
  2. For central triangle DEFDEFDEF, match with CBACBACBA. Repeat the half-length factor for a further midpoint construction; reverse the factor when recovering the outer side.

Compare intervals between parallel cross-lines

  1. Match intervals bounded by the same cross-lines on the two transversals.
  2. If endpoint distances from an intersection are supplied, subtract on one side of it and add when the interval crosses it. A cross-line through the intersection has zero endpoint distances.
  3. Use proportional intervals; for parallel transversals, use equality of corresponding intervals.

Construct exact segment divisions

  1. Draw an auxiliary ray from AAA off line ABABAB. For nnn equal parts, mark nnn equal compass steps; for positive integer ratio p:qp:qp:q, mark p+qp+qp+q steps.
  2. Join the last step to BBB. Construct parallels through the earlier steps for equal parts, or through step ppp for the ratio.
  3. For two supplied positive lengths, copy the first and then the second consecutively on the ray; join the last endpoint to BBB and construct the parallel through their joining point.

Use or identify an internal bisector

  1. Match opposite-side parts to neighboring adjacent sides, retaining endpoint order.
  2. For a known whole base, divide it into the adjacent-side ratio parts. For a proposed bisector, compare ratios and apply the converse with an interior endpoint.
  3. Check part sums, positivity, and the triangle inequality when lengths are supplied algebraically.

Justify an indirect measurement

  1. Identify the model's angle pairs and verify its physical and geometric assumptions before invoking AA.
  2. Match vertical heights with vertical heights and the correct ground legs with ground legs. In a survey, distinguish the perpendicular crossing from the slanted sight line.
  3. Calculate in common units, then round the final estimate to the requested precision. A missing premise calls for checking or repairing the setup.

e.g. In a valid shadow model, staff height and shadow are 1.351.351.35 m and 0.900.900.90 m; tree shadow is 14.214.214.2 m. Find tree height to the nearest tenth: approximately 21.321.321.3 m.

Convert a map, route, or boundary

  1. Convert lengths to common units and name the direction: drawing to real uses nnn; real to drawing uses its reciprocal.
  2. For a route or perimeter, add all requested segments, then apply the uniform length factor. For a uniform reprint, update the drawing-to-real ratio using its new length factor.

e.g. On a uniform 1:45001:45001:4500 map, a path measures 6.26.26.2 cm; find its real length in meters: 279279279 m.

Exam traps

  • Trap Treating a stretch as a dilation

    Fix One center, one positive factor, and the ray condition must work throughout. Different directional length factors rule out a dilation, even if one angle stays unchanged.

  • Trap Multiplying angle measures by the length factor

    Fix Dilations preserve angle measures. Only lengths and perimeter use the positive length factor.

  • Trap Using position instead of named correspondence

    Fix Follow the angle and endpoint matches through turns and reflections. Reverse both ratios together, or take the reciprocal of the mapping factor.

  • Trap Replacing a similarity justification with the triangle-angle sum

    Fix The angle sum supplies only the third angle. Scale a side to match, then use ASA for the included side or AAS for another side; SSS and SAS use their corresponding earlier congruence criteria.

  • Trap Calling insufficient evidence proof of dissimilarity

    Fix One angle, two side ratios alone, or a nonincluded angle do not guarantee similarity. A failed named matching rules out that matching; consider other matchings before ruling out every similarity.

  • Trap Mixing a part with its whole

    Fix Name the denominator: DBDBDB differs from ABABAB, DCDCDC from BCBCBC, and mirror ground leg MBMBMB from whole FBFBFB or slanted EMEMEM.

  • Trap Assuming an angle bisector halves the opposite side

    Fix For an internal bisector, equal side parts occur exactly when the adjacent sides are equal. The two small triangles are not automatically similar; perpendicular-distance statements are a different topic.

  • Trap Measuring instead of constructing exact divisions

    Fix Keep a fixed compass opening for equal steps and construct the parallels. Supplied segment ratios copy those given lengths; they do not assert that every real length is constructible.

  • Trap Trusting a model or a distorted print without its hypotheses

    Fix Verify the angle relationships and a common directional scale. Without a needed assumption, the numerical result is not guaranteed; it need not be impossible.

  • Trap Reporting a rounded measurement as an exact equality

    Fix Keep precision during calculation; report the final measurement with approximately or ≈\approx≈. Agreement between methods supports a check without proving every modeling assumption.
