  1. 1

    No vertex matching is supplied. Triangles have side lengths 5,8,105, 8, 105,8,10 and 7.5,12,167.5, 12, 167.5,12,16. Which conclusion is correct?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    A positive scale factor preserves size order, so sorted sides must correspond.

    7.55=1.5,128=1.5,1610=1.6.\begin{aligned}\frac{7.5}{5}&=1.5,\\\frac{12}{8}&=1.5,\\\frac{16}{10}&=1.6.\end{aligned}57.5​812​1016​​=1.5,=1.5,=1.6.​

    The last ratio differs. Both triples satisfy the triangle inequality, but no alternative matching can repair the unequal sorted-side ratios.

    For one proposed SAS correspondence, the target-to-original side ratios are 5/35/35/3 and 3/53/53/5, and the included angles are equal. What fails?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The two ratios are reciprocal rather than equal.

    53≠35.\frac 53\ne\frac 35.35​=53​.

    That proposed correspondence does not meet SAS. This alone does not settle every other possible matching.

  3. 3

    Under a specified correspondence, original sides are x+2x+2x+2, x+6x+6x+6, and 101010, while image sides are 999, 151515, and yyy. Which pair makes SSS similarity hold with positive triangle lengths?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    First make the first two side ratios agree, then apply the resulting factor to the third.

    9(x+6)=15(x+2),9x+54=15x+30,x=4,k=96,=1.5,y=1.5⋅10,=15.\begin{aligned}9(x+6)&=15(x+2),\\9x+54&=15x+30,\\x&=4,\\k&=\frac 96,\\&=1.5,\\y&=1.5\cdot 10,\\&=15.\end{aligned}9(x+6)9x+54xky​=15(x+2),=15x+30,=4,=69​,=1.5,=1.5⋅10,=15.​

    The original triple is 6, 10, 10 and the image triple is 9, 15, 15; both form nondegenerate triangles.

  4. 4

    All three corresponding side ratios equal 2. A given equal angle pair happens to be nonincluded for two selected sides. Which conclusion is justified?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The three proportional side pairs establish SSS similarity independently of any angle information. A nonincluded angle blocks that particular SAS argument, not a valid SSS argument.

  5. 5

    In an actual counterexample, two side ratios equal 1 and a nonincluded angle pair is equal, but the complete angle sets differ. What does this show?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    One actual pair satisfying the proposed data but failing similarity refutes a universal criterion. It does not claim that every pair with those data is dissimilar.

  6. 6

    Two right triangles have legs 5,125, 125,12 and 8,19.28, 19.28,19.2. Which criterion establishes similarity without finding either hypotenuse?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The two corresponding leg ratios agree, and their included angles are both right angles.

    85=1.6,19.212=1.6.\begin{aligned}\frac 85&=1.6,\\\frac{19.2}{12}&=1.6.\end{aligned}58​1219.2​​=1.6,=1.6.​

    SAS applies directly; no hypotenuse calculation or later right-triangle formula is required.

    Triangles satisfy

    AB=ACDE=DF∠B=∠E,=52∘.\begin{aligned}AB&=AC\\DE&=DF\\\angle B&=\angle E,\\&=52^\circ.\end{aligned}ABDE∠B​=AC=DF=∠E,=52∘.​

    Why can similarity be proved despite the given angle being nonincluded for those equal-side pairs?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The equal sides give equal base angles inside each triangle.

    m∠C=52∘,m∠F=52∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle C&=52^\circ,\\m\angle F&=52^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠Cm∠F​=52∘,=52∘.​

    The B-to-E and C-to-F pairs establish AA. Extra isosceles information supplies a valid route; it does not create a general nonincluded-angle criterion.

  9. 9

    Why is zero excluded from the common factor in SSS and SAS similarity?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The criteria concern nondegenerate triangles with positive side lengths. A factor of zero makes every image length zero, so there is no triangle image.

  10. 10

    A proposed SSS proof starts by assuming that proportional sides already imply equal angles, then uses the claimed similarity to justify those angles. Which repair avoids that circular step?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    After a dilation, the matching sides are equal. SSS congruence then supplies the rigid-motion match, from which similarity and equal corresponding angles follow.

    One triangle has side lengths 5,7,115, 7, 115,7,11. Another has side lengths 10,22,q10, 22,q10,22,q, with no correspondence specified. Which qqq makes the triangles similar?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The ratio of the two known lengths in the second triangle is 22/10. Among the first triangle side pairs, only 11/5 has that ratio. Thus 5 matches 10 and 11 matches 22.

    q=2⋅7,=14.\begin{aligned}q&=2\cdot 7,\\&=14.\end{aligned}q​=2⋅7,=14.​
