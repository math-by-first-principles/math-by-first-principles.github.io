SSS and SAS Similarity

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Watch the video explainer

Intuition

A triangle can be specified by its side lengths as well as its angles. If every side of one triangle is one and a half times its match in another, resizing the first triangle makes all three lengths fit. Two sides can also be enough, provided we know the opening between them. The position of that angle is part of the evidence.

Learning goals

  • Establish triangle similarity by checking three corresponding side ratios for SSS
  • Apply SAS similarity using two proportional sides and their included angle
  • Justify SSS and SAS similarity with dilation followed by congruence
  • Distinguish insufficient evidence from a demonstrated failure of triangle similarity

Three side ratios establish SSS similarity

Three matching lengths can establish similarity without any angle measurement. Triangles with side lengths 5,7,95, 7, 95,7,9 and 7.5,10.5,13.57.5, 10.5, 13.57.5,10.5,13.5 have the same factor 1.51.51.5 for every matched pair. Scaling the first triangle by 1.51.51.5 gives exactly the three lengths of the second.

SSS similarity says that two nondegenerate triangles are similar if all three pairs of corresponding sides have the same positive length ratio. This is different from SSS congruence, where the corresponding lengths are equal. Congruence is the factor-111 case.

Why SSS similarity follows from SSS congruence#

For the numerical triangles above, dilate the 5,7,95, 7, 95,7,9 triangle by 1.51.51.5. Its new lengths are 7.5,10.5,13.57.5, 10.5, 13.57.5,10.5,13.5. By the earlier SSS congruence criterion, the resized triangle is congruent to the target. Rigid motions can therefore complete the match, giving similarity.

The same argument works with any positive side lengths forming nondegenerate triangles. Suppose the chosen correspondence gives

DEAB=k,EFBC=k,FDCA=k.\begin{aligned}\frac{DE}{AB}&=k,\\\frac{EF}{BC}&=k,\\\frac{FD}{CA}&=k.\end{aligned}ABDE​BCEF​CAFD​​=k,=k,=k.​

Dilate ABCABCABC by kkk, calling the image A′B′C′A'B'C'A′B′C′. The accepted length-scaling property gives

A′B′=k⋅AB,=DE,B′C′=k⋅BC,=EF,C′A′=k⋅CA,=FD.\begin{aligned}A'B'&=k\cdot AB,\\&=DE,\\B'C'&=k\cdot BC,\\&=EF,\\C'A'&=k\cdot CA,\\&=FD.\end{aligned}A′B′B′C′C′A′​=k⋅AB,=DE,=k⋅BC,=EF,=k⋅CA,=FD.​

All three matching lengths are now equal, so △A′B′C′≅△DEF\triangle A'B'C'\cong\triangle DEF△A′B′C′≅△DEF by SSS congruence. Its rigid-motion match, together with the dilation, proves △ABC∼△DEF\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF△ABC∼△DEF. The proof uses congruence to establish similarity, not similarity to justify itself.

∎

Worked example 1 Check all three matched pairs

Problem. The sides of ABCABCABC are

AB=8 cmBC=11 cmCA=13 cm.\begin{aligned}AB&=8\text{ cm}\\BC&=11\text{ cm}\\CA&=13\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}ABBCCA​=8 cm=11 cm=13 cm.​

The sides of DEFDEFDEF are

DE=12 cmEF=16.5 cmFD=19.5 cm.\begin{aligned}DE&=12\text{ cm}\\EF&=16.5\text{ cm}\\FD&=19.5\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}DEEFFD​=12 cm=16.5 cm=19.5 cm.​

Decide whether the ordered statement △ABC∼△DEF\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF△ABC∼△DEF is justified, and state the factor from ABCABCABC to DEFDEFDEF.

Solution. Keep target lengths over original lengths in every ratio.

DEAB=128,=1.5,EFBC=16.511,=1.5,FDCA=19.513,=1.5.\begin{aligned}\frac{DE}{AB}&=\frac{12}{8},\\&=1.5,\\\frac{EF}{BC}&=\frac{16.5}{11},\\&=1.5,\\\frac{FD}{CA}&=\frac{19.5}{13},\\&=1.5.\end{aligned}ABDE​BCEF​CAFD​​=812​,=1.5,=1116.5​,=1.5,=1319.5​,=1.5.​

All three ratios agree, so SSS proves the ordered similarity, with scale factor 1.51.51.5. The positive triples satisfy the triangle inequality, so neither describes a flat or impossible triangle.

Check your understanding

The corresponding side ratios for two nondegenerate triangles are 1.61.61.6, 1.61.61.6, and 1.61.61.6. What is justified?

Answer choices

SSS similarity requires one common positive ratio for all three side pairs. The factor need not be an integer. Congruence would require the common factor to be one.

Preserve one matching and one ratio direction

When a correspondence is given, test that correspondence. When no matching is specified, a positive factor preserves the order of side lengths, so compare shortest with shortest, middle with middle, and longest with longest. Then recover the vertex matching from the side endpoints.

Worked example 2 Two matching ratios do not settle the third

Problem. One triangle has side lengths 6,9,126, 9, 126,9,12 and another has side lengths 10,15,2110, 15, 2110,15,21. No correspondence is specified. Are the triangles similar? Explain why another ordering cannot repair a failed test.

Solution. Both triples are positive and satisfy the triangle inequality. Under any similarity, the shortest sides must match, as must the middle and longest sides.

106=53,159=53,2112=74.\begin{aligned}\frac{10}{6}&=\frac53,\\\frac{15}{9}&=\frac53,\\\frac{21}{12}&=\frac74.\end{aligned}610​915​1221​​=35​,=35​,=47​.​

The last ratio differs, so no one scale factor matches all three sides. The triangles are not similar. Matching the sides in a different order would pair unequal size ranks, which a positive scale factor cannot do.

If only a proposed named correspondence fails, say that it fails; another correspondence may work. Here the sorted-side argument rules out all of them.

Two sides need their included angle for SAS

Two lengths and the opening between them can also fix a scaled shape. Suppose two sides of one triangle are 444 and 777, and their included angle is 62∘62^\circ62∘. A second triangle has corresponding sides 666 and 10.510.510.5 with the same included angle. Both sides scale by 1.51.51.5, and the opening stays fixed.

An included angle is the angle formed by the two sides being compared. For ABABAB and ACACAC, it is ∠BAC\angle BAC∠BAC, at AAA. For ABABAB and BCBCBC, it is ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC, at BBB.

SAS similarity says that two nondegenerate triangles are similar if two corresponding side ratios are equal and the angles between those side pairs are equal. The common ratio is positive, and the matched angle must be the included angle in both triangles.

Why SAS similarity follows from SAS congruence#

Dilate the numerical 4,74, 74,7 triangle by 1.51.51.5. The two selected lengths become 666 and 10.510.510.5, while their included angle remains 62∘62^\circ62∘. The resized triangle is congruent to the second by earlier SAS congruence. Thus the original triangles are similar.

In general, suppose

DEAB=k,DFAC=k,\begin{aligned}\frac{DE}{AB}&=k,\\\frac{DF}{AC}&=k,\end{aligned}ABDE​ACDF​​=k,=k,​

and ∠BAC≅∠EDF\angle BAC\cong\angle EDF∠BAC≅∠EDF. These are the included angles at AAA and DDD. Dilate ABCABCABC by kkk. The resulting triangle satisfies

A′B′=DE,A′C′=DF,∠B′A′C′≅∠EDF.\begin{aligned}A'B'&=DE,\\A'C'&=DF,\\\angle B'A'C'&\cong\angle EDF.\end{aligned}A′B′A′C′∠B′A′C′​=DE,=DF,≅∠EDF.​

The first two equalities come from length scaling; the angle equality comes from angle preservation. SAS congruence gives a rigid-motion match between A′B′C′A'B'C'A′B′C′ and DEFDEFDEF. Together with the dilation, that proves △ABC∼△DEF\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF△ABC∼△DEF.

∎

Worked example 3 Establish similarity before using an unknown side

Problem. In △JKL\triangle JKL△JKL,

JK=3 cmJL=8 cmKL=7 cm∠KJL=60∘.\begin{aligned}JK&=3\text{ cm}\\JL&=8\text{ cm}\\KL&=7\text{ cm}\\\angle KJL&=60^\circ.\end{aligned}JKJLKL∠KJL​=3 cm=8 cm=7 cm=60∘.​

In △PQR\triangle PQR△PQR,

QP=7.5 cmQR=20 cm∠PQR=60∘.\begin{aligned}QP&=7.5\text{ cm}\\QR&=20\text{ cm}\\\angle PQR&=60^\circ.\end{aligned}QPQR∠PQR​=7.5 cm=20 cm=60∘.​

Prove a correct ordered similarity statement and find PRPRPR.

Solution. Compare the two sides meeting at the given angle.

QPJK=7.53,=2.5,QRJL=208,=2.5.\begin{aligned}\frac{QP}{JK}&=\frac{7.5}{3},\\&=2.5,\\\frac{QR}{JL}&=\frac{20}{8},\\&=2.5.\end{aligned}JKQP​JLQR​​=37.5​,=2.5,=820​,=2.5.​

The equal 60∘60^\circ60∘ angles are included, so SAS proves △JKL∼△QPR\triangle JKL\sim\triangle QPR△JKL∼△QPR. In this order KLKLKL matches PRPRPR, giving

PR=2.5⋅7,=17.5 cm.\begin{aligned}PR&=2.5\cdot7,\\&=17.5\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}PR​=2.5⋅7,=17.5 cm.​

Check your understanding

The sides being compared are AB,BCAB,BCAB,BC and DE,EFDE,EFDE,EF. Which angle pair must match to apply SAS similarity directly?

Answer choices

AB and BC meet at B. DE and EF meet at E. The included angles must therefore be the angles at B and E.

Check your understanding

In the SAS similarity proof, a dilation first matches the two given sides while preserving their included angle. Which earlier result completes the match?

Answer choices

The resized triangle has two equal corresponding sides and the equal included angle, so earlier SAS congruence applies. Its rigid-motion match, together with the first dilation, establishes similarity.

Insufficient evidence does not mean dissimilar

Worked example 4 Locate the angle before naming the criterion

Problem. The known lengths are

AB=10BC=7DE=15EF=10.5.\begin{aligned}AB&=10\\BC&=7\\DE&=15\\EF&=10.5.\end{aligned}ABBCDEEF​=10=7=15=10.5.​

Also,

∠A=∠D,=30∘.\begin{aligned}\angle A&=\angle D,\\&=30^\circ.\end{aligned}∠A​=∠D,=30∘.​

Does this information establish △ABC∼△DEF\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF△ABC∼△DEF by SAS? State exactly what is missing.

Solution. Both listed side ratios are 1.51.51.5, but the given equal angles are not between those side pairs. SAS would need ∠ABC≅∠DEF\angle ABC\cong\angle DEF∠ABC≅∠DEF.

The stated information does not establish similarity. It also does not establish that the triangles are dissimilar. The missing included-angle equality might hold; it has simply not been supplied. The next example shows why a nonincluded angle cannot replace it as a general criterion.

Worked example 5 An actual counterexample to the nonincluded-angle rule

Problem. Construct a triangle ABDABDABD with angles

A=26∘D=68∘B=86∘.\begin{aligned}A&=26^\circ\\D&=68^\circ\\B&=86^\circ.\end{aligned}ADB​=26∘=68∘=86∘.​

Draw a ray inside angle ABDABDABD making 44∘44^\circ44∘ with BDBDBD, and let it meet ADADAD at CCC. Show that ABCABCABC and ABDABDABD have two pairs of sides in the same ratio and an equal nonincluded angle, yet are not similar.

Solution. The starting triangle exists because the positive angles sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘; its two base angles can be copied at the endpoints of a segment. The 44∘44^\circ44∘ ray lies inside the 86∘86^\circ86∘ angle, so it meets the opposite side between AAA and DDD. Thus A,C,DA,C,DA,C,D are in that order.

In triangle BCDBCDBCD, the angles at BBB and DDD are 44∘44^\circ44∘ and 68∘68^\circ68∘. Its angle at CCC is therefore 68∘68^\circ68∘. The equal base angles give BC=BDBC=BDBC=BD by the isosceles-triangle converse.

Triangles ABCABCABC and ABDABDABD share side ABABAB and the angle at AAA. Hence their two matched side ratios, AB/ABAB/ABAB/AB and BD/BCBD/BCBD/BC, both equal 111, and their equal angle at AAA is nonincluded for these side pairs.

Their angles at BBB are different.

m∠ABC=86∘−44∘,=42∘,m∠ACB=180∘−26∘−42∘,=112∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle ABC&=86^\circ-44^\circ,\\&=42^\circ,\\m\angle ACB&=180^\circ-26^\circ-42^\circ,\\&=112^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠ABCm∠ACB​=86∘−44∘,=42∘,=180∘−26∘−42∘,=112∘.​

The angle sets 26∘,42∘,112∘26^\circ,42^\circ,112^\circ26∘,42∘,112∘ and 26∘,86∘,68∘26^\circ,86^\circ,68^\circ26∘,86∘,68∘ cannot be paired equally in any order. These particular triangles are not similar.

Check your understanding

Two proportional side pairs and an equal nonincluded angle are given. Which statement is always justified from that information alone?

Answer choices

The nonincluded-angle information is not a general similarity criterion. Some pairs satisfying it are similar and some are not, as the constructed counterexample demonstrates.

Choose a criterion from the actual evidence

The earlier AA criterion uses two corresponding angle pairs. SSS uses three proportional side pairs; SAS uses two proportional side pairs and their included angle.

For each problem, fix the correspondence, choose the criterion its givens support, and keep one ratio direction. An unknown side is a consequence of established similarity, not evidence that may be assumed to establish it.

Check your understanding

Which evidence establishes similarity using only side lengths?

Answer choices

SSS similarity uses three proportional corresponding side pairs. Two side pairs alone leave the third side and included angle unconstrained.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Confusing equal with proportional. Congruence needs equal matching lengths; similarity allows one common positive factor.
  • Checking only two SSS ratios. The third corresponding side pair must use that same factor.
  • Missing the included angle. The equal SAS angle must be formed by the two sides actually compared.
  • Reversing one fraction. All ratios in an equality must keep the same mapping direction.
  • Overstating a failed test. Insufficient information does not prove dissimilarity; a failed named matching may leave another matching possible.

Key takeaways

  • SSS similarity uses three equal positive ratios of corresponding side lengths.
  • SAS similarity uses two equal side ratios and equal included angles.
  • Both follow by dilation to match lengths, then an earlier congruence criterion.
  • At factor 111, the same length information gives congruence.
  • Prove similarity from the givens before using it to find a missing side.

A bit of history (optional)

A diagram in an old geometry book can carry marks added centuries after its main text was copied. Henry Mendell’s edition of Euclid’s Book VI, Proposition 5 discusses a diagram based on the manuscript Vatican Greek 190. He dates the main text and diagrams to the ninth century, but identifies its Arabic numerals as later additions, probably from the thirteenth or fourteenth century.

The page therefore preserves more than one reader’s work. Copyists transmitted the words and diagrams; later readers supplied a different numbering system. What looks like one finished illustration can be a record of several periods of use.
