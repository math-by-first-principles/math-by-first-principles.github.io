Proving a Quadrilateral Is a Parallelogram: Practice

Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    Which condition alone can hold in a convex quadrilateral that is not a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The three complete side or angle conditions are sufficient tests. One midpoint condition leaves the other diagonal unrestricted. For example, the perpendicular-diagonal construction with halves 6,66,66,6 and 5,35,35,3 satisfies one bisection and fails the other.

  2. 2

    In convex ABCDABCDABCD, angle measures satisfy the following relations.

    m∠A=p,m∠C=p,m∠B=q,p+q=180∘.\begin{gathered} m\angle A=p,\\ m\angle C=p,\\ m\angle B=q,\\ p+q=180^\circ. \end{gathered}m∠A=p,m∠C=p,m∠B=q,p+q=180∘.​

    What follows for angle DDD?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Use the quadrilateral angle sum:

    m∠D=360∘−2p−q,m∠D=2(p+q)−2p−q,m∠D=q.\begin{aligned} m\angle D&=360^\circ-2p-q,\\ m\angle D&=2(p+q)-2p-q,\\ m\angle D&=q. \end{aligned}m∠Dm∠Dm∠D​=360∘−2p−q,=2(p+q)−2p−q,=q.​

    Thus the second opposite pair is congruent too, completing the angle test.

  3. 3

    A proposed test uses only ∠A≅∠C\angle A\cong\angle C∠A≅∠C. Which sequence of interior angles, in boundary order, is a valid counterexample?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The proposed counterexample has equal angles A,CA,CA,C but unequal angles B,DB,DB,D, which excludes a parallelogram. It exists: two right triangles on opposite sides of a common hypotenuse can have their other angles 30∘,60∘30^\circ,60^\circ30∘,60∘, joined so the combined angles at the hypotenuse endpoints are 60∘60^\circ60∘ and 120∘120^\circ120∘.

    All three other choices have both opposite-angle pairs equal, so they are parallelograms.

  4. 4

    Convex ABCDABCDABCD has a parallel pair and a congruent pair.

    AB∥CD,AD=BC.\begin{gathered} AB\parallel CD,\\ AD=BC. \end{gathered}AB∥CD,AD=BC.​

    Which further measurement would establish that the shape is not a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A parallelogram must have congruent opposite sides. Unequal values 121212 and 666 for AB,CDAB,CDAB,CD contradict that necessary property. Congruent sloping sides, an obtuse angle, and unequal diagonals can all occur in parallelograms.

  5. 5

    Diagonals of convex ABCDABCDABCD meet at EEE. Their parts have lengths in the same unit.

    AE=4s−5,EC=2s+7,BE=3s−1,ED=s+11.\begin{gathered} AE=4s-5,\\ EC=2s+7,\\ BE=3s-1,\\ ED=s+11. \end{gathered}AE=4s−5,EC=2s+7,BE=3s−1,ED=s+11.​

    Which value of sss guarantees a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The first midpoint equality gives

    4s−5=2s+7,s=6.\begin{aligned} 4s-5&=2s+7,\\ s&=6. \end{aligned}4s−5s​=2s+7,=6.​

    The second gives

    3s−1=s+11,s=6.\begin{aligned} 3s-1&=s+11,\\ s&=6. \end{aligned}3s−1s​=s+11,=6.​

    At this value, both half-diagonal pairs are positive and equal within their own diagonal.

  6. 6

    Convex ABCDABCDABCD has this evidence about one side pair.

    AB∥CD,AB=CD.\begin{gathered} AB\parallel CD,\\ AB=CD. \end{gathered}AB∥CD,AB=CD.​

    Using diagonal BDBDBD, SAS proves △ABD≅△CDB\triangle ABD\cong\triangle CDB△ABD≅△CDB. Which corresponding-angle equality proves the remaining sides parallel?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    To prove AD∥BCAD\parallel BCAD∥BC, use BDBDBD as transversal. Its alternate interior angles are ∠ADB\angle ADB∠ADB and ∠CBD\angle CBD∠CBD, corresponding under the given triangle order.

    Diagonals of convex ABCDABCDABCD bisect each other at EEE. A proof first obtains one side equality:

    AB=CD.AB=CD.AB=CD.

    It uses the triangle congruence

    △AEB≅△CED.\triangle AEB\cong\triangle CED.△AEB≅△CED.

    Which triangle comparison supplies the missing opposite-side equality?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Triangles BEC,DEABEC,DEABEC,DEA have equal half-diagonals around equal vertical angles, so SAS applies. Their corresponding sides give BC=DABC=DABC=DA. Together with the first opposite-side equality, this completes the side test.

    Points A,E,CA,E,CA,E,C are collinear, so AECAECAEC is not a triangle.

  8. 8

    Which collection of exact data cannot hold for a convex quadrilateral ABCDABCDABCD, whose diagonals meet at EEE?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The first two facts in the incompatible collection prove a parallelogram by the one-pair test. Its diagonals must then bisect each other, contradicting AE≠ECAE\ne ECAE=EC.

    Each other collection describes valid complete parallelogram evidence with positive lengths or valid interior angles.

  9. 9

    You may retain facts from this record about convex ABCDABCDABCD.

    AB∥CD,AB=CD,BC=AD,∠A≅∠C,∠B≅∠D.\begin{gathered} AB\parallel CD,\\ AB=CD,\\ BC=AD,\\ \angle A\cong\angle C,\\ \angle B\cong\angle D. \end{gathered}AB∥CD,AB=CD,BC=AD,∠A≅∠C,∠B≅∠D.​

    Which listed record uses the fewest facts while still guaranteeing a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The two facts attach parallelism and congruence to the same opposite-side pair, so they suffice. Either one alone can hold in a quadrilateral that is not a parallelogram. The three-fact option is sufficient but contains an extra fact.

  10. 10

    In convex ABCDABCDABCD, diagonals meet at EEE. The parts have lengths in centimeters.

    AE=2v+1,EC=13,BE=v+2,ED=7.\begin{gathered} AE=2v+1,\\ EC=13,\\ BE=v+2,\\ ED=7. \end{gathered}AE=2v+1,EC=13,BE=v+2,ED=7.​

    Which statement about making both diagonals bisect each other is correct?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    For ACACAC to be bisected,

    2v+1=13,2v+1=13,2v+1=13,

    which requires v=6v=6v=6. For BDBDBD to be bisected,

    v+2=7,v+2=7,v+2=7,

    which requires v=5v=5v=5. The same parameter cannot take both values, so this test has no solution.

  11. 11

    In convex ABCDABCDABCD, two consecutive-angle sums are known.

    m∠A+m∠B=180∘,m∠B+m∠C=180∘.\begin{gathered} m\angle A+m\angle B=180^\circ,\\ m\angle B+m\angle C=180^\circ. \end{gathered}m∠A+m∠B=180∘,m∠B+m∠C=180∘.​

    Which explanation proves it is a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The first sum concerns the same-side interior angles along transversal ABABAB, so it proves AD∥BCAD\parallel BCAD∥BC.

    The second concerns the same-side interior angles along transversal BCBCBC, so it proves AB∥CDAB\parallel CDAB∥CD.

    Both parallel pairs establish the definition; the angles need not all be right.

  12. 12

    Convex ABCDABCDABCD has AB=CDAB=CDAB=CD. Which additional condition alone guarantees a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Adding AB∥CDAB\parallel CDAB∥CD makes the same opposite-side pair both parallel and congruent, a sufficient test.

    Perpendicular opposite sides do not meet this test. Perpendicular diagonals or a further adjacent-side equality do not ensure the second opposite-side equality or a parallel pair.
