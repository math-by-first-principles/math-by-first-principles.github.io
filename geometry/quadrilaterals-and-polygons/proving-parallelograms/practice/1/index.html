Proving a Quadrilateral Is a Parallelogram: Practice

12 multiple-choice questions, progressively harder.

Level 1 · Foundational 0 / 12 answered
Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    Convex quadrilateral ABCDABCDABCD has the following side equalities.

    AB=CD,BC=AD.\begin{gathered} AB=CD,\\ BC=AD. \end{gathered}AB=CD,BC=AD.​

    Which conclusion is guaranteed?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Both opposite-side pairs are congruent. The proved converse guarantees both opposite-side pairs parallel, so ABCDABCDABCD is a parallelogram.

    Neither right angles, equal diagonals, nor four equal sides is required.

  2. 2

    Diagonals ACACAC and BDBDBD of convex ABCDABCDABCD meet at EEE. Given AE=ECAE=ECAE=EC, which extra equality completes the diagonal-bisection test?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The two halves of ACACAC already match. The missing midpoint condition is on BDBDBD:

    BE=ED.BE=ED.BE=ED.

    Equalities between different diagonals do not replace it.

  3. 3

    Which angle evidence alone guarantees that a convex quadrilateral ABCDABCDABCD is a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The opposite-angle test requires both opposite-angle pairs. Then the quadrilateral angle sum makes every consecutive pair supplementary, proving both parallel pairs.

    m∠A+m∠B=180∘m∠B+m∠C=180∘\begin{gathered}m\angle A+m\angle B=180^\circ\\m\angle B+m\angle C=180^\circ\end{gathered}m∠A+m∠B=180∘m∠B+m∠C=180∘​
  4. 4

    Convex ABCDABCDABCD has AB∥CDAB\parallel CDAB∥CD. Which extra fact completes the test on that same opposite-side pair?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The one-pair test needs the parallel pair itself to be congruent. Thus the required extra fact is

    AB=CD.AB=CD.AB=CD.

    An equality on another side pair or on the diagonals does not complete this test.

  5. 5

    Having at least one congruent opposite-side pair is which kind of condition for a convex quadrilateral to be a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Every parallelogram has both opposite-side pairs congruent, so it has at least one such pair. But a quadrilateral with unequal parallel bases and congruent sloping sides has one congruent opposite pair and is not a parallelogram.

  6. 6

    In convex ABCDABCDABCD, diagonals meet at EEE, and AE=ECAE=ECAE=EC. Nothing else is known. Which conclusion is justified?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Only ACACAC has been shown to be bisected. A parallelogram is possible, but the other diagonal might have unequal halves. Both midpoint conditions are required.

  7. 7

    In the figure, draw diagonal ACACAC in convex ABCDABCDABCD. Which fact supplies the third side match for an SSS comparison of triangles ABCABCABC and CDACDACDA?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The two opposite-side matches are given. The triangles share segment ACACAC. This supplies the reflexive third side match.

    AC=CA.AC=CA.AC=CA.
  8. 8

    In convex ABCDABCDABCD, diagonals meet at EEE. If ∠BAE≅∠DCE\angle BAE\cong\angle DCE∠BAE≅∠DCE, which parallel pair follows by the alternate interior-angle converse?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The rays AEAEAE and CECECE lie on transversal ACACAC. The other angle sides lie on ABABAB and CDCDCD, so these are the lines proved parallel.

  9. 9

    The interior angles of convex ABCDABCDABCD, in boundary order, are 71∘71^\circ71∘, 109∘109^\circ109∘, 71∘71^\circ71∘, and 109∘109^\circ109∘. Why must it be a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Angles A,CA,CA,C both measure 71∘71^\circ71∘, and angles B,DB,DB,D both measure 109∘109^\circ109∘. Both opposite-angle pairs are congruent, which is a sufficient test.

  10. 10

    A convex quadrilateral has one opposite-side pair parallel. What does this fact alone guarantee?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    One parallel pair does not establish the two pairs in the definition of a parallelogram. A quadrilateral with unequal parallel bases is a counterexample to the stronger conclusions.

  11. 11

    Diagonals of convex ABCDABCDABCD meet at EEE. The two halves of ACACAC are each 444 cm, and the two halves of BDBDBD are each 666 cm. What follows?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Each diagonal is bisected at EEE. The bisection test is sufficient, and it does not require the two whole diagonals to be congruent.

    AE=ECBE=ED\begin{gathered}AE=EC\\BE=ED\end{gathered}AE=ECBE=ED​
  12. 12

    Convex ABCDABCDABCD has the following equalities between adjacent sides.

    AB=AD,BC=CD.\begin{gathered} AB=AD,\\ BC=CD. \end{gathered}AB=AD,BC=CD.​

    What follows about a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The equalities join adjacent sides, not opposite sides. For example, place B,DB,DB,D on one line through EEE, each 666 units from EEE. Place A,CA,CA,C on a perpendicular through EEE, respectively 555 and 333 units from EEE. The two right triangles above BDBDBD have matching legs, as do the two below it, giving exactly the required adjacent-side pairs.

    The two parts of ACACAC have unequal lengths, so the example is not a parallelogram:

    AE≠EC.AE\ne EC.AE=EC.

    A parallelogram with all sides equal can also satisfy the givens, so the givens neither force nor exclude a parallelogram.
