  1. Problem 1 The side record

    Convex quadrilateral ABCDABCDABCD has side lengths in centimeters.

    AB=15CD=15BC=9AD=9\begin{gathered}AB=15\\CD=15\\BC=9\\AD=9\end{gathered}AB=15CD=15BC=9AD=9​

    Decide whether these measurements guarantee a parallelogram. Name the sufficient test, or explain why none is complete.

    Text description of this figure

    Convex quadrilateral ABCD has vertices named in boundary order. The first and third sides have matching single ticks. The second and fourth sides have matching double ticks.

    1. Hint 1

      Match each side with the side opposite it, using boundary order.

    2. Hint 2

      A complete side test requires an equality in each opposite-side pair.

    Answer

    Yes; both opposite-side pairs are congruent.

    Full solution

    The first opposite-side pair has AB=CDAB=CDAB=CD.

    The second opposite-side pair has BC=ADBC=ADBC=AD.

    Both opposite-side pairs are congruent, so the side converse proves ABCDABCDABCD is a parallelogram.

    This conclusion uses exact givens rather than how the drawing looks.

    Answer

    Yes; both opposite-side pairs are congruent.

    Key idea

    Both opposite-side equalities complete a sufficient test.

  2. Problem 2 Two crossing records

    In each separate case, ABCDABCDABCD is convex and its diagonals AC,BDAC,BDAC,BD meet at EEE. Decide whether the evidence proves a parallelogram, excludes a parallelogram, or leaves both possibilities. Give a reason. All lengths use the same unit.

    Case A:

    AE=3EC=3BE=8ED=8\begin{gathered}AE=3\\EC=3\\BE=8\\ED=8\end{gathered}AE=3EC=3BE=8ED=8​

    Case B:

    AE=4EC=4BE=6ED=9\begin{gathered}AE=4\\EC=4\\BE=6\\ED=9\end{gathered}AE=4EC=4BE=6ED=9​

    1. Hint 1

      Check each diagonal separately at the common crossing.

    2. Hint 2

      A complete midpoint test proves a parallelogram; failure of a necessary midpoint condition excludes one.

    Answer

    Case A: proves a parallelogram. Case B: excludes a parallelogram.

    Full solution

    Case A: the two parts of ACACAC match, and the two parts of BDBDBD match.

    Both diagonals bisect each other, so the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

    Case B: BDBDBD is not bisected because its parts have lengths 666 and 999.

    Every parallelogram has both diagonals bisected, so this quadrilateral is not a parallelogram.

    Answer

    Case A: proves a parallelogram. Case B: excludes a parallelogram.

    Key idea

    Incomplete evidence leaves a question open, but a known violation of a necessary property settles it negatively.

    ▾

  3. Problem 3 A paper construction

    Draw two nonparallel lines through a point EEE. On the first line, place A,CA,CA,C on opposite sides of EEE, each 555 cm from EEE. On the second, place B,DB,DB,D on opposite sides of EEE, each 777 cm from EEE. Connect the four endpoints in boundary order A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D.

    Mark only the given equalities. Explain why the constructed quadrilateral is convex and why it must be a parallelogram. Does the argument require the two lines to be perpendicular?

    1. Hint 1

      Think about how four rays from one crossing alternate around the crossing.

    2. Hint 2

      Check the midpoint of each diagonal, and keep unequal whole diagonals distinct from unequal halves.

    Answer

    The alternating endpoints form a convex parallelogram; perpendicular lines are not required.

    Full solution

    The two nonparallel lines make four rays in alternating order.

    Taking one point on each ray and joining them in that order gives a simple convex quadrilateral: each vertex lies between the neighboring ray directions, and each interior angle is less than a straight angle.

    The construction gives the midpoint equalities

    AE=ECBE=ED\begin{gathered}AE=EC\\BE=ED\end{gathered}AE=ECBE=ED​

    Thus both diagonals bisect each other, proving a parallelogram.

    No step required a right angle between the diagonals.

    Answer

    The alternating endpoints form a convex parallelogram; perpendicular lines are not required.

    Key idea

    A shared midpoint forces a parallelogram for any two nonparallel diagonal directions.

  4. Problem 4 A proof from two side pairs

    Convex ABCDABCDABCD satisfies

    AB=CDAD=BC\begin{gathered}AB=CD\\AD=BC\end{gathered}AB=CDAD=BC​

    Prove that both opposite-side pairs are parallel. Use diagonal BDBDBD and triangle congruence, rather than citing the parallelogram side test as a finished theorem.

    Text description of this figure

    Convex quadrilateral ABCD has vertices named in boundary order. The first and third sides have matching single ticks. The second and fourth sides have matching double ticks.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the triangles formed by the specified diagonal.

    2. Hint 2

      Write the vertex correspondence before selecting the corresponding angle pairs.

    3. Hint 3

      Use one alternate interior-angle pair to prove each parallel pair.

    Answer

    AB∥CDAD∥BC\begin{gathered}AB\parallel CD\\AD\parallel BC\end{gathered}AB∥CDAD∥BC​

    Full solution

    The given side matches and the common side BDBDBD give

    △ABD≅△CDB\triangle ABD\cong\triangle CDB△ABD≅△CDB

    by SSS.

    Corresponding angles give

    ∠ABD≅∠CDB.\angle ABD\cong\angle CDB.∠ABD≅∠CDB.

    They are alternate interior angles for ABABAB and CDCDCD with transversal BDBDBD, so AB∥CDAB\parallel CDAB∥CD.

    A second corresponding pair gives

    ∠ADB≅∠CBD.\angle ADB\cong\angle CBD.∠ADB≅∠CBD.

    They are alternate interior angles for ADADAD and BCBCBC, again with transversal BDBDBD, so AD∥BCAD\parallel BCAD∥BC.

    Answer

    AB∥CDAD∥BC\begin{gathered}AB\parallel CD\\AD\parallel BC\end{gathered}AB∥CDAD∥BC​

    Key idea

    Triangle correspondence converts side equalities into the angle equalities needed for parallelism.

    ▾

  5. Problem 5 One angle pair

    Construct a convex quadrilateral ABCDABCDABCD with congruent opposite angles A,CA,CA,C that is not a parallelogram. Give all four interior-angle measures and justify both the existence of your example and the failed parallelogram conclusion.

    1. Hint 1

      A counterexample must meet the stated equality but can make the other opposite pair unequal.

    2. Hint 2

      Try joining two congruent right triangles on opposite sides of a shared hypotenuse.

    3. Hint 3

      A taught special right triangle can provide exact angles for the construction.

    Answer

    One example has angles A,CA,CA,C of 90∘90^\circ90∘, angle BBB of 60∘60^\circ60∘, and angle DDD of 120∘120^\circ120∘, made by joining two congruent 30∘30^\circ30∘-60∘60^\circ60∘-90∘90^\circ90∘ triangles along their hypotenuse.

    Full solution

    Take two congruent 30∘30^\circ30∘-60∘60^\circ60∘-90∘90^\circ90∘ triangles with common hypotenuse BDBDBD, on opposite sides of BDBDBD.

    Place their right-angle vertices at A,CA,CA,C so each triangle has its 30∘30^\circ30∘ angle at BBB and its 60∘60^\circ60∘ angle at DDD.

    The outer boundary is convex, with right angles at A,CA,CA,C.

    Adding the two triangle angles at each hypotenuse endpoint gives

    m∠B=60∘m∠D=120∘\begin{gathered}m\angle B=60^\circ\\m\angle D=120^\circ\end{gathered}m∠B=60∘m∠D=120∘​

    The example satisfies the required opposite-angle equality at A,CA,CA,C.

    It is not a parallelogram because its other opposite angles are unequal, contrary to a necessary parallelogram property.

    Answer

    One example has angles A,CA,CA,C of 90∘90^\circ90∘, angle BBB of 60∘60^\circ60∘, and angle DDD of 120∘120^\circ120∘, made by joining two congruent 30∘30^\circ30∘-60∘60^\circ60∘-90∘90^\circ90∘ triangles along their hypotenuse.

    Key idea

    One opposite-angle equality is weaker than the complete two-pair test.

  6. Problem 6 From angle matches to directions

    In convex ABCDABCDABCD, both opposite-angle pairs are congruent. Write ppp for the common measure of angles A,CA,CA,C, and qqq for the common measure of angles B,DB,DB,D. Prove both opposite-side pairs parallel without citing the opposite-angle parallelogram test.

    Text description of this figure

    Convex quadrilateral ABCD has vertices named in boundary order. Opposite angles have matching arc counts, one arc at the first and third vertices and two at the second and fourth.

    1. Hint 1

      The quadrilateral angle sum relates the two common measures.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the resulting consecutive-angle sum along two different sides as transversals.

    Answer

    p+q=180∘AD∥BCAB∥CD\begin{gathered}p+q=180^\circ\\AD\parallel BC\\AB\parallel CD\end{gathered}p+q=180∘AD∥BCAB∥CD​

    Full solution

    The four angles have measures p,q,p,qp,q,p,qp,q,p,q in boundary order.

    The quadrilateral angle sum gives

    2p+2q=360∘p+q=180∘\begin{aligned}2p+2q&=360^\circ\\p+q&=180^\circ\end{aligned}2p+2qp+q​=360∘=180∘​

    Angles DABDABDAB and ABCABCABC are same-side interior angles for lines AD,BCAD,BCAD,BC along transversal ABABAB.

    Their sum is 180∘180^\circ180∘, so the same-side interior-angle converse proves AD∥BCAD\parallel BCAD∥BC.

    Angles ABCABCABC and BCDBCDBCD are same-side interior angles for lines AB,CDAB,CDAB,CD along transversal BCBCBC.

    Their sum is also 180∘180^\circ180∘, so AB∥CDAB\parallel CDAB∥CD.

    Answer

    p+q=180∘AD∥BCAB∥CD\begin{gathered}p+q=180^\circ\\AD\parallel BC\\AB\parallel CD\end{gathered}p+q=180∘AD∥BCAB∥CD​

    Key idea

    A supplementary consecutive-angle sum must be attached to the correct lines and transversal.

  7. Problem 7 The first unsupported line

    In convex ABCDABCDABCD, diagonals AC,BDAC,BDAC,BD meet at EEE. The only given length equality is AE=ECAE=ECAE=EC. Examine this attempted proof. Identify the first line that does not follow, explain the gap, and state one extra length equality that would repair the proof. Then explain how the repaired proof establishes both parallel pairs.

    Line 1: ∠AEB≅∠CED\angle AEB\cong\angle CED∠AEB≅∠CED because they are vertical angles.

    Line 2: △AEB≅△CED\triangle AEB\cong\triangle CED△AEB≅△CED by SAS.

    Line 3: AB=CDAB=CDAB=CD because corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent.

    Line 4: AB∥CDAB\parallel CDAB∥CD from corresponding alternate interior angles.

    1. Hint 1

      Check the inputs to the first triangle-congruence claim.

    2. Hint 2

      SAS needs two side pairs around the included angle, while only one half-diagonal equality was supplied.

    3. Hint 3

      After repairing that input, compare a second pair of triangles for the other side pair.

    Answer

    Line 2 is the first unsupported line. Add BE=EDBE=EDBE=ED; the two SAS comparisons then prove both opposite-side pairs parallel.

    Full solution

    Line 1 is valid for any two intersecting lines.

    Line 2 is unsupported: SAS has the vertical-angle pair and the given side match

    AE=EC.AE=EC.AE=EC.

    SAS still needs the other side match

    BE=ED.BE=ED.BE=ED.

    With that extra equality, SAS gives

    △AEB≅△CED.\triangle AEB\cong\triangle CED.△AEB≅△CED.

    Corresponding angles BAE,DCEBAE,DCEBAE,DCE are congruent alternate interior angles along ACACAC, proving

    AB∥CD.AB\parallel CD.AB∥CD.

    The other vertical-angle pair and the same two half-diagonal equalities give

    △BEC≅△DEA\triangle BEC\cong\triangle DEA△BEC≅△DEA

    by SAS.

    Corresponding angles BCE,DAEBCE,DAEBCE,DAE are congruent alternate interior angles along ACACAC, proving

    BC∥AD.BC\parallel AD.BC∥AD.

    Answer

    Line 2 is the first unsupported line. Add BE=EDBE=EDBE=ED; the two SAS comparisons then prove both opposite-side pairs parallel.

    Key idea

    A correct vertical-angle statement does not supply a missing side equality for SAS.

  8. Problem 8 A stronger incomplete record

    Construct a convex quadrilateral whose whole diagonals are both 101010 cm long and whose crossing bisects ACACAC but does not bisect BDBDBD. Give the four diagonal-part lengths, explain how to draw the quadrilateral, and prove it is not a parallelogram.

    1. Hint 1

      Equal whole lengths still allow different divisions at the crossing.

    2. Hint 2

      Choose equal halves on one line and unequal positive parts with the same total on the other.

    3. Hint 3

      Place the lines perpendicular if that makes the construction easier; join endpoints in alternating order.

    Answer

    One construction on two perpendicular lines through EEE has

    AE=5 cmEC=5 cmBE=7 cmED=3 cm\begin{gathered}AE=5\text{ cm}\\EC=5\text{ cm}\\BE=7\text{ cm}\\ED=3\text{ cm}\end{gathered}AE=5 cmEC=5 cmBE=7 cmED=3 cm​

    It is not a parallelogram.

    Full solution

    Draw perpendicular lines meeting at EEE.

    Put A,CA,CA,C on opposite rays of one line, each 555 cm from EEE.

    Put B,DB,DB,D on opposite rays of the other, respectively 777 cm and 333 cm from EEE.

    Joining them in alternating order forms a convex quadrilateral.

    The whole diagonals have the same length:

    AC=5+5AC=10BD=7+3BD=10\begin{gathered}AC=5+5\\AC=10\\BD=7+3\\BD=10\end{gathered}AC=5+5AC=10BD=7+3BD=10​

    The crossing is the midpoint of ACACAC but not of BDBDBD, whose two parts have unequal lengths.

    Since every parallelogram has both diagonals bisected, this quadrilateral is not a parallelogram.

    Answer

    One construction on two perpendicular lines through EEE has

    AE=5 cmEC=5 cmBE=7 cmED=3 cm\begin{gathered}AE=5\text{ cm}\\EC=5\text{ cm}\\BE=7\text{ cm}\\ED=3\text{ cm}\end{gathered}AE=5 cmEC=5 cmBE=7 cmED=3 cm​

    It is not a parallelogram.

    Key idea

    Congruent whole diagonals cannot replace a missing midpoint condition.

  9. Problem 9 One pair carries two facts

    Convex ABCDABCDABCD satisfies

    AB∥CDAB=CD\begin{gathered}AB\parallel CD\\AB=CD\end{gathered}AB∥CDAB=CD​

    Prove AD∥BCAD\parallel BCAD∥BC using diagonal BDBDBD. Name the triangle congruence and the angle pair that gives the parallelism; do not simply quote the one-pair parallelogram test.

    Text description of this figure

    Convex quadrilateral ABCD has vertices named in boundary order. The first and third sides have matching single ticks. That same opposite-side pair has matching parallel arrows.

    1. Hint 1

      The given parallel sides provide an angle pair along the chosen diagonal.

    2. Hint 2

      The equal sides and the shared diagonal enclose that angle pair.

    3. Hint 3

      Use the correspondence to obtain alternate interior angles on the other side pair.

    Answer

    △ABD≅△CDB∠ADB≅∠CBDAD∥BC\begin{gathered}\triangle ABD\cong\triangle CDB\\\angle ADB\cong\angle CBD\\AD\parallel BC\end{gathered}△ABD≅△CDB∠ADB≅∠CBDAD∥BC​

    Full solution

    The given parallel lines AB,CDAB,CDAB,CD, cut by BDBDBD, give congruent alternate interior angles ABD,CDBABD,CDBABD,CDB.

    The side equality AB=CDAB=CDAB=CD and shared diagonal surround those angles.

    Thus

    △ABD≅△CDB\triangle ABD\cong\triangle CDB△ABD≅△CDB

    by SAS.

    Corresponding angles give

    ∠ADB≅∠CBD.\angle ADB\cong\angle CBD.∠ADB≅∠CBD.

    These are alternate interior angles for AD,BCAD,BCAD,BC along transversal BDBDBD.

    Their converse proves AD∥BCAD\parallel BCAD∥BC, completing the definition together with the given parallel pair.

    Answer

    △ABD≅△CDB∠ADB≅∠CBDAD∥BC\begin{gathered}\triangle ABD\cong\triangle CDB\\\angle ADB\cong\angle CBD\\AD\parallel BC\end{gathered}△ABD≅△CDB∠ADB≅∠CBDAD∥BC​

    Key idea

    The one-pair test works because the parallel and congruent facts feed the same SAS comparison.

  10. Problem 10 Keep the smallest record

    Convex ABCDABCDABCD is known to satisfy AB∥CDAB\parallel CDAB∥CD. You may request any of the following three evidence cards.

    Card P: AB=CDAB=CDAB=CD.

    Card Q: AD=BCAD=BCAD=BC.

    Card R: AC=BDAC=BDAC=BD.

    Find the minimum number of cards needed to guarantee a parallelogram, and list every single card that suffices. Prove your choice is minimal. Also decide whether cards Q and R together suffice, justifying your decision with exact geometric evidence.

    1. Hint 1

      Keep track of which pair is already known parallel.

    2. Hint 2

      For a rejected record, look for a quadrilateral with unequal parallel bases that still meets every requested equality.

    3. Hint 3

      A centered upper base lets two right-triangle calculations verify both the sloping sides and the diagonals.

    Answer

    The minimum is one card, and P is the only sufficient single card. Q and R together are insufficient.

    Full solution

    Card P puts congruence on the same pair already known parallel.

    The one-pair test therefore proves a parallelogram with one card.

    To test all the weaker choices, construct parallel horizontal bases of lengths 141414 cm and 444 cm, centered one above the other, with perpendicular separation 121212 cm.

    Each horizontal offset is 555.

    Joining corresponding endpoints gives a convex quadrilateral.

    Each sloping side is a right-triangle hypotenuse with legs 5,125,125,12.

    52+122=169AD=13BC=13\begin{gathered}5^2+12^2=169\\AD=13\\BC=13\end{gathered}52+122=169AD=13BC=13​

    Thus card Q holds.

    Each diagonal has horizontal run 999 and vertical rise 121212.

    92+122=225AC=15BD=15\begin{gathered}9^2+12^2=225\\AC=15\\BD=15\end{gathered}92+122=225AC=15BD=15​

    Thus card R holds too.

    The opposite bases have different lengths, so this is not a parallelogram.

    It shows that no cards, Q alone, R alone, and even Q with R leave a non-parallelogram possible.

    Hence one card is minimal, and P is the only sufficient single card.

    Answer

    The minimum is one card, and P is the only sufficient single card. Q and R together are insufficient.

    Key idea

    Minimal sufficient evidence must force the conclusion, and every weaker proposed record needs a valid counterexample.
