Rectangles, Rhombi, and Squares

▾

Intuition

A frame can have equal diagonals without having four right angles. But if you already know that the frame is a parallelogram, equal diagonals settle the question. The same measurement can be decisive or inconclusive depending on the other facts you know.

Learning goals

  • Prove the special diagonal properties of rectangles and rhombi using triangle congruence
  • Calculate lengths and angles by combining special properties with parallelogram properties
  • Choose sufficient classification tests and state their required hypotheses
  • Explain the square hierarchy and disprove unrestricted converses with counterexamples

Special members of one family

A rectangle is a parallelogram with four right angles. A rhombus is a parallelogram with four congruent sides. A square has both features, so every square is both a rectangle and a rhombus.

These definitions keep rectangles and rhombi within the parallelogram family. Their right-angle and equal-side conditions add structure without removing any parallelogram property.

Every parallelogram has congruent opposite sides, supplementary consecutive angles, and diagonals that bisect each other. The parallelogram properties lesson proves these facts; here we use them to investigate the extra conditions imposed by right angles or equal sides.

Throughout this lesson, quadrilaterals are convex and their names list vertices in boundary order. An intersection named OOO lies on both diagonals.

Why a rectangle has congruent diagonals

A rectangle’s two diagonals have equal lengths. For example, a rectangle with adjacent sides 666 cm and 888 cm has a 101010 cm diagonal, by the Pythagorean theorem; the other diagonal crosses a right triangle with the same two leg lengths, so it also measures 101010 cm.

The common side and equal opposite sides give a proof that works without choosing side lengths.

A rectangle has congruent diagonals#

Let ABCDABCDABCD be a rectangle. Compare triangles DABDABDAB and CBACBACBA.

Their sides ADADAD and BCBCBC are congruent because the rectangle is a parallelogram. Side ABABAB is common, and the included angles at AAA and BBB are right angles. SAS gives

△DAB≅△CBA.\triangle DAB\cong\triangle CBA.△DAB≅△CBA.

The corresponding third sides are the diagonals, so

BD=AC.BD=AC.BD=AC.

Nothing in this argument depended on the particular side lengths 666 and 888.

∎

This adds a new fact to diagonal bisection. Bisection compares the two halves of each individual diagonal; congruent diagonals compare one whole diagonal with the other.

Why can the rectangle proof compare triangles DABDABDAB and CBACBACBA by SAS?

Answer choices

The common side AB and congruent opposite sides AD and BC supply two side pairs. The included angles at A and B are both right angles.

△DAB≅△CBA\triangle DAB\cong\triangle CBA△DAB≅△CBA

SAS therefore gives the congruence. Diagonal congruence is the conclusion, so it cannot be assumed as a premise.

Since a rectangle is a parallelogram, OOO bisects BDBDBD. Therefore

What equal sides do to the diagonals

A rhombus’s diagonals cross at right angles and bisect its vertex angles. These are different statements: one describes the intersection, and the other describes how a diagonal splits an angle at an endpoint.

If two equal adjacent angles together make a straight angle, their common measure is 180∘÷2180^\circ\div2180∘÷2, or 90∘90^\circ90∘. We will find that situation inside a rhombus.

A rhombus has perpendicular diagonals#

Let the diagonals of rhombus ABCDABCDABCD meet at OOO. Since the rhombus is a parallelogram, BOBOBO and DODODO are congruent. Also, ABABAB and ADADAD are congruent by the rhombus definition, and AOAOAO is common.

SSS gives

△AOB≅△AOD.\triangle AOB\cong\triangle AOD.△AOB≅△AOD.

Corresponding angles AOBAOBAOB and AODAODAOD are congruent. Since B,O,DB,O,DB,O,D are collinear, these adjacent angles add to a straight angle. Each is 90∘90^\circ90∘, so the diagonals are perpendicular.

∎

For angle bisection, consider a rhombus with a 116∘116^\circ116∘ angle at AAA. The two triangles on either side of ACACAC have matching side lengths, so they split that angle into two 58∘58^\circ58∘ parts. The same matching argument proves the result for every rhombus.

Each rhombus diagonal bisects its endpoint angles#

In rhombus ABCDABCDABCD, triangles ABCABCABC and ADCADCADC share ACACAC. The other two corresponding side pairs are congruent because all four sides of a rhombus are congruent. SSS gives

△ABC≅△ADC.\triangle ABC\cong\triangle ADC.△ABC≅△ADC.

Corresponding angles show that ACACAC bisects the angles at its own endpoints:

∠BAC≅∠DAC∠BCA≅∠DCA.\begin{gathered} \angle BAC\cong\angle DAC\\ \angle BCA\cong\angle DCA. \end{gathered}∠BAC≅∠DAC∠BCA≅∠DCA.​

Likewise, SSS gives congruence of triangles ABDABDABD and CBDCBDCBD. Thus BDBDBD bisects the angles at BBB and DDD. The 116∘116^\circ116∘ example uses this side matching; that particular angle measure was not needed.

∎

Worked example 2 Finding a diagonal and a side

Problem. Rhombus ABCDABCDABCD has diagonals meeting at OOO. The lengths AOAOAO and BOBOBO are 999 cm and 121212 cm. Find ACACAC and ABABAB.

AC=2(9)AC=18 cm.\begin{aligned} AC&=2(9)\\ AC&=18\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}ACAC​=2(9)=18 cm.​

The diagonals are perpendicular, making triangle AOBAOBAOB a right triangle with hypotenuse ABABAB.

Tests that identify special parallelograms

A property starts with a shape’s name and draws a conclusion. A sufficient test starts with evidence and guarantees the shape’s name. The previous lesson proves tests for establishing that a quadrilateral is a parallelogram; establish that fact before using the tests below.

A parallelogram with congruent diagonals must be a rectangle. For instance, if two consecutive angles are proved equal and their sum is 180∘180^\circ180∘, both are 90∘90^\circ90∘.

Congruent diagonals identify a rectangle among parallelograms#

Suppose ABCDABCDABCD is a parallelogram with congruent diagonals. Compare triangles DABDABDAB and CBACBACBA again. Their third sides DBDBDB and CACACA are now given congruent, rather than obtained as a conclusion.

Opposite sides ADADAD and BCBCBC are congruent, and ABABAB is common. SSS gives congruent triangles, so the consecutive angles DABDABDAB and CBACBACBA are congruent. They are also supplementary because ABCDABCDABCD is a parallelogram.

Each of those angles is 90∘90^\circ90∘. Their opposite angles are congruent to them, giving four right angles, so ABCDABCDABCD is a rectangle.

∎

A parallelogram with perpendicular diagonals must be a rhombus. Perpendicularity supplies the included angles needed to compare two small triangles.

Two diagonal tests for a rhombus#

First suppose parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD has perpendicular diagonals meeting at OOO. Side AOAOAO is common to triangles AOBAOBAOB and AODAODAOD. The sides BOBOBO and DODODO are congruent because parallelogram diagonals bisect each other, and the included angles at OOO are right angles.

SAS gives congruent triangles, so ABABAB and ADADAD are congruent. Together with the two congruent opposite-side pairs of a parallelogram, this makes all four sides congruent. Thus ABCDABCDABCD is a rhombus.

Now suppose instead that ABCDABCDABCD is a parallelogram and ACACAC bisects angle DABDABDAB. The bisector gives

∠DAC≅∠BAC.\angle DAC\cong\angle BAC.∠DAC≅∠BAC.

Since ADADAD is parallel to BCBCBC, alternate interior angles give

∠DAC≅∠ACB.\angle DAC\cong\angle ACB.∠DAC≅∠ACB.

Thus triangle ABCABCABC has congruent angles at AAA and CCC, so their opposite sides BCBCBC and ABABAB are congruent. Equal adjacent sides, together with equal opposite sides, make the parallelogram a rhombus.

∎

These tests require the whole angle at a vertex to be bisected. Splitting an unrelated angle somewhere on a side is different evidence.

Two simpler tests follow from earlier properties. A parallelogram with one right angle has four right angles, because consecutive angles are supplementary and opposite angles are congruent. A parallelogram with one pair of congruent adjacent sides has four congruent sides, because opposite sides are congruent.

Worked example 3 Putting classification evidence in order

Problem. Convex quadrilateral JKLMJKLMJKLM has diagonals JLJLJL and KMKMKM meeting at OOO. You know

JO=OLKO=OMJL=KMJL⊥KM.\begin{gathered} JO=OL\\ KO=OM\\ JL=KM\\ JL\perp KM. \end{gathered}JO=OLKO=OMJL=KMJL⊥KM.​

Prove that JKLMJKLMJKLM is a square. Explain which evidence establishes the parallelogram first.

Solution. The first two equalities say that the diagonals bisect each other. The converse parallelogram test therefore makes JKLMJKLMJKLM a parallelogram.

Its congruent diagonals now prove that it is a rectangle.

Its perpendicular diagonals now prove that it is a rhombus.

A quadrilateral that is both a rectangle and a rhombus has four right angles and four congruent sides. It is a square.

Check your understanding

You know only that ABCDABCDABCD is a parallelogram. Which additional fact guarantees a rhombus but does not by itself guarantee a square?

Answer choices

Perpendicular diagonals identify a rhombus among parallelograms. A rhombus can have unequal diagonal lengths, so it need not be a square.

Congruent diagonals or one right angle would identify a rectangle. Diagonal bisection is already true in every parallelogram.

Why the hypothesis cannot disappear

Congruent diagonals alone do not identify a rectangle among all quadrilaterals. Perpendicular diagonals alone do not identify a rhombus either. The following construction satisfies even both diagonal conditions and still fails both conclusions.

The four rays from OOO form right angles, with the given lengths shown. The polygon ABCDABCDABCD is convex. Its diagonals have lengths

AC=4+2AC=6BD=3+3BD=6.\begin{aligned} AC&=4+2\\ AC&=6\\ BD&=3+3\\ BD&=6. \end{aligned}ACACBDBD​=4+2=6=3+3=6.​

They are congruent and perpendicular. But OOO does not bisect ACACAC, so this is not a parallelogram; therefore it is neither a rectangle nor a rhombus. This uses the necessary diagonal-bisection property, not any trapezoid or kite theorem.

Even a diagonal that bisects an endpoint angle is insufficient without the parallelogram hypothesis. In this same figure, right triangles AOBAOBAOB and AODAODAOD are congruent by SAS, using the equal 333-unit legs and their common AOAOAO. Thus ACACAC bisects angle DABDABDAB, yet the shape is not a rhombus.

Squares combine the two conditions

Within parallelograms, the two special diagonal properties work independently: congruent diagonals give a rectangle, and perpendicular diagonals give a rhombus. A square belongs to both families, so its diagonals are congruent, perpendicular, bisect each other, and bisect the vertex angles.

For a square, each vertex angle is 90∘90^\circ90∘, so each part cut by a diagonal is 45∘45^\circ45∘. A rotated square keeps all these properties.

Worked example 4 What can the given facts guarantee?

Problem. In each case below the quadrilateral is a parallelogram. State the strongest classification guaranteed by the givens, and decide whether a square is guaranteed.

Case A. The whole diagonals are congruent.

Case B. The whole diagonals are perpendicular and have different lengths.

Case C. One angle is right, and a diagonal bisects that whole angle.

Solution. Case A guarantees a rectangle. A rectangle with unequal adjacent sides satisfies the evidence, so a square is not guaranteed.

Case B guarantees a rhombus. Its unequal diagonals rule out a rectangle and therefore rule out a square.

Case C gives a rectangle by the right-angle test and a rhombus by the angle-bisector test. It guarantees a square.

Check your understanding

A convex quadrilateral has congruent, perpendicular diagonals. Which further fact guarantees that it is a square?

Answer choices

Diagonal bisection first proves that the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

Among parallelograms, congruent diagonals prove rectangle and perpendicular diagonals prove rhombus. Both classifications together prove square. Merely intersecting inside the quadrilateral does not supply midpoint information.

Predict which conditions will make the diagonals equal or perpendicular. Try each control separately, then combine the right-angle and equal-side conditions. Use the earlier proofs to justify what appears.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Dropping the parallelogram hypothesis. Equal or perpendicular diagonals do not give the special-shape tests for an arbitrary quadrilateral.
  • Confusing equal diagonals with bisection. Equal whole lengths compare different diagonals; bisection compares the two parts of each diagonal.
  • Splitting the wrong angle. A rhombus diagonal bisects the two vertex angles at its own endpoints.
  • Excluding squares. A square satisfies the definitions of both rectangle and rhombus, so it inherits both families’ properties.

Key takeaways

  • Rectangles have congruent diagonals.
  • Rhombi have perpendicular diagonals, and each diagonal bisects its endpoint angles.
  • Squares are both rectangles and rhombi, so they have both sets of properties.
  • To use the converse diagonal tests, first establish that the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.
  • Give a construction satisfying every stated hypothesis when disproving an unrestricted converse.

A bit of history (optional)

In 1847, Oliver Byrne published an unusual version of the first six books of Euclid’s geometry. Instead of repeatedly asking a reader to match letters in a proof to letters on a diagram, he placed small colored lines and angles into the text itself.

Printing it required carefully lining up blue, red, yellow, and black woodblock images. Byrne said the colors were meant to help learning, rather than merely decorate the page. His book became memorable for its striking design; whether it achieved his promised improvement in learning is a separate question.

The Smithsonian Libraries’ account of Byrne’s book includes examples of its pages and explains how the colored printing was made.
