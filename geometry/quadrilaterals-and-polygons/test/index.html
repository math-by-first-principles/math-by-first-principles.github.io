    360∘54=203∘\frac{360^\circ}{54}=\frac{20}{3}^\circ54360∘​=320​∘

    The proposed rotation is not a whole number of those steps.

    50÷203=7.550\div\frac{20}{3}=7.550÷320​=7.5

    Therefore it does not match.

    Answer

    There are 54 sides and 54 reflection axes: 27 of each type. The least positive rotation is 203∘\frac{20}{3}^\circ320​∘; a 50∘50^\circ50∘ turn does not match.

    Key idea

    Regular side count connects angle calculations with whole-figure symmetry, but a matching turn still needs an integer step count.

      The segment AC acts as a transversal. It also belongs to two triangles with a supplied side and included-angle match.

    AB∥CDAB\parallel CDAB∥CD

  6. Problem 6 An equality at the crossing

    Parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD has diagonals meeting at OOO, and AO=BOAO=BOAO=BO. Prove that each of the four triangles AOBAOBAOB, BOCBOCBOC, CODCODCOD, and DOADOADOA is isosceles. Prove also that ABCDABCDABCD is a rectangle.

    Is a square guaranteed? Justify your answer with a possible nonsquare example.

    Text description of this figure

    Parallelogram ABCD has diagonals AC and BD meeting at O. A matching single tick is placed on AO and BO to represent their given equality. The remaining two half-diagonals have no matching ticks supplied. No vertex right angles or boundary-side equalities are marked.

    1. Hint 1

      Combine the given equality with the midpoint facts already available for a parallelogram.

    2. Hint 2

      Relate the recovered half-diagonal equalities to whole diagonal lengths, then check the hypotheses of a special-shape test.

    3. Hint 3

      A rectangle can have unequal adjacent sides while retaining equal, bisected diagonals.

    Answer

    All four triangles are isosceles, and ABCD is a rectangle. A square is not guaranteed; a rectangle with adjacent sides 7 cm and 10 cm is a valid nonsquare example.

    Full solution

    Parallelogram diagonals bisect each other, giving

    AO=OCBO=OD\begin{gathered}AO=OC\\BO=OD\end{gathered}AO=OCBO=OD​

    Together with the given equality AO equals BO, these make all four half-diagonal lengths equal.

    Each named triangle has two of these as sides, so each is isosceles.

    The two whole diagonal lengths therefore agree.

    AC=2AOBD=2BOAC=BD\begin{gathered}AC=2AO\\BD=2BO\\AC=BD\end{gathered}AC=2AOBD=2BOAC=BD​

    The known parallelogram with congruent diagonals is a rectangle.

    A rectangle with adjacent sides 7 cm and 10 cm has congruent whole diagonals that bisect each other, so AO equals BO.

    Its unequal adjacent sides prevent it from being a square.

    This verifies the counterexample against the original condition.

    Answer

    All four triangles are isosceles, and ABCD is a rectangle. A square is not guaranteed; a rectangle with adjacent sides 7 cm and 10 cm is a valid nonsquare example.

    Key idea

    Equal half-diagonals can establish a rectangle without establishing four equal boundary sides.

  7. Problem 7 A whole family of tiles

    Let n be any integer at least seven. Can congruent copies of one convex regular n-gon make a regular tessellation meeting whole side to whole side? Give one argument that covers every such n, rather than testing a few individual polygons.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare an interior corner with the angle available when at least three tiles meet at a point.

    2. Hint 2

      An exterior turn of a regular polygon is a full turn divided by its side count. Bound that turn when n is at least seven.

    3. Hint 3

      Explain both why two corners cannot fill a turn and why three or more then fail.

    Answer

    No such regular tessellation exists for any integer n at least seven.

    Full solution

    Let e and i be the exterior and interior angle measures in degrees.

    Since n is at least seven, the exterior turn is no larger than 360 divided by seven, which is less than 60.

    Thus

    e<60i=180−ei>120\begin{gathered}e<60\\i=180-e\\i>120\end{gathered}e<60i=180−ei>120​

    Convexity also gives i less than 180.

    Two corners therefore total less than a full turn, so at least three tiles must meet at a regular tiling vertex.

    But three of these corners already exceed a full turn.

    3i>3603i>3603i>360

    Any larger number of corners exceeds it as well.

    Consequently no integer corner count fills a full turn, so the necessary local condition fails for every n at least seven.

    This conclusion concerns congruent regular n-gons meeting whole side to whole side; it does not rule out mixed or nonregular tilings.

    Answer

    No such regular tessellation exists for any integer n at least seven.

    Key idea

    A bound can rule out an entire polygon family when every possible vertex count fails the angle condition.

  8. Problem 8 A proposed argument

    Convex kite UVWXUVWXUVWX has UV=UXUV=UXUV=UX and VW=WXVW=WXVW=WX. Its diagonals UWUWUW and VXVXVX meet at TTT. Consider this argument.

    Line 1: VT=TXVT=TXVT=TX by the kite diagonal property.

    Line 2: UT=TWUT=TWUT=TW because the diagonals are perpendicular.

    Line 3: Both diagonals bisect each other, so the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

    Line 4: Its adjacent congruent sides then make it a rhombus.

    Identify the first unsupported line and explain the gap. State an additional hypothesis that repairs the argument. Then give an exact convex counterexample to the original rhombus conclusion and verify its required side pairs.

    1. Hint 1

      Check which diagonal is guaranteed to bisect which other diagonal in this kite.

    2. Hint 2

      Perpendicularity concerns an angle at the crossing, rather than the distances along both lines.

    3. Hint 3

      For a counterexample, use perpendicular lines through T, equal distances toward V and X, and unequal positive distances toward U and W.

    Answer

    Line 2 is unsupported. Add UT=TWUT=TWUT=TW. One convex counterexample on perpendicular rays has UT=8UT=8UT=8, TW=15TW=15TW=15, and VT=TX=6VT=TX=6VT=TX=6, in common units.

    Full solution

    Line 1 is valid: UW joins the equal-side meeting vertices U and W, so it bisects VX.

    Line 2 is unsupported.

    A right angle at T does not imply that T is the midpoint of UW.

    Repair: add the hypothesis

    UT=TWUT=TWUT=TW

    Then both diagonal midpoint conditions hold.

    The parallelogram test is valid, and its adjacent kite pairs make all four sides congruent, proving rhombus.

    Counterexample: draw perpendicular lines through T.

    Put U and W on opposite rays at distances 8 and 15 units.

    Put V and X on the other two rays at 6 units each.

    Join U, V, W, X in order.

    Each edge lies within one right-angle sector, and every vertex angle combines two acute angles, so the boundary is simple and strictly convex.

    Triangles UTV and UTX share UT, have equal sides TV and TX, and have right included angles.

    SAS gives UV equal to UX.

    The same SAS comparison of WTV and WTX gives WV equal to WX.

    Thus both disjoint adjacent kite pairs hold.

    The diagonal parts UT and TW are unequal.

    A rhombus would be a parallelogram with mutual diagonal bisection, so this exact kite is not a rhombus.

    Answer

    Line 2 is unsupported. Add UT=TWUT=TWUT=TW. One convex counterexample on perpendicular rays has UT=8UT=8UT=8, TW=15TW=15TW=15, and VT=TX=6VT=TX=6VT=TX=6, in common units.

    Key idea

    A correct kite bisection does not supply the reverse bisection needed for the parallelogram converse.

    On paper, draw two different convex quadrilaterals. Each must have exactly two reflection axes and exactly two rotational symmetries, including the identity.

    The first must have congruent whole diagonals. The second must have different whole diagonal lengths. Name the family of each example, mark all its reflection axes, and describe all its matching rotations.

    Justify why your drawings meet the two different diagonal requirements.

  10. Problem 10 A configuration with extra conditions

    Convex isosceles trapezoid ABCDABCDABCD has chosen parallel bases ABABAB and CDCDCD. The bases have equal length, and diagonal ACACAC bisects the whole angle DABDABDAB. Prove that ABCDABCDABCD is a square.

    1. Hint 1

      Keep track of the two facts about the chosen bases before using properties of a narrower family.

    2. Hint 2

      The isosceles condition concerns the two angles at base AB. Once a parallelogram is established, those same consecutive angles also have a known sum.

    3. Hint 3

      Use the endpoint-angle bisection only after establishing the family required by its converse test.

    Answer

    ABCD is a square.

    Full solution

    The chosen bases are parallel and congruent.

    They form the same opposite-side pair, so the one-pair converse establishes that ABCD is a parallelogram.

    Because the trapezoid is isosceles relative to AB and CD, its angles at A and B are congruent.

    As consecutive parallelogram angles, their measures also satisfy

    m∠A+m∠B=180∘m\angle A+m\angle B=180^\circm∠A+m∠B=180∘

    The two congruent angles each measure 90 degrees.

    Opposite-angle congruence then supplies the other two right angles, so ABCD is a rectangle.

    The given diagonal AC bisects a whole endpoint angle of the known parallelogram.

    The angle-bisector converse therefore also makes ABCD a rhombus.

    The quadrilateral is both a rectangle and a rhombus.

    It has four right angles and four congruent sides, so it is a square.

    Answer

    ABCD is a square.

    Key idea

    Establish each test’s hypotheses in order when combining inclusive trapezoid, parallelogram, and special-shape facts.
