  1. 1

    A quadrilateral is turned on the page. Which given fact makes it a parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    A parallelogram is defined by both pairs of opposite sides being parallel. Turning the page changes none of those relationships.

    AB∥CD,BC∥AD.\begin{gathered} AB\parallel CD,\\ BC\parallel AD. \end{gathered}AB∥CD,BC∥AD.​

    Solution

  8. 8

    Which statement is NOT guaranteed for every parallelogram?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Bisection compares the two parts of each diagonal separately. It does not compare the full diagonals. A general oblique parallelogram can have unequal diagonal lengths.

    For example, a parallelogram can have these diagonal lengths:

    AC=14 cm,BD=10 cm.\begin{gathered} AC=14\text{ cm},\\ BD=10\text{ cm}. \end{gathered}AC=14 cm,BD=10 cm.​

    A proof compares triangles ABCABCABC and CDACDACDA using diagonal ACACAC of a parallelogram. Which fact about the included side needs no new theorem?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A segment has its own length in either triangle. That common included side, together with two alternate interior-angle pairs, gives ASA.

    AC=CA.AC=CA.AC=CA.
  10. 10

    The diagonals of parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD intersect at EEE. Which equality describes one consequence of bisection?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Bisection means each diagonal is cut into two equal parts. For diagonal BDBDBD, those parts are BEBEBE and EDEDED.

    BE=ED.BE=ED.BE=ED.
  11. 11

    A parallelogram is oblique and has unequal adjacent sides. How many rotational symmetries does it have, counting the identity?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Because the parallelogram is oblique, its two angle sizes differ. A symmetry preserves the diagonal intersection, so a nonidentity rotational symmetry has that intersection as its center. It must send a corner to the equal opposite corner, since the neighboring angles differ and it cannot fix a corner. This forces the half-turn. The two rotation amounts, counting the identity, are

    0∘,180∘.\begin{gathered} 0^\circ,\\ 180^\circ. \end{gathered}0∘,180∘.​

    Which reason correctly applies the opposite-side theorem to a square?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Our definitions are inclusive. A square has both pairs of opposite sides parallel, so it is a parallelogram and inherits every direct property proved here.

    AB∥CD,BC∥AD.\begin{gathered} AB\parallel CD,\\ BC\parallel AD. \end{gathered}AB∥CD,BC∥AD.​
