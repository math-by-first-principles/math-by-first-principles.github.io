    Which proof route establishes the opposite-side property from the definition of a parallelogram without circular reasoning?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The definition supplies parallel lines. Those lines supply angle pairs across a diagonal, whose common included side gives ASA. Corresponding sides may be used only after that congruence is established.

    The key congruence is

    △ABC≅△CDA.\triangle ABC\cong\triangle CDA.△ABC≅△CDA.

    In parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD, diagonals meet at OOO. Point PPP lies on ABABAB and QQQ lies on CDCDCD, with AP=CQAP=CQAP=CQ. Why does the half-turn about OOO send PPP to QQQ?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The half-turn sends AAA to CCC and BBB to DDD, so it maps ray ABABAB to ray CDCDCD. The image of PPP is the unique point on that ray at distance APAPAP from CCC. The given equal distance makes that point QQQ.

    AP=CQ.AP=CQ.AP=CQ.

  7. 7

    Which additional description of a parallelogram guarantees that it is a general member with no reflection axes?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    An axis fixing a vertex would force its incident sides to match. An axis exchanging neighboring vertices would force equal consecutive angles, hence right angles. The stated conditions exclude both; exchanging both opposite pairs cannot be a reflection either.

    Equal consecutive angles would each have to be half a straight angle.

    180∘2=90∘.\frac{180^\circ}{2}=90^\circ.2180∘​=90∘.
  8. 8

    Why cannot a reflection exchange both pairs of opposite vertices of a convex parallelogram at once?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    To exchange AAA with CCC, a reflection axis must perpendicularly bisect ACACAC. To exchange BBB with DDD, the same axis must perpendicularly bisect BDBDBD. Both nonparallel diagonals cross at their common midpoint, and one line cannot be perpendicular to both.

    A proof of diagonal bisection in parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD uses these established facts, where EEE is the diagonal intersection.

    AB=CD,∠BAE≅∠DCE,∠ABE≅∠CDE.\begin{gathered} AB=CD,\\ \angle BAE\cong\angle DCE,\\ \angle ABE\cong\angle CDE. \end{gathered}AB=CD,∠BAE≅∠DCE,∠ABE≅∠CDE.​

    Which conclusion is justified by exactly these facts at this stage of the proof?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The matched angles send AAA to CCC and BBB to DDD; the crossing vertex EEE matches itself. The included sides are ABABAB and CDCDCD, so ASA proves the stated congruence.

    △ABE≅△CDE.\triangle ABE\cong\triangle CDE.△ABE≅△CDE.

    Solution

    The half-turn sends AAA to CCC and DDD to BBB, so side ADADAD maps to side CBCBCB. It preserves the distance from the first endpoint, giving CQ=APCQ=APCQ=AP.

    CQ=AP.CQ=AP.CQ=AP.

    A parallelogram has diagonal lengths 121212 cm and 888 cm. Which statement describes what its half-turn proof needs?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Label the parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD, with diagonals ACACAC and BDBDBD of lengths 121212 cm and 888 cm, respectively. Let EEE be their intersection.

    The half-turn uses opposite rays and equal distances along each diagonal separately. The half lengths here are 666 cm on one diagonal and 444 cm on the other. That still pairs every vertex with its opposite vertex.

    AE=EC,BE=ED.\begin{gathered} AE=EC,\\ BE=ED. \end{gathered}AE=EC,BE=ED.​
