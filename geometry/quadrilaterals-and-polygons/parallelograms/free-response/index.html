  1. Problem 1 Opposite edges

    Parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD has side lengths in centimeters given by

    AB=4x−3,CD=29,BC=12.\begin{gathered}AB=4x-3,\\CD=29,\\BC=12.\end{gathered}AB=4x−3,CD=29,BC=12.​

    Find xxx and the length of ADADAD.

    1. Hint 1

      Match each given side with the side opposite it.

    2. Hint 2

      Use equal lengths for ABABAB and CDCDCD before substituting into the expressions.

    Answer

    x=8,AD=12 cm.\begin{gathered}x=8,\\AD=12\text{ cm}.\end{gathered}x=8,AD=12 cm.​

    Full solution

    Opposite sides of a parallelogram are congruent.

    For ABABAB and CDCDCD, this gives

    4x−3=29.4x-3=29.4x−3=29.

    Add 333 to both sides and then divide by 444.

    4x=32,x=8.\begin{aligned}4x&=32,\\x&=8.\end{aligned}4xx​=32,=8.​

    The side opposite BCBCBC is ADADAD, so ADADAD has length 121212 cm.

    Answer

    x=8,AD=12 cm.\begin{gathered}x=8,\\AD=12\text{ cm}.\end{gathered}x=8,AD=12 cm.​

    Key idea

    Identify the opposite side before setting lengths equal.

  2. Problem 2 Two intersecting diagonals

    The diagonals of parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD meet at EEE. Lengths in centimeters satisfy

    AE=3u+1,EC=5u−9,BE=11.\begin{gathered}AE=3u+1,\\EC=5u-9,\\BE=11.\end{gathered}AE=3u+1,EC=5u−9,BE=11.​

    Find uuu, the full length ACACAC, and the full length BDBDBD.

    1. Hint 1

      Bisection compares the two parts of each diagonal separately.

    2. Hint 2

      Set the expressions for AEAEAE and ECECEC equal, then double a half length for each full diagonal.

    Answer

    u=5,AC=32 cm,BD=22 cm.\begin{gathered}u=5,\\AC=32\text{ cm},\\BD=22\text{ cm}.\end{gathered}u=5,AC=32 cm,BD=22 cm.​

    Full solution

    The diagonals bisect each other, so AEAEAE and ECECEC have equal lengths.

    3u+1=5u−93u+1=5u-93u+1=5u−9

    Solve this equation.

    10=2u,u=5.\begin{aligned}10&=2u,\\u&=5.\end{aligned}10u​=2u,=5.​

    Substitution makes each half of ACACAC equal to 161616 cm.

    AC=2(16),AC=32 cm.\begin{aligned}AC&=2(16),\\AC&=32\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}ACAC​=2(16),=32 cm.​

    Each half of BDBDBD is 111111 cm.

    BD=2(11),BD=22 cm.\begin{aligned}BD&=2(11),\\BD&=22\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}BDBD​=2(11),=22 cm.​

    Answer

    u=5,AC=32 cm,BD=22 cm.\begin{gathered}u=5,\\AC=32\text{ cm},\\BD=22\text{ cm}.\end{gathered}u=5,AC=32 cm,BD=22 cm.​

    Key idea

    A full diagonal is twice either of its own halves.

  3. Problem 3 A second route through the figure

    In parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD, draw diagonal BDBDBD. Prove that ABABAB and CDCDCD are congruent and that ADADAD and CBCBCB are congruent, starting only with the parallel-side definition and previously taught triangle facts.

    Text description of this figure

    Parallelogram ABCD with diagonal BD. Single chevrons match AB with CD and double chevrons match AD with BC. No equal lengths are assumed.

    1. Hint 1

      Look at the two triangles separated by the chosen diagonal.

    2. Hint 2

      Use BDBDBD as a transversal twice, once for each parallel-side pair.

    3. Hint 3

      Find the common included side and write the triangle correspondence in order.

    Answer

    Triangles ABDABDABD and CDBCDBCDB are congruent by ASA, so corresponding opposite sides are congruent.

    Full solution

    Since ABABAB and CDCDCD are parallel, diagonal BDBDBD gives alternate interior angles.

    ∠ABD≅∠CDB\angle ABD\cong\angle CDB∠ABD≅∠CDB

    Since ADADAD and BCBCBC are parallel, the same diagonal gives a second pair.

    ∠ADB≅∠CBD\angle ADB\cong\angle CBD∠ADB≅∠CBD

    Side BDBDBD is common and lies between the matched angles.

    ASA proves

    △ABD≅△CDB.\triangle ABD\cong\triangle CDB.△ABD≅△CDB.

    Corresponding sides of those congruent triangles now give the two requested conclusions.

    AB=CD,AD=CB.\begin{gathered}AB=CD,\\AD=CB.\end{gathered}AB=CD,AD=CB.​

    Answer

    Triangles ABDABDABD and CDBCDBCDB are congruent by ASA, so corresponding opposite sides are congruent.

    Key idea

    Parallel lines can supply the angle evidence needed before side equality is proved.

  4. Problem 4 A paper diagram with useful marks

    On paper, draw a convex parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD that has unequal adjacent sides and no right angle. Draw its diagonals and name their intersection EEE.

    Add conventional markings for the two parallel-side pairs. Mark the half-diagonal lengths that must match, using different tick patterns for the two diagonals.

    Write the vertex mapping for the half-turn about EEE. Explain why your markings do not assert that the full diagonals are equal or perpendicular.

    1. Hint 1

      Draw two pairs of parallel lines with different directions, avoiding a right angle. Their intersections form the four corners.

    2. Hint 2

      A half-turn exchanges opposite vertices. Diagonal bisection gives two distinct equal-length pairs.

    Answer

    Drawings vary. Parallel pairs: ABABAB with CDCDCD, and ADADAD with BCBCBC. Tick pairs: AEAEAE with ECECEC, and BEBEBE with EDEDED. The half-turn exchanges AAA with CCC and BBB with DDD. Do not mark equal or perpendicular full diagonals.

    Full solution

    The drawing uses separate chevron patterns for the two given pairs of parallel sides.

    The diagonal theorem gives equal lengths within each diagonal.

    Use one tick on AEAEAE and ECECEC, and two ticks on BEBEBE and EDEDED.

    Different patterns avoid asserting equality between the two diagonals.

    Because EEE is the midpoint of both diagonals, the half-turn exchanges AAA with CCC and BBB with DDD.

    Bisection does not prove that the crossing is perpendicular, so no square corner mark is added.

    Answer

    Drawings vary. Parallel pairs: ABABAB with CDCDCD, and ADADAD with BCBCBC. Tick pairs: AEAEAE with ECECEC, and BEBEBE with EDEDED. The half-turn exchanges AAA with CCC and BBB with DDD. Do not mark equal or perpendicular full diagonals.

    Key idea

    A marking should assert exactly the relationship you have evidence for.

  5. Problem 5 Two angle totals

    Let ABCDABCDABCD be a parallelogram. Prove that the angle measures at AAA and BBB add to 180∘180^\circ180∘, and that the measures at BBB and CCC also add to 180∘180^\circ180∘. Name the parallel lines and transversal for each conclusion.

    Text description of this figure

    Parallelogram ABCD with the two given parallel-side pairs marked. No angle measure is given.

    1. Hint 1

      Consecutive angles can be same-side interior angles of parallel lines.

    2. Hint 2

      For the first pair, use ABABAB as a transversal. For the second pair, use BCBCBC.

    Answer

    Both sums are 180∘180^\circ180∘. For AAA and BBB, use parallel ADADAD and BCBCBC cut by ABABAB; for BBB and CCC, use parallel ABABAB and CDCDCD cut by BCBCBC.

    Full solution

    Lines ADADAD and BCBCBC are parallel by the parallelogram definition.

    Side ABABAB cuts them as a transversal.

    The interior angles on the same side of that transversal are angles DABDABDAB and ABCABCABC, so

    m∠A+m∠B=180∘.m\angle A+m\angle B=180^\circ.m∠A+m∠B=180∘.

    For the other pair, lines ABABAB and CDCDCD are parallel and BCBCBC is the transversal.

    The same-side interior angles are ABCABCABC and BCDBCDBCD, so

    m∠B+m∠C=180∘.m\angle B+m\angle C=180^\circ.m∠B+m∠C=180∘.

    Answer

    Both sums are 180∘180^\circ180∘. For AAA and BBB, use parallel ADADAD and BCBCBC cut by ABABAB; for BBB and CCC, use parallel ABABAB and CDCDCD cut by BCBCBC.

    Key idea

    A supplementary-angle claim should name the parallel lines and the transversal that support it.

  6. Problem 6 Repair the bisection claim

    The diagonals of parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD meet at EEE. Segment AEAEAE is 777 cm and segment BEBEBE is 444 cm.

    A written solution says, “The diagonals bisect each other, so AEAEAE and BEBEBE must be equal. The given measurements are impossible.” Repair this reasoning. State the actual values of ECECEC and EDEDED, and decide whether these measurements contradict the parallelogram hypothesis.

    Text description of this figure

    Parallelogram ABCD with diagonals meeting at E, AE labeled 7 cm and BE labeled 4 cm. No equality tick joins pieces of different diagonals.

    1. Hint 1

      Ask which whole segment each of the named parts belongs to.

    2. Hint 2

      Match AEAEAE to the other part of ACACAC, and BEBEBE to the other part of BDBDBD.

    Answer

    The measurements are consistent.

    EC=7 cm,ED=4 cm.\begin{gathered}EC=7\text{ cm},\\ED=4\text{ cm}.\end{gathered}EC=7 cm,ED=4 cm.​

    Full solution

    The claim compares parts of different diagonals.

    Bisection says that each diagonal has its own two equal halves.

    Segment AEAEAE is paired with ECECEC, and BEBEBE is paired with EDEDED.

    EC=7 cm,ED=4 cm.\begin{gathered}EC=7\text{ cm},\\ED=4\text{ cm}.\end{gathered}EC=7 cm,ED=4 cm.​

    The two full diagonals are therefore 141414 cm and 888 cm.

    They need not be equal, so the given measurements do not contradict a parallelogram.

    Answer

    The measurements are consistent.

    EC=7 cm,ED=4 cm.\begin{gathered}EC=7\text{ cm},\\ED=4\text{ cm}.\end{gathered}EC=7 cm,ED=4 cm.​

    Key idea

    Equal halves of each diagonal do not mean all four half-diagonal lengths match.

  7. Problem 7 Compare two motions

    Figure ABCDABCDABCD is an oblique parallelogram with adjacent sides 999 cm and 666 cm and an angle of 70∘70^\circ70∘. Figure WXYZWXYZWXYZ is a rectangle with adjacent sides 999 cm and 666 cm. Both are drawn with their longer opposite sides horizontal.

    Case A: Does each shape map onto itself under a half-turn about its own diagonal intersection? Explain.

    Case B: Reflect each shape in the horizontal line halfway between its horizontal sides. For each shape, decide whether the outlines match and explain using vertex images.

    Text description of this figure

    Top: solid blue oblique parallelogram ABCD, with a horizontal line halfway between its horizontal sides. Bottom: dashed red rectangle WXYZ, with its own horizontal middle line. Both shapes have adjacent side ratio 9 to 6.

    1. Hint 1

      The half-turn theorem applies to every parallelogram, including rectangles.

    2. Hint 2

      For the reflection, follow the lower-left vertex straight up across the proposed axis. Does it reach the upper-left vertex?

    Answer

    Case A: Both have the half-turn symmetry. Case B: The oblique parallelogram does not match under this reflection; the rectangle does, exchanging WWW with ZZZ and XXX with YYY.

    Full solution

    Case A: Both figures are parallelograms.

    Their diagonals bisect each other, so the half-turn exchanges opposite vertices and maps every side to its opposite side in each figure.

    Case B: Reflection in a horizontal line preserves the horizontal position of each point.

    In the oblique parallelogram, the image of AAA lies directly above AAA, while DDD lies farther right.

    The image is not an original vertex, so that reflection fails.

    Case B: In the rectangle, the upper and lower vertices are vertically aligned at equal distances from the middle line.

    Reflection sends WWW to ZZZ and XXX to YYY, and the sides match.

    This is one of the extra symmetries possible for a special parallelogram.

    Answer

    Case A: Both have the half-turn symmetry. Case B: The oblique parallelogram does not match under this reflection; the rectangle does, exchanging WWW with ZZZ and XXX with YYY.

    Key idea

    The general half-turn property persists when a special parallelogram gains reflection symmetry.

  8. Problem 8 Across the diagonal intersection

    Diagonals ACACAC and BDBDBD of parallelogram ABCDABCDABCD meet at EEE. Prove that AEAEAE and CECECE have equal lengths and that DEDEDE and BEBEBE have equal lengths. Use triangles ADEADEADE and CBECBECBE without assuming that EEE is already a midpoint.

    Text description of this figure

    Parallelogram ABCD with diagonals AC and BD intersecting at E. Parallel markings are given; no midpoint markings are shown.

    1. Hint 1

      The opposite-side theorem is available before the diagonal theorem.

    2. Hint 2

      Use parallel ADADAD and BCBCBC to find two alternate interior-angle pairs.

    3. Hint 3

      The equal opposite sides lie between the matched angles.

    Answer

    Triangles ADEADEADE and CBECBECBE are congruent by ASA, so the two parts of each diagonal are equal.

    Full solution

    The already-proved opposite-side property gives

    AD=CB.AD=CB.AD=CB.

    Using ACACAC as a transversal of parallel ADADAD and BCBCBC gives

    ∠DAE≅∠BCE.\angle DAE\cong\angle BCE.∠DAE≅∠BCE.

    Using BDBDBD as a transversal of the same parallel pair gives

    ∠ADE≅∠CBE.\angle ADE\cong\angle CBE.∠ADE≅∠CBE.

    The equal included sides give ASA congruence in the required order.

    △ADE≅△CBE\triangle ADE\cong\triangle CBE△ADE≅△CBE

    Corresponding sides then supply both midpoint equalities.

    AE=CE,DE=BE.\begin{gathered}AE=CE,\\DE=BE.\end{gathered}AE=CE,DE=BE.​

    Answer

    Triangles ADEADEADE and CBECBECBE are congruent by ASA, so the two parts of each diagonal are equal.

    Key idea

    A proof of midpoint equality must establish congruence before using the equality as evidence.

  9. Problem 9 Check the available conditions

    A convex quadrilateral ABCDABCDABCD has ABABAB parallel to CDCDCD. Its angle at AAA is 60∘60^\circ60∘ and its angle at CCC is 120∘120^\circ120∘, as shown. No other parallelism is given.

    Can you apply the parallelogram opposite-angle theorem to conclude that those two angles are equal? State the missing hypothesis and explain how this example shows why the known parallel pair alone is insufficient.

    Text description of this figure

    A convex quadrilateral ABCD with AB parallel to CD. Angle A is 60 degrees and opposite angle C is 120 degrees. The nonparallel legs tilt in opposite directions.

    1. Hint 1

      Read the hypothesis of the theorem before its conclusion.

    2. Hint 2

      A parallelogram requires a second parallel opposite-side pair. Compare that requirement with the displayed unequal opposite angles.

    Answer

    No. The second pair, ADADAD and BCBCBC, must also be parallel before that theorem applies. This convex example has the known parallel pair and unequal opposite angles.

    Full solution

    The opposite-angle theorem begins with a parallelogram, which means both opposite-side pairs are parallel.

    Only the pair ABABAB and CDCDCD is supplied here.

    The missing hypothesis is that ADADAD is parallel to BCBCBC.

    In this example the angles at AAA and CCC are 60∘60^\circ60∘ and 120∘120^\circ120∘, so they are unequal despite the one given parallel pair.

    The example satisfies the stated weaker conditions and fails the proposed conclusion.

    It therefore shows why a single parallel pair does not justify importing all parallelogram properties.

    Answer

    No. The second pair, ADADAD and BCBCBC, must also be parallel before that theorem applies. This convex example has the known parallel pair and unequal opposite angles.

    Key idea

    A direct theorem can be used only after all its hypotheses are known.

  10. Problem 10 A fold and a turn

    A convex quadrilateral has a reflection axis along one diagonal. Must a half-turn about its diagonal intersection also map it onto itself?

    Decide the claim by giving either a proof or a specific convex counterexample. Explain which length condition in the parallelogram half-turn proof your argument needs to check.

    1. Hint 1

      Reflection pairs points across an axis, while a half-turn pairs points across a center. These need different distance equalities.

    2. Hint 2

      For a possible counterexample, place two opposite vertices on a vertical axis and put the other two equally far to either side of it. Try unequal distances from the intersection to the two vertices on the axis.

    3. Hint 3

      Verify convexity and the reflection, then follow one vertex under the proposed half-turn.

    Answer

    No. Use perpendicular diagonals meeting at OOO, with AOAOAO of 444 units, OCOCOC of 222 units, and BOBOBO and ODODOD each 333 units. Reflection in ACACAC works, but OOO is not the midpoint of ACACAC, so the half-turn fails.

    Full solution

    Place AAA above OOO at distance 444 and CCC below OOO at distance 222.

    Place BBB to the left and DDD to the right at distance 333 from OOO, on a line perpendicular to ACACAC.

    Join the vertices in order A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D.

    This gives a convex quadrilateral, since the two diagonals cross inside it and each boundary turn goes the same way.

    Reflection in line ACACAC fixes AAA and CCC and exchanges BBB with DDD, so the whole boundary matches.

    It is indeed a reflection axis.

    The half-turn about OOO sends AAA to a point 444 units below OOO.

    That point is not CCC, which is only 222 units below OOO, and it is not any other vertex.

    The shape therefore fails this half-turn.

    The parallelogram argument required the intersection to be the midpoint of both diagonals.

    A reflection in one diagonal does not supply the missing equality on that diagonal.

    Answer

    No. Use perpendicular diagonals meeting at OOO, with AOAOAO of 444 units, OCOCOC of 222 units, and BOBOBO and ODODOD each 333 units. Reflection in ACACAC works, but OOO is not the midpoint of ACACAC, so the half-turn fails.

    Key idea

    A symmetry claim needs its own point mapping; a successful reflection alone does not prove a half-turn.
