Classifying Polygons: Practice

Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    Which information is sufficient by definition to establish a rhombus?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Both opposite-side pairs parallel and all four sides congruent supply a parallelogram with four congruent sides, exactly the rhombus definition used here.

    Parallel pairs alone do not force equal sides. A kite pairing alone does not directly supply the stated parallelogram condition.

  2. 2

    An unmarked convex regular polygon has least positive matching rotation 18 degrees. How many reflection axes does it have?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The least step repeated once per vertex completes a full turn.

    n=360∘÷18∘n=360^\circ\div18^\circn=360∘÷18∘ n=20n=20n=20

    A convex regular 20-gon has 20 reflection axes.

  3. 3

    An unmarked convex regular nonagon is rotated 160 degrees about its symmetry center. What happens?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The least positive step is 40 degrees.

    160∘÷40∘=4160^\circ\div40^\circ=4160∘÷40∘=4

    Four successive matching steps move each vertex four positions forward and match every joining side.

  4. 4

    Which statement about a convex regular polygon with an odd number of sides is true?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    For a half-turn, half of the equal rotation steps must be a whole number of steps.

    An odd side count has no integer half. It still has one mirror and one counted rotation per side.

  5. 5

    A convex equilateral polygon is symmetric across one stated line. Does that evidence alone establish regularity?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Join two congruent equilateral triangles along a common side, with their apexes on opposite sides. The four outside sides have one common length. The two apex angles are 60 degrees, while each side corner combines two 60-degree angles into 120 degrees.

    The line through both apexes reflects the left and right halves onto each other, as in the earlier equilateral-triangle symmetry result. The side and fold conditions hold, but the unequal corner angles prevent regularity.

  6. 6

    Which direct definition argument establishes that every square is an inclusive trapezoid?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The square definition includes the parallelogram condition.

    Two opposite parallel pairs satisfy at least one opposite parallel pair. No diagonal theorem or orientation argument is needed.

  7. 7

    A simple pentagon ABCDE has no crossings, but diagonal BD lies partly outside its region. What must be true?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    In a convex polygon, a segment between any two points of the region stays inside or on it.

    B and D belong to the region, but their joining segment leaves it. Therefore the polygon is not convex; being simple, it is concave.

  8. 8

    An unmarked convex regular polygon has 14 reflection axes. How many rotations other than identity does it have?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Fourteen mirrors identify 14 sides for a convex regular polygon. It has 14 rotational symmetries including identity.

    14−1=1314-1=1314−1=13

    Removing identity leaves 13 nonidentity rotations.

  9. 9

    A convex quadrilateral has both opposite-side pairs parallel and all four sides congruent. Which conclusion needs additional evidence?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The givens establish a rhombus, which is also a kite, parallelogram, and inclusive trapezoid.

    Four right angles are not supplied. An oblique rhombus meets every given but is not a square.

  10. 10

    Why can an axis of a convex regular polygon cross the interior of a side only at that side's midpoint?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    When the mirror crosses a side at an interior point, it carries that side onto itself and exchanges its endpoints.

    A fixed point on the mirror must be equally far from those exchanged endpoints, so their midpoint is the crossing. This uses the reflection rule, not a future polygon theorem.

  11. 11

    For an unmarked convex regular 16-gon, how many axes pass through opposite side midpoints?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    For an even side count, opposite sides pair up. Each pair has one midpoint-to-midpoint mirror.

    16÷2=816\div2=816÷2=8

    The other eight mirrors pass through opposite vertices.

  12. 12

    Which conclusion follows for an unmarked convex quadrilateral with all four sides congruent and all four angles right?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Four right angles give opposite parallel sides by the earlier perpendicular-line result. With four congruent sides, this gives a square.

    Its sides and angles are congruent, making it regular. A convex regular four-gon has four mirrors and four rotations including identity.
