  1. Problem 1 Two cuts through a cylinder

    A right circular cylinder is cut in two ways. Give the true section shape in each case and explain what parts of the cylinder bound it.

    Cut A. The cutting plane is tilted relative to the circular bases. It passes through the interior and misses both end disks.

    Cut B. The cutting plane contains the line through the two base centers.

    1. Hint 1

      Imagine looking straight at each cutting plane so perspective cannot change the apparent shape.

    2. Hint 2

      For Cut A, follow where the plane meets the curved side. For Cut B, trace the line through each end disk and the segments connecting their endpoints.

    Answer

    Cut A gives a noncircular ellipse. Cut B gives a rectangle.

    Full solution

    Cut A.

    The tilt gives a noncircular ellipse because the plane cuts all the way around the curved side without being interrupted by either end disk.

    The curved side supplies the entire boundary.

    Cut B.

    The plane cuts a diameter across each end disk.

    The two diameters are parallel, and their corresponding endpoints are joined by two segments along the side of the cylinder.

    These segments run perpendicular to the end disks, so the four segments bound a rectangle.

    Answer

    Cut A gives a noncircular ellipse. Cut B gives a rectangle.

    Key idea

    A section's true shape depends on which parts of the solid its plane intersects.

  5. Problem 5 A footprint with one missing site

    A solid is built from filled vertical columns of unit cubes on a grid two sites wide and three sites deep. The top footprint contains every site except the middle site in the left column. Every occupied site has positive integer height.

    From left to right, the two front silhouette heights are 4,34,34,3. From front to back, the three right silhouette heights are 2,3,42,3,42,3,4. Only the outer silhouettes are specified.

    Give two different height plans that fit all three views. Label the front, middle, and back rows, giving left and right heights in each row. Which occupied sites have a uniquely determined height? Explain.

    Text description of this figure

    The top footprint has two columns and three rows, with the front at the bottom. Five occupied sites are marked with crosses. Only the middle-left site is empty.

    1. Hint 1

      Each front silhouette height is a maximum in one left-to-right position. Each right silhouette height is a maximum in one front-to-back row.

    2. Hint 2

      Look for a row with just one occupied site. Then ask where the left column can reach height 444 without exceeding a row's maximum.

    3. Hint 3

      To test whether any other site is fixed, try varying the front row and back-right site while preserving all the maxima.

    Answer

    From front to back, one pair is

    Plan 1: (2,1),(0,3),(4,1)Plan 2: (1,2),(0,3),(4,2).\begin{aligned}\text{Plan 1: }&(2,1),(0,3),(4,1)\\ \text{Plan 2: }&(1,2),(0,3),(4,2).\end{aligned}Plan 1: Plan 2: ​(2,1),(0,3),(4,1)(1,2),(0,3),(4,2).​

    Only middle-right (height 333) and back-left (height 444) are fixed.

    Full solution

    The middle row has only its right site occupied, so the row maximum of 333 fixes that height.

    The left column must reach 444.

    Its middle site is empty, and the front row has maximum 222.

    The back-left site therefore has height 444.

    Plan 1.

    Set the front row to (2,1)(2,1)(2,1), the middle row to (0,3)(0,3)(0,3), and the back row to (4,1)(4,1)(4,1).

    Set the front row to (1,2)(1,2)(1,2), the middle row to (0,3)(0,3)(0,3), and the back row to (4,2)(4,2)(4,2).

    The maxima and occupied sites are unchanged.

    The two plans disagree at front-left, front-right, and back-right.

    Each of those heights can vary, so only the middle-right and back-left heights are fixed.

    Answer

    From front to back, one pair is

    Plan 1: (2,1),(0,3),(4,1)Plan 2: (1,2),(0,3),(4,2).\begin{aligned}\text{Plan 1: }&(2,1),(0,3),(4,1)\\ \text{Plan 2: }&(1,2),(0,3),(4,2).\end{aligned}Plan 1: Plan 2: ​(2,1),(0,3),(4,1)(1,2),(0,3),(4,2).​

    Only middle-right (height 333) and back-left (height 444) are fixed.

    Key idea

    Silhouette maxima can force some column heights while leaving others free.

  8. Problem 8 A model of a hollow casting
