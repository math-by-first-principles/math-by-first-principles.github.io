Scaling in Three Dimensions

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Watch the video explainer

Intuition

Double every edge of a cube. Each face becomes four times as large, while the space inside becomes eight times as large. A resize can preserve shape while changing the balance between the outside and the inside.

Learning goals

  • Derive length, surface-area, and volume factors for a positive uniform resize
  • Recover a length factor from surface-area or volume ratios using positive roots
  • Calculate surface area per unit volume and explain its inverse-length units
  • Distinguish whole-solid similarity from changing only one dimension using measurements

One factor for the whole solid

Two solids are similar when one is a uniformly resized copy of the other, allowing a turn, reflection, or move. Every corresponding length uses the same positive factor. This includes radii, perpendicular heights, diagonals, and slant heights, not just the edges you can see.

We use original and image to keep the direction clear. The length scale factor is

k=image lengthoriginal length.k=\frac{\text{image length}}{\text{original length}}.k=original lengthimage length​.

A factor greater than one enlarges; a factor between zero and one reduces. A factor of one preserves size. All solids here have positive volume, and we require k>0k>0k>0 so the image does not collapse.

Before reading the values beside the three cubes, predict the surface-area and volume factors for edges twice and three times as long. Each cube has six square faces, and its volume is its edge cubed.

The length factors 111, 222, and 333 produce area factors 111, 444, and 999, and volume factors 111, 888, and 272727. Counting three directions explains the extra multiplication for volume.

Why the powers are two and three

Area and Similarity established that a positive uniform resize multiplies a plane region’s area by the square of the length factor. Every corresponding face of a polyhedron therefore has that squared factor. Adding the face areas gives the same factor for total surface area.

Surface and volume factors for similar solids#

Take a rectangular box 333 cm by 555 cm by 888 cm. Its surface area is 158158158 square cm, and its volume is 120120120 cubic cm. Enlarge all three lengths by factor 222. Each face product contains two factors of 222, so the new surface area is 632632632 square cm. The volume product contains three factors of 222, giving 960960960 cubic cm.

Now use any positive side lengths a,b,ca,b,ca,b,c and any positive factor kkk. Let SSS denote total surface area and VVV volume. Subscripts ooo and iii mean original and image.

So=2(ab+ac+bc),Si=2((ka)(kb)+(ka)(kc)+(kb)(kc)),Si=k2So.\begin{aligned} S_o&=2(ab+ac+bc),\\ S_i&=2((ka)(kb)+(ka)(kc)+(kb)(kc)),\\ S_i&=k^2S_o. \end{aligned}So​Si​Si​​=2(ab+ac+bc),=2((ka)(kb)+(ka)(kc)+(kb)(kc)),=k2So​.​

For volume, all three perpendicular lengths change:

Vo=abc,Vi=(ka)(kb)(kc),Vi=k3Vo.\begin{aligned} V_o&=abc,\\ V_i&=(ka)(kb)(kc),\\ V_i&=k^3V_o. \end{aligned}Vo​Vi​Vi​​=abc,=(ka)(kb)(kc),=k3Vo​.​

Nothing depended on the particular box or factor 222. For a prism or cylinder, base area contributes k2k^2k2 and perpendicular height contributes kkk, giving k3k^3k3 in BhBhBh. The fixed one-third in a pyramid or cone volume does not change this factor. A sphere’s volume formula contains its radius three times, so it also has factor k3k^3k3.

The same reasoning extends to composite solids when every part, opening, and join is resized together. Corresponding added or removed volumes all acquire k3k^3k3; corresponding exposed areas acquire k2k^2k2. For curved surfaces, such as a sphere, the area formulas already derived have two length factors. More generally, approximating a curved boundary with finer planar patches preserves this squared factor as the approximation improves. Likewise, finer box approximations preserve the cubic factor for enclosed volume.

∎

Thus, for similar solids with the same corresponding boundary included in both measurements,

LiLo=k,SiSo=k2,ViVo=k3.\begin{aligned} \frac{L_i}{L_o}&=k,\\ \frac{S_i}{S_o}&=k^2,\\ \frac{V_i}{V_o}&=k^3. \end{aligned}Lo​Li​​So​Si​​Vo​Vi​​​=k,=k2,=k3.​

An open surface must correspond to the same open surface in the image. Comparing a closed original with an image missing its lid changes the boundary as well as the size.

Worked example 1 Transfer one length factor to the outside and inside

Problem. A rectangular box has dimensions 444, 666, and 999 cm. A similar image has a 999 cm side corresponding to the original 666 cm side. Find the image’s total surface area and volume.

Solution. The image-over-original length factor is 32\tfrac3223​. First calculate the original measurements.

So=2(4⋅6+4⋅9+6⋅9),So=228 cm2,Vo=4⋅6⋅9,Vo=216 cm3.\begin{aligned} S_o&=2(4\cdot6+4\cdot9+6\cdot9),\\ S_o&=228\text{ cm}^2,\\ V_o&=4\cdot6\cdot9,\\ V_o&=216\text{ cm}^3. \end{aligned}So​So​Vo​Vo​​=2(4⋅6+4⋅9+6⋅9),=228 cm2,=4⋅6⋅9,=216 cm3.​

Use the square for the surface and the cube for the enclosed space.

Si=94⋅228,Si=513 cm2,Vi=278⋅216,Vi=729 cm3.\begin{aligned} S_i&=\frac94\cdot228,\\ S_i&=513\text{ cm}^2,\\ V_i&=\frac{27}{8}\cdot216,\\ V_i&=729\text{ cm}^3. \end{aligned}Si​Si​Vi​Vi​​=49​⋅228,=513 cm2,=827​⋅216,=729 cm3.​

The image dimensions 666, 999, and 13.513.513.5 cm give the same results directly.

Recover a length factor with a root

If a similar solid has eight times the volume, its lengths are twice as long, because 23=82^3=823=8. A cube root reverses cubing. Use the positive cube root of image volume divided by original volume.

k=ViVo3.k=\sqrt[3]{\frac{V_i}{V_o}}.k=3Vo​Vi​​​.

Worked example 2 Volume first, then a length

Problem. Similar solids have original volume 375375375 cubic cm and image volume 648648648 cubic cm. A length of 101010 cm in the original corresponds to which image length?

Solution. The volume factor is not the length factor. Reverse cubing.

k3=648375,k3=216125,k=65.\begin{aligned} k^3&=\frac{648}{375},\\ k^3&=\frac{216}{125},\\ k&=\frac65. \end{aligned}k3k3k​=375648​,=125216​,=56​.​

Apply the recovered factor to the corresponding length.

Li=65⋅10,Li=12 cm.\begin{aligned} L_i&=\frac65\cdot10,\\ L_i&=12\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}Li​Li​​=56​⋅10,=12 cm.​

Cubing 65\tfrac6556​ returns the given volume ratio, which checks the factor and its direction.

Worked example 3 Surface area first, then height and volume

Problem. Similar solids have original total surface area 600600600 square cm and image total surface area 294294294 square cm. The original height is 101010 cm and its volume is 100010001000 cubic cm. Find the image height and volume.

Solution. Take the positive square root of the surface-area factor.

k2=294600,k=710.\begin{aligned} k^2&=\frac{294}{600},\\ k&=\frac7{10}. \end{aligned}k2k​=600294​,=107​.​

Height is a length, so it uses this factor once.

hi=710⋅10,hi=7 cm.\begin{aligned} h_i&=\frac7{10}\cdot10,\\ h_i&=7\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}hi​hi​​=107​⋅10,=7 cm.​

Volume uses its cube.

Vi=(710)3⋅1000,Vi=343 cm3.\begin{aligned} V_i&=\left(\frac7{10}\right)^3\cdot1000,\\ V_i&=343\text{ cm}^3. \end{aligned}Vi​Vi​​=(107​)3⋅1000,=343 cm3.​

Check your understanding

Similar solids have image surface area 144 square cm and original surface area 64 square cm. What is the image-to-original volume factor?

Answer choices

Recover the length factor before cubing it.

k=144/64,k=3/2,k3=27/8.\begin{aligned}k&=\sqrt{144/64},\\k&=3/2,\\k^3&=27/8.\end{aligned}kkk3​=144/64​,=3/2,=27/8.​

Similarity must be given or established before either root links the measurements. A volume ratio alone does not establish shape or the ratios of corresponding lengths. Also use one length unit consistently before forming an area or volume ratio.

Surface area per unit volume

The surface-area-to-volume ratio is total surface area divided by enclosed volume. It measures how much surface there is per unit of enclosed space. A cube with edge 222 cm has 242424 square cm of surface and 888 cubic cm of volume, so its ratio is 333 square cm per cubic cm.

24 cm28 cm3=3 cm−1.\frac{24\text{ cm}^2}{8\text{ cm}^3}=3\text{ cm}^{-1}.8 cm324 cm2​=3 cm−1.

The unit cm−1\text{cm}^{-1}cm−1 means per centimeter. Two powers of centimeters cancel, leaving one in the denominator. This ratio has inverse-length units; it is not a unitless fraction or a percentage.

Under a similar enlargement by factor 222, surface area is multiplied by 444 and volume by 888. Their quotient is multiplied by 12\tfrac1221​. In general, for positive volume and positive kkk,

SiVi=k2Sok3Vo,SiVi=1k⋅SoVo.\begin{aligned} \frac{S_i}{V_i}&=\frac{k^2S_o}{k^3V_o},\\ \frac{S_i}{V_i}&=\frac1k\cdot\frac{S_o}{V_o}. \end{aligned}Vi​Si​​Vi​Si​​​=k3Vo​k2So​​,=k1​⋅Vo​So​​.​

A larger similar solid has more total surface but less surface per unit volume. A reduction reverses that comparison.

Worked example 4 More surface, smaller ratio

Problem. Compare cubes with edges 333 cm and 999 cm. Find each surface-area-to-volume ratio, including units. Which cube has the greater total surface area, and which has the greater ratio?

Solution. For a cube of positive edge length sss, its surface area is 6s26s^26s2 and volume is s3s^3s3, so their quotient is 6/s6/s6/s.

For the 333 cm cube:

S=54 cm2,V=27 cm3,S/V=2 cm−1.\begin{aligned} S&=54\text{ cm}^2,\\ V&=27\text{ cm}^3,\\ S/V&=2\text{ cm}^{-1}. \end{aligned}SVS/V​=54 cm2,=27 cm3,=2 cm−1.​

For the 999 cm cube:

S=486 cm2,V=729 cm3,S/V=23 cm−1.\begin{aligned} S&=486\text{ cm}^2,\\ V&=729\text{ cm}^3,\\ S/V&=\frac23\text{ cm}^{-1}. \end{aligned}SVS/V​=486 cm2,=729 cm3,=32​ cm−1.​

The 999 cm cube has more surface in total. The 333 cm cube has the greater surface-area-to-volume ratio. The length factor 333 divides the ratio by 333, agreeing with the calculation.

A stretch changes a different set of lengths

Making a box taller while keeping its base unchanged does not resize the whole box uniformly. Some lengths change and others do not, so one common length factor cannot describe it.

Worked example 5 Compare a height change with a uniform resize

Problem. A closed rectangular box is 222 cm by 555 cm by 777 cm. Compare these two changes.

Case A. Double only the 777 cm height, keeping the base 222 cm by 555 cm.

Case B. Double every length of the original box.

Find the new volume and total surface area in each case. Decide which image is similar to the original.

Solution. The original volume is 707070 cubic cm and total surface area is 118118118 square cm.

Case A. Only height doubles. The base area remains 101010 square cm, so volume doubles. The two base faces keep their areas, while the four side faces double theirs.

VA=2⋅5⋅14,VA=140 cm3,SA=2(2⋅5+2⋅14+5⋅14),SA=216 cm2.\begin{aligned} V_A&=2\cdot5\cdot14,\\ V_A&=140\text{ cm}^3,\\ S_A&=2(2\cdot5+2\cdot14+5\cdot14),\\ S_A&=216\text{ cm}^2. \end{aligned}VA​VA​SA​SA​​=2⋅5⋅14,=140 cm3,=2(2⋅5+2⋅14+5⋅14),=216 cm2.​

This box is not similar to the original. Its base lengths use factor 111 while its height uses factor 222.

Case B. All lengths use factor 222, so similarity is preserved.

VB=8⋅70,VB=560 cm3,SB=4⋅118,SB=472 cm2.\begin{aligned} V_B&=8\cdot70,\\ V_B&=560\text{ cm}^3,\\ S_B&=4\cdot118,\\ S_B&=472\text{ cm}^2. \end{aligned}VB​VB​SB​SB​​=8⋅70,=560 cm3,=4⋅118,=472 cm2.​

Check your understanding

A right cylinder's height doubles while its radius stays fixed. Which statement follows?

Answer choices

The circular base area stays fixed and the perpendicular height doubles, so volume doubles. The radius has factor 1 and height has factor 2, preventing whole-solid similarity. The two circular ends keep their areas while the curved side area doubles.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • One changed length. A doubled height does not establish a uniform doubling. Check the whole shape and all corresponding dimensions.
  • The wrong root. Use a positive square root for a surface-area factor and a positive cube root for a volume factor.
  • Reversed direction. Choose image over original consistently; reverse the factor when returning from image to original.
  • A ratio without units. Surface area divided by volume has inverse-length units. Compare values using the same length unit.

Key takeaways

  • Similar solids with positive length factor kkk have surface-area factor k2k^2k2 and volume factor k3k^3k3.
  • Recover the length factor before transferring an area or volume ratio to another measurement.
  • Surface area per unit volume changes by factor 1/k1/k1/k under a similar resize.
  • When only one dimension changes, use the actual geometry and its formulas.

A bit of history (optional)

Galileo’s 1638 book Two New Sciences opens with a conversation about Venice’s busy shipyard. His characters recall an experienced worker explaining why a large vessel needs substantial supports when it is launched: its own weight can threaten to break it.

Sagredo, one of the speakers, finds the warning puzzling. If a large machine keeps the same proportions as a successful small one, why should size cause trouble? Salviati answers that preserving proportions does not preserve every physical property. The discussion turns from the shipyard to the strength of wooden rods.

This is a written dialogue, so these speeches are part of Galileo’s presentation, not a recorded shipyard interview. Beginning with a craft worker’s practical warning gave him a question that a drawing of two similar shapes could easily hide.
