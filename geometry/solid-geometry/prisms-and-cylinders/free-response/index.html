  1. Problem 1 A constant section

    Every section of a prism parallel to its bases has area 11.511.511.5 square cm. The perpendicular distance between its base planes is 6.46.46.4 cm. Find its volume and explain the units.

    1. Hint 1

      Think about how a prism repeats one base area through its height.

    2. Hint 2

      Multiply the section area by the perpendicular distance.

    Answer

    73.673.673.6 cubic cm.

    Full solution

    Each base-parallel section is a translated copy of the base, so the base area is 11.5 square cm.

    Multiply by the perpendicular height.

    V=11.5(6.4)V=73.6 cm3.\begin{aligned}V&=11.5(6.4)\\V&=73.6\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}VV​=11.5(6.4)=73.6 cm3.​

    Square centimeters times centimeters give cubic centimeters, as required for volume.

    Answer

    73.673.673.6 cubic cm.

    Key idea

    A constant base-parallel section area supplies the base area in the prism volume product.

  2. Problem 2 Smaller measuring cubes

    A rectangular prism is filled without gaps by cubes with edge length 0.50.50.5 cm. There are seven cubes along its length, four along its width, and three along its height.

    Find the prism volume in cubic centimeters. Explain why its cube count and its volume number differ.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify the volume of one measuring cube before totaling the cubes.

    2. Hint 2

      There are three factors of one half in each small cube volume.

    Answer

    10.510.510.5 cubic cm; there are 84 cubes, each occupying 0.1250.1250.125 cubic cm.

    Full solution

    The array contains

    7(4)(3)=847(4)(3)=847(4)(3)=84

    small cubes.

    One cube has volume

    0.53=0.125 cm3.0.5^3=0.125\text{ cm}^3.0.53=0.125 cm3.

    Therefore

    V=84(0.125)V=10.5 cm3.\begin{aligned}V&=84(0.125)\\V&=10.5\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}VV​=84(0.125)=10.5 cm3.​

    The count is 84, but these are not one-cubic-centimeter cubes.

    As a length check, the whole prism is 3.5 cm by 2 cm by 1.5 cm, whose product is also 10.5 cubic cm.

    Answer

    10.510.510.5 cubic cm; there are 84 cubes, each occupying 0.1250.1250.125 cubic cm.

    Key idea

    A cube count becomes a volume only after multiplying by the volume of each measuring cube.

  3. Problem 3 A height hidden in the lean

    An oblique prism has base area 454545 square cm. Its joining edge is 131313 cm long. In a side view perpendicular to the base planes, that edge, the perpendicular height, and a 555 cm horizontal offset form a right triangle.

    Find the perpendicular height and the prism volume.

    Text description of this figure

    A right triangle in a perpendicular side view has slanted hypotenuse 13 cm, horizontal leg 5 cm, and vertical leg h; the prism base area is 45 square centimeters.

    1. Hint 1

      Distinguish the distance between base planes from the slanted joining edge.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the right triangle to recover the perpendicular distance, then find the volume.

    Answer

    Height 121212 cm; volume 540540540 cubic cm.

    Full solution

    The 13 cm joining edge is the hypotenuse of the supplied right triangle.

    Subtract the offset square.

    h2=132−52h2=144h=12 cm.\begin{aligned}h^2&=13^2-5^2\\h^2&=144\\h&=12\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}h2h2h​=132−52=144=12 cm.​

    Every base-parallel section has area 45 square cm.

    V=45(12)V=540 cm3.\begin{aligned}V&=45(12)\\V&=540\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}VV​=45(12)=540 cm3.​

    The positive height checks the right triangle because its square plus 25 is 169.

    Answer

    Height 121212 cm; volume 540540540 cubic cm.

    Key idea

    Recover the perpendicular height before using the base-area volume product.

  4. Problem 4 Different sections, equal amounts

    A right prism has base area 262626 square cm and perpendicular height 6.56.56.5 cm. An oblique circular cylinder has the same base area and perpendicular height.

    Explain why their volumes are equal, checking Cavalieri's hypotheses across the full height. Then give the common volume.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare cuts parallel to the bases at corresponding heights.

    2. Hint 2

      Each prism section repeats its polygonal base; each cylinder section repeats its disk base.

    Answer

    Both volumes are 169169169 cubic cm. Every corresponding base-parallel section has area 262626 square cm over the common 6.56.56.5 cm height.

    Full solution

    Place the lower base planes at the same level.

    Equal perpendicular heights put both upper bases in one parallel plane.

    At every height between these planes, the prism section is a translated base polygon of area 26 square cm.

    The oblique cylinder section is a translated base disk of area 26 square cm.

    The section shapes differ, but all corresponding areas agree.

    Cavalieri therefore gives equal volumes.

    The common volume is

    V=26(6.5)V=169 cm3.\begin{aligned}V&=26(6.5)\\V&=169\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}VV​=26(6.5)=169 cm3.​

    Answer

    Both volumes are 169169169 cubic cm. Every corresponding base-parallel section has area 262626 square cm over the common 6.56.56.5 cm height.

    Key idea

    Cavalieri compares section areas at every height, rather than requiring congruent section shapes.

  5. Problem 5 The two ends and the side strip

    A closed right prism has base area 464646 square cm and base perimeter 323232 cm. Its total surface area is 348348348 square cm.

    Find its perpendicular height and its volume.

    1. Hint 1

      Separate the two end faces from the lateral rectangles.

    2. Hint 2

      After removing the two base areas, divide the remaining area by the base perimeter.

    Answer

    Height 888 cm; volume 368368368 cubic cm.

    Full solution

    Both bases together occupy

    2B=92 cm2.2B=92\text{ cm}^2.2B=92 cm2.

    Subtract them to isolate the side strip.

    L=348−92L=256 cm2.\begin{aligned}L&=348-92\\L&=256\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}LL​=348−92=256 cm2.​

    Because the prism is right, the side rectangles share its height.

    32h=256h=8 cm.\begin{aligned}32h&=256\\h&=8\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}32hh​=256=8 cm.​

    Now find volume.

    V=46(8)V=368 cm3.\begin{aligned}V&=46(8)\\V&=368\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}VV​=46(8)=368 cm3.​

    The surface check adds 256 and 92 to recover 348 square cm.

    Answer

    Height 888 cm; volume 368368368 cubic cm.

    Key idea

    A closed right prism surface area separates into two bases and a perimeter-by-height side strip.

  6. Problem 6 Choose the end disks

    A right circular cylinder has radius 2.52.52.5 cm and height 6.26.26.2 cm. Ignore thickness and seams. Give exact areas in terms of pi for each specified boundary.

    Case A includes the curved side only, with neither end disk.

    Case B includes the curved side and one end disk.

    Case C includes the curved side and both end disks.

    Text description of this figure

    A right circular cylinder with radius 2.5 cm and height 6.2 cm. Circular boundaries locate the possible end disks; the cases specify which disks are included.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify the pieces common to all three boundaries.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the curved-side area and one disk area separately, then include the required number of disks.

    Answer

    A:31π cm2B:37.25π cm2C:43.5π cm2.\begin{aligned}A&:31\pi\text{ cm}^2\\B&:37.25\pi\text{ cm}^2\\C&:43.5\pi\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}ABC​:31π cm2:37.25π cm2:43.5π cm2.​

    Full solution

    The common curved-side area is

    L=2π(2.5)(6.2)L=31π cm2.\begin{aligned}L&=2\pi(2.5)(6.2)\\L&=31\pi\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}LL​=2π(2.5)(6.2)=31π cm2.​

    One disk has area

    B=6.25π cm2.B=6.25\pi\text{ cm}^2.B=6.25π cm2.

    Case A includes no disk, so its area is 31π31\pi31π square cm.

    Case B includes one disk.

    31π+6.25π=37.25π cm2.31\pi+6.25\pi=37.25\pi\text{ cm}^2.31π+6.25π=37.25π cm2.

    Case C includes two disks.

    31π+12.5π=43.5π cm2.31\pi+12.5\pi=43.5\pi\text{ cm}^2.31π+12.5π=43.5π cm2.

    Answer

    A:31π cm2B:37.25π cm2C:43.5π cm2.\begin{aligned}A&:31\pi\text{ cm}^2\\B&:37.25\pi\text{ cm}^2\\C&:43.5\pi\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}ABC​:31π cm2:37.25π cm2:43.5π cm2.​

    Key idea

    The words describing the boundary decide how many base areas to add.

  7. Problem 7 Capacity and sheet material

    An ideal open rectangular tank has inside base dimensions 111111 dm by 888 dm and capacity 1,3201{,}3201,320 liters. It has a bottom and four upright sides, with no lid. Use 111 liter as one cubic decimeter.

    Find its inside height and the area of sheet material needed. Ignore thickness, seams, and waste.

    Text description of this figure

    An open rectangular tank has inside base 11 dm by 8 dm and unknown perpendicular height h; the top rim is open and hidden lower edges are dashed.

    1. Hint 1

      Use capacity to determine the missing inside dimension.

    2. Hint 2

      List the bottom and four sides before totaling the material area.

    Answer

    Height 151515 dm; sheet area 658658658 square dm.

    Full solution

    The inside base area is

    B=88 dm2.B=88\text{ dm}^2.B=88 dm2.

    Capacity is 1320 cubic dm.

    Divide volume by base area.

    h=1320/88h=15 dm.\begin{aligned}h&=1320/88\\h&=15\text{ dm}.\end{aligned}hh​=1320/88=15 dm.​

    The four side rectangles total

    L=2(11+8)(15)L=570 dm2.\begin{aligned}L&=2(11+8)(15)\\L&=570\text{ dm}^2.\end{aligned}LL​=2(11+8)(15)=570 dm2.​

    Add one bottom, since the top is open.

    S=570+88S=658 dm2.\begin{aligned}S&=570+88\\S&=658\text{ dm}^2.\end{aligned}SS​=570+88=658 dm2.​

    The recovered dimensions give 1320 cubic dm.

    A lid would require another 88 square dm, which the stated boundary excludes.

    Answer

    Height 151515 dm; sheet area 658658658 square dm.

    Key idea

    Capacity determines the inside space, while the listed boundary pieces determine material area.

  8. Problem 8 A ratio of different measurements

    First consider a right circular cylinder with radius 666 cm and height 555 cm. Find its volume divided by its curved-side area, including the unit of the quotient.

    Then take any right circular cylinder with positive radius r and height h. Derive its volume divided by its curved-side area in terms of r. Explain why the height cancels.

    1. Hint 1

      Write both measurements before taking their quotient.

    2. Hint 2

      The same positive height and one radius factor occur in numerator and denominator.

    Answer

    Numerical quotient 333 cm. General quotient V/L=r/2V/L=r/2V/L=r/2, a length.

    Full solution

    For the numerical cylinder,

    V=180π cm3L=60π cm2.\begin{aligned}V&=180\pi\text{ cm}^3\\L&=60\pi\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}VL​=180π cm3=60π cm2.​

    Their quotient is

    V/L=3 cm.V/L=3\text{ cm}.V/L=3 cm.

    Cubic units divided by square units leave a length.

    For arbitrary positive r and h,

    VL=πr2h2πrhVL=r2.\begin{aligned}\frac VL&=\frac{\pi r^2h}{2\pi rh}\\\frac VL&=\frac r2.\end{aligned}LV​LV​​=2πrhπr2h​=2r​.​

    The positive common height, pi, and one radius factor cancel.

    Increasing only the height multiplies volume and curved-side area by the same factor, which leaves this quotient unchanged.

    Answer

    Numerical quotient 333 cm. General quotient V/L=r/2V/L=r/2V/L=r/2, a length.

    Key idea

    The volume-to-curved-side-area quotient recovers half the radius of a right circular cylinder.

  9. Problem 9 How much section evidence?

    Two ordinary solids lie between the planes at heights 0 cm and 6 cm. At heights 0 cm, 2 cm, 4 cm, and 6 cm, each solid has section area 9 square cm in the plane parallel to those boundary planes.

    Does this evidence establish equal volumes by Cavalieri? State exactly what additional equality must be known. No volume calculation is required.

    1. Hint 1

      Read the quantifier in Cavalieri's principle.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare the finite list of tested heights with all heights between the planes.

    Answer

    No. Section areas must be equal at every corresponding height throughout the interval from 0 cm to 6 cm.

    Full solution

    The boundary planes already provide a common height.

    The four measurements provide equal section areas only at four selected levels.

    Nothing states that the sections agree between those levels.

    Cavalieri requires equal section areas in every parallel plane throughout the full height.

    The volumes might be equal, but the supplied evidence does not establish that conclusion by Cavalieri.

    Answer

    No. Section areas must be equal at every corresponding height throughout the interval from 0 cm to 6 cm.

    Key idea

    A finite collection of matching samples does not supply an every-height hypothesis.

  10. Problem 10 Recover a cylinder and its boundary

    A right circular cylinder has volume 243π243\pi243π cubic cm and curved-side area 54π54\pi54π square cm. All dimensions are positive.

    Find its radius and height.

    Find the boundary area if the curved side and exactly one base are included.

    Find the extra area required to close the other end. Check the recovered dimensions in both supplied measurements.

    1. Hint 1

      Both measurements contain the height; compare them to isolate the radius.

    2. Hint 2

      Divide volume by curved-side area, then substitute the recovered radius into either original relation.

    3. Hint 3

      The specified boundary has one disk; closing the other end adds one more disk.

    Answer

    Radius 999 cm; height 333 cm. One-base boundary 135π135\pi135π square cm; extra closing disk 81π81\pi81π square cm.

    Full solution

    Divide the two measurements.

    243π54π=r292=r2r=9 cm.\begin{aligned}\frac{243\pi}{54\pi}&=\frac r2\\\frac92&=\frac r2\\r&=9\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}54π243π​29​r​=2r​=2r​=9 cm.​

    Use the curved-side area to find height.

    54π=2π(9)hh=3 cm.\begin{aligned}54\pi&=2\pi(9)h\\h&=3\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}54πh​=2π(9)h=3 cm.​

    One base has area

    B=81π cm2.B=81\pi\text{ cm}^2.B=81π cm2.

    The specified one-base boundary has area

    S=135π cm2.S=135\pi\text{ cm}^2.S=135π cm2.

    Closing the other end requires an additional 81π81\pi81π square cm.

    Check volume and side area separately.

    π(9)2(3)=243π cm32π(9)(3)=54π cm2.\begin{aligned}\pi(9)^2(3)&=243\pi\text{ cm}^3\\2\pi(9)(3)&=54\pi\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}π(9)2(3)2π(9)(3)​=243π cm3=54π cm2.​

    Both agree with the givens.

    Answer

    Radius 999 cm; height 333 cm. One-base boundary 135π135\pi135π square cm; extra closing disk 81π81\pi81π square cm.

    Key idea

    Coupled volume and lateral-area data determine dimensions before the boundary pieces are added.
