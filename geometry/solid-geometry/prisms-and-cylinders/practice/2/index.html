  1. 1

    An oblique circular cylinder has radius 444 cm, perpendicular height 666 cm, and slanted joining segments of length 101010 cm. What is its volume?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The base-parallel disk area stays constant under the lean. Multiply it by the perpendicular height.

    B=16π cm2V=16π(6)V=96π cm3.\begin{aligned}B&=16\pi\text{ cm}^2\\V&=16\pi(6)\\V&=96\pi\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}BVV​=16π cm2=16π(6)=96π cm3.​

    A right prism has right triangular bases with legs 666 cm and 888 cm. The bases are 111111 cm apart. What is its volume?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Find one triangle area, then extend through the prism height.

    B=12(6)(8)B=24 cm2V=24(11)V=264 cm3.\begin{aligned}B&=\tfrac12(6)(8)\\B&=24\text{ cm}^2\\V&=24(11)\\V&=264\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}BBVV​=21​(6)(8)=24 cm2=24(11)=264 cm3.​

    The side of a right circular cylinder unrolls to a rectangle 12π12\pi12π cm wide around the base and 555 cm high. What is the cylinder's volume?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The wrapped width is the circumference, so recover the radius first.

    2πr=12πr=6 cmV=π(6)2(5)V=180π cm3.\begin{aligned}2\pi r&=12\pi\\r&=6\text{ cm}\\V&=\pi(6)^2(5)\\V&=180\pi\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}2πrrVV​=12π=6 cm=π(6)2(5)=180π cm3.​
  11. 11

    Why is multiplying the base perimeter by the perpendicular solid height not a general lateral-area method for oblique prisms?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The surface calculation needs the altitude within each lateral face. A distance perpendicular to the base planes need not be that face altitude. Cavalieri preserves volume in the stated comparison, without supplying a surface-area formula.

  12. 12

    A prism and a circular cylinder both have base area 151515 square cm and perpendicular height 888 cm. Which statement justifies equal volumes?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Both solids keep the base area throughout their heights. Cavalieri applies even though a polygon and a disk have different shapes.

    V=15(8)V=120 cm3.\begin{aligned}V&=15(8)\\V&=120\text{ cm}^3.\end{aligned}VV​=15(8)=120 cm3.​
