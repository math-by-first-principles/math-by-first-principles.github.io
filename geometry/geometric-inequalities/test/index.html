  4. Problem 4 Across a line

    P and Q lie on opposite sides of line l. Their perpendicular feet H and K on l are distinct. The perpendicular lengths, in meters, are

    PH=7.PH=7.PH=7. QK=10.QK=10.QK=10.

    Text description of this figure

    P and Q are on opposite sides of line l. The distinct feet H and K are marked with right angles. PH is 7 meters and QK is 10 meters. Segment PQ is shown without a length or a derived comparison.

    1. Hint 1

      The segment from P to Q crosses the line at some point R.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare each part PR and RQ with the perpendicular from the same point. Include the possibility that R is one of the feet.

    3. Hint 3

      Can both comparisons be equalities when the feet are distinct?

    Answer

    With distinct feet,

    PQ>17.PQ>17.PQ>17.

    If the feet coincide, P and Q lie on the same perpendicular on opposite sides of l, and PQ is 17 meters.

    Full solution

    Let R be the point where segment PQ crosses l.

    It lies between P and Q, so

    PQ=PR+RQ.PQ=PR+RQ.PQ=PR+RQ.

    If R differs from H, then PR is the hypotenuse of right triangle PHR and is strictly longer than PH.

    If R equals H, PR equals PH.

    Thus

    PR≥PH.PR\ge PH.PR≥PH.

    Thus

    RQ≥QK.RQ\ge QK.RQ≥QK.

    At least one of these comparisons is strict.

    Adding gives

    PQ>PH+QK.PQ>PH+QK.PQ>PH+QK.

    The given perpendicular lengths sum to 17 meters, so a length of 17 is impossible with distinct feet.

    If H and K coincide, the two perpendiculars lie on the same line.

    Since P and Q are on opposite sides of l, the common foot lies between them.

    Segment addition then gives

    PQ=17.PQ=17.PQ=17.

    Answer

    With distinct feet,

    PQ>17.PQ>17.PQ>17.

    If the feet coincide, P and Q lie on the same perpendicular on opposite sides of l, and PQ is 17 meters.

    Key idea

    Separate equality cases before adding two shortest-distance comparisons.

  10. Problem 10 A moving point

    V, T, W lie on a line in that order, with T strictly between V and W.

    S may lie anywhere in the plane subject to the condition

    TS=TW.TS=TW.TS=TW.

    Determine every possible comparison between VS and VW. State exactly which allowed position of S gives equality, and justify your classification for all positions, including collinear and coincident points.

    Text description of this figure

    One allowed example has V, T, W on a line in that order and S off the line. TS and TW have matching single ticks. The question permits other positions of S. No comparison between VS and VW is marked.

      Compare the direct segment VS with the two-leg route through T, and keep equality separate from strictness.

    VS≤VW.VS\le VW.VS≤VW.

    VS<VW.VS<VW.VS<VW.

    Both VT and TW are positive.

    VS<VT+TS.VS<VT+TS.VS<VT+TS.

    VT+TS=VT+TW.VT+TS=VT+TW.VT+TS=VT+TW.

    VT+TW=VW.VT+TW=VW.VT+TW=VW.

    In this case,

    VS=VW.VS=VW.VS=VW.

    VW=VS+ST+TW.VW=VS+ST+TW.VW=VS+ST+TW.
