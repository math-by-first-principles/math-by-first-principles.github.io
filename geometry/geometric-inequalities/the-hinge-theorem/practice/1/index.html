The Hinge Theorem: Practice

12 multiple-choice questions, progressively harder.

Level 1 · Foundational 0 / 12 answered
Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    Triangles KLM and RST have

    KL=RS.KL=RS.KL=RS.

    They also have

    KM=RT.KM=RT.KM=RT.

    Which remaining sides are opposite the included angles K and R, respectively?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The matched sides meet at K and R. The side opposite K does not touch K, so it is LM. The side opposite R is ST.

    LM and ST are the third sides whose lengths a hinge comparison would order.

  2. 2

    The two triangles have

    AB=DE.AB=DE.AB=DE.

    Also,

    AC=DF.AC=DF.AC=DF.

    The included angles at A and D measure 35 and 75 degrees, respectively. Which comparison follows?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Both side pairs match. The larger included angle is at D.

    The hinge theorem puts the longer third side opposite D.

    EF>BC.EF>BC.EF>BC.
  3. 3

    Two triangles have matching arms of lengths 5 and 8 units. Their third sides measure 7 and 10 units. Which included angle is larger?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The two corresponding arm pairs match.

    The converse hinge theorem matches the longer third side to the larger included angle.

    10>7.10>7.10>7.
  4. 4

    The two triangles have

    AB=DE.AB=DE.AB=DE.

    Also,

    AC=DF.AC=DF.AC=DF.

    Their included angles are equal. What follows about BC and EF?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The two side pairs and included angles match. SAS makes the triangles congruent.

    Corresponding third sides therefore match.

    BC=EF.BC=EF.BC=EF.
  5. 5

    You know

    AB=DEAB=DEAB=DE

    and that angle BAC is larger than angle EDF. Which additional equality supplies the missing hypothesis for a hinge comparison of BC and EF?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The included angles are at A and D. The sides surrounding them are AB with AC, and DE with DF.

    AB already matches DE; the missing pair is AC with DF.

    AC=DF.AC=DF.AC=DF.
  6. 6

    The two triangles have

    AB=DE.AB=DE.AB=DE.

    Also,

    AC=DF.AC=DF.AC=DF.

    The given angles are ABC and DEF. Are those the included angles for the matched pairs?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The matched sides meet at A in the first triangle and D in the second.

    The given angles have vertices B and E, so their comparison cannot be inserted directly into the hinge theorem.

  7. 7

    Triangles ABC and DEF have

    AB=DF.AB=DF.AB=DF.

    They also have

    AC=DE.AC=DE.AC=DE.

    Angle BAC measures 38 degrees, and angle FDE measures 71 degrees. Which third side is longer?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The correspondence is reversed along the second triangle: AB matches DF, and AC matches DE. These pairs surround angles BAC and FDE.

    The larger included angle is at D. The hinge theorem therefore gives

    EF>BC.EF>BC.EF>BC.
  8. 8

    The two triangles have

    AB=DE.AB=DE.AB=DE.

    Also,

    AC=DF.AC=DF.AC=DF.

    Angle BAC is smaller than angle EDF. What follows?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The side pairs match. Apply the hinge theorem with triangle DEF first.

    Its larger opening faces the longer third side.

    EF>BC.EF>BC.EF>BC.
  9. 9

    In a hinge proof, the copied lengths satisfy

    AC=AX.AC=AX.AC=AX.

    The side AP is common, and AP bisects angle CAX. Which criterion proves triangles ACP and AXP congruent?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    AC and AX supply one equal pair, while AP matches itself.

    The bisector supplies equal angles between those pairs. Those are the SAS hypotheses.

  10. 10

    Which proposed included angle cannot belong to a nondegenerate triangle?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Every triangle angle is positive and less than 180 degrees.

    An opening of 180 degrees places the two arms on opposite rays, producing a flat arrangement.

  11. 11

    Two triangles have the same arm lengths of 3 and 7 units. Their included angles are 40 and 70 degrees. What does the hinge theorem determine?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The arm pairs match, and the opening of 70 degrees is larger.

    Its opposite third side is longer. The hinge theorem gives a strict order; it does not compute the lengths.

  12. 12

    Two triangles satisfy all hinge hypotheses. One drawing is rotated so its included angle points downward. How does the comparison change?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A rotation preserves every side length and angle measure.

    The corresponding pairs and the included-angle order remain the same, so the hinge conclusion remains the same.
