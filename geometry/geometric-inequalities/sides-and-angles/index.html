Sides, Angles, and Shortest Distances

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

A triangular frame has one corner that opens wider than another. Can you tell which opposite edge is longer without measuring either edge? To answer, you need more than a convincing sketch. You need a connection between the sizes of angles and the lengths of sides. That connection will also settle which segment from a point reaches a line by the shortest distance.

Learning goals

  • Compare sides and opposite angles within the same triangle
  • Justify side and angle ordering using an exterior angle and indirect proof
  • Identify the hypotenuse and explain why it is the longest side
  • Determine the shortest segment from a point to a line

Match each angle to its opposite side

The side opposite an angle is the side that does not touch the angle’s vertex. In triangle ABCABCABC, side BCBCBC is opposite angle AAA. Side ACACAC is opposite angle BBB, and side ABABAB is opposite angle CCC.

Here ABABAB without a bar denotes the length of segment ABABAB. Angle sizes are their measures, such as m∠ABCm\angle ABCm∠ABC. We compare lengths with lengths and angle measures with angle measures.

In the diagram, angle BBB measures 80∘80^\circ80∘ and angle CCC measures 40∘40^\circ40∘. The side facing BBB looks longer than the side facing CCC. Rotating the paper would change neither the angles nor that comparison. The two proofs below show why the comparison must hold.

All triangles in this lesson have three noncollinear vertices. A flat arrangement of points is not a triangle here.

Check your understanding

In triangle KLMKLMKLM, angle LLL is larger than angle MMM. Which sides are opposite those angles, in that order?

Answer choices

The side not touching L is KM. The side not touching M is KL. Match the vertices before trying to compare any lengths.

An exterior angle is larger

An exterior angle is larger than either of its remote interior angles, the two interior angles away from its vertex. For example, remote angles of 43∘43^\circ43∘ and 62∘62^\circ62∘ give an exterior angle of

43∘+62∘=105∘.43^\circ+62^\circ=105^\circ.43∘+62∘=105∘.

Their sum is greater than each positive part. This is the inequality consequence of the exterior angle theorem.

In general, the exterior angle theorem gives

m∠ACD=m∠CAB+m∠ABC.m\angle ACD=m\angle CAB+m\angle ABC.m∠ACD=m∠CAB+m∠ABC.

Both remote interior angles have positive measure, so

m∠ACD>m∠CAB.m\angle ACD>m\angle CAB.m∠ACD>m∠CAB.

Also,

m∠ACD>m∠ABC.m\angle ACD>m\angle ABC.m∠ACD>m∠ABC.

The adjacent interior angle at CCC is not one of the remote angles. An exterior angle need not be larger than that adjacent angle.

Why a longer side faces a larger angle

A longer side in a triangle faces a larger angle. We can prove this by cutting the longer side to make an isosceles triangle.

A longer side has a larger opposite angle#

First follow the construction with numbers. Let DDD lie between AAA and CCC, and make ADADAD equal to ABABAB. Imagine an instance where angle ABDABDABD is 70∘70^\circ70∘ and angle DBCDBCDBC is 25∘25^\circ25∘.

The equal sides in triangle ABDABDABD make its base angles equal. Thus angle BDABDABDA also measures 70∘70^\circ70∘.

Angle BDABDABDA is exterior to triangle BDCBDCBDC. Its remote angles at BBB and CCC add to 70∘70^\circ70∘, so

m∠BCD=70∘−25∘.m\angle BCD=70^\circ-25^\circ.m∠BCD=70∘−25∘.

Therefore,

m∠BCD=45∘.m\angle BCD=45^\circ.m∠BCD=45∘.

The full angle at BBB combines the two parts:

m∠ABC=70∘+25∘.m\angle ABC=70^\circ+25^\circ.m∠ABC=70∘+25∘.

Thus angle ABCABCABC measures 95∘95^\circ95∘, greater than angle ACBACBACB, which measures 45∘45^\circ45∘. The larger side ACACAC faces the larger angle.

Now let ABCABCABC be any triangle with

AC>AB.AC>AB.AC>AB.

Choose DDD on segment ACACAC so that

AD=AB.AD=AB.AD=AB.

This point lies strictly between AAA and CCC: the positive length ABABAB is less than ACACAC. Join BBB to DDD. The diagram below keeps only the general construction.

Triangle ABDABDABD is isosceles, so

m∠ABD=m∠BDA.m\angle ABD=m\angle BDA.m∠ABD=m∠BDA.

Since ray DADADA extends ray DCDCDC through DDD, angle BDABDABDA is exterior to triangle BDCBDCBDC. The exterior angle inequality gives

m∠BDA>m∠BCD.m\angle BDA>m\angle BCD.m∠BDA>m∠BCD.

Because DDD lies between AAA and CCC, segment BDBDBD lies inside angle ABCABCABC, so the full angle is greater than its part:

m∠ABC>m∠ABD.m\angle ABC>m\angle ABD.m∠ABC>m∠ABD.

The comparisons connect in this order:

m∠ABC>m∠ABD,m∠ABD=m∠BDA,m∠BDA>m∠BCD,m∠BCD=m∠ACB.\begin{aligned} m\angle ABC&>m\angle ABD,\\ m\angle ABD&=m\angle BDA,\\ m\angle BDA&>m\angle BCD,\\ m\angle BCD&=m\angle ACB. \end{aligned}m∠ABCm∠ABDm∠BDAm∠BCD​>m∠ABD,=m∠BDA,>m∠BCD,=m∠ACB.​

The last equality holds because rays CACACA and CDCDCD coincide. Therefore,

m∠ABC>m∠ACB.m\angle ABC>m\angle ACB.m∠ABC>m∠ACB.

Neither the isosceles step nor the exterior angle inequality needed the special numbers 707070 and 252525. The argument works for every positive difference between the two side lengths.

∎

Reverse the argument

A larger angle also faces a longer side.

A larger angle has a longer opposite side#

For a numerical test, suppose angle BBB is 75∘75^\circ75∘ and angle CCC is 45∘45^\circ45∘. Could ACACAC and ABABAB be equal? That would force the two angles to be equal. Could ACACAC be shorter? The result just proved would force angle BBB to be smaller. Both alternatives contradict the given measures.

Suppose

m∠ABC>m∠ACB.m\angle ABC>m\angle ACB.m∠ABC>m∠ACB.

We use indirect proof: rule out the alternatives to the desired comparison.

If ACACAC equaled ABABAB, the isosceles base angles at BBB and CCC would be equal. That contradicts the given strict inequality.

If ACACAC were less than ABABAB, the longer-side result would make angle ACBACBACB greater than angle ABCABCABC. That also contradicts the given.

Equality and the reverse inequality have both failed. The remaining possibility is

AC>AB.AC>AB.AC>AB.

The numerical test used 757575 and 454545, but the two contradictions depend only on which angle is larger.

∎

The two results apply within the same triangle: compare the sides by comparing their opposite angles, or compare the angles by comparing their opposite sides. Equal sides and equal opposite angles remain the equality case from the isosceles theorems.

Check your understanding

Triangle ABCABCABC has

AC>AB.AC>AB.AC>AB.

In the cutoff proof, DDD lies on ACACAC with

AD=AB.AD=AB.AD=AB.

Which fact connects the equal base angles of triangle ABDABDABD to angle CCC?

Answer choices

DA extends DC through D. The exterior angle BDA is greater than the remote angle BCD.

The isosceles step makes ABD equal to BDA, and the whole angle ABC is greater than its part ABD. No perpendicular or midpoint is required.

Check your understanding

Triangle ABCABCABC has angle BBB larger than angle CCC. In an indirect proof, suppose

AC=AB.AC=AB.AC=AB.

Why does this contradict the given angle comparison?

Answer choices

In triangle ABC, AC is opposite angle B and AB is opposite angle C. The isosceles base-angle theorem would give

m∠B=m∠C.m\angle B=m\angle C.m∠B=m∠C.

This contradicts the given fact that angle B is larger than angle C, so the equality alternative is ruled out.

Put comparisons in order

Worked example 1 Rank three sides

Problem. Triangle PQRPQRPQR has angle PPP of 48∘48^\circ48∘, angle QQQ of 67∘67^\circ67∘, and angle RRR of 65∘65^\circ65∘. List its sides from shortest to longest.

Solution. The smallest angle is at PPP, so its opposite side QRQRQR is shortest. The middle angle is at RRR, so PQPQPQ comes next. The largest angle is at QQQ, so PRPRPR is longest:

QR<PQ.QR<PQ.QR<PQ.

Then,

PQ<PR.PQ<PR.PQ<PR.

Worked example 2 Turn a geometric comparison into algebra

Problem. An existing triangle ABCABCABC has angle BBB larger than angle CCC. Its side lengths are

AC=x+6,AC=x+6,AC=x+6,

and

AB=2x+1.AB=2x+1.AB=2x+1.

Given that xxx is positive, find the necessary restriction on xxx.

Solution. Side ACACAC faces angle BBB, so

x+6>2x+1.x+6>2x+1.x+6>2x+1.

Subtract xxx and then subtract 111:

5>x.5>x.5>x.

Combining this with the given positivity,

0<x<5.0<x<5.0<x<5.

At x=5x=5x=5 the sides would match, which would force equal opposite angles. We exclude that endpoint. This is a necessary restriction from the comparison; it does not specify the triangle’s third side.

The longest side in a right triangle

The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse. It is always the longest side of a right triangle.

For instance, a triangle with one right angle and an acute angle of 35∘35^\circ35∘ has a remaining angle of

180∘−90∘−35∘=55∘.180^\circ-90^\circ-35^\circ=55^\circ.180∘−90∘−35∘=55∘.

The right angle is larger than both acute angles. Its opposite side is therefore longer than both other sides.

The hypotenuse is longer than either leg#

In any right triangle, the two other angles are positive and sum to 90∘90^\circ90∘. Each is therefore less than 90∘90^\circ90∘. The larger-angle result puts the side opposite the right angle ahead of each side opposite an acute angle.

∎

Worked example 3 Find the hypotenuse from the right angle

Problem. Triangle PQRPQRPQR is right at QQQ. Angle PPP measures 58∘58^\circ58∘. Identify the hypotenuse and order the sides from shortest to longest.

Solution. Side PRPRPR is opposite QQQ, so it is the hypotenuse and longest side. The angle sum gives

m∠R=180∘−90∘−58∘.m\angle R=180^\circ-90^\circ-58^\circ.m∠R=180∘−90∘−58∘.

Therefore,

m∠R=32∘.m\angle R=32^\circ.m∠R=32∘.

Angle RRR is smallest, so its opposite side PQPQPQ is shortest. Angle PPP is next, so QRQRQR is between the other two:

PQ<QR.PQ<QR.PQ<QR.

Also,

QR<PR.QR<PR.QR<PR.

Check your understanding

A right triangle XYZXYZXYZ is right at YYY. Which side must be longest?

Answer choices

The right angle is at Y, so the side opposite it is XZ.

The other two angles are positive and together total 90 degrees, making each smaller than angle Y. Their opposite sides are therefore shorter than XZ.

The shortest route to a line

For a point outside a line, the perpendicular segment to the line is shorter than every other segment from that point to the line.

Suppose PHPHPH meets line ℓ\ellℓ at a right angle. A segment to another point QQQ on the line forms right triangle PHQPHQPHQ. Its hypotenuse is PQPQPQ, so PQPQPQ is longer than PHPHPH. A perpendicular segment of 888 centimeters therefore beats every slanted segment to the line, even if none of their lengths is known.

The perpendicular is the shortest segment to a line#

Let PPP be outside line ℓ\ellℓ, and let HHH be the foot of its perpendicular. Take any point QQQ on ℓ\ellℓ other than HHH.

Triangle PHQPHQPHQ is a right triangle because HQHQHQ lies on ℓ\ellℓ. Its hypotenuse is PQPQPQ, so

PQ>PH.PQ>PH.PQ>PH.

This holds for every choice of QQQ other than HHH, on either side. The endpoint HHH gives PHPHPH itself, so HHH is the unique closest point on the line.

∎

The distance from a point to a line is the length of that perpendicular segment. If the point already lies on the line, its distance to the line is zero; there is no perpendicular segment of positive length from the point to the line, so no right triangle to use in that case.

Worked example 4 Check a claimed distance

Problem. Point PPP is outside line ℓ\ellℓ, and HHH is its perpendicular foot. Let QQQ be another point on ℓ\ellℓ. The lengths, in centimeters, are

PH=8,PH=8,PH=8,

and

PQ=5x+3.PQ=5x+3.PQ=5x+3.

What restriction on xxx is necessary?

Solution. Triangle PHQPHQPHQ has hypotenuse PQPQPQ. Its length must exceed the perpendicular length:

5x+3>8.5x+3>8.5x+3>8.

Subtract 333, then divide by positive 555:

x>1.x>1.x>1.

At the excluded endpoint x=1x=1x=1, the slanted segment would have the same length as the perpendicular, contradicting the strict comparison.

Check your understanding

Point P is outside a line. PH is perpendicular to it and has length 7. Q is another point on the line. Which claimed length for PQ is impossible?

Answer choices

PQ is the hypotenuse of right triangle PHQ, so it must be longer than PH.

PQ>7.PQ>7.PQ>7.

A length of 6 violates that necessary comparison.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Adjacent instead of opposite. Match an angle to the side that does not touch its vertex.
  • Appearance instead of evidence. Use the givens and proved comparisons, even when a sketch suggests a different order.
  • Two unrelated triangles. The side-angle ordering theorem compares parts of the same triangle.
  • Ignoring equality. Equal sides face equal angles; a strict side comparison requires a strict angle comparison.
  • Distance to a line. A segment reaching the line is not automatically perpendicular or shortest.

Key takeaways

  • Opposite pairs. Within one triangle, larger sides face larger angles, and larger angles face larger sides.
  • Exterior angles. Each is greater than either remote interior angle, since it equals their positive sum.
  • Hypotenuse. The side opposite the right angle is longer than either leg.
  • Point to line. For a point outside a line, the perpendicular segment is uniquely shortest.

A bit of history (optional)

In 1847, Oliver Byrne published an unusual edition of the first six books of Euclid’s Elements. Instead of making readers trace strings of letters from a proof back to a diagram, he put small colored shapes into the proof itself. His introduction even recommended colored chalk for classrooms and colored pencils for private study. The old arguments survived, but their printed appearance changed dramatically.
