Triangle Congruence and SSS

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Join three rigid strips at their ends to make a triangle. Could you change its shape without changing a strip’s length? Compare that with four strips joined into a four-sided frame: the frame can lean while every strip stays the same length. Three side lengths contain more information about a triangle than a glance at its drawing might suggest.

Learning goals

  • Match corresponding vertices, sides, and angles from an ordered triangle congruence statement
  • Decide whether three pairs of side lengths establish the stated triangle congruence
  • Explain how rigid motions justify the SSS criterion, including the reflected case
  • Construct a triangle from three lengths and explain its possible placements

Read the match in order

Two figures are congruent when a sequence of rigid motions carries one exactly onto the other. A slide, turn, or reflection changes its position but preserves lengths and angle measures, as established in Compositions and Congruence.

The ordered statement is

△ABC≅△RST.\begin{aligned} \triangle ABC&\cong\triangle RST. \end{aligned}△ABC​≅△RST.​

It specifies a match: AAA goes to RRR, BBB to SSS, and CCC to TTT. These are corresponding vertices, the vertices paired by the mapping. The order matters even when a drawing is rotated or reflected.

Follow the endpoints to find corresponding sides. For example, BCBCBC pairs with STSTST, not with RTRTRT. Follow the middle letter to match an angle: ∠BAC\angle BAC∠BAC pairs with ∠SRT\angle SRT∠SRT.

Worked example 1 Read an ordered statement

Problem. The given ordered statement is

△ABC≅△RST.\begin{aligned} \triangle ABC&\cong\triangle RST. \end{aligned}△ABC​≅△RST.​

Name the side corresponding to ACACAC and the angle corresponding to ∠BCA\angle BCA∠BCA. The measurements are

ST=9 cm.m∠R=47∘.\begin{aligned} ST&=9\text{ cm}. \\ m\angle R&=47^\circ. \end{aligned}STm∠R​=9 cm.=47∘.​

Find BCBCBC and m∠Am\angle Am∠A.

Solution. The vertex pairs are

A↔RB↔SC↔T.\begin{aligned} A&\leftrightarrow R \\ B&\leftrightarrow S \\ C&\leftrightarrow T. \end{aligned}ABC​↔R↔S↔T.​

Thus ACACAC corresponds to RTRTRT, and ∠BCA\angle BCA∠BCA corresponds to ∠STR\angle STR∠STR.

The length BCBCBC equals its corresponding length STSTST, so

BC=9 cm.\begin{aligned} BC&=9\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}BC​=9 cm.​

Angle AAA corresponds to angle RRR, so

m∠A=47∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle A&=47^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠A​=47∘.​

Check your understanding

If triangle KLM is congruent to triangle VUW in that order, which side corresponds to KM?

Answer choices

K matches V and M matches W, so the endpoints of KM map to the endpoints of VW.

For congruent triangles, all six corresponding parts match: three pairs of sides and three pairs of angles. The matching motions carry each side and angle onto its partner and preserve lengths and angle measures.

The side pairs are:

AB=RS.BC=ST.CA=TR.\begin{aligned} AB&=RS. \\ BC&=ST. \\ CA&=TR. \end{aligned}ABBCCA​=RS.=ST.=TR.​

The angle pairs are:

m∠A=m∠R.m∠B=m∠S.m∠C=m∠T.\begin{aligned} m\angle A&=m\angle R. \\ m\angle B&=m\angle S. \\ m\angle C&=m\angle T. \end{aligned}m∠Am∠Bm∠C​=m∠R.=m∠S.=m∠T.​

Conversely, if all six pairs match in one consistent correspondence, the triangles are congruent. This follows from SSS below, which shows that matching just the three side pairs already suffices. Equal lengths use ===; congruent segments or triangles use ≅\cong≅.

What three sides determine

Three matching side lengths are enough to force an exact triangle match. Imagine one triangle with side lengths 444, 666, and 888 cm and another with the same three lengths. Slide and turn the first so the two 888 cm sides coincide, matching their endpoints. Its third vertex must be 444 cm from one endpoint and 666 cm from the other.

Those distance requirements place the vertex on two circles. There are two possible positions, one on each side of the common base. They are mirror images; reflecting across the base exchanges them. Either position gives an exact match after a rigid motion. The next section justifies why there are no extra positions hidden in this description.

The Side-Side-Side criterion, abbreviated SSS, says: if all three side lengths of one triangle equal their corresponding side lengths in another, the triangles are congruent. Here and throughout, a triangle has three distinct, noncollinear vertices. A straight chain of three segments is not a triangle.

Worked example 2 Check sides and the vertex order

Problem. Triangle JKLJKLJKL has these side lengths in centimeters:

JK=10.KL=14.JL=16.\begin{aligned} JK&=10. \\ KL&=14. \\ JL&=16. \end{aligned}JKKLJL​=10.=14.=16.​

Triangle UVWUVWUVW has these side lengths in centimeters:

UV=14.VW=16.UW=10.\begin{aligned} UV&=14. \\ VW&=16. \\ UW&=10. \end{aligned}UVVWUW​=14.=16.=10.​

Establish a correct ordered congruence statement.

Solution. Because all three side lengths differ, the two lengths meeting at each vertex identify exactly one partner. Vertex JJJ joins the 101010 and 161616 cm sides. In the second triangle, WWW joins those lengths, so JJJ corresponds to WWW.

Vertex KKK joins the 101010 and 141414 cm sides, so it corresponds to UUU. Vertex LLL therefore corresponds to VVV. Check each pair:

JK=WUJK=10KL=UVKL=14JL=WVJL=16\begin{aligned} JK&=WU \\ JK&=10 \\ KL&=UV \\ KL&=14 \\ JL&=WV \\ JL&=16 \end{aligned}JKJKKLKLJLJL​=WU=10=UV=14=WV=16​

The correct order, by SSS, is

△JKL≅△WUV.\begin{aligned} \triangle JKL&\cong\triangle WUV. \end{aligned}△JKL​≅△WUV.​

Worked example 3 Complete the side data

Problem. A triangle has side lengths 111111, 131313, and 171717 cm. A second actual triangle has corresponding sides 111111, 131313, and 2x+52x+52x+5 cm. With these sides corresponding in the stated order, find xxx so that SSS establishes congruence, and check the third side.

Solution. The first two pairs already match. The remaining lengths must be equal:

2x+5=17\begin{aligned} 2x+5&=17 \end{aligned}2x+5​=17​

Subtract 555 and divide by 222, giving

x=6.\begin{aligned} x&=6. \end{aligned}x​=6.​

Substitute to check the third side:

2(6)+5=17 cm.\begin{aligned} 2(6)+5&=17\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}2(6)+5​=17 cm.​

Thus all three pairs match.

Triangles ABC and DEF have these corresponding side lengths:

AB=DE.AB=12.BC=EF.BC=15.AC=DF.AC=19.\begin{aligned} AB&=DE. \\ AB&=12. \\ BC&=EF. \\ BC&=15. \\ AC&=DF. \\ AC&=19. \end{aligned}ABABBCBCACAC​=DE.=12.=EF.=15.=DF.=19.​

Answer choices

The three side pairs respect A to D, B to E, and C to F. SSS establishes congruence without separately measuring angles.

Why the rigid motions finish the job

Matching a base is easy: translate one endpoint to its partner and rotate the base onto its partner. Equal base lengths make the other endpoints coincide too. The remaining issue is the third vertex. We can settle it using the Pythagorean theorem from Pre-Algebra, which says that the squares of a right triangle’s leg lengths add to the square of its hypotenuse. We use that previously learned fact here; its geometric proof will return later in this course.

Three matching sides guarantee triangle congruence#

First use lengths 888, 777, and 999. Place the base endpoints at (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) and (8,0)(8,0)(8,0). Call a possible third vertex (x,y)(x,y)(x,y), with distance 777 from the first endpoint and 999 from the second. Dropping a perpendicular to the horizontal axis gives right triangles, so the prior Pythagorean fact gives

x2+y2=49(x−8)2+y2=81\begin{aligned} x^2+y^2&=49 \\ (x-8)^2+y^2&=81 \end{aligned}x2+y2(x−8)2+y2​=49=81​

The squares also work if the perpendicular foot lies beyond an endpoint: a negative coordinate has the same square as its positive length. If the foot is an endpoint, that horizontal distance is zero and the vertical distance alone gives the corresponding side length, so its squared equation still holds. Subtract the first equation from the second:

64−16x=32\begin{aligned} 64-16x&=32 \end{aligned}64−16x​=32​

Thus

x=2.\begin{aligned} x&=2. \end{aligned}x​=2.​

Substitution in the first equation gives

y2=45.\begin{aligned} y^2&=45. \end{aligned}y2​=45.​

The only possible vertices are

One vertex: (2,45).Other vertex: (2,−45).\begin{aligned} \text{One vertex: }&(2,\sqrt{45}). \\ \text{Other vertex: }&(2,-\sqrt{45}). \end{aligned}One vertex: Other vertex: ​(2,45​).(2,−45​).​

Reflection across the horizontal base exchanges these points and fixes both base endpoints.

For any two actual triangles with matching side lengths, place their common base at (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) and (d,0)(d,0)(d,0), where

d>0.\begin{aligned} d&>0. \end{aligned}d​>0.​

Let the distances from a third vertex (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) to the endpoints be rrr and sss.

The same reasoning gives

x2+y2=r2(x−d)2+y2=s2\begin{aligned} x^2+y^2&=r^2 \\ (x-d)^2+y^2&=s^2 \end{aligned}x2+y2(x−d)2+y2​=r2=s2​

Subtracting fixes the horizontal coordinate:

d2−2dx=s2−r2x=d2+r2−s22d\begin{aligned} d^2-2dx&=s^2-r^2 \\ x&=\frac{d^2+r^2-s^2}{2d} \end{aligned}d2−2dxx​=s2−r2=2dd2+r2−s2​​

The first equation then fixes y2y^2y2. Because these are noncollinear triangles,

y2>0.\begin{aligned} y^2&>0. \end{aligned}y2​>0.​

The two possible heights have opposite signs and equal size. A reflection across the base changes one to the other. No part of this reasoning depended on choosing 888, 777, or 999.

After the base-matching translation and rotation, either the third vertices already coincide or that reflection makes them coincide. A rigid motion carries each segment onto the segment joining its image endpoints, so all three sides coincide. The whole triangles match.

∎

This argument uses the accepted rigid-motion preservation properties, coordinates, and the Pythagorean theorem from Pre-Algebra. The circle picture locates candidates; the argument proves that they differ at most by reflection.

Check your understanding

After corresponding bases coincide, two third vertices are on opposite sides of the base with the same horizontal position and equal heights in size. Which motion completes the match while leaving the base fixed?

Answer choices

The reflection fixes every point on the base and sends a point to the equal-height position on the other side. A stretch is not rigid, and the other motions generally move the matched base.

Construct a triangle from its sides

A compass can locate a point by distance without measuring an angle. To construct △PQR\triangle PQR△PQR, use these given lengths:

PQ=9 cm.PR=6 cm.QR=7 cm.\begin{aligned} PQ&=9\text{ cm}. \\ PR&=6\text{ cm}. \\ QR&=7\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}PQPRQR​=9 cm.=6 cm.=7 cm.​
  1. Draw PQPQPQ of length 999 cm.
  2. Draw an arc centered at PPP with radius 666 cm.
  3. Draw an arc centered at QQQ with radius 777 cm so it crosses the first arc.
  4. Choose an intersection as RRR and join PRPRPR and QRQRQR.

Every point on the first arc is 666 cm from PPP, and every point on the second is 777 cm from QQQ. Their intersection satisfies both requirements. Choosing the other intersection gives a reflected, congruent triangle by SSS, not a new triangle shape.

Not every three positive lengths produce a triangle. The arcs might miss or merely touch. For example, lengths 222, 333, and 555 cm place the third vertex on the line through the base when the arcs touch, so there is no noncollinear triangle. We will develop a complete test for possible side lengths in the next chapter.

Check your understanding

To construct triangle DEF, the given lengths are

DE=10 cm.DF=8 cm.EF=9 cm.\begin{aligned} DE&=10\text{ cm}. \\ DF&=8\text{ cm}. \\ EF&=9\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}DEDFEF​=10 cm.=8 cm.=9 cm.​

Which arcs locate F after DE is drawn?

Answer choices

F must be 8 cm from D and 9 cm from E. The center of each arc is the endpoint from which that distance is specified.

Why a four-sided frame can move

SSS is a statement about triangles. Four fixed side lengths do not generally determine a four-sided figure. A square made from four equal hinged strips can lean into a slanted frame without changing any strip. Its angles change, so the new frame is not congruent to the square.

Adding a diagonal divides the frame into triangles. Fixing that extra length can stop this leaning, because the pieces must now satisfy three side lengths each. This explains why a diagonal brace can make a frame rigid.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Vertex order: Pair letters by their positions in the statement, not by left and right on the page.
  • Side data: Two matching side pairs leave the third unchecked. SSS requires all three pairs in one consistent correspondence.
  • Mirror images: A reflection is a rigid motion. Turning a triangle over does not stop it being congruent.
  • Degenerate construction: Arcs that only touch on the line through the base do not produce a triangle.

Key takeaways

  • An ordered congruence statement specifies the matching vertices, sides, and angles.
  • Congruent triangles have all six corresponding parts equal in length or measure.
  • Three corresponding side lengths establish triangle congruence by SSS.
  • A base and two compass arcs construct the possible placements, which differ at most by reflection.
  • Fixed side lengths do not generally determine a four-sided figure.

A bit of history (optional)

In Book I, Proposition 8 of Euclid’s Elements, two triangles are compared by placing one on the other. The bases are made to coincide first. The argument then asks whether their other sides could fail to fit, and refers back to an earlier result ruling out two different apexes on the same side of a fixed base with the same distances from its endpoints.

This is often called superposition, placing one figure over another to compare them. Euclid’s text does not give a list of slide, turn, and reflection instructions. His short fitting argument and the modern rigid-motion approach describe the match in different ways.

You can read the translated proposition and its diagram in Euclid’s Elements, Book I, Proposition 8.
