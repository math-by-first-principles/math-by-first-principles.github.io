  1. Problem 1 Translate an ordered match

    Given the ordered statement:

    △ABC≅△WUV.\begin{aligned}\triangle ABC&\cong\triangle WUV.\end{aligned}△ABC​≅△WUV.​

    List the three corresponding side pairs and the three corresponding angle pairs. Use the ordered statement rather than the triangles’ positions in the figure.

    Text description of this figure

    Congruent triangles ABC and WUV have different orientations. No matching side or angle pair is highlighted.

    1. Hint 1

      Match vertices by their position in the two names.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the two endpoint matches for a side, and the vertex match for an angle.

    Answer

    AB↔WU.BC↔UV.AC↔WV.∠A↔∠W.∠B↔∠U.∠C↔∠V.\begin{aligned}AB&\leftrightarrow WU.\\BC&\leftrightarrow UV.\\AC&\leftrightarrow WV.\\\angle A&\leftrightarrow\angle W.\\\angle B&\leftrightarrow\angle U.\\\angle C&\leftrightarrow\angle V.\end{aligned}ABBCAC∠A∠B∠C​↔WU.↔UV.↔WV.↔∠W.↔∠U.↔∠V.​

    Full solution

    The ordered vertex match is

    A↔W.B↔U.C↔V.\begin{aligned}A&\leftrightarrow W.\\B&\leftrightarrow U.\\C&\leftrightarrow V.\end{aligned}ABC​↔W.↔U.↔V.​

    Applying it to the endpoints gives

    AB↔WU.BC↔UV.AC↔WV.\begin{aligned}AB&\leftrightarrow WU.\\BC&\leftrightarrow UV.\\AC&\leftrightarrow WV.\end{aligned}ABBCAC​↔WU.↔UV.↔WV.​

    Applying it to each angle vertex gives

    ∠A↔∠W.∠B↔∠U.∠C↔∠V.\begin{aligned}\angle A&\leftrightarrow\angle W.\\\angle B&\leftrightarrow\angle U.\\\angle C&\leftrightarrow\angle V.\end{aligned}∠A∠B∠C​↔∠W.↔∠U.↔∠V.​

    Answer

    AB↔WU.BC↔UV.AC↔WV.∠A↔∠W.∠B↔∠U.∠C↔∠V.\begin{aligned}AB&\leftrightarrow WU.\\BC&\leftrightarrow UV.\\AC&\leftrightarrow WV.\\\angle A&\leftrightarrow\angle W.\\\angle B&\leftrightarrow\angle U.\\\angle C&\leftrightarrow\angle V.\end{aligned}ABBCAC∠A∠B∠C​↔WU.↔UV.↔WV.↔∠W.↔∠U.↔∠V.​

    Key idea

    One vertex correspondence determines all six corresponding parts.

  3. Problem 3 Recover the correct vertex order

    Two scalene triangles have these side lengths in centimeters:

    DE=6.EF=11.FD=14.\begin{aligned}DE&=6.\\EF&=11.\\FD&=14.\end{aligned}DEEFFD​=6.=11.=14.​ LM=14.MN=6.NL=11.\begin{aligned}LM&=14.\\MN&=6.\\NL&=11.\end{aligned}LMMNNL​=14.=6.=11.​

    Write an ordered congruence statement beginning with △DEF\triangle DEF△DEF, and justify its vertex order.

    Text description of this figure

    The first triangle has DE 6, EF 11, and FD 14 centimeters. The second has LM 14, MN 6, and NL 11 centimeters. Only given length labels and vertex names are shown.

    1. Hint 1

      At each vertex, identify the two side lengths that meet there.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the vertex of LMN where the 6 and 14 cm sides meet.

    Answer

    The triangles are congruent by SSS:

    △DEF≅△MNL.\begin{aligned}\triangle DEF&\cong\triangle MNL.\end{aligned}△DEF​≅△MNL.​

    At DDD, the 666 and 141414 cm sides meet.

    They meet at MMM in the second triangle, so DDD matches MMM.

    At EEE, the 666 and 111111 cm sides meet.

    They meet at NNN, so EEE matches NNN and FFF matches LLL.

    The side pairs in that order are

    DE=MN.DE=6.EF=NL.EF=11.FD=LM.FD=14.\begin{aligned}DE&=MN.\\DE&=6.\\EF&=NL.\\EF&=11.\\FD&=LM.\\FD&=14.\end{aligned}DEDEEFEFFDFD​=MN.=6.=NL.=11.=LM.=14.​

    All three side pairs match.

    SSS establishes the ordered congruence:

    △DEF≅△MNL.\begin{aligned}\triangle DEF&\cong\triangle MNL.\end{aligned}△DEF​≅△MNL.​

    Answer

    The triangles are congruent by SSS:

    △DEF≅△MNL.\begin{aligned}\triangle DEF&\cong\triangle MNL.\end{aligned}△DEF​≅△MNL.​

    When side lengths are distinct, the two lengths meeting at a vertex identify its partner.

  4. Problem 4 Find a matching length and perimeter

  5. Problem 5 Separate six-part information from sufficient information

    Two actual triangles have three corresponding side pairs equal in one vertex order. Must you also measure their angle pairs to establish congruence? Explain what SSS establishes and what rigid-motion preservation then tells you about the angles.

    1. Hint 1

      Distinguish the information sufficient to establish a match from the consequences of that match.

    2. Hint 2

      Recall what a sequence of rigid motions preserves.

    Answer

    No; three corresponding side matches establish congruence by SSS, and rigid motions preserve the three corresponding angle measures.

    Full solution

    SSS says that the three side pairs alone establish congruence.

    There is no need for a separate angle measurement to complete that criterion.

    Congruence means a sequence of rigid motions maps the entire first triangle onto the second.

    Those motions preserve angles, so all three corresponding angle measures agree.

    The complete six-part description is consistent with the shorter sufficient test; it is not a demand for six independent measurements.

    Answer

    No; three corresponding side matches establish congruence by SSS, and rigid motions preserve the three corresponding angle measures.

    Key idea

    A sufficient test can establish more matching parts than the ones it initially checks.

  6. Problem 6 Apply SSS to two sets of measurements

    Case A. One triangle has sides 151515, 181818, and 232323 cm; another has sides 232323, 151515, and 181818 cm. Decide whether the given data establish congruence.

    Case B. One triangle has sides 151515, 181818, and 232323 cm; another has sides 151515, 181818, and 242424 cm. Decide whether they are congruent.

    State a reason in each case.

    1. Hint 1

      A different order in a side-length list does not change the triangle.

    2. Hint 2

      Match equal lengths in Case A. In Case B, ask whether any rigid motion could preserve all distances.

    Answer

    Case A: congruent by SSS. Case B: not congruent, because the three side-length lists differ.

    Full solution

    Case A.

    Each of the three lengths has an equal partner in the other triangle.

    The pairs

    15=15.18=18.23=23.\begin{aligned}15&=15.\\18&=18.\\23&=23.\end{aligned}151823​=15.=18.=23.​

    These three matches establish congruence by SSS.

    Case B.

    A side of length 232323 cm has no equally long side in the other triangle.

    Since a rigid motion preserves every side length, it cannot map one triangle onto the other.

    Answer

    Case A: congruent by SSS. Case B: not congruent, because the three side-length lists differ.

    Key idea

    Compare all three lengths, independently of how a list or drawing orders them.

  7. Problem 7 Compare a triangle and a four-strip frame

    A square frame has four strips of length 101010 cm, joined by hinges. The same strips are moved into a slanted four-sided frame with a 60∘60^\circ60∘ interior angle. Decide whether these two frames are congruent. Then explain whether three fixed strip lengths could produce two noncongruent triangles.

    Text description of this figure

    Both frames use four strips of length 10 centimeters. The blue solid frame is square. The red dashed slanted frame has a given 60-degree interior angle. The diagram makes no claim about triangle rigidity.

    1. Hint 1

      An exact rigid match preserves angles as well as lengths.

    2. Hint 2

      The statement about triangles has a criterion that does not apply to four-sided frames.

    Answer

    The frames are not congruent; one has a 60∘60^\circ60∘ angle while all square angles are 90∘90^\circ90∘. Three fixed lengths cannot give two noncongruent triangles.

    Full solution

    Every angle of the square is 90∘90^\circ90∘.

    The slanted frame has an angle of 60∘60^\circ60∘, so a rigid motion cannot match their angles.

    The four side lengths stayed equal, but that was insufficient to preserve the whole shape.

    For two actual triangles, three equal corresponding side lengths establish congruence by SSS.

    The freedom of the four-sided frame therefore does not occur for a triangle.

    Answer

    The frames are not congruent; one has a 60∘60^\circ60∘ angle while all square angles are 90∘90^\circ90∘. Three fixed lengths cannot give two noncongruent triangles.

    Key idea

    SSS belongs to triangles; matching a larger figure’s side lengths need not fix its angles.

  8. Problem 8 Interpret three circle outcomes

    Each case uses a base with distinct endpoints and two complete circles centered at those endpoints. In each case the base length and two positive radii are fixed; different cases may use different lengths.

    Case A. The circles meet at two points off the base line. How many different triangle shapes, up to congruence, do the two choices produce?

    Case B. The circles touch at one point on the base line. Does choosing that point produce a triangle?

    Case C. The circles have no common point. Can a point on either circle alone supply a third vertex satisfying both prescribed distances?

    Text description of this figure

    Case A shows two off-line circle intersections. Case B shows circles touching on their common base line. Case C shows two circles with no common point. No triangle edges, shape counts, or existence verdicts are added.

    1. Hint 1

      A third vertex must satisfy both distance requirements at once.

    2. Hint 2

      For two off-line intersections compare their triangles by SSS; for the other cases check noncollinearity or simultaneous distances.

    Answer

    Case A: one shape up to congruence. Case B: no triangle. Case C: no third vertex meets both distances.

    Full solution

    Case A.

    The circles are centered at the base endpoints, so each intersection has the same two prescribed distances from those endpoints.

    The base is common.

    Thus all three side pairs match, and SSS gives one shape up to congruence.

    Case B.

    The third point lies on the line through the two base endpoints.

    A triangle requires noncollinear vertices, so this is not a triangle.

    Case C.

    Because the complete circles have no common point, no point has both prescribed distances.

    A point on only one circle satisfies only that circle's distance requirement.

    Answer

    Case A: one shape up to congruence. Case B: no triangle. Case C: no third vertex meets both distances.

    Key idea

    Existence, noncollinearity, and congruence are separate questions in a construction.

  9. Problem 9 Use a shared diagonal

    A four-sided figure has vertices P,Q,R,SP,Q,R,SP,Q,R,S in that order. The given pairs and lengths are

    Full solution

    The two given pairs are

    PQ=RS.PQ=12 cm.QR=SP.QR=20 cm.\begin{aligned}PQ&=RS.\\PQ&=12\text{ cm}.\\QR&=SP.\\QR&=20\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}PQPQQRQR​=RS.=12 cm.=SP.=20 cm.​

    The diagonal has the same length in either direction:

    PR=RP.\begin{aligned}PR&=RP.\end{aligned}PR​=RP.​

    These side pairs match PPP with RRR, QQQ with SSS, and RRR with PPP.

    Thus, by SSS,

    △PQR≅△RSP.\begin{aligned}\triangle PQR&\cong\triangle RSP.\end{aligned}△PQR​≅△RSP.​

    Answer

    △PQR≅△RSP.PQ=RS.QR=SP.PR=RP.\begin{aligned}\triangle PQR&\cong\triangle RSP.\\PQ&=RS.\\QR&=SP.\\PR&=RP.\end{aligned}△PQRPQQRPR​≅△RSP.=RS.=SP.=RP.​

    A shared segment can supply the third side pair without a new measurement.

  10. Problem 10 Construct and account for both placements

    Using a ruler, compass, and straightedge on paper, construct △UVW\triangle UVW△UVW with these lengths:

    UV=11 cm.UW=8 cm.VW=10 cm.\begin{aligned}UV&=11\text{ cm}.\\UW&=8\text{ cm}.\\VW&=10\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}UVUWVW​=11 cm.=8 cm.=10 cm.​

    Keep the relevant arcs.

    Construct the possible third vertex on each side of the same base. Describe a rigid motion mapping one resulting triangle onto the other while keeping UUU and VVV fixed. Explain why there is no third, noncongruent triangle with these three side lengths.

    Text description of this figure

    Base UV is drawn with length 11 centimeters. Required distances UW 8 and VW 10 centimeters are listed separately. No arcs or third-vertex positions are supplied.

    1. Hint 1

      Draw the base first, then use one distance arc from each endpoint.

    2. Hint 2

      The SSS justification leaves at most one possible third vertex on either side of a fixed base.

    3. Hint 3

      Which rigid motion can fix every point of a line while exchanging its two sides?

    Answer

    Base UV of 11 cm; arcs of radius 8 cm centered at U and radius 10 cm centered at V. The two intersections give reflected, congruent triangles. There is no third noncongruent shape.

    Full solution

    Draw the base:

    UV=11 cm.\begin{aligned}UV&=11\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}UV​=11 cm.​

    With center UUU, draw an arc of radius 888 cm on each side of the base.

    With center VVV, draw arcs of radius 101010 cm that cross them.

    Join each crossing to UUU and VVV.

    The circle definition guarantees

    UW=8 cm.VW=10 cm.\begin{aligned}UW&=8\text{ cm}.\\VW&=10\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}UWVW​=8 cm.=10 cm.​

    These lengths hold for either crossing, and the base is unchanged.

    The SSS justification fixes a unique horizontal position and a unique height in size after a base is placed.

    The two signs of that height are the two reflected positions.

    Reflection across the line UVUVUV fixes UUU and VVV and exchanges the two third vertices, so it maps one whole triangle onto the other.

    Every actual triangle with those side lengths can have its 111111 cm side aligned to UVUVUV with the vertex shared by its 111111 cm and 888 cm sides at UUU.

    The other endpoint then matches VVV, where the 101010 cm side joins the base.

    Its third vertex must be one of the two positions satisfying those assigned distances, so no additional noncongruent shape is possible.

    Answer

    Base UV of 11 cm; arcs of radius 8 cm centered at U and radius 10 cm centered at V. The two intersections give reflected, congruent triangles. There is no third noncongruent shape.

    Key idea

    Three side lengths determine a triangle up to rigid motion, even when its construction has two placements.
