SAS and ASA Congruence

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Two strips joined by a loose hinge can open into different shapes. Lock the hinge at one angle, and the free ends have fixed positions relative to each other. Could those two lengths and that angle replace checking all three sides of a triangle?

Learning goals

  • Identify the included angle between two sides and the included side between two angles
  • Justify SAS and ASA congruence by aligning triangles with rigid motions
  • Choose sufficient matching measurements and write the corresponding triangle congruence statement
  • Construct triangles from SAS or ASA data and explain the construction conditions

Which part lies between the others?

In the previous lesson, SSS used three matching side lengths to establish congruence. Congruence still means that a sequence of rigid motions maps the whole first triangle onto the second.

In triangle ABCABCABC, sides ABABAB and ACACAC meet at AAA. The angle they enclose, ∠BAC\angle BAC∠BAC, is their included angle. The angle at BBB touches ABABAB, but it is not between ABABAB and ACACAC.

For angles at AAA and BBB, side ABABAB joins their vertices. It is their included side. Side ACACAC touches only one of those two vertices.

Check your understanding

Which angle is included between sides LMLMLM and MNMNMN?

Answer choices

The two sides meet at MMM, so the included angle has MMM as its middle letter: ∠LMN\angle LMN∠LMN.

Two sides and the angle they enclose

Fix a side ABABAB of length 888 cm. At AAA, open a ray at 55∘55^\circ55∘ to ABABAB, and place CCC exactly 111111 cm along that ray. Once the side of line ABABAB is chosen, there is only one such point CCC: moving it along the ray changes its distance from AAA.

Now build DEFDEFDEF with these measurements:

DE=8 cmDF=11 cm∠EDF=55∘\begin{aligned} DE&=8\text{ cm} \\ DF&=11\text{ cm} \\ \angle EDF&=55^\circ \end{aligned}DEDF∠EDF​=8 cm=11 cm=55∘​

Slide and turn the first triangle until AAA lands on DDD and BBB on EEE. If the two third vertices lie on opposite sides of the aligned base, reflect across that base. The 55∘55^\circ55∘ angles now put CCC and FFF on the same ray from DDD, and the 111111 cm lengths put them at the same point. The third sides match because both now join the same endpoints.

The side-angle-side criterion (SAS) says that two triangles are congruent if two pairs of corresponding sides and the pair of included angles are congruent. The angle must be between the two known sides in each triangle.

Why SAS gives a rigid-motion match#

The given triangles satisfy:

AB=DEAC=DF∠BAC≅∠EDF\begin{aligned} AB&=DE \\ AC&=DF \\ \angle BAC&\cong\angle EDF \end{aligned}ABAC∠BAC​=DE=DF≅∠EDF​

Translate AAA to DDD and rotate about DDD to align ray ABABAB with ray DEDEDE. Length preservation places BBB on EEE.

Call the image of CCC point C′C'C′. If necessary, reflect across line DEDEDE so that C′C'C′ and FFF are in the same half-plane. This fixes DDD and EEE and preserves the angle at DDD. Equal angles opening from the same ray into that half-plane give the same second ray, by the angle-measure foundation accepted in Proving Lines Parallel or Perpendicular, lesson 2.3. Thus C′C'C′ lies on ray DFDFDF. Distance preservation gives DC′=DFDC'=DFDC′=DF.

On a ray, a positive distance from its endpoint selects one point, so C′=FC'=FC′=F.

These are rigid motions, using the preservation properties accepted in chapter 3. The argument used the 888 cm, 111111 cm, and 55∘55^\circ55∘ example’s steps, without depending on those particular measurements. For a triangle the lengths are positive and the included angle is strictly between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘; a flat configuration is not a triangle.

∎

Worked example 1 Check the corner before naming the criterion

Problem. In triangles JKLJKLJKL and STUSTUSTU,

JK=6 cmST=6 cmKL=10 cmTU=10 cm∠JKL≅∠STU\begin{aligned} JK&=6\text{ cm} \\ ST&=6\text{ cm} \\ KL&=10\text{ cm} \\ TU&=10\text{ cm} \\ \angle JKL&\cong\angle STU \end{aligned}JKSTKLTU∠JKL​=6 cm=6 cm=10 cm=10 cm≅∠STU​

Establish congruence and write the matching order.

Solution. The known sides meet at KKK in the first triangle and TTT in the second. Those are the vertices of the known angles, so the angles are included.

The 666 cm sides match JJJ with SSS, and the 101010 cm sides match LLL with UUU. Together with K↔TK\leftrightarrow TK↔T, this gives the ordered match by SAS:

△JKL≅△STU.\triangle JKL\cong\triangle STU.△JKL≅△STU.

AB=PQBC=QR\begin{aligned} AB&=PQ \\ BC&=QR \end{aligned}ABBC​=PQ=QR​

Which additional equality directly completes SAS for ABCABCABC and PQRPQRPQR in that order?

Answer choices

The known sides meet at BBB and QQQ. Their included angles are ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC and ∠PQR\angle PQR∠PQR.

Two angles and the side joining them

Draw UV=9UV=9UV=9 cm.

Above the segment, a ray from UUU makes 43∘43^\circ43∘ with UVUVUV, and a ray from VVV makes 71∘71^\circ71∘ with VUVUVU. Their intersection gives the third vertex WWW. The base fixes where the two rays start, and the angles fix their directions. The two distinct lines carrying the rays can meet at only one point.

If another triangle has that same base length and those same endpoint angles, align its base with UVUVUV, putting its 43∘43^\circ43∘ vertex on UUU and its 71∘71^\circ71∘ vertex on VVV. Reflect in the base if needed so both third vertices are above it. Each corresponding ray then lies on the same ray as its partner. Their intersection is the same point, so the whole triangles coincide.

The angle-side-angle criterion (ASA) says that two triangles are congruent if two pairs of corresponding angles and the pair of included sides are congruent. Here UVUVUV is included because it joins the vertices of the 43∘43^\circ43∘ and 71∘71^\circ71∘ angles.

Why ASA gives a rigid-motion match#

Suppose

∠A≅∠DAB=DE∠B≅∠E\begin{aligned} \angle A&\cong\angle D \\ AB&=DE \\ \angle B&\cong\angle E \end{aligned}∠AAB∠B​≅∠D=DE≅∠E​

Translate and rotate triangle ABCABCABC to put AAA on DDD and BBB on EEE. If necessary, reflect across DEDEDE to put its third vertex C′C'C′ in the same half-plane as FFF.

Angle preservation puts ray DC′DC'DC′ on ray DFDFDF and ray EC′EC'EC′ on ray EFEFEF. The lines containing these rays are distinct, since the given figures are triangles. If they had two different intersection points, two distinct lines would pass through those same two points, contrary to the unique line through two distinct points. Thus C′C'C′ is FFF.

As with the 999 cm base and the 43∘43^\circ43∘ and 71∘71^\circ71∘ rays, the fixed base and its two endpoint directions leave one intersection. The reasoning does not depend on those numbers.

∎

Check your understanding

After aligning a base and putting the third vertices in the same half-plane, what fixes the third vertex in each justification?

Answer choices

For SAS, the included angle fixes a ray and the remaining side length fixes one point along it. For ASA, the two endpoint angles fix rays whose distinct lines can meet at only one point. These are the reasons the third vertices coincide.

Worked example 2 Match the side to its two endpoint angles

Problem. In triangles MNPMNPMNP and XYZXYZXYZ,

∠M≅∠Y∠P≅∠XMP=12 cmYX=12 cm\begin{aligned} \angle M&\cong\angle Y \\ \angle P&\cong\angle X \\ MP&=12\text{ cm} \\ YX&=12\text{ cm} \end{aligned}∠M∠PMPYX​≅∠Y≅∠X=12 cm=12 cm​

Establish congruence and write the first triangle in the order MNPMNPMNP.

Solution. Side MPMPMP lies between the known angles at MMM and PPP; side YXYXYX lies between the matching angles at YYY and XXX. ASA applies.

The full correspondence is:

M↔YP↔XN↔Z\begin{aligned} M&\leftrightarrow Y \\ P&\leftrightarrow X \\ N&\leftrightarrow Z \end{aligned}MPN​↔Y↔X↔Z​

ASA gives the ordered statement:

△MNP≅△YZX.\triangle MNP\cong\triangle YZX.△MNP≅△YZX.

Check your understanding

In triangles HJKHJKHJK and RTSRTSRTS, angles at HHH and JJJ match angles at RRR and TTT, respectively. Which side equality completes ASA for these angle pairs?

Answer choices

The included sides join the named angle vertices: HJHJHJ and RTRTRT. The other choices do not supply that included-side pair.

Build the triangle from its data

A congruence criterion also explains why a construction fixes a triangle’s shape and size. Copying a length with a compass and copying a supplied angle are the procedures from chapter 1. The angle-copy procedure’s justification comes later in this chapter; here we use the copied angle as a specified input, just as we use a given angle in a proof.

Worked example 3 Construct from SAS data

Problem. You are given segments of lengths 555 cm and 777 cm and an angle template measuring 64∘64^\circ64∘. Construct a triangle with those two sides enclosing that angle. Explain why any successful construction has the same shape and size.

Solution. Draw ABABAB equal to the 777 cm segment.

At AAA, copy the supplied angle, using ray ABABAB as its first side. Choose either side of line ABABAB for the new ray.

Set the compass to the 555 cm segment. Mark CCC on the new ray at that distance from AAA, then join BBB to CCC.

This gives

AB=7 cmAC=5 cm∠BAC=64∘\begin{aligned} AB&=7\text{ cm} \\ AC&=5\text{ cm} \\ \angle BAC&=64^\circ \end{aligned}ABAC∠BAC​=7 cm=5 cm=64∘​

Any other successful construction has the same two sides and included angle, so SAS establishes congruence. Choosing the other side of the base makes a reflected copy.

For SAS construction, both lengths must be positive and the included angle must lie strictly between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘. These conditions make two rays that do not lie along one line. Positive lengths on them give three noncollinear vertices.

Worked example 4 Construct from ASA data

Problem. You are given a 101010 cm segment and angle templates measuring 46∘46^\circ46∘ and 78∘78^\circ78∘. Construct a triangle whose known side joins those angles. Explain why the construction works and find the third angle.

Solution. Draw DE=10DE=10DE=10 cm.

At DDD, copy the 46∘46^\circ46∘ angle with one side along ray DEDEDE. At EEE, copy the 78∘78^\circ78∘ angle with one side along ray EDEDED. Draw both new rays into the same half-plane bounded by line DEDEDE.

Their angles toward that half-plane total

46∘+78∘=124∘.46^\circ+78^\circ=124^\circ.46∘+78∘=124∘.

Because this is less than 180∘180^\circ180∘, the rays converge on that side, by the parallel-postulate angle form from chapter 2. Name their intersection FFF. Any other construction with the same data is congruent by ASA.

The triangle angle sum gives

∠F=180∘−124∘∠F=56∘\begin{aligned} \angle F&=180^\circ-124^\circ \\ \angle F&=56^\circ \end{aligned}∠F∠F​=180∘−124∘=56∘​

In general, ASA construction needs a positive base length and two positive interior angles whose sum is less than 180∘180^\circ180∘. For angles individually below 180∘180^\circ180∘, a sum of exactly 180∘180^\circ180∘ makes the rays parallel; a larger sum does not produce an intersection in the chosen half-plane.

Check your understanding

Which data can construct a nonflat triangle with the given side between the two angles?

Answer choices

The side length and both angles must be positive, and the angles must total less than 180 degrees.

Choose the information before the criterion

Start with the proposed correspondence. Mark only what is given or already justified, then look for a complete pattern:

CriterionMatching informationPlacement check
SSSThree side pairsEach pair follows the same vertex correspondence
SASTwo side pairs and an angle pairThe angles are between the known sides
ASATwo angle pairs and a side pairThe sides join the known angle vertices

An angle drawn close to a marked side is not necessarily the included angle. Trace both sides from their shared vertex. Likewise, trace the segment joining two known angle vertices before claiming ASA. A missing mark is missing information, even when the sketch looks convincing.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • An angle in the wrong position. SAS needs the angle between both known sides, not just one touching a known side.
  • A side in the wrong position. For ASA, use the segment joining the two known angle vertices.
  • A mismatched order. Write the congruence statement from the established correspondence, not the drawing’s left-to-right order.
  • Construction rays on different sides. ASA’s endpoint rays must point into the same half-plane to enclose the intended triangle.
  • Invalid data. Zero lengths, SAS angles of 0∘0^\circ0∘ or 180∘180^\circ180∘, or ASA angles totaling at least 180∘180^\circ180∘ do not construct a triangle.

Key takeaways

  • SAS: two matching side pairs and their included angle establish congruence.
  • ASA: two matching angle pairs and their included side establish congruence.
  • Align a known side, then use the remaining data to fix the third vertex.
  • A construction on the other side of the base can be a reflected congruent copy.

A bit of history (optional)

In Book I, Proposition 4 of Euclid’s Elements, the argument for what we call SAS asks the reader to imagine placing one triangle onto another. First one vertex is placed on its partner, then a side is laid along the matching side. The equal lengths and the angle between them are used to bring the other vertices together.

That imagined placing is usually called superposition. It created a puzzle for later readers: what exactly allows a figure to be moved in a geometry built from statements about points and lines? David Joyce’s edition and commentary on Proposition 4 describes the ambiguity, including the extra difficulty of a mirror-image triangle.
