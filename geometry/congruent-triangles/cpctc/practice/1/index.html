Using Congruence in Proofs: Practice

Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    What does CPCTC stand for?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    CPCTC describes corresponding parts after triangle congruence is known.

    For example, from

    △ABC≅△DEF\begin{aligned}\triangle ABC&\cong\triangle DEF\end{aligned}△ABC​≅△DEF​

    it gives

    AB=DE.\begin{aligned}AB&=DE.\end{aligned}AB​=DE.​
  2. 2

    Which proof order correctly uses CPCTC?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    A criterion establishes the whole-triangle match first, for example

    △ABC≅△DEF.\begin{aligned}\triangle ABC&\cong\triangle DEF.\end{aligned}△ABC​≅△DEF.​

    CPCTC then supplies the matching sides or angles needed for the goal.

  3. 3

    A proof establishes

    △ABC≅△RQP.\begin{aligned}\triangle ABC&\cong\triangle RQP\end{aligned}.△ABC​≅△RQP​.

    Which angle corresponds to ∠BCA\angle BCA∠BCA?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Match BBB to QQQ, CCC to PPP, and AAA to RRR.

    The three-letter angle name maps as

    BCA↔QPR.\begin{aligned}BCA&\leftrightarrow QPR.\end{aligned}BCA​↔QPR.​
  4. 4

    Two triangles share segment LM. What justifies LM=LMLM=LMLM=LM as part of the congruence evidence?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The same length equals itself:

    LM=LM.\begin{aligned}LM&=LM.\end{aligned}LM​=LM.​

    This reflexive equality is available before either triangle has been proved congruent to the other.

  5. 5

    Given

    AB=DEAC=DF∠BAC≅∠EDF\begin{aligned}AB&=DE \\ AC&=DF \\ \angle BAC&\cong\angle EDF\end{aligned}ABAC∠BAC​=DE=DF≅∠EDF​

    Which criterion establishes the following congruence?

    △ABC≅△DEF.\begin{aligned}\triangle ABC&\cong\triangle DEF\end{aligned}.△ABC​≅△DEF​.
    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Each angle lies between the two named sides.

    The correspondence is

    ABC↔DEF,\begin{aligned}ABC&\leftrightarrow DEF,\end{aligned}ABC​↔DEF,​

    so the two side pairs and included angle establish SAS.

  6. 6

    The goal is AB=DEAB=DEAB=DE. Which use of that equality would be circular?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The desired equality cannot supply its own justification.

    A valid argument first establishes triangle congruence from other known data. CPCTC then gives the target equality:

    AB=DE.\begin{aligned}AB&=DE.\end{aligned}AB​=DE.​
  7. 7

    What is an auxiliary segment in a proof?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    An auxiliary segment can create useful triangles, such as a diagonal PRPRPR.

    It supplies a shared part

    PR=PR,\begin{aligned}PR&=PR,\end{aligned}PR​=PR,​

    but other equality claims still need reasons.

  8. 8

    Two angles share vertex A. What extra information makes them literally the same opening?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    An angle is determined by its two rays, not just its vertex.

    If ray ABABAB is ray ADADAD and ray ACACAC is ray AEAEAE, then

    ∠BAC=∠DAE.\begin{aligned}\angle BAC&=\angle DAE.\end{aligned}∠BAC​=∠DAE.​
  9. 9

    In an angle-copy construction, two radius pairs and the transferred chord pair are equal. Which criterion matches the source and target triangles?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The compass settings establish three side pairs, such as

    OA=PD,OB=PE,AB=DE.\begin{aligned}OA&=PD, \\ OB&=PE, \\ AB&=DE.\end{aligned}OAOBAB​=PD,=PE,=DE.​

    SSS applies without assuming the target angle already equals the source angle.

  10. 10

    After SSS establishes the source and target angle-copy triangles are congruent, what justifies equality of the copied angles?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The source and target angles are corresponding parts of the established congruent triangles.

    In the order

    OAB↔PDE\begin{aligned}OAB&\leftrightarrow PDE\end{aligned}OAB​↔PDE​

    the conclusion is

    ∠AOB≅∠DPE.\begin{aligned}\angle AOB&\cong\angle DPE.\end{aligned}∠AOB​≅∠DPE.​
  11. 11

    Two triangles share a baseline and have matching angles at both endpoints of that baseline. Which criterion matches them?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The shared baseline lies between the two known angle vertices.

    It is the included side, with equality

    BC=BC.\begin{aligned}BC&=BC.\end{aligned}BC​=BC.​

    The angle-side-angle data give ASA.

  12. 12

    Which facts are needed to use HL in a congruence proof?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Hypotenuse-Leg requires two right triangles.

    The side opposite each right angle is its hypotenuse, and the required lengths match, for example

    AC=DF,AB=DE.\begin{aligned}AC&=DF, \\ AB&=DE.\end{aligned}ACAB​=DF,=DE.​
