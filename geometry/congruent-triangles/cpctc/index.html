Using Congruence in Proofs

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

The side or angle you want to compare may not be one of the measurements you are given. Instead, it may belong to a triangle that can be matched exactly with another triangle. Establishing that larger match gives you the smaller comparison, provided you keep track of which parts correspond.

Learning goals

  • Plan congruence proofs with suitable triangles, a criterion, and an auxiliary segment when needed
  • Separate overlapping triangles and justify a shared side or shared angle
  • Pass a proved corresponding-part equality into a second triangle congruence
  • Use a congruent accessible triangle to determine an inaccessible distance
  • Justify the angle-copy construction using SSS followed by corresponding angles

Establish the triangles before using their parts

An exact triangle match lets us compare parts we did not use to establish it.

For example, suppose

AB=DEDE=7 cmAC=DFDF=10 cmm∠BAC=m∠EDFm∠EDF=52∘.\begin{aligned}AB&=DE \\ DE&=7\text{ cm} \\ AC&=DF \\ DF&=10\text{ cm} \\ m\angle BAC&=m\angle EDF \\ m\angle EDF&=52^\circ\end{aligned}.ABDEACDFm∠BACm∠EDF​=DE=7 cm=DF=10 cm=m∠EDF=52∘​.

The angle is included between the two named sides. Side-Angle-Side (SAS) establishes

△ABC≅△DEF.\begin{aligned}\triangle ABC&\cong\triangle DEF\end{aligned}.△ABC​≅△DEF​.

The matching motions preserve angles, so the remaining corresponding angles agree too:

∠ACB≅∠DFE.\begin{aligned}\angle ACB&\cong\angle DFE\end{aligned}.∠ACB​≅∠DFE​.

That last step is called CPCTC, short for corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent. It uses a congruence already established; it does not establish triangle congruence by itself.

Worked example 1 Keep the two reasons separate

Problem. Given

JK=MNJL=MP∠KJL≅∠NMP\begin{aligned}JK&=MN \\ JL&=MP \\ \angle KJL&\cong\angle NMP\end{aligned}JKJL∠KJL​=MN=MP≅∠NMP​

prove

KL=NP.\begin{aligned}KL&=NP\end{aligned}.KL​=NP​.

Give a reason for the whole-triangle match and a reason for the target side equality.

Solution. The angle at JJJ lies between JKJKJK and JLJLJL. Its partner at MMM lies between MNMNMN and MPMPMP. The two side pairs and their included angle give

△JKL≅△MNP\begin{aligned}\triangle JKL&\cong\triangle MNP\end{aligned}△JKL​≅△MNP​

by SAS. The ordered match sends KKK to NNN and LLL to PPP. Therefore

KL=NP\begin{aligned}KL&=NP\end{aligned}KL​=NP​

by CPCTC.

Check your understanding

A proof has established triangle ABC congruent to triangle QPR. Which conclusion and reason are valid next?

Answer choices

The ordered match sends AAA to QQQ and CCC to RRR.

Thus ACACAC corresponds to QRQRQR, and CPCTC gives

AC=QR.\begin{aligned}AC&=QR.\end{aligned}AC​=QR.​

Work backward from a target: identify triangles containing its two parts, then ask which known criterion the givens can supply. Write the proof forward, using only justified statements. Keep the target equality out of the evidence used to establish congruence; otherwise the proof assumes its own conclusion.

The criteria themselves were justified earlier: Side-Side-Side (SSS), SAS and Angle-Side-Angle (ASA), and Angle-Angle-Side (AAS) and Hypotenuse-Leg (HL). Here the choice depends on the parts actually known, including whether an angle or side is included and whether both triangles are right triangles.

Shared and overlapping parts

A shared segment can match itself:

PR=PR\begin{aligned}PR&=PR\end{aligned}PR​=PR​

is the reflexive property of equality. An angle can also be shared, but its rays must really be the same rays. Sharing a vertex alone does not make two angles equal.

In a crowded figure, redraw the two triangles separately. Preserve every vertex name, given mark, and correspondence; do not introduce a fact because the new drawing looks regular.

Worked example 2 Separate two overlapping triangles

Problem. Points A,B,DA,B,DA,B,D lie on one ray in that order. Points A,C,EA,C,EA,C,E lie on a second ray in that order. The rays are distinct and form an angle between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘. Given

AB=ACAC=6 cmAD=AEAE=15 cm\begin{aligned}AB&=AC \\ AC&=6\text{ cm} \\ AD&=AE \\ AE&=15\text{ cm}\end{aligned}ABACADAE​=AC=6 cm=AE=15 cm​

prove

BE=CD.\begin{aligned}BE&=CD\end{aligned}.BE​=CD​.

Solution. In △ABE\triangle ABE△ABE and △ACD\triangle ACD△ACD, the side pairs are

AB=ACAE=AD.\begin{aligned}AB&=AC \\ AE&=AD\end{aligned}.ABAE​=AC=AD​.

The angle between them is the same opening at AAA: ray ABABAB is ray ADADAD, and ray AEAEAE is ray ACACAC.

Thus

△ABE≅△ACD\begin{aligned}\triangle ABE&\cong\triangle ACD\end{aligned}△ABE​≅△ACD​

by SAS, with

A↔AB↔CE↔D.\begin{aligned}A&\leftrightarrow A \\ B&\leftrightarrow C \\ E&\leftrightarrow D\end{aligned}.ABE​↔A↔C↔D​.

The remaining side pair gives

BE=CD\begin{aligned}BE&=CD\end{aligned}BE​=CD​

by CPCTC.

Check your understanding

In the overlapping figure, why are angle BAE and angle CAD the same angle?

Answer choices

Ray ABABAB is ray ADADAD, and ray AEAEAE is ray ACACAC.

Both angles therefore use the same two rays:

∠BAE=∠CAD.\begin{aligned}\angle BAE&=\angle CAD.\end{aligned}∠BAE​=∠CAD.​

Sharing only a vertex would not establish this.

Add a useful segment

An auxiliary segment is an extra segment drawn to help an argument. A diagonal joins two opposite corners of a four-sided figure. Drawing one creates triangles to compare, but does not by itself make any new lengths or angles equal.

Worked example 3 Use a common diagonal

Problem. A four-sided figure has vertices P,Q,R,SP,Q,R,SP,Q,R,S in order, with every corner pointing outward. Given

PQ=PSRQ=RS\begin{aligned}PQ&=PS \\ RQ&=RS\end{aligned}PQRQ​=PS=RS​

prove

∠QPR≅∠SPR\begin{aligned}\angle QPR&\cong\angle SPR\end{aligned}∠QPR​≅∠SPR​

by adding a segment if needed.

Solution. Draw diagonal PRPRPR. The two triangles have

PQ=PSRQ=RS\begin{aligned}PQ&=PS \\ RQ&=RS\end{aligned}PQRQ​=PS=RS​

by the givens, and

PR=PR\begin{aligned}PR&=PR\end{aligned}PR​=PR​

because it is shared. Therefore

△PQR≅△PSR\begin{aligned}\triangle PQR&\cong\triangle PSR\end{aligned}△PQR​≅△PSR​

by SSS. Under this order, PPP matches PPP, QQQ matches SSS, and RRR matches RRR. Hence

∠QPR≅∠SPR\begin{aligned}\angle QPR&\cong\angle SPR\end{aligned}∠QPR​≅∠SPR​

by CPCTC.

Pass a result to a second match

One triangle match can establish information needed in another. The second comparison needs its own evidence and its own vertex order.

Worked example 4 Carry an intermediate side equality forward

Problem. The named triangles each have three noncollinear vertices. Given

RA=SCSC=7 cmRB=SDSD=12 cmm∠ARB=m∠CSDm∠CSD=50∘\begin{aligned}RA&=SC \\ SC&=7\text{ cm} \\ RB&=SD \\ SD&=12\text{ cm} \\ m\angle ARB&=m\angle CSD \\ m\angle CSD&=50^\circ\end{aligned}RASCRBSDm∠ARBm∠CSD​=SC=7 cm=SD=12 cm=m∠CSD=50∘​

first establish

AB=CD.\begin{aligned}AB&=CD\end{aligned}.AB​=CD​.

Also given

EA=FCFC=8 cmEB=FDFD=11 cm.\begin{aligned}EA&=FC \\ FC&=8\text{ cm} \\ EB&=FD \\ FD&=11\text{ cm}\end{aligned}.EAFCEBFD​=FC=8 cm=FD=11 cm​.

Use your first result to prove

∠AEB≅∠CFD.\begin{aligned}\angle AEB&\cong\angle CFD\end{aligned}.∠AEB​≅∠CFD​.

State both ordered triangle congruences and justify the steps between them.

Solution. Stage 1 compares △RAB\triangle RAB△RAB and △SCD\triangle SCD△SCD. The 777 cm and 121212 cm side pairs include the 50∘50^\circ50∘ angle pair. Thus

△RAB≅△SCD\begin{aligned}\triangle RAB&\cong\triangle SCD\end{aligned}△RAB​≅△SCD​

by SAS. Here

R↔SA↔CB↔D.\begin{aligned}R&\leftrightarrow S \\ A&\leftrightarrow C \\ B&\leftrightarrow D\end{aligned}.RAB​↔S↔C↔D​.

Consequently

AB=CD\begin{aligned}AB&=CD\end{aligned}AB​=CD​

by CPCTC.

Stage 2 compares △EAB\triangle EAB△EAB and △FCD\triangle FCD△FCD. Its known side pairs are

EA=FCFC=8 cmEB=FDFD=11 cm.\begin{aligned}EA&=FC \\ FC&=8\text{ cm} \\ EB&=FD \\ FD&=11\text{ cm}\end{aligned}.EAFCEBFD​=FC=8 cm=FD=11 cm​.

The first stage supplies the third pair,

AB=CD\begin{aligned}AB&=CD\end{aligned}AB​=CD​

so

△EAB≅△FCD\begin{aligned}\triangle EAB&\cong\triangle FCD\end{aligned}△EAB​≅△FCD​

by SSS. This comparison has a new first-vertex match:

E↔F.\begin{aligned}E&\leftrightarrow F\end{aligned}.E​↔F​.

The other two vertex matches remain:

A↔CB↔D.\begin{aligned}A&\leftrightarrow C \\ B&\leftrightarrow D\end{aligned}.AB​↔C↔D​.

Therefore

∠AEB≅∠CFD\begin{aligned}\angle AEB&\cong\angle CFD\end{aligned}∠AEB​≅∠CFD​

by CPCTC.

The equal side pair passed between the stages is a proved result. It is different from a shared segment equaling itself by the reflexive property.

Check your understanding

After the first match gives

AB=CD\begin{aligned}AB&=CD\end{aligned}AB​=CD​

by CPCTC,

EA=FCEB=FD\begin{aligned}EA&=FC \\ EB&=FD\end{aligned}EAEB​=FC=FD​

are also known. What establishes triangle EAB congruent to triangle FCD?

Answer choices

The first-stage result supplies

AB=CD.\begin{aligned}AB&=CD\end{aligned}.AB​=CD​.

Together with

EA=FCEB=FD\begin{aligned}EA&=FC \\ EB&=FD\end{aligned}EAEB​=FC=FD​

it gives three corresponding side pairs. SSS establishes

△EAB≅△FCD.\begin{aligned}\triangle EAB&\cong\triangle FCD.\end{aligned}△EAB​≅△FCD.​

Measure a distance through its partner

Worked example 5 Replace an inaccessible triangle with an accessible one

Problem. Points BBB and CCC are on accessible land, with

BC=19 m.\begin{aligned}BC&=19\text{ m}\end{aligned}.BC​=19 m​.

Point AAA is across a pond. On the other side of line BCBCBC, an accessible point DDD is chosen so that

∠DBC≅∠ABC∠BCD≅∠BCA.\begin{aligned}\angle DBC&\cong\angle ABC \\ \angle BCD&\cong\angle BCA\end{aligned}.∠DBC∠BCD​≅∠ABC≅∠BCA​.

Measurement gives

BD=24 m.\begin{aligned}BD&=24\text{ m}\end{aligned}.BD​=24 m​.

Find ABABAB in this ideal model and justify the transfer.

Solution. The matched angles are at BBB and CCC. Their included side is the same segment BCBCBC. Thus

△ABC≅△DBC\begin{aligned}\triangle ABC&\cong\triangle DBC\end{aligned}△ABC​≅△DBC​

by ASA, with

A↔DB↔BC↔C.\begin{aligned}A&\leftrightarrow D \\ B&\leftrightarrow B \\ C&\leftrightarrow C\end{aligned}.ABC​↔D↔B↔C​.

By CPCTC,

AB=DB\begin{aligned}AB&=DB\end{aligned}AB​=DB​

so the requested distance is 242424 m. The shared side BCBCBC supplies the side equality for ASA; the 242424 m accessible side is the measurement transferred afterward.

Real angle sightings and length measurements have error. A field result based on approximate measurements would be reported as about 242424 m; exact equality belongs to the ideal geometric conditions in the problem.

Check your understanding

In the pond model, which side corresponds to AC after triangle ABC is matched with triangle DBC?

Answer choices

The ordered match sends AAA to DDD and fixes CCC.

Thus ACACAC corresponds to DCDCDC, so CPCTC gives

AC=DC.\begin{aligned}AC&=DC.\end{aligned}AC​=DC.​

Finish the angle-copy proof

In Circles and Construction Tools, the compass procedure copied distances, and the claim about the resulting angle was deferred. We can now prove it without assuming that the copied angle is equal.

Recall the labels. A circle centered at source vertex OOO meets its two rays at AAA and BBB. A circle with the same radius, centered at target vertex PPP, meets the target ray at DDD. An arc centered at DDD with radius ABABAB meets the target circle at EEE, on the chosen side of the baseline. Draw ray PEPEPE. The source opening is strictly between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘, and the initial radius is positive, as in the original construction.

Why the angle-copy construction works#

For a numerical instance, suppose the initial compass radius is 999 cm and the chord ABABAB is 101010 cm. The source triangle has

OA=OBOB=9 cmAB=10 cm.\begin{aligned}OA&=OB \\ OB&=9\text{ cm} \\ AB&=10\text{ cm}\end{aligned}.OAOBAB​=OB=9 cm=10 cm​.

The target triangle has

PD=PEPE=9 cmDE=10 cm.\begin{aligned}PD&=PE \\ PE&=9\text{ cm} \\ DE&=10\text{ cm}\end{aligned}.PDPEDE​=PE=9 cm=10 cm​.

These are three side matches, so

△OAB≅△PDE\begin{aligned}\triangle OAB&\cong\triangle PDE\end{aligned}△OAB​≅△PDE​

by SSS. CPCTC gives

∠AOB≅∠DPE.\begin{aligned}\angle AOB&\cong\angle DPE\end{aligned}.∠AOB​≅∠DPE​.

For any permitted source angle and positive radius, the circle definitions give

OA=PDOB=PE.\begin{aligned}OA&=PD \\ OB&=PE\end{aligned}.OAOB​=PD=PE​.

The transferred chord gives

AB=DE.\begin{aligned}AB&=DE\end{aligned}.AB​=DE​.

Therefore the same ordered triangles are congruent by SSS, regardless of the particular radius or chord length. Their corresponding angles at OOO and PPP agree by CPCTC. The source angle’s rays are OAOAOA and OBOBOB, and the constructed target rays are PDPDPD and PEPEPE, so these are precisely the angles being copied.

The existence of the required arc intersections is the accepted construction fact from Circles and Construction Tools. The three length equalities follow from the circle definitions and compass settings; the angle equality follows only after SSS.

∎

Check your understanding

In the angle-copy construction, why is SSS used before concluding that the source and target angles agree?

Answer choices

The two common-radius pairs and the transferred chord establish SSS:

△OAB≅△PDE.\begin{aligned}\triangle OAB&\cong\triangle PDE.\end{aligned}△OAB​≅△PDE.​

The corresponding angle equality follows afterward by CPCTC:

∠AOB≅∠DPE.\begin{aligned}\angle AOB&\cong\angle DPE.\end{aligned}∠AOB​≅∠DPE.​

The argument also completes the justification of using that angle copy in the parallel-line construction from Proving Lines Parallel or Perpendicular. There the corresponding-angle converse supplies parallelism once the copied angle is established.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Using CPCTC too early: Establish a valid ordered triangle congruence first, without assuming the target equality as evidence.
  • Reusing a vertex order: Each new triangle comparison needs its own ordered correspondence.
  • Losing the match: Read corresponding parts from the established vertex order, including in separated copies.
  • Inventing a shared angle: A common vertex is insufficient; check that both pairs of rays coincide.
  • Reading facts into an added segment: An auxiliary segment creates a useful figure, not new equalities without reasons.

Key takeaways

  • Identify triangles that contain the two target parts, and choose a criterion from justified data.
  • Establish the ordered triangle match before using CPCTC.
  • A CPCTC result can become evidence for a second triangle congruence, with its own correspondence.
  • An accessible corresponding side can supply an inaccessible measurement.
  • Three transferred lengths establish SSS in the angle-copy construction; the angle equality then follows.

A bit of history (optional)

Copying an angle with Euclid’s tool rules can hide several smaller constructions. In David Joyce’s commentary on Book I, Proposition 23, transferring just one length to a new location requires four circles. The angle construction needs more than one transferred length, so its familiar outline conceals substantial compass work.

The choice of points on the source rays matters. Joyce’s commentary credits Apollonius with suggesting that they be taken at equal distances from the angle vertex. That reduces the distinct distances to transfer from three to two: one radius and the chord between the marks.

The translated construction and commentary show both the triangle comparison and the extra steps behind those length transfers.
