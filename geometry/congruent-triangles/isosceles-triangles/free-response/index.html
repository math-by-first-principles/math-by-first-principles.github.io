  1. Problem 1 Choose the base from equal sides

    Triangle UVW has the following givens:

    UV=VW.m∠U=46∘.\begin{aligned}UV&=VW.\\m\angle U&=46^\circ.\end{aligned}UVm∠U​=VW.=46∘.​

    Name the base associated with the equal sides, then find angles V and W.

    Text description of this figure

    Triangle UVW has matching side ticks on UV and VW, meeting at V. Angle U has the given label 46 degrees. Angles V and W are not evaluated.

    1. Hint 1

      Locate the common endpoint of the equal sides.

    2. Hint 2

      The angles opposite the equal sides match; then use the triangle angle sum.

    Answer

    Base: UW.m∠W=46∘.m∠V=88∘.\begin{aligned}\text{Base: }&UW.\\m\angle W&=46^\circ.\\m\angle V&=88^\circ.\end{aligned}Base: m∠Wm∠V​UW.=46∘.=88∘.​

    The equal sides meet at V, so UW is the base.

    Angles U and W are opposite the equal sides and therefore match.

    m∠V=180∘−46∘−46∘.m∠V=88∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle V&=180^\circ-46^\circ-46^\circ.\\m\angle V&=88^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠Vm∠V​=180∘−46∘−46∘.=88∘.​

  2. Problem 2 A wide vertex angle

    An isosceles triangle has a vertex angle of 116 degrees between its chosen equal sides. Find both base angles and classify the triangle by its angles.

    Text description of this figure

    An isosceles triangle has two equal-side ticks and the given obtuse vertex angle of 116 degrees. Its base angles have no numerical labels.

    1. Hint 1

      Here the equal base angles cannot also be 116 degrees, since three such angles would exceed the triangle angle sum.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract the vertex angle from 180 degrees and share the remainder equally.

    Answer

    Each base angle: 32∘.Classification: obtuse.\begin{aligned}\text{Each base angle: }&32^\circ.\\\text{Classification: }&\text{obtuse}.\end{aligned}Each base angle: Classification: ​32∘.obtuse.​

    Full solution

    The two base angles together use the remaining angle measure:

    180∘−116∘=64∘.\begin{aligned}180^\circ-116^\circ&=64^\circ.\end{aligned}180∘−116∘​=64∘.​

    They are equal, so each is

    64∘/2=32∘.\begin{aligned}64^\circ/2&=32^\circ.\end{aligned}64∘/2​=32∘.​

    The 116-degree angle is obtuse, so the whole triangle is classified as obtuse.

    Answer

    Each base angle: 32∘.Classification: obtuse.\begin{aligned}\text{Each base angle: }&32^\circ.\\\text{Classification: }&\text{obtuse}.\end{aligned}Each base angle: Classification: ​32∘.obtuse.​

    Key idea

    An obtuse vertex angle is compatible with equal acute base angles.

  3. Problem 3 Equal angles give a length equation

    Triangle MNP has congruent angles at M and P. The lengths MN and NP in centimeters are

    MN=3a+4.NP=7a−20.\begin{aligned}MN&=3a+4.\\NP&=7a-20.\end{aligned}MNNP​=3a+4.=7a−20.​

    Find a and both lengths.

    Text description of this figure

    Triangle MNP has matching arcs at M and P. MN is labeled 3a plus 4 centimeters and NP 7a minus 20 centimeters. No equal-side ticks or solved values appear.

    1. Hint 1

      Match each equal angle to its opposite side.

    2. Hint 2

      The converse of the base-angle theorem gives the equation

      3a+4=7a−20.3a+4=7a-20.3a+4=7a−20.

    Answer

    a=6.MN=22 cm.NP=22 cm.\begin{aligned}a&=6.\\MN&=22\text{ cm}.\\NP&=22\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}aMNNP​=6.=22 cm.=22 cm.​

    Full solution

    The side opposite M is NP, and the side opposite P is MN.

    The converse gives equal lengths:

    3a+4=7a−20.\begin{aligned}3a+4&=7a-20.\end{aligned}3a+4​=7a−20.​

    Rearrange:

    24=4a.a=6.\begin{aligned}24&=4a.\\a&=6.\end{aligned}24a​=4a.=6.​

    Check each expression separately:

    MN=3(6)+4.MN=22 centimeters.NP=7(6)−20.NP=22 centimeters.\begin{aligned}MN&=3(6)+4.\\MN&=22\text{ centimeters}.\\NP&=7(6)-20.\\NP&=22\text{ centimeters}.\end{aligned}MNMNNPNP​=3(6)+4.=22 centimeters.=7(6)−20.=22 centimeters.​

    Both lengths are positive.

    Answer

    a=6.MN=22 cm.NP=22 cm.\begin{aligned}a&=6.\\MN&=22\text{ cm}.\\NP&=22\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}aMNNP​=6.=22 cm.=22 cm.​

    Key idea

    Equal angles control the opposite sides, not the side joining their vertices.

  4. Problem 4 Side length and angle measure

    An equilateral triangle has perimeter 81 centimeters. Find each side length and each interior angle measure. Explain whether doubling the perimeter would change those angle measures.

    Text description of this figure

    An equilateral triangle has one matching tick on each of its three sides. Its given perimeter is 81 centimeters. No side length or angle measure is shown.

    1. Hint 1

      Equal sides divide the perimeter into three equal lengths.

    2. Hint 2

      The base-angle theorem makes all three angles equal, regardless of the side length.

    Answer

    Each side: 27 centimeters. Each angle: 60∘60^\circ60∘. With doubled perimeter, each angle remains 60∘60^\circ60∘.

    Full solution

    The equal sides share the total perimeter:

    81/3=27.\begin{aligned}81/3&=27.\end{aligned}81/3​=27.​

    Applying the base-angle theorem to two pairs of equal sides makes all three angles equal.

    Their total is 180 degrees, so each is 60∘60^\circ60∘.

    With doubled perimeter, each side is 54 centimeters.

    The triangle remains equilateral, so its angles are still 60∘60^\circ60∘.

    Answer

    Each side: 27 centimeters. Each angle: 60∘60^\circ60∘. With doubled perimeter, each angle remains 60∘60^\circ60∘.

    Key idea

    Changing an equilateral triangle size changes its lengths, while its angles remain fixed.

  6. Problem 6 A triangular fabric panel

    A flat triangular fabric panel has two equal sloping edges and a base of 28 centimeters. A seam runs from the common vertex of the equal edges along the internal angle bisector to the base. One of the two angle parts at that vertex measures 36 degrees. Find each base piece cut by the seam, each base angle, and the angle the seam makes with the base. Assume the seam is straight and follows the stated bisector exactly.

    1. Hint 1

      The internal bisector from the vertex between equal sides is also a median and an altitude.

      The full vertex angle contains two 36-degree parts; then use the triangle angle sum for the equal base angles.

    Answer

    Each base piece: 14 cm.Each base angle: 54∘.Seam-base angle: 90∘.\begin{aligned}\text{Each base piece: }&14\text{ cm}.\\\text{Each base angle: }&54^\circ.\\\text{Seam-base angle: }&90^\circ.\end{aligned}Each base piece: Each base angle: Seam-base angle: ​14 cm.54∘.90∘.​

    Full solution

    The vertex bisector in an isosceles triangle is a median, so each base piece has length

    28/2=14.\begin{aligned}28/2&=14.\end{aligned}28/2​=14.​

    The full vertex angle has measure

    36∘+36∘=72∘.\begin{aligned}36^\circ+36^\circ&=72^\circ.\end{aligned}36∘+36∘​=72∘.​

    The equal base angles divide the remainder:

    (180∘−72∘)/2=54∘.\begin{aligned}(180^\circ-72^\circ)/2&=54^\circ.\end{aligned}(180∘−72∘)/2​=54∘.​

    The same bisector is also an altitude.

  8. Problem 8 Compare two triangular pieces

    Triangle RST is equilateral with side length 6 centimeters. Triangle XYZ is equiangular with perimeter 45 centimeters. Decide whether the triangles are congruent and justify your answer.

    Text description of this figure

    Two schematic triangles are compared. Blue solid RST is equilateral with side length 6 centimeters. Red dashed XYZ has the given equal-angle arcs and perimeter 45 centimeters. No side length for XYZ or congruence conclusion is marked.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the converse of the base-angle theorem to identify the sides of XYZ.

    2. Hint 2

      A rigid motion preserves lengths as well as angles.

    Answer

    The triangles are not congruent. Triangle XYZXYZXYZ has side length 15 centimeters; triangle RSTRSTRST has side length 6 centimeters.

    Full solution

    The three equal angles of XYZ give three equal opposite sides.

    Its perimeter therefore divides into equal thirds:

    45/3=15.\begin{aligned}45/3&=15.\end{aligned}45/3​=15.​

    RST has side lengths 6 centimeters, whereas XYZ has side lengths 15 centimeters.

    Rigid motions cannot change 6 into 15, so there is no congruence.

    Answer

    The triangles are not congruent. Triangle XYZXYZXYZ has side length 15 centimeters; triangle RSTRSTRST has side length 6 centimeters.

    Key idea

    Equal angle measures do not by themselves establish congruence.

  9. Problem 9 Prove the reverse direction

    In nondegenerate triangle ABC, the given angle congruence is

    ∠ABC≅∠ACB.\begin{aligned}\angle ABC&\cong\angle ACB.\end{aligned}∠ABC​≅∠ACB.​

    Text description of this figure

    Triangle ABC has matching angle arcs at B and C. No equal-side ticks are drawn.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the triangle with itself under a different vertex correspondence, using the ordered names BCA and CBA.

    2. Hint 2

      Their angles at the endpoints of BCBCBC match in reversed order. The included side satisfies

      BC=CB.BC=CB.BC=CB.

    Answer

    By ASA,

    △BCA≅△CBA.\triangle BCA\cong\triangle CBA.△BCA≅△CBA.

    Thus

    BA=CA.BA=CA.BA=CA.

    Full solution

    Compare the names BCA and CBA.

    The first angle at B matches the second angle at C by the given.

    The first angle at C matches the second angle at B for the same reason.

    The side between those angles is BC in the first name and CB in the second, so its length equals itself.

    ASA gives

    △BCA≅△CBA.\begin{aligned}\triangle BCA&\cong\triangle CBA.\end{aligned}△BCA​≅△CBA.​

    The correspondence exchanges B with C and keeps A fixed.

    Thus side BA corresponds to CA, giving

    BA=CA.\begin{aligned}BA&=CA.\end{aligned}BA​=CA.​

    Answer

    By ASA,

    △BCA≅△CBA.\triangle BCA\cong\triangle CBA.△BCA≅△CBA.

  10. Problem 10 Start from the median

    Triangle ABC has the equal sides

    AB=AC.\begin{aligned}AB&=AC.\end{aligned}AB​=AC.​

    Point D is the midpoint of BC. Prove that AD is both an internal angle bisector and an altitude. Then state why the line AD is a reflection axis of the triangle.

    Text description of this figure

    Triangle ABC has matching ticks on AB and AC. D is the midpoint of BC, marked by double ticks on BD and DC. AD is drawn without right-angle or equal vertex-angle markings.

    1. Hint 1

      List three equal-side pairs for triangles ABD and ACD before making any angle claim.

    2. Hint 2

      Their congruent angles at D form a linear pair. For the reflection, use the equal base halves on the perpendicular to AD.

    Answer

    Segment ADADAD bisects ∠BAC\angle BAC∠BAC and satisfies

    AD⊥BC.AD\perp BC.AD⊥BC.

    Reflection across line ADADAD fixes AAA and exchanges BBB with CCC, so the triangle matches itself.

    Full solution

    The equal sides give

    AB=AC.\begin{aligned}AB&=AC.\end{aligned}AB​=AC.​

    The midpoint gives

    BD=DC.\begin{aligned}BD&=DC.\end{aligned}BD​=DC.​

    AD is shared.

    Hence, by SSS,

    △ABD≅△ACD.\begin{aligned}\triangle ABD&\cong\triangle ACD.\end{aligned}△ABD​≅△ACD.​

    Corresponding angles at A are equal, so AD bisects ∠BAC\angle BAC∠BAC.

    Corresponding angles at D are also equal.

    Since B, D, C are collinear, those adjacent angles total 180 degrees and are each 90 degrees.

    Therefore

    AD⊥BC.\begin{aligned}AD&\perp BC.\end{aligned}AD​⊥BC.​

    Reflection across AD fixes A and D.

    Points B and C lie equal distances on opposite sides of D along the perpendicular line BC, so the reflection exchanges them.

    The reflected triangle has the same three vertices and sides.

    Answer

    Segment ADADAD bisects ∠BAC\angle BAC∠BAC and satisfies

    AD⊥BC.AD\perp BC.AD⊥BC.

    Reflection across line ADADAD fixes AAA and exchanges BBB with CCC, so the triangle matches itself.

    Key idea

    Prove the two halves congruent before using their equal angles to establish the extra roles.
