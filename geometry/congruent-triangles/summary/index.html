Congruent Triangles: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Congruence and rigid motions
Two figures are congruent when translations, rotations, and reflections map the whole first figure exactly onto the second. Position and orientation may differ; lengths and angle measures do not.
Correspondence and ordered names
In △ABC≅△DEF\triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF△ABC≅△DEF, positions pair AAA with DDD, BBB with EEE, and CCC with FFF. Side endpoints and an angle’s middle letter identify matching parts.
Included angle and included side
The included angle of two sides is at their common endpoint. The included side of two angles joins their vertices. The terms describe placement, not size.
Isosceles triangle and chosen base
An isosceles triangle has at least two congruent sides. For a chosen pair, their common endpoint is the vertex; the opposite side is the base, and its endpoint angles are the base angles.
Equilateral and equiangular
Equilateral means three congruent sides. Equiangular means three congruent interior angles. Every equilateral triangle is also isosceles under the inclusive definition.
Median, altitude, and internal bisector
A median joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. An altitude runs from a vertex perpendicularly to the opposite side line. An internal bisector halves the interior angle at its vertex.
Hypotenuse and legs
In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. The two legs meet at that angle; identify them from the right-angle location, not the drawing’s orientation.
CPCTC
Corresponding parts of congruent triangles are congruent. This is a consequence of an established triangle match, not a criterion that establishes the match.
Shared part and reflexive equality
A shared segment has the same length in both triangles, such as AD=ADAD=ADAD=AD. A shared angle requires the same two rays; a shared vertex alone is insufficient.
Auxiliary segment and diagonal
An auxiliary segment helps a proof. A diagonal joins opposite corners of a four-sided figure. Neither supplies unproved angle or length equalities.
Insufficient information
AAA alone or unrestricted SSA alone leaves congruence undecided; neither alone proves noncongruence.
Nondegenerate triangle
A triangle has three distinct, noncollinear vertices and positive side lengths. A collinear arrangement from a failed construction is not a triangle.

Formulas and theorems

  • Six corresponding parts

    Congruent triangles have three equal corresponding side lengths and three equal corresponding angle measures. Conversely, all six matches in one correspondence establish congruence.

    Text description

    Triangle ABC and triangle DEF rotated half a turn have three corresponding side pairs and three corresponding angle pairs marked alike.

    Use when Use actual triangles and one consistent vertex matching.

  • SSS: Side-Side-Side

    Three equal corresponding side-length pairs establish triangle congruence.

    Text description

    Three matching side pairs have one, two and three ticks. The second triangle is rotated half a turn.

    Use when Start with two actual noncollinear triangles. A proposed list of three positive lengths does not automatically construct a triangle.

    e.g. Two triangles with side lengths 9,16,219,16,219,16,21 cm in matching pairs are congruent by SSS.

  • SAS: Side-Angle-Side

    Two equal corresponding side-length pairs and congruent included angles establish triangle congruence.

    Text description

    Two separate triangle diagrams distinguish the location of a known angle. In the first, sides AB and AC have side ticks, and the marked angle is at their common vertex A. In the second, sides DE and DF have the same side-tick pattern, but the marked angle is at E. The first angle is included between the two marked sides; the second is not.

    Use when In each triangle the known angle is between the two known sides.

    e.g. Matching sides of 131313 and 222222 cm with matching included 61∘61^\circ61∘ angles establish SAS.

  • ASA: Angle-Side-Angle

    Two congruent corresponding angle pairs and equal included side lengths establish triangle congruence.

    Text description

    Angles at A and D have one arc; angles at B and E have two arcs. Their joining sides AB and DE are marked equal.

    Use when The side joins the two known angle vertices in each triangle, following the same correspondence.

    e.g. Matching endpoint angles 28∘,89∘28^\circ,89^\circ28∘,89∘ and the 181818 cm side between them establish ASA.

  • AAS: Angle-Angle-Side

    Two congruent corresponding angle pairs and equal corresponding nonincluded side lengths establish triangle congruence.

    Text description

    The angles at A and B match those at D and E. Marked sides AC and DF are not between those angle vertices.

    Use when The given side is not between the two given angles, and the same vertex correspondence holds throughout.

  • HL: Hypotenuse-Leg

    Two right triangles are congruent if their hypotenuses and one pair of corresponding legs have equal lengths.

    Text description

    Both triangles have a marked right angle. Their opposite hypotenuses match, and one leg pair matches.

    Use when Both right angles must be stated, marked, or proved. Two arbitrary side pairs without that condition do not supply HL.

    e.g. Two right triangles with hypotenuses 414141 cm and matching legs 999 cm are congruent by HL.

  • Base-angle theorem and its converse

    In triangle ABC, these statements are equivalent:

    AB=ACm∠B=m∠C\begin{gathered} AB=AC\\ m\angle B=m\angle C \end{gathered}AB=ACm∠B=m∠C​
    Text description

    In triangle ABC, equal sides AB and AC have one tick each. Their opposite base angles at C and B have matching arcs.

    Use when In nondegenerate triangle ABC, compare each side with its opposite angle. Both directions are valid; the converse is a separate result.

  • Isosceles angle calculations

    b=180∘−v2v=180∘−2b\begin{gathered} b=\frac{180^\circ-v}{2}\\ v=180^\circ-2b \end{gathered}b=2180∘−v​v=180∘−2b​
    Text description

    Triangle ABC has equal sides AB and AC, vertex angle v at A, and equal base angles b at B and C.

    Use when Here v is the vertex angle between the chosen equal sides, and b is either equal base angle.

    e.g. For vertex angle 118∘118^\circ118∘, each base angle is (180∘−118∘)/2=31∘(180^\circ-118^\circ)/2=31^\circ(180∘−118∘)/2=31∘.

  • Equilateral and equiangular equivalence

    A triangle is equilateral exactly when it is equiangular; every interior angle is 60 degrees. Its three medians are also altitudes and internal bisectors; their lines are its three reflection axes.

    Text description

    All three sides and all three angles are marked equal. Dashed medians join each vertex to the midpoint of its opposite side.

    Use when The side length is not fixed by the angle measures. For an equilateral triangle of perimeter P, each side length is P divided by three.

    e.g. For perimeter 999999 cm, each side is 333333 cm and each angle is 60∘60^\circ60∘.

  • The isosceles vertex segment

    The internal vertex bisector, median, and altitude from the vertex between the chosen equal sides are the same segment. Its line is a reflection axis.

    Text description

    In isosceles ABC, AD bisects the vertex angle. The proved conclusions are shown: equal base halves and a right angle at D.

    Use when The triangle is isosceles and the segment starts at the common endpoint of its equal sides. This coincidence need not hold for a different vertex or an arbitrary triangle.

    e.g. If that segment meets a base of 383838 cm, the base pieces are 191919 cm each and the meeting angles are 90∘90^\circ90∘.

  • AAA and unrestricted SSA

    AAA alone does not fix size. Two side pairs and a nonincluded angle pair, without additional restrictions, do not guarantee congruence.

    Text description

    A schematic ray from A contains two possible points B1 and B2. C is on a second ray. The equal-radius segments from C reach both points on the first ray.

    Use when AAA and SSA can hold for congruent pairs too. SSA constructions may yield zero, one, or two triangles. HL is a valid right-triangle case.

  • Construction conditions

    For SAS use two positive lengths and an included angle strictly between zero and 180 degrees. For ASA use a positive base and two positive endpoint angles totaling less than 180 degrees.

    Use when ASA rays open into the same half-plane. A sum of 180 degrees gives parallel rays; a greater sum gives no intersection on the chosen side.

  • CPCTC after the match

    An established ordered match

    △ABC≅△DEF\triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF△ABC≅△DEF

    licenses corresponding-part comparisons such as

    BC=EF∠ACB≅∠DFE\begin{gathered} BC=EF\\ \angle ACB\cong\angle DFE \end{gathered}BC=EF∠ACB≅∠DFE​
    Text description

    An established ordered match pairs A with D, B with E and C with F. Sides BC and EF and angles at C and F are highlighted as corresponding parts.

    Use when First justify an ordered triangle match using a valid criterion. Never use the target equality as its own premise.

  • Transferred lengths in an angle copy

    OA=PDOB=PEAB=DE∠AOB≅∠DPE\begin{gathered} OA=PD\\ OB=PE\\ AB=DE\\ \angle AOB\cong\angle DPE \end{gathered}OA=PDOB=PEAB=DE∠AOB≅∠DPE​
    Text description

    Source OAB and target PDE have recorded radius lengths. The corresponding chords AB and DE have matching double ticks. No angle-equality mark is supplied.

    Use when O and P are source and target vertices; A,B and D,E are radius marks. The source opening is strictly between zero and 180 degrees; the radius is positive. The angle match follows from SSS, then CPCTC.

Problem types, step by step

Read or recover a correspondence

  1. Pair letters in the same positions of the ordered names. If only side matches are given, locate the vertex where each relevant pair of sides meets. Distinct side lengths make each partner unique.
  2. Map both endpoints of each side and all three letters of each angle. Write the whole congruence statement in that same order.

e.g. From △LMN≅△XZY\triangle LMN\cong\triangle XZY△LMN≅△XZY, side LNLNLN corresponds to XYXYXY and ∠LMN\angle LMN∠LMN to ∠XZY\angle XZY∠XZY.

Select a criterion from justified information

  1. Mark the given side and angle pairs in a consistent correspondence. Add only independently justified facts, such as a shared side or vertical-angle equality.
  2. Check three sides for SSS; two sides and their included angle for SAS; two angles and their included or nonincluded side for ASA or AAS.
  3. For HL first establish both right angles, then locate equal hypotenuses and corresponding legs. If no sufficient pattern is present, identify the missing evidence.

Solve for a matching length or angle

  1. Establish the correspondence and the reason the relevant parts are equal. Equate lengths with lengths or angle measures with angle measures.
  2. Solve the resulting equation. Substitute into every requested expression, check positive lengths and valid angle measures, and report the geometric quantity as well as the variable.

e.g. Equal angles opposite lengths 3z+113z+113z+11 and 5z−155z-155z−15 cm give 3z+11=5z−15z=133(13)+11=50\begin{gathered}3z+11=5z-15\\z=13\\3(13)+11=50\end{gathered}3z+11=5z−15z=133(13)+11=50​, so both lengths are 505050 cm.

Construct from three side lengths

  1. Draw one side as the base. From each endpoint draw a distance arc with the radius prescribed by the side joining that endpoint to the unknown vertex.
  2. Join an off-line intersection to both endpoints. The two off-line placements are reflected congruent copies. If the complete distance circles touch on the base line or do not meet, no triangle results.

e.g. To construct sides 14,10,1714,10,1714,10,17 cm with base 141414, use endpoint radii 101010 and 171717 cm.

Construct from SAS, ASA, or AAS data

  1. For SAS, draw one given side, copy the included angle at its appropriate endpoint, and mark the second length on the new ray before joining the free endpoints.
  2. For ASA, draw the included side and the two endpoint-angle rays into one half-plane. Their intersection is the third vertex; check the construction conditions.
  3. For AAS, check that the positive given angles total less than 180 degrees and calculate the third angle. Use the known side and its endpoint angles as ASA data.

Build an SSA counterexample

  1. Draw the given angle at A. Mark C on one ray at the known adjacent length. Draw a circle centered at C with the other known length as radius.
  2. Retain every intersection beyond A on the other ray. Two intersections give triangles with the same SSA data but different third-side lengths, so they are noncongruent. One intersection gives one triangle; none gives no triangle.

Describe why a rigid-motion match finishes

  1. Translate one base endpoint to its partner and rotate to align the base rays. Equal base lengths match the second endpoints.
  2. If required, reflect in the common base line. State what fixes the remaining vertex: SSS leaves only two reflected placements; SAS fixes a point on a ray by distance; ASA fixes an intersection of two distinct ray lines.

Use the special isosceles vertex segment

  1. Locate the common endpoint of the given equal sides. Check that the stated bisector, median, or altitude starts at that vertex.
  2. Use its other proved roles to halve the base or vertex angle and supply a right angle. Use the triangle sum with two equal base angles when another angle is requested.

Plan a congruence proof through an overlap

  1. Choose two triangles containing the target parts. Redraw them separately if needed, preserving names, given marks, and correspondence.
  2. Justify any shared side, genuinely shared angle, vertical angles, or auxiliary segment. Select a valid criterion and establish the ordered match.
  3. Use CPCTC for the target part. If a second comparison is required, carry the proved equality forward and establish that new ordered match with its own evidence before using CPCTC again.

Transfer an inaccessible measurement

  1. Identify the accessible comparison triangle and its exact matching conditions. Establish congruence rather than relying on appearance.
  2. Find the accessible side corresponding to the target, then use CPCTC to transfer its measurement. Keep approximate field measurements approximate.

e.g. With a shared 313131 m baseline and matching endpoint angles, ASA gives a match; a corresponding accessible side of 474747 m gives an ideal target length of 474747 m.

Justify a copied angle

  1. Keep the radius settings and the transferred chord identifiable. List the two radius equalities and the chord equality for the source and target triangles.
  2. Establish the ordered triangle match by SSS. Use CPCTC for the angles at the source and target vertices, without assuming the desired angle equality.

e.g. With common radius 666 cm and transferred chord 888 cm, both comparison triangles have sides 6,6,86,6,86,6,8 cm, so SSS then CPCTC justifies the copied angle.

Exam traps

  • Trap Matching letters by the drawing’s positions

    Fix Use the ordered correspondence. Rotations and reflections can change every left-right visual cue.

  • Trap Confusing side equality with triangle congruence

    Fix Use === for equal lengths or measures and ≅\cong≅ for congruent segments, angles, or triangles. State a criterion for a whole-triangle match.

  • Trap Calling any two sides and any angle SAS

    Fix Locate the common endpoint of the two known sides. The angle at that endpoint is the included one.

  • Trap Ignoring the side’s endpoints in ASA or AAS

    Fix Match the angle vertices first. Their joining side is included; another corresponding side is nonincluded.

  • Trap Using HL because a triangle looks right

    Fix Establish a right angle in each triangle and identify the side opposite it before checking the hypotenuse and leg pairs. Two equal leg pairs and the included right angle give SAS.

  • Trap Treating insufficient data as proof of noncongruence

    Fix AAA or unrestricted SSA alone leaves congruence undecided. An unequal corresponding side or angle can instead rule out a stated match.

  • Trap Doubling the vertex angle instead of the base angle

    Fix An isosceles triangle has two equal base angles and one vertex angle. Identify those roles from the equal-side markings before using the angle sum.

  • Trap Excluding equilateral triangles from isosceles

    Fix Isosceles means at least two equal sides. An equilateral triangle allows any vertex to play the equal-side vertex role.

  • Trap Giving every median or altitude three roles

    Fix The shared roles require an isosceles triangle and its segment from the vertex between the chosen equal sides.

  • Trap Using the target equality as evidence for its own proof

    Fix Build the triangle congruence from other justified data. CPCTC comes afterward, including separately at each stage of a two-stage proof.

  • Trap Inventing facts after drawing a segment or separating triangles

    Fix Preserve the original givens and supply reasons for every new equality. An auxiliary segment alone supplies neither bisection nor perpendicularity.

  • Trap Assuming a compass copy already has the desired angle

    Fix Use the transferred radii and chord for SSS; the angle equality is the CPCTC conclusion, not an SAS premise.

  • Trap Extending SSS to a four-sided frame

    Fix Four fixed side lengths need not fix the angles. SSS applies to triangles; a diagonal brace adds a side condition to each triangular piece.
