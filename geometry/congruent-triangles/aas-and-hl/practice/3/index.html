AAS, HL, and Invalid Tests: Practice

12 multiple-choice questions, progressively harder.

Level 3 · Challenge 0 / 12 answered
Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    In the HL overlay, why must C, A, and F be collinear after the third vertices are put on opposite sides of AB?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The right-angle givens place both third-vertex rays on the one perpendicular to AB at A. Choosing opposite half-planes makes them opposite rays, with A between C and F.

  2. 2

    Which statement would make the isosceles-based HL proof circular?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Equality of the other legs is a conclusion of HL, not one of its givens. Assuming it would supply the missing equality the proof is supposed to establish.

  3. 3

    Angles A and B in triangle ABC match angles D and E in triangle DEF. A record also says

    AC=EFAC=EFAC=EF

    Why does this record not by itself give AAS under the stated matching?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A corresponds to D, B to E, and C to F. Therefore AC maps to DF. An equality involving the different side EF does not supply the required corresponding side pair.

  4. 4

    Two right triangles are right at Q and S. The given hypotenuses satisfy

    PR=RT.\begin{aligned} PR&=RT. \end{aligned}PR​=RT.​

    The given legs satisfy

    PQ=RS.\begin{aligned} PQ&=RS. \end{aligned}PQ​=RS.​

    Which ordered congruence statement follows?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The right angles match Q to S. The matching legs PQ and RS then match P to R, leaving R of the first triangle matched with T of the second. HL gives

    △PQR≅△RST.\triangle PQR\cong\triangle RST.△PQR≅△RST.
  5. 5

    Triangles ABC and DEF have the following data:

    m∠A=37∘m∠D=37∘m∠B=84∘m∠E=84∘AC=DF\begin{aligned} m\angle A&=37^\circ \\ m\angle D&=37^\circ \\ m\angle B&=84^\circ \\ m\angle E&=84^\circ \\ AC&=DF \end{aligned}m∠Am∠Dm∠Bm∠EAC​=37∘=37∘=84∘=84∘=DF​

    Which pair of angle vertices can be used with the equal side in an ASA proof?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The third angles have the same measure:

    180∘−37∘−84∘=59∘.180^\circ-37^\circ-84^\circ=59^\circ.180∘−37∘−84∘=59∘.

    AC is between angles A and C, and DF is between D and F. Those angle pairs provide the ASA route.

  6. 6

    A construction yields two triangles with

    AC=14CB1=CB2CB1=10\begin{aligned} AC&=14 \\ CB_1&=CB_2 \\ CB_1&=10 \end{aligned}ACCB1​CB1​​=14=CB2​=10​

    and the same angle at A. Their bases AB1 and AB2 are unequal. What is the most direct reason these triangles are noncongruent under the shared-vertex correspondence?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The given equalities cover only two side pairs. The different base lengths violate a necessary condition for congruence.

    AB1≠AB2.AB_1\ne AB_2.AB1​=AB2​.

    The triangles share two side lengths, while their third lengths differ. Their full side-length lists therefore cannot match under a different vertex order either.

  7. 7

    A right triangle has legs 12 and 16. Another right triangle has a leg of 12, but its hypotenuse length is not given. Is the one equal leg pair enough to prove congruence?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    There is only one equal side pair and one equal angle pair. HL needs equal hypotenuses as well; SAS on the legs would need an equal second leg.

  8. 8

    In the HL proof, once both small angles at B have been shown equal, which side pairs complete SAS?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The angle at B lies between AB and BC in one triangle, and between AB and BF in the other. Those side pairs were already known equal.

  9. 9

    Which extra fact is sufficient to turn an AAA comparison into a congruence proof when all three angle pairs have a fixed correspondence?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Choose the matching angles at the endpoints of the added equal side pair. ASA now proves congruence.

  10. 10

    For fixed SSA data, a construction circle touches the given ray at exactly one point beyond its endpoint. How many nondegenerate triangles does that intersection supply?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    One intersection supplies one possible third vertex on the ray. SSA failing as a general guarantee does not mean every construction must have two solutions.

  11. 11

    Could triangles satisfying unrestricted SSA measurements still be congruent?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Insufficient information leaves more than one possibility. It does not forbid a congruent pair from meeting the given equalities.

  12. 12

    Two AAS-congruent triangles have corresponding angles (6u−7)∘(6u-7)^\circ(6u−7)∘ and (3u+20)∘(3u+20)^\circ(3u+20)∘. What is that common angle?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Corresponding angles in congruent triangles match.

    6u−7=3u+20.\begin{aligned} 6u-7&=3u+20. \end{aligned}6u−7​=3u+20.​

    Solving gives

    u=9.\begin{aligned} u&=9. \end{aligned}u​=9.​

    Substitute into the numerical expression:

    6(9)−7=47.\begin{aligned} 6(9)-7&=47. \end{aligned}6(9)−7​=47.​

    The common angle therefore has measure

    Common angle=47∘.\begin{aligned} \text{Common angle}&=47^\circ. \end{aligned}Common angle​=47∘.​
