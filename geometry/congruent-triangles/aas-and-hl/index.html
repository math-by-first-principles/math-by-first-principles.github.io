AAS, HL, and Invalid Tests

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

A list of three matching measurements can look convincing. But which three are they? Two angles and a side can fix a triangle even when that side is not between the angles. Two sides and an angle can leave two different triangles. We need to explain the successful cases and build a counterexample to the tempting shortcut.

Learning goals

  • Derive AAS congruence from the triangle angle sum and ASA
  • Justify HL congruence using the isosceles base-angle theorem
  • Distinguish sufficient congruence information from AAA and unrestricted SSA data
  • Construct two noncongruent triangles satisfying one fixed set of SSA measurements

Two angles and a side not between them

The position of a known side need not stop a two-angle comparison. Suppose triangles ABCABCABC and DEFDEFDEF have the following corresponding measurements:

m∠A=43∘m∠D=43∘m∠B=66∘m∠E=66∘BC=9EF=9.\begin{aligned} m\angle A&=43^\circ\\ m\angle D&=43^\circ\\ m\angle B&=66^\circ\\ m\angle E&=66^\circ\\ BC&=9\\ EF&=9.\end{aligned}m∠Am∠Dm∠Bm∠EBCEF​=43∘=43∘=66∘=66∘=9=9.​

The known side is not between the two given angles.

The third angles match because both are what remains after subtracting the same two measures:

180∘−43∘−66∘=71∘.180^\circ-43^\circ-66^\circ=71^\circ.180∘−43∘−66∘=71∘.

Now use angles at B,CB,CB,C and E,FE,FE,F. The side of length 999 lies between those pairs, so angle-side-angle (ASA) proves the triangles congruent. We changed which angles we used, not which measurements were given.

AAS follows from ASA#

Suppose two angles of one triangle match two corresponding angles of another, and one pair of corresponding nonincluded sides has equal lengths.

The third-angle theorem makes the remaining angles congruent. Thus all three angle pairs are available.

Take the two angles at the endpoints of the given equal sides. Those are corresponding angles, with the given equal sides between them. ASA proves the triangles congruent.

∎

This is angle-angle-side (AAS): two corresponding angles and a corresponding side not between them establish congruence. As in the earlier criteria, the correspondence must stay consistent. We use nondegenerate triangles throughout: three noncollinear vertices and positive side lengths.

Worked example 1 Identify the side after matching the angles

Problem. In △JKL\triangle JKL△JKL and △RST\triangle RST△RST,

∠J≅∠R∠L≅∠TJK=15 cmRS=15 cm.\begin{aligned} \angle J&\cong\angle R \\ \angle L&\cong\angle T \\ JK&=15\text{ cm} \\ RS&=15\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}∠J∠LJKRS​≅∠R≅∠T=15 cm=15 cm.​

Decide whether congruence follows and write the ordered statement.

Solution. The angles fix the first two vertex partners, and the remaining vertices must match:

J↔R.L↔T.K↔S.\begin{aligned} J&\leftrightarrow R. \\ L&\leftrightarrow T. \\ K&\leftrightarrow S.\end{aligned}JLK​↔R.↔T.↔S.​

The known side JKJKJK corresponds to RSRSRS under that same matching.

For the angles at J,LJ,LJ,L, the included side would be JLJLJL. The known side JKJKJK is nonincluded, so AAS gives

△JKL≅△RST.\triangle JKL\cong\triangle RST.△JKL≅△RST.

Check your understanding

Two triangles have matching angles of 52 and 73 degrees and matching sides that are not between those angles. What lets you replace AAS with ASA?

Answer choices

Subtract the same two angles from 180 degrees in both triangles.

180∘−52∘−73∘=55∘.180^\circ-52^\circ-73^\circ=55^\circ.180∘−52∘−73∘=55∘.

The third pair then allows the known side to be used between two matching angles.

A right angle changes the two-side case

In a right triangle, the side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse, and the two sides meeting at the right angle are its legs. A right angle can make one hypotenuse and one leg enough to settle a congruence comparison.

The right angles are required information. They may be stated, marked, or established from perpendicular lines. A corner that merely looks square supplies no given.

For example, take two right triangles, each with a hypotenuse of length 171717 and a corresponding leg of length 888. Move one so the legs of length 888 coincide, with both right-angle vertices at AAA, placing their third vertices on opposite sides of that leg. In the diagram, those vertices are CCC and FFF, with the common leg ABABAB.

The hypotenuses BCBCBC and BFBFBF, both 171717, make the large triangle BCFBCFBCF isosceles. Its base angles at C,FC,FC,F match. Each small triangle also has a right angle at AAA, so their remaining angles at BBB match by the third-angle theorem. The known lengths are

AB=8BC=17BF=17.\begin{aligned} AB&=8\\ BC&=17\\ BF&=17.\end{aligned}ABBCBF​=8=17=17.​

The equal angles at BBB are included between these sides, so SAS applies.

This works without calculating the other leg. Neither 888 nor 171717 is essential to the argument.

The hypotenuse-leg criterion (HL) says that two right triangles are congruent when their hypotenuses and one pair of corresponding legs have equal lengths.

HL from the isosceles base-angle theorem#

Let △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC and △DEF\triangle DEF△DEF have right angles at A,DA,DA,D, with

AB=DEBC=EF.\begin{aligned} AB&=DE \\ BC&=EF.\end{aligned}ABBC​=DE=EF.​

Translate and rotate the second triangle so DDD lands on AAA and EEE on BBB. If necessary, reflect across ABABAB so its third vertex, now named F′F'F′, is on the opposite side of ABABAB from CCC. Rigid motions preserve all the given lengths and angles.

Both ACACAC and AF′AF'AF′ are perpendicular to ABABAB at AAA. They lie on the same perpendicular line in opposite directions, so C,A,F′C,A,F'C,A,F′ are collinear with AAA between the other points.

The equal hypotenuses give

BC=BF′.BC=BF'.BC=BF′.

The base-angle theorem in △BCF′\triangle BCF'△BCF′ gives equal angles at CCC and F′F'F′. These are the angles at the third vertices of the two right triangles.

Their right angles also match, so their remaining angles ∠ABC\angle ABC∠ABC and ∠ABF′\angle ABF'∠ABF′ are congruent. Use

AB=ABBC=BF′.\begin{aligned} AB&=AB \\ BC&=BF'.\end{aligned}ABBC​=AB=BF′.​

Together with those included angles, SAS gives

△ABC≅△ABF′.\begin{aligned} \triangle ABC&\cong\triangle ABF'.\end{aligned}△ABC​≅△ABF′.​

Reversing the alignment gives

△ABC≅△DEF.\triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF.△ABC≅△DEF.

∎

Check your understanding

In this lesson's HL overlay, the right triangles share AB and have equal hypotenuses BC and BF. Which reason makes the angles at C and F equal before the angles at B are known to match?

Answer choices

The equal hypotenuses make triangle BCF isosceles. Its equal opposite base angles supply the matching angles at C and F. Only afterward do the equal right angles let the third-angle theorem establish the angles at B.

Worked example 2 Check all three HL conditions

Problem. Triangles PQRPQRPQR and XYZXYZXYZ have right angles at Q,YQ,YQ,Y. Given

PR=XZPQ=XYQR=22 cm.\begin{aligned} PR&=XZ \\ PQ&=XY \\ QR&=22\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}PRPQQR​=XZ=XY=22 cm.​

Decide whether congruence follows and find YZYZYZ.

Solution. The sides opposite the right angles are PR,XZPR,XZPR,XZ, so the first equality matches the hypotenuses. The second equality matches the legs PQ,XYPQ,XYPQ,XY. Both right angles are given.

HL gives the ordered congruence

△PQR≅△XYZ.\begin{aligned} \triangle PQR&\cong\triangle XYZ.\end{aligned}△PQR​≅△XYZ.​

The right-angle vertices match:

Q↔Y.\begin{aligned} Q&\leftrightarrow Y.\end{aligned}Q​↔Y.​

The other legs correspond, so

YZ=QRQR=22 cmYZ=22 cm.\begin{aligned} YZ&=QR \\ QR&=22\text{ cm} \\ YZ&=22\text{ cm} .\end{aligned}YZQRYZ​=QR=22 cm=22 cm.​

Check your understanding

Two triangles have two pairs of equal corresponding sides. Before trying to use HL, which condition must be known about both triangles?

Answer choices

HL is a criterion for right triangles. Check an explicit right angle or a proved perpendicular relationship in each triangle before identifying its hypotenuse and checking the required side pairs.

Three matching angles do not fix the size

Equal angles leave room for different side lengths. Construct an equilateral triangle with side length 777, then another with side length 181818. Every angle in both triangles measures 60∘60^\circ60∘, but a rigid motion cannot turn a side of length 777 into one of length 181818.

Thus angle-angle-angle (AAA) is not a congruence criterion. It can hold for triangles that happen to be congruent, but it does not guarantee congruence.

Check your understanding

Two triangles have three matching angle pairs. Which conclusion is justified about congruence?

Answer choices

Equilateral triangles of unequal side lengths give a counterexample to guaranteed congruence. Equal-size equilateral triangles show that AAA information does not rule congruence out either.

The nonincluded angle can leave two triangles

Side-side-angle (SSA) gives two side lengths and an angle that is not between those sides. Unlike SAS, it is not sufficient in general.

Here is one fixed construction. Draw a ray from AAA. From AAA, draw a second ray making 30∘30^\circ30∘ with it and mark CCC at distance 101010 from AAA. Draw a circle centered at CCC with radius 777. It crosses the first ray at two points, B1B_1B1​ and B2B_2B2​.

Both triangles have the same recorded lengths:

AC=10CB1=7CB2=7.\begin{aligned} AC&=10 \\ CB_1&=7 \\ CB_2&=7.\end{aligned}ACCB1​CB2​​=10=7=7.​

The two radius lengths are equal because they come from one circle. The angle at AAA is also shared. That angle lies between ACACAC and the unknown base, not between the two given sides. The two distinct points on the same ray have different distances from AAA, so

AB1≠AB2.AB_1\ne AB_2.AB1​=AB2​.

The triangles share two side lengths but differ in the third, so their three-side lists cannot match under any vertex order. They are noncongruent.

This is a counterexample from a particular compass construction, not a general test for how many intersections every set of measurements has. SSA data can give no triangle, one triangle, or two. HL is a valid restricted case: the angle opposite the hypotenuse is known to be right, and the proof above settles that case.

Two matching side pairs and a nonincluded matching angle do not supply that right-angle condition. Without it, the general SSA ambiguity can remain.

Worked example 3 SAS or SSA depends on where the angle is

Problem. Compare each pair of nondegenerate triangles with the stated correspondence

A↔DB↔EC↔F.\begin{aligned} A&\leftrightarrow D \\ B&\leftrightarrow E \\ C&\leftrightarrow F.\end{aligned}ABC​↔D↔E↔F.​

In both cases

AB=DEAC=DF.\begin{aligned} AB&=DE \\ AC&=DF.\end{aligned}ABAC​=DE=DF.​

Case A. Also

∠BAC≅∠EDF.\angle BAC\cong\angle EDF.∠BAC≅∠EDF.

Does congruence follow?

Case B. Instead, only ∠ABC≅∠DEF\angle ABC\cong\angle DEF∠ABC≅∠DEF is known, with no right angles specified. Does this information guarantee congruence?

Solution. Case A. The equal angles are between the two given side pairs. SAS gives

△ABC≅△DEF.\triangle ABC\cong\triangle DEF.△ABC≅△DEF.

Case B. The angles are at B,EB,EB,E, while the two given sides meet at A,DA,DA,D. This is unrestricted SSA. The circle construction gives a counterexample to its being a general criterion, so these data do not guarantee congruence.

Case B does not say that this particular pair must be noncongruent. It says the supplied information does not decide.

Check your understanding

In the two-intersection construction, why do both choices of B preserve the given length CB?

Answer choices

A circle is defined by distance from its center. Both intersection points are exactly the radius away from C. Their different distances from A are what prevent congruence.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Ignoring correspondence. Two equal angles and a side work only when that side belongs to the same vertex matching.
  • Using HL without right angles. A drawing’s appearance cannot supply the condition that makes the theorem true.
  • Treating AAA as a size condition. Equal angles do not specify any side length.
  • Reading insufficient as impossible. AAA and unrestricted SSA do not guarantee congruence; they do not forbid it.
  • Renaming SSA as SAS. The included angle is at the common endpoint of the two named sides.

Key takeaways

  • AAS: two corresponding angles and a corresponding nonincluded side establish congruence through ASA.
  • HL: right angles, equal hypotenuses, and one equal corresponding leg establish congruence.
  • AAA: matching angle measures alone leave the lengths undecided.
  • SSA: two sides and a nonincluded angle are insufficient in general; use a valid criterion or additional conditions.

A bit of history (optional)

Euclid did not divide every triangle comparison into the alphabetic labels used in modern textbooks. In Proposition I.26 of the Elements, he treats two ways a known side can sit beside two known angles: between them, or opposite one of them.

The proof handles the cases separately. Euclid temporarily assumes that a side is too long, cuts off a shorter segment, and compares the smaller triangle with the other given triangle. The assumption eventually forces an impossible angle relationship. This is an indirect argument: the unwanted alternative is removed rather than measured away.

The placement of the known side changes his route through the proof, even though both cases reach the same conclusion. Today’s separate names ASA and AAS make that difference visible before the proof begins.

Euclid I.26 in David Joyce’s edition preserves both cases in one proposition.
