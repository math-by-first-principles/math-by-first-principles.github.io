    Four consecutive arcs fill a circle. Their measures in degrees are x+20x+20x+20, 2x2x2x, 3x−103x-103x−10, and 110110110. Find xxx.

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    All four measures add to 360 degrees.

    6x+120=360,6x=240,x=40.\begin{aligned}6x+120&=360,\\6x&=240,\\x&=40.\end{aligned}6x+1206xx​=360,=240,=40.​

    The arc measures are 60, 80, 110, and 110 degrees. They are positive and total 360 degrees.

    The major arc ADB^\widehat{ADB}ADB measures 276∘276^\circ276∘. What is the smaller central angle ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

  3. 3

    In one circle, chords CDCDCD and EFEFEF are equally far from the center. Their lengths in cm are 3x+23x+23x+2 and 5x−145x-145x−14. Find xxx.

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Equal perpendicular center distances in one circle give equal chords.

    3x+2=5x−14,16=2x,x=8.\begin{aligned}3x+2&=5x-14,\\16&=2x,\\x&=8.\end{aligned}3x+216x​=5x−14,=2x,=8.​

    Both lengths then equal 26 cm.

  4. 4

    Equal chords in congruent circles have minor arc measures (8x−1)∘(8x-1)^\circ(8x−1)∘ and (5x+20)∘(5x+20)^\circ(5x+20)∘. Find the common minor arc measure.

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The equal-radius hypothesis permits equal minor arc measures.

    8x−1=5x+20,3x=21,x=7.\begin{aligned}8x-1&=5x+20,\\3x&=21,\\x&=7.\end{aligned}8x−13xx​=5x+20,=21,=7.​

    8(7)−1=55.8(7)-1=55.8(7)−1=55.

    The common minor arc measure is 55 degrees.

  5. 5

    A chord is 16 units long in a circle of radius 17 units. Find its perpendicular center distance.

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Half the chord is 8 units. The radius is the hypotenuse.

    d2=172−82,d2=225,d=15.\begin{aligned}d^2&=17^2-8^2,\\d^2&=225,\\d&=15.\end{aligned}d2d2d​=172−82,=225,=15.​

    Use the nonnegative distance.

  6. 6

    A circle has radius 61\sqrt{61}61​ cm. A chord is 555 cm from its center. Find the chord's full length.

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The half chord is the other leg of a right triangle.

    h2=61−25,h=6.\begin{aligned}h^2&=61-25,\\h&=6.\end{aligned}h2h​=61−25,=6.​

    The full chord is twice 6 cm, or 12 cm.

    Solution

  9. 9

    A circle has radius 26 units. Chord AB is 10 units from its center. Find AB.

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Use perpendicular bisection before Pythagoras.

    h2=262−102,h2=576,h=24.\begin{aligned}h^2&=26^2-10^2,\\h^2&=576,\\h&=24.\end{aligned}h2h2h​=262−102,=576,=24.​

    The full chord has length 48 units.

  10. 10

    Two chords in one circle of radius 130\sqrt{130}130​ cm have the same perpendicular center distance. One is 222222 cm long. Find the other chord's length.

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The radius would matter for computing the common distance, but it is not needed for this equality transfer.

  11. 11

    For a circle of radius 6 cm, a line is 6 cm from the center. Which statement correctly describes a possible chord on that line?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    A chord needs two distinct endpoints. A radius and a half chord would have to satisfy

    h2=62−62,h2=0.\begin{aligned}h^2&=6^2-6^2,\\h^2&=0.\end{aligned}h2h2​=62−62,=0.​

    Zero separation cannot give distinct endpoints, so there is no genuine chord.

  12. 12

    A translation followed by a positive dilation sends each point of a radius-r circle onto a point of a radius-s circle. Both radii are positive. Which check completes the proof that the whole target circle is reached?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Take any point of the target circle. First apply the inverse dilation, then undo the translation.

    The inverse dilation restores the original radius.

    s(rs)=r.s\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)=r.s(sr​)=r.

    The inverse translation returns the center as well. Every target point therefore has a preimage on the original circle. Checking only selected radii, chords, or a drawing does not establish coverage of all target points.
