Angles Inside and Outside Circles

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Watch the video explainer

Intuition

Two straight bars can meet inside a circular frame, on its rim, or beyond it. Each position makes a different angle rule. The reason comes from ordinary triangles: joining two carefully chosen points on the frame turns the hidden angle into a sum or difference of angles we already know.

Learning goals

  • Identify the arc endpoints for inside, on-circle, and outside angles
  • Justify the intersecting-chord half-sum using inscribed angles and a triangle
  • Derive exterior angle rules for secants and tangents from earlier angle facts
  • Calculate angles and missing arcs while keeping the chosen rays fixed
  • Reconstruct a full circle from linked inside and outside information

Locate the vertex before choosing arcs

An angle inside a circle combines two arcs. An angle outside compares two arcs. An angle on the circle uses one arc, as in Inscribed Angles.

These are separate, matched configurations. They are schematic, not successive frames of one moving construction. Every named circle point is distinct, and every angle here is an ordinary angle between specified rays, between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘.

Inside: two chords cross

If arcs ABABAB and CDCDCD measure 70∘70^\circ70∘ and 110∘110^\circ110∘, their half-angles add to 90∘90^\circ90∘. The other two consecutive arcs together measure 180∘180^\circ180∘, completing the circle.

On: one intercepted arc

An inscribed angle intercepting an arc of 140∘140^\circ140∘ measures 70∘70^\circ70∘. Its vertex must be excluded from that arc; the other arc completes 360∘360^\circ360∘.

Outside: two rays point into the circle

The near arc joins the first intersections A,DA,DA,D without passing through B,CB,CB,C. The far arc joins the second intersections B,CB,CB,C without passing through A,DA,DA,D. These names come from ray order, not from left and right on the page.

For far arc BCBCBC of 158∘158^\circ158∘ and near arc DADADA of 42∘42^\circ42∘, the difference of their half-angles is 58∘58^\circ58∘. The other two consecutive arcs total 160∘160^\circ160∘, so the selected arcs fit one full circle.

Why intersecting chords use a half-sum

Let A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D occur in that order around a circle. Chords ACACAC and BDBDBD cross at an interior point PPP. For ∠APB\angle APB∠APB, use arc ABABAB excluding C,DC,DC,D and arc CDCDCD excluding A,BA,BA,B. They face this angle and its vertical angle, not its adjacent angle.

The intersecting-chord angle rule#

With arc ABABAB of 82∘82^\circ82∘ and arc CDCDCD of 116∘116^\circ116∘, the inscribed angles PDAPDAPDA and PADPADPAD measure 41∘41^\circ41∘ and 58∘58^\circ58∘. The exterior-angle theorem adds them, giving 99∘99^\circ99∘. The remaining two arcs total 162∘162^\circ162∘ and may each be 81∘81^\circ81∘.

Write aaa for the degree measure of the selected arc ABABAB, and ccc for that of CDCDCD. Since PPP lies on ACACAC and BDBDBD, the angle PADPADPAD is the inscribed angle CADCADCAD, and PDAPDAPDA is the inscribed angle BDABDABDA.

m∠PAD=c/2,m∠PDA=a/2.\begin{aligned} m\angle PAD&=c/2,\\ m\angle PDA&=a/2. \end{aligned}m∠PADm∠PDA​=c/2,=a/2.​

Ray PBPBPB extends side PDPDPD of triangle APDAPDAPD. The earlier triangle exterior-angle theorem gives

m∠APB=a/2+c/2,m∠APB=(a+c)/2.\begin{aligned} m\angle APB&=a/2+c/2,\\ m\angle APB&=(a+c)/2. \end{aligned}m∠APBm∠APB​=a/2+c/2,=(a+c)/2.​

Nothing depended on 828282 or 116116116. The proof requires two chords crossing strictly inside, with the selected arcs facing a vertical pair. Applying the same argument to ∠BPC\angle BPC∠BPC uses the other two arcs and covers the adjacent, possibly acute or obtuse, angle.

∎

Worked example 1 Choose the vertical pair, then its neighbor

Problem. Points A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D occur in order around a circle. Chords ACACAC and BDBDBD meet at PPP. The consecutive arcs have the following measures. Find m∠APBm\angle APBm∠APB and m∠BPCm\angle BPCm∠BPC.

m(AB^)=78∘,m(BC^)=64∘,m(CD^)=132∘,m(DA^)=86∘.\begin{gathered} m(\widehat{AB})=78^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{BC})=64^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{CD})=132^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{DA})=86^\circ. \end{gathered}m(AB)=78∘,m(BC)=64∘,m(CD)=132∘,m(DA)=86∘.​

Solution. The positive arcs total 360∘360^\circ360∘. Angle APBAPBAPB uses ABABAB and CDCDCD.

m∠APB=(78∘+132∘)/2,m∠APB=105∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle APB&=(78^\circ+132^\circ)/2,\\ m\angle APB&=105^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠APBm∠APB​=(78∘+132∘)/2,=105∘.​

The adjacent angle uses BCBCBC and DADADA.

m∠BPC=(64∘+86∘)/2,m∠BPC=75∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle BPC&=(64^\circ+86^\circ)/2,\\ m\angle BPC&=75^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠BPCm∠BPC​=(64∘+86∘)/2,=75∘.​

Their sum is 180∘180^\circ180∘, matching the straight angle along ACACAC.

Answer choices

The selected arcs face the angle and its vertical angle. Their half-sum gives

m∠APB=(55∘+101∘)/2,m∠APB=78∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle APB&=(55^\circ+101^\circ)/2,\\m\angle APB&=78^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠APBm∠APB​=(55∘+101∘)/2,=78∘.​

Why two secants use a half-difference

Let PPP be strictly outside, with ray orders P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B and P,D,CP,D,CP,D,C. The four circle points occur in order A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D. Join BBB to DDD.

The two-secant exterior angle rule#

Take far arc BCBCBC of 172∘172^\circ172∘ and near arc DADADA of 36∘36^\circ36∘. Inscribed angle BDCBDCBDC is 86∘86^\circ86∘; inscribed angle PBDPBDPBD, also ABDABDABD, is 18∘18^\circ18∘. The exterior angle BDCBDCBDC equals the angle at PPP plus the angle at BBB, so the angle at PPP is 68∘68^\circ68∘. The other arcs may each measure 76∘76^\circ76∘, making a positive full circle.

Let the far arc BCBCBC have degree measure fff, and the near arc DADADA have measure nnn. The inscribed-angle theorem gives

m∠BDC=f/2,m∠PBD=n/2.\begin{aligned} m\angle BDC&=f/2,\\ m\angle PBD&=n/2. \end{aligned}m∠BDCm∠PBD​=f/2,=n/2.​

Because P,D,CP,D,CP,D,C are in that order, BDCBDCBDC is exterior to triangle PBDPBDPBD.

f/2=m∠APD+n/2,m∠APD=(f−n)/2.\begin{aligned} f/2&=m\angle APD+n/2,\\ m\angle APD&=(f-n)/2. \end{aligned}f/2m∠APD​=m∠APD+n/2,=(f−n)/2.​

The numbers 172172172 and 363636 only illustrated these steps. The ray orders force the far arc to exceed the near arc. The rule measures the angle between the two rays pointing toward the circle. Reversing just one ray gives its supplement, not the same angle.

∎

The far arc can be major. Name its route explicitly rather than assuming every two-letter arc is minor. In the formulas, fff always names the selected route between the far endpoints, even when that route exceeds 180∘180^\circ180∘.

Worked example 2 A major far arc

Problem. Two secants have orders P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B and P,D,CP,D,CP,D,C. Around the circle, the consecutive arcs AB,BC,CD,DAAB,BC,CD,DAAB,BC,CD,DA measure 44∘,206∘,60∘,50∘44^\circ,206^\circ,60^\circ,50^\circ44∘,206∘,60∘,50∘. Arc BCBCBC here means the route excluding A,DA,DA,D. Identify the near and far arcs and find m∠APDm\angle APDm∠APD.

Solution. Near arc DADADA excludes B,CB,CB,C and measures 50∘50^\circ50∘. Far arc BCBCBC excludes A,DA,DA,D and is a major arc of 206∘206^\circ206∘. All four positive arcs total 360∘360^\circ360∘.

m∠APD=(206∘−50∘)/2,m∠APD=78∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle APD&=(206^\circ-50^\circ)/2,\\ m\angle APD&=78^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠APDm∠APD​=(206∘−50∘)/2,=78∘.​

Check your understanding

Secant ray orders are P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B and P,D,CP,D,CP,D,C. The far arc BCBCBC excluding A,DA,DA,D measures 164∘164^\circ164∘; near arc DADADA excluding B,CB,CB,C measures 38∘38^\circ38∘. Find m∠APDm\angle APDm∠APD.

Answer choices

m∠APD=(164∘−38∘)/2,m∠APD=63∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle APD&=(164^\circ-38^\circ)/2,\\m\angle APD&=63^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠APDm∠APD​=(164∘−38∘)/2,=63∘.​

A tangent and a secant

The same subtraction works when one ray is tangent. Let PTPTPT touch at TTT and let the secant order be P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B. Near arc TATATA excludes BBB. Far arc BTBTBT excludes AAA. The third arc is the route from AAA to BBB excluding TTT.

The tangent-secant exterior angle rule#

For near arc TATATA of 48∘48^\circ48∘ and far arc BTBTBT of 168∘168^\circ168∘, the tangent-chord theorem gives 24∘24^\circ24∘ at TTT. Inscribed angle TABTABTAB is 84∘84^\circ84∘. Since APAPAP is opposite ABABAB, this is exterior to triangle PTAPTAPTA, leaving 60∘60^\circ60∘ at PPP. The remaining arc ABABAB is 144∘144^\circ144∘.

Let near arc TATATA have degree measure nnn, and far arc BTBTBT have measure fff, using the routes just specified. The tangent-chord theorem for ray TPTPTP and chord TATATA gives n/2n/2n/2 at TTT. The inscribed-angle theorem gives f/2f/2f/2 for angle TABTABTAB.

Angle TABTABTAB is exterior to triangle PTAPTAPTA, so

f/2=m∠TPA+n/2,m∠TPA=(f−n)/2.\begin{aligned} f/2&=m\angle TPA+n/2,\\ m\angle TPA&=(f-n)/2. \end{aligned}f/2m∠TPA​=m∠TPA+n/2,=(f−n)/2.​

The numerical argument used these same ray choices. Reflecting the picture changes no step. A major far arc makes TABTABTAB obtuse, but the exterior-angle reasoning still applies. Strict exterior PPP, a genuine tangent at TTT, and two distinct secant intersections are required.

∎

Worked example 3 An obtuse tangent-secant angle

Problem. Segment PTPTPT is tangent at TTT, and a secant has order P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B. Near arc TATATA excluding BBB measures 40∘40^\circ40∘. Far arc BTBTBT excluding AAA measures 226∘226^\circ226∘. Find m∠TPAm\angle TPAm∠TPA and m∠PATm\angle PATm∠PAT, and check the remaining arc.

Solution. The remaining arc ABABAB excluding TTT is 94∘94^\circ94∘, positive. The three selected arcs total 360∘360^\circ360∘.

m∠TPA=(226∘−40∘)/2,m∠TPA=93∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle TPA&=(226^\circ-40^\circ)/2,\\ m\angle TPA&=93^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠TPAm∠TPA​=(226∘−40∘)/2,=93∘.​

Angle TABTABTAB intercepts the far arc and is 113∘113^\circ113∘. Its supplement is the requested angle at AAA.

m∠PAT=180∘−113∘,m∠PAT=67∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle PAT&=180^\circ-113^\circ,\\ m\angle PAT&=67^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠PATm∠PAT​=180∘−113∘,=67∘.​

The angle at TTT is 20∘20^\circ20∘; the triangle check is 93∘+67∘+20∘=180∘93^\circ+67^\circ+20^\circ=180^\circ93∘+67∘+20∘=180∘.

Two tangents and both angle sizes

Let rays PT,PUPT,PUPT,PU touch the same circle at distinct T,UT,UT,U, with PPP strictly outside. The near arc is the minor arc TUTUTU facing PPP; choose RRR on the other arc to name the far arc TRUTRUTRU.

If the minor arc is 118∘118^\circ118∘, each tangent-chord base angle of triangle PTUPTUPTU is 59∘59^\circ59∘. The angle at PPP is 62∘62^\circ62∘. The far arc is 242∘242^\circ242∘, whose excess over the near arc is 124∘124^\circ124∘, twice that angle.

The two-tangent exterior angle rule#

First use a minor arc of 134∘134^\circ134∘. Both tangent-chord base angles of triangle PTUPTUPTU measure 67∘67^\circ67∘, leaving 46∘46^\circ46∘ at PPP. The major arc is 226∘226^\circ226∘, and half its difference from 134∘134^\circ134∘ is also 46∘46^\circ46∘.

Now let the minor arc measure nnn, strictly between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘. The two contact points are distinct and not diametrically opposite: tangents at opposite diameter endpoints would be parallel and could not meet at finite PPP. Both tangent-chord base angles select the near arc and measure n/2n/2n/2.

The triangle sum gives

m∠TPU=180∘−n,m(TRU^)=360∘−n,(360∘−n)−n2=180∘−n.\begin{aligned} m\angle TPU&=180^\circ-n,\\ m(\widehat{TRU})&=360^\circ-n,\\ \frac{(360^\circ-n)-n}{2}&=180^\circ-n. \end{aligned}m∠TPUm(TRU)2(360∘−n)−n​​=180∘−n,=360∘−n,=180∘−n.​

Thus the two-tangent angle is half the far-minus-near arc difference too. The choice of 134∘134^\circ134∘ was unnecessary. The angle is acute when n>90∘n>90^\circn>90∘, right when n=90∘n=90^\circn=90∘, and obtuse when n<90∘n<90^\circn<90∘.

∎

Worked example 4 Recover both arcs from an obtuse angle

Problem. Rays PTPTPT and PUPUPU are tangent to one circle at distinct points. Given m∠TPU=104∘m\angle TPU=104^\circm∠TPU=104∘, find the minor arc TUTUTU and major arc TRUTRUTRU, where RRR is on the major arc.

Solution. The near minor arc and the angle at PPP sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

m(TU^)=180∘−104∘,m(TU^)=76∘.\begin{aligned} m(\widehat{TU})&=180^\circ-104^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{TU})&=76^\circ. \end{aligned}m(TU)m(TU)​=180∘−104∘,=76∘.​

The other arc completes the circle.

m(TRU^)=360∘−76∘,m(TRU^)=284∘.\begin{aligned} m(\widehat{TRU})&=360^\circ-76^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{TRU})&=284^\circ. \end{aligned}m(TRU)m(TRU)​=360∘−76∘,=284∘.​

Both arcs are positive, the minor arc is below 180∘180^\circ180∘, and half their difference is the given 104∘104^\circ104∘.

Reconstruct the whole circle

Use the vertex position to choose a rule, name its arc routes, and only then write an equation. When several rules share one circle, finish by checking every consecutive arc is positive and their sum is 360∘360^\circ360∘.

Worked example 5 Link an inside sum to an outside difference

Problem. Points A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D occur in order around a circle. Chords AC,BDAC,BDAC,BD meet at XXX. Lines AB,DCAB,DCAB,DC meet at external PPP with orders P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B and P,D,CP,D,CP,D,C. The angle AXBAXBAXB measures 92∘92^\circ92∘, the angle APDAPDAPD measures 36∘36^\circ36∘, and consecutive arc ABABAB measures 81∘81^\circ81∘. Find the other three consecutive arc measures. Here arc BCBCBC excludes A,DA,DA,D and arc DADADA excludes B,CB,CB,C.

Solution. The inside angle uses arcs AB,CDAB,CDAB,CD. Double it before subtracting arc ABABAB.

m(CD^)=2(92∘)−81∘,m(CD^)=103∘.\begin{aligned} m(\widehat{CD})&=2(92^\circ)-81^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{CD})&=103^\circ. \end{aligned}m(CD)m(CD)​=2(92∘)−81∘,=103∘.​

The remaining arcs BC,DABC,DABC,DA total 176∘176^\circ176∘. Their difference is twice the exterior angle, 72∘72^\circ72∘. Let their degree measures be b,db,db,d.

b+d=176,b−d=72,2b=248,b=124,d=52.\begin{aligned} b+d&=176,\\ b-d&=72,\\ 2b&=248,\\ b&=124,\\ d&=52. \end{aligned}b+db−d2bbd​=176,=72,=248,=124,=52.​

The four positive arc measures are 81∘,124∘,103∘,52∘81^\circ,124^\circ,103^\circ,52^\circ81∘,124∘,103∘,52∘, totaling 360∘360^\circ360∘. The far arc exceeds the near arc, as the specified secant orders require. Both original angle equations hold.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a rule from the lines alone. Locate the vertex inside, on, or outside the circle first.
  • Adding adjacent arcs inside. Use arcs facing the angle and its vertical angle.
  • Guessing near and far. Trace each ray from the external vertex to identify its first and second intersections.
  • Always choosing a minor arc. A far arc may be major; name the route by excluded points or a third point.
  • Accepting algebra without geometry. Check positive arcs, a 360∘360^\circ360∘ total, the stated point order, and the actual rays.

Key takeaways

  • Inside: a chord angle is half the sum of the arcs facing its vertical pair.
  • On: an inscribed or tangent-chord angle is half its selected intercepted arc.
  • Outside: rays toward the circle give half the far-minus-near arc difference for two secants, a tangent and a secant, or two tangents.
  • Two tangents: the angle and minor contact arc sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘; the angle can be obtuse.
  • Name endpoints and routes before calculating, then verify the whole circle.

A bit of history (optional)

Euclid’s Book III discusses circles through a sequence of short propositions. In Proposition 32, a tangent and a chord create two different angles at their contact point. Euclid treats both: after establishing one angle, he uses supplementary angles to obtain the other. The old phrase “alternate segment” names the part of the circle on the other side of the chord, where a matching inscribed angle sits.

His diagram labels the opposite tangent directions separately, rather than leaving “the tangent angle” unnamed. The two directions mattered in the ancient statement just as they do in a modern circle diagram. Read Proposition 32 in David Joyce’s edition of the Elements.
