  1. Problem 1 A vertical pair inside a circle

    Points A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D occur in that order around a circle. Chords AC,BDAC,BDAC,BD cross at interior PPP. Consecutive arcs AB,CDAB,CDAB,CD measure 64∘,118∘64^\circ,118^\circ64∘,118∘. Find m∠APBm\angle APBm∠APB and m∠CPDm\angle CPDm∠CPD.

    Text description of this figure

    Chords AC and BD cross at interior P; blue arc AB and dashed red arc CD face vertical angles. Schematic: incidence and point order are exact; use stated measures, not proportions.

    1. Hint 1

      Locate the two arcs facing the vertical pair at P.

    2. Hint 2

      Add their measures, then divide by two. Vertical angles are equal.

    Answer

    m∠APB=91∘,m∠CPD=91∘.\begin{gathered}m\angle APB=91^\circ,\\m\angle CPD=91^\circ.\end{gathered}m∠APB=91∘,m∠CPD=91∘.​

    Full solution

    Angle APB uses consecutive arcs AB and CD.

    Their half-sum is

    (64∘+118∘)/2=91∘.(64^\circ+118^\circ)/2=91^\circ.(64∘+118∘)/2=91∘.

    Angle CPD is vertical to APB, so it also measures 91∘91^\circ91∘.

    The unspecified arcs BC and DA have a positive total of 178∘178^\circ178∘; for instance, each can measure 89∘89^\circ89∘.

    Answer

    m∠APB=91∘,m∠CPD=91∘.\begin{gathered}m\angle APB=91^\circ,\\m\angle CPD=91^\circ.\end{gathered}m∠APB=91∘,m∠CPD=91∘.​

  4. Problem 4 An obtuse angle between tangents

    m(TU^)=180∘−108∘.m(\widehat{TU})=180^\circ-108^\circ.m(TU)=180∘−108∘.

  5. Problem 5 Explain the inside half-sum

    Points A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D occur in that order around a circle. Chords AC,BDAC,BDAC,BD cross at interior PPP. Let aaa be the degree measure of consecutive arc ABABAB and ccc that of consecutive arc CDCDCD. Prove m∠APB=(a+c)/2m\angle APB=(a+c)/2m∠APB=(a+c)/2 by joining AAA to DDD. Name the two inscribed angles and the triangle exterior angle you use.

    Text description of this figure

    Chords AC and BD cross at interior P; blue arc AB and dashed red arc CD face vertical angles. Schematic: incidence and point order are exact; use stated measures, not proportions.

    1. Hint 1

      P lies on both chords, so replace angle sides through P by rays to their circle endpoints.

    2. Hint 2

      In triangle APD, PB extends PD. Identify the two remote interior angles.

    Answer

    m∠APB=(a+c)/2m\angle APB=(a+c)/2m∠APB=(a+c)/2, using inscribed angles CAD,BDACAD,BDACAD,BDA and the exterior angle APBAPBAPB of triangle APDAPDAPD.

    Full solution

    Since A,P,C are collinear in that order, angle PAD is angle CAD.

    It intercepts consecutive arc CD, so

    m∠PAD=c/2.m\angle PAD=c/2.m∠PAD=c/2.

    Since D,P,B are collinear in that order, angle PDA is angle BDA.

    It intercepts consecutive arc AB, so

    m∠PDA=a/2.m\angle PDA=a/2.m∠PDA=a/2.

    Ray PB is opposite PD, making APB an exterior angle of triangle APD.

    The triangle exterior-angle theorem gives

    m∠APB=a/2+c/2.m\angle APB=a/2+c/2.m∠APB=a/2+c/2.

    Combining the fractions gives the required half-sum.

    The argument uses distinct circle points with alternating chord endpoints and an intersection strictly inside.

    Answer

    m∠APB=(a+c)/2m\angle APB=(a+c)/2m∠APB=(a+c)/2, using inscribed angles CAD,BDACAD,BDACAD,BDA and the exterior angle APBAPBAPB of triangle APDAPDAPD.

    Key idea

    An auxiliary chord turns a circle sum into a triangle exterior-angle sum.
