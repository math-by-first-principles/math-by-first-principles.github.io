Circle Geometry: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Circle, radius, chord, and diameter
A circle is the boundary of points at positive distance rrr from center OOO. A chord joins two distinct boundary points; a diameter is a chord through OOO.
Concentric, congruent, and coincident circles
Concentric circles share a center. Congruent circles have equal radii. Coincident circles have both the same center and the same radius, so they are one point set.
Central angle and arc notation
A central angle has vertex OOO. Arc measure records degrees, not length. Two letters select an unambiguous minor arc; a third point, as in ACB^\widehat{ACB}ACB, selects a route.
Minor arc, major arc, and semicircle
A minor arc measures between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘; a major arc between 180∘180^\circ180∘ and 360∘360^\circ360∘. Diameter endpoints bound two semicircles of 180∘180^\circ180∘.
Secant and tangent
A secant line meets a circle twice; a tangent line meets it exactly once at its contact point. A tangent segment is finite, from a strict external point to contact.
Common external or internal tangent
A line tangent to two circles is external when their centers lie on the same side of it, internal when they lie on opposite sides.
Inscribed angle and intercepted arc
An inscribed angle has three distinct circle points: its vertex and two chord endpoints. Its intercepted arc joins the endpoints and excludes the vertex.
Cyclic quadrilateral
A convex quadrilateral with four distinct vertices on one circle, named in boundary order. Opposite pairs in ABCDABCDABCD are A,CA,CA,C and B,DB,DB,D.
Near and far endpoints
Trace a secant ray from its external vertex: its first intersection is near, its second far. The selected near and far arcs exclude the other pair of endpoints.
External, inside, and whole secant segments
In order P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B, PAPAPA is external, ABABAB is the inside chord, and PBPBPB is whole. Unqualified segment symbols denote ordinary lengths, positive for distinct endpoints.

Formulas and theorems

  • Circle congruence and similarity

    All positive-radius circles are similar. Equal radii characterize congruence. After translating the original center to the target center, dilate by k=s/rk=s/rk=s/r.

    Use when Original radius r>0r>0r>0 and target radius s>0s>0s>0; the dilation is about the target center and maps the whole circle onto the target.

  • Central angles and arc addition

    A minor arc equals its smaller central angle in degree measure. The two routes between distinct endpoints total 360∘360^\circ360∘; a semicircle measures 180∘180^\circ180∘. Adjacent selected arcs add.

    Use when Add routes with disjoint interiors that join to form the desired arc. Equal degree measure alone does not make arcs on unequal-radius circles congruent.

  • Equal chords, arcs, and center distances

    Equal chord lengths, equal minor arc measures, and equal perpendicular center distances are equivalent. Equal minor arcs also give equal major arcs. The nearer chord is longer.

    Use when Same circle or congruent circles. The minor/major wording requires non-diameter chords; equal diameters instead bound equal semicircles and have distance zero. Compare distances only at a fixed radius.

  • Perpendicular chord bisection

    A line through OOO that is perpendicular to ABABAB bisects chord ABABAB and both arcs between its endpoints. Conversely, for a non-diameter chord with midpoint MMM, OM⊥ABOM\perp ABOM⊥AB.

    Use when Positive-radius circle. For a diameter, its midpoint is OOO; the zero segment OMOMOM determines no line. A perpendicular diameter still bisects both semicircles.

  • Chord length and perpendicular distance

    h2+d2=r2AB=2hAB=2r2−d2\begin{gathered} h^2+d^2=r^2\\ AB=2h\\ AB=2\sqrt{r^2-d^2} \end{gathered}h2+d2=r2AB=2hAB=2r2−d2​​
    Text description

    A circle has center O and chord AB. The perpendicular from O meets AB at midpoint M. A square at M marks the right angle and matching single ticks mark the two equal chord halves. OA is the radius r, OM is the perpendicular distance d, and MB is the half chord h. The chord shown is not a diameter.

    Use when Radius r>0r>0r>0, perpendicular center distance 0≤d<r0\le d<r0≤d<r, and half chord hhh. At d=0d=0d=0, use the diameter length 2r2r2r directly; d=rd=rd=r gives no genuine chord.

  • Line-circle incidence and tangency

    A line has zero, one, or two intersections when its perpendicular center distance is greater than, equal to, or less than rrr. At a circle point TTT, a line is tangent exactly when perpendicular to OTOTOT.

    Use when Positive radius; use the entire line and the radius to the actual contact point. Through a strict external point there are two tangents; through a circle point one; through an interior point none.

  • Equal tangent segments

    PT=PUPT2=OP2−r2\begin{gathered} PT=PU\\ PT^2=OP^2-r^2 \end{gathered}PT=PUPT2=OP2−r2​
    Text description

    Point P is strictly outside a circle centered at O. Its tangent segments touch at T above and U below. Radii OT and OU meet those segments at square right-angle marks. A matching single tick on each tangent segment marks equal lengths. OP joins the external point to the center and is the hypotenuse of both right triangles. The lower tangent is dashed as well as a different color.

    Use when Two distinct contacts T,UT,UT,U on the same circle from the same strict external PPP, so OP>r>0OP>r>0OP>r>0. The right angles are at contact; OPOPOP is the common hypotenuse.

  • Two-circle positions and common-tangent counts

    d>R+r: (2,2)d=R+r: (2,1)R−r<d<R+r: (2,0)d=R−r>0: (1,0)0≤d<R−r: (0,0)\begin{gathered} d>R+r:\ (2,2)\\ d=R+r:\ (2,1)\\ R-r<d<R+r:\ (2,0)\\ d=R-r>0:\ (1,0)\\ 0\le d<R-r:\ (0,0) \end{gathered}d>R+r: (2,2)d=R+r: (2,1)R−r<d<R+r: (2,0)d=R−r>0: (1,0)0≤d<R−r: (0,0)​

    Coincident circles have infinitely many common external tangents and no common internal tangents.

    Use when Radii R≥r>0R\ge r>0R≥r>0; d≥0d\ge0d≥0 is center distance. Each pair counts distinct (external, internal) lines. Rows describe separate, external contact, intersecting, internal contact, and strict containment. Containment includes unequal concentric circles; equal centers and equal radii give coincidence.

  • Inscribed angles and arc comparisons

    m∠AVB=a2\begin{gathered} m\angle AVB=\frac a2 \end{gathered}m∠AVB=2a​​
    Text description

    Distinct points A, V, and B lie on one circle. Chords VA and VB make an angle at V. The heavy arc from A to B runs along the lower part of the circle and excludes V. Its label a names the selected arc measure. The rest of the circle contains V and is not the arc used for this angle.

    Use when Three distinct circle points; aaa measures arc ABABAB excluding VVV, possibly major. Angles intercepting the same arc are equal; vertices on opposite open arcs between the same endpoints give supplementary angles.

  • Diameter and right-angle equivalence

    ABABAB is a diameter if and only if m∠ACB=90∘m\angle ACB=90^\circm∠ACB=90∘.

    Use when All three distinct points A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C lie on the same positive-radius circle. The diameter is opposite the right angle.

  • Tangent-chord ray choices

    m∠RTA=θ2m∠STA=360∘−θ2\begin{gathered} m\angle RTA=\frac\theta2\\ m\angle STA=\frac{360^\circ-\theta}{2} \end{gathered}m∠RTA=2θ​m∠STA=2360∘−θ​​
    Text description

    T and A lie on a circle centered at O. The vertical tangent through T has ray TR upward and opposite ray TS downward. A and R are on the same side of the horizontal radius line OT. Chord TA is drawn. The solid minor arc TA lies above, and the dashed major arc returns through the lower part of the circle. A right-angle square marks the tangent and contact radius. The angle using TR is half the minor arc; the angle using TS is half the major arc.

    Use when Tangent rays TR,TSTR,TSTR,TS are opposite at TTT. For a non-diameter chord TATATA, RRR and AAA lie on the same side of line OTOTOT; θ\thetaθ is the minor arc measure. A diameter gives 90∘90^\circ90∘ for either ray.

  • Cyclic opposite and exterior angles

    m∠A+m∠C=180∘m∠B+m∠D=180∘m∠DCE=m∠BAD\begin{gathered} m\angle A+m\angle C=180^\circ\\ m\angle B+m\angle D=180^\circ\\ m\angle DCE=m\angle BAD \end{gathered}m∠A+m∠C=180∘m∠B+m∠D=180∘m∠DCE=m∠BAD​
    Text description

    Four distinct circle points follow the convex boundary order A, B, C, D. The polygon sides join consecutive points. Side BC is extended through C to E, so B, C, E are in that order. A is opposite C and B is opposite D. The exterior angle at C is formed by CD and CE, not by an unspecified extension.

    Use when Convex ABCDABCDABCD in boundary order, four distinct vertices, no three collinear. Either supplementary opposite pair proves cyclicity; so does the exterior equality when B,C,EB,C,EB,C,E are collinear in that order. Cyclicity gives all three relations.

  • Inside angle: half-sum

    m∠APB=a+c2\begin{gathered} m\angle APB=\frac{a+c}{2} \end{gathered}m∠APB=2a+c​​
    Text description

    Distinct points follow circle order A, B, C, D. Chords AC and BD cross at the strictly interior point P. The solid arc AB faces angle APB. The dashed arc CD faces its vertical angle CPD. These two arcs are the selected pair for the half-sum; the adjacent angle uses the other two arcs.

    Use when Distinct circle points in order A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D; chords AC,BDAC,BDAC,BD cross strictly inside at PPP. Measures a,ca,ca,c belong to arcs AB,CDAB,CDAB,CD facing this vertical pair, each excluding the other endpoints.

  • Two-secant angle: half-difference

    m∠APD=f−n2\begin{gathered} m\angle APD=\frac{f-n}{2} \end{gathered}m∠APD=2f−n​​
    Text description

    P is strictly outside a circle. One ray reaches A first and B second. The other reaches D first and C second. The solid far arc runs from B to C around the right side without passing through A or D. The dashed near arc runs from D to A around the left side without passing through B or C. The angle is between rays PA and PD, both pointing toward the circle.

    Use when Two distinct secant rays from strict external PPP have orders P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B and P,D,CP,D,CP,D,C. Far arc BCBCBC excludes A,DA,DA,D and has measure fff; near arc DADADA excludes B,CB,CB,C and has measure nnn. Use rays toward the circle; f>nf>nf>n.

  • Tangent-secant angle: half-difference

    m∠TPA=f−n2\begin{gathered} m\angle TPA=\frac{f-n}{2} \end{gathered}m∠TPA=2f−n​​
    Text description

    PT is tangent to the circle at T above the horizontal secant. The secant ray reaches A first and B second from P. The solid near arc TA runs around the upper left without B. The dashed far arc BT runs across the upper right without A. The unhighlighted third arc is AB below the secant and excludes T. The square at T marks the tangent perpendicular to contact radius OT.

    Use when Strict external PPP, tangent contact TTT, and distinct secant intersections in order P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B. Near arc TATATA excludes BBB; far arc BTBTBT excludes AAA. Their measures are n,fn,fn,f. The far route may be major.

  • Two-tangent angle

    m∠TPU=180∘−n\begin{gathered} m\angle TPU=180^\circ-n \end{gathered}m∠TPU=180∘−n​

    Use when Strict external PPP; distinct tangent contacts T,UT,UT,U. Minor contact arc measure 0∘<n<180∘0^\circ<n<180^\circ0∘<n<180∘. The major arc is 360∘−n360^\circ-n360∘−n; reversing just one tangent ray gives the supplement. The original angle may be obtuse.

  • Inside chord products and correspondence

    PA⋅PB=PC⋅PD△APC∼△DPB\begin{gathered} PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD\\ \triangle APC\sim\triangle DPB \end{gathered}PA⋅PB=PC⋅PD△APC∼△DPB​
    Text description

    Chords AB and CD cross strictly inside one circle at P. Their orders are A, P, B and C, P, D. Joining A to C and D to B gives the solid triangle APC and the dashed triangle DPB. The triangle names match A with D, P with P, and C with B. Each product uses the two opposite parts on one straight chord, rather than adjacent parts from different chords.

    Use when Distinct chords in one circle with orders A,P,BA,P,BA,P,B and C,P,DC,P,DC,P,D, PPP strictly inside. Join AC,DBAC,DBAC,DB for the stated AA correspondence. Pair the two positive parts on each straight chord.

  • Two-secant products and correspondence

    PA⋅PB=PC⋅PDPB=PA+AB△PAD∼△PCB\begin{gathered} PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD\\ PB=PA+AB\\ \triangle PAD\sim\triangle PCB \end{gathered}PA⋅PB=PC⋅PDPB=PA+AB△PAD∼△PCB​
    Text description

    P is strictly outside one circle. The upper secant has order P, A, B and the lower has order P, C, D. A and C are near endpoints; B and D are far. The solid auxiliary chord AD and dashed auxiliary chord CB identify triangles PAD and PCB. Their ordered correspondence matches P with P, A with C, and D with B. PA is external and PB is whole; PC is external and PD is whole.

    Use when Two distinct secant rays to one circle from strict external PPP, with orders P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B and P,C,DP,C,DP,C,D. Join AD,CBAD,CBAD,CB for the correspondence. Every product is external times whole, with whole greater than external.

  • Tangent-secant product and correspondence

    PT2=PA⋅PB△PTA∼△PBT\begin{gathered} PT^2=PA\cdot PB\\ \triangle PTA\sim\triangle PBT \end{gathered}PT2=PA⋅PB△PTA∼△PBT​

    Use when Tangent contact TTT and genuine secant order P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B, from the same strict external point to the same circle. Join TA,TBTA,TBTA,TB for the correspondence; all factors are positive.

  • Signed power and ordinary products

    Pow⁡(P)=OP2−r2inside: PA⋅PB=−Pow⁡(P)outside: PA⋅PB=Pow⁡(P)\begin{gathered} \operatorname{Pow}(P)=OP^2-r^2\\ \text{inside: }PA\cdot PB=-\operatorname{Pow}(P)\\ \text{outside: }PA\cdot PB=\operatorname{Pow}(P) \end{gathered}Pow(P)=OP2−r2inside: PA⋅PB=−Pow(P)outside: PA⋅PB=Pow(P)​

    Use when Positive radius. Power is negative inside, zero on, positive outside. Inside use opposite chord parts; outside use external and whole secant lengths. Outside it also equals the tangent length squared. On-circle secant products have a zero factor.

    e.g. For radius 14 units and center distance 9 units, find signed power: −115-115−115 square units.

Problem types, step by step

Recover or compare chords

  1. Check equal radii before transferring chord, arc, or center-distance equality.
  2. Use a perpendicular and half chord in Pythagoras; handle a diameter directly.
  3. For parallel chord lines, add center distances on opposite sides or subtract them on the same side. For concentric circles, keep the same center-to-line distance; under a similarity, scale every corresponding length.

e.g. For radius 205\sqrt{205}205​ cm and perpendicular center distance 6 cm, find the full chord: 26 cm.

Classify two circles and count tangents

  1. Check equal-center cases separately; otherwise compare center distance with radius sum and positive difference.
  2. Classify common tangents using centers on the same or opposite sides; count merged contact lines once.

e.g. For radii 18 and 5 with center distance 13, determine contact and common tangents: internal contact, one external tangent.

Select an inscribed angle or diameter

  1. Exclude the vertex from the intercepted arc, then halve its measure.
  2. With an auxiliary diameter, add matching pieces when its ray is inside the angle; subtract nested pieces when outside; use equal radii in the side case.
  3. Transfer angles only after checking the same selected arc; apply the diameter/right-angle equivalence with all three points on the circle.

e.g. Arc AVBAVBAVB measures 102∘102^\circ102∘; find the inscribed angle at VVV: 129∘129^\circ129∘.

Choose a tangent-chord branch

  1. Name the tangent ray and compare its side of the contact-radius line with the chord endpoint.
  2. Choose the minor or major arc accordingly; the opposite tangent ray gives a supplementary angle.

e.g. For minor arc TATATA of 168∘168^\circ168∘, find the angle using the tangent ray opposite AAA across OTOTOT: 96∘96^\circ96∘.

Prove cyclicity before transferring angles

  1. Reassemble whole angles from diagonal parts and check distinct convex boundary-order vertices.
  2. Test an opposite supplementary pair or the specified exterior equality.
  3. After cyclicity is established, use opposite pairs, triangles, and same-arc angles to find the requested parts.

e.g. A convex boundary-order quadrilateral has opposite angles 97.5∘97.5^\circ97.5∘ and 82.5∘82.5^\circ82.5∘; decide cyclicity: yes, by the converse.

Link inside and outside arc information

  1. Locate each vertex and trace the actual rays before naming the selected arcs.
  2. Write inside sums, outside differences, and the full-circle total as separate equations.
  3. Solve and check every consecutive arc is positive, the total is 360 degrees, and all original angle equations hold.

e.g. In circle order A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D, chords AC,BDAC,BDAC,BD meet at XXX and secants have orders P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B and P,D,CP,D,CP,D,C; given angles AXB,APDAXB,APDAXB,APD of 88∘,37∘88^\circ,37^\circ88∘,37∘ and arc ABABAB of 69∘69^\circ69∘, find arcs BC,CD,DABC,CD,DABC,CD,DA: 129∘,107∘,55∘129^\circ,107^\circ,55^\circ129∘,107∘,55∘.

Find an inside chord part and whole

  1. Pair opposite positive parts on each chord through the interior point.
  2. Equate products, solve the missing part, then add both parts for a full chord.

e.g. Two chords cross inside one circle; their parts are 8 and 11 cm, and 4 cm and an unknown. Find the unknown: 22 cm.

Build whole secants before multiplying

  1. Trace P,A,BP,A,BP,A,B and form the whole from external plus inside.
  2. Equate external-times-whole products or a tangent square.
  3. Subtract external from whole for an inside chord, and take a positive square root for a tangent length.

e.g. Two secants from external PPP meet one circle. The first has external 7 cm and inside 18 cm; the second has external 5 cm. Find the second whole: 35 cm.

Check every algebraic candidate

  1. Choose the product or signed-power relation for the point position and solve the equation.
  2. Substitute every root into all actual lengths; require positive lengths and the stated endpoint order.
  3. Keep all admissible candidates, even negative parameters; reject a negative value when the variable itself is a length. Compute any further requested length from the valid case.

e.g. From strict external PPP, a secant has external and whole lengths x+9,x+14x+9,x+14x+9,x+14 and a tangent has length 525\sqrt252​; xxx is a parameter. Find valid xxx: −4-4−4.

Exam traps

  • Trap Using a half chord as the full answer

    Fix The right triangle contains half the chord. Use the perpendicular center distance, double the half length, and retain the diameter boundary.

  • Trap Treating a circle point as a strict external point

    Fix An on-circle point has zero power and one tangent line, with no positive segment to a different tangent contact. Inside points have no tangent through them.

  • Trap Choosing the arc containing an inscribed vertex

    Fix Use the other route between the chord endpoints. It can be major; do not automatically halve the minor arc.

  • Trap Naming the tangent line but ignoring its ray

    Fix Opposite tangent rays generally give supplementary angles. Reversing one external-angle ray also changes the angle to its supplement.

  • Trap Using consecutive angles or diagonal parts in a cyclicity test

    Fix Use whole opposite interior angles with distinct convex boundary-order vertices. Establish the circle before same-arc transfers.

  • Trap Calling every exterior angle acute

    Fix Far arcs can be major. Trace near and far endpoints from the vertex, preserve exclusions, and verify positive arcs completing a full circle.

  • Trap Multiplying external by inside

    Fix Outside, use external times whole. Inside, use the two opposite parts of one chord. Ordinary inside products are positive, although signed power is negative.

  • Trap Rejecting every negative parameter or keeping every root

    Fix Check the expressions that actually represent lengths and their point order. A parameter may be negative; an ordinary positive segment length may not.
