    In convex cyclic ABCDABCDABCD, whole angles A,C,B measure (3x+7)∘(3x+7)^\circ(3x+7)∘, (7x−17)∘(7x-17)^\circ(7x−17)∘, and (2x+30)∘(2x+30)^\circ(2x+30)∘, respectively. Find the whole angle at D.

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Use A and C first.

    (3x+7)+(7x−17)=180,10x−10=180,x=19.\begin{aligned}(3x+7)+(7x-17)&=180,\\10x-10&=180,\\x&=19.\end{aligned}(3x+7)+(7x−17)10x−10x​=180,=180,=19.​

    Then B measures 68 degrees. Its opposite angle is

    m∠D=180∘−68∘.m\angle D=180^\circ-68^\circ.m∠D=180∘−68∘.

    The whole angle at D is 112 degrees.

    In convex ABCDABCDABCD, triangle ABC has m∠BAC=36∘m\angle BAC=36^\circm∠BAC=36∘ and m∠ABC=81∘m\angle ABC=81^\circm∠ABC=81∘. Also m∠CAD=27∘m\angle CAD=27^\circm∠CAD=27∘ and m∠ACD=54∘m\angle ACD=54^\circm∠ACD=54∘. Which conclusion has a complete valid reason?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The angle at C in triangle ABC is 63 degrees. Add the diagonal pieces at A and at C.

    m∠BAD=63∘,m∠BCD=117∘.\begin{gathered}m\angle BAD=63^\circ,\\m\angle BCD=117^\circ.\end{gathered}m∠BAD=63∘,m∠BCD=117∘.​

    Their sum is 180 degrees. The converse proves cyclicity.

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    First find the angle BCA, which is 64 degrees. Add each pair split by AC.

    m∠BAD=63∘,m∠BCD=116∘.\begin{gathered}m\angle BAD=63^\circ,\\m\angle BCD=116^\circ.\end{gathered}m∠BAD=63∘,m∠BCD=116∘.​

    Noncollinear points A,B,C determine a circle. A point D makes convex ABCDABCDABCD, and m∠ABC=115∘m\angle ABC=115^\circm∠ABC=115∘, m∠CDA=65∘m\angle CDA=65^\circm∠CDA=65∘. Must D lie on the circle through A,B,C?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The opposite angles at B,D total a straight angle.

    115∘+65∘=180∘.115^\circ+65^\circ=180^\circ.115∘+65∘=180∘.

    The converse puts all four vertices on a circle. Its first three vertices determine the unique given circle.

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Equal opposite angles A,C are also supplementary, so each is right.

    A right angle at A intercepts the semicircle with endpoints B,D. Thus BD is a diameter. The whole angle at B is 81 degrees, so AC is not a diameter.

  10. 10

    A convex isosceles trapezoid ABCD has chosen parallel bases AB and CD. Which reasoning proves that it is cyclic?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The isosceles-trapezoid condition gives equal angles at the chosen base AB. Parallel bases give supplementary same-side angles B,C. Replacing B by equal angle A supplies the opposite pair required by the converse.

  12. 12

    In the converse proof, X lies strictly inside chord AC of the circle through A,B,C. Line BX has another circle intersection E. Which point order follows, and why?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    A point strictly inside the circle has center distance less than the radius. The line through it has perpendicular center distance less than the radius, so it is a secant. Along a secant, the interior points lie strictly between the two endpoints. Since B is one endpoint, X comes before E on ray BX.
