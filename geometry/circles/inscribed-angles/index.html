Inscribed Angles

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Two people standing at different points on a circular track look toward the same two posts. Their viewing angles can stay equal as they move. Crossing to the other arc changes which part of the circle lies between their sight lines. The arc you choose is the key to the angle.

Learning goals

  • Identify the intercepted arc that excludes an inscribed angle's vertex
  • Prove the inscribed-angle theorem for all three positions of the center
  • Compare inscribed angles with vertices on the same or opposite arcs
  • Apply the semicircle right-angle theorem and its converse
  • Choose the correct arc for an angle between a tangent ray and a chord

Choose the arc away from the vertex

An inscribed angle has its vertex on a circle and its sides along chords to two other, distinct points on that circle. For ∠AVB\angle AVB∠AVB, the intercepted arc is the arc from AAA to BBB that does not contain VVV.

Recall that an arc’s degree measure comes from its central angle; this is a measure of turning, not a length. Circles, Chords, and Arcs gives the arc notation and addition rules.

An inscribed angle measures half its intercepted arc. An arc of 82∘82^\circ82∘ gives an angle of 41∘41^\circ41∘. Equal radii create an isosceles triangle whose two equal angles explain the factor of two, as we will prove for every position of the center.

Worked example 1 An intercepted arc can be major

Problem. Points A,V,B,RA,V,B,RA,V,B,R lie on a circle in that order. The minor arc AVBAVBAVB measures 94∘94^\circ94∘. Find m∠AVBm\angle AVBm∠AVB and name its intercepted arc.

Solution. The intercepted arc is the major arc ARBARBARB, because the other arc contains VVV.

m(ARB^)=360∘−94∘,m(ARB^)=266∘.\begin{aligned} m(\widehat{ARB})&=360^\circ-94^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{ARB})&=266^\circ. \end{aligned}m(ARB)m(ARB)​=360∘−94∘,=266∘.​

Half of this arc gives the angle.

m∠AVB=266∘/2,m∠AVB=133∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle AVB&=266^\circ/2,\\ m\angle AVB&=133^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠AVBm∠AVB​=266∘/2,=133∘.​

Check your understanding

Points A,V,B,CA,V,B,CA,V,B,C occur in that order on a circle. Which arc does ∠AVB\angle AVB∠AVB intercept?

Answer choices

The intercepted arc must have endpoints AAA and BBB and exclude the vertex VVV. The arc ACBACBACB has exactly those properties.

Why half the arc works in every case

We use equal-radius isosceles triangles and the triangle angle sum. The earlier Isosceles and Equilateral Triangles explains why equal sides give equal opposite angles.

Case 1: the center is on one side

Let VAVAVA be a diameter, with center OOO between VVV and AAA. Join OOO to BBB.

First use 34∘34^\circ34∘ for the angle at VVV. Radii OVOVOV and OBOBOB are equal, so the angle at BBB in triangle VOBVOBVOB is also 34∘34^\circ34∘. The triangle angle sum gives the center angle on the VVV side.

m∠VOB=180∘−34∘−34∘,m∠VOB=112∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle VOB&=180^\circ-34^\circ-34^\circ,\\ m\angle VOB&=112^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠VOBm∠VOB​=180∘−34∘−34∘,=112∘.​

Rays OVOVOV and OAOAOA are opposite, so the remaining central angle is 68∘68^\circ68∘.

m∠BOA=180∘−112∘,m∠BOA=68∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle BOA&=180^\circ-112^\circ,\\ m\angle BOA&=68^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠BOAm∠BOA​=180∘−112∘,=68∘.​

That angle measures arc BABABA, twice the original 34∘34^\circ34∘ angle.

Inscribed-angle theorem when the center is on a side#

Now let m∠AVB=xm\angle AVB=xm∠AVB=x. Since OOO lies on ray VAVAVA, the angle OVBOVBOVB also measures xxx. Equal radii make triangle VOBVOBVOB isosceles, so its angle VBOVBOVBO measures xxx.

The triangle angle sum gives

m∠VOB=180∘−2x.m\angle VOB=180^\circ-2x.m∠VOB=180∘−2x.

The straight angle at OOO then gives

m∠BOA=180∘−(180∘−2x),m∠BOA=2x.\begin{aligned} m\angle BOA&=180^\circ-(180^\circ-2x),\\ m\angle BOA&=2x. \end{aligned}m∠BOAm∠BOA​=180∘−(180∘−2x),=2x.​

Arc BABABA excluding VVV has this measure. Nothing depended on choosing 34∘34^\circ34∘, so the inscribed angle is half that arc in every such configuration.

∎

Case 2: the center is inside the angle

Draw diameter VDVDVD. Here ray VDVDVD splits ∠AVB\angle AVB∠AVB, and DDD splits its intercepted arc ABABAB.

For example, suppose the angles AVDAVDAVD and DVBDVBDVB measure 24∘24^\circ24∘ and 39∘39^\circ39∘. Case 1 makes their arcs 48∘48^\circ48∘ and 78∘78^\circ78∘. Add the angles, and separately add the arcs.

m∠AVB=24∘+39∘,m∠AVB=63∘,m(ADB^)=48∘+78∘,m(ADB^)=126∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle AVB&=24^\circ+39^\circ,\\ m\angle AVB&=63^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{ADB})&=48^\circ+78^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{ADB})&=126^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠AVBm∠AVBm(ADB)m(ADB)​=24∘+39∘,=63∘,=48∘+78∘,=126∘.​

Inscribed-angle theorem when the center is inside#

Let the parts AVDAVDAVD and DVBDVBDVB measure uuu and vvv. Case 1 applies to each because VDVDVD is a diameter. Arc ADADAD measures 2u2u2u, and arc DBDBDB measures 2v2v2v.

Angle addition gives

m∠AVB=u+v.m\angle AVB=u+v.m∠AVB=u+v.

Arc addition gives

m(ADB^)=2u+2v,m(ADB^)=2(u+v).\begin{aligned} m(\widehat{ADB})&=2u+2v,\\ m(\widehat{ADB})&=2(u+v). \end{aligned}m(ADB)m(ADB)​=2u+2v,=2(u+v).​

The whole arc is twice the whole angle, just as in the numerical instance.

∎

Worked example 2 Add the two pieces

Problem. Diameter VDVDVD lies inside ∠AVB\angle AVB∠AVB. Point DDD lies on the intercepted arc ABABAB. Arc ADADAD measures 58∘58^\circ58∘, and arc DBDBDB measures 86∘86^\circ86∘. Find the angle parts AVDAVDAVD and DVBDVBDVB, and the whole angle AVBAVBAVB.

Solution. Each part has the diameter along one side, so Case 1 gives

m∠AVD=58∘/2,m∠AVD=29∘,m∠DVB=86∘/2,m∠DVB=43∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle AVD&=58^\circ/2,\\ m\angle AVD&=29^\circ,\\ m\angle DVB&=86^\circ/2,\\ m\angle DVB&=43^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠AVDm∠AVDm∠DVBm∠DVB​=58∘/2,=29∘,=86∘/2,=43∘.​

Add the angle parts.

m∠AVB=29∘+43∘,m∠AVB=72∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle AVB&=29^\circ+43^\circ,\\ m\angle AVB&=72^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠AVBm∠AVB​=29∘+43∘,=72∘.​

Case 3: the center is outside the angle

Draw diameter VDVDVD again. This time rays VAVAVA and VBVBVB lie on the same side of VDVDVD. Take the names so that VAVAVA lies between VDVDVD and VBVBVB. Along the arc away from VVV, the order is D,A,BD,A,BD,A,B.

Suppose DVBDVBDVB measures 47∘47^\circ47∘ and DVADVADVA measures 19∘19^\circ19∘. Case 1 gives arcs of 94∘94^\circ94∘ and 38∘38^\circ38∘. Subtract on both the angle side and the arc side.

m∠AVB=47∘−19∘,m∠AVB=28∘,m(AB^)=94∘−38∘,m(AB^)=56∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle AVB&=47^\circ-19^\circ,\\ m\angle AVB&=28^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{AB})&=94^\circ-38^\circ,\\ m(\widehat{AB})&=56^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠AVBm∠AVBm(AB)m(AB)​=47∘−19∘,=28∘,=94∘−38∘,=56∘.​

Inscribed-angle theorem when the center is outside#

Let the angles DVBDVBDVB and DVADVADVA measure ppp and qqq. The ray order gives p>qp>qp>q. Case 1 makes arc DBDBDB measure 2p2p2p and arc DADADA measure 2q2q2q.

Subtract the smaller angle from the larger.

m∠AVB=p−q.m\angle AVB=p-q.m∠AVB=p−q.

Subtract the smaller arc from the larger.

m(AB^)=2p−2q,m(AB^)=2(p−q).\begin{aligned} m(\widehat{AB})&=2p-2q,\\ m(\widehat{AB})&=2(p-q). \end{aligned}m(AB)m(AB)​=2p−2q,=2(p−q).​

The remaining arc is twice the remaining angle. Reflecting the picture, or swapping AAA and BBB, covers the other orientation.

∎

The center is on a side, inside the angle, or outside it, so these cases cover every inscribed angle. The inscribed-angle theorem is therefore

m∠AVB=12 m(arc AB excluding V).m\angle AVB=\frac12\,m(\text{arc }AB\text{ excluding }V).m∠AVB=21​m(arc AB excluding V).

Check your understanding

Diameter VDVDVD lies outside ∠AVB\angle AVB∠AVB, with arc order D,A,BD,A,BD,A,B away from VVV. Arc DBDBDB measures 170∘170^\circ170∘ and arc DADADA measures 74∘74^\circ74∘. Find m∠AVBm\angle AVBm∠AVB.

Answer choices

Subtract the arc pieces, then halve the intercepted arc.

m∠AVB=(170∘−74∘)/2,m∠AVB=48∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle AVB&=(170^\circ-74^\circ)/2,\\m\angle AVB&=48^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠AVBm∠AVB​=(170∘−74∘)/2,=48∘.​

Move the vertex, keep track of its arc

If a vertex moves along one arc between fixed chord endpoints, its intercepted arc stays the same. An intercepted arc of 138∘138^\circ138∘ gives 69∘69^\circ69∘ at every valid vertex on the other arc.

Moving the vertex to the opposite arc changes the intercepted arc to 222∘222^\circ222∘, so the new angle is 111∘111^\circ111∘. At an endpoint itself there is no inscribed angle with three distinct points.

In general, let one arc measure aaa. Vertices on the other arc see half of aaa. Vertices on the first arc see half of the remaining 360∘−a360^\circ-a360∘−a.

first angle=a/2,other angle=180∘−a/2.\begin{aligned} \text{first angle}&=a/2,\\ \text{other angle}&=180^\circ-a/2. \end{aligned}first angleother angle​=a/2,=180∘−a/2.​

Thus angles intercepting the same arc are equal. Angles whose vertices lie on opposite arcs between the same endpoints sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘. If the endpoints form a diameter, both arcs are semicircles and both angles are 90∘90^\circ90∘.

Worked example 3 Same arc and opposite arc

Problem. Points P,QP,QP,Q lie on the same open arc between A,BA,BA,B. Point RRR lies on the other open arc. Given m∠APB=68∘m\angle APB=68^\circm∠APB=68∘, find m∠AQBm\angle AQBm∠AQB and m∠ARBm\angle ARBm∠ARB.

Solution. Vertices P,QP,QP,Q intercept the same arc, so

m∠AQB=68∘.m\angle AQB=68^\circ.m∠AQB=68∘.

That intercepted arc measures 136∘136^\circ136∘. The other arc measures 224∘224^\circ224∘, giving

m∠ARB=224∘/2,m∠ARB=112∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle ARB&=224^\circ/2,\\ m\angle ARB&=112^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠ARBm∠ARB​=224∘/2,=112∘.​

Explore the intercepted arc choice

Before moving a vertex, predict which arc it will exclude. Positions on the same open arc give equal angles; positions on opposite arcs give angles that sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘. Measurements illustrate the proved theorem.

Check your understanding

P,QP,QP,Q lie on one open arc between A,BA,BA,B, and RRR on the other. Given m∠APB=52∘m\angle APB=52^\circm∠APB=52∘, which pair is correct?

Answer choices

Q excludes the same arc as P, so its angle is 52 degrees. R excludes the other arc.

m∠ARB=180∘−52∘,m∠ARB=128∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle ARB&=180^\circ-52^\circ,\\m\angle ARB&=128^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠ARBm∠ARB​=180∘−52∘,=128∘.​

A diameter and a right angle

A diameter gives a right angle at every other point on the circle. Its intercepted semicircle measures 180∘180^\circ180∘, and half of 180∘180^\circ180∘ is 90∘90^\circ90∘.

The semicircle right-angle theorem and its converse#

If ABABAB is a diameter and CCC is any other point on the circle, the arc ABABAB excluding CCC is a semicircle. The inscribed-angle theorem gives m∠ACB=90∘m\angle ACB=90^\circm∠ACB=90∘.

For the converse, suppose A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C are three distinct points on a circle and m∠ACB=90∘m\angle ACB=90^\circm∠ACB=90∘. Doubling the angle makes the intercepted arc 180∘180^\circ180∘. The radii to A,BA,BA,B form a straight angle, so ABABAB passes through the center and is a diameter.

∎

All three points must lie on the given circle for the converse.

Worked example 4 Use the converse, then the triangle sum

Problem. Triangle ACBACBACB has all three vertices on a circle with center OOO. Given m∠ACB=90∘m\angle ACB=90^\circm∠ACB=90∘ and m∠CAB=37∘m\angle CAB=37^\circm∠CAB=37∘, identify a diameter and find m∠ABCm\angle ABCm∠ABC.

Solution. The right angle at CCC intercepts an arc of 180∘180^\circ180∘. By the converse, ABABAB is a diameter.

The triangle’s angles sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

m∠ABC=180∘−90∘−37∘,m∠ABC=53∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle ABC&=180^\circ-90^\circ-37^\circ,\\ m\angle ABC&=53^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠ABCm∠ABC​=180∘−90∘−37∘,=53∘.​

Check your understanding

Distinct points A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C lie on a circle, and m∠ACB=90∘m\angle ACB=90^\circm∠ACB=90∘. Which segment must be a diameter?

Answer choices

The side opposite the right inscribed angle joins the endpoints of its semicircle. The converse makes AB a diameter.

A tangent ray and a chord

The angle between a tangent and a chord also comes from half an arc, but choosing the tangent ray matters. A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at its contact point, as proved in Tangents and Secants.

Take a chord TATATA that is not a diameter. Let the minor central angle TOATOATOA be 104∘104^\circ104∘. Equal radii make the base angles of triangle OTAOTAOTA equal.

m∠OTA=(180∘−104∘)/2,m∠OTA=38∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle OTA&=(180^\circ-104^\circ)/2,\\ m\angle OTA&=38^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠OTAm∠OTA​=(180∘−104∘)/2,=38∘.​

Choose tangent ray TRTRTR on the same side of line OTOTOT as AAA. It makes a right angle with TOTOTO. Subtract the triangle angle.

m∠RTA=90∘−38∘,m∠RTA=52∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle RTA&=90^\circ-38^\circ,\\ m\angle RTA&=52^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠RTAm∠RTA​=90∘−38∘,=52∘.​

The opposite tangent ray TSTSTS gives the supplementary angle, 128∘128^\circ128∘. It corresponds to the major arc, whose measure is 256∘256^\circ256∘.

The tangent-chord theorem with both ray choices#

Let the minor arc TATATA measure θ\thetaθ, where 0∘<θ<180∘0^\circ<\theta<180^\circ0∘<θ<180∘. Triangle OTAOTAOTA is isosceles because OTOTOT and OAOAOA are radii. Its angle sum gives

m∠OTA=180∘−θ2.m\angle OTA=\frac{180^\circ-\theta}{2}.m∠OTA=2180∘−θ​.

For tangent ray TRTRTR on the same side of line OTOTOT as AAA, chord ray TATATA lies inside the right angle RTORTORTO. Subtracting gives

m∠RTA=90∘−180∘−θ2,m∠RTA=θ/2.\begin{aligned} m\angle RTA&=90^\circ-\frac{180^\circ-\theta}{2},\\ m\angle RTA&=\theta/2. \end{aligned}m∠RTAm∠RTA​=90∘−2180∘−θ​,=θ/2.​

For opposite tangent ray TSTSTS, use a straight angle.

m∠STA=180∘−θ/2,m∠STA=(360∘−θ)/2.\begin{aligned} m\angle STA&=180^\circ-\theta/2,\\ m\angle STA&=(360^\circ-\theta)/2. \end{aligned}m∠STAm∠STA​=180∘−θ/2,=(360∘−θ)/2.​

Thus RTARTARTA is half the minor arc TATATA, and STASTASTA is half the major arc TATATA. The numerical argument followed these same steps. Reflecting the configuration covers a chord on the other side of line OTOTOT.

If TATATA is a diameter, it lies on the radius line. Both tangent rays are perpendicular to it, giving 90∘90^\circ90∘, half of either semicircle.

∎

This is the tangent-chord theorem. Equivalently, a tangent-chord angle equals an inscribed angle intercepting the same chosen arc: put the vertex on the other arc, so it is excluded from the chosen one.

Worked example 5 Keep the tangent rays separate

Problem. A circle has center OOO and minor arc TATATA of 138∘138^\circ138∘. Line RTSRTSRTS is tangent at TTT. Ray TRTRTR lies on the same side of line OTOTOT as AAA, and TSTSTS is its opposite ray. Find m∠RTAm\angle RTAm∠RTA and m∠STAm\angle STAm∠STA, naming each intercepted arc.

Solution. Ray TRTRTR selects the minor arc TATATA.

m∠RTA=138∘/2,m∠RTA=69∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle RTA&=138^\circ/2,\\ m\angle RTA&=69^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠RTAm∠RTA​=138∘/2,=69∘.​

Ray TSTSTS selects the major arc, whose measure is 222∘222^\circ222∘.

m∠STA=222∘/2,m∠STA=111∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle STA&=222^\circ/2,\\ m\angle STA&=111^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠STAm∠STA​=222∘/2,=111∘.​

Answer choices

TR selects the minor arc; TS selects the major arc of 204 degrees.

m∠RTA=156∘/2,m∠RTA=78∘,m∠STA=204∘/2,m∠STA=102∘.\begin{aligned}m\angle RTA&=156^\circ/2,\\m\angle RTA&=78^\circ,\\m\angle STA&=204^\circ/2,\\m\angle STA&=102^\circ.\end{aligned}m∠RTAm∠RTAm∠STAm∠STA​=156∘/2,=78∘,=204∘/2,=102∘.​

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • The wrong arc. An inscribed angle excludes its own vertex from the intercepted arc; that arc may be major.
  • The wrong factor. Halve an arc to find an inscribed angle. Double an inscribed angle to find its arc.
  • An unproved converse. The right-angle converse needs three distinct points on the given circle.
  • An unnamed tangent ray. The opposite tangent ray generally gives a supplementary angle. One can be obtuse.
  • A measurement used as proof. Moving points can reveal a pattern. The equal-radius and angle-sum arguments establish it.

Key takeaways

  • An inscribed angle is half the arc between its endpoints that excludes its vertex.
  • A diameter through the vertex proves the theorem by addition or subtraction of the side case.
  • Vertices on the same open arc give equal angles; vertices on opposite arcs give supplementary angles.
  • A diameter gives a right angle, and a right inscribed angle has a diameter opposite it.
  • For a tangent and chord, identify the ray before choosing the minor arc, major arc, or semicircle.

A bit of history (optional)

Euclid organized his circle arguments so that one result could carry several later ones. In Book III of the Elements, Proposition 20 joins a point on the circle to the center and extends that line. The auxiliary diameter lets him add two angle results in one picture and subtract them in another.

The right angle in a semicircle appears later in Proposition 31. The order is useful to notice: a striking special case sits inside a larger network of circle arguments, rather than standing as an isolated diagram to memorize.
