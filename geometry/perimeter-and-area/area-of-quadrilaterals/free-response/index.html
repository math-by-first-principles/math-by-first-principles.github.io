  1. Problem 1 The parallel sides

    A convex trapezoid has parallel bases 111111 cm and 232323 cm, perpendicular height 555 cm, and a slanted leg of 131313 cm. Find its area.

    Text description of this figure

    Schematic trapezoid, not to scale. Parallel top and bottom bases are labeled 11 cm and 23 cm. A dashed perpendicular between them is labeled 5 cm, with a right-angle square. The right slanted side is labeled 13 cm.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify the two parallel lengths and the distance measured perpendicular to them.

    2. Hint 2

      Average the bases before multiplying by the height.

    Answer

    858585 square cm.

    Full solution

    The height is 555 cm; the 131313 cm leg is not perpendicular to the bases.

    The area is

    A=11+232⋅5.A=\frac{11+23}{2}\cdot5.A=211+23​⋅5.

    The average base length is 171717 cm, giving area 858585 square cm.

    Answer

    858585 square cm.

    Key idea

    The trapezoid formula pairs parallel bases with their perpendicular separation.

  2. Problem 2 The diagonal lengths

    A rhombus has full diagonals 151515 cm and 222222 cm. Find its area.

    Text description of this figure

    Schematic rhombus ABCD, not to scale. A is above O, B right, C below, D left. Diagonals AC and BD intersect at O with a right-angle square. BD is labeled 15 cm. AC is labeled 22 cm. A single tick on every side marks all four sides congruent.

    1. Hint 1

      Recall the angle between a rhombus's diagonals.

    2. Hint 2

      Use both full diagonal lengths in the area formula.

    Answer

    165165165 square cm.

    Full solution

    Rhombus diagonals are perpendicular, so half their product gives the area.

    Compute

    A=12⋅15⋅22.A=\frac12\cdot15\cdot22.A=21​⋅15⋅22.

    The area is 165165165 square cm.

    Answer

    165165165 square cm.

    Key idea

    A rhombus supplies the perpendicularity needed for half the diagonal product.

  3. Problem 3 The divided diagonal

    Convex kite ABCDABCDABCD has equal-side pairs meeting at AAA and CCC. Diagonals intersect at OOO. Given AOAOAO is 777 cm, OCOCOC is 101010 cm, and BOBOBO is 444 cm, find the full lengths AC and BD, then the area.

    Text description of this figure

    Schematic convex kite ABCD, not to scale. A is above O, B right, C below, D left. Diagonals AC and BD intersect at O with a right-angle square. AO is 7 cm. OC is 10 cm. Single side ticks pair AB with AD; double ticks pair BC with CD.

    1. Hint 1

      Which diagonal is bisected by the kite's symmetry diagonal?

    2. Hint 2

      Add AO and OC for one full diagonal, and double BO for the other.

    Answer

    AC=17 cm,BD=8 cm,A=68 cm2.\begin{aligned}AC&=17\text{ cm},\\BD&=8\text{ cm},\\A&=68\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}ACBDA​=17 cm,=8 cm,=68 cm2.​

    The equal-side pairs meet at A and C, so AC perpendicularly bisects BD.

    Hence

    BD=2⋅4,BD=8 cm.\begin{aligned}BD&=2\cdot4, \\BD&=8\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}BDBD​=2⋅4,=8 cm.​

    The other full diagonal has length

    AC=7+10,AC=17 cm.\begin{aligned}AC&=7+10, \\AC&=17\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}ACAC​=7+10,=17 cm.​

    Use the perpendicular full diagonals:

    A=12⋅17⋅8,A=68 cm2.\begin{aligned}A&=\frac12\cdot17\cdot8, \\A&=68\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}AA​=21​⋅17⋅8,=68 cm2.​

    Answer

    AC=17 cm,BD=8 cm,A=68 cm2.\begin{aligned}AC&=17\text{ cm},\\BD&=8\text{ cm},\\A&=68\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}ACBDA​=17 cm,=8 cm,=68 cm2.​

  4. Problem 4 The two altitude feet

    In convex quadrilateral ABCD, diagonal ACACAC is 181818 cm. The perpendicular distances from B and D to line AC are 444 cm and 999 cm. Find the area. State whether you needed BD to be perpendicular to AC.

    Text description of this figure

    Schematic convex quadrilateral ABCD, not to scale. AC is a horizontal diagonal labeled 18 cm. Vertex B lies above AC and vertex D below it. Dashed perpendicular heights from B and D to AC are labeled 4 cm and 9 cm. Right-angle squares mark their feet. BD is not drawn or asserted perpendicular to AC.

    1. Hint 1

      Consider the two triangles on opposite sides of AC.

    2. Hint 2

      Each given distance is a height to AC; the feet need not coincide.

    Answer

    Area 117117117 square cm; perpendicularity of BD and AC is not needed.

    Full solution

    Let A1A_1A1​ and A2A_2A2​ denote the areas of triangles ABC and ACD, respectively.

    Their interiors do not overlap.

    For triangle ABC,

    A1=12⋅18⋅4,A1=36 cm2.\begin{aligned}A_1&=\frac12\cdot18\cdot4, \\A_1&=36\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}A1​A1​​=21​⋅18⋅4,=36 cm2.​

    For triangle ACD,

    A2=12⋅18⋅9,A2=81 cm2.\begin{aligned}A_2&=\frac12\cdot18\cdot9, \\A_2&=81\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}A2​A2​​=21​⋅18⋅9,=81 cm2.​

    Add to obtain

    A=36+81,A=117 cm2.\begin{aligned}A&=36+81, \\A&=117\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}AA​=36+81,=117 cm2.​

    The separate perpendicular distances supply the heights.

    There is no need to assume BD is perpendicular to AC.

    Answer

    Area 117117117 square cm; perpendicularity of BD and AC is not needed.

    Key idea

    A chosen diagonal can be a base for two triangles with different altitude feet.

  5. Problem 5 The joined copies

    Two congruent copies of a convex trapezoid are joined along one leg by a half turn to form a parallelogram. The trapezoid's parallel bases are b1b_1b1​ and b2b_2b2​, and their perpendicular separation is h. Explain why the joined parallelogram has base b1+b2b_1+b_2b1​+b2​ and height h, and derive the trapezoid area formula.

    1. Hint 1

      Track where each parallel base goes under the half turn.

    2. Hint 2

      The joined area contains two congruent trapezoids with no overlap of interiors.

    Answer

    The joined base is b1+b2b_1+b_2b1​+b2​ and height is hhh; the trapezoid area is A=12(b1+b2)hA=\tfrac12(b_1+b_2)hA=21​(b1​+b2​)h.

    Full solution

    The half turn sends each base to a parallel segment on the other base line.

    Along either outside base of the joined figure, one original base and one copied base meet end to end, so their lengths add.

    The two outer legs are half-turn images, hence parallel and congruent.

    The other opposite sides lie on the two original parallel base lines, making a parallelogram with perpendicular height h.

    Convexity puts the two copies on opposite sides of the shared leg, so their interiors do not overlap.

    Congruence gives them equal areas.

    The parallelogram area is

    2A=(b1+b2)h.2A=(b_1+b_2)h.2A=(b1​+b2​)h.

    Dividing by two gives

    A=12(b1+b2)h.A=\frac12(b_1+b_2)h.A=21​(b1​+b2​)h.

    Answer

    The joined base is b1+b2b_1+b_2b1​+b2​ and height is hhh; the trapezoid area is A=12(b1+b2)hA=\tfrac12(b_1+b_2)hA=21​(b1​+b2​)h.

    Key idea

    A rigidly moved congruent copy has equal area, so the joined total is twice one area.

  6. Problem 6 The fabric insert

    A convex kite-shaped fabric insert has full diagonals 242424 cm and 353535 cm. A rectangular piece of fabric measures 363636 cm by 252525 cm. The insert is cut from that piece without stretching. How much fabric area remains?

    Text description of this figure

    Schematic convex kite ABCD, not to scale. A is above O, B right, C below, D left. Diagonals AC and BD intersect at O with a right-angle square. BD is labeled 24 cm. AC is labeled 35 cm. Single side ticks pair AB with AD; double ticks pair BC with CD.

    1. Hint 1

      Find the original rectangular area and the area removed.

    2. Hint 2

      A kite has perpendicular diagonals; subtract its area from the original rectangular area.

    Answer

    480480480 square cm.

    Full solution

    The rectangular fabric has area

    36⋅25=900 cm2.36\cdot25=900\text{ cm}^2.36⋅25=900 cm2.

    The kite has perpendicular diagonals, so its area is

    A=12⋅24⋅35,A=420 cm2.\begin{aligned}A&=\frac12\cdot24\cdot35,\\A&=420\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}AA​=21​⋅24⋅35,=420 cm2.​

    The remaining area is

    900−420=480 cm2.900-420=480\text{ cm}^2.900−420=480 cm2.

    Answer

    480480480 square cm.

    Key idea

    Subtract an area only after checking the formula for the removed region.

  7. Problem 7 The right trapezoid

    A convex trapezoid has one leg perpendicular to its bases. Its parallel bases have lengths 131313 cm and 282828 cm. Its other leg is 171717 cm. Find the height and area.

    Text description of this figure

    Schematic trapezoid, not to scale. Parallel top and bottom bases are labeled 13 cm and 28 cm. A dashed perpendicular between them is labeled h, with a right-angle square. The left leg is perpendicular to the bases. The right slanted side is labeled 17 cm.

    1. Hint 1

      Drop a perpendicular from the shorter base's other endpoint.

    2. Hint 2

      The right triangle beside the rectangle has horizontal leg equal to the difference of the bases.

    3. Hint 3

      Use the Pythagorean theorem to find the perpendicular separation.

    Answer

    h=8 cm,A=164 cm2.\begin{aligned}h&=8\text{ cm},\\A&=164\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}hA​=8 cm,=164 cm2.​

    Full solution

    The difference of the parallel bases is 151515 cm.

    Together with the 171717 cm slanted leg, this forms a right triangle with the trapezoid's height h.

    The Pythagorean theorem gives

    h2=172−152,h2=64.\begin{aligned}h^2&=17^2-15^2, \\h^2&=64.\end{aligned}h2h2​=172−152,=64.​

    The positive height is 888 cm.

    Use it with the parallel bases:

    A=13+282⋅8,A=164 cm2.\begin{aligned}A&=\frac{13+28}{2}\cdot8, \\A&=164\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}AA​=213+28​⋅8,=164 cm2.​

    Answer

    h=8 cm,A=164 cm2.\begin{aligned}h&=8\text{ cm},\\A&=164\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}hA​=8 cm,=164 cm2.​

    Key idea

    A slanted leg can help recover the height without becoming the height.

  8. Problem 8 The diagonal condition

    Test the statement: every convex quadrilateral with equal diagonals has area equal to half their product. Give a numerical counterexample using a nonsquare rectangle, including its diagonal length, actual area, and half-product value.

    1. Hint 1

      A rectangle has equal diagonals, but they need not be perpendicular.

    2. Hint 2

      Choose unequal side lengths from a Pythagorean triple to keep the diagonal exact.

    Answer

    One counterexample is a 555 cm by 121212 cm rectangle: each diagonal is 131313 cm, actual area 606060 square cm, and the half-product is 84.584.584.5 square cm. Other valid counterexamples are accepted.

    Full solution

    Choose a rectangle with sides 555 cm and 121212 cm.

    Its diagonals are equal by the rectangle theorem.

    The Pythagorean theorem gives each diagonal length 131313 cm because

    52+122=132.5^2+12^2=13^2.52+122=132.

    The actual area is

    A=5⋅12,A=60 cm2.\begin{aligned}A&=5\cdot12, \\A&=60\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}AA​=5⋅12,=60 cm2.​

    Half the diagonal product is

    12⋅13⋅13=84.5 cm2.\frac12\cdot13\cdot13=84.5\text{ cm}^2.21​⋅13⋅13=84.5 cm2.

    These values differ.

    Equal diagonals do not supply the perpendicularity needed for the diagonal area formula.

    Answer

    One counterexample is a 555 cm by 121212 cm rectangle: each diagonal is 131313 cm, actual area 606060 square cm, and the half-product is 84.584.584.5 square cm. Other valid counterexamples are accepted.

    Key idea

    A formula's numerical inputs do not replace its geometric hypotheses.

  9. Problem 9 The rhombus measurements

    A rhombus has side length 171717 cm and one diagonal 161616 cm. Find its other full diagonal, its area, and its perpendicular height to a side. Give exact answers.

    Text description of this figure

    Schematic rhombus ABCD, not to scale. A is above O, B right, C below, D left. Diagonals AC and BD intersect at O with a right-angle square. BD is labeled 16 cm. AC is labeled d. A single tick on every side marks all four sides congruent.

    1. Hint 1

      A diagonal and its perpendicular bisector form right triangles whose hypotenuse is a rhombus side.

    2. Hint 2

      Use half of the known diagonal to find half of the other diagonal.

    3. Hint 3

      After finding area from diagonals, use the side as the base in the parallelogram formula.

    Answer

    d=30 cm,A=240 cm2,h=24017 cm.\begin{aligned}d&=30\text{ cm},\\A&=240\text{ cm}^2,\\h&=\frac{240}{17}\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}dAh​=30 cm,=240 cm2,=17240​ cm.​

    Full solution

    The known diagonal is bisected into 888 cm parts.

    The other half-diagonal is a leg of a right triangle with hypotenuse 171717 cm.

    Its square is

    172−82=225.17^2-8^2=225.172−82=225.

    The half-diagonal is 151515 cm, so the full other diagonal is 303030 cm.

    The perpendicular diagonal formula gives

    A=12⋅16⋅30,A=240 cm2.\begin{aligned}A&=\frac12\cdot16\cdot30, \\A&=240\text{ cm}^2.\end{aligned}AA​=21​⋅16⋅30,=240 cm2.​

    A rhombus is a parallelogram.

    Using its side as base gives

    17h=240.17h=240.17h=240.

    h=24017 cm.h=\frac{240}{17}\text{ cm}.h=17240​ cm.

    Answer

    d=30 cm,A=240 cm2,h=24017 cm.\begin{aligned}d&=30\text{ cm},\\A&=240\text{ cm}^2,\\h&=\frac{240}{17}\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}dAh​=30 cm,=240 cm2,=17240​ cm.​

  10. Problem 10 Two area specifications

    Case A. A convex trapezoid has area 175175175 square cm. Its parallel bases are (2x+3)(2x+3)(2x+3) cm and (x+8)(x+8)(x+8) cm, and its perpendicular height is 777 cm. Find x and both bases.

    Case B. A convex kite ABCD has equal-side pairs meeting at A and C. Its diagonals intersect at O, its area is 175175175 square cm, BDBDBD is 141414 cm, and AOAOAO is 999 cm. Find the full diagonal AC and the part OC. Explain why the diagonal area formula applies.

    Text description of this figure

    Schematic trapezoid, not to scale. Parallel top and bottom bases are labeled (2x+3) cm and (x+8) cm. A dashed perpendicular between them is labeled 7 cm, with a right-angle square.

    1. Hint 1

      Case A: recover the base sum from the area and perpendicular height.

      Case B: find the full unknown diagonal before subtracting its known part.
