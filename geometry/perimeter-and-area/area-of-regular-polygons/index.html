Areas of Regular Polygons

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

A regular polygon can look like one complicated region. Join its center to its vertices, and the region becomes a ring of matching triangles. Their bases run along the boundary, and their heights all reach from the center straight to a side. Adding these triangle areas turns a perimeter measurement into an area calculation.

Learning goals

  • Distinguish a regular polygon's center, radius, apothem, and central angle
  • Explain why congruent center triangles fill a convex regular polygon
  • Calculate area or recover an apothem using perimeter and perpendicular height
  • Apply special right triangles to regular triangles, squares, and hexagons
  • Use right-triangle trigonometry to find polygon dimensions and check their compatibility

Two distances from the center

A regular polygon has congruent sides and congruent interior angles. Throughout this lesson it is simple, convex, and nondegenerate, with an integer side count n≥3n\ge3n≥3 and positive lengths. Its center OOO is the common center of its rotational symmetries. Chapter 8 established that a rotation by one vertex step matches its boundary to itself.

A radius is a segment from OOO to a vertex; its length is rrr. An apothem is a perpendicular segment from OOO to a side; its length is aaa. Rotational symmetry makes all the vertex distances equal and all the side distances equal.

For a regular hexagon of side 2 units, a center triangle has two equal radii and a 2-unit base. Its perpendicular height is 3\sqrt33​ units, as the special-triangle calculation below will explain. Each such triangle has area 3\sqrt33​ square units; the six together have area 636\sqrt363​ square units.

In any center triangle, the equal radii make it isosceles. Its altitude from OOO bisects the side and the angle at OOO: the two right halves are congruent by hypotenuse-leg. Thus the foot MMM is a side midpoint, and the apothem is shorter than the radius, which is a hypotenuse.

The angles and triangles fit exactly

A central angle is the angle at OOO between radii to adjacent vertices. The eight central angles of a regular octagon are equal and fill one full turn, so each measures 45 degrees. This uses angles around a point, rather than a circle theorem.

Why the center triangles cover the polygon#

For a regular square of side 10 units, the one-vertex-step rotation carries each directed side to the next, turning it through the square’s 90-degree exterior turn. It turns each radius through the same angle. The four radius sectors fill one turn, with a triangle in each sector extending from OOO to a square side. Neighboring triangles share a whole radius, and they surround OOO with no gap. Their only outside edges are the square’s four sides, so OOO is inside the square. Every ray from the center meets the boundary in one point. Depending on which side it meets, the part of that ray inside the square lies in the triangle on that side. The four triangles have disjoint interiors and cover the square. Each has base 10 and height 5, so each has area 25 square units. Their sum is 100 square units.

For any convex regular nnn-gon, Chapter 8’s one-vertex-step rotation carries each directed side to the next. Its angle is the regular polygon’s exterior turn, 360∘/n360^\circ/n360∘/n, as proved in Polygon Angles and Tessellations. The radii turn through that same angle, so their nnn consecutive sectors fill one turn. Since n≥3n\ge3n≥3, each sector is less than 180 degrees. Its two radii and the polygon side joining their endpoints form a nondegenerate triangle contained in that sector.

Neighboring triangles share a whole radius. All radial edges pair up, leaving just the polygon sides on the outer boundary. Each side-base stays a positive distance from OOO, so the triangles cover a small region all around OOO within the full turn of sectors. Thus OOO lies strictly inside the region bounded by the polygon sides.

Now convexity keeps each center-to-boundary segment inside. A ray from OOO meets exactly one boundary side, except at a vertex shared by neighboring sides. The part of the ray in the polygon lies in the triangle on that side. This covers every point. Triangles on different sides have disjoint interiors because their directions at OOO lie in separate consecutive angle sectors; neighboring triangles share only a radius. Each center triangle has two sides of length rrr and one of length sss, so SSS makes all nnn triangles congruent. Nothing here depended on four sides.

Their equal angles around OOO sum to a full turn, giving

central angle=360∘n.\text{central angle}=\frac{360^\circ}{n}.central angle=n360∘​.

The apothem bisects this angle, giving

half-central angle=180∘n.\text{half-central angle}=\frac{180^\circ}{n}.half-central angle=n180∘​.

∎

Worked example 1 Read a center triangle

Problem. A convex regular octagon has center OOO, and B,CB,CB,C are adjacent vertices. Segment OMOMOM is perpendicular to BCBCBC at MMM. Find the measures of angle BOCBOCBOC and angle BOMBOMBOM, and identify the radius and apothem in triangle OMBOMBOMB.

Solution. Eight congruent center triangles fill one turn:

∠BOC=360∘÷8∠BOC=45∘.\begin{aligned}\angle BOC&=360^\circ\div8\\\angle BOC&=45^\circ.\end{aligned}∠BOC∠BOC​=360∘÷8=45∘.​

Since OMOMOM bisects the isosceles triangle’s vertex angle,

∠BOM=45∘÷2∠BOM=22.5∘.\begin{aligned}\angle BOM&=45^\circ\div2\\\angle BOM&=22.5^\circ.\end{aligned}∠BOM∠BOM​=45∘÷2=22.5∘.​

The radius is OBOBOB, the hypotenuse of right triangle OMBOMBOMB. The apothem is OMOMOM, perpendicular to the side. The base part MBMBMB is half of BCBCBC.

Check your understanding

A convex regular dodecagon has 12 sides. What are its central angle and half-central angle, respectively?

Answer choices

The 12 center angles fill one turn, so each is 360 divided by 12, or 30 degrees. Bisecting one gives 15 degrees. The 150-degree interior angle is at a polygon vertex, not at the center.

Add the center-triangle areas

Each triangle contributes half its side-base times the common apothem. For a regular hexagon with side 7 units and apothem 73/27\sqrt3/273​/2 units, one center triangle has area 493/449\sqrt3/4493​/4 square units. Six triangles give 1473/2147\sqrt3/21473​/2 square units. Alternatively, the perimeter is 42 units, and half of 42 times 73/27\sqrt3/273​/2 gives the same total.

The triangle area formula uses a perpendicular height. The radius is slanted relative to a side-base and cannot replace that height.

The perimeter-apothem area formula#

For a square of side 16 units and apothem 8 units, summing the four triangle areas gives

A=4(12⋅16⋅8).A=4\left(\frac12\cdot16\cdot8\right).A=4(21​⋅16⋅8).

Grouping the four bases first gives

A=12⋅64⋅8.A=\frac12\cdot64\cdot8.A=21​⋅64⋅8.

For a convex regular polygon of positive side length sss, there are nnn center triangles, each with base sss and perpendicular height aaa. By area addition,

A=n(12sa).A=n\left(\frac12 sa\right).A=n(21​sa).

Its perimeter is

P=ns.P=ns.P=ns.

Substitution gives

A=12Pa.\boxed{A=\frac12 Pa}.A=21​Pa​.

The square’s side count did not matter: the argument uses the nonoverlapping center decomposition and equal perpendicular heights. Regularity guarantees these heights agree. For an arbitrary polygon, one center-to-side distance need not be the height of every triangle, so a perimeter and one distance alone do not justify this formula.

∎

Worked example 2 Use the full perimeter

Problem. A convex regular hexagon has perimeter 54 cm and apothem 932\tfrac{9\sqrt3}{2}293​​ cm. Find its exact area.

Solution. The given perpendicular distance is the height of every center triangle, so

A=12(54)932A=24332 cm2.\begin{aligned} A&=\frac12(54)\frac{9\sqrt3}{2}\\ A&=\frac{243\sqrt3}{2}\text{ cm}^2. \end{aligned}AA​=21​(54)293​​=22433​​ cm2.​

The perimeter already includes all six bases. Multiplying this answer by six again would count each triangle six times.

The same formula can recover an unknown apothem. Since PPP is positive, multiply by 2 and divide by PPP:

a=2AP.a=\frac{2A}{P}.a=P2A​.

For example, a square of area 169 square cm and perimeter 52 cm has apothem 6.5 cm. This matches half its 13 cm side.

Check your understanding

A square has perimeter 36 cm and apothem 4.5 cm. Which area and justification are correct?

Answer choices

The four center triangles have disjoint interiors and fill the square. Add their areas, using the whole perimeter with the common perpendicular apothem.

A=12(36)(4.5)A=81.\begin{aligned}A&=\frac12(36)(4.5)\\A&=81.\end{aligned}AA​=21​(36)(4.5)=81.​

The square's side is 9 cm, whose square confirms the result.

Familiar special cases

Special right triangles let us find exact apothems without a calculator. For example, a regular hexagon of side 6 units has central angle 60 degrees. Each center triangle is equilateral: its two base angles are also 60 degrees. Its radius is therefore 6, its half-side is 3, and its apothem is 333\sqrt333​. The total area is 54354\sqrt3543​ square units.

Worked example 3 Locate the shorter leg

Problem. An equilateral triangle has side length 14 cm. Find its apothem and exact area.

Solution. The 7 cm half-side is opposite the 60-degree angle. The apothem is the shorter leg of this 30-60-90 triangle:

a=73a=733 cm.\begin{aligned} a&=\frac7{\sqrt3}\\ a&=\frac{7\sqrt3}{3}\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}aa​=3​7​=373​​ cm.​

The perimeter is 42 cm. Therefore

A=12(42)733A=493 cm2.\begin{aligned} A&=\frac12(42)\frac{7\sqrt3}{3}\\ A&=49\sqrt3\text{ cm}^2. \end{aligned}AA​=21​(42)373​​=493​ cm2.​

The triangle’s full altitude is 737\sqrt373​ cm. Its apothem is the distance from the center, so those two heights have different starting points.

Find the apothem with trigonometry

For other side counts, use the same right half-triangle with degree-mode trigonometry. In a regular pentagon of side 2 units, the half-central angle is 36 degrees. Tangent compares the half-side 1 with the adjacent apothem, giving an apothem of 1/tan⁡36∘1/\tan36^\circ1/tan36∘ units. Keeping that expression unrounded avoids carrying a rounded height into the area.

Here the angle θ\thetaθ is

θ=180∘n.\theta=\frac{180^\circ}{n}.θ=n180∘​.

In right triangle OMBOMBOMB, the opposite leg is s/2s/2s/2, the adjacent leg is aaa, and the hypotenuse is rrr. From the right-triangle ratios,

tan⁡θ=s/2a.\tan\theta=\frac{s/2}{a}.tanθ=as/2​.

Solving gives

a=s2tan⁡θ.\boxed{a=\frac{s}{2\tan\theta}}.a=2tanθs​​.

If the radius is known instead, cosine gives

a=rcos⁡θ.\boxed{a=r\cos\theta}.a=rcosθ​.

Sine finds the half-side:

s2=rsin⁡θ.\frac{s}{2}=r\sin\theta.2s​=rsinθ.

These are right-triangle ratios, not a new general-triangle area formula. Since n≥3n\ge3n≥3, the half-central angle is positive and at most 60 degrees, so the denominators above are nonzero.

Worked example 4 Start from a side length

Problem. A convex regular pentagon has side length 8 cm. Find its apothem and area to the nearest tenth, using a calculator in degree mode.

Solution. The pentagon’s half-central angle is 36 degrees. Tangent gives

a=4tan⁡36∘a≈5.5 cm.\begin{aligned} a&=\frac4{\tan36^\circ}\\ a&\approx5.5\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}aa​=tan36∘4​≈5.5 cm.​

Its perimeter is 40 cm. Use the unrounded expression for the apothem:

A=12(40)4tan⁡36∘A≈110.1 cm2.\begin{aligned} A&=\frac12(40)\frac4{\tan36^\circ}\\ A&\approx110.1\text{ cm}^2. \end{aligned}AA​=21​(40)tan36∘4​≈110.1 cm2.​

The displayed 5.5 cm is rounded, so substituting it as an exact height would lose precision.

Check your understanding

A convex regular heptagon has side length 6 cm. Which expression gives its apothem in centimeters? All angles use degrees.

Answer choices

The half-side is 3 cm and is opposite the half-central angle. Tangent equals half-side divided by apothem, so the apothem is 3 divided by that tangent.

Start from a radius, or check the dimensions

Given a radius, find the apothem before using it as a height. In a square of radius 424\sqrt242​ units, the half-central angle is 45 degrees, so cosine gives apothem 4 units. Sine gives half-side 4 units. The perimeter is 32 units, and the area is 64 square units.

Worked example 5 Use a radius in two right-triangle ratios

Problem. A convex regular octagon has radius 9 cm. Find its apothem, side length, and area to the nearest tenth. Check that the dimensions are compatible with the given radius. Use degree mode and round only the final results.

Solution. The half-central angle is 22.5 degrees. First find the two legs:

a=9cos⁡22.5∘a≈8.3 cm.\begin{aligned} a&=9\cos22.5^\circ\\ a&\approx8.3\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}aa​=9cos22.5∘≈8.3 cm.​s=18sin⁡22.5∘s≈6.9 cm.\begin{aligned} s&=18\sin22.5^\circ\\ s&\approx6.9\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}ss​=18sin22.5∘≈6.9 cm.​

Use eight sides for the perimeter and retain both exact trig expressions:

P=8(18sin⁡22.5∘)A=12P(9cos⁡22.5∘)A≈229.1 cm2.\begin{aligned} P&=8(18\sin22.5^\circ)\\ A&=\frac12 P(9\cos22.5^\circ)\\ A&\approx229.1\text{ cm}^2. \end{aligned}PAA​=8(18sin22.5∘)=21​P(9cos22.5∘)≈229.1 cm2.​

Using the unrounded sine and cosine expressions, the Pythagorean identity from Chapter 7 gives, in square centimeters,

a2+(s/2)2=81.a^2+(s/2)^2=81.a2+(s/2)2=81.

This matches the square of the given 9 cm radius. Testing the rounded 8.3 and 6.9 instead would produce a small rounding discrepancy.

Dimensions in a regular polygon must fit the same half-triangle. Pythagoras gives

r2=a2+(s2)2.r^2=a^2+\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^2.r2=a2+(2s​)2.

Tangent also links the side and apothem to the side count. For example, side 434\sqrt343​ units and apothem 2 units give tangent 3\sqrt33​ for the half-central angle. The acute angle is 60 degrees, so the side count is 3. These are compatible equilateral-triangle dimensions. For given sss and aaa, find the acute angle with inverse tangent, then check that 180∘/θ180^\circ/\theta180∘/θ is an integer at least 3. An exact noninteger side count cannot describe a regular polygon.

Check your understanding

A convex regular nonagon has radius 10 cm. Which expression is its apothem in centimeters? Use degrees.

Answer choices

The half-central angle is 180 divided by 9, or 20 degrees. The apothem is adjacent to that angle and the radius is the hypotenuse, so multiply 10 by the cosine.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Radius for height. The radius ends at a vertex; the apothem is perpendicular to a side.
  • Whole angle for half-triangle. The right triangle uses half the central angle and half the side.
  • Counting twice. The full perimeter already includes all the center-triangle bases.
  • Unrelated dimensions. A side, apothem, radius, and side count must fit one right half-triangle.
  • Early rounding. Keep exact expressions or calculator precision until the requested final rounding.

Key takeaways

  • A convex regular polygon splits into congruent, nonoverlapping triangles from its center.
  • The apothem is the common perpendicular height; area is half the perimeter times that height.
  • The half-central angle is 180 degrees divided by the integer side count.
  • Special right triangles or degree-mode trigonometry supply missing dimensions.
  • Check that the dimensions and side count can belong to the same regular polygon.

A bit of history (optional)

Euclid’s Elements ends its fourth book with a construction of a regular fifteen-sided polygon. Rather than begin a new construction from nothing, Euclid puts an equilateral triangle and a regular pentagon in the same circle. Their neighboring vertex positions leave a gap that can be halved to locate consecutive vertices of the fifteen-sided figure. A construction already solved becomes a tool for solving another.

The translated proposition and David Joyce’s commentary describe the next puzzle too: which other regular polygons can straightedge and compass construct? Many centuries later, Gauss showed that a regular seventeen-sided polygon could also be constructed with those tools.
