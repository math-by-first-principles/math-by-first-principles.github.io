    A simple polygon of area 777 square units has a similar image with length scale factor 444. What is the image area?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Area uses the square of the length factor.

    Aimage=42⋅7,Aimage=112 square units.\begin{aligned}A_{\text{image}}&=4^2\cdot7,\\A_{\text{image}}&=112\text{ square units}.\end{aligned}Aimage​Aimage​​=42⋅7,=112 square units.​

  6. 6

    Two similar images of one polygon use length factors 1 and 2, respectively. Which statement is guaranteed?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Length factor 111 preserves both shape and size, so the first image is congruent to the original. The second image uses squared area factor

    22=4.2^2=4.22=4.

    Thus the second has four times the original area.

    A polygon has area measured in square millimeters. It is carried to a similar image with dimensionless length factor 333. Which unit belongs to the image area after multiplying by the squared factor?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Squaring the length scale factor changes the number of square units, not the unit itself. The multiplier is dimensionless.

    Aimage=9Aoriginal.A_{\text{image}}=9A_{\text{original}}.Aimage​=9Aoriginal​.

    The image area is still measured in square millimeters.

  9. 9

    A polygon is partitioned into nonoverlapping triangles. Under one similarity, which fact allows their image areas to be added to obtain the whole image area?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    A similarity carries every cut and piece to the image. Coverage and nonoverlap are preserved, so the area-addition postulate applies.
