Similarity in Right Triangles

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Draw a perpendicular from the square corner of a right triangle to its longest side. The cut makes two smaller triangles. They face different directions, but each has the same shape as the whole triangle. That repeated shape lets a short measurement inside the triangle determine a much longer side.

Learning goals

  • Locate the altitude foot on the interior of a right triangle's hypotenuse
  • Justify the ordered similarity of all three triangles using AA
  • Derive the geometric-mean relationships for the altitude and both legs
  • Find missing lengths while distinguishing a whole hypotenuse from its pieces

A perpendicular cut

In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle. The other two sides are the legs. An altitude is a perpendicular segment from a vertex to the line containing the opposite side. Its meeting point with that line is its foot.

We will use nondegenerate △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC, right at CCC. Draw altitude CHCHCH to hypotenuse ABABAB. Use these length names throughout:

a=BCb=CAc=ABp=AHq=HBh=CH.\begin{aligned} a&=BC\\ b&=CA\\ c&=AB\\ p&=AH\\ q&=HB\\ h&=CH. \end{aligned}abcpqh​=BC=CA=AB=AH=HB=CH.​

Here HHH is strictly between AAA and BBB. To see why, recall that both endpoint angles AAA and BBB are acute. If HHH were outside the segment, the small triangle at the nearer endpoint would contain both a right angle at HHH and an obtuse angle supplementary to that acute endpoint angle. Its angle sum would exceed 180∘180^\circ180∘. If HHH were an endpoint, that endpoint would itself be a second right angle in ABCABCABC. Both possibilities are impossible.

Thus p,q,hp,q,hp,q,h are positive, and the two pieces make the whole:

c=p+q.c=p+q.c=p+q.

The foot need not be the midpoint. Perpendicularity supplies right angles; it does not supply equal lengths.

Check your understanding

Why does the altitude from the right-angle vertex meet the open hypotenuse segment?

Answer choices

An exterior foot would make an obtuse angle at an endpoint in a small right triangle, contradicting the triangle-angle sum. An endpoint foot would give the original triangle two right angles. No midpoint or equal-leg assumption is needed.

Keep the three triangles in order

The cut preserves enough angles to make all three triangles similar. The AA criterion needs two matching angle pairs; one pair of right angles alone is not enough.

Three triangles from one altitude#

First suppose angle AAA is 32∘32^\circ32∘ and angle BBB is 58∘58^\circ58∘. Triangle ACHACHACH has the same 32∘32^\circ32∘ angle at AAA and a 90∘90^\circ90∘ angle at HHH, so its angle at CCC is 58∘58^\circ58∘. Triangle CBHCBHCBH has the same 58∘58^\circ58∘ angle at BBB and a 90∘90^\circ90∘ angle at HHH, so its angle at CCC is 32∘32^\circ32∘.

Writing each triangle in the angle order 32∘32^\circ32∘, 58∘58^\circ58∘, 90∘90^\circ90∘ gives

△ABC∼△ACH∼△CBH.\triangle ABC\sim\triangle ACH\sim\triangle CBH.△ABC∼△ACH∼△CBH.

The particular acute angles did not matter. In general, call the angle at AAA α\alphaα and the angle at BBB β\betaβ. Their sum is 90∘90^\circ90∘. The same shared-angle and right-angle argument gives this correspondence:

PositionWhole triangle ABCABCABCLeft triangle ACHACHACHRight triangle CBHCBHCBH
Angle α\alphaαAAAAAACCC
Angle β\betaβBBBCCCBBB
Right angleCCCHHHHHH

Each small triangle shares an acute angle with the whole triangle and has a matching right angle. AA proves each similarity. The table also gives two angle matches between the small triangles, so AA proves their similarity directly.

∎

The two angles at CCC in the small triangles have different names and usually different measures. The common point CCC does not have to match itself.

Worked example 1 Angles at the cut

Problem. Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC, HHH lies on ABABAB, and CH⊥ABCH\perp ABCH⊥AB. The angle at AAA measures 41∘41^\circ41∘.

(a) Find ∠ACH\angle ACH∠ACH and ∠HCB\angle HCB∠HCB.

(b) Write all three triangles in the same corresponding order.

Solution. (a) Triangle ACHACHACH contains the given angle at AAA and the right angle at HHH.

m∠ACH=180∘−90∘−41∘m∠ACH=49∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle ACH&=180^\circ-90^\circ-41^\circ\\ m\angle ACH&=49^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠ACHm∠ACH​=180∘−90∘−41∘=49∘.​

The whole angle at CCC is 90∘90^\circ90∘, so

m∠HCB=90∘−49∘m∠HCB=41∘.\begin{aligned} m\angle HCB&=90^\circ-49^\circ\\ m\angle HCB&=41^\circ. \end{aligned}m∠HCBm∠HCB​=90∘−49∘=41∘.​

(b) Read each triangle in the order 41∘41^\circ41∘, 49∘49^\circ49∘, 90∘90^\circ90∘:

△ABC∼△ACH∼△CBH.\triangle ABC\sim\triangle ACH\sim\triangle CBH.△ABC∼△ACH∼△CBH.

Check your understanding

In △ABC∼△ACH\triangle ABC\sim\triangle ACH△ABC∼△ACH, which side of the small triangle corresponds to the whole hypotenuse ABABAB?

Answer choices

The right angle at C in the whole triangle matches the right angle at H in the small one. Their opposite sides, AB and AC, correspond. AC is a leg of the whole triangle but the hypotenuse of ACH.

The altitude and the two pieces

The altitude lies between the two pieces in a repeated ratio. This gives a multiplication rule, rather than a rule about adding lengths.

The altitude geometric-mean relationship#

Take hypotenuse pieces 444 and 999. From the ordered small-triangle similarity ACH∼CBHACH\sim CBHACH∼CBH, side AHAHAH matches CHCHCH, while CHCHCH matches BHBHBH. Therefore

4h=h9.\frac4h=\frac h9.h4​=9h​.

Multiply by 9h9h9h, which is positive.

h2=36h=6.\begin{aligned} h^2&=36\\ h&=6. \end{aligned}h2h​=36=6.​

Nothing depended on the chosen pieces. With positive lengths ppp and qqq, the same correspondence gives

ph=hqh2=pqh=pq.\begin{aligned} \frac ph&=\frac hq\\ h^2&=pq\\ h&=\sqrt{pq}. \end{aligned}hp​h2h​=qh​=pq=pq​.​

∎

The geometric mean of two positive numbers is the positive square root of their product. For 444 and 999, it is 666, since 4/64/64/6 and 6/96/96/9 are equal. It is different from their arithmetic mean, (4+9)/2(4+9)/2(4+9)/2.

The altitude to the hypotenuse is the geometric mean of the two hypotenuse pieces. This requires a right triangle and the perpendicular from its right-angle vertex to its hypotenuse.

Worked example 2 Find the inner length

Problem. In the standard right-triangle configuration,

AH=3 cmHB=12 cm.\begin{aligned} AH&=3\text{ cm}\\ HB&=12\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}AHHB​=3 cm=12 cm.​

Find CHCHCH exactly.

Solution. The two given lengths are the hypotenuse pieces, so the altitude relation uses their product.

CH2=3⋅12CH2=36CH=6 cm.\begin{aligned} CH^2&=3\cdot12\\ CH^2&=36\\ CH&=6\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}CH2CH2CH​=3⋅12=36=6 cm.​

The positive root is required because CHCHCH is a length.

Check your understanding

The hypotenuse pieces are 222 cm and 777 cm. What is the exact altitude?

Answer choices

The squared altitude is the product of the two pieces, so h2=14h^2=14h2=14. Since the altitude is positive, h=14h=\sqrt{14}h=14​ cm. Averaging the pieces or leaving their product unsquared gives a different quantity.

Each leg uses the whole hypotenuse

A leg connects to one endpoint of the hypotenuse. Its geometric-mean relation uses the whole hypotenuse and the piece touching that endpoint.

The two leg geometric-mean relationships#

For a numerical instance, take a whole hypotenuse of 202020, with pieces 555 at AAA and 151515 at BBB. The similarity ABC∼ACHABC\sim ACHABC∼ACH pairs ABABAB with ACACAC and ACACAC with AHAHAH.

20b=b5b2=100b=10.\begin{aligned} \frac{20}{b}&=\frac b5\\ b^2&=100\\ b&=10. \end{aligned}b20​b2b​=5b​=100=10.​

The similarity ABC∼CBHABC\sim CBHABC∼CBH pairs ABABAB with CBCBCB and BCBCBC with BHBHBH.

20a=a15a2=300a=103.\begin{aligned} \frac{20}{a}&=\frac a{15}\\ a^2&=300\\ a&=10\sqrt3. \end{aligned}a20​a2a​=15a​=300=103​.​

For arbitrary positive lengths, the same two correspondences give

cb=bpb2=cp\begin{aligned} \frac cb&=\frac bp\\ b^2&=cp \end{aligned}bc​b2​=pb​=cp​

and

ca=aqa2=cq.\begin{aligned} \frac ca&=\frac aq\\ a^2&=cq. \end{aligned}ac​a2​=qa​=cq.​

Thus CACACA uses AHAHAH, while BCBCBC uses HBHBHB. The whole length ccc occurs in both leg relations.

∎

Worked example 3 Recover both outer lengths

Problem. In the standard configuration,

AB=18 cmAH=8 cm.\begin{aligned} AB&=18\text{ cm}\\ AH&=8\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}ABAH​=18 cm=8 cm.​

(a) Find HBHBHB.

(b) Find both legs CACACA and BCBCBC exactly.

Solution. (a) Subtract the known piece from the whole.

HB=18−8HB=10 cm.\begin{aligned} HB&=18-8\\ HB&=10\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}HBHB​=18−8=10 cm.​

(b) The leg at AAA uses piece AHAHAH.

CA2=18⋅8CA=12 cm.\begin{aligned} CA^2&=18\cdot8\\ CA&=12\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}CA2CA​=18⋅8=12 cm.​

The leg at BBB uses piece HBHBHB.

BC2=18⋅10BC=65 cm.\begin{aligned} BC^2&=18\cdot10\\ BC&=6\sqrt5\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}BC2BC​=18⋅10=65​ cm.​

Work backward from an altitude

Changing the triangle’s orientation does not change the correspondences. Identify the right angle, hypotenuse, and foot before choosing a relation.

Worked example 4 Recover the other piece

Problem. Triangle EFGEFGEFG is right at FFF. The perpendicular FTFTFT meets the interior of hypotenuse EGEGEG at TTT. The lengths are

ET=6 cmFT=9 cm.\begin{aligned} ET&=6\text{ cm}\\ FT&=9\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}ETFT​=6 cm=9 cm.​

(a) Find TGTGTG.

(b) Find the whole hypotenuse EGEGEG.

Solution. (a) Here the altitude is FTFTFT, and its pieces are ETETET and TGTGTG.

FT2=ET⋅TG81=6TGTG=13.5 cm.\begin{aligned} FT^2&=ET\cdot TG\\ 81&=6TG\\ TG&=13.5\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}FT281TG​=ET⋅TG=6TG=13.5 cm.​

(b) Add the pieces, rather than using either one as the whole.

EG=6+13.5EG=19.5 cm.\begin{aligned} EG&=6+13.5\\ EG&=19.5\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}EGEG​=6+13.5=19.5 cm.​

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Matching by location. A vertex that appears at the top need not match the top vertex of another triangle. Match the right angles and then the acute angles.
  • Treating the foot as a midpoint. A perpendicular cut need not split the hypotenuse equally.
  • Choosing the wrong pair of lengths. The altitude uses the two pieces. A leg uses the whole hypotenuse and the piece at its endpoint.
  • Forgetting the square root. A relation for a squared length does not yet give the length. Take the positive root.

Key takeaways

  • The perpendicular from a right-angle vertex meets the interior of the hypotenuse.
  • With the stated labels, the correspondence is △ABC∼△ACH∼△CBH\triangle ABC\sim\triangle ACH\sim\triangle CBH△ABC∼△ACH∼△CBH by AA.
  • The altitude satisfies h2=pqh^2=pqh2=pq.
  • Each leg uses the whole hypotenuse and its touching piece.

b2=cpa2=cqc=p+q.\begin{aligned} b^2&=cp\\ a^2&=cq\\ c&=p+q. \end{aligned}b2a2c​=cp=cq=p+q.​

A bit of history (optional)

Different routes through this right-triangle problem appear in separate books of Euclid’s Elements. The well-known theorem about squares on a right triangle’s sides appears in Book I. The perpendicular cut studied here appears in Book VI, Proposition 8, after the book develops proportions for similar triangles.

In that proposition, Euclid first compares the whole triangle with each small triangle. He then draws attention to the perpendicular itself: it is a “mean proportional” between the two pieces of the base. That older phrase describes the equal ratios on either side of the altitude. The translated proposition and its guide also describe how this later similarity result supplies another route to the earlier square theorem.
