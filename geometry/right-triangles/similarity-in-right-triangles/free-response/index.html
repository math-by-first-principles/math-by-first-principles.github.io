  1. Problem 1 Two given pieces

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. Its altitude CHCHCH meets hypotenuse ABABAB at interior point HHH.

    The pieces are

    AH=5 cm.AH=5\text{ cm}.AH=5 cm.

    The other piece is

    HB=20 cm.HB=20\text{ cm}.HB=20 cm.

    Find the altitude and the whole hypotenuse.

    Text description of this figure

    ABC is right at C. CH is perpendicular to AB at interior point H. AH is 5 centimeters and HB is 20 centimeters. Neither requested length is shown.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify which lengths are pieces of the hypotenuse.

    2. Hint 2

      The altitude uses the product of the pieces, while the whole uses their sum.

    Answer

    CH=10 cmAB=25 cm.\begin{aligned}CH&=10\text{ cm}\\AB&=25\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}CHAB​=10 cm=25 cm.​

    Full solution

    The altitude relation gives

    CH2=5⋅20.CH^2=5\cdot20.CH2=5⋅20.

    The positive root gives

    CH=10 cm.CH=10\text{ cm}.CH=10 cm.

    The whole consists of both pieces, so

    AB=5+20.AB=5+20.AB=5+20.

    Thus

    AB=25 cm.AB=25\text{ cm}.AB=25 cm.

    Answer

    CH=10 cmAB=25 cm.\begin{aligned}CH&=10\text{ cm}\\AB&=25\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}CHAB​=10 cm=25 cm.​

    Key idea

    The altitude and the whole hypotenuse use the same pieces in different ways.

    AB=30 cm.AB=30\text{ cm}.AB=30 cm.

    Also,

    BC=18 cm.BC=18\text{ cm}.BC=18 cm.

    Find both pieces, the other leg, and the altitude. Give exact decimal values.

    Text description of this figure

    The right triangle has hypotenuse AB 30 centimeters and leg BC 18 centimeters. CH is its hypotenuse altitude, with no unknown lengths labeled numerically.

    1. Hint 1

      The known leg touches one particular piece of the hypotenuse.

    2. Hint 2

      Find HBHBHB from the leg relation before subtracting it from the whole.

    3. Hint 3

      Use the recovered pieces to find the altitude, and use the whole with AHAHAH to find CACACA.

    CA=24 cm.CA=24\text{ cm}.CA=24 cm.

    AH=19.2 cmHB=10.8 cmCA=24 cmCH=14.4 cm.\begin{aligned}AH&=19.2\text{ cm}\\HB&=10.8\text{ cm}\\CA&=24\text{ cm}\\CH&=14.4\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}AHHBCACH​=19.2 cm=10.8 cm=24 cm=14.4 cm.​

    BC2=21⋅14.BC^2=21\cdot14.BC2=21⋅14.

  4. Problem 4 Three ordered names

    Triangle JKLJKLJKL is right at KKK. The perpendicular KMKMKM meets the interior of hypotenuse JLJLJL at MMM.

    Prove by AA that all three triangles are similar. Write one ordered statement containing the whole triangle and both small triangles. Give the angle correspondences explicitly.

    Text description of this figure

    JKL is right at K. KM is perpendicular to hypotenuse JL at interior point M. The diagram gives incidence and perpendicularity only.

    1. Hint 1

      Locate the right angle in each of the three triangles.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the shared endpoint angle for each small triangle.

    3. Hint 3

      Choose the whole triangle's order so the right angle is last, and match that order in both small triangles.

    Answer

    △JLK∼△JKM∼△KLM.\triangle JLK\sim\triangle JKM\sim\triangle KLM.△JLK∼△JKM∼△KLM.

    The corresponding angle measures are the whole angle at JJJ, the whole angle at LLL, and 90∘90^\circ90∘, in that order.

    Full solution

    The whole triangle has acute endpoint angles at JJJ and LLL, whose measures sum to 90∘90^\circ90∘.

    Triangle JKMJKMJKM has the same angle at JJJ as the whole and a right angle at MMM.

    Its angle JKMJKMJKM therefore equals the whole angle at LLL.

    Triangle KLMKLMKLM has the same angle at LLL as the whole and a right angle at MMM.

    Its angle LKMLKMLKM therefore equals the whole angle at JJJ.

    Each comparison has two matching angle pairs, so AA gives

    △JLK∼△JKM∼△KLM.\triangle JLK\sim\triangle JKM\sim\triangle KLM.△JLK∼△JKM∼△KLM.

    Answer

    △JLK∼△JKM∼△KLM.\triangle JLK\sim\triangle JKM\sim\triangle KLM.△JLK∼△JKM∼△KLM.

    The corresponding angle measures are the whole angle at JJJ, the whole angle at LLL, and 90∘90^\circ90∘, in that order.

    Give a shared acute angle as well as a right angle when applying AA.

  5. Problem 5 Explain the two leg relations

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. Its altitude CHCHCH meets hypotenuse ABABAB at interior point HHH.

    Use ordered similarity and corresponding sides to prove the relation for leg CACACA and then the relation for leg BCBCBC. State each result using segment names, rather than remembered letter formulas.

    Text description of this figure

    The standard right triangle is labeled with segment names. CA touches AH at A, and BC touches HB at B. The altitude and both right angles are marked.

    1. Hint 1

      For each leg, compare the whole triangle with the small triangle that has that leg as its hypotenuse.

    2. Hint 2

      In ABCABCABC to ACHACHACH, ABABAB matches ACACAC and ACACAC matches AHAHAH.

    3. Hint 3

      For the other leg, use ABCABCABC to CBHCBHCBH and keep the same ratio direction.

    Answer

    CA2=AB⋅AHBC2=AB⋅HB.\begin{aligned}CA^2&=AB\cdot AH\\BC^2&=AB\cdot HB.\end{aligned}CA2BC2​=AB⋅AH=AB⋅HB.​

    Full solution

    AA gives △ABC∼△ACH\triangle ABC\sim\triangle ACH△ABC∼△ACH.

    The matching pairs AB,ACAB,ACAB,AC and AC,AHAC,AHAC,AH give

    ABAC=ACAH.\frac{AB}{AC}=\frac{AC}{AH}.ACAB​=AHAC​.

    All lengths are positive, so cross multiplication is valid and gives

    CA2=AB⋅AH.CA^2=AB\cdot AH.CA2=AB⋅AH.

    AA also gives △ABC∼△CBH\triangle ABC\sim\triangle CBH△ABC∼△CBH.

    The matching pairs AB,CBAB,CBAB,CB and BC,BHBC,BHBC,BH give

    ABBC=BCHB.\frac{AB}{BC}=\frac{BC}{HB}.BCAB​=HBBC​.

    Cross multiplication gives

    BC2=AB⋅HB.BC^2=AB\cdot HB.BC2=AB⋅HB.

    Answer

    CA2=AB⋅AHBC2=AB⋅HB.\begin{aligned}CA^2&=AB\cdot AH\\BC^2&=AB\cdot HB.\end{aligned}CA2BC2​=AB⋅AH=AB⋅HB.​

    Key idea

    The piece in a leg relationship shares the leg's hypotenuse endpoint.

    Text description of this figure

    ABC is right at C, and CH is perpendicular to AB. The measured pieces AH and HB are 2 and 18 centimeters. No calculated lengths appear in the givens.

    1. Hint 1

      Distinguish an altitude relationship from a leg relationship.

    The computed length 6106\sqrt{10}610​ cm belongs to BCBCBC.

    Full solution

    The whole is correctly found as 202020 cm, but the altitude uses AHAHAH and HBHBHB, not ABABAB and HBHBHB.

    Thus

    CH=6 cm.CH=6\text{ cm}.CH=6 cm.

    The student's product is the leg relationship

    BC2=20⋅18.BC^2=20\cdot18.BC2=20⋅18.

    Its positive root is indeed

  7. Problem 7 Draw and annotate

    Draw a nondegenerate triangle UVWUVWUVW with a right angle at VVV. Place VVV below hypotenuse UWUWUW. The acute angle at UUU measures 25∘25^\circ25∘.

    Add the perpendicular VDVDVD to hypotenuse UWUWUW, with DDD on the interior. Mark every right angle, label the other acute angles in the two small triangles, and write all three triangles in corresponding order.

    1. Hint 1

      Rotation or reflection changes placement, but not the angle matching.

    2. Hint 2

      The two small right triangles share the angles at the two endpoints of the hypotenuse.

    3. Hint 3

      Read each triangle in the order 25∘25^\circ25∘, 65∘65^\circ65∘, 90∘90^\circ90∘.

    Answer

    Right angles: ∠UVW,∠UDV,∠VDW\angle UVW,\angle UDV,\angle VDW∠UVW,∠UDV,∠VDW. Angles VWD,UVDVWD,UVDVWD,UVD are 65∘65^\circ65∘; DVWDVWDVW is 25∘25^\circ25∘.

    △UWV∼△UVD∼△VWD.\triangle UWV\sim\triangle UVD\sim\triangle VWD.△UWV∼△UVD∼△VWD.

    Full solution

    Draw UWUWUW as a segment and put VVV below it so the angle at VVV is right.

    Since the angle at UUU is 25∘25^\circ25∘, the angle at WWW is 65∘65^\circ65∘.

    The altitude meets the open hypotenuse segment at DDD.

    Mark the right angle of the whole triangle at VVV and the right angle on each side of VDVDVD at DDD.

    In UVDUVDUVD, subtract 25∘25^\circ25∘ and 90∘90^\circ90∘ from 180∘180^\circ180∘, giving 65∘65^\circ65∘ at VVV.

    The remaining part of the whole right angle at VVV is 25∘25^\circ25∘.

    Reading each triangle as 25∘25^\circ25∘, 65∘65^\circ65∘, 90∘90^\circ90∘ gives

  8. Problem 8 Test the missing hypothesis

    A claim says, “Drawing an altitude to a side of any triangle makes two smaller triangles that are both similar to the whole triangle.”

    Decide whether the claim is true. If it is false, give a nondegenerate triangle with an interior altitude foot that refutes it. Use angle measures to verify your counterexample, and state the hypothesis that repairs the three-triangle result.

    1. Hint 1

      A right angle at the altitude foot does not force the original triangle to have a right angle.

    2. Hint 2

      Try a triangle with two 40∘40^\circ40∘ angles and one 100∘100^\circ100∘ angle.

    3. Hint 3

      Compare the full angle sets of the whole triangle and either small triangle.

    Answer

    False. A 40°, 40°, 100° triangle gives small triangles with angles 40°, 50°, 90°. Repair: start with a right triangle and draw the altitude from its right-angle vertex to its hypotenuse.

    Full solution

    Choose ABCABCABC with angles 40∘40^\circ40∘ at AAA and BBB and 100∘100^\circ100∘ at CCC.

    Its acute endpoint angles ensure that the perpendicular from CCC meets the interior of ABABAB.

    Each small triangle has a 40∘40^\circ40∘ endpoint angle and a 90∘90^\circ90∘ angle at the foot, leaving 50∘50^\circ50∘ as its third angle.

    Neither small triangle can be similar to the whole: the whole contains a 100∘100^\circ100∘ angle, whereas both small triangles contain a 90∘90^\circ90∘ angle.

    Requiring the original triangle to be right and choosing the altitude from that right-angle vertex to the hypotenuse supplies the three ordered similar triangles.

    Answer

    False. A 40°, 40°, 100° triangle gives small triangles with angles 40°, 50°, 90°. Repair: start with a right triangle and draw the altitude from its right-angle vertex to its hypotenuse.

    Key idea

    Check the whole triangle's angle hypothesis, as well as the perpendicular cut.

  9. Problem 9 Equal legs and equal pieces

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. Its altitude CHCHCH meets hypotenuse ABABAB at interior point HHH.

    Prove that CA=BCCA=BCCA=BC exactly when AH=HBAH=HBAH=HB. Then determine the altitude as a fraction of the whole hypotenuse in this equal-leg case.

    1. Hint 1

      The two leg squares share the same positive factor, the whole hypotenuse.

    2. Hint 2

      Prove both directions of the stated equivalence.

    3. Hint 3

      When the pieces are equal, their geometric mean equals either piece.

    Answer

    The two equalities are equivalent: CA=BCCA=BCCA=BC exactly when AH=HBAH=HBAH=HB. In this case,

    CH=AB2.CH=\frac{AB}{2}.CH=2AB​.

    Full solution

    The leg relations are

    CA2=AB⋅AH.CA^2=AB\cdot AH.CA2=AB⋅AH.

    The second leg relation is

    BC2=AB⋅HB.BC^2=AB\cdot HB.BC2=AB⋅HB.

    If CA=BCCA=BCCA=BC, the squared lengths are equal.

    Divide both products by positive ABABAB to obtain AH=HBAH=HBAH=HB.

    Conversely, if AH=HBAH=HBAH=HB, the two products are equal, so the leg squares are equal.

    Positive side lengths with equal squares are equal, giving CA=BCCA=BCCA=BC.

    In this case, both pieces have length AB/2AB/2AB/2.

    The altitude relation becomes

    CH2=(AB2)2.CH^2=\left(\frac{AB}{2}\right)^2.CH2=(2AB​)2.

    Taking the positive root gives

    CH=AB2.CH=\frac{AB}{2}.CH=2AB​.

    Answer

    The two equalities are equivalent: CA=BCCA=BCCA=BC exactly when AH=HBAH=HBAH=HB. In this case,

    CH=AB2.CH=\frac{AB}{2}.CH=2AB​.

    Key idea

    An equivalence needs a separate justification in each direction.
