  1. 1

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The altitude CHCHCH meets the interior of hypotenuse ABABAB at HHH. Which side is the hypotenuse of the whole triangle?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle of the triangle being named. The whole triangle is right at CCC, so its hypotenuse is ABABAB.

    BCBCBC and CACACA are its legs. CHCHCH is an interior altitude, not a side of the whole triangle.

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The altitude CHCHCH meets the interior of hypotenuse ABABAB at HHH. The angle at AAA is 31∘31^\circ31∘. Which order makes △ABC∼△ABC\triangle ABC\sim\triangle\boxed{\phantom{ABC}}△ABC∼△ABC​ correct for the triangle containing A,C,HA,C,HA,C,H?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The angle order in ABCABCABC is 31∘31^\circ31∘, 59∘59^\circ59∘, 90∘90^\circ90∘. In the small triangle, these angles are at AAA, CCC, and HHH, respectively.

    △ABC∼△ACH.\triangle ABC\sim\triangle ACH.△ABC∼△ACH.

    The other orders put either the right angle or an acute angle in the wrong position.

  3. 3

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The altitude CHCHCH meets the interior of hypotenuse ABABAB at HHH. If m∠CAB=29∘m\angle CAB=29^\circm∠CAB=29∘, what is m∠HCBm\angle HCBm∠HCB?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    In ACHACHACH, the angle at CCC complements the 29∘29^\circ29∘ angle at AAA.

    m∠ACH=61∘.m\angle ACH=61^\circ.m∠ACH=61∘.

    The two angles at CCC split the whole right angle.

    m∠HCB=90∘−61∘.m\angle HCB=90^\circ-61^\circ.m∠HCB=90∘−61∘.

    Thus ∠HCB\angle HCB∠HCB measures 29∘29^\circ29∘.

  4. 4

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The altitude CHCHCH meets the interior of hypotenuse ABABAB at HHH. Which fact is needed in addition to the right angles to prove the whole triangle similar to ACHACHACH by AA?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Since HHH lies between AAA and BBB, rays AHAHAH and ABABAB coincide. Therefore ∠CAH\angle CAH∠CAH is the whole angle at AAA.

    Together with the right angles at CCC and HHH, this supplies two matching angle pairs for AA. Equal areas, equal hypotenuses, and a midpoint are not given or needed.

  5. 5

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The altitude CHCHCH meets the interior of hypotenuse ABABAB at HHH. Find CHCHCH from the following lengths.

    AH=6AH=6AH=6 cm

    HB=24HB=24HB=24 cm

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The altitude uses the two hypotenuse pieces.

    CH2=6⋅24.CH^2=6\cdot24.CH2=6⋅24.

    Therefore CH2=144CH^2=144CH2=144. Taking the positive square root gives

    CH=12 cm.CH=12\text{ cm}.CH=12 cm.

    Adding, averaging, or leaving the product as the length gives the other choices.

    Solution

    The other piece is 29−9=2029-9=2029−9=20 cm. The altitude would be 9⋅20=65\sqrt{9\cdot20}=6\sqrt59⋅20​=65​ cm. The sum 383838 and the square 261261261 are not the requested leg length.

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The altitude CHCHCH meets the interior of hypotenuse ABABAB at HHH. Find BCBCBC from the following lengths.

    AB=25AB=25AB=25 cm

    HB=9HB=9HB=9 cm

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Leg BCBCBC uses the piece HBHBHB at endpoint BBB.

    BC2=25⋅9.BC^2=25\cdot9.BC2=25⋅9.

    Thus

    BC=15 cm.BC=15\text{ cm}.BC=15 cm.

    The altitude is 121212 cm because the other piece is 161616 cm. Adding the given lengths or keeping their product does not give BCBCBC.

  8. 8

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The altitude CHCHCH meets the interior of hypotenuse ABABAB at HHH. Find ABABAB from the following lengths.

    AH=7AH=7AH=7 cm

    HB=11HB=11HB=11 cm

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The foot is inside the hypotenuse, so the pieces add.

    AB=7+11.AB=7+11.AB=7+11.

    Thus AB=18AB=18AB=18 cm. The square root of the product would give the altitude, not the whole hypotenuse.

  9. 9

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The altitude CHCHCH meets the interior of hypotenuse ABABAB at HHH. Find HBHBHB from the following lengths.

    CH=5CH=5CH=5 cm

    AH=2AH=2AH=2 cm

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The altitude relation is

    52=2⋅HB.5^2=2\cdot HB.52=2⋅HB.

    Divide 252525 by 222.

    HB=12.5 cm.HB=12.5\text{ cm}.HB=12.5 cm.

    Multiplying the given lengths, subtracting them, or forgetting to divide by 222 gives the other choices.

  10. 10

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The altitude CHCHCH meets the interior of hypotenuse ABABAB at HHH. Which proportion must always follow from △ACH∼△CBH\triangle ACH\sim\triangle CBH△ACH∼△CBH in that order?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The ordered matching is AAA to CCC, CCC to BBB, and HHH to HHH. Hence AHAHAH matches CHCHCH, while CHCHCH matches BHBHBH.

    AHCH=CHHB.\frac{AH}{CH}=\frac{CH}{HB}.CHAH​=HBCH​.

    The first two wrong proportions would require equal hypotenuse pieces. The last introduces the whole hypotenuse into a small-triangle correspondence.

  11. 11

    What is the geometric mean of the positive numbers 444 and 252525?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Multiply the two numbers, then take the positive square root.

    4⋅25=10.\sqrt{4\cdot25}=10.4⋅25​=10.

    The arithmetic mean is 14.514.514.5, the product is 100100100, and the difference is 212121. These are different operations.

  12. 12

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC, and HHH is an interior point of ABABAB. Which additional condition makes CHCHCH the altitude used in the three geometric-mean relationships?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The required construction is the perpendicular from the right-angle vertex to the hypotenuse.

    CH⊥AB.CH\perp AB.CH⊥AB.

    A midpoint, a length equality involving CHCHCH, or equal legs does not by itself make this particular segment CHCHCH perpendicular.
