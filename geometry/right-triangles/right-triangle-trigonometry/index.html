Sine, Cosine, and Tangent

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Watch the video explainer

Intuition

Enlarge a right triangle while keeping its angles fixed. Every side grows, but a leg divided by the hypotenuse stays the same. That ratio describes the triangle’s shape rather than its size. Sine, cosine, and tangent give names to three such ratios, each measured from a chosen acute angle.

Learning goals

  • Identify opposite and adjacent legs relative to a selected acute angle
  • Prove that AA similarity makes side ratios independent of triangle size
  • Evaluate sine, cosine, and tangent from exact right-triangle lengths
  • Connect complementary-angle ratios and derive exact values for special angles
  • Justify the quotient and Pythagorean identities for acute angles

Choose an angle before naming a leg

In this lesson, every reference angle is acute, between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 90∘90^\circ90∘, in a nondegenerate right triangle. We use degrees throughout.

A reference angle is the acute angle selected for comparing sides. The opposite leg lies across from it. The adjacent leg meets it and the right angle. The hypotenuse also meets the reference angle, but it is excluded from the term adjacent leg.

For example, take △ABC\triangle ABC△ABC, right at CCC, with lengths

BC=10CA=24AB=26.\begin{aligned} BC&=10\\ CA&=24\\ AB&=26. \end{aligned}BCCAAB​=10=24=26.​

Choose angle AAA, labeled θ\thetaθ (the Greek letter theta).

For θ\thetaθ at AAA, the opposite leg is BCBCBC, the adjacent leg is CACACA, and the hypotenuse is ABABAB. Choosing BBB instead exchanges the two leg roles. The hypotenuse stays opposite the right angle, so it does not change.

Check your understanding

Triangle ABC is right at C. If B is selected as the acute reference angle, which side is its adjacent leg?

Answer choices

BC meets B and the right angle at C. AB also touches B, but it is the hypotenuse and is excluded from the adjacent leg. CA is opposite B.

Why the ratios depend on the angle

Two right triangles with the same selected acute angle have the same three angle measures. The AA criterion therefore makes them similar. Their sizes can differ while their matching side ratios stay fixed.

An acute angle fixes the three side ratios#

First compare a triangle with opposite, adjacent, and hypotenuse lengths 666, 888, and 101010 with one whose corresponding lengths are 151515, 202020, and 252525. Both are right triangles, and the second is a dilation of the first with factor 2.52.52.5. Their chosen acute angles match.

Each ratio cancels that common factor:

1525=6102025=8101520=68.\begin{aligned} \frac{15}{25}&=\frac6{10}\\ \frac{20}{25}&=\frac8{10}\\ \frac{15}{20}&=\frac68. \end{aligned}2515​2520​2015​​=106​=108​=86​.​

Now consider any two nondegenerate right triangles with the same acute reference angle. They have a matching right angle and the given matching acute angle, so AA proves similarity. Write a,b,ca,b,ca,b,c for the first triangle’s opposite leg, adjacent leg, and hypotenuse. If k>0k>0k>0 is the scale factor to the second triangle, its corresponding lengths are ka,kb,kcka,kb,kcka,kb,kc.

kakc=ackbkc=bckakb=ab.\begin{aligned} \frac{ka}{kc}&=\frac ac\\ \frac{kb}{kc}&=\frac bc\\ \frac{ka}{kb}&=\frac ab. \end{aligned}kcka​kckb​kbka​​=ca​=cb​=ba​.​

The positive scale factor cancels in each ratio. Nothing depended on 2.52.52.5 or on the particular side lengths: all three ratios are fixed by the acute angle.

∎

2a2c=ac.\frac{2a}{2c}=\frac ac.2c2a​=ca​.

Give the three ratios names

For an acute angle θ\thetaθ in a right triangle, define its sine, cosine, and tangent by

sin⁡θ=opposite leghypotenusecos⁡θ=adjacent leghypotenusetan⁡θ=opposite legadjacent leg.\begin{aligned} \sin\theta&=\frac{\text{opposite leg}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\\ \cos\theta&=\frac{\text{adjacent leg}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\\ \tan\theta&=\frac{\text{opposite leg}}{\text{adjacent leg}}. \end{aligned}sinθcosθtanθ​=hypotenuseopposite leg​=hypotenuseadjacent leg​=adjacent legopposite leg​.​

Here sin⁡\sinsin, cos⁡\coscos, and tan⁡\tantan abbreviate the three names. They refer to ratios, not lengths. The units cancel when both lengths use the same unit.

The mnemonic SOH CAH TOA records these definitions: sine uses opposite over hypotenuse, cosine uses adjacent over hypotenuse, and tangent uses opposite over adjacent. It works only after the reference angle and side roles have been identified.

Worked example 1 Read three ratios in a turned triangle

Problem. Triangle LMNLMNLMN is right at MMM. Its side lengths are

LM=24 cmMN=7 cmLN=25 cm.\begin{aligned} LM&=24\text{ cm}\\ MN&=7\text{ cm}\\ LN&=25\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}LMMNLN​=24 cm=7 cm=25 cm.​

Use LLL as the reference angle. Find its sine, cosine, and tangent exactly.

Solution. Opposite LLL is MNMNMN, adjacent to LLL is leg LMLMLM, and the hypotenuse is LNLNLN.

sin⁡L=725cos⁡L=2425tan⁡L=724.\begin{aligned} \sin L&=\frac7{25}\\ \cos L&=\frac{24}{25}\\ \tan L&=\frac7{24}. \end{aligned}sinLcosLtanL​=257​=2524​=247​.​

Turning the triangle changes none of these side roles relative to LLL.

Because a hypotenuse is longer than either positive leg, sine and cosine of an acute angle are strictly between 000 and 111. Tangent is positive, but it can exceed 111 when the opposite leg is longer than the adjacent leg.

Switch to the other acute angle

The two acute angles of a right triangle are complementary: their measures add to 90∘90^\circ90∘. Switching between them exchanges opposite and adjacent legs.

Worked example 2 Use the other reference angle

Problem. Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The acute angles at AAA and BBB are labeled θ\thetaθ and ϕ\phiϕ. Its lengths are

BC=4CA=65AB=9.\begin{aligned} BC&=4\\ CA&=\sqrt{65}\\ AB&=9. \end{aligned}BCCAAB​=4=65​=9.​

(a) Identify the opposite leg, adjacent leg, and hypotenuse for ϕ\phiϕ.

(b) Find all three ratios for ϕ\phiϕ, and compare cos⁡ϕ\cos\phicosϕ with sin⁡θ\sin\thetasinθ.

Solution. (a) For ϕ\phiϕ at BBB, the opposite leg is CACACA, the adjacent leg is BCBCBC, and the hypotenuse is still ABABAB.

(b) Substitute those roles into the definitions.

sin⁡ϕ=659cos⁡ϕ=49tan⁡ϕ=654.\begin{aligned} \sin\phi&=\frac{\sqrt{65}}9\\ \cos\phi&=\frac49\\ \tan\phi&=\frac{\sqrt{65}}4. \end{aligned}sinϕcosϕtanϕ​=965​​=94​=465​​.​

For θ\thetaθ, the opposite leg is BCBCBC, so sin⁡θ=4/9\sin\theta=4/9sinθ=4/9. The same side ratio gives cos⁡ϕ\cos\phicosϕ.

In any right triangle, a leg opposite one acute angle is adjacent to the other. Thus, for acute θ\thetaθ and its acute complement,

sin⁡(90∘−θ)=cos⁡θcos⁡(90∘−θ)=sin⁡θ.\begin{aligned} \sin(90^\circ-\theta)&=\cos\theta\\ \cos(90^\circ-\theta)&=\sin\theta. \end{aligned}sin(90∘−θ)cos(90∘−θ)​=cosθ=sinθ.​

Tangent swaps its two positive legs, so

tan⁡(90∘−θ)=1tan⁡θ.\tan(90^\circ-\theta)=\frac1{\tan\theta}.tan(90∘−θ)=tanθ1​.

For example, the tangent of θ\thetaθ in the worked triangle is 4/654/\sqrt{65}4/65​, while the tangent of ϕ\phiϕ is 65/4\sqrt{65}/465​/4. The two values multiply to 111. Sine and cosine exchange; tangent becomes its reciprocal.

Check your understanding

Angles θ and φ are acute and complementary. Given that sin⁡θ=27\sin\theta=\frac27sinθ=72​, what is cos⁡ϕ\cos\phicosϕ?

Answer choices

The side opposite θ is adjacent to φ, and both ratios use the same hypotenuse. Therefore

cos⁡ϕ=sin⁡θ.\cos\phi=\sin\theta.cosϕ=sinθ.

So the value is two sevenths, with no angle calculation needed.

Exact values from the special triangles

The previous lesson derived the two special side ratios. Their trig values come from dividing those already justified lengths.

In a 30∘30^\circ30∘-60∘60^\circ60∘-90∘90^\circ90∘ triangle, use short leg 111, long leg 3\sqrt33​, and hypotenuse 222.

Relative to 30∘30^\circ30∘, the definitions give

sin⁡30∘=12cos⁡30∘=32tan⁡30∘=13tan⁡30∘=33.\begin{aligned} \sin30^\circ&=\frac12\\ \cos30^\circ&=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\\ \tan30^\circ&=\frac1{\sqrt3}\\ \tan30^\circ&=\frac{\sqrt3}{3}. \end{aligned}sin30∘cos30∘tan30∘tan30∘​=21​=23​​=3​1​=33​​.​

Switching to 60∘60^\circ60∘ exchanges the legs, so its sine and cosine exchange those values and its tangent is 3\sqrt33​.

For 45∘45^\circ45∘, the established side lengths can be 111, 111, and 2\sqrt22​.

Both sine and cosine are 1/21/\sqrt21/2​, which rationalizes to 2/2\sqrt2/22​/2. Tangent is 1/11/11/1, or 111. The complete exact values are

Worked example 3 Combine two exact values

Problem. Evaluate the following expression exactly, with no decimal approximation:

sin⁡45∘tan⁡30∘.\frac{\sin45^\circ}{\tan30^\circ}.tan30∘sin45∘​.

Solution. Substitute the values derived from the two special triangles.

sin⁡45∘tan⁡30∘=2/23/3=3223=62.\begin{aligned} \frac{\sin45^\circ}{\tan30^\circ}&=\frac{\sqrt2/2}{\sqrt3/3}\\ &=\frac{3\sqrt2}{2\sqrt3}\\ &=\frac{\sqrt6}{2}. \end{aligned}tan30∘sin45∘​​=3​/32​/2​=23​32​​=26​​.​

Dividing the two ratios is ordinary fraction arithmetic.

Two identities from the same sides

An identity is an equality that holds for every allowed input. Here the allowed angles are acute angles in nondegenerate right triangles.

The notation sin⁡2θ\sin^2\thetasin2θ means (sin⁡θ)2(\sin\theta)^2(sinθ)2, the square of the sine value. It does not mean sin⁡(θ2)\sin(\theta^2)sin(θ2). Cosine squared and tangent squared use the same convention.

The quotient and Pythagorean identities for acute angles#

Take a right triangle with opposite leg 555, adjacent leg 121212, and hypotenuse 131313, relative to θ\thetaθ.

Dividing sine by cosine cancels their common hypotenuse.

5/1312/13=512sin⁡θcos⁡θ=tan⁡θ.\begin{aligned} \frac{5/13}{12/13}&=\frac5{12}\\ \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}&=\tan\theta. \end{aligned}12/135/13​cosθsinθ​​=125​=tanθ.​

Squaring sine and cosine and adding gives

(513)2+(1213)2=25+144169(513)2+(1213)2=1.\begin{aligned} \left(\frac5{13}\right)^2+\left(\frac{12}{13}\right)^2&=\frac{25+144}{169}\\ \left(\frac5{13}\right)^2+\left(\frac{12}{13}\right)^2&=1. \end{aligned}(135​)2+(1312​)2(135​)2+(1312​)2​=16925+144​=1.​

For a general right triangle ABCABCABC, let θ\thetaθ be at AAA, with a=BCa=BCa=BC, b=CAb=CAb=CA, and c=ABc=ABc=AB. These lengths are positive. The same fraction cancellation gives

sin⁡θcos⁡θ=a/cb/csin⁡θcos⁡θ=abtan⁡θ=sin⁡θcos⁡θ.\begin{aligned} \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}&=\frac{a/c}{b/c}\\ \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}&=\frac ab\\ \tan\theta&=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}. \end{aligned}cosθsinθ​cosθsinθ​tanθ​=b/ca/c​=ba​=cosθsinθ​.​

Division is valid because the adjacent leg bbb is positive, so cos⁡θ\cos\thetacosθ is positive. The Pythagorean theorem gives

a2+b2=c2.a^2+b^2=c^2.a2+b2=c2.

Therefore

sin⁡2θ+cos⁡2θ=a2+b2c2sin⁡2θ+cos⁡2θ=1.\begin{aligned} \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta&=\frac{a^2+b^2}{c^2}\\ \sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta&=1. \end{aligned}sin2θ+cos2θsin2θ+cos2θ​=c2a2+b2​=1.​

The numerical triangle only illustrated the two cancellations. AA makes these definitions valid for any triangle with the chosen angle, and the two arguments apply to every acute θ\thetaθ.

∎

Worked example 4 Recover related ratios

Problem. An acute angle θ\thetaθ satisfies

cos⁡θ=38.\cos\theta=\frac38.cosθ=83​.

Find sin⁡θ\sin\thetasinθ and tan⁡θ\tan\thetatanθ exactly.

Solution. The squared-value identity gives

sin⁡2θ=1−(38)2sin⁡2θ=5564.\begin{aligned} \sin^2\theta&=1-\left(\frac38\right)^2\\ \sin^2\theta&=\frac{55}{64}. \end{aligned}sin2θsin2θ​=1−(83​)2=6455​.​

The sine is positive for an acute angle, so

sin⁡θ=558.\sin\theta=\frac{\sqrt{55}}8.sinθ=855​​.

Now divide sine by cosine.

tan⁡θ=55/83/8tan⁡θ=553.\begin{aligned} \tan\theta&=\frac{\sqrt{55}/8}{3/8}\\ \tan\theta&=\frac{\sqrt{55}}3. \end{aligned}tanθtanθ​=3/855​/8​=355​​.​

Check your understanding

For an acute θ, which statement is correct?

Answer choices

Pythagoras gives the sum of the squared ratios, not the sum of the ratios themselves.

sin⁡2θ+cos⁡2θ=1.\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1.sin2θ+cos2θ=1.

Tangent is sine divided by cosine, and cosine is positive for an acute angle.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Reference angle. Name the selected acute angle before calling a leg opposite or adjacent.
  • Adjacent leg. Exclude the hypotenuse, even though it touches the reference angle.
  • Size and ratio. Enlarging a triangle changes its side lengths but preserves ratios at corresponding angles.
  • Complements. Sine and cosine exchange for complementary acute angles. They do not usually equal each other at the same angle.
  • Squares. The identity adds squared sine and cosine values, not unsquared values.

Key takeaways

  • Sine, cosine, and tangent compare sides relative to a selected acute angle.
  • AA similarity proves that their values depend on the angle rather than the triangle’s size.
  • Switching to the other acute angle exchanges sine and cosine and reverses the tangent ratio.
  • Special right triangles supply exact values at 30∘30^\circ30∘, 45∘45^\circ45∘, and 60∘60^\circ60∘.
  • For acute angles, tangent is sine divided by cosine, and the squared sine and cosine values add to 111.

A bit of history (optional)

The word sine traveled through several languages before reaching English. An Indian word associated with a bowstring, jya, was carried into Arabic as a similar-sounding word. Arabic mathematical writing often left out vowels. Later readers interpreted the consonants as a different word, one associated with a bay or inlet.

A Latin translation used sinus, a word with a related meaning, and English eventually adopted sine. The broad journey is well documented, but accounts differ about which translator first made the Latin choice. The MacTutor account of the word preserves those competing attributions.
