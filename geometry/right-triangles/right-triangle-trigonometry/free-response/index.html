  1. Problem 1 Three side ratios

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. Use AAA as the acute reference angle.

    The lengths are

    BC=28 cm.BC=28\text{ cm}.BC=28 cm.

    Also,

    CA=45 cm.CA=45\text{ cm}.CA=45 cm.

    The hypotenuse is

    AB=53 cm.AB=53\text{ cm}.AB=53 cm.

    Find sine, cosine, and tangent of AAA exactly, and state whether these three answers carry length units.

    Text description of this figure

    ABC is right at C. BC is 28 centimeters, CA is 45 centimeters, and AB is 53 centimeters. The selected reference angle is A.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify the leg across from AAA and the leg meeting AAA.

    2. Hint 2

      Each ratio compares lengths measured in the same unit, so the units cancel.

    Answer

    sin⁡A=2853cos⁡A=4553tan⁡A=2845.\begin{aligned}\sin A&=\frac{28}{53}\\\cos A&=\frac{45}{53}\\\tan A&=\frac{28}{45}.\end{aligned}sinAcosAtanA​=5328​=5345​=4528​.​

    All three values are unitless.

    Full solution

    For angle AAA, the opposite leg is BCBCBC and the adjacent leg is CACACA.

    The hypotenuse is ABABAB.

    The definitions give

    sin⁡A=2853.\sin A=\frac{28}{53}.sinA=5328​.

    The adjacent-to-hypotenuse ratio gives

    cos⁡A=4553.\cos A=\frac{45}{53}.cosA=5345​.

    The opposite-to-adjacent ratio gives

    tan⁡A=2845.\tan A=\frac{28}{45}.tanA=4528​.

    Each ratio divides centimeters by centimeters, leaving a number with no length unit.

    Answer

    sin⁡A=2853cos⁡A=4553tan⁡A=2845.\begin{aligned}\sin A&=\frac{28}{53}\\\cos A&=\frac{45}{53}\\\tan A&=\frac{28}{45}.\end{aligned}sinAcosAtanA​=5328​=5345​=4528​.​

    All three values are unitless.

    Key idea

    Select the angle first, then divide the named side lengths.

  3. Problem 3 One side, two roles

    Triangle PQRPQRPQR is right at QQQ.

    (a) Select PPP as the reference angle. Identify its opposite leg, adjacent leg, and hypotenuse.

    (b) Select RRR instead. Identify all three roles again.

    (c) Which role of side PRPRPR changes when the selected angle changes?

    Text description of this figure

    PQR is right at Q. Both acute angles P and R are marked with distinct arc counts. No side-role labels are supplied.

    1. Hint 1

      The hypotenuse is fixed by the right angle.

    2. Hint 2

      The side across from one acute angle touches the other acute angle.

    Answer

    Part (a): opposite QRQRQR, adjacent PQPQPQ, hypotenuse PRPRPR. Part (b): opposite PQPQPQ, adjacent QRQRQR, hypotenuse PRPRPR. Part (c): none; PRPRPR stays the hypotenuse.

    Full solution

    Part (a): QRQRQR is across from PPP.

    Leg PQPQPQ touches PPP, while PRPRPR is across from the right angle at QQQ.

    Part (b): PQPQPQ is across from RRR.

    Leg QRQRQR touches RRR, and PRPRPR is still opposite the right angle.

    Part (c): the opposite and adjacent leg roles exchange, but PRPRPR remains the hypotenuse.

    Answer

    Part (a): opposite QRQRQR, adjacent PQPQPQ, hypotenuse PRPRPR. Part (b): opposite PQPQPQ, adjacent QRQRQR, hypotenuse PRPRPR. Part (c): none; PRPRPR stays the hypotenuse.

    Key idea

    Opposite and adjacent depend on the selected acute angle; hypotenuse does not.

  4. Problem 4 Two sizes with one angle

    Triangles ABCABCABC and DEFDEFDEF are right at CCC and FFF. Their acute angles at AAA and DDD have the same measure θ\thetaθ.

    Prove that the sine, cosine, and tangent values from these two reference angles are equal. State the ordered correspondence and explain how the length scale cancels.

    Text description of this figure

    Two right triangles have equal selected acute angles. Their corresponding sides are labeled a,b,c and ka,kb,kc. The right angles and selected acute angles identify the ordered correspondence.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the common acute angle together with the two right angles.

    2. Hint 2

      Match opposite with opposite, adjacent with adjacent, and hypotenuse with hypotenuse.

    3. Hint 3

      Write the second triangle's side lengths as one positive factor times the corresponding first lengths.

    Answer

    △ABC∼△DEF\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF△ABC∼△DEF

    by AA, and

    sin⁡A=sin⁡Dcos⁡A=cos⁡Dtan⁡A=tan⁡D.\begin{aligned}\sin A&=\sin D\\\cos A&=\cos D\\\tan A&=\tan D.\end{aligned}sinAcosAtanA​=sinD=cosD=tanD.​

    Full solution

    The angle at AAA matches the angle at DDD, and the right angle at CCC matches the right angle at FFF.

    AA gives the order ABCABCABC to DEFDEFDEF, with BBB matching EEE.

    Let the first triangle's opposite leg, adjacent leg, and hypotenuse be a,b,ca,b,ca,b,c.

    For a positive scale factor kkk to the second triangle, the corresponding lengths are ka,kb,kcka,kb,kcka,kb,kc.

    The sine ratio is unchanged because

    kakc=ac.\frac{ka}{kc}=\frac ac.kcka​=ca​.

    The cosine ratio is unchanged because

    kbkc=bc.\frac{kb}{kc}=\frac bc.kckb​=cb​.

    The tangent ratio is unchanged because

    kakb=ab.\frac{ka}{kb}=\frac ab.kbka​=ba​.

    The side lengths are positive, so these divisions are valid.

    The three common values depend on the angle rather than the size.

    Answer

    △ABC∼△DEF\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF△ABC∼△DEF

    by AA, and

    sin⁡A=sin⁡Dcos⁡A=cos⁡Dtan⁡A=tan⁡D.\begin{aligned}\sin A&=\sin D\\\cos A&=\cos D\\\tan A&=\tan D.\end{aligned}sinAcosAtanA​=sinD=cosD=tanD.​

    Key idea

    AA establishes the correspondence before common factors are canceled.

  5. Problem 5 A recorded set of ratios

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. The chosen reference angle is BBB.

    The lengths are

    BC=39.BC=39.BC=39.

    Also,

    CA=80.CA=80.CA=80.

    The hypotenuse is

    AB=89.AB=89.AB=89.

    A student writes the following ratios.

    sin⁡B=3989.\sin B=\frac{39}{89}.sinB=8939​. cos⁡B=8089.\cos B=\frac{80}{89}.cosB=8980​. tan⁡B=3980.\tan B=\frac{39}{80}.tanB=8039​.

    Explain the common side-role error and replace all three ratios with correct values.

    Text description of this figure

    ABC is right at C, with BC 39, CA 80, and AB 89. Angle B is the selected reference angle. The triangle is turned.

    1. Hint 1

      Locate the side across from the chosen angle BBB.

    2. Hint 2

      The student's numerators would fit the other acute reference angle.

    3. Hint 3

      Keep the hypotenuse fixed while exchanging the two leg roles.

    Answer

    The student used angle A's leg roles.

    sin⁡B=8089cos⁡B=3989tan⁡B=8039.\begin{aligned}\sin B&=\frac{80}{89}\\\cos B&=\frac{39}{89}\\\tan B&=\frac{80}{39}.\end{aligned}sinBcosBtanB​=8980​=8939​=3980​.​

    Full solution

    For reference angle BBB, the opposite leg is CACACA, with length 808080.

    The adjacent leg is BCBCBC, with length 393939, and the hypotenuse stays ABABAB.

    The student's ratios use BCBCBC as opposite and CACACA as adjacent.

    Those are the roles for AAA, not BBB.

    Using the correct opposite leg gives

    sin⁡B=8089.\sin B=\frac{80}{89}.sinB=8980​.

    Using the correct adjacent leg gives

    cos⁡B=3989.\cos B=\frac{39}{89}.cosB=8939​.

    Dividing the correct opposite leg by the adjacent leg gives

    tan⁡B=8039.\tan B=\frac{80}{39}.tanB=3980​.

    Answer

    The student used angle A's leg roles.

    sin⁡B=8089cos⁡B=3989tan⁡B=8039.\begin{aligned}\sin B&=\frac{80}{89}\\\cos B&=\frac{39}{89}\\\tan B&=\frac{80}{39}.\end{aligned}sinBcosBtanB​=8980​=8939​=3980​.​

    Key idea

    One wrong reference angle can exchange every leg ratio in a calculation.

  6. Problem 6 Turn the picture, then change the angle

    Triangle JKLJKLJKL is right at JJJ. Initially, KKK is selected as the reference angle.

    Case A: Rotate the drawing through a half-turn while keeping KKK selected. Name the opposite leg, adjacent leg, and hypotenuse after the turn.

    Case B: Keep that turned drawing, but select LLL instead. Name the three side roles now and explain which change came from switching the angle.

    Text description of this figure

    JKL is right at J, and K is initially marked as the reference angle. No numerical lengths or side-role labels are given.

    1. Hint 1

      A rotation preserves the named vertices and all incidence.

    2. Hint 2

      A new reference angle can change the leg roles even when the picture stays still.

    Answer

    Case A: opposite JLJLJL, adjacent JKJKJK, hypotenuse KLKLKL. Case B: opposite JKJKJK, adjacent JLJLJL, hypotenuse KLKLKL. Only switching from K to L exchanges the legs.

    Full solution

    Case A: the original side across from KKK is JLJLJL, and rotation does not change that incidence.

    The adjacent leg remains JKJKJK, and KLKLKL remains the hypotenuse.

    Case B: side JKJKJK is across from LLL, so it is now opposite.

    Side JLJLJL is the adjacent leg.

    The side across from the unchanged right angle at JJJ is still KLKLKL.

    Answer

    Case A: opposite JLJLJL, adjacent JKJKJK, hypotenuse KLKLKL. Case B: opposite JKJKJK, adjacent JLJLJL, hypotenuse KLKLKL. Only switching from K to L exchanges the legs.

    Key idea

    Rotation changes placement; a reference-angle switch changes opposite and adjacent roles.

  7. Problem 7 Compare complementary angles

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. Its acute angles are α\alphaα at AAA and β\betaβ at BBB.

    Prove that the sine of either acute angle equals the cosine of the other. Also prove that their tangent values multiply to 111.

    Text description of this figure

    ABC is right at C. The acute angles alpha at A and beta at B are marked with distinct arc counts. No unknown ratio values are supplied.

    1. Hint 1

      The two acute angles total 90∘90^\circ90∘, and the hypotenuse is the same for both.

    2. Hint 2

      A leg opposite one acute angle is adjacent to the other.

    3. Hint 3

      Write the two tangent values using the two positive leg lengths in opposite orders.

    Answer

    sin⁡α=cos⁡βcos⁡α=sin⁡βtan⁡αtan⁡β=1.\begin{aligned}\sin\alpha&=\cos\beta\\\cos\alpha&=\sin\beta\\\tan\alpha\tan\beta&=1.\end{aligned}sinαcosαtanαtanβ​=cosβ=sinβ=1.​

    Full solution

    Both acute angles share hypotenuse ABABAB.

    The side BCBCBC is opposite α\alphaα and adjacent to β\betaβ.

    Therefore the identical side ratio gives

    sin⁡α=cos⁡β.\sin\alpha=\cos\beta.sinα=cosβ.

    Similarly, side CACACA is adjacent to α\alphaα and opposite β\betaβ, with the same hypotenuse.

    Thus

    cos⁡α=sin⁡β.\cos\alpha=\sin\beta.cosα=sinβ.

    The tangent product is

    tan⁡αtan⁡β=BCCA⋅CABC.\tan\alpha\tan\beta=\frac{BC}{CA}\cdot\frac{CA}{BC}.tanαtanβ=CABC​⋅BCCA​.

    Both legs are positive, so both factors cancel, leaving 111.

    Answer

    sin⁡α=cos⁡βcos⁡α=sin⁡βtan⁡αtan⁡β=1.\begin{aligned}\sin\alpha&=\cos\beta\\\cos\alpha&=\sin\beta\\\tan\alpha\tan\beta&=1.\end{aligned}sinαcosαtanαtanβ​=cosβ=sinβ=1.​

    Key idea

    Complementary-angle relationships come from exchanging two positive legs.

  8. Problem 8 Two identities from three lengths

    Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC, with acute reference angle θ\thetaθ at AAA.

    Use the positive length names

    a=BC.a=BC.a=BC.

    Also,

    b=CA.b=CA.b=CA.

    Finally,

    c=AB.c=AB.c=AB.

    Prove that tangent is sine divided by cosine and that the squares of sine and cosine add to 111. Explain why each division in the argument is allowed.

    Text description of this figure

    ABC is right at C, with acute theta at A. BC is labeled a, CA is b, and AB is c. The turned picture preserves the specified side names.

    1. Hint 1

      Write all three ratios using a,b,ca,b,ca,b,c.

    2. Hint 2

      For the quotient, divide two fractions with the same denominator.

    3. Hint 3

      For the squared-value identity, use the already established Pythagorean theorem and divide by c2c^2c2.

    Answer

    tan⁡θ=sin⁡θcos⁡θsin⁡2θ+cos⁡2θ=1.\begin{aligned}\tan\theta&=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\\\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta&=1.\end{aligned}tanθsin2θ+cos2θ​=cosθsinθ​=1.​

    All denominators are positive.

    Full solution

    The side definitions give

    sin⁡θ=ac.\sin\theta=\frac ac.sinθ=ca​.

    They give

    cos⁡θ=bc.\cos\theta=\frac bc.cosθ=cb​.

    They also give

    tan⁡θ=ab.\tan\theta=\frac ab.tanθ=ba​.

    Dividing sine by cosine cancels their common hypotenuse:

    a/cb/c=ab.\frac{a/c}{b/c}=\frac ab.b/ca/c​=ba​.

    The division is allowed because bbb and ccc are positive, making cosine positive.

    Pythagoras gives

    a2+b2=c2.a^2+b^2=c^2.a2+b2=c2.

    Divide by positive c2c^2c2 to obtain

    a2c2+b2c2=1.\frac{a^2}{c^2}+\frac{b^2}{c^2}=1.c2a2​+c2b2​=1.

    The two fractions on the left are the squared sine and cosine, respectively.

    Answer

    tan⁡θ=sin⁡θcos⁡θsin⁡2θ+cos⁡2θ=1.\begin{aligned}\tan\theta&=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\\\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta&=1.\end{aligned}tanθsin2θ+cos2θ​=cosθsinθ​=1.​

    All denominators are positive.

    Key idea

    These identities share the same side definitions and require positive denominators.

  9. Problem 9 Four written lines

    An acute angle θ\thetaθ is known to satisfy sin⁡θ=8/17\sin\theta=8/17sinθ=8/17. A student writes:

    Line 1:

    sin⁡2θ=64289.\sin^2\theta=\frac{64}{289}.sin2θ=28964​.

    Line 2:

    cos⁡2θ=225289.\cos^2\theta=\frac{225}{289}.cos2θ=289225​.

    Line 3:

    cos⁡θ=−1517.\cos\theta=-\frac{15}{17}.cosθ=−1715​.

    Line 4:

    tan⁡θ=−815.\tan\theta=-\frac8{15}.tanθ=−158​.

    Identify the first invalid line. Explain why it fails, and give the corrected cosine and tangent values.

    1. Hint 1

      Check both the squared-value calculation and the allowed signs.

    2. Hint 2

      Sine, cosine, and tangent are all positive for acute angles.

    3. Hint 3

      After choosing the allowed cosine sign, use the quotient identity for tangent.

    Answer

    Line 3 is first invalid because acute-angle cosine is positive.

    cos⁡θ=1517tan⁡θ=815.\begin{aligned}\cos\theta&=\frac{15}{17}\\\tan\theta&=\frac8{15}.\end{aligned}cosθtanθ​=1715​=158​.​

    Full solution

    Line 1 correctly squares the supplied value.

    Line 2 correctly uses

    1−64289=225289.1-\frac{64}{289}=\frac{225}{289}.1−28964​=289225​.

    Line 3 chooses a negative cosine, but cosine is a positive adjacent leg divided by a positive hypotenuse in this acute-angle domain.

    The permitted root is

    cos⁡θ=1517.\cos\theta=\frac{15}{17}.cosθ=1715​.

    The quotient identity then gives

    tan⁡θ=8/1715/17.\tan\theta=\frac{8/17}{15/17}.tanθ=15/178/17​.

    Cancel the common denominator to get

    tan⁡θ=815.\tan\theta=\frac8{15}.tanθ=158​.

    Answer

    Line 3 is first invalid because acute-angle cosine is positive.

    cos⁡θ=1517tan⁡θ=815.\begin{aligned}\cos\theta&=\frac{15}{17}\\\tan\theta&=\frac8{15}.\end{aligned}cosθtanθ​=1715​=158​.​

    A squared equality alone does not decide the sign; the angle domain does.

  10. Problem 10 Test proposed pairs

    Each case proposes a sine value first and a cosine value second for one acute angle. Decide whether such an angle can exist, and justify each decision.

    Case A: 12/1312/1312/13 and 5/135/135/13.

    Case B: 3/73/73/7 and 4/74/74/7.

    Case C: 000 and 111.

    State the general positivity and squared-value tests for such a pair. Explain why passing both tests allows a right-triangle model.

    1. Hint 1

      A necessary identity connects the squares of the two values.

    2. Hint 2

      Strict positivity matters in the acute-angle domain, even when the squared values add to 111.

    3. Hint 3

      For two positive numbers whose squares sum to 111, construct perpendicular legs of those lengths.

    Answer

    Case A is possible; Cases B and C are not. In general,

    s>0d>0s2+d2=1.\begin{aligned}s&>0\\d&>0\\s^2+d^2&=1.\end{aligned}sds2+d2​>0>0=1.​

    These conditions give a nondegenerate right-triangle model with hypotenuse 111.

    Full solution

    Case A passes because both values are positive and

    (1213)2+(513)2=1.\left(\frac{12}{13}\right)^2+\left(\frac5{13}\right)^2=1.(1312​)2+(135​)2=1.

    A right triangle with opposite leg 121212, adjacent leg 555, and hypotenuse 131313 realizes this pair.

    Case B fails the identity because

    (37)2+(47)2=2549.\left(\frac37\right)^2+\left(\frac47\right)^2=\frac{25}{49}.(73​)2+(74​)2=4925​.

    Case C passes the squared-value identity but fails strict positivity.

    A zero opposite leg would collapse the right-triangle model, so it cannot represent an acute reference angle.

    For the general test, let sss and ddd be the proposed sine and cosine.

    Both must be strictly positive, and their squares must sum to 111.

    Conversely, construct perpendicular legs of positive lengths sss and ddd.

    Pythagoras gives hypotenuse 111 from the assumed squared sum.

    The angle opposite leg sss is acute, and its sine and cosine are exactly sss and ddd.

    Answer

    Case A is possible; Cases B and C are not. In general,

    s>0d>0s2+d2=1.\begin{aligned}s&>0\\d&>0\\s^2+d^2&=1.\end{aligned}sds2+d2​>0>0=1.​

    These conditions give a nondegenerate right-triangle model with hypotenuse 111.

    Key idea

    An identity and a domain restriction must both be checked.
