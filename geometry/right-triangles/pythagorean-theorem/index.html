The Pythagorean Theorem and Converse

▾

Watch the video explainer

Intuition

Two perpendicular rods fix the distance between their free ends. You may already know a rule for calculating that distance. Now the similar triangles made by a hypotenuse altitude will explain why the rule works. Then we will turn the question around: can three lengths tell you whether a corner is right?

Learning goals

  • Identify the hypotenuse before finding an exact missing side
  • Derive the Pythagorean theorem from the hypotenuse altitude relations
  • Prove and apply the converse using a constructed right triangle
  • Classify possible triangles by comparing the squares of their sides
  • Recognize common Pythagorean triples and their positive multiples

Choose the side across from the right angle

The hypotenuse is the side opposite the right angle in a right triangle. The two sides meeting at that angle are its legs. The hypotenuse is the longest side because it faces the largest angle, as proved in Sides, Angles, and Shortest Distances.

For example, a right triangle with legs 888 and 151515 units has a hypotenuse of 171717 units. The squares fit a useful pattern:

82+152=64+225,82+152=289,172=289.\begin{aligned} 8^2+15^2&=64+225,\\ 8^2+15^2&=289,\\ 17^2&=289. \end{aligned}82+15282+152172​=64+225,=289,=289.​

This relationship is about the side opposite the right angle, wherever the triangle points. A letter such as ccc names a length; it does not make that side a hypotenuse.

Why similarity gives the sum of squares

Take a nondegenerate triangle ABCABCABC with a right angle at CCC. Use the chapter’s notation: aaa names BCBCBC, bbb names CACACA, and ccc names ABABAB. Let HHH be the perpendicular foot from CCC to the open segment ABABAB. Write ppp for AHAHAH, qqq for HBHBHB, and hhh for CHCHCH.

In Similarity in Right Triangles, AA gave the correctly ordered similarities

△ABC∼△ACH∼△CBH.\triangle ABC\sim\triangle ACH\sim\triangle CBH.△ABC∼△ACH∼△CBH.

The corresponding side ratios gave these prior results:

b2=cp,a2=cq.\begin{aligned} b^2&=cp,\\ a^2&=cq. \end{aligned}b2a2​=cp,=cq.​

Each leg’s square is a product of the whole hypotenuse and the piece next to that leg. Adding those products uses both pieces of the same hypotenuse.

The Pythagorean theorem from similarity#

First use a right triangle whose legs are 999 and 121212 units, with hypotenuse 151515 units. Its altitude divides the hypotenuse into pieces 5.45.45.4 and 9.69.69.6 units, adjacent to the 999-unit and 121212-unit legs, respectively. The prior similarity relations give

92=15(5.4),122=15(9.6).\begin{aligned} 9^2&=15(5.4),\\ 12^2&=15(9.6). \end{aligned}92122​=15(5.4),=15(9.6).​

Add these relations and factor out the common whole hypotenuse:

92+122=15(5.4+9.6),92+122=15(15),92+122=152.\begin{aligned} 9^2+12^2&=15(5.4+9.6),\\ 9^2+12^2&=15(15),\\ 9^2+12^2&=15^2. \end{aligned}92+12292+12292+122​=15(5.4+9.6),=15(15),=152.​

For the general triangle above, the same addition gives

a2+b2=cq+cp,a2+b2=c(q+p).\begin{aligned} a^2+b^2&=cq+cp,\\ a^2+b^2&=c(q+p). \end{aligned}a2+b2a2+b2​=cq+cp,=c(q+p).​

Since HHH lies between AAA and BBB, segment addition gives

p+q=c.p+q=c.p+q=c.

Substitute that whole length for the two pieces:

a2+b2=c2.a^2+b^2=c^2.a2+b2=c2.

Nothing in the argument depends on the numerical example. The two similar-triangle relations and the interior foot are what make it work for every right triangle.

∎

The Pythagorean theorem states that in a nondegenerate right triangle, the sum of the squares of the legs equals the square of the hypotenuse. All three lengths must use the same unit.

Find a missing side

For a missing hypotenuse, add the leg squares. For a missing leg, subtract the known leg square from the hypotenuse square. Take the positive root because a side length is positive. Keep a radical for an exact answer; use an approximation sign for a rounded decimal.

Worked example 1 Two known legs

Problem. A right triangle has legs 777 cm and 101010 cm. Find its hypotenuse exactly and to the nearest tenth of a centimeter.

Solution. The unknown side ttt is opposite the marked right angle, so

t2=72+102,t2=149,t=149.\begin{aligned} t^2&=7^2+10^2,\\ t^2&=149,\\ t&=\sqrt{149}. \end{aligned}t2t2t​=72+102,=149,=149​.​

There is no perfect-square factor greater than 111 in 149149149. The exact length is 149\sqrt{149}149​ cm. Rounding only at the end gives

t≈12.2 cm.t\approx12.2\text{ cm}.t≈12.2 cm.

This is longer than either leg and shorter than their sum, as expected.

Worked example 2 A rotated triangle

Problem. Triangle QRTQRTQRT has a right angle at RRR. Side QTQTQT is 141414 m and side RQRQRQ is 999 m. Find RTRTRT exactly.

Solution. The hypotenuse is QTQTQT, across from RRR. The unknown RTRTRT is a leg. Therefore,

RT2+92=142,RT2=196−81,RT2=115,RT=115.\begin{aligned} RT^2+9^2&=14^2,\\ RT^2&=196-81,\\ RT^2&=115,\\ RT&=\sqrt{115}. \end{aligned}RT2+92RT2RT2RT​=142,=196−81,=115,=115​.​

The exact answer is 115\sqrt{115}115​ m. Its square is less than 196196196, so this leg is shorter than the hypotenuse.

The missing side is a leg. Subtract its companion leg's square from the hypotenuse square.

x2=112−62,x2=85,x=85.\begin{aligned}x^2&=11^2-6^2,\\x^2&=85,\\x&=\sqrt{85}.\end{aligned}x2x2x​=112−62,=85,=85​.​

Taking the positive square root gives a length shorter than 11 units.

Turn the theorem around

The theorem starts with a right angle and concludes an equality of squares. Its converse starts with that equality and concludes a right angle. That direction needs its own proof.

For example, lengths 121212, 161616, and 202020 units satisfy

122+162=202.12^2+16^2=20^2.122+162=202.

Construct a right triangle with legs 121212 and 161616. The theorem forces its hypotenuse to be 202020. SSS then matches any triangle with these three side lengths to the constructed right triangle.

The converse of the Pythagorean theorem#

In the numerical construction, the right triangle’s hypotenuse ddd satisfies

d2=122+162,d2=400,d=20.\begin{aligned} d^2&=12^2+16^2,\\ d^2&=400,\\ d&=20. \end{aligned}d2d2d​=122+162,=400,=20.​

Its three sides match the given triangle’s 121212, 161616, and 202020 units. SSS congruence transfers the right angle to the angle opposite the 202020-unit side. The particular numbers served only to match the third sides.

Now let a nondegenerate triangle have positive side lengths aaa, bbb, and ccc, with

a2+b2=c2.a^2+b^2=c^2.a2+b2=c2.

Construct perpendicular rays and copy lengths aaa and bbb onto them. Join their endpoints to make a right triangle, and call its hypotenuse ddd. Apply the already proved theorem to this constructed triangle:

d2=a2+b2.d^2=a^2+b^2.d2=a2+b2.

The assumed equality gives

d2=c2.d^2=c^2.d2=c2.

Since both lengths are positive, they are equal. SSS makes the constructed triangle congruent to the given triangle. The angle opposite ccc therefore matches the constructed right angle.

∎

Worked example 3 Three supplied sides

Problem. A triangle has side lengths 202020, 212121, and 292929 cm. Determine whether it has a right angle and identify the side opposite that angle.

Solution. The lengths form a triangle because the two shorter lengths add to more than the longest:

20+21>29.20+21>29.20+21>29.

Compare the squares with 292929 as the longest length:

202+212=841,292=841.\begin{aligned} 20^2+21^2&=841,\\ 29^2&=841. \end{aligned}202+212292​=841,=841.​

By the converse, the angle opposite the 292929 cm side is right. That side is the hypotenuse.

Answer choices

Only the constructed triangle is known to be right at first. The established theorem gives

d2=c2.d^2=c^2.d2=c2.

Positive side lengths give the third matching pair. SSS then transfers the right angle to the given triangle.

Compare with a right angle

Before classifying lengths, check that they are positive and can make a triangle. When the longest is zzz and the others are xxx and yyy, it is enough to check

x+y>z.x+y>z.x+y>z.

For fixed sides 666 and 888, a right triangle has third side 101010. A third side of 999 closes the included angle below 90∘90^\circ90∘; a third side of 121212 opens it above 90∘90^\circ90∘.

Here is why this comparison works without a new formula. Construct a right triangle with legs xxx and yyy, and call its hypotenuse rrr. The theorem gives

r2=x2+y2.r^2=x^2+y^2.r2=x2+y2.

The original triangle and this reference triangle have two matching side pairs. By the converse hinge theorem, a third side shorter than rrr faces a smaller included angle; a third side longer than rrr faces a larger one. Equal third sides give a right angle by the converse proved above. Positive lengths have the same order as their squares.

Since zzz is longest, its opposite angle is largest. If that angle is acute, all three angles are acute. Thus, for an existing nondegenerate triangle:

Longest-side comparisonTriangle type
z2<x2+y2z^2<x^2+y^2z2<x2+y2Acute
z2=x2+y2z^2=x^2+y^2z2=x2+y2Right
z2>x2+y2z^2>x^2+y^2z2>x2+y2Obtuse

Worked example 4 Three length sets

Problem. Decide whether each set makes a nondegenerate triangle. Classify each triangle that exists.

Case A: 333, 777, and 101010 units.

Case B: 777, 999, and 111111 units.

Case C: 777, 999, and 131313 units.

Solution. Case A is flat because

3+7=10.3+7=10.3+7=10.

It is not a nondegenerate triangle, so it receives no acute, right, or obtuse classification.

Case B exists because 7+9>117+9>117+9>11. Compare the squares:

72+92=130,112=121.\begin{aligned} 7^2+9^2&=130,\\ 11^2&=121. \end{aligned}72+92112​=130,=121.​

The longest-side square is smaller, so Case B is acute.

Case C exists because 7+9>137+9>137+9>13. Its longest-side square is

132=169.13^2=169.132=169.

This exceeds 130130130, so Case C is obtuse. Failing the right-triangle equality alone would not distinguish these last two cases.

Recognize triples and their multiples

A Pythagorean triple is a set of three positive integers whose two smaller squares add to the largest square. Common examples are (3,4,5)(3,4,5)(3,4,5), (5,12,13)(5,12,13)(5,12,13), (7,24,25)(7,24,25)(7,24,25), and (8,15,17)(8,15,17)(8,15,17). Verify a triple by squaring, rather than relying on a partly remembered list.

Multiplying all three lengths by the same positive factor preserves the right angle by similarity. Algebra gives the same reason: for example, multiplying (3,4,5)(3,4,5)(3,4,5) by 222 multiplies both sides of the squared equality by 444.

62+82=4(32+42),62+82=4(52),62+82=102.\begin{aligned} 6^2+8^2&=4(3^2+4^2),\\ 6^2+8^2&=4(5^2),\\ 6^2+8^2&=10^2. \end{aligned}62+8262+8262+82​=4(32+42),=4(52),=102.​

In general, a positive factor kkk gives

(ka)2+(kb)2=(kc)2.(ka)^2+(kb)^2=(kc)^2.(ka)2+(kb)2=(kc)2.

An integer multiple is another integer triple. A noninteger positive factor still gives a right triangle, though its lengths need not be integers. Adding the same number to all three lengths does not have this property.

Worked example 5 A scaled set

Problem. A triangle has sides 151515, 363636, and 393939 cm. Identify a common triple and its scale factor, then decide whether the triangle is right.

Solution. Divide every length by 333 to obtain (5,12,13)(5,12,13)(5,12,13). The scale factor from that triangle to the given triangle is 333. Check the original integer triple:

52+122=132.5^2+12^2=13^2.52+122=132.

The given sides are all multiplied by the same positive factor, so their squares are all multiplied by 999. The given triangle is right, with the 393939 cm hypotenuse.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Hypotenuse identification. Locate the right angle first; the side across from it is the hypotenuse.
  • Missing legs. Subtract squares when the hypotenuse is already known. A leg cannot exceed it.
  • Roots of sums. The square root of a sum is not generally the sum of its square roots.
  • Classification. Check triangle existence, then compare the longest side’s square with the sum of the others.
  • Exactness. Rounded measurements that nearly satisfy the equality do not prove an exactly right angle.

Key takeaways

  • In a right triangle, the leg squares add to the hypotenuse square.
  • Similarity proves the theorem by joining the two hypotenuse pieces in the leg-square relations.
  • The converse uses a constructed right triangle and SSS to recover a right angle from three lengths.
  • For a valid triangle, a longest-side square below, equal to, or above the other square sum means acute, right, or obtuse.
  • Positive multiples of a Pythagorean triple preserve its right angle.

A bit of history (optional)

Euclid’s Elements gives the right-triangle theorem and its converse separate propositions. In Book I, Proposition 48, he constructs a perpendicular side, copies a given length, and uses the preceding theorem to match a third side. Congruence then transfers the right angle. The separate proposition records a distinction that matters in proof: reversing a statement requires an argument of its own. You can read the translated proposition and construction.
