Solving Right Triangles

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

A right angle leaves two acute angles. If you know one of them and a side whose role relative to that angle is specified, the shape and size are fixed. If you know two sides, their ratio can reveal an angle instead. Solving a right triangle means finding its missing side lengths and angle measures. The first decision is which information can work together.

Learning goals

  • Choose a useful ratio or theorem from the given information
  • Find missing sides by solving a trigonometric ratio equation
  • Recover acute angles with valid inverse operations in degree mode
  • Preserve calculator precision and check lengths, angles, and ratios
  • Decide whether the supplied data determine a right triangle

Match a method to the data

The ratios from Sine, Cosine, and Tangent connect an acute reference angle to two sides. For that chosen angle, write the opposite leg as opp\text{opp}opp, the adjacent leg as adj\text{adj}adj, and the hypotenuse as hyp\text{hyp}hyp. The adjacent leg excludes the hypotenuse.

For example, with a 30∘30^\circ30∘ angle and hypotenuse 181818 units, the opposite leg is 999 units:

sin⁡30∘=918,9=18sin⁡30∘.\begin{aligned} \sin30^\circ&=\frac9{18},\\ 9&=18\sin30^\circ. \end{aligned}sin30∘9​=189​,=18sin30∘.​

The known angle supplies a ratio; algebra then supplies a length.

Choose a ratio containing the known side and the side you want. Writing an equation first keeps multiplying and dividing from becoming a guess.

Side pair relative to the selected acute angleRatio
Opposite and hypotenusesin⁡θ=opp/hyp\sin\theta=\text{opp}/\text{hyp}sinθ=opp/hyp
Adjacent leg and hypotenusecos⁡θ=adj/hyp\cos\theta=\text{adj}/\text{hyp}cosθ=adj/hyp
Opposite and adjacent legstan⁡θ=opp/adj\tan\theta=\text{opp}/\text{adj}tanθ=opp/adj

With two known sides and a missing third side, Pythagoras gives a direct route. With two known sides and a missing acute angle, reverse a ratio using an inverse operation, introduced below. The other acute angle follows by subtracting from 90∘90^\circ90∘.

Check your understanding

A right triangle has a known hypotenuse and a known acute angle θ\thetaθ. Write the opposite leg as opp\text{opp}opp and the hypotenuse as hyp\text{hyp}hyp. Which equation directly finds the opposite leg?

Answer choices

Opposite and hypotenuse are the two sides in the sine ratio.

sin⁡θ=opphyp.\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}.sinθ=hypopp​.

Multiplying by the hypotenuse gives

opp=hypsin⁡θ.\text{opp}=\text{hyp}\sin\theta.opp=hypsinθ.

Find sides from a known angle

Use degree mode for every calculator calculation in this lesson. A quick check is that entering the sine of 30∘30^\circ30∘ gives 0.50.50.5. Enter the angle as the input to the trigonometric function, then use the resulting ratio in the equation.

Worked example 1 A given hypotenuse

Problem. Triangle ABCABCABC is right at CCC. Its hypotenuse ABABAB is 141414 cm, and angle AAA measures 37∘37^\circ37∘. Find both legs to the nearest tenth of a centimeter and find angle BBB.

Solution. Relative to AAA, side BCBCBC is opposite and CACACA is the adjacent leg. Use each with the original known hypotenuse:

sin⁡37∘=BC14,BC=14sin⁡37∘,BC≈8.4 cm.\begin{aligned} \sin37^\circ&=\frac{BC}{14},\\ BC&=14\sin37^\circ,\\ BC&\approx8.4\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}sin37∘BCBC​=14BC​,=14sin37∘,≈8.4 cm.​

For the other leg,

cos⁡37∘=CA14,CA=14cos⁡37∘,CA≈11.2 cm.\begin{aligned} \cos37^\circ&=\frac{CA}{14},\\ CA&=14\cos37^\circ,\\ CA&\approx11.2\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}cos37∘CACA​=14CA​,=14cos37∘,≈11.2 cm.​

The acute angles add to 90∘90^\circ90∘, so

m∠B=53∘.m\angle B=53^\circ.m∠B=53∘.

Both legs are shorter than 141414 cm. Using the original data for both calculations avoids passing a rounded leg into a second calculation.

Worked example 2 The unknown is below the fraction bar

Problem. Triangle QRTQRTQRT is right at RRR. Angle TTT measures 64∘64^\circ64∘, and leg RTRTRT is 7.57.57.5 m. Find hypotenuse QTQTQT to the nearest tenth of a meter.

Solution. Relative to TTT, the given side is the adjacent leg. Cosine uses this side and the unknown hypotenuse:

cos⁡64∘=7.5QT.\cos64^\circ=\frac{7.5}{QT}.cos64∘=QT7.5​.

Multiply by QTQTQT, then divide by the positive cosine value:

QTcos⁡64∘=7.5,QT=7.5cos⁡64∘,QT≈17.1 m.\begin{aligned} QT\cos64^\circ&=7.5,\\ QT&=\frac{7.5}{\cos64^\circ},\\ QT&\approx17.1\text{ m}. \end{aligned}QTcos64∘QTQT​=7.5,=cos64∘7.5​,≈17.1 m.​

The hypotenuse exceeds the given leg. Multiplying 7.57.57.5 by the cosine would instead make the supposed hypotenuse shorter than that leg.

Check your understanding

A right triangle has adjacent leg 9 units and unknown hypotenuse h, relative to an acute angle of 40 degrees. Which expression gives h?

Answer choices

Start with the adjacent-to-hypotenuse ratio.

cos⁡40∘=9h.\cos40^\circ=\frac9h.cos40∘=h9​.

Isolating the unknown denominator gives

h=9cos⁡40∘.h=\frac9{\cos40^\circ}.h=cos40∘9​.

The result must exceed 9 units.

Reverse a ratio to find an acute angle

A trigonometric function takes an angle and returns a ratio. An inverse trigonometric operation takes a valid ratio and returns the angle that produces it. Here we use these operations only to recover acute angles of nondegenerate right triangles.

For example, a right triangle with adjacent leg 777 and hypotenuse 101010 has

cos⁡θ=0.7.\cos\theta=0.7.cosθ=0.7.

The inverse cosine operation in degree mode gives

θ=cos⁡−1(0.7),θ≈45.6∘.\begin{aligned}\theta&=\cos^{-1}(0.7),\\\theta&\approx45.6^\circ.\end{aligned}θθ​=cos−1(0.7),≈45.6∘.​

There is only one acute angle with that ratio. Fixing the adjacent leg at 777 and the hypotenuse at 101010 fixes the remaining leg by Pythagoras, and SSS then fixes the triangle’s angles. Scaling both lengths preserves the angle. The same argument works for any valid adjacent-to-hypotenuse ratio. Fixing the opposite leg and hypotenuse establishes the corresponding fact for sine. For tangent, choosing an adjacent leg of one unit fixes the opposite leg from its ratio, so SAS fixes the right triangle’s shape.

The notation has a specific meaning: sin⁡−1\sin^{-1}sin−1, cos⁡−1\cos^{-1}cos−1, and tan⁡−1\tan^{-1}tan−1 ask for an angle. They are also called arcsine, arccosine, and arctangent. An inverse-function mark after the function name is not a reciprocal of its value. For example,

sin⁡−1(0.5)=30∘.\sin^{-1}(0.5)=30^\circ.sin−1(0.5)=30∘.

Taking a reciprocal instead would give

10.5=2.\frac1{0.5}=2.0.51​=2.

That number is not the requested angle. Calculator keys may require a shift or second-function key; check the displayed operation and degree mode.

For the acute-angle problems here, the allowed inputs are strict:

OperationInput that can come from a nondegenerate right triangle’s acute angle
Inverse sine or inverse cosineA ratio strictly between 000 and 111
Inverse tangentAny positive ratio

The hypotenuse exceeds each leg, but one leg may exceed the other. Thus tangent may exceed 111. A sine or cosine ratio of 1.21.21.2 cannot describe an acute angle. Inputs 000 and 111 for sine or cosine produce boundary angles rather than an acute angle. A zero or negative tangent ratio also fails the positive-leg condition.

Worked example 3 Two given legs

Problem. A right triangle has legs 131313 and 191919 cm. Let θ\thetaθ be the angle opposite the 131313 cm leg. Find both acute angles to the nearest tenth of a degree.

Solution. Both known sides are legs. Relative to θ\thetaθ, their opposite-to-adjacent ratio gives

tan⁡θ=1319.\tan\theta=\frac{13}{19}.tanθ=1913​.

The ratio is positive, so it gives an acute angle. Use inverse tangent:

θ=tan⁡−1 ⁣(1319).\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{13}{19}\right).θ=tan−1(1913​).

In degree mode,

θ≈34.4∘.\theta\approx34.4^\circ.θ≈34.4∘.

Keep the unrounded inverse value to find the other acute angle ϕ\phiϕ:

ϕ=90∘−θ,ϕ≈55.6∘.\begin{aligned} \phi&=90^\circ-\theta,\\ \phi&\approx55.6^\circ. \end{aligned}ϕϕ​=90∘−θ,≈55.6∘.​

The angle opposite the longer leg is larger, matching side-angle ordering.

Check your understanding

An acute angle θ\thetaθ in a right triangle has cosine 0.35. Which operation finds θ\thetaθ in degrees?

Answer choices

The input is an adjacent-to-hypotenuse ratio strictly between zero and one. Reverse cosine using its inverse, in degree mode.

θ=cos⁡−1(0.35).\theta=\cos^{-1}(0.35).θ=cos−1(0.35).

This gives about 69.5∘69.5^\circ69.5∘. A reciprocal returns a number, and sine inverse would find the complementary angle.

Complete a triangle from two sides

Two positive leg lengths determine a right triangle by SAS. A positive leg and a longer hypotenuse determine the missing leg by Pythagoras, then determine the triangle by SSS. To find an acute angle, select the inverse operation that uses the original known pair directly.

Worked example 4 A leg and the hypotenuse

Problem. A right triangle has hypotenuse 232323 m and one leg 999 m. Let α\alphaα be the acute angle opposite that leg. Find the missing leg to the nearest tenth of a meter and both acute angles to the nearest tenth of a degree.

Solution. Subtract squares for the missing leg xxx:

x2=232−92,x2=448,x=87,x≈21.2 m.\begin{aligned} x^2&=23^2-9^2,\\ x^2&=448,\\ x&=8\sqrt7,\\ x&\approx21.2\text{ m}. \end{aligned}x2x2xx​=232−92,=448,=87​,≈21.2 m.​

The known 999 m leg is opposite α\alphaα. Its ratio to the hypotenuse is between zero and one:

α=sin⁡−1 ⁣(923).\alpha=\sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac9{23}\right).α=sin−1(239​).

Thus,

α≈23.0∘.\alpha\approx23.0^\circ.α≈23.0∘.

Subtract the unrounded angle from 90∘90^\circ90∘ to obtain the other acute angle, approximately 67.0∘67.0^\circ67.0∘. The smaller leg faces the smaller angle. The exact leg 878\sqrt787​ also checks the side calculation:

92+(87)2=232.9^2+(8\sqrt7)^2=23^2.92+(87​)2=232.

Keep precision, then check

An expression such as 14sin⁡37∘14\sin37^\circ14sin37∘ can be left exact until its final decimal is needed. Keep the calculator’s stored values or re-enter the original expression, rather than copying a rounded ratio or angle into the next step.

Worked example 5 Two calculator records

Problem. A right triangle has an acute angle of 17.3∘17.3^\circ17.3∘ and an adjacent leg of 323232 cm. Find the opposite leg to the nearest tenth of a centimeter. Compare the result with rounding the tangent value to 0.310.310.31 before multiplying.

Solution. Use the opposite-to-adjacent ratio, keeping the whole expression:

x=32tan⁡17.3∘,x≈9.966890 cm,x≈10.0 cm.\begin{aligned} x&=32\tan17.3^\circ,\\ x&\approx9.966890\text{ cm},\\ x&\approx10.0\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}xxx​=32tan17.3∘,≈9.966890 cm,≈10.0 cm.​

The middle line shows extra digits for comparison, not a new exact value. Rounding the ratio first gives a different computation:

32(0.31)=9.92.32(0.31)=9.92.32(0.31)=9.92.

That altered computation rounds to 9.99.99.9 cm. The required result from the original data is 10.010.010.0 cm. Rounding belongs at the requested final result.

Check results against the geometry and the original data. Each leg must be positive and shorter than the hypotenuse. The acute angles must lie strictly between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 90∘90^\circ90∘ and add to 90∘90^\circ90∘. Substitute into the original ratio, and use Pythagoras as a side check when all three sides are available. Rounded values usually satisfy these equations only approximately.

Check your understanding

Two known legs are 6 and 13 units. You recover their acute angle θ\thetaθ opposite 6, then use the adjacent leg to find the hypotenuse h. Which process preserves the original data until final rounding?

Answer choices

Keep the inverse result in calculator memory or nest the expression.

h=13cos⁡(tan⁡−1(6/13)).h=\frac{13}{\cos(\tan^{-1}(6/13))}.h=cos(tan−1(6/13))13​.

Pythagoras independently gives

h=205.h=\sqrt{205}.h=205​.

Both routes use the original exact lengths.

Decide whether there is enough information

The right angle is essential. These methods are for nondegenerate right triangles, not an arbitrary triangle with a familiar-looking sketch.

An acute angle fixes the shape, but it does not fix the size. A right triangle with angles 28∘28^\circ28∘, 62∘62^\circ62∘, and 90∘90^\circ90∘ can have a hypotenuse of 101010 cm or 202020 cm. The second triangle is a dilation of the first. Angles alone cannot determine its side lengths.

One side alone is also insufficient: with a leg fixed, the other leg can change, changing the acute angles. In contrast, a valid pair of sides, or one acute angle and a side whose role relative to that angle is specified, fixes both the shape and size.

A different size measurement can sometimes replace a side length. For example, if the perimeter is PPP and an acute angle is θ\thetaθ, write the two legs using the hypotenuse hhh. Their sum with the hypotenuse gives

P=hsin⁡θ+hcos⁡θ+h.P=h\sin\theta+h\cos\theta+h.P=hsinθ+hcosθ+h.

Factoring hhh lets you recover the size. This uses the same ratio algebra, not a new triangle theorem.

Check consistency too. A stated hypotenuse of 666 cm with a leg of 888 cm is impossible. So is an acute-angle cosine ratio of 111. Getting a calculator output does not make the geometric data valid.

Check your understanding

Which data allow more than one noncongruent right triangle? Each set includes a right angle.

Answer choices

The two acute angles fix shape but allow every positive scale. Their sum is

29∘+61∘=90∘.29^\circ+61^\circ=90^\circ.29∘+61∘=90∘.

A dilation preserves those angles while changing every side. Each other choice supplies enough valid length data to fix size too.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Side roles. Choose the reference angle before identifying opposite and adjacent; exclude the hypotenuse from the adjacent leg.
  • Unknown denominators. Solve the ratio equation instead of guessing whether to multiply or divide.
  • Inverse notation. An inverse operation returns an angle; a reciprocal of a ratio does not.
  • Calculator settings. Use degree mode, retain full precision, and mark final rounded results as approximate.
  • Data checks. Reject impossible ratios or side roles, and recognize when only the shape is determined.

Key takeaways

  • A known acute angle and side lead to a ratio equation for a missing side.
  • Two known sides lead to an acute inverse operation for an unknown angle.
  • Sine and cosine inverse inputs must lie strictly between zero and one for these acute-angle problems; tangent inverse inputs must be positive.
  • Keep original expressions or stored values until the final requested rounding, then check against the givens.
  • A right triangle needs information fixing both shape and size before every side can be found.

A bit of history (optional)

Before pocket calculators, trigonometric calculations could be read from scales on instruments. The Smithsonian’s collection describes sine and tangent scales on rules and sectors from the seventeenth century, followed by trigonometric scales on slide rules. Such instruments became familiar tools for scientists and engineers around 1900 and continued to sell until inexpensive electronic calculators arrived in the 1970s. The collection’s rules, sectors, and slide rules show how much calculating work once depended on reading a physical scale.
