Special Right Triangles

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Watch the video explainer

Intuition

A square cut from corner to opposite corner makes two right triangles. An equilateral triangle split down its middle makes another familiar pair. Their angles fix their shapes, so one side length can determine the others. The square roots in their lengths come from Pythagoras, rather than from measuring the drawing.

Learning goals

  • Derive both special right triangle side ratios from congruence and Pythagoras
  • Identify side roles from angle markings in any orientation
  • Recover missing lengths exactly from any known side
  • Connect square diagonals and equilateral altitudes to the special triangles

Equal legs and a diagonal

Two equal legs in a right triangle force two equal acute angles. If the legs each measure 444 centimeters, their opposite angles match by the isosceles base-angle theorem. Those angles total 90∘90^\circ90∘, so each is 45∘45^\circ45∘.

Pythagoras gives the hypotenuse length ddd:

d2=42+42.d2=32.d=42 centimeters.\begin{aligned} d^2&=4^2+4^2.\\ d^2&=32.\\ d&=4\sqrt2\text{ centimeters}. \end{aligned}d2d2d​=42+42.=32.=42​ centimeters.​

A 45-45-90 triangle is a right triangle whose acute angles each measure 45∘45^\circ45∘. Conversely, those equal angles force equal opposite legs by the converse of the base-angle theorem. The equal-leg condition and the angle condition describe the same triangles.

The equal-leg side relationship#

For a numerical instance inside the argument, take legs of 333 units. Pythagoras gives

h2=32+32.h2=18.h=32.\begin{aligned} h^2&=3^2+3^2.\\ h^2&=18.\\ h&=3\sqrt2. \end{aligned}h2h2h​=32+32.=18.=32​.​

The same steps work for any positive leg length sss:

h2=s2+s2.h2=2s2.h=s2.\begin{aligned} h^2&=s^2+s^2.\\ h^2&=2s^2.\\ h&=s\sqrt2. \end{aligned}h2h2h​=s2+s2.=2s2.=s2​.​

We take the positive root because hhh is a length. The number 333 played no special role; equal positive legs were the only length condition.

∎

For the two legs followed by the hypotenuse, the ordered side ratio is

1:1:2.1:1:\sqrt2.1:1:2​.

Worked example 1 One given leg

Problem. In triangle RSTRSTRST, the givens are

m∠R=90∘.m∠S=45∘.RS=6 cm.\begin{aligned} m\angle R&=90^\circ.\\ m\angle S&=45^\circ.\\ RS&=6\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}m∠Rm∠SRS​=90∘.=45∘.=6 cm.​

Find RTRTRT and STSTST exactly.

Solution. The angle at TTT is also 45∘45^\circ45∘. Equal angles have equal opposite sides, so

RT=6 cm.RT=6\text{ cm}.RT=6 cm.

The side opposite the right angle is STSTST. Multiply a leg by 2\sqrt22​:

ST=62 cm.ST=6\sqrt2\text{ cm}.ST=62​ cm.

The hypotenuse is longer than either leg, as required.

Check your understanding

A right triangle has two equal legs, each 8 units long. Why is its hypotenuse not 16 units?

Answer choices

The sum of lengths is the broken path along two sides. Pythagoras instead gives the square of the direct side.

h2=82+82.h=82.\begin{aligned} h^2&=8^2+8^2.\\ h&=8\sqrt2. \end{aligned}h2h​=82+82.=82​.​

Work backward from the hypotenuse

Knowing the hypotenuse reverses the multiplication. For example, if an equal-leg right triangle has hypotenuse 12212\sqrt2122​ cm, each leg measures 121212 cm. In general, divide the hypotenuse by 2\sqrt22​:

s=h2.s=\frac{h}{\sqrt2}.s=2​h​.

Rationalizing writes the same exact length with a rational denominator:

s=h22.s=\frac{h\sqrt2}{2}.s=2h2​​.

Dividing by 222 alone would give half the hypotenuse, which belongs to a different special triangle.

Worked example 2 A diagonal gives the edge

Problem. Square PQRSPQRSPQRS has diagonal PRPRPR of length 101010 m. Find the exact side length and perimeter of the square. A square has four equal sides and four right angles.

Solution. In triangle PQRPQRPQR, the equal sides PQPQPQ and QRQRQR meet at a right angle. Thus PRPRPR is its hypotenuse, and the side length sss satisfies

s=102.s=52 m.\begin{aligned} s&=\frac{10}{\sqrt2}.\\ s&=5\sqrt2\text{ m}. \end{aligned}ss​=2​10​.=52​ m.​

The perimeter is four side lengths:

P=202 m.P=20\sqrt2\text{ m}.P=202​ m.

The diagonal is a hypotenuse, even when the square is turned.

Check your understanding

An equal-leg right triangle has hypotenuse 969\sqrt696​ cm. What is each leg?

Answer choices

Divide by the multiplier relating a leg to its hypotenuse.

962=93.\frac{9\sqrt6}{\sqrt2}=9\sqrt3.2​96​​=93​.

The result stays exact; no decimal is needed.

Split an equilateral triangle

Halving an equilateral triangle gives a second fixed side pattern. Start with an equilateral triangle ABCABCABC of side length 121212 cm. Let MMM be the midpoint of BCBCBC, and join AAA to MMM.

The triangles ABMABMABM and ACMACMACM have equal outer sides, equal half-base lengths of 666 cm, and shared side AMAMAM. Side-side-side (SSS) makes them congruent. Corresponding angles at MMM are equal and form a straight angle, so each is 90∘90^\circ90∘. Corresponding angles at AAA are equal and split 60∘60^\circ60∘, so each is 30∘30^\circ30∘. Each angle at BBB or CCC remains 60∘60^\circ60∘.

Only after establishing the right angle can we apply Pythagoras to the altitude AMAMAM:

AM2=122−62.AM2=108.AM=63 cm.\begin{aligned} AM^2&=12^2-6^2.\\ AM^2&=108.\\ AM&=6\sqrt3\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}AM2AM2AM​=122−62.=108.=63​ cm.​

In either half, the short leg is half the original equilateral side. The altitude is the long leg; the original sloping side is the hypotenuse.

The half-equilateral side relationship#

Take an equilateral triangle of side 888 units and join a vertex to the opposite midpoint. SSS gives congruent halves: the outer sides are 888, the half-base lengths are 444, and the dividing side is shared. The equal adjacent angles at the midpoint are each 90∘90^\circ90∘, and the equal halves of the vertex angle are each 30∘30^\circ30∘. The other acute angle in each half is 60∘60^\circ60∘.

For the altitude lll, Pythagoras gives

l2=82−42.l2=48.l=43.\begin{aligned} l^2&=8^2-4^2.\\ l^2&=48.\\ l&=4\sqrt3. \end{aligned}l2l2l​=82−42.=48.=43​.​

For a general positive short-leg length sss, start instead with an equilateral triangle of side 2s2s2s. The midpoint join again gives congruent halves by SSS, right angles at the midpoint, and 30∘30^\circ30∘ vertex halves. Now

l2=(2s)2−s2.l2=3s2.l=s3.\begin{aligned} l^2&=(2s)^2-s^2.\\ l^2&=3s^2.\\ l&=s\sqrt3. \end{aligned}l2l2l​=(2s)2−s2.=3s2.=s3​.​

Nothing depended on the particular side length 888. Every 30∘30^\circ30∘-60∘60^\circ60∘-90∘90^\circ90∘ triangle is similar to one of these halves by AA, so it has the same ordered side ratio.

∎

A 30-60-90 triangle is a right triangle whose acute angles measure 30∘30^\circ30∘ and 60∘60^\circ60∘. Its side lengths, in order opposite those angles and the right angle, are

short leg=s.long leg=s3.hypotenuse=2s,\begin{aligned} \text{short leg}&=s.\\ \text{long leg}&=s\sqrt3.\\ \text{hypotenuse}&=2s, \end{aligned}short leglong leghypotenuse​=s.=s3​.=2s,​

where sss is positive. Thus the ordered ratio of short leg to long leg to hypotenuse is

1:3:2.1:\sqrt3:2.1:3​:2.

Check your understanding

M is the midpoint of BC in equilateral triangle ABC. Why is AM perpendicular to BC?

Answer choices

The outer sides match, BM and CM match, and AM is shared. SSS gives equal angles AMB and AMC. Their sides MB and MC point in opposite directions along one line, so their total is 180 degrees and each is 90 degrees.

Match the side before choosing an operation

Rotating a triangle changes no angle or length. Locate the right angle first, then identify the short leg as the side opposite 30∘30^\circ30∘. In particular, a vertical side can be either leg or the hypotenuse.

Worked example 3 A tilted triangle

Problem. In triangle JKLJKLJKL, the givens are

m∠J=90∘.m∠K=30∘.JL=5 cm.\begin{aligned} m\angle J&=90^\circ.\\ m\angle K&=30^\circ.\\ JL&=5\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}m∠Jm∠KJL​=90∘.=30∘.=5 cm.​

Find JKJKJK and KLKLKL exactly.

Solution. Side JLJLJL is opposite 30∘30^\circ30∘, so it is the short leg. The long leg is JKJKJK:

JK=53 cm.JK=5\sqrt3\text{ cm}.JK=53​ cm.

The side opposite the right angle is KLKLKL:

KL=10 cm.KL=10\text{ cm}.KL=10 cm.

The side roles come from the angles rather than the page orientation.

Worked example 4 A given longer leg

Problem. A 30∘30^\circ30∘-60∘60^\circ60∘-90∘90^\circ90∘ triangle has long leg 767\sqrt676​ meters. Find its short leg and hypotenuse in simplest exact form.

Solution. The long leg is 3\sqrt33​ times the short leg. Divide to recover the short leg sss:

s=763.s=72 m.\begin{aligned} s&=\frac{7\sqrt6}{\sqrt3}.\\ s&=7\sqrt2\text{ m}. \end{aligned}ss​=3​76​​.=72​ m.​

Double the short leg to get the hypotenuse hhh:

h=142 m.h=14\sqrt2\text{ m}.h=142​ m.

Check by squares, keeping the values exact:

(72)2=98.(76)2=294.(142)2=392.\begin{aligned} (7\sqrt2)^2&=98.\\ (7\sqrt6)^2&=294.\\ (14\sqrt2)^2&=392. \end{aligned}(72​)2(76​)2(142​)2​=98.=294.=392.​

The first two squares sum to the third.

Worked example 5 An equilateral altitude

Problem. An equilateral triangle has side length 181818 mm. Find the exact length of its altitude.

Solution. The vertex-to-midpoint segment is perpendicular to the opposite side by the SSS argument above. Each half-base is 999 mm. In either right triangle the short leg is 999 mm, and the altitude is the long leg:

h=93 mm.h=9\sqrt3\text{ mm}.h=93​ mm.

For any equilateral side length eee, the same reasoning gives

h=e32.h=\frac{e\sqrt3}{2}.h=2e3​​.

The altitude is shorter than the sloping side, which is the hypotenuse of each half.

The ratios also identify angles when all three positive sides are given. Construct a special triangle with the same short side, then compare the three sides by SSS. For instance, sides 222, 232\sqrt323​, and 444 match a half-equilateral triangle, so their opposite angles are 30∘30^\circ30∘, 60∘60^\circ60∘, and 90∘90^\circ90∘ respectively. Knowing only one pair of lengths in that ratio, without a right angle or third side, would not justify that conclusion.

Check your understanding

In a 30-60-90 triangle the hypotenuse is 22 cm. Which is the long leg?

Answer choices

Halve the hypotenuse first to obtain short leg 11 cm. Then multiply that short leg by the square root of 3.

long leg=113 cm.\text{long leg}=11\sqrt3\text{ cm}.long leg=113​ cm.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Side roles. The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. The short leg of a 30-60-90 triangle is opposite 30∘30^\circ30∘.
  • Working backward. Divide by the multiplier that was used going forward. Halving a hypotenuse works for 30-60-90 triangles; equal-leg right triangles require division by 2\sqrt22​.
  • Half-base and altitude. In the equilateral construction, the half-base is the short leg and the altitude is the long leg.
  • Exact lengths. Leave radicals exact unless a decimal approximation is requested. A measured-looking sketch is not evidence for a ratio.

Key takeaways

  • 45-45-90. The legs match and the hypotenuse is 2\sqrt22​ times either leg.
  • 30-60-90. From the positive short leg sss, the long leg is s3s\sqrt3s3​ and the hypotenuse is 2s2s2s.
  • Square diagonals. A diagonal forms an equal-leg right triangle with two sides of the square.
  • Equilateral altitudes. SSS justifies the perpendicular midpoint join; each half is a 30-60-90 triangle.

A bit of history (optional)

In Plato’s dialogue Meno, Socrates asks an enslaved boy how to make a square with twice the area of a given square. The boy first proposes doubling its side. Questions about the drawing reveal the problem: that produces four times the area, not twice.

Socrates arranges four equal squares and draws their corner-to-corner lines. Those lines enclose a new square made from four half-squares. The boy recognizes that its area is twice the original square’s area. The side they needed was already present as the original square’s diagonal.

This is a scene in a philosophical dialogue, not a transcript documenting an actual lesson. Plato uses the boy’s changing answers in a larger discussion about learning. You can read the exchange in the translated Meno.
