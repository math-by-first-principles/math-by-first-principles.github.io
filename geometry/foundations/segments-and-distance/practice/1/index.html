Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    Which notation names the numerical length of segment AB‾\overline{AB}AB?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The bar names the segment as an object. The unmarked pair ABABAB denotes its numerical length in a chosen unit.

  2. 2

    Points CCC and DDD have number-line coordinates −3-3−3 and 888. What is CDCDCD?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Subtract the smaller coordinate from the larger.

    CD=8−(−3)=11CD=8-(-3)=11CD=8−(−3)=11

    The distance includes the three units from −3-3−3 to zero and eight more to 888.

  3. 3

    The coordinates of EEE and FFF are −17-17−17 and −9-9−9. What is the distance between them?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Use the difference of the coordinates, not the sum of their distances from zero.

    EF=∣−9−(−17)∣=8EF=|-9-(-17)|=8EF=∣−9−(−17)∣=8

  4. 4

    What is the distance from a point to itself?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Both coordinate readings are the same, so their difference is zero. The distance from a location to itself is 000.

  5. 5

    Point BBB is between AAA and CCC, with AB=11AB=11AB=11 cm and BC=14BC=14BC=14 cm. What is ACACAC?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Betweenness makes the two given lengths adjacent parts of the whole segment.

    AC=11+14=25 cmAC=11+14=25\text{ cm}AC=11+14=25 cm

  6. 6

    Point NNN is between LLL and PPP. If LP=41LP=41LP=41 mm and LN=16LN=16LN=16 mm, what is NPNPNP?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The whole is LPLPLP, so subtract the known part.

    NP=41−16=25 mmNP=41-16=25\text{ mm}NP=41−16=25 mm

  7. 7

    Two segments each have length 777 cm, but one is drawn vertically and the other horizontally. Which statement is correct?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Congruent segments have equal lengths. Direction and position do not change that definition.

  8. 8

    Point MMM is the midpoint of RS‾\overline{RS}RS, whose length is 323232 units. What is RMRMRM?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    A midpoint divides a segment into equal halves.

    RM=32÷2=16RM=32\div2=16RM=32÷2=16

  9. 9

    What is the midpoint coordinate of a segment with number-line endpoints −10-10−10 and 666?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Average the endpoint coordinates.

    −10+62=−2\frac{-10+6}{2}=-22−10+6​=−2

    The distances from −2-2−2 to the endpoints are both 888 units.

  10. 10

    A line meets a segment at its midpoint, at a slanted angle rather than a square corner. Is it a segment bisector?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    A segment bisector passes through the midpoint. Perpendicularity is an additional condition and is not required for bisection.

  11. 11

    A segment's endpoints align with the 333 cm and 101010 cm ruler marks. What length does the ruler show?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Subtract the endpoint readings.

    10−3=7 cm10-3=7\text{ cm}10−3=7 cm

    The endpoint does not have to start at zero.

  12. 12

    Segments AB‾\overline{AB}AB and CD‾\overline{CD}CD have lengths AB=2.7AB=2.7AB=2.7 cm and CD=27CD=27CD=27 mm. Which statement must be true?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Compare the lengths in the same unit. Each centimeter contains ten millimeters.

    2.7×10=27 mm2.7\times10=27\text{ mm}2.7×10=27 mm

    Thus ABABAB and CDCDCD are equal lengths, so the segments are congruent.

    Different unit labels do not change that length.

    Also, CD=2.7CD=2.7CD=2.7 centimeters and AB=27AB=27AB=27 millimeters, so the other numerical claims are false.
