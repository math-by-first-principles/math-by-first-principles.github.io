Points, Lines, and Planes: Practice

12 multiple-choice questions, progressively harder.

Level 3 · Challenge 0 / 12 answered
Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    Two distinct planes each contain both distinct points AAA and BBB. A third point CCC belongs to both planes. What must be true?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    All common points of the distinct planes form one line.

    Since this line contains the distinct points AAA and BBB, the unique-line foundation identifies it as AB↔\overleftrightarrow{AB}AB.

    The common point CCC must also lie on that line, so the three points are collinear.

  2. 2

    Planes P\mathcal PP and Q\mathcal QQ both contain the noncollinear points JJJ, KKK, and LLL. Which conclusion follows?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Three noncollinear points determine exactly one plane. Both plane names must identify that same plane.

  3. 3

    Two distinct lines lie in a common plane and are not parallel. What must be true?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    If the distinct coplanar lines had no common point, they would be parallel by definition. They therefore meet.

    Distinct lines share at most one point, so the intersection is exactly one point.

  4. 4

    Three distinct points AAA, BBB, and CCC are noncollinear. What is the intersection of lines ABABAB and ACACAC?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The lines share AAA.

    They are distinct, since otherwise AAA, BBB, and CCC would be collinear.

    Distinct lines share at most one point, so their intersection is only AAA.

  5. 5

    A rectangular prism has front face ABFEABFEABFE, back face DCGHDCGHDCGH, and connecting edges ADADAD, BCBCBC, FGFGFG, and EHEHEH. Each face is given to be a rectangle with parallel opposite edge lines and square corners, and opposite faces lie in parallel planes. Which pair of complete lines is skew?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Any plane containing lines EFEFEF and CGCGCG would contain the noncollinear points E,F,GE,F,GE,F,G, so it would be the top-face plane.

    But CCC is in the parallel bottom-face plane, outside the top-face plane. Thus no plane contains both lines, so they are skew.

    The other pairs are respectively parallel, perpendicular, and parallel in the given rectangular prism.

  6. 6

    Three planes P\mathcal PP, Q\mathcal QQ, and R\mathcal RR are pairwise distinct. The intersection of P\mathcal PP and Q\mathcal QQ is line ℓ\ellℓ, and the intersection of P\mathcal PP and R\mathcal RR is also ℓ\ellℓ. What is the intersection of Q\mathcal QQ and R\mathcal RR?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Planes Q\mathcal QQ and R\mathcal RR both contain every point of ℓ\ellℓ.

    Because they are distinct, all their common points form a line kkk. Choose two distinct points U,VU,VU,V on ℓ\ellℓ; they also lie on kkk.

    The unique-line foundation makes kkk and ℓ\ellℓ the same line, so the intersection is ℓ\ellℓ.

  7. 7

    Distinct lines rrr and sss meet at VVV. Can they be skew?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Choose a point XXX on rrr other than VVV and a point YYY on sss other than VVV.

    Distinct lines have at most one common point, already VVV.

    Thus YYY is off rrr, the unique line through VVV and XXX, so V,X,YV,X,YV,X,Y are noncollinear.

    Their plane contains both lines by the line-in-plane foundation, so the lines are not skew. Coplanarity does not require perpendicularity.

  8. 8

    On one line, distinct points occur in order WWW, XXX, YYY, ZZZ. Which statement is correct?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Both rays in the correct statement start at XXX and extend toward YYY and ZZZ.

    The other ray pairs point in opposite directions.

    Segments WXWXWX and WZWZWZ have different endpoints, so they are different sets of points.

  9. 9

    Plane P\mathcal PP contains line ℓ\ellℓ. Point TTT lies outside P\mathcal PP. Which statement is correct?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Choose distinct points UUU and VVV on ℓ\ellℓ.

    Point TTT is off ℓ\ellℓ, because ℓ\ellℓ lies in P\mathcal PP while TTT lies outside that plane.

    The noncollinear points U,V,TU,V,TU,V,T determine one plane. The line-in-plane foundation puts all of ℓ\ellℓ in that plane.

    Any plane containing both ℓ\ellℓ and TTT must contain the same noncollinear triple, so it must be this unique plane. It differs from P\mathcal PP.

  10. 10

    Two distinct planes intersect in line kkk. A line mmm lies in both planes. What follows?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Every point of mmm belongs to the intersection, which is kkk.

    Choose two distinct points on mmm. They also belong to kkk.

    The unique-line foundation makes mmm and kkk the same line.

  11. 11

    Which information is sufficient to conclude that two named planes coincide?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Three noncollinear points determine a unique plane. One point or one line can be shared by distinct planes, so neither of those guarantees coincidence.

  12. 12

    Two distinct lines have no common point. No information about a common plane is given. Which conclusion is justified?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Disjoint coplanar lines are parallel. Disjoint noncoplanar lines are skew. Without coplanarity information, both cases remain possible.
