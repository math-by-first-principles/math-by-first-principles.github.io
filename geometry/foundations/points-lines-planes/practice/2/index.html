Points, Lines, and Planes: Practice

12 multiple-choice questions, progressively harder.

Level 2 · Intermediate 0 / 12 answered
Question 1 of 12
  1. 1

    Distinct points AAA and CCC lie in plane R\mathcal RR. What must be true about AC↔\overleftrightarrow{AC}AC?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The line-in-plane foundation applies because two distinct points of the line lie in the plane. It gives containment of the entire line, including beyond AAA and CCC.

  2. 2

    Distinct collinear points DDD, EEE, and FFF occur in that order. Which two rays are opposite?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Opposite rays share an endpoint and extend in opposite directions along the same line. From EEE, point DDD is in one direction and FFF is in the other.

  3. 3

    Points LLL, MMM, and NNN are noncollinear. Which name identifies their plane?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Three noncollinear points name the unique plane containing them. A line cannot contain all three, because they are noncollinear.

  4. 4

    Two distinct planes both contain distinct points SSS and TTT. What is their intersection?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Their intersection is a line because the planes are distinct and meet.

    Both SSS and TTT lie on that line, so the unique-line foundation identifies it as ST↔\overleftrightarrow{ST}ST.

  5. 5

    Line ℓ\ellℓ passes through distinct points PPP and QQQ, and line mmm also passes through PPP and QQQ. Which conclusion follows?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Exactly one line contains two distinct points. Both names therefore refer to that unique line through PPP and QQQ.

  6. 6

    A rectangular prism has front face ABFEABFEABFE and back face DCGHDCGHDCGH, with corresponding connecting edges ADADAD, BCBCBC, FGFGFG, and EHEHEH. Each face is given to be a rectangle with parallel opposite edge lines and square corners, and opposite faces lie in parallel planes. Which pair of lines is perpendicular?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    In the rectangular front face, ABABAB and BFBFBF meet at a given square corner, so they are perpendicular.

    Lines ABABAB and EFEFEF are opposite edge lines of the front face, and ABABAB and CDCDCD are opposite edge lines of the bottom face, so both pairs are parallel by the givens.

    For ABABAB and FGFGFG, the sole candidate for a common plane is the front-face plane through noncollinear A,B,FA,B,FA,B,F.

    It excludes GGG, which lies in the parallel back-face plane, so those lines are skew.

  7. 7

    Distinct planes P\mathcal PP and Q\mathcal QQ have no common point. Which classification fits?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Distinct planes with no common point are parallel. The word skew classifies lines, not planes.

  8. 8

    Four points belong to plane P\mathcal PP, but no line contains all four. Which statement is justified?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Their membership in P\mathcal PP makes them coplanar. The stated absence of one line through all four means they are not all collinear.

  9. 9

    A drawing shows two line strokes crossing, but the text says the actual lines are skew. Which statement should you use?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    A perspective drawing can show a projected crossing of lines that do not meet in space. The stated skew relationship means the actual lines are noncoplanar and disjoint.

  10. 10

    Point XXX is the only named point of line ttt. Can the notation XX↔\overleftrightarrow{XX}XX identify ttt uniquely?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    One point lies on many lines. To use two-point line notation, name a second point different from XXX. A lowercase label such as ttt can still name the given line.

  11. 11

    A line passes through a point of plane P\mathcal PP. Is that enough to conclude the line lies in P\mathcal PP?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The containment foundation requires two distinct points of the line in the plane.

    One shared point allows a line that passes through the plane without lying in it.

  12. 12

    Points AAA, BBB, and CCC are distinct and collinear. Why do they fail to determine a unique plane?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Their common line can belong to many planes. The unique-plane foundation needs a third point off the line, not merely a third distinct point on it.
