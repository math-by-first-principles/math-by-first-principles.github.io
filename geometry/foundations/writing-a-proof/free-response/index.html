  1. Problem 1 Record only the givens

    On paper, draw three distinct collinear points in the order R, S, T.

    The givens are RS = 5 cm and ST = 11 cm.

    The goal is to find RT.

    Annotate the two given lengths and identify the whole segment sought.

    Then find its length and name the geometric reason.

    1. Hint 1

      The point order identifies which point is between the other two.

    2. Hint 2

      Separate the two given parts from the whole requested in the goal.

    Answer

    Label RS as 5 cm and ST as 11 cm, with RT identified as the goal. RT = 16 cm by segment addition, since S is between R and T.

    Full solution

    Place S between R and T and label only the two given part lengths as supplied data.

    Segment addition applies because S is between R and T, so

    RT=5+11.RT=5+11.RT=5+11.

    The sum is 16 centimeters.

    Answer

    Label RS as 5 cm and ST as 11 cm, with RT identified as the goal. RT = 16 cm by segment addition, since S is between R and T.

    Key idea

    A diagram should distinguish information supplied from the quantity to be established.

  2. Problem 2 Identify three properties

    Name the property used in each step:

    Step A: from KL=MNKL=MNKL=MN, write MN=KLMN=KLMN=KL.

    Step B: state that segment RS is congruent to itself.

    Step C: from ∠A≅∠B\angle A\cong\angle B∠A≅∠B and ∠B≅∠C\angle B\cong\angle C∠B≅∠C, conclude ∠A≅∠C\angle A\cong\angle C∠A≅∠C. The selected angles are unambiguous.

    1. Hint 1

      One step reverses a relationship, one relates an object to itself, and one connects two relationships.

    2. Hint 2

      The same reflexive, symmetric, and transitive patterns apply to congruence through equal measures.

    Answer

    In order: symmetric property of equality; reflexive property of segment congruence; transitive property of angle congruence.

    Full solution

    Step A: The first step reverses the sides of an equality, so it uses symmetry.

    Step B: The segment is congruent to itself because its length equals itself, which is reflexivity.

    Step C: The selected angles have equal measures in succession, so transitivity gives congruence of the first and third.

    Answer

    In order: symmetric property of equality; reflexive property of segment congruence; transitive property of angle congruence.

    Key idea

    Name a property by the change it justifies, not by the shape of the drawing.

  3. Problem 3 Connect congruence and betweenness

    Segments UV and WX are congruent, with lengths UV=4x+3UV=4x+3UV=4x+3 and WX=47WX=47WX=47 centimeters.

    Point X lies strictly between W and Y, and XY=24XY=24XY=24 centimeters.

    Find xxx and WYWYWY, explaining how the given congruence and point order justify the equations for the two results.

    1. Hint 1

      Translate the given segment congruence into an equality of lengths.

    2. Hint 2

      After finding the shared length, use the stated point order to identify the parts of WY.

    Answer

    x = 11; WY = 71 cm. Congruent segments have equal lengths, so the definition and substitution justify 4x + 3 = 47; X between W and Y justifies segment addition for WY.

    Full solution

    Congruent segments have equal lengths, so UV = WX.

    Substitution gives

    4x+3=47.4x+3=47.4x+3=47.

    Subtract 3 from both sides to obtain

    4x=44.4x=44.4x=44.

    Divide both sides by the nonzero number 4 to obtain

    x=11.x=11.x=11.

    Because X is strictly between W and Y, segment addition gives

    WY=WX+XY.WY=WX+XY.WY=WX+XY.

    Substituting the given part lengths gives

    WY=47+24.WY=47+24.WY=47+24.

    Adding them gives

    WY=71 cm.WY=71\text{ cm}.WY=71 cm.

    Answer

    x = 11; WY = 71 cm. Congruent segments have equal lengths, so the definition and substitution justify 4x + 3 = 47; X between W and Y justifies segment addition for WY.

    Key idea

    Use each geometric given for its own step: congruence supplies equal lengths, and betweenness permits adding parts.

  4. Problem 4 Justify an angle equation

    Ray OB lies inside the selected angle AOC. The selected angles AOB and BOC measure 46 and 38 degrees respectively. Find the measure of AOC and explain which hypothesis makes addition legitimate.

    1. Hint 1

      Identify the two parts and the selected whole angle.

    2. Hint 2

      The location of the dividing ray is the condition needed for angle addition.

    Answer

    The measure of AOC is 84 degrees; OB lying inside the selected angle justifies angle addition.

    Full solution

    The interior ray makes AOB and BOC the two parts of the selected angle AOC.

    Angle addition therefore gives

    46+38=84∘.46+38=84^\circ.46+38=84∘.

    Without the interior-ray condition, the two named measures alone would not justify that sum as the selected whole.

    Answer

    The measure of AOC is 84 degrees; OB lying inside the selected angle justifies angle addition.

    Key idea

    An addition equation needs the geometric condition that its parts form the stated whole.

  5. Problem 5 Supply the reasons

    M is the midpoint of segment QR, whose length is 74 cm. A two-column proof has these successive statements:

    Statement 1: QM = MR.

    Statement 2: QM + MR = 74.

    Statement 3: 2QM = 74.

    Statement 4: QM = 37.

    Supply a reason for each statement.

    1. Hint 1

      Start by translating the midpoint definition.

    2. Hint 2

      Then connect the two parts to the whole before replacing equal terms.

    Answer

    In order: midpoint definition and congruence-to-length equality; segment addition because M is between Q and R, then substitution of the given QR = 74; substitution and combining like terms; division of both sides by 2.

    Full solution

    Statement 1: The midpoint gives equal part lengths QM = MR.

    Statement 2: The midpoint condition places M between Q and R, so segment addition gives QM + MR = QR.

    Substituting the given whole length produces QM + MR = 74.

    Statement 3: Replace MR with QM and combine the equal terms to obtain 2QM = 74.

    Statement 4: Divide by the nonzero number 2 to obtain QM = 37.

    Answer

    In order: midpoint definition and congruence-to-length equality; segment addition because M is between Q and R, then substitution of the given QR = 74; substitution and combining like terms; division of both sides by 2.

    Key idea

    Every row needs a reason, including the geometric step that produces an equation.

    Identify the two boxes whose arrows feed III, and supply a reason for each of the three statements.

    1. Hint 1

      Which given permits an equation connecting the selected whole angle to its two parts?

    2. Hint 2

      Translate the angle congruence into equality of measures before replacing a term.

    Answer

    The inputs are m∠AOC=m∠AOB+m∠BOCm\angle AOC=m\angle AOB+m\angle BOCm∠AOC=m∠AOB+m∠BOC by angle addition and m∠BOC=m∠DEFm\angle BOC=m\angle DEFm∠BOC=m∠DEF by congruence. Substitution gives m∠AOC=m∠AOB+m∠DEFm\angle AOC=m\angle AOB+m\angle DEFm∠AOC=m∠AOB+m∠DEF.

  7. Problem 7 Check the geometric conditions

    1. Hint 1

      One equation joins segment lengths and the other joins selected angles.

    2. Hint 2

    Answer

    Full solution

    For segment addition, B must be between A and C so AB and BC form the whole AC.

    For angle addition, OB must be an interior ray of the selected angle AOC, and the selected parts must be AOB and BOC on that turn.

    Answer

  8. Problem 8 Check a plane argument

    Three distinct points P, Q, and R lie in both planes α\alphaα and β\betaβ.

    An argument claims: “Three points determine exactly one plane, so these two planes must be the same.” Evaluate the argument, state the missing hypothesis that would justify its conclusion, and describe a configuration where the original givens hold but the planes are different.

    1. Hint 1

      Which condition, besides distinctness, does the unique-plane foundation require?

    2. Hint 2

      Test a configuration where the points share a line, and consider sheets that could meet along that line.

    Answer

    Noncollinearity is missing. If P, Q, and R are noncollinear, the planes coincide. The original givens also allow three distinct points on the common line of two different intersecting planes.

    Full solution

    The unique-plane foundation applies to three noncollinear points; distinctness alone does not supply that condition.

    If noncollinearity is added, both planes contain those three noncollinear points and therefore are the unique plane through them.

    For a counterconfiguration to the original claim, take two different planes intersecting along a line and choose three distinct points P, Q, and R on that line.

    All three points lie in both planes even though the planes are different.

    Answer

    Noncollinearity is missing. If P, Q, and R are noncollinear, the planes coincide. The original givens also allow three distinct points on the common line of two different intersecting planes.

    Key idea

    A uniqueness foundation applies only after all its hypotheses, including noncollinearity, are available.

  9. Problem 9 Challenge a proposed equality

    Points A, B, and C are distinct and collinear, with B between A and C. Prove by contradiction that AB≠ACAB\ne ACAB=AC. Name the accepted distance fact that the temporary assumption would violate.

    1. Hint 1

      Which addition equation follows from the given point order?

    2. Hint 2

      Assume the two named lengths are equal, cancel their common contribution, and compare the remaining length with its distinct endpoints.

    Answer

    AB≠ACAB\ne ACAB=AC; assuming equality forces BC=0BC=0BC=0, violating the positive distance between distinct points B and C.

    Full solution

    Assume for contradiction that AB=ACAB=ACAB=AC.

    Because B is between A and C, segment addition gives

    AC=AB+BC.AC=AB+BC.AC=AB+BC.

    Substitute the assumed equality to obtain

    AB=AB+BC.AB=AB+BC.AB=AB+BC.

    Subtract AB from both sides, giving

    BC=0.BC=0.BC=0.

    The distinct points B and C have positive distance, so BC>0BC>0BC>0.

    This contradicts BC=0BC=0BC=0.

    Reject the temporary equality and conclude AB≠ACAB\ne ACAB=AC.

    Answer

    AB≠ACAB\ne ACAB=AC; assuming equality forces BC=0BC=0BC=0, violating the positive distance between distinct points B and C.

    Key idea

    A contradiction can arise when an assumed equality forces distinct points to have zero distance.

  10. Problem 10 Choose a proof route

    B is between A and C, and E is between D and F.

    The givens also say AC=DFAC=DFAC=DF and BC=EFBC=EFBC=EF.

    Choose and justify a direct or contradiction approach, then write a complete paragraph proof that AB=DEAB=DEAB=DE.

    Name the conditions that permit your geometric equations.

    1. Hint 1

      How do the stated point orders express the equal whole lengths as sums of parts?

    2. Hint 2

      Use the equality of the removed parts to rewrite both sums with the same term before canceling it.

    Answer

    AB = DE. Direct cancellation suits the equal wholes and equal removed parts; B between A and C and E between D and F justify segment addition. A valid contradiction argument is also acceptable.

    Full solution

    Choose direct reasoning because the givens supply equal whole lengths and equal parts to remove.

    Because B is between A and C, segment addition gives

    AC=AB+BC.AC=AB+BC.AC=AB+BC.

    Because E is between D and F, segment addition gives

    DF=DE+EF.DF=DE+EF.DF=DE+EF.

    Substitute these expressions into AC=DFAC=DFAC=DF and replace EF with its equal length BC, obtaining

    AB+BC=DE+BC.AB+BC=DE+BC.AB+BC=DE+BC.

    Subtract BC from both sides to conclude

    AB=DE.AB=DE.AB=DE.

    Answer

    AB = DE. Direct cancellation suits the equal wholes and equal removed parts; B between A and C and E between D and F justify segment addition. A valid contradiction argument is also acceptable.

    Key idea

    Choose a route supported by the givens, and justify the geometric part equations before canceling equal terms.
