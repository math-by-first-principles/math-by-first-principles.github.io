  1. Problem 1 Place the points

    A circle has center KKK and radius 888 cm.

    Points A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D all lie in the circle's plane, and their distances from KKK are 8,5,10,08,5,10,08,5,10,0 cm, respectively.

    List the points on the circle, the points in the disk, and the points outside the disk.

    1. Hint 1

      Separate exactly the radius from less than the radius.

    2. Hint 2

      The disk includes its boundary and its center.

    Answer

    On the circle: AAA. In the disk: A,B,DA,B,DA,B,D. Outside the disk: CCC.

    Full solution

    All tested points lie in the circle's plane.

    Only AAA is exactly 888 cm from KKK, so only AAA is on the circle.

    Distances 8,5,08,5,08,5,0 are at most 888 cm, so A,B,DA,B,DA,B,D are in the disk.

    Point CCC is 101010 cm away, farther than the radius, so it is outside the disk.

    Answer

    On the circle: AAA. In the disk: A,B,DA,B,DA,B,D. Outside the disk: CCC.

    Key idea

    In the circle's plane, a circle uses an exact distance condition and a disk includes smaller distances too.

  2. Problem 2 Draw the named parts

    On paper, draw a circle with center OOO.

    Mark opposite boundary points AAA and BBB and draw segment ABABAB through OOO.

    Choose another boundary point CCC off line ABABAB, then draw OCOCOC and ACACAC.

    Identify a radius, a diameter, and a chord that is not a diameter among the segments you drew.

    1. Hint 1

      Check the endpoints first, then whether a segment goes through the center.

    2. Hint 2

      A radius begins at the center; a diameter joins boundary points through the center.

    Answer

    OCOCOC is a radius, ABABAB is a diameter, and ACACAC is a chord that is not a diameter.

    Full solution

    Segment OCOCOC joins the center to a boundary point, so it is a radius.

    Segment ABABAB joins boundary points through OOO, so it is a diameter.

    Both endpoints of ACACAC lie on the circle, so it is a chord.

    The positive radius makes AAA different from OOO, so the unique line through them is line ABABAB.

    The line through AAA and CCC differs from line ABABAB because CCC is off ABABAB.

    If that line also contained OOO, uniqueness through AAA and OOO would force it to be ABABAB, placing CCC on ABABAB.

    Thus ACACAC misses OOO and is not a diameter.

    Answer

    OCOCOC is a radius, ABABAB is a diameter, and ACACAC is a chord that is not a diameter.

    Key idea

    Circle-segment names depend on endpoint and center conditions, not orientation.

    ▾

  3. Problem 3 Two uses of one formula

    A circle has center OOO and radius 777 cm.

    Chord ABABAB passes through OOO, while chord CDCDCD with distinct endpoints does not.

    An argument says, "The radius is 7 cm, so both chords have length 2r=142r=142r=14 cm." Assess the formula's use for each chord, find ABABAB, and decide whether the exact length CDCDCD is determined by these givens.

    1. Hint 1

      Which condition makes a chord a diameter?

    2. Hint 2

      Check the proposed formula separately for ABABAB and CDCDCD; the latter chord's endpoint positions remain unspecified.

    Answer

    AB=14AB=14AB=14 cm, and the diameter formula is justified for ABABAB. Its use for CDCDCD is unjustified because that chord misses the center; the exact length CDCDCD is not determined.

    Full solution

    Chord ABABAB passes through OOO, so it is a diameter.

    Segment addition adds its two radius lengths in centimeters:

    7+7=14.7+7=14.7+7=14.

    Thus AB=14AB=14AB=14 cm, and the argument's first use of 2r2r2r is justified.

    Its use for CDCDCD lacks the diameter condition, because CDCDCD does not pass through OOO.

    The givens do not specify the positions of CCC and DDD along the circle, so they do not determine that chord's exact length.

    Applying the diameter formula to both chords is not justified.

    Answer

    AB=14AB=14AB=14 cm, and the diameter formula is justified for ABABAB. Its use for CDCDCD is unjustified because that chord misses the center; the exact length CDCDCD is not determined.

    Key idea

    Check the center condition separately before applying the diameter formula to a chord.

  4. Problem 4 Make a separate copy

    On paper, draw a source segment ABABAB of length 666 cm and a separate ray PXPXPX.

    Put the ruler away.

    Construct its copy PQPQPQ on that ray using a compass and unmarked straightedge.

    Leave the compass circle visible and explain why the copied length equals ABABAB.

    1. Hint 1

      Which tool can carry a distance without reading a number?

    2. Hint 2

      The point on the target ray must lie on a circle centered at its starting point.

    Answer

    The constructed segment satisfies PQ=AB=6PQ=AB=6PQ=AB=6 cm; PQPQPQ is a radius drawn with the retained opening ABABAB.

    Full solution

    Set the compass point at AAA and the pencil at BBB.

    Without changing the opening, draw a circle centered at PPP and mark its intersection with ray PXPXPX as QQQ.

    That circle has radius ABABAB, so the definition of a circle gives PQ=AB=6PQ=AB=6PQ=AB=6 cm.

    Answer

    The constructed segment satisfies PQ=AB=6PQ=AB=6PQ=AB=6 cm; PQPQPQ is a radius drawn with the retained opening ABABAB.

    Key idea

    Keeping the compass opening transfers an exact distance in the ideal construction.

  5. Problem 5 Reproduce an opening

    On paper, use a protractor to draw a source angle of 65∘65^\circ65∘ and draw a separate target ray PXPXPX.

    Put the protractor away.

    Copy the source opening above the target ray with a compass and unmarked straightedge.

    Leave the helper circles visible and identify the two different distance settings you used.

    1. Hint 1

      The first setting marks a point on each source ray and is retained at the target vertex.

    2. Hint 2

      The second setting is the distance between the two source-circle marks.

    Answer

    The target opening is 65∘65^\circ65∘ in the ideal construction. The settings are the common source/target circle radius and the chord between the two source marks.

    Full solution

    Draw a circle centered at the source vertex, meeting the rays at AAA and BBB.

    With the same radius, draw a circle centered at PPP, meeting the target baseline at DDD.

    Set the compass to ABABAB, draw the circle centered at DDD, and choose its intersection EEE with the target circle above the target baseline.

    Draw ray PEPEPE to complete the copy.

    The settings were the common radius and then chord ABABAB; the full proof of the copied angle is deferred to lesson 4.5.

    Answer

    The target opening is 65∘65^\circ65∘ in the ideal construction. The settings are the common source/target circle radius and the chord between the two source marks.

    Key idea

    An angle copy transfers a shared radius first and the distance between the source marks second.

  6. Problem 6 Construct three matching sides

    On paper, draw a segment ABABAB of length 444 cm.

    Construct an equilateral triangle on it using a compass and unmarked straightedge.

    Retain the two circles and explain why each new side has the same length as ABABAB.

    1. Hint 1

      The third vertex must be a distance ABABAB from both endpoints.

    2. Hint 2

      Use one circle condition for each of the two required distances.

    Answer

    The construction gives AB=AC=BC=4AB=AC=BC=4AB=AC=BC=4 cm; ACACAC and BCBCBC are radii of circles with the common radius ABABAB.

    Full solution

    Draw a circle centered at AAA with radius ABABAB and a circle centered at BBB with the same radius.

    Choose either intersection as CCC and join ACACAC and BCBCBC.

    The first circle gives AC=AB=4AC=AB=4AC=AB=4 cm; the second gives BC=AB=4BC=AB=4BC=AB=4 cm.

    All three sides are equal, which is the definition of an equilateral triangle.

    Answer

    The construction gives AB=AC=BC=4AB=AC=BC=4AB=AC=BC=4 cm; ACACAC and BCBCBC are radii of circles with the common radius ABABAB.

    Key idea

    The two circle conditions separately justify the two newly drawn side lengths.

  7. Problem 7 Different radius settings

    Segment ABABAB has length 444 cm.

    A circle centered at AAA has radius 444 cm and a circle centered at BBB has radius 666 cm.

    They meet at CCC.

    Find the three side lengths of triangle ABCABCABC and decide whether it is equilateral.

    1. Hint 1

      Read the distance to each center from that circle's own radius.

    2. Hint 2

      Compare all three lengths rather than assuming different circles have equal radii.

    Answer

    AB=4AB=4AB=4 cm, AC=4AC=4AC=4 cm, and BC=6BC=6BC=6 cm; the triangle is not equilateral.

    Full solution

    Because CCC is on the circle centered at AAA, AC=4AC=4AC=4 cm.

    Because CCC is on the circle centered at BBB, BC=6BC=6BC=6 cm.

    The given base is 444 cm, so the three sides are 4,4,64,4,64,4,6 cm and are not all equal.

    Answer

    AB=4AB=4AB=4 cm, AC=4AC=4AC=4 cm, and BC=6BC=6BC=6 cm; the triangle is not equilateral.

    Key idea

    Radii from different circles agree only when their radius settings agree.

  8. Problem 8 Choose a source mark

    A source angle has vertex OOO.

    One mark AAA lies on its first ray and on a circle centered at OOO with radius 555 cm.

    A proposed second mark BBB lies on the other ray, inside that circle, with OB=3OB=3OB=3 cm.

    Decide whether these are the two source marks specified by the angle-copy procedure, and state where the second mark must be placed.

    1. Hint 1

      The first circle is meant to give equal distances along the two source rays.

    2. Hint 2

      The second mark must satisfy the same exact radius condition as the first.

    Answer

    They are not the specified source marks. Place the second mark on the other ray at its intersection with the same circle, 555 cm from OOO.

    Full solution

    The first source circle must meet both source rays, so both marked points must be 555 cm from OOO.

    The proposed BBB is only 333 cm away and lies inside the disk rather than on the circle.

    Replace it with the intersection of the other ray and the circle, where the distance from OOO is 555 cm.

    Answer

    They are not the specified source marks. Place the second mark on the other ray at its intersection with the same circle, 555 cm from OOO.

    Key idea

    The angle-copy marks must satisfy equal radius conditions before their chord is transferred.

  9. Problem 9 Follow a construction chain

    A source segment has length (3t+1)(3t+1)(3t+1) cm.

    It is copied exactly as the base of an equilateral triangle, whose base is also labeled (5t−9)(5t-9)(5t−9) cm.

    Find ttt and the triangle's perimeter.

    1. Hint 1

      The original segment and copied base have equal lengths.

    2. Hint 2

      After solving, use the equal side lengths to find the perimeter.

    Answer

    t=5t=5t=5; each side is 161616 cm and the perimeter is 484848 cm.

    Full solution

    The segment copy gives 3t+1=5t−93t+1=5t-93t+1=5t−9.

    Solving gives 10=2t10=2t10=2t, so t=5t=5t=5.

    The base has length 3(5)+1=163(5)+1=163(5)+1=16 cm, and the equilateral construction makes each other side that length.

    The perimeter is

    3×16=48 cm.3\times16=48\text{ cm}.3×16=48 cm.

    Answer

    t=5t=5t=5; each side is 161616 cm and the perimeter is 484848 cm.

    Key idea

    Follow each preserved length through the construction before doing the final calculation.

  10. Problem 10 Build from an unmeasured source

    On paper, draw a nonzero source segment UVUVUV and a separate ray PXPXPX.

    Construct an equilateral triangle PQRPQRPQR with QQQ on ray PXPXPX and each side equal in length to UVUVUV.

    Use only a compass and unmarked straightedge after drawing the givens.

    Leave all helper circles visible and mark the resulting equal lengths.

    1. Hint 1

      First create the required base on the target ray.

    2. Hint 2

      Then use that new base as the common radius of two endpoint-centered circles.

    Answer

    A valid construction has QQQ on ray PXPXPX and PQ=PR=QR=UVPQ=PR=QR=UVPQ=PR=QR=UV, with the circle at PPP reused from the segment copy and the circle at QQQ both retained.

    Full solution

    Set the compass to UVUVUV and draw a circle centered at PPP, meeting ray PXPXPX at QQQ.

    The radius condition gives PQ=UVPQ=UVPQ=UV.

    Keep that opening and draw a circle centered at QQQ.

    The first circle already supplies the required circle centered at PPP.

    Choose either intersection of these two circles as RRR and join PRPRPR and QRQRQR.

    Because RRR lies on the circle centered at PPP with radius UVUVUV, PR=UVPR=UVPR=UV.

    Because RRR lies on the circle centered at QQQ with radius UVUVUV, QR=UVQR=UVQR=UV.

    Mark the four lengths equal using the separately established equalities PQ=UVPQ=UVPQ=UV, PR=UVPR=UVPR=UV, and QR=UVQR=UVQR=UV.

    Answer

    A valid construction has QQQ on ray PXPXPX and PQ=PR=QR=UVPQ=PR=QR=UVPQ=PR=QR=UV, with the circle at PPP reused from the segment copy and the circle at QQQ both retained.

    Key idea

    A copied segment can become the given side of a second construction without ever reading its numerical length.
