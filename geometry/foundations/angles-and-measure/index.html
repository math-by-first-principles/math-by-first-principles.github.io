Angles and Their Measure

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

A short door and a long door can open through the same turn. The distance to the far edge changes, but the opening does not. An angle measures that opening. Drawing longer pieces of its rays does not make the angle larger.

Learning goals

  • Name an angle with its vertex in the middle and its opening specified
  • Measure and draw angles using degrees and the correct protractor scale
  • Classify angles and turns by comparing their measures with quarter, half, and full turns
  • Find missing measures by adding nonoverlapping parts of a selected angle
  • Use an angle bisector to relate equal parts to the whole opening

Name the rays and the opening

An angle is formed by two rays with a common endpoint, called its vertex. If the rays are OA→\overrightarrow{OA}OA and OB→\overrightarrow{OB}OB, their vertex is OOO. Name the angle ∠AOB\angle AOB∠AOB or ∠BOA\angle BOA∠BOA: the vertex goes in the middle. The outer letters name points on the two rays, not endpoints where the rays stop.

The rays bound two openings: one on either side. Here the short arc selects the smaller opening. Unless a different opening is marked or named, three-letter angle notation means the opening of at most a half turn. To ask for the larger opening, we will say reflex explicitly. You may shorten the name to ∠O\angle O∠O only when the intended angle at OOO is unambiguous.

Check your understanding

Three distinct rays RS→\overrightarrow{RS}RS, RT→\overrightarrow{RT}RT, and RU→\overrightarrow{RU}RU meet at RRR. The selected opening between RS→\overrightarrow{RS}RS and RT→\overrightarrow{RT}RT is strictly less than a half turn. Which name identifies it unambiguously?

Answer choices

The selected opening uses rays RS and RT with vertex R, so its three-letter name is ∠SRT\angle SRT∠SRT. The name ∠TRS\angle TRS∠TRS would also identify it.

With three distinct rays at R, ∠R\angle R∠R alone is ambiguous. ∠SRU\angle SRU∠SRU uses the wrong second ray, and ∠RST\angle RST∠RST names S as its vertex.

Degrees count the turn

A degree is 1360\frac{1}{360}3601​ of a full turn. Write 65∘65^\circ65∘ for 65 degrees. The notation m∠AOB=65∘m\angle AOB = 65^\circm∠AOB=65∘ says that the measure of angle AOBAOBAOB is 65 degrees. The angle is the geometric object; its measure is the number with the degree unit.

A full turn measures 360∘360^\circ360∘ by this choice of unit. We accept angle addition, the additivity of angle measures, as a measurement foundation: the measures of consecutive parts with nonoverlapping interiors that fill a selected opening or turn exactly once add to the whole measure. This includes parts that fill a full turn. The selected route around the vertex and its parts must be specified.

A quarter turn is 360∘÷4=90∘360^\circ \div 4 = 90^\circ360∘÷4=90∘. A half turn is 180∘180^\circ180∘. For example, 35∘35^\circ35∘ is less than a quarter turn, while 115∘115^\circ115∘ lies between a quarter turn and a half turn. The names below describe the selected opening.

NameMeasure
Acute angleGreater than 0∘0^\circ0∘ and less than 90∘90^\circ90∘
Right angleExactly 90∘90^\circ90∘
Obtuse angleGreater than 90∘90^\circ90∘ and less than 180∘180^\circ180∘
Straight angleExactly 180∘180^\circ180∘; its sides are opposite rays
Reflex angleGreater than 180∘180^\circ180∘ and less than 360∘360^\circ360∘
Full turnExactly 360∘360^\circ360∘; the turning ray returns to its starting direction

A 90∘90^\circ90∘ right angle is the square corner used to describe perpendicular lines in lesson 1.1.

A 0∘0^\circ0∘ turn makes no rotation. Its final ray points in the same direction as its starting ray, just as after a full turn, but the turns are different. Their directions alone do not record how far you turned.

Take two rays with different directions that bound a selected 125∘125^\circ125∘ opening. The other route between the rays completes one full turn with that opening. By the accepted additivity foundation, the other opening measures

360∘−125∘=235∘.360^\circ - 125^\circ = 235^\circ.360∘−125∘=235∘.

This is between a half turn and a full turn, so it is the reflex opening.

For any two rays with different directions, following their two openings around the vertex fills one full turn with no overlapping interiors. Additivity and the degree unit make the two measures total 360∘360^\circ360∘, so subtracting either measure from 360∘360^\circ360∘ gives the other. When the smaller opening is strictly between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘, the other is reflex. For opposite rays, both openings are 180∘180^\circ180∘ instead.

Change the turn below, then change each drawn ray length while keeping the turn fixed. Try 90∘90^\circ90∘, 180∘180^\circ180∘, 235∘235^\circ235∘, and 360∘360^\circ360∘. The full-circle protractor counts counterclockwise from the fixed blue ray; it has a different layout from the semicircular protractor used in the next section.

Check your understanding

A ray makes a turn of 235∘235^\circ235∘. How is that selected opening classified?

Answer choices

A straight angle is 180∘180^\circ180∘, and a full turn is 360∘360^\circ360∘. Since 180∘<235∘<360∘180^\circ < 235^\circ < 360^\circ180∘<235∘<360∘, this is a reflex angle.

Use a protractor from its zero

A protractor is a tool with a degree scale for measuring or drawing angles. Its center mark belongs on the vertex. Its straight baseline must lie along one ray. Read the scale whose 000 lies on that starting ray, following that scale to the other ray. If a drawn ray is too short to reach the scale, extend its drawing along the same direction. That does not change the angle.

Worked example 1 Read 65∘65^\circ65∘ and draw 115∘115^\circ115∘

Problem. In the protractor diagram above, the starting ray meets the inner zero and the other ray meets inner 656565 and outer 115115115. Read the selected opening and explain which scale to use. Then, on a separate sheet, draw and label a new 115∘115^\circ115∘ angle KJLKJLKJL using ray JKJKJK as its starting side.

Solution.

The starting ray meets the inner 000, so follow the inner scale to the other ray. It reads 65∘65^\circ65∘.

The other printed number, 115115115, belongs to the scale that starts at the left. The two scales start on opposite baseline rays and count equal degree steps toward the same mark. Their readings count the two parts of the 180∘180^\circ180∘ semicircle:

65∘+115∘=180∘.65^\circ + 115^\circ = 180^\circ.65∘+115∘=180∘.

The acute appearance is a useful check, but the aligned center, baseline, and selected scale establish the reading.

On a separate sheet, draw a new ray JKJKJK to begin a 115∘115^\circ115∘ angle. Place the protractor’s center at JJJ and align its baseline with JKJKJK. Select the scale whose zero lies on that ray.

On that same scale, mark 115∘115^\circ115∘ at a new point LLL. Remove the protractor and draw ray JLJLJL from JJJ through the mark. Label the selected opening ∠KJL\angle KJL∠KJL as 115∘115^\circ115∘; it is obtuse. This is a new drawing, separate from the 65∘65^\circ65∘ angle in the figure above.

To measure a reflex opening with a semicircular protractor, measure the smaller opening first and subtract it from 360∘360^\circ360∘. This works because the two selected openings make one full turn.

Check your understanding

The baseline ray meets the outer 000. At the other ray, the outer scale reads 115115115 and the inner scale reads 656565. What is the angle measure?

Answer choices

Use one scale from start to finish. The starting ray meets the outer 000, so the outer reading gives 115∘115^\circ115∘. The inner 656565 starts from the opposite direction.

Add parts only when they fill the opening

Suppose a ray inside a 100∘100^\circ100∘ opening cuts it into parts of 35∘35^\circ35∘ and 65∘65^\circ65∘. Following the first turn and then the second reaches the same final ray as the whole turn:

35∘+65∘=100∘.35^\circ + 65^\circ = 100^\circ.35∘+65∘=100∘.

Apply the angle-addition foundation already stated in the degree section. When OB→\overrightarrow{OB}OB lies inside the selected angle AOCAOCAOC and its two selected parts fill that opening without overlapping interiors,

m∠AOB+m∠BOC=m∠AOC.m\angle AOB + m\angle BOC = m\angle AOC.m∠AOB+m∠BOC=m∠AOC.

The parts must fill the selected opening exactly once. Merely sharing a vertex is not enough. For a reflex opening, specify that opening explicitly and follow its parts around it. For three consecutive parts, combine the first two into one angle, then apply angle addition again to combine that angle with the third part.

Worked example 2 Find a missing part of an opening

Problem. Ray PB→\overrightarrow{PB}PB lies inside the selected angle APCAPCAPC. The whole measures 148∘148^\circ148∘, and m∠APB=57∘m\angle APB=57^\circm∠APB=57∘. Find m∠BPCm\angle BPCm∠BPC and justify the angle-addition equation.

Solution.

Write angle addition before subtracting:

57∘+m∠BPC=148∘.57^\circ + m\angle BPC = 148^\circ.57∘+m∠BPC=148∘.

Subtract the known part from the whole:

m∠BPC=148∘−57∘=91∘.m\angle BPC = 148^\circ - 57^\circ = 91^\circ.m∠BPC=148∘−57∘=91∘.

Check your understanding

Three nonoverlapping consecutive parts fill one selected opening. They measure 28∘28^\circ28∘, 46∘46^\circ46∘, and 39∘39^\circ39∘. What is the whole measure?

Answer choices

Apply angle addition to the first two consecutive parts.

28∘+46∘=74∘28^\circ + 46^\circ = 74^\circ28∘+46∘=74∘

Apply it again to combine that angle with the remaining part.

74∘+39∘=113∘74^\circ + 39^\circ = 113^\circ74∘+39∘=113∘

A bisector makes equal angular parts

An angle bisector is a ray from the vertex inside the selected opening that divides it into two angles of equal measure. For a 130∘130^\circ130∘ opening, let each part measure xxx degrees. The parts are equal by the bisector definition and add to the whole by angle addition:

x+x=130.x + x = 130.x+x=130.

Thus 2x=1302x = 1302x=130, so each part measures

130∘÷2=65∘.130^\circ \div 2 = 65^\circ.130∘÷2=65∘.

Angles with equal measures are called congruent angles. Write ∠AOB≅∠BOC\angle AOB \cong \angle BOC∠AOB≅∠BOC for the angles, and m∠AOB=m∠BOCm\angle AOB = m\angle BOCm∠AOB=m∠BOC for their measures. Congruence markings on a diagram record that equality; they do not follow merely from the way the sketch looks. In the diagram below, the single tick on each angle arc is the matching mark. Equal drawn ray lengths are not required.

In the explorer, set the turn to 130∘130^\circ130∘ and show the bisector. Then change the drawn ray lengths. Each part remains 65∘65^\circ65∘ because the bisector divides the turn. For 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 360∘360^\circ360∘, this explorer leaves the bisector hidden.

Worked example 3 Use equal parts before adding them

Problem. Ray OB→\overrightarrow{OB}OB bisects the selected angle AOCAOCAOC. Its two parts measure (3x+7)∘(3x+7)^\circ(3x+7)∘ and (5x−19)∘(5x-19)^\circ(5x−19)∘. Find xxx, each part measure, and the whole measure m∠AOCm\angle AOCm∠AOC.

Solution.

A bisector makes the parts equal, so

3x+7=5x−19.3x + 7 = 5x - 19.3x+7=5x−19.

Subtract 3x3x3x and add 191919 to obtain 26=2x26 = 2x26=2x, so x=13x = 13x=13. Each part measures 3(13)+7=463(13) + 7 = 463(13)+7=46 degrees. Angle addition gives the whole:

m∠AOC=46∘+46∘=92∘.m\angle AOC = 46^\circ + 46^\circ = 92^\circ.m∠AOC=46∘+46∘=92∘.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Naming the wrong vertex. The middle letter names the common endpoint of the rays.
  • Reading the other scale. Start at the zero on your baseline ray and stay on that scale.
  • Judging by ray length. Rays continue indefinitely; drawing more of one does not enlarge the opening.
  • Adding overlapping angles. Angle addition counts parts that fill the selected opening without overlap.
  • Halving the wrong quantity. A bisector halves the angular measure, not a drawn ray length.

Key takeaways

  • Degrees measure turns; a quarter turn is 90∘90^\circ90∘, a half turn 180∘180^\circ180∘, and a full turn 360∘360^\circ360∘.
  • Angle names put the vertex in the middle. Specify a reflex opening when you intend it.
  • Align a protractor’s center and baseline, then read from the zero on the starting ray.
  • Angle addition adds consecutive nonoverlapping parts that fill the selected opening or turn.
  • Angle bisectors divide the opening into two congruent angles, each with half the whole measure.

A bit of history (optional)

The Royal Society keeps a brass protractor made by the instrument maker Jesse Ramsden in the late eighteenth century. It is a full circle rather than the familiar classroom semicircle. An arm pivots at its center, and a small secondary scale lets a reader measure finer divisions than the main marks alone.

The instrument is engraved with Ramsden’s name and London. Its exact early history is unknown. The Society’s catalog suggests a possible connection with a survey comparing the positions of the Greenwich and Paris observatories, but does not present that connection as certain. The surviving tool shows the care instrument makers put into recording a direction precisely.

The Royal Society’s catalog entry describes the protractor and separates its known features from its uncertain history.
