Intuition

Two observers on a trail can both see a lookout across a gorge. They can measure the trail between them and the direction to the lookout from each end. How can those readings tell either observer how far away the lookout is?

Learning goals

  • Derive the law of sines by writing one triangle's area in three ways
  • Solve ASA and AAS triangles using a known opposite pair
  • Model an inaccessible distance from a measured baseline and two angles
  • Classify SSA data by counting the valid intersections of a circle and a ray
  • Test both angle candidates and find all missing measurements in SSA triangles

One area connects the opposite pairs

The sine of an angle and the length across from it form the same ratio at every corner of a triangle. To see where the ratio comes from, start with a triangle whose legs are 5 and 12 and whose hypotenuse is 13. Use capital letters for angles and lowercase letters for their opposite side lengths. The lengths of aaa, bbb, and ccc are 5, 12, and 13, respectively.

The legs are perpendicular, so half their product gives an area KKK of 30 square units. Multiply each included-angle area expression by 2, then divide by the product of the three side lengths. Each calculation gives the same quotient.

2K5⋅12⋅13=113.sin⁡A5=113.sin⁡B12=113.sin⁡C13=113.\begin{aligned} \frac{2K}{5\cdot12\cdot13}&=\frac{1}{13}.\\[2pt] \frac{\sin A}{5}&=\frac{1}{13}.\\[2pt] \frac{\sin B}{12}&=\frac{1}{13}.\\[2pt] \frac{\sin C}{13}&=\frac{1}{13}. \end{aligned}5⋅12⋅132K​5sinA​12sinB​13sinC​​=131​.=131​.=131​.=131​.​

For example, using sides 12 and 13 gives twice the area as 12⋅13sin⁡A12\cdot13\sin A12⋅13sinA. Dividing by all three lengths cancels 12 and 13, so the 5 across from AAA stays below sin⁡A\sin AsinA. The same cancellation works at the other two corners. The argument uses the included-angle formula from Triangle Area with Sine Advanced. This lesson goes beyond core Geometry. Why opposite side and sine ratios agree#

Take any nondegenerate triangle, with positive sides aaa, bbb, and ccc. Apply the area formula once at each corner.

2K=bcsin⁡A.2K=casin⁡B.2K=absin⁡C.\begin{aligned} 2K&=bc\sin A.\\ 2K&=ca\sin B.\\ 2K&=ab\sin C. \end{aligned}2K2K2K​=bcsinA.=casinB.=absinC.​

Divide each equation by abcabcabc. The same quotient on the left appears three times.

2Kabc=sin⁡Aa.2Kabc=sin⁡Bb.2Kabc=sin⁡Cc.\begin{aligned} \frac{2K}{abc}&=\frac{\sin A}{a}.\\[2pt] \frac{2K}{abc}&=\frac{\sin B}{b}.\\[2pt] \frac{2K}{abc}&=\frac{\sin C}{c}. \end{aligned}abc2K​abc2K​abc2K​​=asinA​.=bsinB​.=csinC​.​

This common value is positive. Taking reciprocals gives the law of sines in side-over-sine form.

asin⁡A=bsin⁡B.bsin⁡B=csin⁡C.\begin{aligned} \frac{a}{\sin A}&=\frac{b}{\sin B}.\\[2pt] \frac{b}{\sin B}&=\frac{c}{\sin C}. \end{aligned}sinAa​sinBb​​=sinBb​.=sinCc​.​

Two angles and a side fix the triangle

In ASA, the known side joins the two known angle vertices. In AAS, it joins one known angle vertex to the unknown one. Either way, subtract the known angles from 180∘180^\circ180∘. A positive third angle gives the shape, and the known side fixes its scale. Use the known side and its opposite angle to find the missing sides.

Worked example 1 Use the side between two angles

Problem. In triangle ABCABCABC, the angles at AAA and BBB measure 48∘48^\circ48∘ and 67∘67^\circ67∘, respectively. The side ABABAB is 19 cm long. Find CCC, aaa, and bbb, rounding lengths to the nearest tenth of a centimeter.

Solution. The known side lies across from CCC. Find its partner angle.

C=180∘−48∘−67∘=65∘.\begin{aligned} C&=180^\circ-48^\circ-67^\circ\\ &=65^\circ. \end{aligned}C​=180∘−48∘−67∘=65∘.​

Use the known pair c=19c=19c=19 cm and C=65∘C=65^\circC=65∘ in each proportion.

a=19sin⁡48∘sin⁡65∘ cm≈15.6 cm.\begin{aligned} a&=\frac{19\sin48^\circ}{\sin65^\circ}\text{ cm}\\ &\approx15.6\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}a​=sin65∘19sin48∘​ cm≈15.6 cm.​b=19sin⁡67∘sin⁡65∘ cm≈19.3 cm.\begin{aligned} b&=\frac{19\sin67^\circ}{\sin65^\circ}\text{ cm}\\ &\approx19.3\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}b​=sin65∘19sin67∘​ cm≈19.3 cm.​

The length order matches the angle order, with AAA smallest and BBB largest.

Worked example 2 Start with an opposite pair already given

Problem. In triangle ABCABCABC, angle AAA measures 37∘37^\circ37∘, angle CCC measures 108∘108^\circ108∘, and BCBCBC is 11 m long. Find BBB, bbb, and ccc, rounding lengths to the nearest tenth of a meter.

Solution. The side BCBCBC lies across from AAA, so aaa and AAA form the known pair. The endpoints of the known side are BBB and CCC, so this is AAS.

B=180∘−37∘−108∘=35∘.\begin{aligned} B&=180^\circ-37^\circ-108^\circ\\ &=35^\circ. \end{aligned}B​=180∘−37∘−108∘=35∘.​b=11sin⁡35∘sin⁡37∘ m≈10.5 m.\begin{aligned} b&=\frac{11\sin35^\circ}{\sin37^\circ}\text{ m}\\ &\approx10.5\text{ m}. \end{aligned}b​=sin37∘11sin35∘​ m≈10.5 m.​c=11sin⁡108∘sin⁡37∘ m≈17.4 m.\begin{aligned} c&=\frac{11\sin108^\circ}{\sin37^\circ}\text{ m}\\ &\approx17.4\text{ m}. \end{aligned}c​=sin37∘11sin108∘​ m≈17.4 m.​

In triangle ABCABCABC, BBB is 44∘44^\circ44∘, CCC is 71∘71^\circ71∘, and ACACAC is 9 cm long. Which expression gives ABABAB in centimeters?

Answer choices

Measure from a baseline

The triangle needs a straight measured baseline and two directions to the same target. Use the interior angle from the baseline ray pointing toward the other observer at each station. A compass reading by itself gives a direction from north, so it would first need to be converted to an interior angle.

Worked example 3 Find the distance to a lookout

Problem. Stations AAA and BBB lie 76 m apart on a straight trail segment. Both stations and lookout CCC lie in one horizontal plane. The interior angle from ABABAB to ACACAC at AAA is 38∘38^\circ38∘. The interior angle from BABABA to BCBCBC at BBB is 54∘54^\circ54∘. How far is the lookout from station AAA, to the nearest tenth of a meter?

Solution. The baseline ABABAB is opposite the lookout angle.

C=180∘−38∘−54∘=88∘.\begin{aligned} C&=180^\circ-38^\circ-54^\circ\\ &=88^\circ. \end{aligned}C​=180∘−38∘−54∘=88∘.​

The requested distance is ACACAC, across from BBB.

AC=76sin⁡54∘sin⁡88∘ m≈61.5 m.\begin{aligned} AC&=\frac{76\sin54^\circ}{\sin88^\circ}\text{ m}\\ &\approx61.5\text{ m}. \end{aligned}AC​=sin88∘76sin54∘​ m≈61.5 m.​

The angle at BBB is smaller than the angle at CCC, so ACACAC should be shorter than the baseline. The result passes that check.

Two sides and a nonincluded angle can leave a choice

With SSA, you know two sides and an angle opposite one of them. Suppose the data are an angle AAA strictly between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘ and positive lengths aaa and bbb. Fix ACACAC with length bbb and draw the ray from AAA on which BBB must lie. The point BBB must be exactly aaa units from CCC, so it must also lie on the circle of radius aaa centered at CCC. Each crossing on the ray past AAA gives a triangle. A reflected drawing gives the same measurements and adds no new triangle up to congruence.

Explain the thresholds

For any fixed 0∘<A<180∘0^\circ<A<180^\circ0∘<A<180∘, the height is h=bsin⁡Ah=b\sin Ah=bsinA, and the signed position of HHH along the line from AAA is bcos⁡Ab\cos AbcosA. A positive projection puts HHH ahead of AAA. Zero puts HHH at AAA, and a negative projection puts HHH behind AAA.

Acute AAA. No point on the line is closer to CCC than HHH. When the radius reaches hhh, only HHH is available. For a larger radius, two line intersections sit on opposite sides of HHH, equally far from it. As the radius grows, the farther intersection stays on the ray, while the nearer one moves toward AAA. It reaches AAA exactly when the radius equals ACACAC, which is bbb. At that endpoint, B=AB=AB=A would give a zero-length side, so count only the farther intersection.

Right or obtuse AAA. Here HHH is at AAA or behind it. The distance from CCC to a point on the ray is shortest at AAA and grows strictly as the point moves forward. To see this, the perpendicular height stays fixed while the horizontal distance from HHH increases. The Pythagorean theorem then makes the distance from CCC increase as well. Every distance greater than bbb occurs exactly once, since the distance increases continuously from bbb without bound.

Find and check the SSA angles

The circle explains how many solutions to expect. The law of sines gives the angle values.

sin⁡B=bsin⁡Aa.\sin B=\frac{b\sin A}{a}.sinB=absinA​.

Call the value on the right qqq. For valid givens it is positive. If q>1q>1q>1, no angle works. If 0<q≤10<q\le10<q≤1, use degree mode to compute B1=arcsin⁡qB_1=\arcsin qB1​=arcsinq. The inverse sine key gives the angle up to 90∘90^\circ90∘. On the unit semicircle, a horizontal line at height qqq can meet the arc at most twice. The two angles are supplementary, so also try 180∘−B1180^\circ-B_1180∘−B1​ when it differs from B1B_1B1​. At q=1q=1q=1 the line touches the arc at its top. Test 90∘90^\circ90∘ once.

For each distinct candidate, find the remaining angle and keep the candidate only if that remaining angle is positive.

C=180∘−A−B.C=180^\circ-A-B.C=180∘−A−B.

A candidate that passes this check really does give a triangle. Construct it from AAA, BBB, and the given side aaa using two angles and a side. The sine law then gives b=asin⁡B/sin⁡Ab=a\sin B/\sin Ab=asinB/sinA, exactly the supplied length because the candidate was chosen to satisfy the sine equation. Find ccc using that triangle’s value of CCC. Keep full precision through the angle tests and side calculation, especially near a boundary.

Worked example 4 Keep both angles when both fit

Problem. Triangle ABCABCABC has these measurements.

A=34∘a=10 cmb=14 cm\begin{gathered} A=34^\circ\\ a=10\text{ cm}\\ b=14\text{ cm} \end{gathered}A=34∘a=10 cmb=14 cm​

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Opposite pair. The side between two given angles pairs with the third angle.
  • Supplement. An inverse sine value can give one of two candidate angles. Test each distinct candidate against the angle sum.
  • Boundary. A candidate with zero remaining angle is degenerate. At the tangent threshold, the two sine candidates coincide and count once.
  • Rounding. Use degree mode. Keep full precision before deciding if an angle is positive or a side reaches a threshold.

Key takeaways

  • Equal expressions for one area give the same ratio at all three opposite pairs.
  • With two angles and a side, find the third angle and use the known pair to set the scale.
  • With SSA, count circle intersections strictly beyond the ray’s start and check the remaining angle for each sine candidate.

A bit of history (optional)

While working on an account of Ptolemy’s astronomy, the fifteenth-century mathematician Regiomontanus promised to write a separate guide to triangles. In that guide, he pointed readers who were stuck on a theorem to its numerical examples.

He divided the work into five books. Book II used the law of sines to solve triangles on a plane. The last three books turned to triangles on a sphere, which mattered for locating objects in the sky. MacTutor’s biography of Regiomontanus
