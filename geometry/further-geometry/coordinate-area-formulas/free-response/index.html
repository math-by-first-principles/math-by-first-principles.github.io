  1. Problem 1 Two numbers for one edge

    A directed boundary edge goes from P(−2,4)P(-2,4)P(−2,4) to Q(4,7)Q(4,7)Q(4,7). Find the signed trapezoid contribution relative to y=1y=1y=1 and the shoelace product difference in the original coordinates.

    1. Hint 1

      The signed width is the starting horizontal coordinate minus the ending horizontal coordinate.

    2. Hint 2

      Measure the trapezoid heights above the reference line. Use the original coordinates in the diagonal products.

    Answer

    The trapezoid contribution is −27-27−27 square units. The product difference is −30-30−30.

    Full solution

    The edge moves right by 6 units, so its signed width is −6-6−6.

    The heights above the reference line are 3 and 6.

    Multiply the signed width by the average height.

    E=−6⋅3+62E=−27.\begin{aligned}E&=-6\cdot\tfrac{3+6}{2}\\E&=-27.\end{aligned}EE​=−6⋅23+6​=−27.​

    Use the original coordinates for the product difference.

    dPQ=(−2)⋅7−4⋅4dPQ=−30.\begin{aligned}d_{PQ}&=(-2)\cdot7-4\cdot4\\d_{PQ}&=-30.\end{aligned}dPQ​dPQ​​=(−2)⋅7−4⋅4=−30.​

    Answer

    The trapezoid contribution is −27-27−27 square units. The product difference is −30-30−30.

    Key idea

    The signed trapezoid and the product difference describe different quantities on one edge.

  4. Problem 4 One outline, two methods

    A pentagon has vertices A(−2,0)A(-2,0)A(−2,0), B(5,0)B(5,0)B(5,0), C(5,3)C(5,3)C(5,3), D(1,6)D(1,6)D(1,6), and E(−2,3)E(-2,3)E(−2,3) in boundary order.

    Find the ordinary area by decomposition and by shoelace. Explain which method needs less computation here.

    Text description of this figure

    A and B lie on the horizontal axis. C and E lie three units above it on opposite sides of the shape. D is above both.

    1. Hint 1

      For decomposition, draw the horizontal segment CECECE.

    2. Hint 2

      The two methods must measure the same region. Retain the closing edge in the shoelace sum.

    Answer

    Both methods give 63/263/263/2 square units. The rectangle and triangle make decomposition shorter here.

    Full solution

    Decomposition.

    The rectangle below CECECE has width 7 and height 3.

    The triangle above CECECE has base 7 and perpendicular height 3.

    K=7⋅3+12(7)(3)K=63/2.\begin{aligned}K&=7\cdot3+\tfrac12(7)(3)\\K&=63/2.\end{aligned}KK​=7⋅3+21​(7)(3)=63/2.​

    Shoelace.

    The five product differences are 000, 151515, 272727, 151515, and 666.

    2S=0+15+27+15+6S=63/2.\begin{aligned}2S&=0+15+27+15+6\\S&=63/2.\end{aligned}2SS​=0+15+27+15+6=63/2.​

    Taking the magnitude gives 63/263/263/2 square units.

    Here the decomposition uses just two familiar areas, while shoelace needs a difference for each of five edges.

    Answer

    Both methods give 63/263/263/2 square units. The rectangle and triangle make decomposition shorter here.

    Key idea

    Choose decomposition when one cut produces pieces with easy bases and heights.

  5. Problem 5 Pair both parts of a strip

    A simple polygon follows A(0,0)A(0,0)A(0,0), B(7,0)B(7,0)B(7,0), C(7,2)C(7,2)C(7,2), D(3,2)D(3,2)D(3,2), E(3,5)E(3,5)E(3,5), F(7,5)F(7,5)F(7,5), G(7,8)G(7,8)G(7,8), and H(0,8)H(0,8)H(0,8). Close at AAA.

    Use the reference line y=−2y=-2y=−2. Find the net signed trapezoid contributions within the left strip between x=0x=0x=0 and x=3x=3x=3 and the right strip between x=3x=3x=3 and x=7x=7x=7. Find the signed area.

    Text description of this figure

    A and B lie along the bottom. C is directly above B, D is left of C, E is directly above D, and F is right of E. G is above F, and H lies left of G. The eight sides form a rectangular notch from the right.

    1. Hint 1

      The right strip contains a lower piece and an upper piece. Pair the top and bottom of each piece separately.

    2. Hint 2

      Every horizontal boundary edge moving left adds a rectangle down to the reference line. Every horizontal edge moving right subtracts one.

    Answer

    The left strip contributes 242424 square units. The right strip contributes 202020 square units. The signed area is 444444 square units.

    Full solution

    Left strip.

    The leftward upper edge has height 10 above the reference line.

    The rightward lower edge has height 2.

    The net contribution is 3(10−2)3(10-2)3(10−2), or 24.

    Right strip, lower piece.

    The upper and lower heights are 4 and 2.

    The net contribution is 4(4−2)4(4-2)4(4−2), or 8.

    Right strip, upper piece.

    The upper and lower heights are 10 and 7.

    The net contribution is 4(10−7)4(10-7)4(10−7), or 12.

    Right strip total.

    Add 8 and 12 to get 20.

    Signed area.

    The left and right strips account for all the directed contributions.

    The vertical edges have zero width.

    S=24+20S=44.\begin{aligned}S&=24+20\\S&=44.\end{aligned}SS​=24+20=44.​

    Answer

    The left strip contributes 242424 square units. The right strip contributes 202020 square units. The signed area is 444444 square units.

    Key idea

    Separate the inside pieces of a concave strip before pairing their upper and lower edges.

  6. Problem 6 Lower the reference line

    A simple quadrilateral follows A(1,1)A(1,1)A(1,1), B(5,3)B(5,3)B(5,3), C(4,6)C(4,6)C(4,6), and D(−2,4)D(-2,4)D(−2,4). The reference line drops from y=0y=0y=0 to y=−3y=-3y=−3.

    Find the change in the signed trapezoid contribution for each directed edge. Explain why dropping a reference line below any simple polygon leaves the sum unchanged.

    1. Hint 1

      The average height for every edge increases by 3.

    2. Hint 2

      Add the signed widths around the walk and track the horizontal position where it closes.

    Answer

    The changes for ABABAB, BCBCBC, CDCDCD, and DADADA in that direction are −12-12−12, 333, 181818, and −9-9−9 square units. The total change is zero for any closed boundary.

    Full solution

    The signed widths from AAA around the boundary are −4-4−4, 111, 666, and −3-3−3.

    Each average height increases by 3, so multiplying each signed width by 3 gives −12-12−12, 333, 181818, and −9-9−9.

    These changes sum to zero.

    For any simple polygon, a downward change in the reference line increases every average height by the same amount.

    The change in each contribution is that amount times its signed width.

    The signed widths add to zero because each horizontal coordinate appears once with a plus sign and once with a minus sign.

    The total contribution therefore stays the same.

    Answer

    The changes for ABABAB, BCBCBC, CDCDCD, and DADADA in that direction are −12-12−12, 333, 181818, and −9-9−9 square units. The total change is zero for any closed boundary.

    Key idea

    Moving the reference line adds a multiple of the total signed width, which is zero.

  7. Problem 7 Orient both pieces

    The order A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D follows a simple quadrilateral counterclockwise. Its interior diagonal ACACAC divides the region into two triangles.

    Triangle ABCABCABC has signed area 151515 in that order. Triangle ADCADCADC has signed area −10-10−10 in that order.

    Find the signed area of ABCDABCDABCD. Name the directed copies of the diagonal that cancel and explain why.

    Text description of this figure

    A is left of C. B lies below the segment AC and D lies above it. A dashed diagonal joins A to C within the quadrilateral.

    1. Hint 1

      Choose the same orientation for both triangle boundaries.

    2. Hint 2

      List the closing edge of the reoriented triangle, along with its first edge.

    Answer

    The signed area is 252525 square units. The directed diagonals C→AC\to AC→A and A→CA\to CA→C cancel.

    Full solution

    Keep the order A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C for the first triangle.

    Reverse A,D,CA,D,CA,D,C to A,C,DA,C,DA,C,D for the second, so its signed area becomes positive 10.

    The first triangle closes with C→AC\to AC→A.

    The second opens with A→CA\to CA→C.

    Their shoelace products swap order, so the two differences add to zero.

    The remaining edges follow A,B,C,DA,B,C,DA,B,C,D and return to AAA.

    Its signed area is 15+1015+1015+10, or 25 square units.

    Answer

    The signed area is 252525 square units. The directed diagonals C→AC\to AC→A and A→CA\to CA→C cancel.

    Key idea

    When neighboring pieces share a diagonal, give both the same orientation to cancel the opposite copies of that edge.

  9. Problem 9 Insert a vertex on a vertical edge

    A simple polygon has a vertical boundary edge from P(a,b)P(a,b)P(a,b) to R(a,d)R(a,d)R(a,d) with b<db<db<d. Insert Q(a,c)Q(a,c)Q(a,c) between them, where b<c<db<c<db<c<d.

    Show that the original product difference is the sum of the two new ones. Explain why the same ordinary area follows even if some of the coordinates are negative.

    1. Hint 1

      The two endpoints of each vertical edge have the same horizontal coordinate.

    2. Hint 2

      Keep the middle height ccc visible until the two new terms are added.

    Answer

    The new differences add to a(d−b)a(d-b)a(d−b), the original difference. Signed and ordinary area are unchanged.

    Full solution

    The original product difference is ad−abad-abad−ab, or a(d−b)a(d-b)a(d−b).

    The difference for P→QP\to QP→Q is ac−abac-abac−ab.

    The difference for Q→RQ\to RQ→R is ad−acad-acad−ac.

    When added, the terms acacac and −ac-ac−ac cancel.

    The remaining expression is ad−abad-abad−ab, the original difference.

    The other edges stay the same, and the split keeps the same simple boundary.

    The signed sum is unchanged, so its magnitude is unchanged.

    Cancellation works for all real values of aaa, bbb, ccc, and ddd satisfying the stated order.

    Answer

    The new differences add to a(d−b)a(d-b)a(d−b), the original difference. Signed and ordinary area are unchanged.

    A vertex inserted on a straight vertical edge replaces one height change with two consecutive changes.

  10. Problem 10 Which lists describe the same boundary?

    The simple pentagon with vertices A(0,0)A(0,0)A(0,0), B(7,0)B(7,0)B(7,0), C(7,3)C(7,3)C(7,3), D(3,7)D(3,7)D(3,7), and E(0,3)E(0,3)E(0,3) has signed area 353535 in order A,B,C,D,EA,B,C,D,EA,B,C,D,E. Close each list below at its first vertex.

    List I. D,E,A,B,CD,E,A,B,CD,E,A,B,C.

    List II. E,D,C,B,AE,D,C,B,AE,D,C,B,A.

    List III. A,C,D,E,BA,C,D,E,BA,C,D,E,B.

    For each list that traces the same boundary, give its signed and ordinary areas. For any other list, explain how its segments differ and check for a crossing.

    Explain why every list that uses each original boundary edge once must be a cyclic shift of the original order or of its reversal.

    Text description of this figure

    The original boundary joins A, B, C, D, and E. A and B lie on the bottom, C and E share a horizontal level, and D is above them.

    1. Hint 1

      Check which edge comes immediately after the new starting vertex. A changed start keeps every directed edge.

    2. Hint 2

      The four vertices A,B,C,EA,B,C,EA,B,C,E are the corners of a rectangle.

    Answer

    List I has signed and ordinary area 353535. List II has signed area −35-35−35 and ordinary area 353535. List III crosses at (7/2,3/2)(7/2,3/2)(7/2,3/2). Along the original boundary, the starting vertex and first direction force the rest of the list.

    Full solution

    List I.

    Starting at DDD keeps the same directed edges.

    The signed and ordinary areas are both 35 square units.

    List II.

    Starting at EEE and walking backward reverses every edge.

    The signed area is −35-35−35 square units, and the ordinary area is 35 square units.

    List III.

    The new edges ACACAC and EBEBEB are diagonals of rectangle ABCEABCEABCE.

    They cross at their common midpoint (7/2,3/2)(7/2,3/2)(7/2,3/2).

    This walk cannot serve as the simple boundary of the pentagon.

    Any list using each original boundary edge once has a choice of starting vertex and of which of its two neighbors comes first.

    At every later vertex, the arrival edge has already been used, so the other boundary edge must come next.

    This forces a complete walk around the cycle in the chosen direction.

    Those two choices give exactly the cyclic shifts of the original order or its reversal.

    Answer

    List I has signed and ordinary area 353535. List II has signed area −35-35−35 and ordinary area 353535. List III crosses at (7/2,3/2)(7/2,3/2)(7/2,3/2). Along the original boundary, the starting vertex and first direction force the rest of the list.

    Key idea

    A new starting point and a reversal preserve the boundary, while an arbitrary rearrangement can change its segments.
