Further Geometry: Chapter Review

A rapid review before the test: the chapter's vocabulary and notation, every formula with the conditions to use it, the standard problem types step by step, and the traps that cost points.

Vocabulary and notation

Pythagorean triple and primitive triple
A Pythagorean triple has positive integer legs and hypotenuse satisfying the Pythagorean theorem. It is primitive when the greatest common factor (GCF) of all three entries is 111.
Coprime and opposite parity
Coprime integers have GCF 111. Integers of opposite parity have one even member and one odd member.
Included angle and opposite pair
In triangle ABCABCABC, the side lengths a,b,ca,b,ca,b,c lie across from angles A,B,CA,B,CA,B,C, respectively. An opposite pair matches a side to the angle across from it. An included angle is at the shared endpoint of two sides.
Unit semicircle and signed horizontal projection
The unit semicircle is the upper half of the radius-one circle centered at the origin. A ray turns counterclockwise from the positive horizontal direction. The horizontal coordinate of its tip at a chosen radius is the signed horizontal projection.
Supplementary angles
Two angles whose measures add to 180∘180^\circ180∘.
ASA, AAS, SAS, SSS, and SSA
ASA gives two angles and the side between them. AAS gives two angles and another side. SAS gives two sides and their included angle. SSS gives all three sides. SSA gives two sides and the angle opposite one of them.
Nondegenerate triangle and semiperimeter
A nondegenerate triangle encloses positive area. Its semiperimeter sss is half the perimeter.
Simple polygon and lattice polygon
A simple polygon has a closed boundary whose straight edges meet only at shared endpoints of consecutive edges. A lattice polygon has integer coordinates at every vertex.
Signed area
With positive xxx to the right and positive yyy up, signed area SSS is positive for a counterclockwise walk around a simple polygon and negative for a clockwise walk. KKK denotes ordinary area.
Auxiliary circle for tangents
For a given circle centered at OOO and an outside point PPP, the auxiliary circle has diameter OPOPOP and center MMM, the midpoint of OPOPOP. Its intersections T1,T2T_1,T_2T1​,T2​ with the given circle are the contacts.

Problem types, step by step

Classify or reverse a Pythagorean triple

  1. For given parameters, check m>n>0m>n>0m>n>0, then use their GCF and parity to classify the output. To generate a scaled copy, multiply the primitive entries by the stated scale.
  2. For given side lengths, identify the hypotenuse and verify the triple if needed. Divide all three entries by their GCF to recover the primitive triple.
  3. Use the primitive odd leg with the hypotenuse in the half-sum and half-difference. Take the positive square roots and check the even leg by doubling their product.

Use an included angle to find or compare areas

  1. Locate the common vertex of the two known sides. Use its interior angle and match the lengths to a common unit.
  2. Multiply half the side product by the sine of that angle in degrees. Keep the exact value if available and round only the final area if asked.
  3. For a fixed side product, compare sines. Supplementary settings share a height for the same arm length and give equal areas.
  4. To recover an included angle from positive area and two sides, solve for its sine. A value above one is impossible. At one use a right angle. Between zero and one use both supplementary angles.

Solve ASA or AAS with the Law of Sines

  1. If using sight lines from a baseline, find the interior angle from the ray toward the other station at each endpoint.
  2. Subtract the two angles from 180∘180^\circ180∘ and check that the third is positive. Match the given side to its opposite angle, which may be the angle just found.
  3. Find each missing side by multiplying the given side by the ratio of the target sine to the known opposite sine. Check that side order matches angle order.

Find every triangle for SSA data

  1. Label the known opposite pair A,aA,aA,a and the other known side bbb. Compute q=bsin⁡A/aq=b\sin A/aq=bsinA/a. If q>1q>1q>1, there is no solution.
  2. For 0<q≤10<q\le10<q≤1, compute B1=arcsin⁡qB_1=\arcsin qB1​=arcsinq in degrees. Also test 180∘−B1180^\circ-B_1180∘−B1​ if distinct. The right-angle candidate counts once.
  3. For each candidate BBB, keep it only if C=180∘−A−B>0C=180^\circ-A-B>0C=180∘−A−B>0. Compute c=asin⁡C/sin⁡Ac=a\sin C/\sin Ac=asinC/sinA separately for each survivor.
  4. Check each recovered side against ∣a−b∣<c<a+b|a-b|<c<a+b∣a−b∣<c<a+b. Use full precision for the decisions and round the reported measurements at the end.

Choose a triangle law for SAS or SSS

  1. For SAS, use the Law of Cosines with the shared angle to get the opposite side. You now have SSS data.
  2. For SSS, check closure. For each angle to find, subtract the square of its opposite side in the cosine numerator and divide by twice the product of its adjacent sides. Use inverse cosine in degrees.
  3. Use the angle sum for the last angle if two are known. Check that larger sides face larger angles and that the angles sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

Find area or height from three sides

  1. Convert to one length unit and test positivity and the strict triangle inequality.
  2. Halve the perimeter. Multiply sss by the three side differences and take the positive square root. To compare two valid areas exactly, compare the products under the roots.
  3. For a height, divide twice the area by the length of the side chosen as the base.

Measure a polygon given its coordinates

  1. Follow the boundary and check that edges meet only at shared endpoints of consecutive edges. Repeat the first vertex after the last.
  2. Form the two ordered products for each consecutive pair and keep their difference signed. Add the differences and halve the sum.
  3. Read the sign to identify orientation and take the absolute value for area. Compare with decomposition when convenient pieces make a useful check.

Construct both tangents from an outside point

  1. Draw equal-opening arcs from OOO and PPP using an opening greater than half of OPOPOP. Join the two arc crossings to bisect OPOPOP at MMM.
  2. Draw the auxiliary circle centered at MMM with radius MO=MPMO=MPMO=MP. Mark its two intersections with the given circle.
  3. Draw a line from PPP through each intersection. Verify that each line is perpendicular to the corresponding contact radius.

Exam traps

  • Trap Using the shorter leg to recover the parameters

    Fix Reduce by the GCF first, then use the odd leg. It can be either the shorter or the longer leg.

  • Trap Checking only parity or only coprimality

    Fix A primitive output requires both. Two odd inputs still give a triple, with all entries even. Shared input factors enter the outputs squared.

  • Trap Turning an obtuse angle's negative projection into a negative height

    Fix Cosine gives the signed horizontal coordinate and sine gives the positive height factor for an interior angle. Extend the base line if the foot is outside, which can also happen with an acute included angle.

  • Trap Confusing an included angle with an opposite pair

    Fix For area with sine or the cosine correction, use the angle at the common endpoint of the two factors. For the Law of Sines, pair each side with the angle across from it.

  • Trap Stopping after the first inverse sine answer or counting a flat SSA case

    Fix Test each distinct candidate with the angle sum. Keep only positive remaining angles. Use degrees and delay rounding until after testing.

  • Trap Using the magnitude of cosine in the Law of Cosines

    Fix Keep the sign. Subtracting a negative correction product makes the opposite side squared larger than the sum of the adjacent squares.

  • Trap Treating the perimeter or squared area as the final quantity in Heron's formula

    Fix Use half the perimeter to form the factors. After confirming strict closure, take the positive square root and report square units.

  • Trap Sorting polygon vertices or taking absolute values edge by edge

    Fix Preserve the simple boundary and include the closing edge. Sum signed differences first, then take the absolute value of the final signed area.

  • Trap Joining the two contacts or using any circle that crosses twice

    Fix Check that OOO and PPP are opposite endpoints of the auxiliary diameter. Draw each tangent through PPP and its own contact. The line through both contacts is a secant.
