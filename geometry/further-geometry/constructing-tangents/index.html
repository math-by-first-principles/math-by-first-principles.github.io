Constructing Tangents from an External Point Advanced. This lesson goes beyond core Geometry. You can skip it.

▾

Progress can't be saved in this browser, so this lesson's status can't be set.

Intuition

Put a straightedge against a circular lid so it passes through a dot on the table. You can pivot the straightedge about the dot to touch the lid on either side. A compass can locate the two exact contact points. The trick is to draw a second circle where the right angle you need comes for free.

Learning goals

  • Construct the circle whose diameter joins the center and the external point
  • Verify both tangents by using the right angles at their contact points
  • Prove that every possible contact point lies on the auxiliary circle
  • Decide how many tangents pass through an outside, boundary, or inside point

Find the midpoint and draw a second circle

Suppose the given circle has center OOO and positive radius rrr. The point PPP is outside the circle, so OP>rOP>rOP>r. We want every line through PPP that touches the circle at just one point.

The radius and tangent make a right angle at the point of contact. In triangle OTPOTPOTP, with the contact point TTT still unknown, the hypotenuse must be OPOPOP. The semicircle theorem tells us where to look for that right angle. At any point of a circle other than the two ends of a diameter, the angle made by joining the point to those ends is right.

Worked example 1 Construct both tangent lines

Problem. A circle centered at OOO has radius 888 cm. The given point PPP is 202020 cm from OOO. Use a compass and unmarked straightedge to construct both tangent lines through PPP. Identify the center and radius of the second circle.

Solution. Use the given lengths to follow the drawing. Once the circle and point are in place, the tools locate each new point by intersection.

Step 1. Draw segment OPOPOP.

Step 2. Open the compass to a width greater than half of OPOPOP. From OOO, draw arcs to either side of OPOPOP. Keep the same opening and draw arcs from PPP so the two pairs cross at UUU and VVV. Draw line UVUVUV and mark its intersection MMM with OPOPOP. Since UUU and VVV are each equally far from OOO and PPP, line UVUVUV is the perpendicular bisector of OPOPOP.

Step 3. Center the compass at MMM and open it to OOO. Draw the auxiliary circle, the extra circle that helps locate the contacts. Its radius is 101010 cm because MMM bisects the 202020 cm segment. It also passes through PPP. Label its intersections with the original circle T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​.

Step 4. Draw the lines through PPP and T1T_1T1​ and through PPP and T2T_2T2​. The short segments from PPP to the contact points determine the two lines, which extend in both directions.

Check your understanding

Which compass opening draws the circle with diameter OPOPOP after you have marked its midpoint MMM?

Answer choices

The auxiliary circle is centered at M and reaches the endpoints of the diameter. Set the opening from M to O, or from M to P.

Why these lines are tangent

Take either intersection, say T1T_1T1​. It is on the auxiliary circle, whose diameter is OPOPOP. The semicircle theorem gives a right angle between T1OT_1OT1​O and T1PT_1PT1​P. The other circle supplies the next fact. Since T1T_1T1​ lies on the given circle, OT1OT_1OT1​ is a radius of that circle. A line perpendicular to the radius at its endpoint on the circle is tangent there. The same two facts prove tangency at T2T_2T2​.

Check your understanding

What does membership in the original circle establish for the point T2T_2T2​?

Answer choices

The original circle is centered at O, so OT₂ is a radius. Membership in the auxiliary circle gives the right angle at T₂.

Worked example 2 Test a proposed contact from exact lengths

Problem. A point T1T_1T1​ lies on a circle of radius 121212 mm centered at OOO. A point PPP is 373737 mm from OOO and 353535 mm from T1T_1T1​. Decide whether line PT1PT_1PT1​ is tangent to the circle at T1T_1T1​. Justify your decision.

Solution. The longest side in triangle OT1POT_1POT1​P is OPOPOP. Compare its square with the sum of the other squares.

OP2=372=1369.\begin{aligned} OP^2&=37^2\\ &=1369. \end{aligned}OP2​=372=1369.​OT12+PT12=122+352=1369.\begin{aligned} OT_1^2+PT_1^2&=12^2+35^2\\ &=1369. \end{aligned}OT12​+PT12​​=122+352=1369.​

By the converse of the Pythagorean theorem, the angle opposite OPOPOP is right. Therefore line PT1PT_1PT1​ is perpendicular to the contact radius at T1T_1T1​, so it is tangent there.

Why the construction finds every tangent

There are two crossings for any outside point. On the line OPOPOP, the nearest point of the given circle to PPP lies strictly between OOO and PPP, so it is inside the auxiliary circle. The opposite point of the given circle lies beyond OOO and is outside the auxiliary circle. Each semicircle of the given circle runs from the inside point to the outside point, so each crosses the auxiliary circle. There can be no third crossing. Three different points on the given circle would form a triangle with two different circumcenters, OOO and MMM, whereas the perpendicular bisectors determine just one.

Every tangent supplies an intersection#

Suppose a tangent through PPP touches the original circle at TTT. Then ∠OTP\angle OTP∠OTP is right because OTOTOT is the contact radius. Take the circle through the three vertices of triangle OTPOTPOTP. The converse of the semicircle theorem makes OPOPOP a diameter of that circle. Its center must therefore be the midpoint MMM, so it is the auxiliary circle and contains TTT.

Thus every tangent through PPP must use one of the two intersections as its contact point. Each contact point and PPP determine one line. The lines are distinct because a tangent cannot contain both contact points. These are all the tangents through PPP.

∎

Check your understanding

You start with a tangent through PPP and its contact point TTT. Which result places TTT on the circle with diameter OPOPOP?

Answer choices

The right angle is already known from tangency. The converse uses that angle to place its vertex on the circle with diameter OP.

Worked example 3 Recover a missing tangent by reflection

Problem. A completed construction for an outside point PPP shows only the tangent at T1T_1T1​, above the line OPOPOP in the drawing. Describe a reflection that recovers the other contact point and explain why the reflected line is a different tangent through PPP.

Solution. Reflect across line OPOPOP. The center OOO stays fixed, so the circle maps onto itself. The point PPP also stays fixed, so the reflected line still passes through it.

Reflection preserves intersections. The reflected line meets the circle only at the image T2T_2T2​ of T1T_1T1​, so it is tangent. Since T1T_1T1​ is above the mirror line, T2T_2T2​ is below it and the contact points differ. A single tangent has just one contact point, so these are different tangents.

Move the point to the circle or inside it

For a point on the circle, the tangent can be drawn directly. Construct the perpendicular to OPOPOP through PPP. The point of contact is PPP itself. Any line through PPP that is tangent would have to touch there, and there is just one perpendicular through that point.

For a point strictly inside, every line through it is a secant. You can follow the line from the point in either direction until it leaves the disk, crossing the circle once in each direction. This includes P=OP=OP=O, when every line through PPP cuts out a diameter.

Worked example 4 Count the tangents at three positions

Problem. A circle has center OOO and radius 111111 m. For each position of PPP, give the number of distinct tangent lines through PPP. In any case with just one line, describe how to construct it.

Case A. The distance OPOPOP is 131313 m.

Case B. The distance OPOPOP is 111111 m.

Case C. The distance OPOPOP is 666 m.

Solution. Case A. The distance exceeds the radius. The auxiliary circle gives two contact points, so there are two lines.

Case B. The distance equals the radius. Construct the perpendicular to OPOPOP at PPP. This is the one tangent.

Case C. The distance is smaller than the radius. A line through PPP crosses the circle in both directions from PPP, so there are no tangents.

Check your understanding

The point PPP is already on a circle with center OOO. How do you construct the tangent through PPP?

Answer choices

At a point on the circle, the line perpendicular to the contact radius at that point is tangent. Here the contact point is P.

Check the auxiliary circle before drawing the lines

The key check is whether both OOO and PPP lie at opposite ends of a diameter. A circle can cross the original circle twice without giving the right angles needed for tangency.

Worked example 5 Repair a compass setup

Problem. A circle of radius 555 cm has center OOO. The external point PPP is 121212 cm from OOO. A second circle is drawn with center PPP and compass opening POPOPO. Can an intersection XXX of these circles give a tangent line PXPXPX? Explain and give the center and compass opening that repair the construction.

Solution. The circle centered at PPP makes PXPXPX equal to POPOPO. If PXPXPX were tangent at XXX, triangle OXPOXPOXP would be right at XXX. Then POPOPO would be its hypotenuse and would be longer than the leg PXPXPX. So no intersection found by that setup gives a tangent.

Put the compass at the midpoint MMM of OPOPOP and open it to OOO. The opening is 666 cm. Now OOO and PPP are opposite ends of a diameter, so an intersection gives the needed right angle.

Check your understanding

A student already has the correct midpoint MMM but draws the auxiliary circle with radius OPOPOP. What is the effect?

Answer choices

Both endpoints are half of OP from M. The chosen radius is the whole of OP, so both endpoints are inside the circle.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Changing the compass width between midpoint arcs. Use the same opening from both endpoints so each arc crossing is equally far from OOO and PPP.
  • Drawing a line through the two intersections. Join each intersection to PPP. A line through both intersections cuts the original circle twice.
  • Looking for a second tangent from a boundary point. At a point on the circle the contact is already known. The perpendicular to its radius gives the single tangent.

Key takeaways

  • For an outside point PPP, the two crossings of the given circle and the circle with diameter OPOPOP are exactly the two tangent points.
  • The semicircle theorem verifies the constructed right angles. Its converse accounts for every possible contact.
  • At a point on the circle there is one tangent, perpendicular to the radius at that point. From an inside point there are none.

A bit of history (optional)

In Proposition 17 of Book III of the Elements, Euclid asks for a straight line that touches a circle and passes through a given point. He draws an extra circle with the same center as the given one, stretching it out to pass through the given point. He then draws a right angle where he can reach it and uses two triangles with two matching sides and a matching included angle. Their congruence carries that right angle to the point of contact.
