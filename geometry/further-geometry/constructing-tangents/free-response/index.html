  1. Problem 1 Finish the compass setup

    A circle of radius 999 cm has center OOO. The outside point PPP is 262626 cm from OOO.

    Two pairs of arcs drawn with one fixed compass opening from OOO and PPP have already met at UUU and VVV, as shown.

    Describe how to locate the auxiliary circle's center. State the compass opening for that circle and explain why the arc crossings determine its center.

    Text description of this figure

    Arcs with equal openings from O and P meet at U and V. Segment OP is drawn but its midpoint is unmarked.

    1. Hint 1

      Each arc crossing is a fixed compass distance from both endpoints.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the line that contains both arc crossings, then use its intersection with the segment.

    Answer

    Draw line UVUVUV and take its crossing MMM with OPOPOP as the center. The compass opening is 131313 cm.

    Full solution

    The fixed compass opening gives equal distances from UUU to OOO and PPP.

    It also gives equal distances from VVV to OOO and PPP.

    Both arc crossings lie on the perpendicular bisector of OPOPOP.

    Draw line UVUVUV to locate its crossing MMM with OPOPOP.

    Open the compass from MMM to OOO.

    The opening is half of 262626 cm, or 131313 cm.

    Answer

    Draw line UVUVUV and take its crossing MMM with OPOPOP as the center. The compass opening is 131313 cm.

    Key idea

    Two points equally far from the same endpoints locate the perpendicular bisector.

  2. Problem 2 Use the two circles in the drawing

    The diagram shows a given circle centered at OOO and the auxiliary circle with diameter OPOPOP. Point PPP is outside the given circle. Their crossings are T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​.

    Name the two lines to draw through PPP. Explain separately how the auxiliary circle and the given circle establish that the line through T2T_2T2​ is tangent.

    Text description of this figure

    The solid circle is centered at O. The dashed circle is centered at M and passes through O and P. Both T₁ and T₂ are on the circles.

    1. Hint 1

      The two ends of the auxiliary diameter are on the rays of the angle with vertex T2T_2T2​.

    2. Hint 2

      Once you have a right angle, identify which of its sides lies along a radius of the given circle.

    Answer

    Draw line PT1PT_1PT1​ and line PT2PT_2PT2​. The auxiliary circle gives a right angle at T2T_2T2​, where OT2OT_2OT2​ is a radius of the given circle.

    Full solution

    Draw one line through PPP and T1T_1T1​ and a second line through PPP and T2T_2T2​.

    For the auxiliary circle, OPOPOP is a diameter and T2T_2T2​ is on the circle.

    The semicircle theorem makes ∠OT2P\angle OT_2P∠OT2​P a right angle.

    For the given circle, OT2OT_2OT2​ is a radius because T2T_2T2​ is on that circle.

    Line PT2PT_2PT2​ is perpendicular to the radius at its endpoint, so it is tangent there.

    Answer

    Draw line PT1PT_1PT1​ and line PT2PT_2PT2​. The auxiliary circle gives a right angle at T2T_2T2​, where OT2OT_2OT2​ is a radius of the given circle.

    Key idea

  3. Problem 3 Find the compass opening and the tangent lengths

    The given circle has radius 171717 cm and center OOO. The outside point PPP is 333333 cm from OOO.

    Find the auxiliary compass opening and the exact lengths of both tangent segments from PPP to their contact points T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​.

    1. Hint 1

      The segment joining the given center and PPP serves as a diameter of one circle and a hypotenuse of two triangles.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the midpoint for the compass opening and the Pythagorean theorem for the tangent lengths.

    Answer

    MO=16.5 cmPT1=202 cmPT2=202 cm.\begin{gathered}MO=16.5\text{ cm}\\PT_1=20\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}\\PT_2=20\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}.\end{gathered}MO=16.5 cmPT1​=202​ cmPT2​=202​ cm.​

    Full solution

    The auxiliary radius is half the diameter OPOPOP, so the opening is 16.516.516.5 cm.

    Each constructed line is perpendicular to its contact radius.

    In either triangle the hypotenuse is OPOPOP, and the radius is a leg.

    For either contact TTT, Pythagoras gives

    PT2=332−172=800PT=202 cm.\begin{aligned}PT^2&=33^2-17^2\\&=800\\PT&=20\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}PT2PT​=332−172=800=202​ cm.​

    The positive root gives the length for each of the two contacts.

    Answer

    MO=16.5 cmPT1=202 cmPT2=202 cm.\begin{gathered}MO=16.5\text{ cm}\\PT_1=20\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}\\PT_2=20\sqrt{2}\text{ cm}.\end{gathered}MO=16.5 cmPT1​=202​ cmPT2​=202​ cm.​

    Key idea

    The tangent triangles have a common hypotenuse and equal contact radii.

  4. Problem 4 Write the equation of a boundary tangent

    Find an equation of the tangent line through PPP. Explain why no other tangent passes through PPP.

    1. Hint 1

      If a tangent passes through a point that is already on the circle, that point must be its contact.

    2. Hint 2

      Find the slope of OPOPOP and take its negative reciprocal. Use the coordinates of PPP in the line equation.

    Answer

    The unique tangent is y−2=−43(x−6)y-2=-\frac43(x-6)y−2=−34​(x−6), or any equivalent equation.

    Full solution

    A tangent through PPP has PPP as its contact point and must be perpendicular to OPOPOP there.

    The horizontal change from OOO to PPP is 444 and the vertical change is 333.

    The radius slope is 34\frac3443​.

    The perpendicular slope is −43-\frac43−34​.

    Using PPP gives

    y−2=−43(x−6).y-2=-\frac43(x-6).y−2=−34​(x−6).

    There is exactly one perpendicular to OPOPOP through PPP, so it gives the unique tangent.

  5. Problem 5 Compare positions on opposite rays

    A circle has center OOO and radius 232323 units. The point AAA lies on the circle.

    Case A. Start from AAA itself.

    Case B. Start from BBB, on the ray opposite OAOAOA, at a distance of 202020 units from OOO.

    Case C. Start from CCC, on ray OAOAOA and 444 units beyond AAA.

    Let nAn_AnA​, nBn_BnB​, and nCn_CnC​ be the tangent counts in Cases A, B, and C. Find each count and explain how you decided.

    Text description of this figure

    A is where the right ray from O meets the circle. C lies farther along that ray. B is on the left ray from O.

    1. Hint 1

      For each starting point, compare its distance from OOO with the radius.

    2. Hint 2

      Changing the direction of a ray changes its side of the center. It does not change how far along that ray the circle is.

    Answer

    nA=1nB=0nC=2.\begin{gathered}n_A=1\\n_B=0\\n_C=2.\end{gathered}nA​=1nB​=0nC​=2.​

    Full solution

    Case A.

    Since AAA lies on the circle, the single tangent is the perpendicular to OAOAOA at AAA.

    Case B.

    The distance OBOBOB is 202020 units, smaller than the radius.

    Every line through this interior point crosses the circle twice, so there is no tangent.

    Case C.

    The distance OCOCOC is 272727 units.

    The point is outside the circle, so the circle with diameter OCOCOC gives two contacts and two tangents.

    Answer

    nA=1nB=0nC=2.\begin{gathered}n_A=1\\n_B=0\\n_C=2.\end{gathered}nA​=1nB​=0nC​=2.​

    Key idea

    The center distance determines the tangent count on either ray.

  6. Problem 6 Recover the contacts from one surviving line

    A diagram shows a positive-radius circle centered at OOO, an outside point PPP, and one tangent line through PPP. Its point of contact has not been marked.

    Using a compass and straightedge, describe how to find that contact point and construct the other tangent. Explain why the other line must be different.

    Text description of this figure

    A solid circle is centered at O. One tangent line through P is drawn, but its contact point is not marked.

    1. Hint 1

      The original contact point is the foot of a perpendicular from OOO.

    2. Hint 2

      Use OPOPOP as a mirror line after locating the first contact point. To reflect a point, copy its perpendicular distance to the opposite side.

    Answer

    Drop a perpendicular from OOO to the given line to find T1T_1T1​. Reflect T1T_1T1​ across OPOPOP to obtain T2T_2T2​, then draw line PT2PT_2PT2​.

    Full solution

    Construct the perpendicular from OOO to the given line.

    The foot T1T_1T1​ is its point of contact because a tangent is perpendicular to its contact radius.

    The contact T1T_1T1​ lies off line OPOPOP.

    If it lay on OPOPOP, the given line through T1T_1T1​ and PPP would be OPOPOP, a secant through the center.

    Construct the perpendicular from T1T_1T1​ to line OPOPOP.

    From its foot on OPOPOP, copy the distance to T1T_1T1​ along the perpendicular on the opposite side to locate T2T_2T2​.

    Reflection fixes OOO and PPP.

    It carries the circle onto itself and carries the given tangent to the line through PPP and T2T_2T2​.

    That line has just one contact with the circle.

    The points T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​ differ because they lie on opposite sides of OPOPOP.

    A tangent has just one contact, so these are different lines.

    Answer

    Drop a perpendicular from OOO to the given line to find T1T_1T1​. Reflect T1T_1T1​ across OPOPOP to obtain T2T_2T2​, then draw line PT2PT_2PT2​.

    Key idea

    A perpendicular recovers the hidden contact before reflection gives the other one.

  7. Problem 7 Place an anchor for two equal cable runs

    A circular rim has center OOO and radius 111111 cm. An anchor PPP must be placed on a specified ray from OOO outside the rim.

    Two straight cables from the anchor are to touch the rim at distinct points, and each segment from the anchor to its contact point must be 606060 cm long.

    Find the required distance OPOPOP. Once the anchor is marked, explain how to locate both cable contact points.

    1. Hint 1

      At a point where a cable touches, its line is perpendicular to the radius.

    2. Hint 2

      The two given lengths are legs of the contact triangle. Once you know the hypotenuse, use it as a diameter.

    Answer

    Place the anchor 616161 cm from OOO along the specified ray. The two intersections of the rim and the circle with diameter OPOPOP are the cable contact points.

    Full solution

    A radius and a tangent cable meet at a right angle, so the distance OPOPOP is the hypotenuse.

    Use the two given leg lengths.

    OP2=112+602=3721OP=61 cm.\begin{aligned}OP^2&=11^2+60^2\\&=3721\\OP&=61\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}OP2OP​=112+602=3721=61 cm.​

    Mark PPP that far from OOO on the specified ray.

    This is outside the rim because 616161 cm exceeds 111111 cm.

    Bisect OPOPOP and use its midpoint as the center of a circle passing through OOO and PPP.

    Mark both intersections with the rim and join each to PPP.

    Each resulting cable segment has the same hypotenuse and contact radius in its right triangle, so each length is 606060 cm.

    Answer

    Place the anchor 616161 cm from OOO along the specified ray. The two intersections of the rim and the circle with diameter OPOPOP are the cable contact points.

    Key idea

    Use the contact triangle to place the anchor before constructing the lines.

  8. Problem 8 Find the bisector of the shared chord

    For a positive-radius circle centered at OOO and an outside point PPP, construct the auxiliary circle with center MMM and diameter OPOPOP.

    Its two crossings with the given circle are T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​. Let HHH be where line OPOPOP meets chord T1T2T_1T_2T1​T2​.

    Prove that HHH bisects the chord and that the chord is perpendicular to OPOPOP.

    1. Hint 1

      The endpoints of the chord lie on both circles. Compare their distances from each center.

    2. Hint 2

      Any two distinct points equally far from the endpoints of a segment determine its perpendicular bisector.

    Answer

    Line OPOPOP is the perpendicular bisector of T1T2T_1T_2T1​T2​. Its crossing HHH is the chord's midpoint.

    Full solution

    The distances from OOO to T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​ are equal because they are radii of the given circle.

    The distances from MMM to T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​ are equal because they are radii of the auxiliary circle.

    Thus both OOO and MMM lie on the perpendicular bisector of T1T2T_1T_2T1​T2​.

    The centers are distinct and lie on OPOPOP, so line OPOPOP is that bisector.

    Its intersection HHH with the chord therefore bisects it and makes a right angle there.

    Answer

    Line OPOPOP is the perpendicular bisector of T1T2T_1T_2T1​T2​. Its crossing HHH is the chord's midpoint.

    Key idea

    Both centers are equally far from the two shared endpoints.

  9. Problem 9 Locate the chord when the circles match in size

    A circle centered at OOO has radius 161616 mm. Point PPP is 323232 mm from OOO. The auxiliary circle with diameter OPOPOP has center MMM and crosses the given circle at T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​.

    Line T1T2T_1T_2T1​T2​ meets OPOPOP at HHH. Find OHOHOH and prove that your value follows from the two circles' sizes.

    1. Hint 1

      Compute the auxiliary radius and compare it with the original radius.

    2. Hint 2

      Consider which segment has endpoints OOO and MMM. Ask whether each contact point is equally far from those two endpoints.

    Answer

    OH=8OH=8OH=8 mm.

    Full solution

    The auxiliary radius is half of 323232 mm, which is 161616 mm.

    The two circles have equal radii.

    Each of T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​ is 161616 mm from both OOO and MMM.

    They therefore lie on the perpendicular bisector of OMOMOM.

    Line T1T2T_1T_2T1​T2​ is that perpendicular bisector.

    Its crossing HHH with OMOMOM is the midpoint of OMOMOM.

    Since OMOMOM is 161616 mm, OHOHOH is 888 mm.

    Answer

    OH=8OH=8OH=8 mm.

    Key idea

    Equal radii put both contacts on the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining the centers.

  10. Problem 10 Critique a circle that passes through both endpoints

    A circle of positive radius has center OOO, and PPP is outside it. The midpoint of OPOPOP is MMM.

    A student chooses a point NNN on the perpendicular bisector of OPOPOP, different from MMM, and draws a circle centered at NNN through OOO. The student says the construction works because this circle also passes through PPP.

    Suppose this new circle crosses the original circle at XXX. Can line PXPXPX be tangent at XXX? Explain why passing through both endpoints is insufficient and describe the correction.

    1. Hint 1

      The semicircle theorem uses a diameter. Where must a diameter's midpoint be?

    2. Hint 2

      If line PXPXPX were tangent, use the right angle at XXX to put XXX on the correct auxiliary circle.

    3. Hint 3

      Two distinct circles that already share OOO and PPP cannot share a third point. Check that XXX differs from both endpoints.

    Answer

    Line PXPXPX cannot be tangent at XXX. Use the circle centered at MMM with radius MOMOMO so OPOPOP is a diameter.

    Full solution

    The distances from NNN to OOO and PPP are equal, so the student's circle really does pass through both points.

    Its center is off OPOPOP, so OPOPOP is only a chord of that circle.

    If line PXPXPX were tangent at XXX, its angle with radius OXOXOX would be right.

    The semicircle converse would put XXX on the circle with diameter OPOPOP.

    The circle centered at NNN and the diameter circle centered at MMM are distinct.

    They already share OOO and PPP.

    Three common points would give a triangle with two different circumcenters, which is impossible.

    Since OOO is the original circle's center and PPP is outside it, neither can be XXX.

    Thus XXX cannot be on the correct diameter circle or be a point of tangency.

    Center the compass at MMM and open it to OOO.

    Now OPOPOP is a diameter, and the crossings give the required right angles.

    Answer

    Line PXPXPX cannot be tangent at XXX. Use the circle centered at MMM with radius MOMOMO so OPOPOP is a diameter.

    Key idea

    The center of the auxiliary circle must be the midpoint of the diameter.
