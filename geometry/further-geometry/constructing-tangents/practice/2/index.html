  2. 2

    The near point AAA of the given solid circle is inside the dashed circle. The opposite point BBB is outside. Which argument shows there are at least two crossings?

    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The points A and B are on opposite sides of the dashed circle's boundary, whose radius is MO.

    MA<MOMB>MO.\begin{gathered}MA<MO\\MB>MO.\end{gathered}MA<MOMB>MO.​

    Each arc of the solid circle from A to B must cross the dashed circle on its way from inside to outside. The two arcs have no points in common between A and B.

  6. 6

    Point XXX is on the auxiliary circle with diameter OPOPOP and differs from both endpoints. Its distance from OOO is less than the given circle's radius. What can you conclude about line PXPXPX at XXX?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Since X is on the auxiliary circle with diameter OP, the semicircle theorem gives

    PX⊥OX.PX\perp OX.PX⊥OX.

    The shorter distance from O puts X inside the given circle, so it cannot be a contact point.

    The original circle has radius 242424 mm. The outside point PPP gives an auxiliary radius of 161616 mm. The ray from OOO through PPP crosses the original circle at AAA. What is the distance APAPAP?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    The auxiliary radius is half the length of OP. Since A is between O and P, subtract the original radius from that diameter.

    AP=2(16)−24=8 mm.\begin{aligned}AP&=2(16)-24\\&=8\text{ mm}.\end{aligned}AP​=2(16)−24=8 mm.​

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    OM=MP.OM=MP.OM=MP.
  11. 11

    Suppose two distinct circles share three different points. Why would this contradict the uniqueness used in the tangent construction?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Three distinct points on a circle are not collinear. If the circle centers were O and M, both would have to lie at the unique intersection of the triangle's perpendicular bisectors.

    O=M.O=M.O=M.

    The distance from that common center to a shared point would also fix the same radius, contradicting distinct circles.
