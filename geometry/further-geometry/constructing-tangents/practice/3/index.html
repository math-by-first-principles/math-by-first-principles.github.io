  1. 1

    A line through PPP is tangent at TTT. A proof needs to show that TTT is on the circle with diameter OPOPOP. Which sequence proves the claim?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Tangency gives perpendicularity at T.

    m∠OTP=90∘.m\angle OTP=90^\circ.m∠OTP=90∘.

    The semicircle converse uses that right angle to place T on the circle whose diameter is the hypotenuse OP.

    The given circle has center O=(0,0)O=(0,0)O=(0,0) and radius 555. The outside point is P=(10,5)P=(10,5)P=(10,5). Which pair can be the two contact points?

    Here are the four candidates.

    A=(0,5)B=(4,−3)C=(3,4)D=(−5,0)\begin{gathered} A=(0,5)\\ B=(4,-3)\\ C=(3,4)\\ D=(-5,0) \end{gathered}A=(0,5)B=(4,−3)C=(3,4)D=(−5,0)​
    Answer choices for question 2

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Each candidate is on the given circle. Test which ones also lie on the auxiliary circle. The midpoint is (5,2.5)(5,2.5)(5,2.5), and the squared radius is 31.2531.2531.25.

    MA2=52+2.52=31.25.\begin{aligned} MA^2&=5^2+2.5^2\\ &=31.25. \end{aligned}MA2​=52+2.52=31.25.​ MB2=12+5.52=31.25.\begin{aligned} MB^2&=1^2+5.5^2\\ &=31.25. \end{aligned}MB2​=12+5.52=31.25.​

    A and B give two distinct common points, so they exhaust the possibilities.

    Point PPP lies on the given circle. A diameter circle on OPOPOP has been drawn anyway, as shown. Why can you not draw the tangent by joining PPP to the common point?

    Answer choices for question 4

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    T=P.T=P.T=P.

  5. 5

    The compass opening for finding the midpoint is exactly half of OPOPOP. The arcs meet once. Why does this fail as the perpendicular-bisector construction shown in the lesson?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The compass arcs meet once, at the midpoint M. This meeting point has the two equal distances

    OM=OP2MP=OP2.\begin{gathered}OM=\frac{OP}{2}\\MP=\frac{OP}{2}.\end{gathered}OM=2OP​MP=2OP​.​

    The usual arc method needs two distinct crossings to define the perpendicular bisector. Widen the compass and use the same wider opening at both ends.

  6. 6

    One contact point is above OPOPOP and the other below it. Which argument shows that the two tangent segments from PPP have equal lengths?

    Answer choices for question 6

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    Let rrr be the radius of the given circle. In either contact triangle, with contact T, the Pythagorean theorem gives

    PT2=OP2−r2.PT^2=OP^2-r^2.PT2=OP2−r2.

    The values of OP and rrr agree for both triangles, so the positive tangent lengths also agree.

  7. 7

    A circle centered at OOO has radius 101010 cm and OPOPOP is 252525 cm. In a correct construction, what is the exact length of either tangent segment?

    Answer choices for question 7

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    In the contact triangle, OP is the hypotenuse. For either contact T, the Pythagorean theorem gives

    PT2=252−102=525PT=521 cm.\begin{aligned} PT^2&=25^2-10^2\\ &=525\\ PT&=5\sqrt{21}\text{ cm}. \end{aligned}PT2PT​=252−102=525=521​ cm.​

    Take the positive root because PT is a length.

  8. 8

    For an outside point PPP, the compass is centered at the correct midpoint MMM but opened to OPOPOP. If this larger circle happens to cross the given circle, can either crossing be a contact point?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Tangency requires a right angle between OT and PT at any contact point T. The converse places such a point on the correct diameter circle, so

    MT=MO.MT=MO.MT=MO.

    The larger circle is centered at M and has radius OP, which exceeds MO. Its crossings cannot satisfy that distance.

  9. 9

    Point PPP is strictly inside the original circle and differs from OOO. Why does the circle with diameter OPOPOP have no common point with the original circle?

    Answer choices for question 9

    Solution

    Correct answer: D

    The greatest distance from O to any point X on the auxiliary circle is its diameter OP. If rrr is the radius of the given circle, then

    OX≤OPOP<r.\begin{gathered}OX\le OP\\OP<r.\end{gathered}OX≤OPOP<r.​

    Thus all points on the auxiliary circle are inside the given circle.

  10. 10

    The two tangents from PPP are drawn. A point QQQ is chosen on one tangent, with QQQ different from both PPP and its contact point. Can both of those same tangent lines also pass through QQQ?

    Answer choices for question 10

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Name the two distinct tangent lines ℓ1\ell_1ℓ1​ and ℓ2\ell_2ℓ2​. They meet only at P.

    ℓ1∩ℓ2={P}.\ell_1\cap\ell_2=\{P\}.ℓ1​∩ℓ2​={P}.

    The line on which Q was chosen remains tangent. If Q were also on the other line, the two lines would share a second point.

  11. 11

    The circles intersect at T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​. A solution argues that both lines through PPP are tangent because T1T_1T1​ and T2T_2T2​ lie on the given circle. What fact is missing?

    Answer choices for question 11

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Membership in the given circle makes each segment from O to a crossing a radius. The auxiliary circle supplies the missing right angles.

    m∠OT1P=90∘m∠OT2P=90∘.\begin{gathered}m\angle OT_1P=90^\circ\\m\angle OT_2P=90^\circ.\end{gathered}m∠OT1​P=90∘m∠OT2​P=90∘.​

    The perpendicularity at each contact radius proves tangency.

  12. 12

    A proposed tangent through an outside point PPP is claimed to have the direction of line OPOPOP. What actually happens to a line through PPP in that direction?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    Call the proposed line ℓ\ellℓ. There is only one line through P in the direction of OP.

    ℓ=OP↔.\ell=\overleftrightarrow{OP}.ℓ=OP.

    That line contains O, so it crosses at the two ends of a diameter and is a secant.
