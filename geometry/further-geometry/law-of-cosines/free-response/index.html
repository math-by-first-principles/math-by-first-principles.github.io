  1. Problem 1 A corner of a frame

    A rigid triangular frame XYZXYZXYZ has sides XZXZXZ, YZYZYZ, and XYXYXY of lengths 101010, 161616, and 212121 units, respectively.

    Write the expression for the cosine of the angle at ZZZ using the given lengths.

    Text description of this figure

    Frame XYZ has side lengths XZ = 10 units, YZ = 16 units, and XY = 21 units. The angle at Z is the target and is not labeled.

    1. Hint 1

      Locate the side opposite the angle at ZZZ.

    2. Hint 2

      Use the squares of the other two sides as the positive terms in the numerator.

    Answer

    cos⁡Z=102+162−2122(10)(16).\cos Z=\frac{10^2+16^2-21^2}{2(10)(16)}.cosZ=2(10)(16)102+162−212​.

    Full solution

    The two shorter lengths sum to 262626, so the three lengths can form a triangle.

    Side XYXYXY faces ZZZ.

    The sides meeting at ZZZ are XZXZXZ and YZYZYZ.

    Subtract the square of XYXYXY from the sum of the squares of the other sides, then divide by twice their product.

    cos⁡Z=102+162−2122(10)(16).\cos Z=\frac{10^2+16^2-21^2}{2(10)(16)}.cosZ=2(10)(16)102+162−212​.

    Answer

    cos⁡Z=102+162−2122(10)(16).\cos Z=\frac{10^2+16^2-21^2}{2(10)(16)}.cosZ=2(10)(16)102+162−212​.

    Key idea

    The sides that meet at the target angle give the denominator.

  2. Problem 2 Opening a pair of arms

    Two arms of lengths 999 cm and 141414 cm meet at an angle of 135∘135^\circ135∘. Find the exact distance between the free ends.

    1. Hint 1

      The unknown distance is across from the opening.

    2. Hint 2

      The cosine of 135∘135^\circ135∘ is the negative of the cosine of 45∘45^\circ45∘.

    Answer

    The free ends are 277+1262\sqrt{277+126\sqrt2}277+1262​​ cm apart.

    Full solution

    The angle between the arms is the included angle.

    Its cosine is −22-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}−22​​.

    Substitute the cosine with its sign.

    d2=92+142−2(9)(14)(−22)=277+1262.\begin{aligned}d^2&=9^2+14^2\\&\quad-2(9)(14)(-\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2})\\&=277+126\sqrt2.\end{aligned}d2​=92+142−2(9)(14)(−22​​)=277+1262​.​

    Taking the positive square root gives 277+1262\sqrt{277+126\sqrt2}277+1262​​ cm.

    Answer

    The free ends are 277+1262\sqrt{277+126\sqrt2}277+1262​​ cm apart.

    Key idea

    Keeping an exact cosine value gives an exact length.

  3. Problem 3 Classify a corner from side squares

    A triangular sign has side lengths 181818, 323232, and 383838 units.

    Classify the angle opposite the longest side as acute, right, or obtuse. Justify your decision without evaluating an inverse trigonometric function.

    1. Hint 1

      Use the sign of the cosine to classify the angle.

    2. Hint 2

      The denominator of the cosine quotient is positive. Which squares appear in its numerator?

    Answer

    The angle opposite the side of length 383838 is obtuse.

    Full solution

    The two shorter lengths add to 505050, so the sides close to make a nondegenerate triangle.

    The cosine numerator for the angle opposite 383838 is negative.

    182+322−382=−96.18^2+32^2-38^2=-96.182+322−382=−96.

    The denominator is positive.

    The cosine is negative, which puts this angle between a right angle and a straight angle.

    Answer

    The angle opposite the side of length 383838 is obtuse.

    Key idea

    The sign of the cosine classifies the angle without giving its measure.

  4. Problem 4 Recover a triangle from three lengths

    Triangle ABCABCABC has lengths 202020, 313131, and 424242 units for sides aaa, bbb, and ccc, respectively.

    Find all three angles to the nearest tenth of a degree and check their order against the side lengths.

    1. Hint 1

      Start with the angle opposite the longest side.

    2. Hint 2

      Find a second angle using its own adjacent sides. Use unrounded values when subtracting to find the last angle.

    Answer

    A≈26.8∘,B≈44.3∘,C≈109.0∘.\begin{aligned}A&\approx26.8^\circ,\\B&\approx44.3^\circ,\\C&\approx109.0^\circ.\end{aligned}ABC​≈26.8∘,≈44.3∘,≈109.0∘.​

    The order from smallest to largest is AAA, BBB, CCC, matching the order of the opposite lengths.

    Full solution

    The two shorter sides sum to 515151, which exceeds 424242.

    Angle CCC is across from the longest side.

    cos⁡C=202+312−4222(20)(31),C≈109.0∘.\begin{aligned}\cos C&=\frac{20^2+31^2-42^2}{2(20)(31)},\\C&\approx109.0^\circ.\end{aligned}cosCC​=2(20)(31)202+312−422​,≈109.0∘.​

    Use the two sides that meet at AAA to find that angle.

    cos⁡A=312+422−2022(31)(42),A≈26.8∘.\begin{aligned}\cos A&=\frac{31^2+42^2-20^2}{2(31)(42)},\\A&\approx26.8^\circ.\end{aligned}cosAA​=2(31)(42)312+422−202​,≈26.8∘.​

    Subtract the unrounded measures of AAA and CCC from the angle sum.

    B=180∘−A−C≈44.3∘.\begin{aligned}B&=180^\circ-A-C\\&\approx44.3^\circ.\end{aligned}B​=180∘−A−C≈44.3∘.​

    The order of the angles agrees with their opposite sides.

    Rounding each angle separately gives a displayed sum of 180.1∘180.1^\circ180.1∘.

    The unrounded measures sum to 180∘180^\circ180∘.

    Answer

    A≈26.8∘,B≈44.3∘,C≈109.0∘.\begin{aligned}A&\approx26.8^\circ,\\B&\approx44.3^\circ,\\C&\approx109.0^\circ.\end{aligned}ABC​≈26.8∘,≈44.3∘,≈109.0∘.​

    The order from smallest to largest is AAA, BBB, CCC, matching the order of the opposite lengths.

    Key idea

    Keep full precision for angle subtraction even when each displayed angle is rounded.

  5. Problem 5 Complete a layout

    In triangle ABCABCABC, sides aaa and bbb are 525252 cm and 616161 cm, respectively. Angle CCC is 74∘74^\circ74∘.

    Find ccc to the nearest tenth of a centimeter and find AAA and BBB to the nearest tenth of a degree.

    1. Hint 1

      The known angle is between the two given sides.

    2. Hint 2

      After finding ccc, keep it unrounded and use the three sides to find BBB. The angle sum then gives AAA.

    Answer

    c≈68.4 cm,A≈47.0∘,B≈59.0∘.\begin{aligned}c&\approx68.4\text{ cm},\\A&\approx47.0^\circ,\\B&\approx59.0^\circ.\end{aligned}cAB​≈68.4 cm,≈47.0∘,≈59.0∘.​

    Full solution

    The missing length is across from the included angle.

    c2=522+612−2(52)(61)cos⁡74∘,c≈68.4 cm.\begin{aligned}c^2&=52^2+61^2\\&\quad-2(52)(61)\cos74^\circ,\\c&\approx68.4\text{ cm}.\end{aligned}c2c​=522+612−2(52)(61)cos74∘,≈68.4 cm.​

    Keep the unrounded positive root in the expression for BBB.

    cos⁡B=522+c2−6122(52)c,B≈59.0∘.\begin{aligned}\cos B&=\frac{52^2+c^2-61^2}{2(52)c},\\B&\approx59.0^\circ.\end{aligned}cosBB​=2(52)c522+c2−612​,≈59.0∘.​

    Subtract the known angle and the unrounded value of BBB to get AAA.

    A=180∘−74∘−B≈47.0∘.\begin{aligned}A&=180^\circ-74^\circ-B\\&\approx47.0^\circ.\end{aligned}A​=180∘−74∘−B≈47.0∘.​

    The longest side is across from CCC, the largest angle.

    The shortest side is across from AAA, the smallest angle.

    Answer

    c≈68.4 cm,A≈47.0∘,B≈59.0∘.\begin{aligned}c&\approx68.4\text{ cm},\\A&\approx47.0^\circ,\\B&\approx59.0^\circ.\end{aligned}cAB​≈68.4 cm,≈47.0∘,≈59.0∘.​

    Key idea

    The new side turns SAS into three known lengths, enough to use the cosine equation again.

  6. Problem 6 Two directions from a survey station

    From survey station SSS, marker EEE is 636363 m away in a direction 52∘52^\circ52∘ east of north. Marker WWW is 878787 m away in a direction 67∘67^\circ67∘ west of north. All three points are on level ground.

    Find the straight distance between EEE and WWW to the nearest tenth of a meter.

    Text description of this figure

    Two lines leave station S, with a dashed north ray between them. The sight line to E measures 63 m at 52 degrees east of north. The line to W measures 87 m at 67 degrees west of north. Segment WE is the unknown separation.

    1. Hint 1

      The two sight lines lie on opposite sides of the north ray.

    2. Hint 2

      Add the two given angles to obtain the included angle at SSS.

    Answer

    The markers are approximately 129.8129.8129.8 m apart.

    Full solution

    The sight lines turn away from north on different sides.

    ∠ESW=52∘+67∘=119∘.\begin{aligned}\angle ESW&=52^\circ+67^\circ\\&=119^\circ.\end{aligned}∠ESW​=52∘+67∘=119∘.​

    The sought separation is opposite that angle.

    EW2=632+872−2(63)(87)cos⁡119∘,EW≈129.8 m.\begin{aligned}EW^2&=63^2+87^2\\&\quad-2(63)(87)\cos119^\circ,\\EW&\approx129.8\text{ m}.\end{aligned}EW2EW​=632+872−2(63)(87)cos119∘,≈129.8 m.​

    The separation is less than the sum of the two sight-line lengths, 150150150 m.

    Answer

    The markers are approximately 129.8129.8129.8 m apart.

    Key idea

    When two directions are measured from opposite sides of the same ray, add their angles to find the opening.

  7. Problem 7 Two field records

    Each field record describes a different triangle ABCABCABC. For each record, name the first triangle law that can find aaa, and explain why.

    Record A. Side bbb is 464646 m, angle AAA is 57∘57^\circ57∘, and angle BBB is 82∘82^\circ82∘. Also find aaa to the nearest tenth of a meter.

    Record B. Side bbb is 333333 m, side ccc is 474747 m, and angle AAA is 73∘73^\circ73∘.

    1. Hint 1

      Mark every known side and look directly across from it.

    2. Hint 2

      Record A gives a complete opposite pair. In Record B, notice the common endpoint of the two given sides.

    Answer

    Record A uses the Law of Sines because bbb and BBB are a known opposite pair. The length aaa is approximately 39.039.039.0 m. Record B uses the Law of Cosines because AAA is the angle included between the known sides.

    Full solution

    Record A.

    The known side bbb faces the known angle BBB, so the sine law pairs it with side aaa and angle AAA.

    asin⁡57∘=46sin⁡82∘,a≈39.0 m.\begin{aligned}\frac{a}{\sin57^\circ}&=\frac{46}{\sin82^\circ},\\a&\approx39.0\text{ m}.\end{aligned}sin57∘a​a​=sin82∘46​,≈39.0 m.​

    Record B.

    Sides bbb and ccc meet at AAA.

    Those two lengths and their included angle supply all three inputs to the cosine equation for a2a^2a2.

    Answer

    Record A uses the Law of Sines because bbb and BBB are a known opposite pair. The length aaa is approximately 39.039.039.0 m. Record B uses the Law of Cosines because AAA is the angle included between the known sides.

    Key idea

    An opposite pair starts the sine law, while two sides and the angle between them start the cosine law.

  8. Problem 8 Check a reported distance

    Two sides of lengths 555 m and 121212 m meet at 130∘130^\circ130∘. Riley reports that the opposite side is 9.69.69.6 m after subtracting the absolute value of the cosine correction.

    Explain how to rule out the reported value without a calculator, and find the opposite side to the nearest tenth of a meter.

    1. Hint 1

      A right opening with the same sides gives a comparison length.

    2. Hint 2

      What happens when you subtract the negative cosine correction with its sign intact?

    Answer

    The obtuse opening requires a length greater than 131313 m. The opposite side is approximately 15.715.715.7 m.

    Full solution

    At a right opening, the opposite length would be 131313 m.

    52+122=169.5^2+12^2=169.52+122=169.

    The obtuse angle has negative cosine.

    Subtracting the signed correction makes the opposite side square greater than 169169169, so the length must exceed 131313 m.

    Use the signed cosine and take the positive square root.

    d2=169−120cos⁡130∘,d≈15.7 m.\begin{aligned}d^2&=169-120\cos130^\circ,\\d&\approx15.7\text{ m}.\end{aligned}d2d​=169−120cos130∘,≈15.7 m.​

    Answer

    The obtuse opening requires a length greater than 131313 m. The opposite side is approximately 15.715.715.7 m.

    Key idea

    A right opening gives a benchmark for judging a result at an obtuse opening.

  9. Problem 9 Recover the foot from three sides

    Triangle ABCABCABC has sides BCBCBC, CACACA, and ABABAB of lengths 292929, 121212, and 252525 units, respectively.

    Put CCC at (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) and AAA on the positive horizontal axis. Place BBB above that axis.

    Find the signed horizontal coordinate of BBB. Is its perpendicular foot between CCC and AAA, beyond AAA, or to the left of CCC?

    1. Hint 1

      The signed coordinate xxx is aaa times the cosine of CCC.

    2. Hint 2

      You can also isolate xxx directly in the squared-distance formula before replacing it by the cosine expression.

    Answer

    The signed horizontal coordinate is 151515. The perpendicular foot lies beyond AAA.

    Full solution

    For the horizontal coordinate xxx of BBB, the cosine derivation gives

    c2=a2+b2−2bx.c^2=a^2+b^2-2bx.c2=a2+b2−2bx.

    Substitute the three given lengths and isolate xxx.

    252=292+122−24x,24x=360,x=15.\begin{aligned}25^2&=29^2+12^2-24x,\\24x&=360,\\x&=15.\end{aligned}25224xx​=292+122−24x,=360,=15.​

    Point AAA has horizontal coordinate 121212.

    The coordinate of the perpendicular foot is 151515, which puts it beyond AAA on the positive axis.

    Answer

    The signed horizontal coordinate is 151515. The perpendicular foot lies beyond AAA.

    Key idea

    A positive projection can extend past the endpoint of the base.

  10. Problem 10 Which measurement fixes the opening?

    Two rigid arms CACACA and CBCBCB have known positive lengths bbb and aaa. Their opening at CCC lies strictly between zero and a straight angle.

    Plan A measures the straight distance between the free ends AAA and BBB.

    Plan B drops a perpendicular from BBB to the line through CACACA. It records the distance ppp from CCC to the foot, where ppp is positive and smaller than aaa. It leaves out which side of CCC contains the foot.

    Decide which plan always determines the opening uniquely. Prove your decision and explain how two openings can fit the other plan.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the inputs for the cosine quotient with the information in each plan.

    2. Hint 2

      Place CACACA along the positive horizontal axis. The foot's unsigned distance from CCC allows two signed coordinates.

    3. Hint 3

      Check whether each of those coordinates can belong to a point BBB above the axis at distance aaa from CCC.

    Answer

    Plan A determines one opening. Plan B allows two openings, one acute and one obtuse.

    Full solution

    Plan A.

    The measured distance is ccc, opposite the opening.

    The three sides give a single cosine.

    cos⁡C=a2+b2−c22ab.\cos C=\frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}.cosC=2aba2+b2−c2​.

    Cosine takes each value strictly between −1-1−1 and 111 exactly once for an interior triangle angle, so the actual measured triangle has one possible opening.

    Plan B.

    With CCC at the origin and CACACA pointing to the right, the signed coordinate of the foot can be ppp or −p-p−p.

    Either choice can put BBB above the axis at distance aaa from CCC.

    Use the same positive height in both cases.

    y=a2−p2.y=\sqrt{a^2-p^2}.y=a2−p2​.

    The two possible cosine values come from dividing each signed projection by aaa.

    cos⁡C1=pa,cos⁡C2=−pa.\begin{aligned}\cos C_1&=\frac{p}{a},\\\cos C_2&=-\frac{p}{a}.\end{aligned}cosC1​cosC2​​=ap​,=−ap​.​

    Both ratios lie strictly between −1-1−1 and 111.

    One is positive and the other negative, so they produce distinct valid angles, one acute and one obtuse.

    Answer

    Plan A determines one opening. Plan B allows two openings, one acute and one obtuse.

    Key idea

    A horizontal projection determines the cosine only when its direction is known.
