Intuition

Put two rulers on a table and join them at one end. Keep one ruler still and turn the other. The area of the triangle formed by the hinge and the two free ends grows as the moving tip gets farther from the line of the fixed ruler. What happens to that distance once the hinge opens past a right angle?

Learning goals

  • Read sine and cosine from the coordinates of a point on the unit semicircle
  • Explain why supplementary angles give equal heights and opposite horizontal projections
  • Derive the triangle area formula for acute, right, and obtuse included angles
  • Find exact and approximate areas from two sides and their included angle

From a right triangle to a semicircle

In [Sine, Cosine, and Tangent](/geometry/right-triangles/right-triangle-trigonometry/), you found sine and cosine by dividing right-triangle leg lengths by the hypotenuse. For a hypotenuse of length 111, the ratios are just the leg lengths. Coordinates let us use the same idea when the angle opens past 90∘90^\circ90∘.

Draw the upper half of the circle with radius 111 centered at the origin. Start on the positive xxx-axis and turn counterclockwise through θ\thetaθ degrees, from 0∘0^\circ0∘ to 180∘180^\circ180∘. Let P=(x,y)P=(x,y)P=(x,y) be the point where the ray meets the unit semicircle.

cos⁡θ=x,sin⁡θ=y.\begin{aligned} \cos\theta&=x,\\ \sin\theta&=y. \end{aligned}cosθsinθ​=x,=y.​

For an acute angle, dropping a perpendicular from PPP to the horizontal axis gives a right triangle. Its horizontal leg is adjacent to the angle, its vertical leg is opposite, and its hypotenuse has length 111. Thus the coordinate definitions agree with the ratios you already know.

The xxx-coordinate is the signed horizontal projection of the radius. A point to the right of the vertical axis gives a positive projection. A point to the left gives a negative one. Its distance from the vertical axis is ∣x∣|x|∣x∣. The height above the horizontal axis is yyy, which stays nonnegative on the upper semicircle.

At the three axis points, read the coordinates directly.

What a reflection preserves

Two angles are supplementary when their measures add to 180∘180^\circ180∘. A ray at 30∘30^\circ30∘ makes a 30∘30^\circ30∘ angle with the positive axis. Reflect it in the vertical axis and the new ray makes a 30∘30^\circ30∘ angle with the negative axis. Its turn from the positive axis is now 150∘150^\circ150∘. The two endpoints on the semicircle have the same height, and their horizontal coordinates have opposite signs.

Sine and cosine of a supplementary angle#

The same reflection sends the radius at any angle θ\thetaθ to the radius at 180∘−θ180^\circ-\theta180∘−θ. The two turns fit a half-turn because the reflected ray makes the same angle with the negative axis that the original ray makes with the positive axis. Reflection preserves yyy and replaces xxx by −x-x−x. Reading the coordinates gives

sin⁡(180∘−θ)=sin⁡θ,cos⁡(180∘−θ)=−cos⁡θ.\begin{aligned} \sin(180^\circ-\theta)&=\sin\theta,\\ \cos(180^\circ-\theta)&=-\cos\theta. \end{aligned}sin(180∘−θ)cos(180∘−θ)​=sinθ,=−cosθ.​

This includes the two ends of the semicircle and the top, where the horizontal coordinate is zero.

Worked example 1 Read an obtuse angle from a familiar acute angle

Problem. Find the exact sine and cosine of 120∘120^\circ120∘ using the exact values at 60∘60^\circ60∘.

Solution. The supplement of 120∘120^\circ120∘ is 60∘60^\circ60∘. Keep the sine and reverse the sign of the cosine.

sin⁡120∘=sin⁡60∘,sin⁡120∘=32.\begin{aligned} \sin120^\circ&=\sin60^\circ,\\ \sin120^\circ&=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}. \end{aligned}sin120∘sin120∘​=sin60∘,=23​​.​cos⁡120∘=−cos⁡60∘,cos⁡120∘=−12.\begin{aligned} \cos120^\circ&=-\cos60^\circ,\\ \cos120^\circ&=-\frac12. \end{aligned}cos120∘cos120∘​=−cos60∘,=−21​.​

Scale the projections

Place one end of a segment of length rrr at the origin. Let it make an angle θ\thetaθ above the positive horizontal axis. Its endpoint lies rrr times as far along the ray as the unit point, so a dilation about the origin multiplies both coordinates by rrr.

(x,y)=(rcos⁡θ, rsin⁡θ).(x,y)=(r\cos\theta,\ r\sin\theta).(x,y)=(rcosθ, rsinθ).

The projection rcos⁡θr\cos\thetarcosθ records left or right. The height rsin⁡θr\sin\thetarsinθ records the perpendicular distance to the horizontal line.

Worked example 2 Locate the tip of a tilted sign

Problem. A sign arm is 130130130 cm long. Its hinge is at the origin, and the arm points up and left. The unit point on its ray has coordinates (−1665,6365)\left(-\tfrac{16}{65},\tfrac{63}{65}\right)(−6516​,6563​). Find the signed horizontal projection and the height of the tip above the horizontal line through the hinge.

Solution. Multiply the coordinates of the unit point by 130130130 to reach the tip.

x=130(−1665),x=−32.\begin{aligned} x&=130\left(-\frac{16}{65}\right),\\ x&=-32. \end{aligned}xx​=130(−6516​),=−32.​y=130(6365),y=126.\begin{aligned} y&=130\left(\frac{63}{65}\right),\\ y&=126. \end{aligned}yy​=130(6563​),=126.​

Turn the height into area

The [base-height formula](/geometry/perimeter-and-area/area-of-triangles/) uses the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to the line containing the base. For a base of 202020 cm and a 171717 cm side hinged at its left endpoint, try turns of 30∘30^\circ30∘, 90∘90^\circ90∘, and 150∘150^\circ150∘.

At 30∘30^\circ30∘ the perpendicular height is half the slanted side, or 8.58.58.5 cm.

At 90∘90^\circ90∘ the height is the full 171717 cm.

At 150∘150^\circ150∘ the tip has reflected across the vertical through the hinge, so the height returns to 8.58.58.5 cm. The equal heights at 30∘30^\circ30∘ and 150∘150^\circ150∘ give equal areas with the same base.

For triangle ABCABCABC, let AAA, BBB, and CCC also denote the angle measures. The opposite sides have lengths aaa, bbb, and ccc, respectively. Place AAA at the left end of the horizontal base ABABAB of length ccc. Put CCC above the line ABABAB, with ACACAC of length bbb. A rigid motion, and a reflection if needed, puts any triangle in this position. Angle AAA is the included angle because the two given sides meet there.

Triangle area from two sides and the angle between them#

Let DDD be the foot of the perpendicular from CCC to the line ABABAB, and write hhh for the length of CDCDCD. The location of DDD changes with the angle. In each case we find the same expression for hhh.

Acute angle at AAA. The projection is positive, so DDD lies on the ray from AAA through BBB. It lies inside the base in the picture. If the projection reaches or goes beyond BBB, the same right triangle ACDACDACD still gives the height. Its hypotenuse is bbb, and the leg opposite AAA is hhh.

sin⁡A=hb,h=bsin⁡A.\begin{aligned} \sin A&=\frac{h}{b},\\ h&=b\sin A. \end{aligned}sinAh​=bh​,=bsinA.​

Right angle at AAA. The perpendicular foot is AAA itself. Side ACACAC gives the full height. The sine at 90∘90^\circ90∘ is 111, so h=bsin⁡Ah=b\sin Ah=bsinA in this case too.

Obtuse angle at AAA. The projection is negative, so DDD lies on the extension of the base to the left of AAA. The acute angle ∠DAC\angle DAC∠DAC, marked α\alphaα, in the right triangle ACDACDACD measures 180∘−A180^\circ-A180∘−A. Its opposite leg is the height.

h=bsin⁡(180∘−A),h=bsin⁡A.\begin{aligned} h&=b\sin(180^\circ-A),\\ h&=b\sin A. \end{aligned}hh​=bsin(180∘−A),=bsinA.​

Multiply the base ccc by the height and take half. The area KKK is

K=12bcsin⁡A.K=\frac12bc\sin A.K=21​bcsinA.

The same argument works from either of the other vertices. Use the two sides that meet at the angle you choose.

K=12acsin⁡B,K=12absin⁡C.\begin{aligned} K&=\frac12ac\sin B,\\ K&=\frac12ab\sin C. \end{aligned}KK​=21​acsinB,=21​absinC.​

Worked example 3 Find an exact area from the shared vertex

Problem. In triangle ABCABCABC, ABABAB measures 181818 cm, BCBCBC measures 131313 cm, and angle BBB measures 45∘45^\circ45∘. Find the exact area.

Solution. Both given sides end at BBB, so use sin⁡B\sin BsinB. The exact value of sin⁡45∘\sin45^\circsin45∘ is 22\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}22​​.

K=12(18)(13)sin⁡45∘,K=12(18)(13)22,K=11722 cm2.\begin{aligned} K&=\frac12(18)(13)\sin45^\circ,\\ K&=\frac12(18)(13)\frac{\sqrt2}{2},\\ K&=\frac{117\sqrt2}{2}\text{ cm}^2. \end{aligned}KKK​=21​(18)(13)sin45∘,=21​(18)(13)22​​,=21172​​ cm2.​

Worked example 4 Estimate the area marked by two sight lines

Problem. From one corner of a triangular wetland plot, a surveyor measures straight horizontal distances of 23.623.623.6 m and 15.415.415.4 m to the other two corners. The angle between these sight lines is 127∘127^\circ127∘. Find the horizontal area of the plot to the nearest tenth of a square meter.

Solution. The distances are sides of the triangle, and the angle is measured where they meet. Enter the sine in degree mode and round only the final area.

K=12(23.6)(15.4)sin⁡127∘,K≈145.1 m2.\begin{aligned} K&=\frac12(23.6)(15.4)\sin127^\circ,\\ K&\approx 145.1\text{ m}^2. \end{aligned}KK​=21​(23.6)(15.4)sin127∘,≈145.1 m2.​

Check your understanding

In triangle ABCABCABC, you know the lengths aaa and ccc and the measure of angle BBB. Which expression gives the area?

Answer choices

Side aaa is BCBCBC and side ccc is ABABAB. They meet at BBB. Use one half of their product times the sine of that angle.

Worked example 5 Compare two hinge settings without calculating sine

Problem. In two triangles, sides of lengths 121212 units and 191919 units meet at a hinge. One included angle is 28∘28^\circ28∘ and the other is 152∘152^\circ152∘. Keep the 191919-unit side horizontal and directed right from the hinge in each triangle. Compare their areas and the signed horizontal projections of their 121212-unit sides.

Solution. The angles are supplementary. Their sines agree, so the areas agree because the two side lengths also agree.

sin⁡152∘=sin⁡28∘.\sin152^\circ=\sin28^\circ.sin152∘=sin28∘.

Their cosines have opposite signs. Multiplying by 121212 preserves that relationship.

12cos⁡152∘=−12cos⁡28∘.12\cos152^\circ=-12\cos28^\circ.12cos152∘=−12cos28∘.

The projection at 28∘28^\circ28∘ points right, and the projection at 152∘152^\circ152∘ points left by the same distance.

For fixed side lengths, a particular positive area fixes the height of the moving tip. On the unit semicircle, a horizontal line below the top meets the arc in two reflected points. At the top it meets the arc in one point. That top point gives the greatest area.

Check your understanding

Two fixed positive side lengths form a hinge. At which included angle is the triangle's area greatest?

Answer choices

The area grows with the height for a fixed base. The greatest height on the unit semicircle is 111, at the top, where the turn is 90∘90^\circ90∘.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Using the angle opposite one given side. Trace the two given sides to their shared vertex. The sine belongs to the angle there.
  • Giving an obtuse angle a negative height. The negative coordinate is the horizontal projection. The height is positive for any turn strictly between 0∘0^\circ0∘ and 180∘180^\circ180∘.
  • Stopping an altitude at the base segment. Extend the base line when the perpendicular foot falls outside the segment.
  • Leaving a calculator in radian mode. Choose degree mode for the angles in this lesson, and round at the end.

Key takeaways

  • Cosine gives the horizontal coordinate on the unit semicircle. Sine gives the vertical coordinate.
  • Supplementary angles give the same sine and opposite cosines.
  • With two sides and their included angle, take half the product of the side lengths and multiply by the sine of that angle.

A bit of history (optional)

Long before calculators, astronomers kept tables that paired angles with lengths in a circle. Ptolemy used full chords, the straight segments joining two points on the circle. Around 500 CE, the Indian mathematician and astronomer Aryabhata tabulated half-chords instead. When a chord is cut in half by a perpendicular from the center, its half is a leg of a right triangle. Aryabhata’s choice put that leg directly in the table. MacTutor History of Mathematics
