  1. Problem 1 The longest side and the semiperimeter

    For each proposed set of side lengths, find the semiperimeter minus the longest length. Use that difference to explain whether the lengths give a nondegenerate triangle, a flat arrangement, or a gap. All lengths are in centimeters.

    Case A. The lengths are 14, 23, and 31.

    Case B. The lengths are 15, 25, and 40.

    Case C. The lengths are 17, 24, and 44.

    1. Hint 1

      The longest length gives the smallest of the three differences from the semiperimeter.

    2. Hint 2

      For positive lengths, twice the difference for the longest length is the sum of the two shorter lengths minus the longest.

    Answer

    Case A. Triangle, 3 cmCase B. Flat, 0 cmCase C. Gap, −1.5 cm\begin{gathered}\text{Case A. Triangle, }3\text{ cm}\\ \text{Case B. Flat, }0\text{ cm}\\ \text{Case C. Gap, }-1.5\text{ cm}\end{gathered}Case A. Triangle, 3 cmCase B. Flat, 0 cmCase C. Gap, −1.5 cm​

    Full solution

    Case A.

    The semiperimeter is 34 cm.

    34−31=3 cm.34-31=3\text{ cm}.34−31=3 cm.

    The difference is positive, so the two shorter lengths total more than the longest.

    The triangle is nondegenerate.

    Case B.

    The semiperimeter is 40 cm.

    40−40=0 cm.40-40=0\text{ cm}.40−40=0 cm.

    The two shorter lengths exactly reach across the longest.

    The arrangement is flat.

    Case C.

    The semiperimeter is 42.5 cm.

    42.5−44=−1.5 cm.42.5-44=-1.5\text{ cm}.42.5−44=−1.5 cm.

    The two shorter lengths together fall short of the longest, so a gap remains.

    Answer

    Case A. Triangle, 3 cmCase B. Flat, 0 cmCase C. Gap, −1.5 cm\begin{gathered}\text{Case A. Triangle, }3\text{ cm}\\ \text{Case B. Flat, }0\text{ cm}\\ \text{Case C. Gap, }-1.5\text{ cm}\end{gathered}Case A. Triangle, 3 cmCase B. Flat, 0 cmCase C. Gap, −1.5 cm​

    Key idea

    With positive proposed lengths, the sign of the smallest difference tells you whether they can enclose a region.

  2. Problem 2 A radical from decimal lengths

    A triangular template has sides 6.5 cm, 8.5 cm, and 10 cm. Use Heron's formula to find its exact area in simplified radical form.

    1. Hint 1

      Check the shorter two lengths against the longest. Then find half the perimeter.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract each side from the semiperimeter. After multiplying, look for a perfect square in the product.

    Answer

    530 cm25\sqrt{30}\text{ cm}^2530​ cm2.

    Full solution

    The positive shorter lengths add to 15 cm, which is greater than 10 cm.

    The semiperimeter is 12.5 cm.

    Subtracting each side in order gives 6 cm, 4 cm, and 2.5 cm.

    K2=12.5⋅6⋅4⋅2.5,K2=750.\begin{aligned} K^2&=12.5\cdot6\cdot4\cdot2.5,\\ K^2&=750. \end{aligned}K2K2​=12.5⋅6⋅4⋅2.5,=750.​

    Write 750 as 25 times 30 and take the positive root.

    K=530 cm2.K=5\sqrt{30}\text{ cm}^2.K=530​ cm2.

    Answer

    530 cm25\sqrt{30}\text{ cm}^2530​ cm2.

    Key idea

    Exact decimal side lengths can give an exact radical area.

  3. Problem 3 Round the area of a cut tile

    1. Hint 1

    The third digit after the decimal point is 3.

    K≈198.41 cm2.K\approx198.41\text{ cm}^2.K≈198.41 cm2.

    Answer

    Approximately 198.41 cm2198.41\text{ cm}^2198.41 cm2.

    Key idea

    Round the evaluated square root to the requested place.

  5. Problem 5 Height across a triangular fabric panel

    A triangular fabric panel has sides 13 m, 20 m, and 25 m. Find the exact perpendicular height from the opposite vertex to the 25 m side.

    Text description of this figure

    The triangle has sides of 13, 20, and 25 meters. The 25-meter side is horizontal. From the vertex opposite that side, a dashed perpendicular segment labeled h meets the base at a right angle.

    1. Hint 1

      Heron's formula gives the area from the three sides without a measured height.

    2. Hint 2

      Once you have the area, double it and divide by the chosen base.

    Answer

    482925 m\frac{48\sqrt{29}}{25}\text{ m}254829​​ m.

    Full solution

    All three lengths are positive.

    The two shorter sides total 33 m, more than the 25 m base.

    The semiperimeter is 29 m.

    The differences from 29 m are 16 m, 9 m, and 4 m.

    K2=29⋅16⋅9⋅4,K2=29⋅242.\begin{aligned} K^2&=29\cdot16\cdot9\cdot4,\\ K^2&=29\cdot24^2. \end{aligned}K2K2​=29⋅16⋅9⋅4,=29⋅242.​
    K=2429 m2.K=24\sqrt{29}\text{ m}^2.K=2429​ m2.

    Half of 25 times the height equals the area in square meters.

    Solve for the height in meters.

    12⋅25h=2429,h=482925.\begin{aligned} \tfrac12\cdot25h&=24\sqrt{29},\\ h&=\frac{48\sqrt{29}}{25}. \end{aligned}21​⋅25hh​=2429​,=254829​​.​

    Answer

    482925 m\frac{48\sqrt{29}}{25}\text{ m}254829​​ m.

    Key idea

    Use the base named in the question when recovering a height from area.

  6. Problem 6 Do the two panels need equal paint?

    A maker will paint one face of each triangular panel with a uniform coat. Use an exact area comparison to decide whether the two faces need the same amount of paint.

    Panel A. The sides measure 19 cm, 22 cm, and 25 cm.

    Panel B. The sides measure 11 cm, 37 cm, and 40 cm.

    Text description of this figure

    At a common scale, panel A has a solid outline, 25-centimeter horizontal side, 19-centimeter left side and 22-centimeter right side. Panel B has a dashed outline, 40-centimeter horizontal side, 11-centimeter left side and 37-centimeter right side.

    1. Hint 1

      Compare the two products under the square roots to compare the exact areas.

    2. Hint 2

      Compute a separate semiperimeter and three differences for each panel.

    Answer

    Yes. Both faces have the same area and need the same amount of paint for the same coverage.

    Full solution

    Panel A.

    All lengths are positive, and 41 cm exceeds 25 cm.

    The semiperimeter is 33 cm.

    Its differences from the sides are 14 cm, 11 cm, and 8 cm.

    KA2=33⋅14⋅11⋅8,KA2=40656.\begin{aligned} K_A^2&=33\cdot14\cdot11\cdot8,\\ K_A^2&=40656. \end{aligned}KA2​KA2​​=33⋅14⋅11⋅8,=40656.​

    Panel B.

    All lengths are positive, and 48 cm exceeds 40 cm.

    The semiperimeter is 44 cm.

    Its differences from the sides are 33 cm, 7 cm, and 4 cm.

    KB2=44⋅33⋅7⋅4,KB2=40656.\begin{aligned} K_B^2&=44\cdot33\cdot7\cdot4,\\ K_B^2&=40656. \end{aligned}KB2​KB2​​=44⋅33⋅7⋅4,=40656.​

    The squared areas are equal.

    The areas are positive, so they are equal as well.

    Answer

    Yes. Both faces have the same area and need the same amount of paint for the same coverage.

    Key idea

    Triangles can have different perimeters and the same exact area.

  7. Problem 7 Match the factors in two calculations

  9. Problem 9 Rebuild the sides from the three differences

    For a nondegenerate triangle, the three differences s−as-as−a, s−bs-bs−b, and s−cs-cs−c are 5 cm, 8 cm, and 16 cm, respectively. Find the sides and the exact area.

    1. Hint 1

      Add the three differences in symbols and use the fact that twice the semiperimeter equals the perimeter.

    2. Hint 2

      Subtract each known difference from the semiperimeter to recover its side. Then check the recovered lengths before applying Heron's formula.

    Answer

    The sides are 24 cm, 21 cm, and 13 cm, respectively. The area is 8290 cm28\sqrt{290}\text{ cm}^28290​ cm2.

    Full solution

    Group the three copies of sss and the three side lengths.

    (s−a)+(s−b)+(s−c)=3s−(a+b+c).\begin{aligned} &(s-a)+(s-b)+(s-c)\\ &\qquad=3s-(a+b+c). \end{aligned}​(s−a)+(s−b)+(s−c)=3s−(a+b+c).​

    The parenthesized sum is the perimeter, or 2s2s2s.

    Subtracting it from 3s3s3s leaves sss.

    Their numerical sum is 29 cm.

    Recover the sides by subtracting.

    a=29−5,a=24.\begin{aligned} a&=29-5,\\ a&=24. \end{aligned}aa​=29−5,=24.​
    b=29−8,b=21.\begin{aligned} b&=29-8,\\ b&=21. \end{aligned}bb​=29−8,=21.​
    c=29−16,c=13.\begin{aligned} c&=29-16,\\ c&=13. \end{aligned}cc​=29−16,=13.​

    All three are positive, and the shorter sides total 34 cm, more than 24 cm.

    The squared area uses the three supplied differences.

  10. Problem 10 Why the sign of the product detects closure

    Let aaa, bbb, and ccc be positive proposed side lengths, with ccc a largest length. Let sss be half their sum, and call the product under the square root in Heron's formula PPP.

    P=s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c).P=s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c).P=s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c).

    Case A. Prove that PPP is positive exactly when the lengths form a nondegenerate triangle.

    Case B. Prove that PPP is zero exactly when the lengths give a flat arrangement.

    Case C. Prove that PPP is negative exactly when the shorter lengths fall short of the longest.

    1. Hint 1

      First decide which factors are positive regardless of whether a triangle exists.

    2. Hint 2

      In twice the factor s−as-as−a, group ccc with the subtracted aaa. Use the fact that ccc is at least aaa. Do the corresponding check for s−bs-bs−b.

    3. Hint 3

      The sign of a+b−ca+b-ca+b−c decides whether the three lengths close with room to bend, meet flat, or fall short.

    Answer

    Case A. P>0 exactly if a+b>c.Case B. P=0 exactly if a+b=c.Case C. P<0 exactly if a+b<c.\begin{gathered}\text{Case A. }P>0\text{ exactly if }a+b>c.\\ \text{Case B. }P=0\text{ exactly if }a+b=c.\\ \text{Case C. }P<0\text{ exactly if }a+b<c.\end{gathered}Case A. P>0 exactly if a+b>c.Case B. P=0 exactly if a+b=c.Case C. P<0 exactly if a+b<c.​

    Full solution

    The semiperimeter is positive because all three proposed side lengths are positive.

    2(s−a)=b+(c−a).2(s-a)=b+(c-a).2(s−a)=b+(c−a).

    Since bbb is positive and ccc is at least aaa, the right side is positive.

    Thus s−as-as−a is positive.

    2(s−b)=a+(c−b).2(s-b)=a+(c-b).2(s−b)=a+(c−b).

    Since aaa is positive and ccc is at least bbb, this right side is positive as well.

    Thus s−bs-bs−b is positive.

    Multiplying s−cs-cs−c by the other three factors preserves its sign.

    2(s−c)=a+b−c.2(s-c)=a+b-c.2(s−c)=a+b−c.

    Case A.

    The product is positive exactly when a+b>ca+b>ca+b>c.

    With three positive lengths and ccc a largest one, this is the strict triangle condition.

    Case B.

    The product is zero exactly when a+b=ca+b=ca+b=c.

    The two shorter lengths must stretch straight to meet.

    Case C.

    The product is negative exactly when a+b<ca+b<ca+b<c.

    The two shorter lengths cannot reach across the longest, even in a straight line.

    Answer

    Case A. P>0 exactly if a+b>c.Case B. P=0 exactly if a+b=c.Case C. P<0 exactly if a+b<c.\begin{gathered}\text{Case A. }P>0\text{ exactly if }a+b>c.\\ \text{Case B. }P=0\text{ exactly if }a+b=c.\\ \text{Case C. }P<0\text{ exactly if }a+b<c.\end{gathered}Case A. P>0 exactly if a+b>c.Case B. P=0 exactly if a+b=c.Case C. P<0 exactly if a+b<c.​

    Key idea

    For positive proposed lengths, only the difference for the longest side can be zero or negative.
