    A triangular skylight has sides 13 dm, 18 dm, and 25 dm. What is its area to the nearest tenth of a square decimeter?

    Answer choices for question 1

    Solution

    Correct answer: B

    The sides are positive and the shorter pair totals 31 dm, more than 25 dm. The semiperimeter is 28 dm. The differences are 15 dm, 10 dm, and 3 dm.

    K=28⋅15⋅10⋅3 dm2,K=12600 dm2,K≈112.2 dm2.\begin{aligned} K&=\sqrt{28\cdot15\cdot10\cdot3}\text{ dm}^2,\\ K&=\sqrt{12600}\text{ dm}^2,\\ K&\approx112.2\text{ dm}^2. \end{aligned}KKK​=28⋅15⋅10⋅3​ dm2,=12600​ dm2,≈112.2 dm2.​

    A triangle has semiperimeter 27 units. Two of its sides measure 16 and 20 units. Which is the remaining side length?

    Answer choices for question 3

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The full perimeter is 54 units, so subtract the two known lengths.

    54−16−20=18.54-16-20=18.54−16−20=18.

    The lengths are positive and 16 plus 18 exceeds 20.

    Two valid triangular panels each have perimeter 56 cm.

    Panel A has sides 18 cm, 18 cm, and 20 cm.

    Panel B has sides 15 cm, 19 cm, and 22 cm.

    Which comparison of their areas is correct?

    Answer choices for question 5

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    The positive shorter sides total 36 cm in A and 34 cm in B, exceeding the longest side in each case. Both semiperimeters are 28 cm. In square centimeters, compare the squares of the areas.

    KA2=28⋅10⋅10⋅8,KA2=22400.\begin{aligned} K_A^2&=28\cdot10\cdot10\cdot8,\\ K_A^2&=22400. \end{aligned}KA2​KA2​​=28⋅10⋅10⋅8,=22400.​ KB2=28⋅13⋅9⋅6,KB2=19656.\begin{aligned} K_B^2&=28\cdot13\cdot9\cdot6,\\ K_B^2&=19656. \end{aligned}KB2​KB2​​=28⋅13⋅9⋅6,=19656.​

    The areas are positive, so A has the greater area.

    A valid triangle has side lengths 7, 9, and 14 units. Angle CCC is opposite the 14-unit side.

    The Law of Cosines gives 2abcos⁡C=−662ab\cos C=-662abcosC=−66 when lengths are expressed in these units. Which expression equals 16K216K^216K2?

    Answer choices for question 8

    Solution

    Correct answer: A

    Two students name the same three sides of a valid triangle. The second student interchanges aaa and ccc. How does this change the area from Heron's formula?

    Answer choices for question 12

    Solution

    Correct answer: C

    Interchanging the two labels leaves sss unchanged. The factors s−as-as−a and s−cs-cs−c trade places without changing the squared area.

    K2=s(s−c)(s−b)(s−a).K^2=s(s-c)(s-b)(s-a).K2=s(s−c)(s−b)(s−a).

    The positive square root therefore gives the same area.
