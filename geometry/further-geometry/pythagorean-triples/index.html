Intuition

Stretch every side of a 3,4,53,4,53,4,5 triangle by the same whole number and you get another right triangle with whole number sides. What if you want a different shape? The sum and difference of two squares can give you one. They can also tell you how a given triple was made.

Learning goals

  • Reduce a Pythagorean triple to its primitive triple and identify the scale factor
  • Derive and use the formulas that generate Pythagorean triples
  • Explain how parity and coprimality determine whether the formulas give a primitive triple
  • Recover the two parameters of a primitive triple and justify why the recovery works

Reduce a triple to its smallest integer copy

A Pythagorean triple consists of positive integers aaa, bbb, and ccc with

a2+b2=c2.a^2+b^2=c^2.a2+b2=c2.

By the Pythagorean theorem and its converse, these are the lengths of a right triangle, with hypotenuse ccc. To see that the lengths make a triangle, build a right corner with legs aaa and bbb. The third side has positive length ccc by the theorem. The two leg entries can go in either order.

A triple is primitive when the greatest common factor (GCF) of its three entries is 111. The triangles below have the same shape. The small one has no common factor greater than 111, and each length of the larger one is three times its partner.

Multiplying all lengths by a positive integer kkk multiplies both sides of the squared equation by k2k^2k2. Dividing all lengths by their GCF reverses that operation and leaves a primitive triple. The GCF must be removed in full. Any common factor still left in the quotient would give a larger common factor of the original lengths.

Worked example 1 Find the original shape

Problem. A right triangle has side lengths 363636, 105105105, and 111111111 units. Find its primitive triple and the integer scale factor from the primitive triangle to this triangle.

Solution. The common factors of 363636 and 105105105 are 111 and 333. Since 111111111 is also divisible by 333, the GCF of all three lengths is 333.

Divide all three entries by 333.

(363,1053,1113)=(12,35,37).\left(\frac{36}{3},\frac{105}{3},\frac{111}{3}\right) =(12,35,37).(336​,3105​,3111​)=(12,35,37).

The primitive triple is (12,35,37)(12,35,37)(12,35,37). The scale factor to the given triangle is 333.

Build a triple from two squares

A sum and difference can give two sides whose squares differ by a perfect square. Start with 494949 and 444. Their sum is 535353 and their difference is 454545.

Factor the difference of the new squares. In the sum, the 444 terms cancel. In the difference, the 494949 terms cancel.

532−452=(53+45)(53−45)=(2⋅49)(2⋅4)=(2⋅7⋅2)2=282.\begin{aligned} 53^2-45^2&=(53+45)(53-45)\\ &=(2\cdot49)(2\cdot4)\\ &=(2\cdot7\cdot2)^2\\ &=28^2. \end{aligned}532−452​=(53+45)(53−45)=(2⋅49)(2⋅4)=(2⋅7⋅2)2=282.​

So 454545, 282828, and 535353 are side lengths of a right triangle. The product became a square because both starting numbers were squares.

Why the two-parameter construction gives a right triangle#

Take integers m>n>0m>n>0m>n>0. Write aaa for the difference of their squares and ccc for the sum.

a=m2−n2c=m2+n2\begin{aligned} a&=m^2-n^2\\ c&=m^2+n^2 \end{aligned}ac​=m2−n2=m2+n2​

Factoring uses the same sums and differences as with 494949 and 444.

c2−a2=(c+a)(c−a)=(2m2)(2n2)=(2mn)2.\begin{aligned} c^2-a^2&=(c+a)(c-a)\\ &=(2m^2)(2n^2)\\ &=(2mn)^2. \end{aligned}c2−a2​=(c+a)(c−a)=(2m2)(2n2)=(2mn)2.​

Use 2mn2mn2mn as the remaining leg bbb. The factored equation then gives a2+b2=c2a^2+b^2=c^2a2+b2=c2. All entries are positive because m>n>0m>n>0m>n>0. The choice of 777 and 222 in the numerical argument only supplied the two squares.

∎

The three entries (m2−n2, 2mn, m2+n2)(m^2-n^2,\ 2mn,\ m^2+n^2)(m2−n2, 2mn, m2+n2) are called Euclid’s formulas. The second entry is always even, but it may be the shorter or the longer leg.

Worked example 2 The even leg comes second

Problem. Generate the triple for the parameter pair (8,3)(8,3)(8,3). Give the two legs in increasing order and name the hypotenuse.

Solution. The difference gives one leg, the doubled product gives the other, and the sum gives the hypotenuse.

a=64−9a=55b=2⋅8⋅3b=48c=64+9c=73.\begin{aligned} a&=64-9\\ a&=55\\[2pt] b&=2\cdot8\cdot3\\ b&=48\\[2pt] c&=64+9\\ c&=73. \end{aligned}aabbcc​=64−9=55=2⋅8⋅3=48=64+9=73.​

The legs in increasing order are 484848 and 555555. The hypotenuse is 737373.

Tell which parameters give a primitive triple

Some pairs build a triangle whose sides have a common factor. A pair of integers is coprime if its GCF is 111. It has opposite parity if one integer is even and the other is odd.

Worked example 3 Two tests for the inputs

Problem. For each pair below, decide whether the output of Euclid’s formulas is primitive. When it is scaled, find the primitive triple and the scale factor.

Case A uses the parameter pair (5,4)(5,4)(5,4).

Case B uses the parameter pair (5,3)(5,3)(5,3).

Case C uses the parameter pair (6,3)(6,3)(6,3).

Solution. Case A gives (9,40,41)(9,40,41)(9,40,41). The GCF of 999 and 404040 is 111, so the triple is primitive. The parameters are coprime and have opposite parity.

Case B gives (16,30,34)(16,30,34)(16,30,34). Both parameters are odd, so both the difference and sum of their squares are even. The GCF of all three entries is 222, giving primitive triple (8,15,17)(8,15,17)(8,15,17) and scale factor 222.

Case C gives (27,36,45)(27,36,45)(27,36,45). Both parameters are multiples of 333, so the formulas use multiples of 323^232. The GCF of the three entries is 999, giving primitive triple (3,4,5)(3,4,5)(3,4,5) and scale factor 999.

The formulas give a primitive triple exactly when the parameters are coprime and have opposite parity.

Why coprimality and opposite parity give a primitive triple#

If the parameters share a factor d>1d>1d>1, each formula contains a factor d2d^2d2, just as 323^232 divided all the outputs in Case C.

If the parameters have the same parity, their square sum and square difference are even. The doubled product is already even, so 222 divides all three outputs.

Now take coprime parameters of opposite parity. For example, with the parameter pair (9,2)(9,2)(9,2), both 777777 and 858585 are odd, so 222 cannot divide the whole triple. Any odd prime dividing both 777777 and 858585 would divide their sum and difference.

85+77=2⋅8185−77=2⋅4.\begin{aligned} 85+77&=2\cdot81\\ 85-77&=2\cdot4. \end{aligned}85+7785−77​=2⋅81=2⋅4.​

That prime would have to divide both 999 and 222, since it is odd and every prime factor of a square comes from its base. The two parameters are coprime.

For any coprime parameters of opposite parity, aaa and ccc are odd, and the same sum and difference are 2m22m^22m2 and 2n22n^22n2. No odd prime can divide both because that prime would divide both mmm and nnn. So no prime divides all three outputs, and their GCF is 111.

∎

Recover the parameters of a primitive triple

The formulas can be reversed. For the primitive triple with legs 333333 and 565656 and hypotenuse 656565, use the odd leg for aaa and the even leg for bbb. The squares we want come from adding and subtracting the odd leg.

65+332=4965−332=16.\begin{aligned} \frac{65+33}{2}&=49\\ \frac{65-33}{2}&=16. \end{aligned}265+33​265−33​​=49=16.​

The two results are positive and coprime. Their product is (56/2)2(56/2)^2(56/2)2, a square. Since a prime can appear in at most one of the two factors, each factor must contain its own primes in pairs. Here 494949 contains two copies of 777, and 161616 contains four copies of 222. Taking positive square roots gives 777 and 444, which are coprime and have opposite parity.

Why every primitive triple has these parameters#

A primitive triple has exactly one even leg. Two even legs would make c2c^2c2 even and hence ccc even, giving a common factor of 222. Two odd legs would make c2c^2c2 leave remainder 222 on division by 444. A square has remainder 000 or 111: an even square is divisible by 444, and an odd square has the form 4t(t+1)+14t(t+1)+14t(t+1)+1 for some integer ttt. With one odd and one even leg, c2c^2c2 is odd, so ccc is odd.

Name the odd leg aaa and the even leg bbb. Define the positive integers

u=c+a2v=c−a2.\begin{aligned} u&=\frac{c+a}{2}\\ v&=\frac{c-a}{2}. \end{aligned}uv​=2c+a​=2c−a​.​

Both are integers since aaa and ccc are odd, and u>v>0u>v>0u>v>0 since c>a>0c>a>0c>a>0. Factoring the difference of squares gives

uv=(c+a)(c−a)4=c2−a24=(b2)2.\begin{aligned} uv&=\frac{(c+a)(c-a)}{4}\\ &=\frac{c^2-a^2}{4}\\ &=\left(\frac b2\right)^2. \end{aligned}uv​=4(c+a)(c−a)​=4c2−a2​=(2b​)2.​

Any prime shared by uuu and vvv would divide their sum ccc and their difference aaa. It would then divide b2b^2b2, since b2=c2−a2b^2=c^2-a^2b2=c2−a2, and so divide bbb as well. Primitivity excludes any such prime.

Because uvuvuv is a square, every prime in its factorization occurs an even number of times. Since uuu and vvv share no primes, those even counts already occur in each factor separately. The factors are the squares of positive integers mmm and nnn, respectively, with m>nm>nm>n. A shared prime of mmm and nnn would also be shared by uuu and vvv, so mmm and nnn are coprime. Their squares add to the odd integer ccc, which means mmm and nnn have opposite parity.

Add and subtract the two squares to recover ccc and aaa. Take the positive square root of uvuvuv to recover half of bbb.

c=m2+n2a=m2−n2b=2mn.\begin{aligned} c&=m^2+n^2\\ a&=m^2-n^2\\ b&=2mn. \end{aligned}cab​=m2+n2=m2−n2=2mn.​

The argument used the same sum, difference and prime-pairing steps as the numerical example. The odd leg and hypotenuse determine uuu and vvv, so they also determine the unique positive parameters with m>nm>nm>n.

∎

Solution. Use 111111 as the odd leg and 616161 as the hypotenuse.

m2=61+112=36n2=61−112=25.\begin{aligned} m^2&=\frac{61+11}{2}\\ &=36\\[2pt] n^2&=\frac{61-11}{2}\\ &=25. \end{aligned}m2n2​=261+11​=36=261−11​=25.​

2⋅6⋅5=60.2\cdot6\cdot5=60.2⋅6⋅5=60.

Separate the scale factor from the parameters

For any Pythagorean triple, divide by the GCF first. The quotient is primitive, so it has a unique pair of coprime, opposite-parity parameters. Then multiply the three generated entries by the original GCF kkk to recover the given triple. Leg order can be swapped.

Worked example 5 Recover both the scale and the shape

Problem. A right triangle has sides 132132132, 351351351, and 375375375 units. Find its integer scale factor from the primitive triangle, and find the parameters of that primitive triple.

Solution. The GCF of 132132132 and 351351351 is 333. Since 375375375 is divisible by 333, the GCF of all three is 333. Dividing gives primitive triple (44,117,125)(44,117,125)(44,117,125).

Use the odd leg 117117117 and the hypotenuse 125125125.

m2=125+1172=121n2=125−1172=4.\begin{aligned} m^2&=\frac{125+117}{2}\\ &=121\\[2pt] n^2&=\frac{125-117}{2}\\ &=4. \end{aligned}m2n2​=2125+117​=121=2125−117​=4.​

The parameters are 111111 and 222. Their doubled product is 444444, which matches the even leg of the primitive triple. The original triangle uses scale factor 333.

Common mistakes

Common mistakes

  • Same-parity parameters. They still give a right triangle. All three outputs are even, so it is scaled.
  • Shared parameter factors. Opposite parity alone cannot make a triple primitive. A factor shared by both parameters appears squared in all three outputs.
  • Leg order. In a primitive triple, use the odd leg with the hypotenuse to recover the squares. Either leg can be shorter.

Key takeaways

  • A primitive Pythagorean triple has GCF 111. Divide any triple by its GCF to find its primitive triangle.
  • Integers m>n>0m>n>0m>n>0 generate the triple (m2−n2, 2mn, m2+n2)(m^2-n^2,\ 2mn,\ m^2+n^2)(m2−n2, 2mn, m2+n2). It is primitive exactly when mmm and nnn are coprime and of opposite parity.
  • To reverse the formulas for a primitive triple, take the positive square roots of the half-sum and half-difference of the hypotenuse and odd leg.

A bit of history (optional)

A clay tablet now in Columbia University’s collection has rows of numbers pressed into it in cuneiform. In the early 1940s, historians Otto Neugebauer and Abraham Sachs interpreted the numbers on the tablet, known as Plimpton 322, as a record of what we call Pythagorean triples. That made the tablet evidence of knowledge about these triples long before Pythagoras.

The rows still leave room for a historical question. What job did this table do? Historian Eleanor Robson proposed that it was a teacher’s aid for preparing problems involving right triangles and pairs of numbers whose product is one. The surviving table preserves the numbers, while its purpose remains a matter of interpretation. Columbia University Libraries’ exhibition on Plimpton 322.
